## Why this project?

As AI advances, mathematical research and work in many other professions will increasingly be automated. Mathematics pursued for enjoyment is therefore likely to become more important, both for professional mathematicians and for mathematically interested people in other fields.

We believe that a mathematical analogue of the [Internet Movie Database (IMDb)](https://www.imdb.com/) could soon be useful in helping people find the results and proofs
they would most enjoy exploring.

We hope that this repository can be a step towards creating such an Internet Proof Database.

The basic idea of this project is to use a coding agent to extract mathematical results and their proofs and generate structured metadata for them. Even before feedback from external users is available, explicit, semi-objective criteria can help identify results and proofs that readers may find interesting or noteworthy.

## How to use the browser

We recommend using the Theorem Browser to identify interesting results or proofs, then exploring them in more detail with a chatbot or an agent.
Finding an interesting proof without exploring it further in this way would often be like finding a film on IMDb and watching only the trailer rather than the full film (though a short, complete proof may need no further exploration).

Of course, one can also ask a chatbot to suggest interesting theorems or proofs, just as one can ask it to recommend films. Like IMDb, the Theorem Browser has complementary strengths.
