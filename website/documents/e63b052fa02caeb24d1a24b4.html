Statement

For 1<p\le\infty, let T:L^p([0,1])\to C([0,1]) be (Tg)(x)=\int_0^x g(t)\,dt, using Lebesgue integration and the uniform norm on C([0,1]). Then T is a compact linear operator. Its composition with the natural inclusion C([0,1])\hookrightarrow L^p([0,1]) is also compact.

Proof 1: Hölder control and Arzelà–Ascoli

Dependency

On a compact Hausdorff space X, a pointwise bounded equicontinuous family in C(X,\mathbb R) has compact closure for the uniform norm. Equicontinuity means that for each x and \varepsilon>0 there is a neighborhood U of x with |f(y)-f(x)|<\varepsilon for every f in the family and y\in U.

Hölder estimate

To every function g that is p-integrable on [0, 1], with 1 < p ≤ ∞, associate the function G defined on [0, 1] by

{\displaystyle G(x)=\int _{0}^{x}g(t)\,\mathrm {d} t.}

Let F be the set of functions G corresponding to functions g in the unit ball of the space Lp([0, 1]). If q is the Hölder conjugate of p, defined by ⁠1/p⁠ + ⁠1/q⁠ = 1, then Hölder's inequality implies that all functions in F satisfy a Hölder condition with α = ⁠1/q⁠ and constant M = 1.

Consequently, the correspondence g → G defines a compact linear operator T between the Banach spaces Lp([0, 1]) and C([0, 1]). Composing with the injection of C([0, 1]) into Lp([0, 1]), one sees that T acts compactly from Lp([0, 1]) to itself.

Level justification

Apply Lebesgue Holder to an Lp unit ball, use Arzela-Ascoli and compose with the inclusion. Lp equivalence classes, Lebesgue integration and compact operators require measure-theoretic functional analysis.

Routine omissions

Uniform boundedness follows from the stated Hölder bound and G(0)=0; boundedness of the inclusion into Lp is routine. Source additionally calls the image F compact, but its closedness is not argued and is not needed for the retained operator conclusion.
