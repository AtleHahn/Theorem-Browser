Statement

If X is a centered real Gaussian vector with covariance matrix M, then \operatorname{Cov}(X_i^2,X_j^2)=2M_{ij}^2.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A centered finite Gaussian vector is a linear image of independent standard normal coordinates; covariance records expected products, and covariance of squares is a fourth moment minus a product of second moments. These are linked finite-dimensional density/moment definitions over undergraduate integration and linear algebra, not infinite Gaussian-process theory.

Proof 1: Wick fourth-moment substitution

Let {\displaystyle X} be an {\displaystyle n}-dimensional centered Gaussian random variable with covariance {\displaystyle \langle X_{i}X_{j}\rangle =M_{ij}}. Here angle brackets denote expectation. Then the covariance of {\displaystyle X_{i}^{2}} and {\displaystyle X_{j}^{2}} is

{\displaystyle \operatorname {Cov} \left(X_{i}^{2},X_{j}^{2}\right)=\left\langle X_{i}^{2}X_{j}^{2}\right\rangle -\left\langle X_{i}^{2}\right\rangle \left\langle X_{j}^{2}\right\rangle }

Using Wick's theorem to develop {\displaystyle \left\langle X_{i}^{2}X_{j}^{2}\right\rangle =2\left\langle X_{i}X_{j}\right\rangle ^{2}+\left\langle X_{i}^{2}\right\rangle \left\langle X_{j}^{2}\right\rangle } we have

{\displaystyle \operatorname {Cov} \left(X_{i}^{2},X_{j}^{2}\right)=2\left\langle X_{i}X_{j}\right\rangle ^{2}=2M_{ij}^{2}}

Level justification

Substitute the supplied Gaussian fourth-moment identity into the definition of covariance and cancel the product of variances. Finite-dimensional probability and moments suffice.
