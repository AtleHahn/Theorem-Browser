Statement

Let q be prime and let a be an integer with 1\le a\le q-1. Let \pi(X) count all primes at most X, and \pi(X;q,a) count those congruent to a modulo q. Then \lim_{X\to\infty}\pi(X;q,a)/\pi(X)=1/(q-1).

Proof 1: Substitute a prime modulus into the proportion theorem

Use the prime-proportion theorem: the number of feasible progressions modulo d—those where a and d do not have a common factor other than 1—is given by Euler's totient function \varphi(d), and the proportion of primes in each of those is 1/\varphi(d).

If d is a prime number q, each of the q-1 progressions

\begin{gathered} q+1,\ 2q+1,\ 3q+1,\ \dots,\\ q+2,\ 2q+2,\ 3q+2,\ \dots,\\ \dots\\ q+q-1,\ 2q+q-1,\ 3q+q-1,\ \dots \end{gathered}

(all except q,2q,3q,\dots) contains a proportion 1/(q-1) of the primes. Adding the finitely many omitted initial integers does not change the limiting proportion.
