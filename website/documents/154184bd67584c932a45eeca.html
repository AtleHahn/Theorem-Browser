Statement

For any two classical propositions P and Q,

(P\to Q)\ \leftrightarrow\ (\neg Q\to\neg P)

is true under every assignment of truth values. Thus a conditional and its contrapositive have the same truth value. Here each proposition is either true or false, \neg reverses its truth value, A\to B is false exactly when A is true and B is false, and \leftrightarrow asserts equality of truth values.

Proof 1: Definition of material implication

Using the definition of the conditional, commutativity of disjunction and classical double negation,

P\to Q\ \equiv\ \neg P\lor Q\ \equiv\ Q\lor\neg P\ \equiv\ \neg Q\to\neg P.

Here A\lor B is true when at least one of A,B is true.

Proof 2: Contradiction in both directions

Assume P\to Q and \neg Q. If P were true, Q would be true, a contradiction. Hence \neg P, giving \neg Q\to\neg P.

Conversely, assume \neg Q\to\neg P and P. If Q were false, \neg P would follow, contradicting P. Since Q is either true or false, Q is true. This gives P\to Q and proves the equivalence.

Proof 3: Identical falsity conditions

The conditional P\to Q is true exactly when \neg(P\land\neg Q) is true. Commutativity of conjunction gives \neg(\neg Q\land P). Put R=\neg Q and S=\neg P; classical double negation gives \neg S=P. The last expression is therefore \neg(R\land\neg S), which is precisely the truth condition for R\to S, that is, \neg Q\to\neg P. Here A\land B is true exactly when both propositions are true.
