Statement

Let k\ge0 be an integer, G a group, and let N_1,\ldots,N_k be a finite collection of nilpotent normal subgroups of G. Then \langle N_1,\ldots,N_k\rangle is nilpotent.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory group and ring theory supplies subgroups, quotients, commutators and homomorphisms. Any added terms such as perfect, nilpotent or virtually free describe explicit subgroup conditions; they are small algebraic extensions without representation or geometric-group theory.

Proof 1: Iterate the two-subgroup theorem — outline

Use Fitting's theorem: if M and N are nilpotent normal subgroups of a group G, then their product MN is also a nilpotent normal subgroup of G.

By induction it follows also that the subgroup generated by a finite collection of nilpotent normal subgroups is nilpotent.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Iterate the supplied two-subgroup theorem. Nilpotence adds the terminating commutator-series definition to introductory groups and normal subgroups; the proof requires no theory establishing Fitting theorem.
