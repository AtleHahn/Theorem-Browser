Statement

Let G have vertex set \mathbb R, with an edge from x to x+q\pm\sqrt2 for each rational q. Then G has no cycle of odd length, and each finite subgraph admits a proper two-coloring.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The graph is explicitly defined by rational differences plus or minus the square root of two. Odd cycles and a two-coloring are finite combinatorial concepts using elementary arithmetic. This rating concerns understanding the statement, independently of the difficulty of its proof.

Proof 1: Track rational-plus-irrational parity along paths — outline

A path starting at x alternates between numbers differing from x by a rational plus an even multiple of \sqrt2 and numbers differing by a rational plus an odd multiple. These two types cannot coincide, so an odd-length path cannot return to its starting point. Thus there are no odd cycles and each finite subgraph is two-colorable.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Track parity of the integer coefficient of square root two along a finite path and use its irrationality. The finite two-coloring criterion is an explicit graph fact; no infinite graph theory is required.
