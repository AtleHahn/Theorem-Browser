Statement

Let a\le b be real numbers, and let f:[a,b]\to[-\infty,\infty) satisfy \limsup_{y\to x}f(y)\le f(x) at every x\in[a,b]. Then f is bounded above and attains its supremum (possibly -\infty).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Real interval compactness; Upper semicontinuity defined by limsup; Extended real values. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Replace continuity by the upper-limit inequality

If f(x)=-\infty for all x in [a,b], then the supremum is also -\infty and the theorem is true. In all other cases we use the sequential proofs of the boundedness and extreme value theorems, with upper semi-continuity in place of continuity, as follows.

Suppose the function f is not bounded above on the interval [a,b]. Pick a sequence (x_n)_{n\geq1} such that x_n\in[a,b] and f(x_n)>n. Because [a,b] is bounded, the Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem implies that there exists a convergent subsequence (x_{n_k})_{k\geq1} of (x_n). Denote its limit by x. As [a,b] is closed, it contains x. The upper semi-continuity of f at x implies that the limit superior of the subsequence \{f(x_{n_k})\} is bounded above by f(x)<\infty. But f(x_{n_k})>n_k\geq k for every k, which implies that f(x_{n_k}) diverges to +\infty, a contradiction. Therefore, f is bounded above on [a,b].

By the Dedekind-completeness of the real numbers, the least upper bound (supremum) M of f exists and is finite. It is necessary to find a point d in [a,b] such that M=f(d). Let n be a positive integer. As M is the least upper bound, M-1/n is not an upper bound for f. Therefore, there exists d_n in [a,b] so that M-1/n<f(d_n). This defines a sequence \{d_n\}. Since M is an upper bound for f, we have M-1/n<f(d_n)\leq M for all n. Therefore, the sequence \{f(d_n)\} converges to M.

The Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem tells us that there exists a subsequence \{d_{n_k}\}, which converges to some d and, as [a,b] is closed, d is in [a,b]. Upper semi-continuity of f at d implies that the limit superior of the subsequence \{f(d_{n_k})\} is bounded above by f(d). But \{f(d_{n_k})\} is a subsequence of \{f(d_n)\} that converges to M, so M\leq f(d)\leq M. Therefore, f attains its supremum M at d.
