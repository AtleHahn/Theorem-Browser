Simpler proof assessment

Likely alternative prerequisite level: Undergraduate level; lowest recorded proof level: Beyond undergraduate level. Apply the maximum-modulus principle on the annulus to g(z)^q z^p, with integer p and positive integer q. Choose p/q tending to the balancing logarithmic slope of the two positive boundary maxima, take qth roots, then pass to the limit. Zero boundary maxima follow by a limiting bound. This directly proves the full annular inequality and convexity using the stated elementary maximum principle plus real limits, without the strip theorem or its unbounded-domain framework.

Statement

Let 0<r<R and let g be continuous on the closed annulus r\le|z|\le R and holomorphic in its interior. Put M(\rho)=\max_{|z|=\rho}|g(z)|. If \log\rho=t\log r+(1-t)\log R with 0<t<1, then M(\rho)\le M(r)^tM(R)^{1-t}. The endpoint cases are equality; wherever M is positive, \log M(\rho) is convex in \log\rho.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex differentiability extends the calculus derivative. Starlikeness is explicitly radial segment containment, the primary factor is an explicit exponential-polynomial formula, and the remaining bounds use ordinary suprema and convexity. No canonical infinite products or complex geometry are required.

Proof 1: Three-circle inequality by exponential substitution — source argument

Set f(w)=g(e^w) on \log r\le\Re w\le\log R. This is bounded and continuous on the closed strip and holomorphic inside. As \Im w varies, e^w runs around the circle of radius e^{\Re w}, so the strip supremum at real part s equals M(e^s). Apply the three-lines inequality to f to obtain the displayed annular inequality.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Lift the annulus to a complex strip by the exponential and apply the three-lines theorem. Retain beyond undergraduate provisionally for general holomorphic strip/annulus functions and maximum-modulus convexity, beyond a single elementary complex differentiation rule.
