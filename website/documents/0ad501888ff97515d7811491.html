Simpler proof assessment

Likely alternative prerequisite level: Undergraduate level; lowest recorded proof level: Beyond undergraduate level. DeVos's checked finite-set induction proves |A+B|>=|A+H|+|B+H|-|H| for every abelian group. In the quotient by the finite stabilizer H, this is |(A+B)/H|>=|A/H|+|B/H|-1. The strict hypothesis |A+B|<|A|+|B|<=|A+H|+|B+H| gives the reverse integer bound in that quotient, hence equality. Finite groups, cosets and set counting suffice, with no Haar measure.

Statement

Let G be an abelian group and A,B\subseteq G finite nonempty sets with |A+B|<|A|+|B|. Put H=\{g\in G:g+(A+B)=A+B\}. Then |A+B|=|A+H|+|B+H|-|H|, where A+B=\{a+b:a\in A,b\in B\}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory abelian group theory supplies addition and cosets. Sumsets and their translation stabilizer are explicitly defined finite sets and subgroups, so the cardinality inequality adds only elementary notation.

Proof 1: Specialize Haar measure to counting measure

Apply the locally compact abelian group version of Kneser’s theorem to G with the discrete topology and counting Haar measure. That theorem states, under the strict sumset-measure inequality, that m(A+B)=m(A+H)+m(B+H)-m(H). Counting measure turns this identity into the stated cardinality identity.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Specialize the locally compact Haar-measure theorem to the discrete group and counting Haar measure. Unlike a purely finite counting proof, the supplied argument invokes topological groups and Haar measure, a substantial advanced prerequisite.
