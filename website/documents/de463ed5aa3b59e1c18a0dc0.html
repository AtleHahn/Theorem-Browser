First-order Peano arithmetic has uncountable models and therefore does not uniquely characterize the standard natural numbers. This exposes a limit of first-order axiomatization: agreement with all the axioms can coexist with a radically different underlying domain.

Statement

For every uncountable cardinal \kappa, first-order Peano arithmetic has a model of cardinality at least \kappa. Such a model is not isomorphic to the standard arithmetic structure on \mathbb N.

Proof 1: Apply the large-model theorem to arithmetic

The standard natural numbers form an infinite model of Peano arithmetic. Let T be this first-order theory and let \kappa be the given uncountable cardinal number. Add to the language of T one constant symbol for every element of \kappa. Then add to T a collection of sentences that say that the objects denoted by any two distinct constant symbols from the new collection are distinct (this is a collection of \kappa^2 sentences). Since every finite subset of this new theory is satisfiable by the infinite standard model, the entire extended theory is satisfiable by the compactness theorem. But any model of the extended theory has cardinality at least \kappa.

Thus there are nonstandard models of Peano arithmetic with uncountably many “natural numbers”: such a model cannot be isomorphic to the countable standard model.
