Statement

Here a formal term is an expression built from variable, constant and function symbols, compared as written without evaluating it. A listed substitution simultaneously replaces each occurrence of its listed variables by the assigned terms, fixes all other variables, and leaves constant and function symbols unchanged; replacements are not repeated inside the inserted terms in the same application. Let x,y,x_1,y_2 be pairwise distinct variables. The substitution \sigma=\{x\mapsto x_1,y\mapsto y_2\}, fixing all others, is flat and linear on its domain but is not a permutation of all variables. Flat means every replacement is a variable; linear on the replaced domain means an expression containing precisely those domain variables once each acquires no repeated variable; a renaming is a permutation, that is, a bijection of the variables.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Flatness, linearity and renaming are defined as finite replacement and one-to-one variable rules.

Proof 1: Two different variables acquire the same image

Both replacement terms are variables, and they are distinct, giving flatness and linearity on the replaced domain. But both y and the unchanged variable y_2 map to y_2, so the total variable map is not injective and hence not a renaming.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The given replacements are distinct variables, but y and the fixed variable y2 have the same image. The explicit definitions reduce the claim to elementary symbolic substitution and failure of one-to-one mapping.
