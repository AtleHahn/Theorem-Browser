Self-adjoint operators can be represented as multiplication by real functions on suitable measure spaces. This grounds the spectral description of quantum observables, while the physical interpretation of measurement requires additional postulates.

Statement

Let A:D(A)\subseteq H\to H be a self-adjoint operator on a complex Hilbert space, possibly unbounded. Self-adjointness includes equality of the dense domains and actions of A and its Hilbert-space adjoint A^*.

There exist a measure space (X,\Sigma,\mu), a real-valued measurable function \lambda on X, and a unitary map U:H\to L^2(X,\mu) such that

U(D(A))=D(M_\lambda)=\{f\in L^2(X,\mu):\lambda f\in L^2(X,\mu)\}, \qquad UAU^{-1}f=M_\lambda f=\lambda f.

If A is bounded, \lambda can be chosen essentially bounded, and

\|A\|=\|\lambda\|_{L^\infty(X,\mu)}.

Proof 1: Cyclic measure representations and the bounded transform (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorem4.1; Sections4.2–4.4, pp.50–56; Theorem2.1, pp.21–22).

The cyclic decomposition in Section4.3 is indexed by an arbitrary set, so no separability assumption is needed. For self-adjoint A, its bounded transform has a real multiplier \zeta with |\zeta|<1 almost everywhere; the reconstructed multiplier is \lambda=\zeta/\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}, set to 0 on the null exceptional set. Lemma4.9 identifies the closed operators themselves, including their domains. Theorem2.1 defines the maximal multiplication domain and proves \|M_\lambda\|=\|\lambda\|_\infty in the bounded case.
