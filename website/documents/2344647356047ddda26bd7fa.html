Statement

Let X_1,\ldots,X_n be independent real random variables satisfying a_i\le X_i\le b_i almost surely, and put S_n=\sum_iX_i and V=\sum_i(b_i-a_i)^2>0. For t>0, \mathbb P(S_n-\mathbb ES_n\ge t)\le e^{-2t^2/V} and \mathbb P(|S_n-\mathbb ES_n|\ge t)\le2e^{-2t^2/V}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A finite family of random variables adds event probabilities, independence as factorization, and expectations as averages/integrals to undergraduate analysis. Almost-sure bounds mean exceptions have probability zero. These linked elementary probability definitions suffice for this finite-sum tail estimate; no random-sequence limit theory or measure-space construction is needed.

Proof 1: Exponential Markov bound and quadratic optimization

We quote:

Hoeffding's lemma—Suppose X is a real random variable such that {\displaystyle X\in \left[a,b\right]} almost surely. Then

{\displaystyle \mathrm {E} \left[e^{s\left(X-\mathrm {E} \left[X\right]\right)}\right]\leq \exp \left({\tfrac {1}{8}}s^{2}(b-a)^{2}\right).}

The proof of Hoeffding's inequality then follows similarly to concentration inequalities like Chernoff bounds.

Proof

As in the theorem statement, suppose X1, ..., Xn are n independent random variables such that {\displaystyle X_{i}\in [a_{i},b_{i}]} almost surely for all i, and let {\displaystyle S_{n}=X_{1}+\cdots +X_{n}}.

Then for s, t > 0, Markov's inequality and the independence of Xi implies:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\operatorname {P} \left(S_{n}-\mathrm {E} \left[S_{n}\right]\geq t\right)&=\operatorname {P} \left(\exp(s(S_{n}-\mathrm {E} \left[S_{n}\right]))\geq \exp(st)\right)\\&\leq \exp(-st)\mathrm {E} \left[\exp(s(S_{n}-\mathrm {E} \left[S_{n}\right]))\right]\\&=\exp(-st)\prod _{i=1}^{n}\mathrm {E} \left[\exp(s(X_{i}-\mathrm {E} \left[X_{i}\right]))\right]\\&\leq \exp(-st)\prod _{i=1}^{n}\exp {\Big (}{\frac {s^{2}(b_{i}-a_{i})^{2}}{8}}{\Big )}\\&=\exp \left(-st+{\tfrac {1}{8}}s^{2}\sum _{i=1}^{n}(b_{i}-a_{i})^{2}\right)\end{aligned}}}

This upper bound is the best for the value of s minimizing the value inside the exponential. This can be done easily by optimizing a quadratic, giving

{\displaystyle s={\frac {4t}{\sum _{i=1}^{n}(b_{i}-a_{i})^{2}}}.}

Writing the above bound for this value of s, we get the desired bound:

{\displaystyle \operatorname {P} \left(S_{n}-\mathrm {E} \left[S_{n}\right]\geq t\right)\leq \exp \left(-{\frac {2t^{2}}{\sum _{i=1}^{n}(b_{i}-a_{i})^{2}}}\right).}

Apply the same upper-tail bound to -X_1,\ldots,-X_n and add the two tail probabilities to obtain the absolute-deviation bound.
