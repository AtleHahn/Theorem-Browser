No first-order theory uniquely characterizes the standard natural-number structure among all models. This separates an intended interpretation of arithmetic from what its first-order axioms alone can determine.

Statement

The structure (\mathbb N,+,\times,0,1) cannot be the unique model up to isomorphism of a first-order theory in its displayed signature.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

First-order satisfaction and cardinalities of models require model theory and set theory beyond the elementary baselines.

Proof 1: Apply the model-cardinality theorem

Use Löwenheim–Skolem: an infinite structure in signature \sigma has an elementarily equivalent structure of every infinite cardinality \kappa\ge|\sigma|, smaller ones as elementary substructures and larger ones as elementary extensions.

It follows from this theorem that the theory of (\mathbb N,+,\times,0,1) (the theory of true first-order arithmetic) has uncountable models. Thus these models cannot be isomorphic to the countable natural-number structure. The Löwenheim–Skolem theorem shows that axiomatizations characterizing (\mathbb N,+,\times,0,1) up to isomorphism cannot be first-order.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the model-cardinality theorem to true arithmetic and distinguish models by cardinality. First-order theories, structures and elementary equivalence/extensions require substantial model-theoretic background, even though the final cardinality obstruction is simple.
