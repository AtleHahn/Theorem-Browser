No continuous surjection F:[0,1]\to[0,1]^2 is injective.

Proof 1: Contrast the cut points of the interval and square

A non-self-intersecting continuous curve cannot fill the unit square because that will make the curve a homeomorphism from the unit interval onto the unit square (any continuous bijection from a compact space onto a Hausdorff space is a homeomorphism). But a unit square has no cut-point, and so cannot be homeomorphic to the unit interval, in which all points except the endpoints are cut-points.

A cut-point is a point whose removal disconnects the space.

Proof 2: Apply the dimensional obstruction to a surjection

Use the stated special case of Netto’s theorem: no continuous bijection exists from the unit interval to the unit square. If the given surjection were also injective, it would be such a continuous bijection. Hence some point of the square has more than one preimage.
