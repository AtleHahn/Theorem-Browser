Statement

Let a\le b be real and let \delta:[a,b]\to(0,\infty) be any function. A tagged partition of [a,s] consists of finitely many endpoints and tags a=x_0\le t_1\le x_1\le\cdots\le t_n\le x_n=s; it is \delta-fine if each [x_{j-1},x_j] is contained in (t_j-\delta(t_j),t_j+\delta(t_j)). Suppose a \delta-fine partition ends at s<r\le b and s>\max(a,r-\delta(r)/2). Append the interval [s,u] tagged at r, where u=\min(b,r+\delta(r)/2). The result is a \delta-fine partition of [a,u] and u\ge r.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Tagged partitions and fineness are explicitly finite lists of real inequalities, with no integration theory needed.

Proof 1: Append an interval tagged at the target (outline)

Set the new tag to r and the new endpoint to \min(b,r+\delta(r)/2). This endpoint is greater than s, and the new interval is contained in the open ball of radius \delta(r) about its tag. Appending it gives a valid fine partition reaching the target.

Check the new interval endpoints lie within the explicitly defined radius about r and that the tag lies inside the interval. This uses only real inequalities, minima and finite lists.
