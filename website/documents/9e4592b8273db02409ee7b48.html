Statement

Let G=\{g_1,\ldots,g_n\} be a finite group, and associate an independent commuting indeterminate x_g to each g\in G. Form the n\times n matrix

X_G=(x_{g_i g_j})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}.

If r is the number of conjugacy classes of G, then there are pairwise nonproportional irreducible polynomials P_1,\ldots,P_r\in\mathbb C[x_g:g\in G] such that

\det X_G=\prod_{j=1}^r P_j^{\deg P_j}.

Thus the number of distinct irreducible factors is r, and each has multiplicity equal to its total degree. Two polynomials are proportional if one is a nonzero constant multiple of the other; a conjugacy class consists of elements hgh^{-1} for a fixed g and varying h\in G.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Finite groups, determinants and irreducibility are undergraduate algebra. Multivariable total degree and multiplicity are small extensions of polynomial factorization; no representation theory is required to understand this formulation.

Proof 1: Matrix blocks of the complex group algebra (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Section5, pp.11–13, including Theorem4).

Conrad uses (x_{gh^{-1}}). Let \varepsilon be the sign of the column permutation h\mapsto h^{-1}; then the stored determinant is \varepsilon\det(x_{gh^{-1}}). Absorb \varepsilon into the degree-one trivial-character factor \sum_g x_g. For a factor of degree d, the identity character value is d; the coefficient of x_e^{d-1}x_h gives the character value only for h\ne e. Finally, the number of matrix blocks is the dimension of the group-algebra center: class sums form a basis of that center, giving exactly r factors.

The source identifies the multiplication-table matrix with a regular-representation matrix without accounting for inverse indexing and its determinant sign. Its nonproportionality argument also uses a coefficient formula that fails for the identity group element. No complete proof is retained, and these defects have not been silently repaired.

Level justification

The complete statement uses finite groups, multivariable polynomials and determinants. The linked group-algebra decomposition follows from invariant inner products, orthogonal complements and scalar intertwiners. Generic determinant irreducibility follows from its degree one in each row and column. These dependency proofs use undergraduate abstract algebra and linear algebra, with representation terminology describing finite-dimensional matrix actions.
