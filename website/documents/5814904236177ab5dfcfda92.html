Statement

Let X,Y be compact Hausdorff spaces. If f:X\to Y has closed graph in X\times Y, then f is continuous.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Basic point-set topology and real analysis supply compactness, closed sets, continuity, metrics and completeness. The Cantor-set construction or cofinite topology, where present, is a direct set-based example learnable briefly. The complete assertion needs no algebraic or differential topology.

Proof 1: Use compact-to-Hausdorff mapping on the graph

Let {\displaystyle f:X\to Y} be a map with closed graph between compact Hausdorff spaces. Then it factors as

{\displaystyle f:X{\overset {i}{\to }}\Gamma _{f}{\overset {q}{\to }}Y}.

Here \Gamma_f is the graph, i(x)=(x,f(x)), and q is the second projection. Now, {\displaystyle i} is the inverse of the projection {\displaystyle p:\Gamma _{f}\to X}. So, if the open mapping theorem holds for {\displaystyle p}; i.e., {\displaystyle p} is an open mapping, then {\displaystyle i} is continuous and then {\displaystyle f} is continuous (as the composition of continuous maps).

The argument applies here because \Gamma_f is compact and p:\Gamma_f\to X is a continuous bijection onto a Hausdorff space; by the compact-to-Hausdorff mapping theorem, p is a homeomorphism and hence open.

Level justification

The graph is a closed subset of a compact product. Its first projection is a continuous bijection to a Hausdorff space, hence a homeomorphism; compose its inverse with the second projection. All ingredients are basic point-set topology.
