Statement

Let f(x,y)=g(\sqrt{x^2+y^2}) be an integrable radial function on \mathbb R^2. Define its Abel projection by (Ag)(x)=\int_{\mathbb R}g(\sqrt{x^2+y^2})\,dy almost everywhere. With the Fourier convention e^{-2\pi i xk} and zeroth-order Hankel transform normalized by

(Hg)(\rho)=2\pi\int_0^\infty g(r)J_0(2\pi r\rho)r\,dr,

where J_0 is the Bessel function of order zero, one has \widehat{Ag}(k)=(Hg)(|k|).

Proof 1: Identify the radial Fourier slice (outline)

If the two-dimensional function f is circularly symmetric, it may be represented as f(x,y)=g(r), where r=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}. In this case the projection onto any projection line will be the Abel transform Ag. The two-dimensional Fourier transform of f will be a circularly symmetric function given by the zeroth-order Hankel transform Hg, with the normalization in the statement, which will therefore also represent any slice through the origin. The projection-slice theorem then states that the Fourier transform of the projection equals the slice, or

\widehat{Ag}(k)=(Hg)(|k|).

Level justification

Identify a radial Lebesgue Fourier transform with a Bessel-kernel Hankel transform and apply the projection-slice theorem to an almost-everywhere projection. These linked transforms and measure-theoretic hypotheses require advanced analysis.
