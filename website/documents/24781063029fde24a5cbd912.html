Statement

Let K\in\mathbb N_0 and S_K=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb N_0^2:xy\ge K\}. Order pairs coordinatewise: (u,v)\le(x,y) means u\le x and v\le y. Call a pair minimal in S_K if no distinct pair in S_K is below it. Every minimal pair (x,y) satisfies x\le K and y\le K. Consequently there are finitely many minimal pairs.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Coordinatewise order and minimality are explicitly defined on nonnegative integer pairs. Only integer products and finite bounds are needed.

Proof 1: Decrease a coordinate beyond the threshold

If a minimal pair had x>K, then (x-1,y) would still lie in S_K, contradicting minimality. The same argument with the coordinates exchanged rules out y>K. Thus all minimal pairs lie among the finitely many nonnegative integer pairs with both coordinates at most K.

Level

Level justification

Decrease any coordinate exceeding K and preserve the product threshold; then count the finite bounding square. Elementary integer inequalities suffice, including the K=0 case.
