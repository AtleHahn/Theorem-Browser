Statement

Let w:[0,2\pi]\to(0,\infty) be Lebesgue integrable, and define

c_k=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}w(\theta)e^{-ik\theta}\,d\theta,\qquad T_n(w)=(c_{j-k})_{0\le j,k<n}.

Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}(\det T_n(w))^{1/n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\det T_n(w)}{\det T_{n-1}(w)}=\exp\left(\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log w(\theta)\,d\theta\right).

The integral of \log w may equal -\infty, with \exp(-\infty)=0. The matrices T_n are Toeplitz (constant along diagonals); i^2=-1, and logarithms are natural. Set \det T_0=1.
