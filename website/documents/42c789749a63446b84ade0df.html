Statement

Let n\ge1, 0<\lambda<1 and -1/n\le p\le+\infty. Let f,g,h:\mathbb R^n\to[0,\infty) be Lebesgue integrable. Define M_s(a,b;\lambda)=0 whenever ab=0. For a,b>0, define M_s=((1-\lambda)a^s+\lambda b^s)^{1/s} for finite s\ne0, M_0=a^{1-\lambda}b^\lambda, M_{+\infty}=\max(a,b) and M_{-\infty}=\min(a,b). Suppose for every x,y\in\mathbb R^n,

h((1-\lambda)x+\lambda y)\ge M_p(f(x),g(y);\lambda).

Then \int h\ge M_q(\int f,\int g;\lambda), where all integrals are over \mathbb R^n and q=p/(np+1), with q=-\infty at p=-1/n and q=1/n at p=+\infty.
