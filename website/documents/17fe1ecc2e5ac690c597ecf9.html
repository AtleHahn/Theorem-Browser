Statement

For positive integers N and i, there are integers j,n_j,\ldots,n_i such that 1\le j\le i, n_i>n_{i-1}>\cdots>n_j\ge j, and

N=\binom{n_i}{i}+\binom{n_{i-1}}{i-1}+\cdots+\binom{n_j}{j}.

Here \binom ab is the usual binomial coefficient for nonnegative integers a\ge b.

Proof 1: Greedy construction — outline

Start with remainder N and lower index i. Choose n_i maximal with \binom{n_i}{i}\le N. Subtract that coefficient from the remainder, decrease the lower index by one, and repeat until the remainder is zero. This is the greedy construction of the displayed expansion.

Level justification

Choose each largest admissible binomial coefficient and decrease the lower index. Pascal’s identity bounds the remainder and ensures strictly decreasing upper indices. The source outlines a finite algorithm using elementary integer binomial arithmetic.
