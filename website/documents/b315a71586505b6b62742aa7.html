Statement

Let N>1 and b\in\mathbb Z satisfy b^2\equiv1\pmod N but b\not\equiv1,-1\pmod N. Then both \gcd(b-1,N) and \gcd(b+1,N) are proper nontrivial divisors of N, so N is composite.

Proof 1: Take gcds with the two linear factors — outline

When b^2\equiv1\pmod N with b\not\equiv\pm1\pmod N, the source gives the nontrivial divisors of N as

\gcd(b-1,N)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\gcd(b+1,N).

It supplies this factor recipe without the elementary divisibility verification.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Factor b^2-1 and use modular invertibility to show each gcd is nontrivial; the excluded residues keep them proper. Elementary gcds and modular arithmetic suffice for the stated recipe.
