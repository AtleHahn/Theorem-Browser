Statement

Let A,B be Hermitian d\times d complex matrices, d\ge1, and let e^A denote the matrix exponential. For p\ge1 put \|X\|_p=(\operatorname{tr}((XX^*)^{p/2}))^{1/p}. For every integer N\ge1, \|e^{A+B}\|_{2^N}\le\|e^Ae^B\|_{2^N}.

Proof 1: Stop the Golden chain at the squared exponential factors

Use Golden’s dyadic trace-power inequality as an explicit prerequisite: for positive semidefinite Hermitian matrices X,Y of the same finite size, integers n\ge0 and 0\le j\le n, \operatorname{tr}((XY)^{2^n})\le\operatorname{tr}((X^{2^j}Y^{2^j})^{2^{n-j}}).

It suffices to show that

\operatorname{tr}((e^{A+B})^{2^N}) \le\operatorname{tr}((e^{2A}e^{2B})^{2^{N-1}}).

By the Lie product formula and Golden's inequality (for n\ge N-1),

\begin{aligned} \operatorname{tr}((e^{A+B})^{2^N}) &=\lim_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{tr} \left((e^{2^{N-n}A}e^{2^{N-n}B})^{2^n}\right)\\ &\le\lim_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{tr} \left((e^{2A}e^{2B})^{2^{N-1}}\right)\\ &=\operatorname{tr}((e^{2A}e^{2B})^{2^{N-1}}). \end{aligned}

Here the Golden chain is stopped at the factors e^{2A},e^{2B}. By cyclicity and XX^*=e^Ae^{2B}e^A for X=e^Ae^B, the last trace is \|e^Ae^B\|_{2^N}^{2^N}; the left side is \|e^{A+B}\|_{2^N}^{2^N}. Taking 2^Nth roots proves the inequality.
