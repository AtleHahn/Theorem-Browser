Among distributions on a fixed finite set, the uniform distribution maximizes entropy. This supports interpreting entropy as uncertainty relative to specified possible outcomes, rather than as an assumption-free measure of ignorance.

Statement

Let n\ge1, b>1 and p_i\ge0 with \sum_{i=1}^n p_i=1. Then H_b(P)=-\sum_i p_i\log_b p_i\le\log_b n, with 0\log_b0=0. Equality holds if and only if every p_i=1/n.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Entropy is explicitly a finite logarithmic sum with its zero-term convention; its upper bound is an elementary expression.

Proof 1: Compare with the uniform distribution

In Gibbs’ inequality choose the uniform comparison distribution q_i=1/n. Its cross entropy is -\sum_i p_i\log_b(1/n)=\log_b n. Gibbs’ equality condition says equality holds exactly when p_i=q_i=1/n for every i.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Substitute q_i=1/n into the supplied finite logarithmic inequality and use sum p_i=1. Its stated equality condition gives the uniform distribution. Only finite sums, logarithms and probabilities are needed.
