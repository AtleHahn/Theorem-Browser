Statement

Let X be a closed oriented smooth simply connected four-manifold with negative-definite intersection form Q on H^2(X;\mathbb Z), and let P be a principal SU(2) bundle of second Chern number one. Let n(Q) count unordered pairs \{\alpha,-\alpha\} with Q(\alpha,\alpha)=-1. Then X is oriented cobordant to a disjoint union of n(Q) copies of \mathbb{CP}^2, each with an unspecified choice of orientation.

Proof 1: Cobordism from the reducible cones and the moduli-space end — outline

Choose a generic Riemannian metric. Let \mathcal M be the five-dimensional moduli space of anti-self-dual connections on P, modulo gauge. This moduli space is non-compact and smooth away from the points corresponding to reducible connections.

Results of Clifford Taubes and Karen Uhlenbeck show that whilst {\displaystyle {\mathcal {M}}} is non-compact, its structure at infinity can be readily described. Namely, there is an open subset of {\displaystyle {\mathcal {M}}}, say {\displaystyle {\mathcal {M}}_{\varepsilon }}, such that for sufficiently small choices of parameter {\displaystyle \varepsilon }, there is a diffeomorphism

{\displaystyle {\mathcal {M}}_{\varepsilon }{\xrightarrow {\quad \cong \quad }}X\times (0,\varepsilon )}.

Donaldson observed that the singular points in the interior of \mathcal M corresponding to reducible connections could also be described: they looked like cones over the complex projective plane \mathbb{CP}^2. The reducible connections modulo gauge are in a one-to-one correspondence with pairs \{\alpha,-\alpha\}\subset H^2(X;\mathbb Z) such that Q(\alpha,\alpha)=-1, so there are n(Q) such points.

It is thus possible to truncate the moduli space as follows: First, cut off each cone at a reducible singularity, leaving a boundary copy of \mathbb{CP}^2. Secondly, cut off the product end at a copy of X. The resulting compact manifold is a cobordism between X and a disjoint union of n(Q) copies of \mathbb{CP}^2 (of unknown orientations).
