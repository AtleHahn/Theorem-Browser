Statement

Let d\ge2 and let E\subset\mathbb R^d be Lebesgue measurable. Suppose each coordinate projection \pi_j(E) is Lebesgue measurable and has finite measure, where \pi_j deletes coordinate j. Then |E|^{d-1}\le\prod_{j=1}^d|\pi_j(E)|. Here |\cdot| denotes Lebesgue measure in the corresponding dimension.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement involves general Lebesgue measurable sets and their projected measures, beyond ordinary calculus.

Proof 1: Insert indicators of the coordinate projections

We use the functional Loomis–Whitney inequality

\int_{\mathbb R^d}\prod_{j=1}^d f_j(\pi_j(x))^{1/(d-1)}\,dx \le\prod_{j=1}^d\left(\int_{\mathbb R^{d-1}}f_j\right)^{1/(d-1)}

for nonnegative integrable functions f_j.

Let E be some measurable subset of {\displaystyle \mathbb {R} ^{d}} and let

{\displaystyle f_{j}=\mathbf {1} _{\pi _{j}(E)}}

be the indicator function of the projection of E onto the jth coordinate hyperplane. It follows that for any point x in E,

{\displaystyle \prod _{j=1}^{d}f_{j}(\pi _{j}(x))^{1/(d-1)}=\prod _{j=1}^{d}1=1.}

Hence, by the Loomis–Whitney inequality,

{\displaystyle \int _{\mathbb {R} ^{d}}\mathbf {1} _{E}(x)\,\mathrm {d} x=|E|\leq \prod _{j=1}^{d}|\pi _{j}(E)|^{1/(d-1)},}

The indicator functions are measurable and integrable by the hypotheses on the projections. Outside E their product is nonnegative, so it dominates \mathbf1_E everywhere. Raising the displayed inequality to the power d-1 gives the statement.

Level justification
