Statement

For every context-free language L there is an integer p\ge1 such that every s\in L with |s|\ge p admits s=uvwxy with |vx|\ge1, |vwx|\le p, and uv^nwx^ny\in L for all integers n\ge0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Context-free languages are generated by finite word-substitution rules with one nonterminal on each left side. Learning that concrete grammar model is a small extension of finite strings and induction; the pumping decomposition and quantifiers are explicitly given.

Proof 1: Marking every position

Ogden's lemma in its language form states: if a language L is context-free, then there exists some number p\geq1 such that for any string s of length at least p in L and every way of marking p or more of the positions in s, s can be written as

s=uvwxy

with strings u,v,w,x,y, such that

  1. vx has at least one marked position,
  2. vwx has at most p marked positions, and
  3. uv^nwx^ny\in L for all n\geq0.

In the special case where every position is marked, Ogden's lemma gives the pumping lemma for context-free languages: the first two conditions become |vx|\geq1 and |vwx|\leq p.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Mark every position in the explicitly supplied lemma, turning marked counts into substring lengths. Context-free grammars and finite word concatenation are a small introductory discrete-mathematics extension; no proof of Ogden is needed.
