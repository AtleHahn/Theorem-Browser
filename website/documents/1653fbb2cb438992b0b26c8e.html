Statement

For a closed oriented smooth manifold M of real dimension 2r, write e(TM) for the Euler class and \chi(M) for its Euler characteristic. Integration of a top-degree characteristic class means evaluation on the fundamental class [M]. The topological Chern–Gauss–Bonnet theorem is

\chi(M)=\int_M e(TM).
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Closed oriented smooth manifolds; Tangent bundles and Euler classes; Euler characteristic; Fundamental classes and cohomology evaluation. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: de Rham index and Chern-root cancellation

Index-theorem dependency

For an elliptic operator from bundles E to F on a closed oriented 2r-manifold, the source uses the characteristic-root form of the Atiyah–Singer formula \operatorname{ind}D=(-1)^r\int_M[(\operatorname{ch}E-\operatorname{ch}F)/e(TM)]\operatorname{Td}(TM\otimes\mathbb C). The displayed quotient in this computation is canceled as a formal expression in characteristic roots. Here ch denotes Chern character and Td the Todd class.

de Rham operator and characteristic roots

Suppose that {\displaystyle M} is a compact oriented manifold of dimension {\displaystyle n=2r}. If we take {\displaystyle \Lambda ^{\text{even}}} to be the sum of the even exterior powers of the cotangent bundle, and {\displaystyle \Lambda ^{\text{odd}}} to be the sum of the odd powers, define {\displaystyle D=d+d^{*}}, considered as a map from {\displaystyle \Lambda ^{\text{even}}} to {\displaystyle \Lambda ^{\text{odd}}}. Then the analytical index of {\displaystyle D} is the Euler characteristic {\displaystyle \chi (M)} of the Hodge cohomology of {\displaystyle M}, and the topological index is the integral of the Euler class over the manifold. The index formula for this operator yields the Chern–Gauss–Bonnet theorem.

The concrete computation goes as follows: according to one variation of the splitting principle, if {\displaystyle E} is a real vector bundle of dimension {\displaystyle n=2r}, in order to prove assertions involving characteristic classes, we may suppose that there are complex line bundles {\displaystyle l_{1},\,\ldots ,\,l_{r}} such that {\displaystyle E\otimes \mathbb {C} =l_{1}\oplus {\overline {l_{1}}}\oplus \dotsm l_{r}\oplus {\overline {l_{r}}}}. Therefore, we can consider the Chern roots {\displaystyle x_{i}(E\otimes \mathbb {C} )=c_{1}(l_{i})}, {\displaystyle x_{r+i}(E\otimes \mathbb {C} )=c_{1}{\mathord {\left({\overline {l_{i}}}\right)}}=-x_{i}(E\otimes \mathbb {C} )}, {\displaystyle i=1,\,\ldots ,\,r}.

Using Chern roots as above and the standard properties of the Euler class, we have that {\textstyle e(TM)=\prod _{i}^{r}x_{i}(TM\otimes \mathbb {C} )}. As for the Chern character and the Todd class,

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\operatorname {ch} {\mathord {\left(\Lambda ^{\text{even}}-\Lambda ^{\text{odd}}\right)}}&=1-\operatorname {ch} (T^{*}M\otimes \mathbb {C} )+\operatorname {ch} {\mathord {\left(\Lambda ^{2}T^{*}M\otimes \mathbb {C} \right)}}-\ldots +(-1)^{n}\operatorname {ch} {\mathord {\left(\Lambda ^{n}T^{*}M\otimes \mathbb {C} \right)}}\\&=1-\sum _{i}^{n}e^{-x_{i}}(TM\otimes \mathbb {C} )+\sum _{i<j}e^{-x_{i}}e^{-x_{j}}(TM\otimes \mathbb {C} )+\ldots +(-1)^{n}e^{-x_{1}}\dotsm e^{-x_{n}}(TM\otimes \mathbb {C} )\\&=\prod _{i}^{n}\left(1-e^{-x_{i}}\right)(TM\otimes \mathbb {C} )\\[3pt]\operatorname {Td} (TM\otimes \mathbb {C} )&=\prod _{i}^{n}{\frac {x_{i}}{1-e^{-x_{i}}}}(TM\otimes \mathbb {C} )\end{aligned}}}

Applying the index theorem,

{\displaystyle \chi (M)=(-1)^{r}\int _{M}{\frac {\prod _{i}^{n}\left(1-e^{-x_{i}}\right)}{\prod _{i}^{r}x_{i}}}\prod _{i}^{n}{\frac {x_{i}}{1-e^{-x_{i}}}}(TM\otimes \mathbb {C} )=(-1)^{r}\int _{M}(-1)^{r}\prod _{i}^{r}x_{i}(TM\otimes \mathbb {C} )=\int _{M}e(TM)}
