Statement

Let K be a convex body in Euclidean space \mathbb R^n and let \gamma:I\to\partial K be a unit-speed minimizing geodesic for the intrinsic length metric on its surface. For every p\in K, the function

f(t)=\|\gamma(t)-p\|^2-t^2\qquad(t\in I)

is concave: f((1-s)u+sv)\ge(1-s)f(u)+sf(v) for u,v\in I and 0\le s\le1. Here I is an interval, a convex body is a compact convex set with nonempty interior, and the intrinsic distance on \partial K is the infimum of lengths of curves in \partial K. Unit-speed minimizing means the length of each subarc from u to v is |u-v| and equals that intrinsic distance. The norm in the formula is ambient Euclidean distance to p.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Lengths of curves and a minimum-length metric are small extensions of undergraduate calculus and metric topology; no smooth-surface theory is needed to understand the assertion.
