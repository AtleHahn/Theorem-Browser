Statement

If a Euclidean triangle has positive side lengths a,b,c satisfying a^2+b^2=c^2, then the angle opposite c is right.

Level

Proof 1: Compare with a constructed right triangle by side-side-side

Let ABC be a triangle with side lengths a,b,c, with a^2+b^2=c^2. Construct a second triangle with sides of length a and b containing a right angle. By the Pythagorean theorem, it follows that the hypotenuse of this triangle has length

c=\sqrt{a^2+b^2},

the same as the side of length c of the first triangle. Since both triangles' sides are the same lengths a,b,c, the triangles are congruent by side-side-side and must have the same angles. Therefore, the angle between the sides of lengths a and b in the original triangle is a right angle.

Proof 2: Substitute the squared-length equality into the cosine law — outline

Let \theta\in(0,\pi) be the angle between the positive sides a,b. The law of cosines gives c^2=a^2+b^2-2ab\cos\theta. Substituting c^2=a^2+b^2 yields 2ab\cos\theta=0. Since a,b>0, \cos\theta=0, and the triangle-angle range implies \theta=\pi/2.
