Statement

A finite simple graph has finitely many vertices, at most one edge per vertex pair, and no loops. A proper coloring assigns distinct colors to adjacent vertices; a plane graph is drawn without crossings. Suppose G is not four-colorable but G-v is four-colorable for every vertex v. If G is planar, then every vertex has degree at least five.

Level
High school only
Level justification

A graph is a finite set of vertices joined by edges; planarity means it can be drawn without crossings, and proper coloring assigns different colors across each edge. These finite combinatorial and planar notions are explicit.

Proof 1: Exclude degrees zero through four

The graph G cannot have a vertex of degree 3 or less, because if d(v)\leq3, we can remove v from G, four-color the smaller graph, then add back v and extend the four-coloring to it by choosing a color different from its neighbors.

Nor can G have a vertex of degree 4. Fix a plane embedding of G. Remove the vertex v and four-color the remaining vertices. If fewer than four colors occur among its neighbors, add v back with a missing color. If all four neighbors of v are different colors, say red, green, blue, and yellow in clockwise order, we look for an alternating path of vertices colored red and blue joining the red and blue neighbors. Such a path is called a Kempe chain. There may be a Kempe chain joining the red and blue neighbors, and there may be a Kempe chain joining the green and yellow neighbors, but not both, since these two paths would necessarily intersect, and the vertex where they intersect cannot be colored.

Suppose it is the red and blue neighbors that are not chained together; the other case is symmetric. Explore all vertices attached to the red neighbor by red-blue alternating paths, and then reverse the colors red and blue on all these vertices. The result is still a valid four-coloring, and v can now be added back and colored red.

Thus any vertex of degree at most 4 would allow a four-coloring of G, contradicting the hypothesis.

Level justification

Exclude degree at most three by counting and degree four by a plane-separation Kempe-chain recoloring. The second step needs undergraduate plane graph embedding and separation reasoning.
