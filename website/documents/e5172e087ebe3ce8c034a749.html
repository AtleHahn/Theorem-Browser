Proof 1: Every compact Hausdorff group is an inverse limit of Lie groups — outline

From the Peter–Weyl theorem, one can deduce a significant general structure theorem. Let G be a compact topological group, which we assume Hausdorff. For any finite-dimensional G-invariant subspace V in L^2(G), where G acts on the left, we consider the image of G in GL(V). It is closed, since G is compact, and a subgroup of the Lie group GL(V). It follows by the closed subgroup theorem of Élie Cartan that the image of G is a Lie group also.

If we now take the limit (in the sense of category theory) over all such spaces V, we get a result about G: because G acts faithfully on L^2(G), G is an inverse limit of Lie groups. Here the subspaces are directed by finite sums, and the last step uses the compact-group reconstruction fact that a separating directed family of quotient maps identifies a compact Hausdorff group with the inverse limit of those quotients.
