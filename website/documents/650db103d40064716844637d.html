Statement

Let d\geq2 be an integer, and let \rho,\sigma be density matrices of size d\times d over \mathbb C, meaning positive semidefinite Hermitian matrices of trace 1. For a density matrix with eigenvalues \lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_d, put

S(\rho)=-\sum_{j=1}^d\lambda_j\log\lambda_j.

Use natural logarithms and the convention 0\log0=0. Define the trace distance by

T=\frac12\operatorname{Tr}\sqrt{(\rho-\sigma)^*(\rho-\sigma)},

where * is conjugate transpose and the square root is the positive semidefinite matrix square root. Then 0\leq T\leq1 and

|S(\rho)-S(\sigma)|\leq T\log(d-1)-T\log T-(1-T)\log(1-T).

This bound is optimal for each fixed d and T\in[0,1]: some pair of density matrices at that trace distance attains equality.

Level justification

Undergraduate spectral linear algebra and calculus suffice. Density matrices, entropy and trace distance are supplied as concrete finite-matrix formulas; interpreting the square root uses diagonalization, not quantum theory. These are a small extension of familiar matrix concepts.
