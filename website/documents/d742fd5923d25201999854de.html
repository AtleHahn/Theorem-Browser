Statement

Let G be finite, P a Sylow p-subgroup, and A,B\subseteq G subsets normalized by P. Then A and B are conjugate in G if and only if they are conjugate by an element of N_G(P). Write A^g=g^{-1}Ag and N_G(A)=\{g:A^g=A\}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory abstract algebra supplies finite groups, actions, normal subgroups, quotient groups and Sylow subgroups. The displayed definitions of orbits, centralizers, solvability or series equivalence are direct uses or small extensions of these familiar structures; no representation theory is needed for comprehension.

Proof 1: Sylow conjugacy inside a set normalizer

The proof is a simple application of Sylow's theorem: if B=A^g, then the normalizer of B contains not only P but also P^g (since P^g is contained in the normalizer of A^g). By Sylow's theorem P and P^g are conjugate not only in G, but in the normalizer of B. Hence gh^{-1} normalizes P for some h that normalizes B, and then

A^{gh^{-1}}=B^{h^{-1}}=B,

so that A and B are N_G(P)-conjugate. The reverse implication follows from N_G(P)\subseteq G.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The argument uses only Sylow conjugacy, subgroup containment and multiplication of conjugating elements. Set normalizers are defined in the statement, and checking that P and P^g are Sylow subgroups of N_G(B) is an elementary order comparison.
