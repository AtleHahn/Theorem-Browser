Statement

An equitable k-coloring of a finite simple undirected graph assigns one of k colors to every vertex, gives adjacent vertices different colors, and has any two color-class sizes differing by at most one; empty classes are allowed. Every finite simple undirected graph which is a disjoint union of paths and cycles admits an equitable three-coloring. A path has vertices in a linear order with edges between consecutive vertices; a cycle additionally joins the last vertex to the first, with at least three vertices.

High school only

Proof 1: Repeat three colors along components — outline

Any union of paths and cycles may be equitably colored by using a repeated pattern of three colors, with minor adjustments to the repetition when closing a cycle.
