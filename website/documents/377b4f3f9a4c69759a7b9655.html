Statement

For f(x)=x+1 and g(x)=2x+1, \lim_{x\to1}f(x)/g(x)=2/3, whereas \lim_{x\to1}f^{\prime}(x)/g^{\prime}(x)=1/2. Thus the derivative-ratio conclusion need not hold without a 0/0 or unbounded-denominator hypothesis.

Proof 1: Evaluate the two different limits

The indeterminate-form condition is not satisfied because {\displaystyle \lim _{x\to 1}f(x)=\lim _{x\to 1}(x+1)=(1)+1=2\neq 0} and {\displaystyle \lim _{x\to 1}g(x)=\lim _{x\to 1}(2x+1)=2(1)+1=3\neq 0}. This means that the form is not indeterminate.

Differentiability and the nonvanishing-denominator-derivative condition are satisfied by {\displaystyle f(x)} and {\displaystyle g(x)}. The derivative ratio also has a limit:

{\displaystyle \lim _{x\to 1}{\frac {f'(x)}{g'(x)}}=\lim _{x\to 1}{\frac {(x+1)'}{(2x+1)'}}=\lim _{x\to 1}{\frac {1}{2}}={\frac {1}{2}}.}

But the conclusion fails, since

{\displaystyle \lim _{x\to 1}{\frac {f(x)}{g(x)}}=\lim _{x\to 1}{\frac {x+1}{2x+1}}={\frac {\lim _{x\to 1}(x+1)}{\lim _{x\to 1}(2x+1)}}={\frac {2}{3}}\neq {\frac {1}{2}}.}
