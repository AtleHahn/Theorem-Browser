Statement

Let f be entire and suppose it has nonzero periods a,b\in\mathbb C with a/b\notin\mathbb R. Then f is constant.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex differentiability is a direct extension of calculus. Periods, continuity and the specified planar domains are familiar; no singularities, contours or Riemann surfaces are needed to understand these assertions.

Proof 1: Bound the function on a fundamental parallelogram

If {\displaystyle a} and {\displaystyle b} are two periods of {\displaystyle f} such that {\displaystyle {\tfrac {a}{b}}} is not real, consider the parallelogram {\displaystyle P} whose vertices are 0, {\displaystyle a}, {\displaystyle b}, and {\displaystyle a+b}. Then the image of {\displaystyle f} is equal to {\displaystyle f(P)}. Since {\displaystyle f} is continuous and {\displaystyle P} is compact, {\displaystyle f(P)} is also compact and, therefore, it is bounded. So, f is constant by Liouville's theorem.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The compact fundamental parallelogram bounds the function everywhere by periodicity. Beyond undergraduate compactness and complex coordinates, only the definition of an entire function and the supplied bounded-entire conclusion are needed; no proof of Liouville or contour integration is used.
