Statement

In the free group on a,b, every even-length reduced word belongs to the subgroup generated by aa,ab,ba; these three words generate exactly the even-length subgroup.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A free group is the reduced-word group on the displayed generators, with adjacent inverse cancellation. The subgroup is specified by word-length parity and free rank counts generators in a free basis. These concrete word definitions are a small extension of group theory.

Proof 1: Pair letters and eliminate three redundant generators

Let G be the free group with two generators a,b, and let H be the subgroup consisting of all reduced words of even length (products of an even number of letters a,b,a^{-1},b^{-1}). Then H is generated by its six elements p=aa, q=ab, r=ba, s=bb, t=ab^{-1}, u=a^{-1}b. A factorization of any reduced word in H into these generators and their inverses may be constructed simply by taking consecutive pairs of letters in the reduced word. However, this is not a set of free generators of H (not a basis of H) because the last three generators can be written in terms of the first three as

s=rp^{-1}q,\qquad t=pr^{-1},\qquad u=p^{-1}q.

Thus H is generated by the three elements p=aa,q=ab,r=ba. All three have even length, and cancellation removes letters in pairs, so they generate exactly the even-length subgroup.
