Statement

Let G be a finite group. Its 2-rank is the largest integer r for which it has a subgroup isomorphic to (C_2)^r, where C_2 is the cyclic group of order two. If the 2-rank of G is zero, then G is solvable.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory field/group theory and linear algebra supply all objects. Commutator, graph automorphism and 2-rank are explicit group or finite-coordinate definitions; the displayed identities and subgroup assertions require no specialist structural theory.

Proof 1: Identify odd order and apply Feit–Thompson

Groups of 2-rank zero are precisely groups of odd order. They are therefore solvable by the Feit–Thompson theorem.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use Cauchy to identify absence of order-two elements with odd group order, then apply the exact Feit-Thompson consequence. Introductory group theory and solvability suffice; the advanced proof of Feit-Thompson is not imported.
