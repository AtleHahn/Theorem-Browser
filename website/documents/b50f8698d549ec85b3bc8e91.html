Statement

Let C be a smooth projective complex curve of genus g, with diagonal \Delta\subset C\times C. Let T be an algebraic correspondence on C with degrees d,e in the two directions and valence k: for some point p\in C and integer coefficients a,b, its homology class has the form a[C\times\{p\}]+b[\{p\}\times C]-k[\Delta]. Then its intersection number with the diagonal is T\cdot\Delta=d+e+2kg. When the intersection is proper, this counts united points with intersection multiplicities.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Algebraic correspondences on projective curves, their homology classes and intersection multiplicities require several layers of algebraic geometry and topology beyond a half-hour introduction. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Intersect the valence class with the diagonal — outline

Write F_1=[C\times\{p\}] and F_2=[\{p\}\times C] for the fiber classes.

The number of united points of a proper intersection, counted with intersection multiplicities, is the intersection number of the correspondence with the diagonal Δ of C×C. The correspondence has valence k if and only if it is homologous to a linear combination aF_1+bF_2-k\Delta where Δ is the diagonal of C×C. The Chasles–Cayley–Brill formula follows easily from this together with the fact that the self-intersection number of the diagonal is 2 – 2g.

The background product-surface pairing is F_1^2=F_2^2=0, F_1\cdot F_2=1, and F_i\cdot\Delta=1; the two correspondence degrees are intersections with the two fibers.
