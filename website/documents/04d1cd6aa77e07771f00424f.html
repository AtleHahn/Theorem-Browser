Statement

Let T be an infinite prefix-closed set of finite sequences of natural numbers. Suppose each sequence in T has finitely many one-term extensions in T. Then there is an infinite sequence all of whose finite prefixes belong to T.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A graph, path and finite branching have elementary set-based definitions. Infinite sequences and countability are undergraduate concepts. Understanding the existence of a ray requires no set-theoretic machinery used by its proof.

Proof 1: View the sequence tree as a locally finite graph

Join each nonempty sequence of T to the sequence obtained by deleting its last term. This is a connected tree rooted at the empty sequence. Each node has finitely many children and at most one parent, hence finite degree. Apply the rooted construction in Kőnig’s infinity lemma starting at the empty sequence: it yields a ray there. Such a ray successively extends its prefixes, and their union is the required infinite sequence.

Level justification

Turn prefixes into a locally finite rooted graph and read a ray as an infinite sequence. Elementary undergraduate discrete mathematics suffices; the infinity lemma is a concrete graph assertion and infinite prefix unions are a small extension of finite trees.
