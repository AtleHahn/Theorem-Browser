Statement

Let \mathcal S be a finite family of axis-parallel squares of positive side length in \mathbb R^2, and let A=\operatorname{area}(\bigcup_{Q\in\mathcal S}Q)>0. There exists a subfamily \mathcal I\subseteq\mathcal S consisting of pairwise disjoint squares such that

\sum_{Q\in\mathcal I}\operatorname{area}(Q)\ge\frac{1}{8.4797}\,A=\frac{10000}{84797}\,A.

Squares may have different sizes; their corresponding sides are parallel. Area denotes ordinary planar area, and disjoint means disjoint as sets.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement uses only planar squares, finite selection, area and a numerical fraction; the extremal covering constant is expanded into these elementary quantifiers.
