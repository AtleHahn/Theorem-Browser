Statement

Let f be a continuously differentiable orientation-preserving diffeomorphism between plane domains, satisfying f_{\bar z}=\mu f_z with |\mu|<1, where f_z=(f_x-if_y)/2 and f_{\bar z}=(f_x+if_y)/2. The ratio of major to minor differential stretches is K(z)=(1+|\mu(z)|)/(1-|\mu(z)|). If \|\mu\|_\infty<1, the essential maximal stretch ratio is (1+\|\mu\|_\infty)/(1-\|\mu\|_\infty).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Multivariable calculus and linear algebra give a plane diffeomorphism, its real derivative and the stretches of a circle into an ellipse. The complex derivative combinations are supplied. Essential supremum adds ignoring sets of zero area; since the derivative and mu are continuous here, the relevant bound agrees with the ordinary supremum. This concrete interpretation of the formula and global bound is a small extension within about 30 minutes, without general quasiconformal geometry.

Proof 1: Take the ratio of pullback-metric eigenvalues — outline

Let D be the domain of f. Without appeal to an auxiliary metric, consider the effect of the pullback under f of the usual Euclidean metric. The resulting metric is then given by

{\displaystyle \left|{\frac {\partial f}{\partial z}}\right|^{2}\left|dz+\mu (z)\,d{\bar {z}}\right|^{2}}

which, relative to the background Euclidean metric {\displaystyle dz\,d{\bar {z}}}, has eigenvalues

{\displaystyle (1+|\mu |)^{2}\left|{\frac {\partial f}{\partial z}}\right|^{2},\qquad (1-|\mu |)^{2}\left|{\frac {\partial f}{\partial z}}\right|^{2}.}

The eigenvalues represent, respectively, the squared length of the major and minor axes of the ellipse obtained by applying the differential of f to the unit circle in the tangent plane.

Accordingly, the dilatation of f at a point z is defined by

{\displaystyle K(z)={\frac {1+|\mu (z)|}{1-|\mu (z)|}}.}

The (essential) supremum of K(z) is given by

{\displaystyle K=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{z\in D}|K(z)|={\frac {1+\|\mu \|_{\infty }}{1-\|\mu \|_{\infty }}}}

and is called the dilatation of f.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The metric in this planar calculation is the explicit positive quadratic form Df transpose Df, so multivariable calculus and matrix eigenvalues suffice. The Wirtinger derivatives are defined combinations of real derivatives. Writing Df(zeta)=a zeta+b conjugate(zeta), its largest and smallest stretches are |a|+|b| and |a|-|b|; with b=mu a this gives the formula. Essential supremum adds only the notion of ignoring zero-area exceptional sets; here mu is continuous, so its supremum and essential supremum agree on the open domain. No general quasiconformal or Riemannian theory is needed.

Statement used:

For an invertible real linear map L of the plane, the squared major and minor stretches of the image of the unit circle are the eigenvalues of L transpose L.

Application in proof:

Take square roots of the two displayed eigenvalues and divide.

Undergraduate level

This is finite-dimensional linear algebra and ellipse geometry; the pullback metric is simply the matrix L transpose L.

Review notes:

Orientation preservation gives |f_z|>|f_bar z| and hence f_z nonzero. The map t->(1+t)/(1-t) is increasing and continuous below one, which justifies passing to the supremum.
