Statement

There exists an infinite finitely generated torsion group: a group generated by finitely many elements in which every element has finite order. Consequently the assertion that every finitely generated torsion group is finite is false.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory group theory supplies subgroups, quotients, actions, products and homomorphisms. Perfect means generated by commutators, torsion means finite element orders, and the chain conditions are explicit sequences of subgroups. The relevant one of these closely related definitions is a small extension, without representation theory.

Proof 1: Classify the supplied three-generator p-torsion example

Use the Golod–Shafarevich existence result: for each prime p there is an infinite group G generated by three elements such that every element has order a power of p. Three generators make G finitely generated, and a power of p is finite, so every element has finite order. Hence G is an infinite finitely generated torsion group and a counterexample to the asserted finiteness.

Level justification

Apply the supplied existence theorem and check that three generators are finite in number and each prime-power order is finite. Undergraduate groups, generators and element orders suffice; no Golod–Shafarevich construction is required.
