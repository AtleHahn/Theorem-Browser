Statement

Let f:|K|\to|K| be a fixed-point-free continuous map on a finite geometric simplicial complex. Put \delta=\inf_x|x-f(x)|>0 and assume every simplex of K has diameter less than \delta/2. If g:|K|\to|K| is a simplicial approximation to f defined on a subdivision, then g(\sigma)\cap\sigma=\varnothing for every closed simplex \sigma of K. Here approximation means that g(x) belongs to the smallest closed target simplex containing f(x).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A finite geometric complex consists of finitely many Euclidean simplices meeting in common faces; subdivision refines them. These are concrete convex-hull and finite-gluing definitions over undergraduate geometry and topology. The approximation condition is explicitly given, so no homology or general approximation theorem is needed.

Proof 1: Displacement lower bound

Since f does not have a fixed point and X=|K| is compact, we have \delta=\inf |x-f(x)|>0. By assumption the diameter of each simplex in K is <\delta/2.

Let K' be the subdivision on which the simplicial approximation g:K'\to K is defined. Then, by the definition of a simplicial approximation, for each x in X=|K'|, f(x),g(x) belong to the same closed simplex; thus, by the diameter assumption,

|f(x)-g(x)|<\delta/2.

It follows:

|x-g(x)|\geq |x-f(x)|-|f(x)-g(x)|>\delta-\delta/2=\delta/2.

Thus, for each closed simplex \sigma in K,

g(\sigma)\cap\sigma=\varnothing.

Indeed, if x and g(x) both belonged to \sigma, its diameter bound would contradict the preceding inequality.

Level justification

The approximation property is explicitly given as a same-simplex condition. After learning finite geometric simplices and subdivisions, the proof is a compact positive-minimum argument and the triangle inequality; no simplicial homology or approximation theorem is used.
