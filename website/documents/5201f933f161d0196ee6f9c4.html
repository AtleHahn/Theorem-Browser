Statement

Let y_n be polynomials over \mathbb R or \mathbb C with \deg y_n=n and y_0=1. There is at most one linear functional \Lambda on the polynomial space satisfying \Lambda(1)=1 and \Lambda(y_my_n)=0 for m\ne n. Its values are specified by \Lambda(y_0)=1 and \Lambda(y_n)=0 for n>0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Polynomials; Linear functionals; Polynomial bases. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Specify the functional on the polynomial basis — outline

The source specifies the functional by \Lambda(1)=1 and \Lambda(y_n)=0 for n>0. These are its values on the polynomial basis (y_n).

Level justification

The distinct degrees give a triangular polynomial basis, and orthogonality against y0 fixes each basis value. Standard linear algebra on the polynomial space suffices.
