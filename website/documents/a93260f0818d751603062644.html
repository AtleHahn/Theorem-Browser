Statement

If n\ge1 is an integer and p is an odd prime satisfying 2n/3<p\le n, then p does not divide \binom{2n}{n}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Only elementary integer arithmetic and finite sums are needed.

Proof 1: Cancel the two numerator prime factors

There are exactly two factors of {\displaystyle p} in the numerator of the expression {\displaystyle {\tbinom {2n}{n}}=(2n)!/(n!)^{2}}, coming from the two terms {\displaystyle p} and {\displaystyle 2p} in {\displaystyle (2n)!}, and also two factors of {\displaystyle p} in the denominator from one copy of the term {\displaystyle p} in each of the two factors of {\displaystyle n!}. These factors all cancel, leaving no factors of {\displaystyle p} in {\displaystyle {\tbinom {2n}{n}}}. (The bound on {\displaystyle p} in the statement ensures that {\displaystyle 3p} is too large to be a term of the numerator, and the assumption that {\displaystyle p} is odd is needed to ensure that {\displaystyle 2p} contributes only one factor of {\displaystyle p} to the numerator.)

Level
High school only
Level justification

The only multiples of p up to 2n are p and 2p, and oddness ensures neither contributes an extra p factor. Each denominator factorial contains p once. Canceling those two factors is elementary prime-factor counting.
