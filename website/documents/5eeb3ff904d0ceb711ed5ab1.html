Statement

Let real sequences a_n,b_n define A_0=0,A_1=a_1,B_0=1,B_1=b_1 and A_n=b_nA_{n-1}+a_nA_{n-2}, B_n=b_nB_{n-1}+a_nB_{n-2} for n\geq2. Then for every n\geq1, A_nB_{n-1}-A_{n-1}B_n=(-1)^{n-1}a_1\cdots a_n.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The sequences are explicitly defined by recurrence, and the identity uses only finite products and elementary algebra.

Proof 1: Induction on the recurrence

The case {\displaystyle n=1} is trivial.

Suppose the claim is true for {\displaystyle n=k}. By using the two recurrences in the statement, it follows that

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}A_{k+1}B_{k}-A_{k}B_{k+1}&=\left(b_{k+1}A_{k}+a_{k+1}A_{k-1}\right)B_{k}-A_{k}\left(b_{k+1}B_{k}+a_{k+1}B_{k-1}\right)\\&=b_{k+1}A_{k}B_{k}+a_{k+1}A_{k-1}B_{k}-b_{k+1}A_{k}B_{k}-a_{k+1}A_{k}B_{k-1}\\&=a_{k+1}A_{k-1}B_{k}-a_{k+1}A_{k}B_{k-1}\\&=-a_{k+1}\left(A_{k}B_{k-1}-A_{k-1}B_{k}\right)\\&=-a_{k+1}\cdot (-1)^{k-1}a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{k}\\&=(-1)^{k}a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{k}a_{k+1}\end{aligned}}}

which finishes the induction step.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Substitute both recurrences, cancel the b terms, and multiply the previous alternating product by minus a_(k+1). The base case and induction are elementary algebra.
