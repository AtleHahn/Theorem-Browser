Statement

A positive integer is perfect if the sum of its positive proper divisors equals itself. Let p\ge2 be an integer, q=2^p-1 prime and t=2^{p-1}. Then qt is even and perfect.

Proof 1: Sum the two disjoint geometric divisor families

The geometric series 1,2,4,\ldots,t has sum q=2t-1.

Euclid proves that qt is perfect by observing that the geometric series with ratio 2 starting at q, with the same number of terms, is proportional to the original series; therefore, since the original series sums to q = 2t − 1, the second series sums to q(2t − 1) = 2qt − q, and both series together add to 2qt, two times the supposed perfect number. However, these two series are disjoint from each other and (by the primality of q) exhaust all the divisors of qt, so qt has divisors that sum to 2qt, showing that it is perfect.
