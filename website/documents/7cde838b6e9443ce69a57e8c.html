Statement

For a_i\geq0, the extended sum \sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_i equals

\sup_{\substack{J\subset\mathbb N\\|J|<\infty}}\sum_{j\in J}a_j \quad\text{in }[0,\infty].

Consequently every permutation of its terms has the same sum.

Proof 1: Compare finite subsets with initial segments

In the extended real numbers every set has a supremum, which of course may be \infty if the set is unbounded. For a series of nonnegative numbers,

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_i =\lim_{k\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_i =\sup_k\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_i =\sup_{\substack{J\subset\mathbb N\\|J|<\infty}}\sum_{j\in J}a_j.

In particular the sum of a series of nonnegative numbers does not depend on the order of summation.

Level justification

Every finite subset lies in an initial segment, while each initial segment is a finite subset; positivity gives equal suprema. Undergraduate real analysis of increasing partial sums and extended limits suffices.
