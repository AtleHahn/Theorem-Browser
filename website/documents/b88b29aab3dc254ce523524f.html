Statement

Let independent remaining lifetimes T_1,\ldots,T_m have survival probabilities p_j=\Pr(T_j>t) at a fixed time t. A contract pays the real amount c_n when exactly n survive. Put S_k=\sum_{|J|=k}\prod_{j\in J}p_j, with S_0=1. Its expected payout is \sum_{k=0}^mS_k\sum_{l=0}^k(-1)^{k-l}\binom kl c_l.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Only the finitely many survival events at one fixed time matter. Independence gives products of their probabilities, and expected payout is a finite weighted sum. Elementary finite probability and binomial coefficients suffice without stochastic-process theory.

Proof 1: Form survival events and multiply their probabilities

Set A_j=\{T_j>t\}. Independence gives \Pr(\cap_{j\in J}A_j)=\prod_{j\in J}p_j, so the event-intersection sums are precisely the displayed S_k. Apply the probabilistic Schuette–Nesbitt identity \mathbb E[c_N]=\sum_kS_k\sum_{l=0}^k(-1)^{k-l}\binom kl c_l to the number N of surviving lives.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Express each survival intersection probability as an independent product and insert those into the supplied finite expectation formula. Undergraduate finite probability and binomial coefficients suffice; no stochastic-process theory is used.
