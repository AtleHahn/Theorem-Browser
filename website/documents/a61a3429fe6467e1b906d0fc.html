Statement

In classical mathematics, let S be a set of finite binary sequences such that every infinite binary sequence has a prefix in S. Then there is N∈N such that every infinite binary sequence has a prefix in S of length at most N.

Proof 1: Take a finite subcover of the binary sequence space

Give \{0,1\}^{\omega}=\{0,1\}^{\mathbb N} its product topology with discrete factors. The set S is called a bar, and a finite sequence represents the basic open set of all its infinite extensions.

The assertion can be proven in a classical setting by considering the bar as an open covering of the compact topological space {\displaystyle \{0,1\}^{\omega }}. Each sequence in the bar represents a basic open set of this space, and these basic open sets cover the space by assumption. By compactness, this cover has a finite subcover. The N of the fan theorem can be taken to be the length of the longest sequence whose basic open set is in the finite subcover.

Interpret prefixes as basic open cylinder sets in the binary product space and take a finite subcover. Compactness and product topology are ordinary point-set topology; the longest of finitely many prefixes supplies N.
