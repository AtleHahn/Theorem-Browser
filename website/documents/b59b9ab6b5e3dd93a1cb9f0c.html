Statement

Let A=(a_{ij})_{i,j\geq0} be an infinite matrix of complex numbers. It is a regular summability matrix if, for every convergent complex sequence x_j\to\ell, each series

(Ax)_i=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}a_{ij}x_j

converges and (Ax)_i\to\ell as i\to\infty.

The matrix A is regular if and only if all three conditions hold:

  1. For each fixed j, a_{ij}\to0 as i\to\infty.
  2. \displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}a_{ij}\to1 as i\to\infty.
  3. \displaystyle\sup_{i\geq0}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|a_{ij}|<\infty.

No triangularity or finite-row-support assumption is imposed.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The full characterization uses standard undergraduate sequence/series analysis. It does not require the functional-analytic techniques sometimes used to prove necessity.

Proof 1: Absolute row summability and the closed graph theorem (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Sections1–3, especially pp.3–4).

For sufficiency, put C=\sup_i\sum_j|a_{ij}|. Every convergent sequence is bounded, so each row series converges absolutely. If y_j\to0, then

|(Ay)_i|\le\sum_{j\le N}|a_{ij}y_j|+C\sup_{j>N}|y_j|.

Choose N to make the tail small, then let i\to\infty in the finite prefix. For x_j\to\ell, apply this to y_j=x_j-\ell and use the row sums tending to 1. In the source’s operator-norm display on p.3, take the supremum over convergent sequences, not all bounded sequences; its finitely supported phase tests still prove the required norm identity.

Supplied partial proof and limitations

The source treats triangular sums. For a null sequence, it splits the sum into a fixed finite prefix and a tail: columnwise convergence controls the prefix and the uniform absolute-row bound controls the tail. For a nonzero limit, it applies the null-sequence result to x_j-\ell and uses convergence of the row sums to 1.

One displayed estimate in the source incorrectly makes the entire absolute row sum small; the following calculation needs only the fixed finite prefix to become small. The article also supplies no necessity proof and does not develop the general infinite-row case. This is a record of its partial argument and the erroneous estimate, not a claim that the full characterization has been proved here.

The statement uses complex series and limits. The linked necessity proof views convergent sequences with the uniform norm as a complete normed space and applies the closed graph theorem. Its dependency chain follows from nested-ball Baire category, geometric-series approximation and the product norm on the graph. Together with the finite-prefix/tail estimates, this uses undergraduate real analysis and linear algebra.
