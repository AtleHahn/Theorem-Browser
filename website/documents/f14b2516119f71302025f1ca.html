Statement

Let n\ge1 and let (S,\Sigma,\mu) be a sigma-finite measure space, and let f=(f_1,\ldots,f_n),g=(g_1,\ldots,g_n) have measurable real or complex components. For p,q\in(1,\infty) conjugate,

\int_S\sum_{k=1}^n|f_kg_k|\,d\mu\le\left(\int_S\sum|f_k|^p\,d\mu\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_S\sum|g_k|^q\,d\mu\right)^{1/q}.

With both right integrals finite, equality holds iff \alpha|f_k|^p=\beta|g_k|^q almost everywhere for every k, for some \alpha,\beta\ge0 not both zero. Interpret 0\cdot\infty=0.

Proof 1: Hölder inequality for vector-valued functions — outline

Apply product-measure Hölder to S\times\{1,\ldots,n\} with counting measure in the second variable, using F(x,k)=f_k(x) and G(x,k)=g_k(x). The integrals become the displayed sums. The scalar equality condition on that product space is exactly the componentwise condition; finitely many null sets combine into one null set.

Level justification

Use Holder on the product of the original measure space with a finite counting set and transfer its equality condition componentwise. The arbitrary base measure, null sets and Lp norms require measure theory.
