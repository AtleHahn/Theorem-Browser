In a finite-measure ergodic system, the fraction of time spent in a region converges almost surely to its relative measure. This supplies a precise bridge between temporal frequencies and ensemble probabilities, central to statistical descriptions of deterministic dynamics and dependent on the ergodicity assumption.

Statement

Let (X,\Sigma,\mu) be a measure space with 0<\mu(X)<\infty, and let T:X\to X be measure preserving and ergodic: every measurable set invariant modulo null sets has measure zero or full measure. For every A\in\Sigma, n^{-1}\#\{0\le k<n:T^kx\in A\}\to\mu(A)/\mu(X) for almost every x.

Proof 1: Substitute the indicator in the ergodic theorem

An immediate consequence of the ergodic theorem is that, in an ergodic system, the relative measure of A is equal to the mean sojourn time:

{\displaystyle {\frac {\mu (A)}{\mu (X)}}={\frac {1}{\mu (X)}}\int \chi _{A}\,d\mu =\lim _{n\to \infty }\;{\frac {1}{n}}\sum _{k=0}^{n-1}\chi _{A}(T^{k}x)}

for all x except for a set of measure zero, where χA is the indicator function of A.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the ergodic theorem to an indicator on a general finite measure space. Measure preservation, ergodicity and almost-everywhere orbit limits remain substantial prerequisites.
