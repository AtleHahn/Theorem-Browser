Statement

On a two-dimensional Riemannian surface, use the covariant curvature convention with \operatorname{Ric}_{ab}=g^{cd}R_{cadb} and scalar S=g^{ab}\operatorname{Ric}_{ab}. Then R_{abcd}=K(g_{ac}g_{db}-g_{ad}g_{cb}), \operatorname{Ric}_{ab}=Kg_{ab} and S=2K, where K is Gaussian curvature.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The Riemann, Ricci and scalar curvature tensors and their index contractions require substantial differential geometry. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Use one independent component and contract — outline

For a two-dimensional surface, the algebraic curvature symmetries imply that the Riemann tensor has only one independent component, which means that the scalar curvature completely determines the Riemann tensor. There is only one form for the Riemann tensor which fits the required symmetries:

R_{abcd}=c(g_{ac}g_{db}-g_{ad}g_{cb}),

and by contracting with the metric twice we find the explicit form:

R_{abcd}=K(g_{ac}g_{db}-g_{ad}g_{cb}),

where g_{ab} is the metric tensor and K=S/2 is the Gaussian curvature, and a,b,c,d take values either 1 or 2. The Gaussian curvature coincides with the sectional curvature of the surface. It is also exactly half the scalar curvature of the 2-manifold, while the Ricci curvature tensor of the surface is simply given by

\operatorname{Ric}_{ab}=Kg_{ab}.
