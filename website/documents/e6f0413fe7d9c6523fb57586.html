Statement

A function f:I\to\mathbb R on a real interval is convex if f(tx+(1-t)y)\leq tf(x)+(1-t)f(y) for x,y\in I and 0\leq t\leq1; it is strictly convex if the inequality is strict for distinct x,y and 0<t<1. Let n\geq1, and let x_1\geq\cdots\geq x_n and y_1\geq\cdots\geq y_n lie in I, with \sum_{j=1}^i x_j\geq\sum_{j=1}^i y_j for 1\leq i<n. Suppose f is convex and \sum_{j=1}^n x_j=\sum_{j=1}^n y_j. Then \sum_{j=1}^n f(x_j)\geq\sum_{j=1}^n f(y_j). If f is strictly convex, equality holds exactly when the two lists agree (equivalently, their unsorted lists are permutations of one another).

Level
High school only
Level justification

The full convexity, strict convexity and partial-sum hypotheses are supplied as real inequalities. Equality concerns finite lists, with no analytic limit notion.

Proof 1: Ordered secant slopes and summation by parts

We may assume that the numbers are in decreasing order as in the statement.

If xi = yi for all i ∈ {1, …, n}, then the asserted inequality holds with equality, hence we may assume in the following that xi ≠ yi for at least one i.

If xi = yi for an i ∈ {1, …, n}, then the asserted inequality and the partial-sum inequalities and equality of the total sums are not affected if we remove xi and yi. Relabel the remaining lists with length n. Hence we may assume that xi ≠ yi for all i ∈ {1, …, n}.

It is a property of convex functions that for two numbers x ≠ y in the interval I the slope

{\displaystyle {\frac {f(x)-f(y)}{x-y}}}

of the secant line through the points (x, f (x)) and (y, f (y)) of the graph of f  is a monotonically non-decreasing function in x for y fixed (and vice versa). This implies that

{\displaystyle c_{i+1}:={\frac {f(x_{i+1})-f(y_{i+1})}{x_{i+1}-y_{i+1}}}\leq {\frac {f(x_{i})-f(y_{i})}{x_{i}-y_{i}}}=:c_{i}}

for all i ∈ {1, …, n − 1}. Define A0 = B0 = 0 and

{\displaystyle A_{i}=x_{1}+\cdots +x_{i},\qquad B_{i}=y_{1}+\cdots +y_{i}}

for all i ∈ {1, …, n}. By the partial-sum inequalities, Ai ≥ Bi for all i ∈ {1, …, n − 1} and by equality of the total sums, An = Bn. Hence,

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\sum _{i=1}^{n}{\bigl (}f(x_{i})-f(y_{i}){\bigr )}&=\sum _{i=1}^{n}c_{i}(x_{i}-y_{i})\\&=\sum _{i=1}^{n}c_{i}{\bigl (}\underbrace {A_{i}-A_{i-1}} _{=\,x_{i}}{}-(\underbrace {B_{i}-B_{i-1}} _{=\,y_{i}}){\bigr )}\\&=\sum _{i=1}^{n}c_{i}(A_{i}-B_{i})-\sum _{i=1}^{n}c_{i}(A_{i-1}-B_{i-1})\\&=c_{n}(\underbrace {A_{n}-B_{n}} _{=\,0})+\sum _{i=1}^{n-1}(\underbrace {c_{i}-c_{i+1}} _{\geq \,0})(\underbrace {A_{i}-B_{i}} _{\geq \,0})-c_{1}(\underbrace {A_{0}-B_{0}} _{=\,0})\\&\geq 0,\end{aligned}}}

which proves Karamata's inequality.

To discuss the case of equality in the asserted inequality, note that x1 > y1 by the first partial-sum inequality and our assumption xi ≠ yi for all i ∈ {1, …, n}. Let i be the smallest index such that (xi, yi) ≠ (xi+1, yi+1), which exists due to equality of the total sums. Then Ai > Bi. If f  is strictly convex, then there is strict inequality in the displayed secant-slope comparison, meaning that ci+1 < ci. Hence there is a strictly positive term in the sum on the right hand side of the displayed summation identity and equality in the asserted inequality cannot hold.
