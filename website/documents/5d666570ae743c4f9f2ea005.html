Statement

Let V be a real or complex inner-product space with \|x\|^2=\langle x,x\rangle. Then \|x+y\|^2+\|x-y\|^2=2\|x\|^2+2\|y\|^2 for all x,y\in V.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Norms and inner products are linear-algebra concepts. The entire claim is an explicit identity or equivalence of inequalities quantified over vector pairs, without completeness or operator-theoretic prerequisites.

Proof 1: Cancel mixed terms in the two squared norms

In an inner product space, the norm is determined using the inner product:

{\displaystyle \|x\|^{2}=\langle x,x\rangle .}

As a consequence of this definition, in an inner product space the parallelogram law is an algebraic identity, readily established using the properties of the inner product:

{\displaystyle \|x+y\|^{2}=\langle x+y,x+y\rangle =\langle x,x\rangle +\langle x,y\rangle +\langle y,x\rangle +\langle y,y\rangle ,}{\displaystyle \|x-y\|^{2}=\langle x-y,x-y\rangle =\langle x,x\rangle -\langle x,y\rangle -\langle y,x\rangle +\langle y,y\rangle .}

Adding these two expressions:

{\displaystyle \|x+y\|^{2}+\|x-y\|^{2}=2\langle x,x\rangle +2\langle y,y\rangle =2\|x\|^{2}+2\|y\|^{2},}

as required.

Level justification

Expand both inner-product squares and cancel mixed terms. Undergraduate linear algebra suffices, whether the space is finite dimensional or not.
