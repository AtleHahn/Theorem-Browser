Statement

There is a nonconvex polyhedron with the same vertex-edge-face incidences as a regular icosahedron and congruent corresponding triangular faces, but not congruent to that regular icosahedron.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The claim concerns triangular faces, incidences and congruence of finite three-dimensional solids.

Proof 1: Reflecting a pentagonal cap inward — outline

To see that convexity is necessary in the face-congruence rigidity theorem, consider a regular icosahedron. One can "push in" a vertex to create a nonconvex polyhedron that is still combinatorially equivalent to the regular icosahedron; that is, one can take five faces of the icosahedron meeting at a vertex, which form the sides of a pentagonal pyramid, and reflect the pyramid with respect to its base.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Reflect the five triangular faces of a vertex cap through the plane of its pentagonal base. Their edge lengths and incidences stay unchanged, while the resulting inward cap makes a nonconvex solid, excluding congruence with the convex original. This is elementary three-dimensional Euclidean geometry.
