Statement

Let I,J:C\to\mathbb R be homogeneous of the same integer degree k, where C\subseteq\mathbb R^2 is closed under nonzero real scaling. Along a differentiable curve y=y(x) with x\ne0, (x,y(x))\in C and I(x,y(x))\ne0, the substitution v=y/x converts I(x,y)y^{\prime}+J(x,y)=0 into xv^{\prime}=-J(1,v)/I(1,v)-v.

Proof 1: Reduction of a homogeneous first-order differential equation — outline

The substitution {\displaystyle v=y/x} converts the ordinary differential equation

{\displaystyle I(x,y){\frac {\mathrm {d} y}{\mathrm {d} x}}+J(x,y)=0,}

where {\displaystyle I} and {\displaystyle J} are homogeneous functions of the same degree, into the separable differential equation

{\displaystyle x{\frac {\mathrm {d} v}{\mathrm {d} x}}=-{\frac {J(1,v)}{I(1,v)}}-v.}
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Write y=xv, differentiate by the product rule and cancel the common homogeneity factor x^k. Undergraduate differential calculus suffices for this first-order ODE substitution.
