Statement

Fix \tau=x+iy with y>0 and real u,v not both integers. For \operatorname{Re}s>1 put

E_{u,v}(\tau,s)=\sum_{(m,n)\in\mathbb Z^2\setminus\{(0,0)\}}e^{2\pi i(mu+nv)}\frac{y^s}{|m\tau+n|^{2s}}.

Set q=e^{2\pi i\tau}, and for a complex z put p=e^{2\pi iz} and

f(z;\tau)=q^{1/12}(p^{1/2}-p^{-1/2})\prod_{n=1}^\infty(1-q^np)(1-q^n/p),

where q^a=e^{2\pi ia\tau} and p^a=e^{2\pi iaz}. Then the value at s=1 of the analytic continuation is

E_{u,v}(\tau,1)=-2\pi\log\left|f(u-v\tau;\tau)q^{v^2/2}\right|.

Proof 1: Fourier expansion of the completed theta integral (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Proposition IV.3, printed pp.64–65 (PDF p.71); Chapter III Section 3.1 normalization; Section 4.2 proof of Theorem IV.1, ending printed p.79, and specialization noted p.81.).

Use Kopp Proposition IV.3 and the proof of Theorem IV.1 in Section 4.2. Set his characteristic vector p=(v,u), his lattice integer vector (n1,n2)=(n,m), and M=y^{-1}[[1,x],[x,|tau|^2]]. Then Q_M(n,m)=|m tau+n|^2/(2y), so his Mellin-completed zeta is pi^{-s}Gamma(s) times the stated E_{u,v}. This follows directly from definitions (3.1)–(3.6), with the zero term removed/regularized as in his continuation. At s=1 multiply Proposition IV.3 by pi. His u_product=e(tau) is our q, his v_product=e(p2-p1 tau)=e(u-v tau) is our p, and his product prefactor is q^(v^2/2+1/12). Thus his -2 logarithm becomes exactly the stated -2 pi logarithm. His proposition allows all real characteristics outside Z^2; reduction to a unit square in the intermediate theorem is removed by the quasi-periodicity of the absolute-value product, or by his stated specialization.
