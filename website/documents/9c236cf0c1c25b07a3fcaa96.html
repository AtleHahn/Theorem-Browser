Statement

Let V\subset\mathbb R^3 be a bounded region with piecewise smooth boundary S, outward unit normal n, and O an open neighborhood of \overline V. If f\in C^1(O,\mathbb R), then \int_V\nabla f\,dV=\int_S f n\,dS.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Multivariable differentiation; Volume and surface integrals; Outward normals. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Test against an arbitrary constant vector

With \mathbf F=f\mathbf c for a scalar function f and vector field \mathbf c, the divergence theorem gives

\iiint_V\mathbf c\cdot\nabla f\,dV =\iint_S(f\mathbf c)\cdot\mathbf n\,dS-\iiint_V f(\nabla\cdot\mathbf c)\,dV.

The last term on the right vanishes for constant \mathbf c or any divergence free (solenoidal) vector field. In particular, taking \mathbf c to be an arbitrary constant vector gives

\iiint_V\nabla f\,dV=\iint_S f\mathbf n\,dS.
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the divergence theorem to f times an arbitrary constant vector and use its zero divergence. Taking all constant vectors identifies the vector integrals. Undergraduate vector calculus and linear algebra suffice.
