Statement

Let g be a smooth nondegenerate symmetric metric on a smooth manifold M, and let \nabla be an affine connection. In a coordinate chart (x^1,\ldots,x^m) write \partial_i=\partial/\partial x^i and (\nabla_XY)^i=X^j\partial_jY^i+X^jY^k\Gamma^i_{jk}. Repeated upper/lower indices are summed from 1 to m. Put g_{ij}=g(\partial_i,\partial_j). Metric compatibility, meaning Xg(Y,Z)=g(\nabla_XY,Z)+g(Y,\nabla_XZ) for all smooth X,Y,Z, is equivalent to \partial_kg_{ij}=\Gamma^l_{ki}g_{lj}+\Gamma^l_{kj}g_{il} in every chart.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Metric compatibility of an affine connection on a general manifold requires substantial differential geometry, even with a coordinate formula. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Expand metric compatibility in a coordinate frame — outline

Relative to a local chart, the connection is given by

(\nabla_XY)^i=X^j\partial_jY^i+X^jY^k\Gamma^i_{jk}

for any vector fields X and Y. Written in terms of local coordinates, the condition of metric-compatibility is equivalent to the condition

\partial_k g_{ij}=\Gamma^l_{ki}g_{lj}+\Gamma^l_{kj}g_{il}.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Expand the affine-connection compatibility identity in a local frame. The coordinate calculation is elementary, but smooth manifolds, vector fields, metrics and affine connections are substantial differential-geometric prerequisites.
