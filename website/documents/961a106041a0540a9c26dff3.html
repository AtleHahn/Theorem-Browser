Statement

Let T(t) be a differentiable real 3\times3 rotation matrix, so T^\mathsf{T}T=I and \det T=1, on an open time interval. Let u(t)\in\mathbb R^3 be differentiable and f=T u. Put D_B f=T u'. Define the angular velocity vector \Omega in the fixed frame by (T' T^{-1})v=\Omega\times v for every v\in\mathbb R^3. Then f'=D_B f+\Omega\times f.

Proof 1: Rotation generator in the moving-basis derivative — outline

Let \boldsymbol e_i be the fixed orthonormal basis, and let \boldsymbol b_i:=T\boldsymbol e_i be the basis vectors of the rotating frame B, as seen from the fixed frame. Denote the components of \boldsymbol f in B by u_i. Let

G:=T'T^{-1}

so that this coordinate transformation is generated, in time, according to T'=GT. Applying the product rule with implicit summation convention,

\begin{aligned} \boldsymbol f' &=(u_i\boldsymbol b_i)' =(u_iT)'\boldsymbol e_i\\ &=(u_i'T+u_iGT)\boldsymbol e_i =u_i'\boldsymbol b_i+u_iG\boldsymbol b_i\\ &=D_B\boldsymbol f+G\boldsymbol f. \end{aligned}

For rotations one has T^{-1}=T^\mathsf T. In three dimensions, the generator G equals the cross product operation from the left, a skew-symmetric linear map [\boldsymbol\Omega]_\times\boldsymbol g:=\boldsymbol\Omega\times\boldsymbol g for any vector \boldsymbol g. Hence

\boldsymbol f'=D_B\boldsymbol f+\boldsymbol\Omega\times\boldsymbol f.
