Statement

Let G=(V,E) be a finite undirected graph and let X=(X_v)_{v\in V} be a family of random variables with a strictly positive joint probability mass function or joint density p on the Cartesian product of their value spaces. Densities are taken with respect to the product of the coordinate reference measures. For A\subseteq V, write X_A=(X_v)_{v\in A}.

The following conditions are equivalent:

  1. Pairwise Markov property: for every nonadjacent pair u,v, X_u and X_v are conditionally independent given X_{V\setminus\{u,v\}}.
  2. Local Markov property: for every v, X_v is conditionally independent of X_{V\setminus(\{v\}\cup N(v))} given X_{N(v)}, where N(v) is its neighbor set.
  3. Global Markov property: for disjoint vertex sets A,B,S, if every path from A to B meets S, then X_A and X_B are conditionally independent given X_S.
  4. Gibbs factorization: there are positive functions \psi_C of the variables in each maximal clique C and a normalizing constant Z>0 such that
p(x)=\frac1Z\prod_{C\text{ maximal clique of }G}\psi_C(x_C).

A clique is a set of pairwise adjacent vertices, and maximal means not properly contained in another clique. Conditional independence means that the conditional joint law factors as the product of the two conditional marginal laws. Conditional-law and density identities are understood almost everywhere.

Level justification

Graph notions are elementary, but the full density formulation requires conditional laws, almost-everywhere identities and product-reference measures, in addition to multiple Markov properties. This cumulative probability background exceeds the conservative undergraduate reading allowance.
