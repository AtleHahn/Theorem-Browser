Statement

Let S be the common endpoint of two distinct noncollinear rays. Let A,B lie on the first ray in the order S,A,B, and C,D on the second in the order S,C,D. Then AC\parallel BD if and only if |SA|/|AB|=|SC|/|CD|. When these equivalent conditions hold, \frac{|SB|}{|AB|}=\frac{|SD|}{|CD|},\qquad \frac{|SA|}{|SB|}=\frac{|SC|}{|SD|}=\frac{|AC|}{|BD|}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Only elementary plane geometry and proportions are needed to understand the full ray equivalence.

Proof 1: Equal triangle areas, an auxiliary parallel, and contradiction

Write [XYZ] for the area of triangle XYZ. Let F be the foot of the perpendicular from A to the line SC, and E the foot of the perpendicular from C to the line SA.

Parallel lines imply the ratios

Since CA\parallel BD, the altitudes of \triangle CDA and \triangle CBA are of equal length. As those triangles share the same baseline, their areas are identical. So we have [CDA]=[CBA]. Now, we add the area of triangle \triangle SCA to both sides of this equality. On the left-hand side, the union of \triangle CDA and \triangle SCA forms triangle \triangle SDA, and on the right-hand side, the union of \triangle CBA and \triangle SCA forms triangle \triangle SCB. Hence, we obtain [SCB]=[SDA] as well. This yields

\frac{[SCA]}{[CDA]}=\frac{[SCA]}{[CBA]} \quad\text{and}\quad \frac{[SCA]}{[SDA]}=\frac{[SCA]}{[SCB]}.

Plugging in the formula for triangle areas (\tfrac{\text{baseline}\cdot\text{altitude}}2) transforms that into

\frac{|SC||AF|}{|CD||AF|}=\frac{|SA||EC|}{|AB||EC|} \quad\text{and}\quad \frac{|SC||AF|}{|SD||AF|}=\frac{|SA||EC|}{|SB||EC|}.

Canceling the common factors results in:

\text{(a)}\quad\frac{|SC|}{|CD|}=\frac{|SA|}{|AB|} \quad\text{and}\quad \text{(b)}\quad\frac{|SC|}{|SD|}=\frac{|SA|}{|SB|}.

Now use (b) to replace |SA| and |SC| in (a):

\frac{\frac{|SA||SD|}{|SB|}}{|CD|} =\frac{\frac{|SB||SC|}{|SD|}}{|AB|}.

Using (b) again this simplifies to:

\text{(c)}\quad\frac{|SD|}{|CD|}=\frac{|SB|}{|AB|}.

Draw an additional parallel to SD through A. This parallel intersects BD in G. Then one has |AC|=|DG| and, by the ray ratios just proved applied to the auxiliary configuration,

\frac{|SA|}{|SB|}=\frac{|DG|}{|BD|},

and therefore

\frac{|SA|}{|SB|}=\frac{|AC|}{|BD|}.

The ratio implies parallel lines

Suppose the ratio condition holds. Assume AC and BD are not parallel. Then the parallel line to AC through D intersects the ray SA in B_0\neq B. Since the given ratio is equivalently |SB|:|SA|=|SD|:|SC|, we have

|SB|=\frac{|SD||SA|}{|SC|},

and on the other hand from the forward ray ratios we have

|SB_0|=\frac{|SD||SA|}{|SC|}.

So B and B_0 are on the same side of S and have the same distance to S, which means B=B_0. This is a contradiction, so the assumption could not have been true, which means AC and BD are indeed parallel.

Level justification

Compare equal triangle areas for the forward ratios, draw an auxiliary parallel and use uniqueness of a point at a fixed ray distance for the converse. Elementary geometry and ratio algebra suffice.
