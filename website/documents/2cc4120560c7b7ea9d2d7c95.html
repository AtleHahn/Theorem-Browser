Statement

If a=a^* in a unital C*-algebra A, then u=\exp(ia) is unitary.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Proof 1: Modulus-one exponential identity

Write e for the unit of A.

It is {\displaystyle u=f(a)} with {\displaystyle f\colon \mathbb {R} \to \mathbb {C} ,\ x\mapsto \mathrm {e} ^{\mathrm {i} x}}, since {\displaystyle a} is self-adjoint, it follows that {\displaystyle \sigma (a)\subset \mathbb {R} }, i.e. {\displaystyle f} is a function on the spectrum of {\displaystyle a}. Since {\displaystyle f\cdot {\overline {f}}={\overline {f}}\cdot f=1}, using the functional calculus {\displaystyle uu^{*}=u^{*}u=e} follows, i.e. {\displaystyle u} is unitary.

Level justification

Apply continuous functional calculus on the real spectrum of a self-adjoint C*-algebra element. Spectra, C*-algebras and functional calculus require substantial advanced analysis, despite the simple scalar exponential identity.
