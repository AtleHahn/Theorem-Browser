Statement

For a nonzero real polynomial f, let V(f) count sign changes between successive nonzero coefficients in descending exponent order, and let Z(f) count positive real roots with multiplicity (a root has multiplicity k when (x-r)^k is its exact power factor). If f has degree d and nonzero constant term, then it has at least d-V(f)-V(f(-x)) nonreal complex roots, counted with multiplicity.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The assertion is about explicit polynomial substitutions, complex roots, trigonometric identities or finite products. All necessary nonstandard notation is explained, and elementary algebra suffices to understand the claim. Proof 1: Subtract the two real-root bounds

Write n=d.

Any nth degree polynomial has exactly n roots in the complex plane, if counted according to multiplicity. So if f(x) is a polynomial with real coefficients which does not have a root at 0 (that is a polynomial with a nonzero constant term) then the number of nonreal roots is at least

{\displaystyle n-(p+q),}

where p denotes the upper bound V(f) for the number of positive roots, q denotes the upper bound V(f(-x)) for the number of negative roots (both of which can be found using Descartes' rule of signs), and n denotes the degree of the polynomial.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Use the two supplied sign bounds for positive and negative roots, exclude zero by the constant term and subtract from the total complex-root count. Only finite counting and polynomial roots are needed.
