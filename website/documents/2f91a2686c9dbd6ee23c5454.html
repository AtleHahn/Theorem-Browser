Likely alternative prerequisite level: High school only; lowest recorded proof level: Undergraduate level. For each part a not divisible by d, write its multiplicity in base d and replace d^j copies of a by one part d^j a for each digit. The output multiplicities are below d. Conversely split each part uniquely into d^j times a d-indivisible part and expand it into copies of a. This is a finite weight-preserving bijection for every d>=2, including the empty partition, with no infinite generating products.

Statement

A partition of a nonnegative integer is a multiset of positive integers with that sum; zero has the empty partition. For integers d\ge2 and n\ge0, the number of partitions of n with no part divisible by d equals the number in which every part occurs fewer than d times.

Level
High school only
Level justification

A partition is explicitly a finite sum of positive integers considered without order; divisibility and repetitions are elementary.

Proof 1: Cancel matching factors in the partition generating functions

A proof of the theorem can be obtained with generating functions. If we denote by p_d(n) the number of partitions with no parts divisible by d and by q_d(n) the number of partitions with no parts repeated more than d-1 times, then the theorem means that for all n, p_d(n)=q_d(n). The uniqueness of ordinary generating functions implies that instead of proving that p_d(n)=q_d(n) for all n, it suffices to prove that the generating functions of p_d(n) and q_d(n) are equal, i.e. that

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p_d(n)x^n=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q_d(n)x^n.

Each generating function can be rewritten as an infinite product, using the product generating function for independent part multiplicities:

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p_d(n)x^n =\prod_{\substack{n\ge1\\d\nmid n}}\frac1{1-x^n},

i.e. the product of terms where n is not divisible by d, and

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q_d(n)x^n =\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1-x^{dn}}{1-x^n}.

These are formal power series; for each coefficient only finitely many factors contribute. If we expand the infinite product for q_d(n):

\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1-x^{dn}}{1-x^n} =\frac{1-x^d}{1-x}\frac{1-x^{2d}}{1-x^2}\cdots \frac{1-x^{kd}}{1-x^k}\cdots,

we see that each term in the numerator cancels with the corresponding multiple of d in the denominator. What remains after canceling all the numerator terms is exactly the infinite product for p_d(n).

Hence the generating functions for p_d(n) and q_d(n) are equal, proving equality of their coefficients.
