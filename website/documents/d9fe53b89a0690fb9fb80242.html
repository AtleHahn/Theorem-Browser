Statement

Let H be a finite undirected simple graph with vertex set V and adjacency relation \operatorname{adj}. A proper three-coloring assigns one of three colors to each vertex so that adjacent vertices have different colors. Such a coloring exists if and only if the following formula holds, where R,G,B range over subsets of V and u,v over vertices:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\exists R\ \exists G\ \exists B\ {\Bigl (}&\forall v\ (v\in R\vee v\in G\vee v\in B)\wedge {}\\&\forall u\ \forall v\ {\bigl (}(u\in R\wedge v\in R)\rightarrow \lnot \operatorname {adj} (u,v){\bigr )}\wedge {}\\&\forall u\ \forall v\ {\bigl (}(u\in G\wedge v\in G)\rightarrow \lnot \operatorname {adj} (u,v){\bigr )}\wedge {}\\&\forall u\ \forall v\ {\bigl (}(u\in B\wedge v\in B)\rightarrow \lnot \operatorname {adj} (u,v){\bigr )}{\Bigr )}\end{aligned}}}
Level
High school only
Level justification

All quantifiers range over finite vertices or their subsets and the clauses express a cover by independent sets.

Proof 1: Interpreting the covering and independence clauses (outline)

The first part of this formula ensures that the three color classes cover all the vertices of the graph, and the rest ensures that they each form an independent set. (It would also be possible to add clauses to the formula to ensure that the three color classes are disjoint, but this makes no difference to the result.)

Conversely, the color classes of a proper three-coloring satisfy the formula.

Level justification

Read the explicitly explained clauses as a cover by independent sets and keep one color per vertex if necessary. Elementary finite coloring and set logic suffice.
