Statement

An ideal in a Boolean algebra is a nonempty lower set closed under finite joins; a filter is a nonempty upper set closed under finite meets. An ideal or filter is proper if it is not the whole algebra. A proper ideal J is prime if a\wedge b\in J implies a\in J or b\in J. A maximal ideal is a proper ideal maximal among proper ideals. Write BPI for the assertion that whenever an ideal I and a filter F of a Boolean algebra are disjoint, some prime ideal contains I and is disjoint from F. Then BPI is equivalent to the assertion obtained by replacing “prime ideal” with “maximal ideal”.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The ideal, filter, properness, primeness, maximality and extension assertion are defined in the statement. From abstract algebra, the remaining Boolean-algebra setting can be learned as a complemented distributive lattice with the powerset as a concrete model. These related definitions and universal quantifiers fit about 30 minutes, without set-theoretic independence or a construction of maximal ideals.

Proof 1: Replace primeness by maximality

For any proper ideal I of a Boolean algebra B, the following are equivalent:

  • I is a prime ideal.
  • I is a maximal ideal, i.e. for any proper ideal J, if I is contained in J then I=J.

This theorem is a well-known fact for Boolean algebras. Thus the following (strong) maximal ideal theorem for Boolean algebras is equivalent to BPI:

Let B be a Boolean algebra, let I be an ideal and let F be a filter of B, such that I and F are disjoint. Then I is contained in some maximal ideal of B that is disjoint from F.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof substitutes the supplied equivalence of prime and maximal proper ideals into an extension statement. The Boolean ideal and filter definitions are stated explicitly and are a small order-algebra extension of undergraduate abstract algebra. It does not analyze the logical strength of BPI.
