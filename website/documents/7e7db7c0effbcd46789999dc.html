Statement

For every integer n\geq1, some multiset of 2n-2 integers has no size-n submultiset whose sum is divisible by n. Thus any universal threshold guaranteeing such a submultiset is at least 2n-1.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The counterexample uses a finite multiset of zeros and ones and elementary divisibility.

Proof 1: Zeros-and-ones counterexample

Take n-1 copies of 0 and n-1 copies of 1. This multiset contains no size-n submultiset summing to a multiple of n.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Choose n-1 zeros and n-1 ones. For n>1 a size-n selection has between one and n-1 ones, so its sum is not divisible by n; for n=1 the empty multiset has no size-one selection.
