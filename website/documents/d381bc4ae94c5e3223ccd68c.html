Statement

For nonzero u,v in a real inner-product space, the number \langle u,v\rangle/(\|u\|\|v\|) lies in [-1,1], so it determines an angle \theta=\arccos(\langle u,v\rangle/(\|u\|\|v\|))\in[0,\pi].

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Set cardinalities, functions and inner products are undergraduate concepts. Cardinal powers count function sets as explicitly stated, and transcendental means not a polynomial root. No ordinal arithmetic or algebraic-number-theory structure is required.

Proof 1: Bound the normalized inner product

Take \mathbf u,\mathbf v\ne0.

The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality allows one to extend the notion of "angle between two vectors" to any real inner-product space by defining:

{\displaystyle \cos \theta _{\mathbf {u} \mathbf {v} }={\frac {\langle \mathbf {u} ,\mathbf {v} \rangle }{\|\mathbf {u} \|\|\mathbf {v} \|}}.}

The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality proves that this definition is sensible, by showing that the right-hand side lies in the interval [−1, 1] and justifies the notion that (real) Hilbert spaces are simply generalizations of the Euclidean space.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Divide Cauchy–Schwarz by the positive norms and apply arccos on its domain. Undergraduate real inner-product linear algebra suffices, regardless of dimension; no Hilbert-space operator theory is used.
