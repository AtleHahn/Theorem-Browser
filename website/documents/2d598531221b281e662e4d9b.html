Statement

For a nonconstant real polynomial f, write Z(f) and Z(f') for their numbers of positive real roots counted with multiplicity. Then Z(f')\ge Z(f)-1.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves real polynomials; root multiplicities; integer inequalities; Polynomial differentiation. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Count intervening and repeated derivative roots

Between two distinct positive roots of f, Rolle’s theorem supplies a positive root of f'. A root of multiplicity k in f contributes multiplicity k-1 in f'. Counting these interior and repeated roots gives Z(f')\ge Z(f)-1.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use Rolle between consecutive distinct positive roots and differentiation of repeated factors at each root, then count multiplicities. Undergraduate calculus and polynomial algebra suffice.
