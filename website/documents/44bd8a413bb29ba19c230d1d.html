Statement

Let Z_1,Z_2,\ldots be independent uniform symbols from an alphabet of q\ge2 symbols, and let a_1,a_2,\ldots be any prescribed infinite symbol sequence. Then \mathbb P(Z_k=a_k\text{ for every }k)=0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The experiment uses independent choices from a finite alphabet or a uniform finite permutation. Elementary probability supplies products and averages, while analysis supplies infinite sequences and limits. The Poisson mass formula is explicit where used; no general stochastic-process machinery is needed.

Proof 1: Take the limit of exact finite-prefix probabilities

The probability that the first n symbols match the prescribed prefix is q^{-n}. These probabilities tend to zero as n\to\infty, so the probability of matching every symbol is zero.
