Statement

Let M_\epsilon be a smooth family of four-manifolds in a five-manifold, with a smooth tensor field T and background restriction T_0. Let two transverse identification flows have generators X,Y whose parameter components are one. Their difference is tangent to the slices; write \xi=(X-Y)|_{M_0}. If \delta_XT,\delta_YT denote the linear terms of the pulled-back perturbations, then \delta_XT-\delta_YT=\epsilon\mathcal L_\xi T_0.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Tensor fields; Smooth families of manifolds; Gauge identification flows; Lie derivatives. Proof 1: Subtracting the two pullback expansions

Write h_\epsilon for the flow generated by X, mapping a point p_0\in M_0 to p_\epsilon\in M_\epsilon. This also defines a pullback h_\epsilon^* that maps the tensor field T_\epsilon back onto M_0. Given sufficient smoothness, a Taylor expansion can be defined:

h_\epsilon^*(T_\epsilon) =T_0+\epsilon(\mathcal L_XT)_0+O(\epsilon^2).

Thus \delta_XT=\epsilon(\mathcal L_XT)_0 is the linear perturbation of T in this gauge. However, since the choice of X is dependent on the choice of gauge, another gauge can be taken. Therefore the difference of the linear perturbations becomes

\delta_XT-\delta_YT =\epsilon(\mathcal L_XT)_0-\epsilon(\mathcal L_YT)_0 =\epsilon(\mathcal L_{X-Y}T)_0.

Picking a chart where X^a=(\xi^\mu,1) and Y^a=(0,1), we have

X^a-Y^a=(\xi^\mu,0),

which is tangent to each M_\epsilon. Its restriction to M_0 is \xi, giving the result

\delta_XT-\delta_YT=\epsilon\mathcal L_\xi T_0.
