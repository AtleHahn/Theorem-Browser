GHZ three-qubit products admit no predetermined local signs

The GHZ correlations contradict every assignment of predetermined local outcomes to the specified measurements. This provides an exact, nonstatistical obstruction to a classical local value picture of quantum observables; it does not by itself establish a unique interpretation of quantum mechanics or permit faster-than-light signalling.

Statement

Let \sigma_x=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}, \sigma_y=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\i&0\end{pmatrix}, and \sigma_z=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix} be the Pauli matrices on \mathbb C^2, with |0\rangle,|1\rangle their standard \sigma_z eigenbasis. Let |\psi\rangle=(|000\rangle-|111\rangle)/\sqrt2 in (\mathbb C^2)^{\otimes3}, where juxtaposition denotes tensor product. In this state the observables \sigma_x\otimes\sigma_y\otimes\sigma_y, \sigma_y\otimes\sigma_x\otimes\sigma_y, and \sigma_y\otimes\sigma_y\otimes\sigma_x have certain outcome +1, while \sigma_x\otimes\sigma_x\otimes\sigma_x has certain outcome -1. No six predetermined local signs, one for each x/y measurement at each of the three sites, reproduce all four outcomes.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

All matrices and the vector are explicit. The tensor product is the finite Kronecker construction on an eight-dimensional complex space, and a certain measurement outcome is the eigenvalue in the stated eigenstate rule. These two linked additions to undergraduate complex linear algebra suffice; no quantum dynamics or general measurement theory is needed.

Proof 1: GHZ eigenvalue predictions and a sign-product contradiction

Applying the Born rule to the three-qubit state {\displaystyle |\psi \rangle }, the prediction is that whenever the three measurements include one {\displaystyle \sigma _{x}} and two {\displaystyle \sigma _{y}}'s, the product of the outcomes will always be {\displaystyle +1}. This follows because {\displaystyle |\psi \rangle } is an eigenvector of {\displaystyle \sigma _{x}\otimes \sigma _{y}\otimes \sigma _{y}} with eigenvalue {\displaystyle +1}, and likewise for {\displaystyle \sigma _{y}\otimes \sigma _{x}\otimes \sigma _{y}} and {\displaystyle \sigma _{y}\otimes \sigma _{y}\otimes \sigma _{x}}.

For the predetermined signs, use the following local algebraic fact. Let a_x,a_y,b_x,b_y,c_x,c_y\in\{-1,1\}. If a_xb_yc_y=a_yb_xc_y=a_yb_yc_x=1, then a_xb_xc_x=1. In particular these three equations are incompatible with a_xb_xc_x=-1.

{\displaystyle (a_{x}b_{y}c_{y})(a_{y}b_{x}c_{y})(a_{y}b_{y}c_{x})=a_{x}b_{x}c_{x}a_{y}^{2}b_{y}^{2}c_{y}^{2}=a_{x}b_{x}c_{x}\,,}

because the square of either {\displaystyle -1} or {\displaystyle +1} is {\displaystyle 1}. Each factor in parentheses equals {\displaystyle +1}, so

{\displaystyle a_{x}b_{x}c_{x}=+1\,,}

The product of the three predetermined x-results would be {\displaystyle +1} with probability unity. But this is inconsistent with quantum physics: the Born rule applied to the state {\displaystyle |\psi \rangle } predicts that the measurement {\displaystyle \sigma _{x}\otimes \sigma _{x}\otimes \sigma _{x}} will instead yield {\displaystyle -1} with probability unity.
