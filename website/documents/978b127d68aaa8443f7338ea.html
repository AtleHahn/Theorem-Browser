Statement

Let \mathcal F be a family of finite graphs. The maximum haven orders of graphs in \mathcal F are unbounded if and only if, for every positive integer k, some graph in \mathcal F has the k-by-k square grid as a minor. Here a haven of order h chooses a component of G−X for every vertex set X with |X|<h, monotonically under deletion: for X⊆Y its component for Y is contained in its component for X. A minor is obtained by vertex or edge deletions and edge contractions.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Quantified families of graph minors and haven component assignments use graph-theoretic structures outside the rubric’s calculus/linear-algebra baseline; the statement does not require the proof’s treewidth or excluded-grid theorem.

Proof 1: Unbounded haven orders and unbounded grid minors

For a finite graph, the maximum haven order is one more than its treewidth. Therefore haven orders are unbounded in the family exactly when treewidth is unbounded. The finite excluded-grid theorem says that this is equivalent to the family’s minors containing arbitrarily large square grids, which is the conclusion.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The argument passes through treewidth, havens as coherent component choices and graph minors. Retain beyond undergraduate provisionally because understanding these three linked structural graph notions is not confidently a small extension, despite the numerical equivalence being short.
