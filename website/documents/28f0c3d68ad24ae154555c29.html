Statement

Let a<b be real and let K:[a,b]^2\to\mathbb R be continuous and symmetric. Suppose it is positive definite in the non-strict sense: \sum_{i,j}K(x_i,x_j)c_ic_j\ge0 for all finite point lists and real coefficients. On real L^2[a,b], put (T_Kf)(t)=\int_a^bK(t,s)f(s)\,ds. Then T_K is trace class and \operatorname{tr}(T_K)=\sum_j\lambda_j=\int_a^bK(t,t)\,dt, with eigenvalues counted with multiplicity.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Trace class and the operator trace are infinite-dimensional functional-analytic notions.

Proof 1: Integrating the Mercer diagonal expansion — outline

Use Mercer's theorem: K has the absolutely and uniformly convergent representation

K(s,t)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_j e_j(s)e_j(t),

with nonnegative eigenvalues and orthonormal eigenfunctions. The following is immediate:

\int_a^b K(t,t)\,dt=\sum_i\lambda_i.

This shows that the operator T_K is a trace class operator and

\operatorname{trace}(T_K)=\int_a^b K(t,t)\,dt.

Here the trace criterion for a positive compact operator identifies its trace with the convergent sum of its eigenvalues.
