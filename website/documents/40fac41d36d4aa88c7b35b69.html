Statement

Let ABC be a nondegenerate Euclidean triangle and let its internal angle bisector AD meet the opposite side at D between B and C. Then |BD|/|CD|=|AB|/|AC|.

Proof 1: Reflection and similar triangles

The triangle {\displaystyle \triangle ABC} is reflected across the line through A perpendicular to the angle bisector {\displaystyle AD}, resulting in the triangle {\displaystyle \triangle AB_{2}C_{2}} with bisector {\displaystyle AD_{2}}. The fact that the bisection-produced angles {\displaystyle \angle BAD} and {\displaystyle \angle CAD} are equal means that {\displaystyle BAC_{2}} and {\displaystyle CAB_{2}} are straight lines. This allows the construction of triangle {\displaystyle \triangle C_{2}BC} that is similar to {\displaystyle \triangle ABD}. Because the ratios between corresponding sides of similar triangles are all equal, it follows that {\displaystyle |AB|/|AC_{2}|=|BD|/|CD|}. However, {\displaystyle AC_{2}} was constructed as a reflection of the line {\displaystyle AC}, and so those two lines are of equal length. Therefore, {\displaystyle |AB|/|AC|=|BD|/|CD|}, yielding the result stated by the theorem.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Reflection preserves lengths and the bisected angles put the reflected vertex on the opposite side ray. The resulting similar triangles give AB/(AB+AC)=BD/(BD+CD), equivalent to the claimed ratio by cross multiplication. All reasoning is elementary Euclidean reflection, similarity and algebra.

Proof 3: Altitudes and similar right triangles

Let D be a point on the line BC, not equal to B or C and such that AD is not an altitude of triangle △ABC.

Let B1 be the base (foot) of the altitude in the triangle △ABD through B and let C1 be the base of the altitude in the triangle △ACD through C.

∠ DB1B, ∠ DC1C are right angles, while the angles ∠ B1DB, ∠ C1DC are congruent if D lies on the segment BC (that is, between B and C) and they are identical in the other cases being considered, so the triangles △DB1B, △DC1C are similar (AAA), which implies that:

{\displaystyle {\frac {|BD|}{|CD|}}={\frac {|BB_{1}|}{|CC_{1}|}}={\frac {|AB|\sin \angle BAD}{|AC|\sin \angle CAD}}.}

If D is the foot of an altitude, then,

{\displaystyle {\frac {|BD|}{|AB|}}=\sin \angle \ BAD{\text{ and }}{\frac {|CD|}{|AC|}}=\sin \angle \ DAC,}

and the displayed ratio follows.

For the internal angle bisector, \angle BAD=\angle CAD, so the sine factors cancel.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Perpendicular feet on the bisector give similar right triangles, whose height ratio equals BD/CD. Express the heights using AB sin(BAD) and AC sin(CAD), then cancel the equal sine factors. The altitude case is handled directly. No prerequisites beyond Euclidean similarity and elementary trigonometry are used.

Proof 4: Auxiliary isosceles triangle

Construct point {\displaystyle D'} on the bisector such that {\displaystyle \triangle ABD\sim \triangle ACD'}. We aim to show that {\displaystyle |CD|=|CD'|}.

If D'=D, the required equality is immediate. Otherwise, consider the following two cases.

In the case where {\displaystyle D'} lies on {\displaystyle {\overline {AD}}}, we have that

{\displaystyle \angle CD'D=180^{\circ }-\angle CD'A=180^{\circ }-\angle BDA=\angle CDD',}

and in the case where {\displaystyle D'} does not lie on {\displaystyle {\overline {AD}}}, we have that

{\displaystyle \angle CD'D=\angle CD'A=\angle BDA=\angle CDD'.}

Either way, {\displaystyle \triangle CDD'} is isosceles, implying that {\displaystyle |CD|=|CD'|}. Therefore,

{\displaystyle {\frac {|AB|}{|AC|}}={\frac {|BD|}{|CD'|}}={\frac {|BD|}{|CD|}},}

which was the desired result.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The construction and argument are elementary Euclidean geometry.

Proof 5: Ratio of triangle areas

A quick proof can be obtained by looking at the ratio of the areas of the two triangles △BAD, △CAD, which are created by the angle bisector in A. Computing those areas twice using different formulas, that is {\displaystyle {\tfrac {1}{2}}gh} with base {\displaystyle g} and altitude h and {\displaystyle {\tfrac {1}{2}}ab\sin(\gamma )} with sides a, b and their enclosed angle γ, will yield the desired result.

Let h denote the height of the triangles on base BC and {\displaystyle \alpha } be half of the angle in A. Then

{\displaystyle {\frac {|\triangle ABD|}{|\triangle ACD|}}={\frac {{\frac {1}{2}}|BD|h}{{\frac {1}{2}}|CD|h}}={\frac {|BD|}{|CD|}}}

and

{\displaystyle {\frac {|\triangle ABD|}{|\triangle ACD|}}={\frac {{\frac {1}{2}}|AB||AD|\sin(\alpha )}{{\frac {1}{2}}|AC||AD|\sin(\alpha )}}={\frac {|AB|}{|AC|}}}

yields

{\displaystyle {\frac {|BD|}{|CD|}}={\frac {|AB|}{|AC|}}.}
Level
High school only
Level justification

Compute the ratio of the two triangle areas first using their common altitude to BC and then using their common side AD and equal included angles. Cancelling the common positive factors gives the desired ratio. This is high-school area geometry and trigonometry.
