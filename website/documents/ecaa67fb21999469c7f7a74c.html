Statement

Let N,d\ge1 and x_1,\ldots,x_N\in\mathbb R^d. Let C(x_1,\ldots,x_N) be the number of binary labelings (\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_N)\in\{-1,1\}^N for which there exists w\in\mathbb R^d satisfying

\varepsilon_i(w\cdot x_i)>0\quad\text{for every }i.

Then

C(x_1,\ldots,x_N)=2\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\binom{N-1}{j}

if and only if the vectors are in homogeneous general position: every subfamily of at most d vectors is linearly independent. A binomial coefficient with j>N-1 is taken to be zero.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Undergraduate linear algebra and elementary counting suffice; homogeneous separation is explicitly defined.

Proof 1: Separating cones and the binomial counting recurrence (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorem 1, Lemma 1 and proof of Theorem 3, pp.326–328).

For necessity, write F(N,d)=2\sum_{j<d}\binom{N-1}{j}. Lemma 1 gives the universal increment bound C_{N+1}-C_N\le F(N,d-1), hence C_N\le F(N,d) by induction. If a subfamily of size m\le d is dependent, a nonzero relation \sum a_ix_i=0 forbids the labeling \varepsilon_i=\operatorname{sign}(a_i) on its nonzero coefficients. Its count is strictly below 2^m=F(m,d). Order this subfamily first; the increment bound preserves the strict deficit for all subsequent vectors. A zero vector admits no strict labeling.
