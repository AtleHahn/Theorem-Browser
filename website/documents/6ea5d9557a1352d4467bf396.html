Statement

Let f be meromorphic on a punctured disk 0<|z-z_0|<r_0 and admit no meromorphic extension across z_0. For every 0<\delta<r_0, its finite values on 0<|z-z_0|<\delta are dense in \mathbb C.

Beyond undergraduate level

The statement allows poles inside the punctured domain and excludes a meromorphic extension at the missing point.

Proof 1: Reciprocal extension across poles and the puncture — outline

Take as given that function f is meromorphic on some punctured neighborhood V \ {z0}, and that it admits no meromorphic extension across z0. Assume by way of contradiction that some value b exists that the function can never get close to; that is: assume that there is some complex value b and some ε > 0 such that ‖f(z) − b‖ ≥ ε for all z in the punctured neighborhood at which f is finite.

Then the new function:

{\displaystyle g(z)={\frac {1}{f(z)-b}}}

must be holomorphic on V \ {z0}, with zeroes at the poles of f, and bounded by 1/ε. It can therefore be analytically continued (or continuously extended, or holomorphically extended) to all of V by Riemann's removable-singularity theorem. So the original function can be expressed in terms of g:

{\displaystyle f(z)={\frac {1}{g(z)}}+b}

for all arguments z in V \ {z0}. The extended g is not identically zero, since it is a reciprocal wherever f is finite; thus any zero at z_0 has finite order. Consider the two possible cases for

{\displaystyle \lim _{z\to z_{0}}g(z).}

If the limit is 0, then f has a pole at z0 . If the limit is not 0, then z0 is a removable singularity of f . Both possibilities contradict the assumption that f admits no meromorphic extension across z0 . Hence the assumption is false and the theorem holds.
