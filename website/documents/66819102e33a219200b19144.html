Statement

A finite simple undirected graph G=(V,E) has vertices V and edges consisting of unordered pairs of distinct vertices. A proper vertex coloring assigns different colors to the ends of every edge. A proper edge coloring gives different colors to edges sharing an endpoint. Assume V is nonempty. If \chi'(G) is the least number of edge colors and \Delta(G) the maximum vertex degree, then \chi'(G)\ge\Delta(G).

Level
High school only
Level justification

The finite objects and coloring constraints are defined locally; only elementary counting and algebra are needed.

Proof 1: Maximum degree lower bound for edge coloring

All edges incident to one vertex need different colors. At a vertex of maximum degree this requires \Delta(G) colors.
