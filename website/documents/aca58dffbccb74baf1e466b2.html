Statement

Let X_1,\ldots,X_n be independent identically distributed real random variables with cumulative distribution function F, and let X_{(1)}\le\cdots\le X_{(n)} be their order statistics. Fix 1\le n_1<\cdots<n_k\le n and x_1<\cdots<x_k. No continuity of F is required. Then

\Pr\bigl(X_{(n_j)}\le x_j\text{ for }1\le j\le k\bigr) =\sum_{\substack{n_j\le i_j\ (1\le j\le k)\\ i_1\le\cdots\le i_k\le n}} \frac{n!F(x_1)^{i_1}(1-F(x_k))^{n-i_k}}{i_1!(n-i_k)!} \prod_{j=2}^k\frac{(F(x_j)-F(x_{j-1}))^{i_j-i_{j-1}}}{(i_j-i_{j-1})!}.

Use 0^0=1 and the empty-product convention.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The additional probability concepts are independence, a distribution function P(X<=x), and order statistics obtained by sorting a sample. These have concrete finite-sample interpretations. With factorial sums and inequalities supplied, an undergraduate can understand the event and formula after a short introduction without measure-theoretic probability.

Proof 1: Reduce the identical-row permanent

Bapat–Beg formula used (dependency, equal marginals)

Under the stated hypotheses, the Bapat–Beg formula expresses the joint probability as

\sum_{\substack{n_j\le i_j\ (1\le j\le k)\\i_1\le\cdots\le i_k\le n}} \frac{\operatorname{per}M_{i_1,\ldots,i_k}}{i_1!(i_2-i_1)!\cdots(i_k-i_{k-1})!(n-i_k)!}.

Each row of the n\times n matrix M_{i_1,\ldots,i_k} has i_1 copies of F(x_1), then i_j-i_{j-1} copies of F(x_j)-F(x_{j-1}) for each 2\le j\le k, and finally n-i_k copies of 1-F(x_k). The permanent is \operatorname{per}M=\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}\prod_{r=1}^nM_{r,\sigma(r)}.

Identical-marginal reduction

In the case when the variables {\displaystyle X_{1},X_{2},\ldots ,X_{n}} are independent and identically distributed with cumulative probability distribution function {\displaystyle F_{i}=F} for all i the theorem reduces to

\Pr\bigl(X_{(n_j)}\le x_j\text{ for }1\le j\le k\bigr) =\sum_{\substack{n_j\le i_j\ (1\le j\le k)\\ i_1\le\cdots\le i_k\le n}} \frac{n!F(x_1)^{i_1}(1-F(x_k))^{n-i_k}}{i_1!(n-i_k)!} \prod_{j=2}^k\frac{(F(x_j)-F(x_{j-1}))^{i_j-i_{j-1}}}{(i_j-i_{j-1})!}.
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof substitutes equal distribution functions into the supplied finite permanent formula. A cumulative distribution function is the probability of X<=x, and order statistics are the sorted observations. The permanent differs from the familiar determinant only by omitting signs; with identical rows each permutation contributes the same product, giving n! copies. These are small extensions of undergraduate probability, combinatorics and linear algebra; the complicated indexing does not introduce advanced probability theory.
