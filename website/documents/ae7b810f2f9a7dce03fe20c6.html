Statement

Let A be a compact self-adjoint operator on a complex Hilbert space H, with infinitely many positive eigenvalues \lambda_1\ge\lambda_2\ge\cdots>0, repeated with multiplicity. For every integer k\ge1,

\lambda_k=\min_{\substack{S\subset H\text{ a linear subspace}\\\dim S=k-1}} \max_{\substack{x\in S^\perp\\\|x\|=1}}\langle Ax,x\rangle.

Proof 1: Orthogonal intersection and the compact spectral theorem — outline

Choose orthonormal eigenvectors u_j for the listed positive eigenvalues. Consider a (k-1)-dimensional subspace S_{k-1}, whose orthogonal complement is denoted by S_{k-1}^{\perp}. If S'=\operatorname{span}\{u_1,\ldots,u_k\}, the dimension count gives

S'\cap S_{k-1}^{\perp}\ne\{0\}.

So

\exists x\in S_{k-1}^{\perp},\quad \|x\|=1,\quad \langle Ax,x\rangle\geq\lambda_k.

This implies

\max_{\substack{x\in S_{k-1}^{\perp}\\\|x\|=1}}\langle Ax,x\rangle\geq\lambda_k.

Here the maximum exists by the compact self-adjoint spectral theorem applied to the compression of A to S_{k-1}^{\perp}: a positive supremum of its unit quadratic form is its largest positive eigenvalue and is attained.

Indexing the above by the collection of (k-1)-dimensional subspaces gives

\inf_{S_{k-1}}\max_{\substack{x\in S_{k-1}^{\perp}\\\|x\|=1}}\langle Ax,x\rangle\geq\lambda_k.

Pick S_{k-1}=\operatorname{span}\{u_1,\ldots,u_{k-1}\}. On its orthogonal complement the remaining positive eigenvalues are at most \lambda_k, while the negative spectral part and the kernel contribute nonpositive values; equality is attained at u_k. We deduce

\min_{S_{k-1}}\max_{\substack{x\in S_{k-1}^{\perp}\\\|x\|=1}}\langle Ax,x\rangle=\lambda_k.
