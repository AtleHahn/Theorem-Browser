Statement

For a positive integer n, let \sigma(n)=\sum_{d\mid n,d>0}d. Define \mu(1)=1, \mu(m)=0 if a prime square divides m, and \mu(m)=(-1)^r if m is a product of r distinct primes. Then n=\sum_{d\mid n,d>0}\sigma(d)\mu(n/d).

Level
High school only
Level justification

The identity and the Möbius function use only finite arithmetic on prime factors.

Proof 1: Invert the divisor convolution

For arithmetic functions define (f*g)(n)=\sum_{d\mid n,d>0}f(d)g(n/d), let \mathrm{Id}(n)=n, and let \mathbf1(n)=1. The divisor-sum definition gives \sigma=\mathrm{Id}*\mathbf1. Möbius inversion gives \mathrm{Id}=\sigma*\mu.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The convolution notation is explicitly a finite divisor sum. Substitute the identity function into the supplied Mobius inversion formula; prime factors, finite sums and the locally defined sign function suffice.
