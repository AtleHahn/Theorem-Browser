Statement

Let (A_i)_{i\in I} be a possibly infinite indexed family of finite sets, repetitions allowed. Form a bipartite graph with disjoint tagged copies of I and U=\bigcup_i A_i, joining i to a exactly when a∈A_i. The I-saturating matchings are in bijection with injective functions f:I→U satisfying f(i)∈A_i. Each left degree is |A_i| and hence finite; right degrees need not be finite.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Graphs, finite subset colorings and infinite sequences are elementary discrete mathematics. Matchings are disjoint edges and a ray is an infinite nonrepeating path; the conditions are explicitly given. No graph-minor or set-theoretic independence machinery is needed.

Proof 1: Incidence matchings and representative assignments — outline

Given a matching saturating I, assign to i the element at the other end of its unique matching edge. Disjointness of matching edges gives injectivity. Conversely, the edges (i,f(i)) from an injective representative assignment form a matching saturating I. These constructions undo each other. The neighbors of i are precisely A_i, so the degree assertion follows.

Level justification

Read each matching edge as a distinct selected representative and reverse that construction. Undergraduate sets and functions suffice for the arbitrary indexed family; no infinite matching existence theorem or choice construction is used.
