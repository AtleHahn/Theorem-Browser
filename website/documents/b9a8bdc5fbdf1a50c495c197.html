Statement

Let S\subset\mathbb R^3 be a compact smooth oriented surface with smooth boundary, induced boundary orientation and unit normal n. For a smooth vector field F=(P,Q,R) on a neighborhood of S, \int_{\partial S}F\cdot dl=\int_S(\nabla\times F)\cdot n\,dS.

Proof 1: Identify the one-form and its exterior derivative

The classical Stokes' theorem relates the surface integral of the curl of a vector field over a surface {\displaystyle \Sigma } in Euclidean three-space to the line integral of the vector field over its boundary. It is a special case of the general Stokes theorem (in dimension 2) once we identify the vector field F=(P,Q,R) with the 1-form \omega=P\,dx+Q\,dy+R\,dz using the metric on Euclidean 3-space. The curve of the line integral, ⁠{\displaystyle \partial \Sigma }⁠, must have positive orientation, meaning that {\displaystyle \partial \Sigma } points counterclockwise when the surface normal, ⁠{\displaystyle n}⁠, points toward the viewer.

The general Stokes formula \int_S d\omega=\int_{\partial S}\omega therefore reads, with \Sigma=S,

{\displaystyle \iint _{\Sigma }{\Biggl (}\left({\frac {\partial R}{\partial y}}-{\frac {\partial Q}{\partial z}}\right)dy\,dz+\left({\frac {\partial P}{\partial z}}-{\frac {\partial R}{\partial x}}\right)dz\,dx+\left({\frac {\partial Q}{\partial x}}-{\frac {\partial P}{\partial y}}\right)dx\,dy{\Biggr )}=\oint _{\partial \Sigma }{\Big (}P\,dx+Q\,dy+R\,dz{\Big )}\,,}

where {\displaystyle P,Q} and {\displaystyle R} are the components of ⁠{\displaystyle {\textbf {F}}}⁠, and {\displaystyle \partial \Sigma } is the boundary of the region ⁠{\displaystyle \Sigma }⁠.
