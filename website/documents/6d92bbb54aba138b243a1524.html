Statement

Let a finite simple graph have a proper partial edge coloring with uv uncolored. Let p_0=v,p_1,\ldots,p_k=w be distinct neighbors of u such that up_j is colored for j\geq1 and its color is missing at p_{j-1}. If a color c is missing at both u and w, the coloring extends to uv by recoloring just these edges. Proper means that colored edges sharing a vertex have different colors; a color is missing at a vertex if no colored incident edge has that color.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Partial properness, missing colors and the finite neighbor list are specified. Understanding the proposed extension requires tracking finitely many colors and avoiding equality conflicts.

Proof 1: Shift toward the initially uncolored edge

Recoloring edge uw with color c allows the remaining edge colors to be shifted one step along this path: for each vertex p other than w in the path, edge up takes the color that was previously used by the successor of p in the path. This leads to a new coloring that includes edge uv.

Here the path is p_0=v,p_1,\ldots,p_k=w from the statement, and the colors used in the shift are the original colors. If k=0, simply color uv with c.

Level justification

Shift the original colors along the specified finite list and place c at its end. The missing-color conditions prevent conflicts at neighbors, and distinct original colors plus c prevent conflicts at u.
