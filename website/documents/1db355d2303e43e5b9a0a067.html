Statement

Let (P,\le) be a finite partially ordered set and let x,y,z be three distinct pairwise incomparable elements. Choose uniformly from all total orders of P that respect \le; such orders are called linear extensions. Then

\mathbb P(x\text{ precedes }y)\,\mathbb P(x\text{ precedes }z)\le\mathbb P(x\text{ precedes both }y\text{ and }z).

A partial order is reflexive, antisymmetric and transitive; incomparable means neither element is at most the other. The probabilities are the corresponding proportions of the finite set of linear extensions.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Local order definitions and uniform probabilities as finite proportions require only elementary reasoning; proof inequalities are not statement topics.

Level justification

Finite counting, coordinatewise minima and maxima, induction and nonnegative inequalities suffice. The required correlation inequality follows from the two-point four-functions inequality by induction on finite sets. An explicit count bounds collisions and transfers the inequality to linear extensions.
