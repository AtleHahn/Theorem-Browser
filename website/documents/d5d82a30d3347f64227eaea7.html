Statement

For unit vectors a,b in \mathbb R^3, set P(a,b)=-a\cdot b. This correlation violates the Bell constraint |P(a,b)-P(a,c)|\leq1+P(b,c) for some unit vectors a,b,c. Consequently it cannot be reproduced for all settings by a local sign-response hidden-variable model with a setting-independent probability law and perfect same-setting anticorrelation. Here such a model has sign responses A(a,\lambda),B(b,\lambda) under one probability law for \lambda, its correlation is \mathbb E[A(a,\lambda)B(b,\lambda)], and perfect anticorrelation means A(a,\lambda)=-B(a,\lambda) almost surely for every fixed setting.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The hidden-variable model is defined through finite-dimensional dot products and scalar sign-response expectations. Probability-one equality and one common law add limited probability definitions, without quantum mechanics.

Proof 1: Orthogonal directions and a 45-degree intermediate setting

Use the external Bell constraint for such a model: if sign responses A(a,\lambda),B(b,\lambda)\in\{-1,1\} depend only on their local settings, share one setting-independent probability law, and satisfy A(a,\lambda)=-B(a,\lambda), then their correlations satisfy

|P(a,b)-P(a,c)|\leq1+P(b,c).

For the stated correlation, let the vectors {\displaystyle {\vec {a}}} and {\displaystyle {\vec {b}}} be orthogonal, and let {\displaystyle {\vec {c}}} lie in their plane at a 45° angle from both of them. Then

{\displaystyle P({\vec {a}},{\vec {b}})=0,}

while

{\displaystyle P({\vec {a}},{\vec {c}})=P({\vec {b}},{\vec {c}})=-{\frac {\sqrt {2}}{2}},}

but

{\displaystyle {\frac {\sqrt {2}}{2}}\nleq 1-{\frac {\sqrt {2}}{2}}.}

Therefore no such model reproduces P(a,b)=-a\cdot b for all settings.
