Statement

For 0<t<1, put

M=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\t^{-2}&0\end{pmatrix},\quad R=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad N=M+R.

Although \|R\|_2=1, both eigenvalues of M are zero while those of N are 1/t and -1/t. Thus the Hermitian eigenvalue perturbation bound fails for these nonnormal matrices.

Proof 1: A two-by-two matrix example — outline

{\displaystyle M={\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\1/t^{2}&0\end{bmatrix}},\qquad N=M+R={\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\1/t^{2}&0\end{bmatrix}},\qquad R={\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{bmatrix}}.}

The eigenvalues of M and N, respectively, are {\displaystyle \mu _{1}=\mu _{2}=0} and {\displaystyle \nu _{1}=+1/t,\nu _{2}=-1/t}; they do not satisfy {\displaystyle |\mu _{i}-\nu _{i}|\leq \|R\|_{2}=1}.
