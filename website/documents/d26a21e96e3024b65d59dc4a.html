Statement

Let a_n,b_n be real, with b_n>0, and set A_n=\sum_{k=1}^n a_k, B_n=\sum_{k=1}^n b_k. If B_n\to+\infty, then \liminf a_n/b_n\le\liminf A_n/B_n\le\limsup A_n/B_n\le\limsup a_n/b_n.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves real sequences; positive weights; partial sums; limit inferior and limit superior. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Bound the tail by a fixed limsup level

First we notice that:

  • \liminf_{n\to\infty} A_n/B_n\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty} A_n/B_n holds by definition of limit superior and limit inferior;
  • the lower bound follows by applying the upper bound to the sequence (-a_n), because \liminf_{n\to\infty}x_n=-\limsup_{n\to\infty}(-x_n) for any sequence (x_n)_{n\geq1}.

Therefore we need only to show that \limsup_{n\to\infty} A_n/B_n\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}a_n/b_n. If L:=\limsup_{n\to\infty}a_n/b_n=+\infty there is nothing to prove, hence we can assume L<+\infty (it can be either finite or -\infty). By definition of \limsup, for all real l>L there is a natural number \nu>0 such that

\frac{a_n}{b_n}<l,\quad \forall n>\nu.

We can use this inequality so as to write

A_n=A_\nu+a_{\nu+1}+\dots+a_n<A_\nu+l(B_n-B_\nu),\quad \forall n>\nu.

Because b_n>0, we also have B_n>0 and we can divide by B_n to get

\frac{A_n}{B_n}<\frac{A_\nu-lB_\nu}{B_n}+l,\quad \forall n>\nu.

Since B_n\to+\infty as n\to+\infty, the sequence

\frac{A_\nu-lB_\nu}{B_n}\to0\text{ as }n\to+\infty\text{ (keeping }\nu\text{ fixed)},

and we obtain

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{A_n}{B_n}\leq l,\quad \forall l>L.

Since this holds for every real l>L, this means that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{A_n}{B_n}\leq L=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_n}{b_n},

including when L=-\infty, and we are done.

Level justification

Bound every sufficiently late ratio by a fixed level above its limsup, sum using positive weights, and let the fixed initial contribution vanish after division. Undergraduate real sequences, including infinite limsup and liminf, suffice.
