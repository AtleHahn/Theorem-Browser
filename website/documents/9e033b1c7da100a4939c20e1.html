Statement

Let p_n be the nth prime in increasing order, beginning with p_1=2. For every integer n\ge2,

p_n>n(\log n+\log\log n-1),

where \log is the natural logarithm.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Elementary algebra and logarithms suffice, including nested logarithm for n>=2.

Proof 1: Explicit prime estimates and strictness (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (1999 Math. Comp.68 pp.411–414, Theorems 1–3; 2010 Propositions 6.2 and 6.7 pp.5–8).

Supporting passages: Dusart, Estimates of some functions over primes, Proposition 6.7.

Use Dusart 1999 Theorems 1–3: explicit Chebyshev estimates from verified zeta zeros close the middle range; Robin handles primes below 10^11 and Schoenfeld estimates handle the remaining ranges. The published zero verification and finite-range estimates are named dependencies. This independently establishes the weak baseline used in Dusart 2010 Proposition 6.2, so Proposition 6.7 follows with that baseline: p_n>=n(log n+loglog n-1+(loglog n-2.1)/log n). For n>=4000 the extra term is positive because loglog4000>2.1155>2.1. For 2<=n<=4000 a sieve below 50000 and 60-digit Decimal logarithms gives strict inequality; minimum margin exceeds 4.148 at n=4. The finite check uses a sieve and high-precision logarithms; it is a numerical finite-range check. The positive correction term gives strictness for the remaining range. The finite-range calculation records the range, precision and minimum margin.
