Statement

Let X_1,\ldots,X_n be pairwise uncorrelated real random variables with finite common variance \sigma^2, where n\ge1. Then \operatorname{Var}\left(\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^nX_j\right)=\frac{\sigma^2}{n}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Variance and uncorrelatedness are related first/second integral-moment definitions extending undergraduate analysis.

Proof 1: Add uncorrelated variances and rescale

This proof uses the assumption of finite variance {\displaystyle \operatorname {Var} (X_{i})=\sigma ^{2}} (for all {\displaystyle i}). The random variables are pairwise uncorrelated, and, writing \overline X_n=n^{-1}(X_1+\cdots+X_n), we have that

{\displaystyle \operatorname {Var} ({\overline {X}}_{n})=\operatorname {Var} ({\tfrac {1}{n}}(X_{1}+\cdots +X_{n}))={\frac {1}{n^{2}}}\operatorname {Var} (X_{1}+\cdots +X_{n})={\frac {n\sigma ^{2}}{n^{2}}}={\frac {\sigma ^{2}}{n}}.}
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Scale the variance and add uncorrelated variances. Undergraduate probability suffices; the argument uses moment identities without measure-theoretic conditional expectation.
