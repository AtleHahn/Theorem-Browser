Statement

For every real x, let \operatorname{arccot}x\in(0,\pi) be the inverse of cotangent. Then (\operatorname{arccot}x)'=-1/(1+x^2).

Proof 1: Differentiate the cotangent inverse equation

We let

{\displaystyle y=\operatorname {arccot} x}

where {\displaystyle 0<y<\pi }. Then

{\displaystyle \cot y=x}

Taking the derivative with respect to {\displaystyle x} on both sides and solving for dy/dx:

{\displaystyle {\frac {d}{dx}}\cot y={\frac {d}{dx}}x}

Left side:

{\displaystyle {d \over dx}\cot y=-\csc ^{2}y\cdot {dy \over dx}=-(1+\cot ^{2}y){dy \over dx}} using the Pythagorean identity

Right side:

{\displaystyle {d \over dx}x=1}

Therefore,

{\displaystyle -(1+\cot ^{2}y){\frac {dy}{dx}}=1}

Substituting {\displaystyle x=\cot y},

{\displaystyle -(1+x^{2}){\frac {dy}{dx}}=1}{\displaystyle {\frac {dy}{dx}}=-{\frac {1}{1+x^{2}}}}
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Cotangent has derivative -csc^2 y, which is nonzero on (0,pi). Inverse differentiation and 1+cot^2 y=csc^2 y therefore give -1/(1+x^2) for all real x. This is standard one-variable calculus with the explicitly chosen branch.
