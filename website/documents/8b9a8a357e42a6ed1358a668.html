Simpler proof assessment

Likely alternative prerequisite level: Undergraduate level; lowest recorded proof level: Beyond undergraduate level. Milnor's analytic sphere argument uses derivatives, determinants and volume rather than Poincare-Hopf. For a continuous nonvanishing field, compactness gives a positive lower norm bound; uniformly approximate it by smooth ambient fields and project tangentially, keeping the error below that bound. Normalization gives a smooth nonvanishing field, contradicting the analytic result. This covers every n>=1 in the full continuous statement using undergraduate approximation and multivariable calculus.

Statement

For n\ge1, every continuous tangent vector field on S^{2n} has a zero. Here S^{2n}=\{x\in\mathbb R^{2n+1}:\|x\|=1\} and tangency means v(x)\cdot x=0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves continuous maps in finite-dimensional Euclidean space; unit spheres; dot products. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Compare the empty sum with the sphere Euler characteristic

Suppose a continuous tangent field on S^{2n} has no zero. Poincaré–Hopf gives \chi(S^{2n})=0, because its index sum is empty. But the sphere has Betti numbers 1 in degrees 0 and 2n and 0 otherwise, so \chi(S^{2n})=1+1=2. This contradiction proves the claim.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Use Poincare–Hopf to equate an empty index sum with Euler characteristic, then compute that characteristic from sphere Betti numbers. Indices of tangent fields and homology require graduate topology despite the final numerical contradiction.
