Statement

Let p\ge1 be an integer, V a positive-definite symmetric real p\times p matrix, and n>p-1. Let X have the Wishart density on positive-definite symmetric matrices, with density \exp(\operatorname{tr}(\eta_1X)+\eta_2\log|X|-A) relative to Lebesgue measure on independent symmetric-matrix coordinates, whose natural statistic is (X,\log|X|) and parameters are \eta_1=-V^{-1}/2, \eta_2=(n-p-1)/2. Here |X| denotes determinant, matrix products in the exponential pairing are traces, and A(\eta_1,n)=-(n/2)\log|-\eta_1|+\log\Gamma_p(n/2), with \Gamma_p(a)=\pi^{p(p-1)/4}\prod_{j=1}^p\Gamma(a+(1-j)/2). Then \mathbb EX=nV.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The Wishart law is supplied as an explicit density on finite symmetric-matrix coordinates; its Gamma normalization is a finite product. These linked density/special-function definitions extend undergraduate multivariable integration and linear algebra.

Proof 1: Differentiate the matrix natural parameter

Use the exponential-family mean identity \operatorname E[T_j]=\partial A/\partial\eta_j, with \mathbf X=X and \mathbf V=V, holding n fixed. The matrix gradient is taken with respect to the trace pairing.

To differentiate with respect to η1, we need the following matrix calculus identity:

{\displaystyle {\frac {\partial \log |a\mathbf {X} |}{\partial \mathbf {X} }}=(\mathbf {X} ^{-1})^{\mathsf {T}}}

Then:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\operatorname {E} [\mathbf {X} ]&={\frac {\partial }{\partial {\boldsymbol {\eta }}_{1}}}A\left({\boldsymbol {\eta }}_{1},\ldots \right)\\[1ex]&={\frac {\partial }{\partial {\boldsymbol {\eta }}_{1}}}\left[-{\frac {n}{2}}\log \left|-{\boldsymbol {\eta }}_{1}\right|+\log \Gamma _{p}{\left({\frac {n}{2}}\right)}\right]\\[1ex]&=-{\frac {n}{2}}({\boldsymbol {\eta }}_{1}^{-1})^{\mathsf {T}}\\[1ex]&={\frac {n}{2}}(-{\boldsymbol {\eta }}_{1}^{-1})^{\mathsf {T}}\\[1ex]&=n(\mathbf {V} )^{\mathsf {T}}\\[1ex]&=n\mathbf {V} \end{aligned}}}

The last line uses the fact that V is symmetric, and therefore it is the same when transposed.

Level justification

The full density includes Gamma_p even though it is constant in this derivative; its finite Gamma product and ordinary density meaning must be understood. These are limited additions to matrix calculus. Differentiate log determinant under the trace pairing, substitute eta1=-V inverse/2 and use symmetry.
