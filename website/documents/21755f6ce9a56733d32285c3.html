Statement

For every positive integer k there are at least k distinct primitive Pythagorean triples with one common leg.

Proof 1: Source calculation — outline

By Euclid’s parameterization, the primitive triples are uniquely given, with odd leg first, by (m^2-n^2,2mn,m^2+n^2), where m>n>0 are coprime and exactly one is even.

The length of the even leg is 2mn, and it suffices to choose a with many factorizations, for example a=4b, where b is a product of k different odd primes; this produces at least 2^k different primitive triples.

The source leaves the factorization count implicit.

Level justification
