Statement

Let M\subseteq B(H) be a unital von Neumann algebra on a complex Hilbert space H, and let \Omega be a unit vector such that M\Omega and M^{\prime}\Omega are dense in H and (ab\Omega,\Omega)=(ba\Omega,\Omega) for all a,b\in M. Here M^{\prime} is the commutant and * denotes operator adjoint. For the conjugate-linear isometry determined by J(a\Omega)=a^*\Omega, one has JMJ=M^{\prime}.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

This is an equality of von Neumann commutants for a trace-vector representation.

Proof 1: Symmetry of the self-adjoint real form — outline

The vector \Omega is called a cyclic-separating trace vector. It is called a trace vector because the condition (ab\Omega,\Omega)=(ba\Omega,\Omega) means that the matrix coefficient corresponding to \Omega defines a tracial state on M. It is called cyclic since \Omega generates H as a topological M-module. It is called separating because if a\Omega=0 for a in M, then aM'\Omega=(0), and hence a=0.

It follows that the map

Ja\Omega=a^*\Omega

for a in M defines a conjugate-linear isometry of H with square the identity, J^2=I. The operator J is usually called the modular conjugation operator.

It is immediately verified that JMJ and M commute on the subspace M\Omega, so that

JMJ\subseteq M'.

One of the easiest ways to see the opposite inclusion is to introduce K, the closure of the real subspace M_{\rm sa}\Omega, where M_{\rm sa} denotes the self-adjoint elements in M. It follows that

H=K\oplus iK,

an orthogonal direct sum for the real part of the inner product. This is just the real orthogonal decomposition for the \pm1 eigenspaces of J. On the other hand for a in M_{\rm sa} and b in M'_{\rm sa}, the inner product (ab\Omega,\Omega) is real, because ab is self-adjoint. Hence K is unaltered if M is replaced by M'.

In particular \Omega is a trace vector for M' and J is unaltered if M is replaced by M'. So the opposite inclusion

JM'J\subseteq M

follows by reversing the roles of M and M'. Since J^2=I, this gives M'\subseteq JMJ.
