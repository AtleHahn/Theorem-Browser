Statement

Define polynomials B_0(x)=1, B_j^{\prime}(x)=jB_{j-1}(x) and \int_0^1B_j(x)\,dx=0 for j\ge1. Write b_j=B_j(1), so b_1=1/2, and P_j(x)=B_j(x-\lfloor x\rfloor). Let f be a real or complex polynomial, let p\ge1 exceed its degree, and let 0\le m<n be integers. Then \sum_{i=m+1}^nf(i)=\int_m^nf+\sum_{j=1}^p b_j(f^{(j-1)}(n)-f^{(j-1)}(m))/j!.

Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Polynomials; Higher derivatives and integration; Bernoulli polynomial definition. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. Proof 1: Make the remainder derivative vanish — outline

For a polynomial and sufficiently large p, the derivative in the Euler–Maclaurin remainder vanishes, so the remainder term is zero.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the explicit remainder formula and note the derivative above the polynomial degree vanishes identically. Undergraduate calculus and the locally defined Bernoulli polynomials suffice.
