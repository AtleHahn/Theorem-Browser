Simpler proof assessment

Likely alternative prerequisite level: Undergraduate level; lowest recorded proof level: Beyond undergraduate level. Use the weak Nullstellensatz by a residue-field argument, then apply it to the ideal generated by J and 1-yf in K[X,y]. If f vanishes on V(J), this ideal has no zero and hence contains 1. Substitute y=1/f in that finite polynomial identity and clear denominators to obtain f^N in J. The reverse inclusion is immediate by evaluation. This gives the full strong theorem with elementary ring algebra and the weak theorem, avoiding the prime-intersection/localization route in the stored proof.

Statement

Let K be algebraically closed and A=K[X_1,\ldots,X_n]. For an ideal J\subseteq A, let V(J)=\{x\in K^n:g(x)=0\text{ for every }g\in J\} and I(V)=\{f\in A:f(x)=0\text{ for every }x\in V\}. Then I(V(J))=\sqrt J, where \sqrt J=\{f:f^r\in J\text{ for some }r\ge1\}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory ring theory supplies ideals and polynomial zeros. The zero-set, vanishing-ideal and radical operations are all explicitly defined, and algebraically closed means every nonconstant one-variable polynomial has a root. No scheme or projective geometry is needed to understand the equality.

Proof 1: Localize away from a polynomial and take a residue field — outline

Let {\displaystyle A=K[X_{1},\ldots ,X_{n}]} for algebraically closed field K, and let J be an ideal of A and {\displaystyle V=\mathrm {V} (J)} be the common zeros of J in {\displaystyle K^{n}}. Recall that {\displaystyle \mathrm {I} (V)} is radical, and therefore {\displaystyle {\sqrt {J}}\subseteq \mathrm {I} (V)}, where {\displaystyle \mathrm {I} (V)} is the ideal of polynomials in A vanishing on V. To show the opposite inclusion, let {\displaystyle f\not \in {\sqrt {J}}}. Then {\displaystyle f\not \in {\mathfrak {p}}} for some prime ideal {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {p}}\supseteq J} in A. Let {\displaystyle R=(A/{\mathfrak {p}})[1/{\bar {f}}]}, where {\displaystyle {\bar {f}}}is the image of f under the natural map {\displaystyle A\to A/{\mathfrak {p}}}, and {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {m}}} be a maximal ideal in R. By Zariski's lemma, {\displaystyle R/{\mathfrak {m}}} is a finite extension of K, and thus, is K since K is algebraically closed. Let {\displaystyle x_{i}} be the images of {\displaystyle X_{i}} under the natural map {\displaystyle A\to A/{\mathfrak {p}}\to R\to R/{\mathfrak {m}}\cong K}. It follows that, by construction, {\displaystyle x=(x_{1},\ldots ,x_{n})\in V} but {\displaystyle f(x)\neq 0}, so {\displaystyle f\notin \mathrm {I} (V)}.

Level justification

Choose a prime avoiding f, localize its quotient, and apply Zariski to a residue field to produce a nonvanishing point. Prime spectra, localization and finite algebra extensions together exceed the small-extension allowance.
