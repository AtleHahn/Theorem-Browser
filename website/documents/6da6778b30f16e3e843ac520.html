Statement

Let E be Banach, F normed over the same real or complex field, and f:E\to F continuous and linear with nonmeager image. Then f is open and surjective, and F is complete.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Completeness and category of operator images are functional-analytic notions beyond the baseline.

Proof 1: Near openness, summable corrections and the quotient — outline

Near openness

Let U be a neighborhood of zero in E.

Shrinking {\displaystyle U}, we can assume {\displaystyle U} is an open ball centered at zero. We have {\displaystyle f(E)=f\left(\bigcup _{n\in \mathbb {N} }nU\right)=\bigcup _{n\in \mathbb {N} }f(nU)}. Thus, some {\displaystyle {\overline {f(nU)}}} contains an interior point {\displaystyle y}; that is, for some radius {\displaystyle r>0},

{\displaystyle B(y,r)\subset {\overline {f(nU)}}.}

Then for any {\displaystyle v} in {\displaystyle F} with {\displaystyle \|v\|<r}, by linearity, convexity and {\displaystyle (-1)U\subset U},

{\displaystyle v=v-y+y\in {\overline {f(-nU)}}+{\overline {f(nU)}}\subset {\overline {f(2nU)}}},

which proves near openness by dividing by {\displaystyle 2n}.{\displaystyle \square }

Removing the closure

Suppose B_F(0,\delta)\subset\overline{f(B_E(0,1))} for some \delta>0, as provided by near openness. Here B(x,r) denotes the open ball of radius r about x.

Let {\displaystyle y} be in {\displaystyle B(0,\delta )} and {\displaystyle c_{n}>0} some sequence. If y=0, take x=0; assume y\ne0. We have: {\displaystyle {\overline {B(0,\delta )}}\subset {\overline {f(B(0,1))}}}. Thus, for each {\displaystyle \epsilon >0} and {\displaystyle z} nonzero in {\displaystyle F}, we can find an {\displaystyle x} with {\displaystyle \|x\|<\delta ^{-1}\|z\|} and {\displaystyle z} in {\displaystyle B(f(x),\epsilon )}. Thus, taking {\displaystyle z=y}, we find an {\displaystyle x_{1}} such that

{\displaystyle \|y-f(x_{1})\|<c_{1},\,\|x_{1}\|<\delta ^{-1}\|y\|.}

Applying the same argument with {\displaystyle z=y-f(x_{1})}, we then find an {\displaystyle x_{2}} such that

{\displaystyle \|y-f(x_{1})-f(x_{2})\|<c_{2},\,\|x_{2}\|<\delta ^{-1}c_{1}}

where we observed {\displaystyle \|x_{2}\|<\delta ^{-1}\|z\|<\delta ^{-1}c_{1}}. Then so on. If a residual is zero, stop and take all remaining terms zero. Thus, if {\displaystyle c:=\sum c_{n}<\infty }, we found a sequence {\displaystyle x_{n}} such that {\displaystyle x=\sum _{1}^{\infty }x_{n}} converges and {\displaystyle f(x)=y}. Also,

{\displaystyle \|x\|\leq \sum _{1}^{\infty }\|x_{n}\|\leq \delta ^{-1}\|y\|+\delta ^{-1}c.}

Since {\displaystyle \delta ^{-1}\|y\|<1}, by making {\displaystyle c} small enough, we can achieve {\displaystyle \|x\|<1}. {\displaystyle \square }

Scaling and translating show that f is open, and scaling the included zero neighborhood makes f surjective. Furthermore, there is a canonical factorization

E\longrightarrow E/\ker f\longrightarrow F.

The quotient E/\ker f is Banach, since \ker f is closed, and the induced map to F is an isomorphism of topological vector spaces. Hence F is complete.

Level justification

The argument combines category in normed spaces, summable corrections, quotient norms, Banach quotients and the passage from a topological linear isomorphism to equivalence of norms. Each step can be related to undergraduate analysis, but the cumulative preparation for all these functional-analytic constructions is not confidently a small extension. Equivalent norms preserve completeness
Kind:

Technique

Treatment:

Implicitly assumed

Statement used:

If two norms satisfy a||v||_1<=||v||_2<=b||v||_1 for positive constants a,b, completeness for one implies completeness for the other. A linear homeomorphism between normed spaces yields such inequalities via boundedness of it and its inverse.

Application in proof:

Transfer completeness from E/ker f to F through the induced linear homeomorphism.

Application level:

Undergraduate level

Level justification:

The transfer follows directly by comparing Cauchy sequences under two fixed inequalities; boundedness versus continuity for linear maps is a small analysis extension.

Review notes:

A mere homeomorphism of general metric spaces does not preserve completeness. Linearity and the resulting norm bounds are essential here.
