Statement

Let L(q,v,t) and a curve q(t)\in\mathbb R^n be sufficiently smooth for the derivatives below. Along the curve put p_i=L_{v_i}(q,\dot q,t) and assume \dot p_i=L_{q_i}(q,\dot q,t). Let \varphi(q,\varepsilon) be a smooth flow with \varphi(q,0)=q, and let T\in\mathbb R be constant. Suppose the transformation t^\prime=t+\varepsilon T, q^\prime(t^\prime)=\varphi(q(t),\varepsilon) leaves the action \int_a^bL(q,\dot q,t)\,dt invariant on every subinterval, with the endpoints transformed too. Set Q(q)=\partial_\varepsilon\varphi(q,0). Then (p\cdot\dot q-L)T-p\cdot Q is constant.

Proof 1: Differentiation of the transformed action

Under the flow, the velocity transforms as

\dot q(t)\longmapsto\dot q'(t') =D_q\varphi(q(t'-\varepsilon T),\varepsilon)\dot q(t'-\varepsilon T).

The action integral flows to

\begin{aligned} I(\varepsilon) &=\int_{a+\varepsilon T}^{b+\varepsilon T}L(q'(t'),\dot q'(t'),t')\,dt'\\ &=\int_{a+\varepsilon T}^{b+\varepsilon T} L\bigl(\varphi(q(t'-\varepsilon T),\varepsilon), D_q\varphi(q(t'-\varepsilon T),\varepsilon)\dot q(t'-\varepsilon T),t'\bigr)\,dt', \end{aligned}

which may be regarded as a function of \varepsilon. In the calculations below, all derivatives of \varphi are evaluated at (q(t),0) and those of L at (q(t),\dot q(t),t). The notation D_q^2\varphi[u,v] denotes the second derivative applied to the two vectors u,v.

Calculating the derivative at \varepsilon=0 and using Leibniz's rule, we get

\begin{aligned} 0=I'(0)={}&L(q(b),\dot q(b),b)T-L(q(a),\dot q(a),a)T\\ &+\int_a^b\bigl[ L_q\cdot(-D_q\varphi\,\dot qT+\partial_\varepsilon\varphi)\\ &\hspace{42mm} +p\cdot(-D_q^2\varphi[\dot q,\dot q]T +D_q\partial_\varepsilon\varphi\,\dot q -D_q\varphi\,\ddot qT)\bigr]\,dt. \end{aligned}

Notice that the Euler–Lagrange equations imply

\begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dt}(p\cdot D_q\varphi\,\dot qT) &=\dot p\cdot D_q\varphi\,\dot qT +p\cdot\left(\frac{d}{dt}D_q\varphi\right)\dot qT +p\cdot D_q\varphi\,\ddot qT\\ &=L_q\cdot D_q\varphi\,\dot qT +p\cdot D_q^2\varphi[\dot q,\dot q]T +p\cdot D_q\varphi\,\ddot qT. \end{aligned}

Substituting this into the previous equation, one gets

\begin{aligned} 0=I'(0)={}&L(q(b),\dot q(b),b)T-L(q(a),\dot q(a),a)T\\ &-(p\cdot D_q\varphi\,\dot q)(b)T +(p\cdot D_q\varphi\,\dot q)(a)T\\ &+\int_a^b\left[ L_q\cdot\partial_\varepsilon\varphi +p\cdot D_q\partial_\varepsilon\varphi\,\dot q\right]\,dt. \end{aligned}

Again using the Euler–Lagrange equations we get

\begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dt}(p\cdot\partial_\varepsilon\varphi) &=\dot p\cdot\partial_\varepsilon\varphi +p\cdot D_q\partial_\varepsilon\varphi\,\dot q\\ &=L_q\cdot\partial_\varepsilon\varphi +p\cdot D_q\partial_\varepsilon\varphi\,\dot q. \end{aligned}

Substituting this into the previous equation, one gets

\begin{aligned} 0={}&L(q(b),\dot q(b),b)T-L(q(a),\dot q(a),a)T\\ &-(p\cdot D_q\varphi\,\dot q)(b)T +(p\cdot D_q\varphi\,\dot q)(a)T\\ &+(p\cdot\partial_\varepsilon\varphi)(b) -(p\cdot\partial_\varepsilon\varphi)(a). \end{aligned}

Since the endpoints are arbitrary, one can see that

(p\cdot D_q\varphi\,\dot q-L)T-p\cdot\partial_\varepsilon\varphi

is a constant of the motion, i.e., it is a conserved quantity. Since \varphi(q,0)=q, we get D_q\varphi(q,0)=I, and so the conserved quantity simplifies to

(p\cdot\dot q-L)T-p\cdot Q.

This derivation assumes that the coordinate flow does not change as time passes.
