Statement

A finite planar graph with n vertices has a branch decomposition of width O(\sqrt n). A branch decomposition uses a tree of maximum degree three whose leaves bijectively represent graph edges (the empty and single-edge cases have width zero); deleting a decomposition-tree edge partitions those leaves into two sets, and its width counts graph vertices incident with edges on both sides. The decomposition width is the maximum of these counts.

Proof 1: Square-root bound for planar branchwidth (outline)

Use the planar separator theorem: an m-vertex planar graph can be separated by O(\sqrt m) vertices into two sets, each of size at most 2m/3, with no edges between them.

Form a hierarchy based on separations, in which the two children of the root node are roots of recursively constructed hierarchies for the two edge-disjoint subgraphs G_1 and G_2 of a separation of the given graph. Continue until individual edges form the leaves. The overall structure forms a branch decomposition.

The width of any separation in this decomposition is bounded by the sum of the sizes of the separators on a path from any node to the root of the hierarchy. The sizes of the graphs go down by a constant factor at each level of the tree: for sufficiently large parts, retaining the O(\sqrt m) shared separator vertices in both children still leaves a constant shrink factor. The upper bounds on the sizes of the separators also go down by a constant factor at each level, so their sizes on these paths add in a geometric series to O(\sqrt n). Thus any branch decomposition formed in this way has width O(\sqrt n), and any planar graph has branchwidth O(\sqrt n).
