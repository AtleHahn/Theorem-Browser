Statement

Let \mathcal C be a family of real random variables on a probability space, and suppose \lim_{K\to\infty}\sup_{X\in\mathcal C}\mathbb E(|X|1_{\{|X|\ge K\}})=0. Then \sup_{X\in\mathcal C}\mathbb E|X|<\infty.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The uniform-tail condition is fully displayed; nonnegative expectation adds an integral-average interpretation to undergraduate suprema and limits.

Proof 1: A common cutoff splits the expectation

For X in the uniformly integrable family \mathcal C, by splitting

\operatorname E(|X|)=\operatorname E(|X|1_{\{|X|\ge K\}})+\operatorname E(|X|1_{\{|X|<K\}})

and bounding each of the two, it can be seen that a uniformly integrable family is bounded in L^1. The cutoff K is chosen uniformly over \mathcal C, as required by the uniform tail condition.

The source leaves the two bounds implicit.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Choose one cutoff making all tail expectations at most one and bound each remaining expectation by the cutoff. Only splitting and monotonicity of nonnegative expectation are needed; elementary probability plus the explicit uniform-tail definition suffice.
