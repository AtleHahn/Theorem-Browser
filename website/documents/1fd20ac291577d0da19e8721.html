Statement

Let X be a compact Kähler manifold and let \alpha\in\Omega^{p,q}(X) be d-closed, with integers p,q\ge1. If \alpha is orthogonal to the \bar\partial-harmonic (p,q)-forms, then \alpha=\bar\partial\eta for some \eta\in\Omega^{p,q-1}(X).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Compact Kähler manifolds; Complex differential forms; Dolbeault operators; Harmonic orthogonality. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Vanishing norm of the adjoint component

Use the orthogonal decomposition \Omega^{p,q}=\mathcal H_{\bar\partial}^{p,q}\oplus\operatorname{im}\bar\partial\oplus\operatorname{im}\bar\partial^*, where the adjoint is for the metric inner product. Also d=\partial+\bar\partial.

Suppose that {\displaystyle \alpha } is orthogonal to the subspace {\displaystyle {\mathcal {H}}_{\bar {\partial }}^{p,q}\subset \Omega ^{p,q}(X)}. Then {\displaystyle \alpha \in \operatorname {im} {\bar {\partial }}\oplus \operatorname {im} {\bar {\partial }}^{*}}. Since {\displaystyle \alpha } is {\displaystyle d}-closed and {\displaystyle d=\partial +{\bar {\partial }}}, it is also {\displaystyle {\bar {\partial }}}-closed (that is {\displaystyle {\bar {\partial }}\alpha =0}). If {\displaystyle \alpha =\alpha '+\alpha ''} where {\displaystyle \alpha '\in \operatorname {im} {\bar {\partial }}} and {\displaystyle \alpha ''={\bar {\partial }}^{*}\gamma } is contained in {\displaystyle \operatorname {im} {\bar {\partial }}^{*}} then since this sum is from an orthogonal decomposition with respect to the inner product {\displaystyle \langle -,-\rangle } induced by the Riemannian metric,

{\displaystyle \langle \alpha '',\alpha ''\rangle =\langle \alpha ,\alpha ''\rangle =\langle \alpha ,{\bar {\partial }}^{*}\gamma \rangle =\langle {\bar {\partial }}\alpha ,\gamma \rangle =0}

or in other words {\displaystyle \|\alpha ''\|^{2}=0} and {\displaystyle \alpha ''=0}. Thus it is the case that {\displaystyle \alpha =\alpha '\in \operatorname {im} {\bar {\partial }}}. This allows us to write {\displaystyle \alpha ={\bar {\partial }}\eta } for some differential form {\displaystyle \eta \in \Omega ^{p,q-1}(X)}.
