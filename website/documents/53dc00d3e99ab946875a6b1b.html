Statement

In a complex inner-product space, \langle x,y\rangle is real if and only if \|x+iy\|=\|x-iy\|, and is purely imaginary if and only if \|x+y\|=\|x-y\|. Zero is allowed in both descriptions.

Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Inner products; Norms; Linear algebra. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Set the real or imaginary polarization expression to zero

Polarization gives the real part as \tfrac14(\|x+y\|^2-\|x-y\|^2) and the imaginary part, up to the sign determined by the linearity convention, as \tfrac14(\|x+iy\|^2-\|x-iy\|^2). The inner product is real exactly when the latter vanishes, and purely imaginary exactly when the former vanishes. Norms are nonnegative, so equality of their squares is equality of the norms.
