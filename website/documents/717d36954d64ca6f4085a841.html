Statement

Let X be a real vector space, M\subseteq X a vector subspace, f:M\to\mathbb R linear, and p:X\to\mathbb R sublinear: p(u+v)\le p(u)+p(v) and p(tu)=tp(u) for t\ge0. Assume f\le p on M. If the quotient vector space X/M has a finite or countable basis, then f has a linear extension F:X\to\mathbb R dominated by p.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra supplies subspaces and linear functionals; analysis supplies norm bounds and countability. Sublinear, convex or seminorm conditions are explicitly given inequalities, and the functional norm is a unit-ball supremum. These direct extensions suffice without locally convex or dual-topology theory.

Proof 1: Dominated extension in countable codimension — outline

Apply the one-dimensional dominated extension lemma successively along a finite or countable list of basis directions for X/M. Induction gives compatible dominated extensions. Their union is an extension to X.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Choose representatives of the stated countable quotient basis, extend successively along each and take the union of compatible maps. Undergraduate vector spaces and elementary countable induction suffice; sublinear domination is defined explicitly.
