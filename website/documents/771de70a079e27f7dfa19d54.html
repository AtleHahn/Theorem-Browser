Statement

Let P be a projective space of dimension at least two defined over a division ring (multiplication need not be commutative), or an abstract projective space of dimension at least three. Let ABC and abc be two triangles, each consisting of three noncollinear points. In a configuration with distinct corresponding vertices and distinct corresponding side lines, the following are equivalent:

  1. The joining lines Aa, Bb and Cc are concurrent (the triangles are centrally perspective).
  2. The corresponding-side intersections AB\cap ab, AC\cap ac and BC\cap bc exist and are collinear (the triangles are axially perspective).

Here AB denotes the projective line through A and B; concurrency means that the lines have a common point, and collinearity that the points lie on a common line. A division ring is a ring in which every nonzero element has a multiplicative inverse, without an assumption that multiplication commutes.

Proof 1: Division-ring coordinates and projective duality (external)

The following editorial argument completes the stated scope. A supporting source is Foundations of Projective Geometry (Theorem 2.1, printed pp.8–9; division-ring model in Section 6.).

Use standard coordinatization for abstract projective spaces of dimension at least three. If the common center O is none of the six vertices, choose representatives over the division ring so a=A+O, b=B+O, c=C+O. The corresponding-side intersections have representatives A-B, A-C, B-C, which span at most a two-dimensional right subspace. If O=A (or by symmetry another vertex), b lies on AB and c on AC; distinct corresponding sides make the first two intersections b,c, while the third lies on bc. This remains valid when a noncorresponding vertex equals O. For coplanar triangles the converse is the forward theorem in the dual plane (over the opposite division ring); the hypotheses translate exactly, including coincident intersection points. For distinct triangle planes, collinear side intersections give their common line: at least two of the three intersections are distinct, since otherwise the three sides of triangle ABC would be concurrent. The planes then span a projective 3-space. The three planes spanned respectively by AB and ab, AC and ac, BC and bc are pairwise distinct, with pairwise intersections Aa,Bb,Cc; their common intersection is nonempty by dimension. This proves concurrency. Explain coordinatization and duality as standard dependencies.
