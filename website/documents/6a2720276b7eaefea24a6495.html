A fractional Sobolev interpolation inequality

Statement

Let n\ge1 and let \Omega\subset\mathbb R^n be the whole space, a half-space or a bounded Lipschitz domain. For every real u\in W^{1/2,2}(\Omega)\cap W^{2/3,4}(\Omega),

\|u\|_{W^{7/12,8/3}(\Omega)}\le C\|u\|_{W^{1/2,2}(\Omega)}^{1/2}\|u\|_{W^{2/3,4}(\Omega)}^{1/2}.

For the orders and finite exponents used here, W^{s,p} consists of functions with finite norm

\|u\|_{W^{s,p}}=\left(\|u\|_p^p+\int_\Omega\int_\Omega\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^p}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dx\,dy\right)^{1/p}.

The constant depends on the domain and fixed parameters, not on u.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The explicitly displayed norm still requires Lebesgue integration and fractional Sobolev function spaces.

Proof 1: Substitute two fractional orders and Lebesgue exponents

Use the external Brezis–Mironescu interpolation theorem: for these domains, 0<s_1\le s_2<1, 1<p_1,p_2<\infty and 0<\theta<1, it gives

\|u\|_{W^{s,p}}\le C\|u\|_{W^{s_1,p_1}}^\theta\|u\|_{W^{s_2,p_2}}^{1-\theta}

when s=\theta s_1+(1-\theta)s_2 and 1/p=\theta/p_1+(1-\theta)/p_2, for u in both spaces. Set p_1=2,p_2=4,s_1=1/2,s_2=2/3,\theta=1/2. This gives s=7/12 and p=8/3. The general theorem's exceptional conjunction requires p_2=1, which does not occur here.
