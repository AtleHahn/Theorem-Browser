Statement

If f,g:X\to[0,\infty] are measurable on a measure space and f\le g, then \int_Xf\,d\mu\le\int_Xg\,d\mu.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement concerns measurable functions and measure integrals, beyond the calculus/linear-algebra baseline.

Proof 1: Include the sets of simple minorants

Denote by \operatorname{SF}(h) the set of nonnegative finite-valued measurable simple functions s such that 0\leq s\leq h on X. Since f\leq g, we have

\operatorname{SF}(f)\subseteq\operatorname{SF}(g).

By definition of Lebesgue integral and the properties of supremum,

\int_X f\,d\mu =\sup_{s\in\operatorname{SF}(f)}\int_X s\,d\mu \leq\sup_{s\in\operatorname{SF}(g)}\int_X s\,d\mu =\int_X g\,d\mu.
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Include the sets of measurable simple minorants and compare their integral suprema. The order argument is elementary, but the underlying general Lebesgue integral construction requires measure-theoretic background.
