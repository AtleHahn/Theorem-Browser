Statement

Let A,B,C,D be four distinct points, in that cyclic order, on a circle in the Euclidean plane. Let M_1,M_2,M_3,M_4 be the incenters of triangles ABD,ABC,BCD,ACD, respectively. Then M_1M_2M_3M_4 is a rectangle. The incenter of a triangle is the center of its inscribed circle, equivalently the common intersection of its internal angle bisectors.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement uses a circle, four ordered points and triangle incircles. Incenter is defined by elementary angle bisectors; the conclusion is a rectangle. No prerequisite beyond high-school Euclidean geometry is needed.

Proof 1: Auxiliary circles through adjacent incenters (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Proof, equations(1)–(3)).

Here I_{ABD},I_{ABC},I_{BCD},I_{ACD} are M_1,M_2,M_3,M_4. One can avoid assumptions about the drawing by taking directed angles modulo 180^\circ. Orient A,B,C,D counterclockwise. The two auxiliary circles give

\angle(M_2B,M_2M_1)=\tfrac12\angle BAD,\qquad \angle(M_2M_3,M_2B)=\tfrac12\angle BCD.

Their sum shows that the lines M_2M_1,M_2M_3 are perpendicular, because \angle BAD+\angle BCD=180^\circ. Cyclic relabeling gives the other right angles. Adjacent incenters are distinct: equality would force the internal bisectors of two distinct rays from a shared vertex to coincide. Thus these are genuine sides, and their successive perpendicularities give the stated rectangle.
