Statement

In intuitionistic propositional logic, the inference schema taking P\to Q to \neg P\lor Q, for arbitrary formulas P,Q, is not derivable.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Unlike a locally specified symbolic calculus, this statement names an intuitionistic deductive system without giving its rules. Understanding derivability and its constructive implication/disjunction rules is a limited formal-logic extension of undergraduate proof reasoning.

Proof 1: Substitute identical antecedent and consequent

Given {\displaystyle P\to Q}, one can constructively transform a proof of {\displaystyle P} into a proof of {\displaystyle Q}. In particular, {\displaystyle P\to P} holds in intuitionistic logic. If {\displaystyle P\to Q\Rightarrow \neg P\lor Q} would hold, then {\displaystyle \neg P\lor P} could be derived. However, the latter is the law of excluded middle, which is not accepted by intuitionistic logic (one cannot assume {\displaystyle \neg P\lor P} without knowing which case applies).

The nonderivability of the excluded-middle schema in intuitionistic logic is used as a prerequisite.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Substitute Q=P and use identity implication to derive the excluded-middle schema if the proposed rule existed. Introductory formal logic suffices; the only extra fact is the explicitly supplied nonderivability of excluded middle, not its semantic proof.
