Statement

Let A be a nonempty set and W\subset A^{\mathbb N}. Players I and II alternately choose a_1,a_2,\ldots\in A, with I choosing the odd-indexed terms. Player I wins exactly when (a_j)_{j\ge1}\in W, and otherwise II wins. A strategy for I (respectively II) assigns a next move to each even-length (respectively odd-length) finite history. A strategy is winning if every infinite play consistent with it is won by that player. The two players cannot both have winning strategies.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A strategy is explicitly a next-move function on finite histories, and winning is quantified over compatible infinite plays. Sets, functions and sequences suffice; no determinacy axiom or descriptive-set theory is needed to understand mutual exclusivity.

Proof 1: Play the two strategies against one another

If both players had winning strategies, play those strategies against one another. The resulting infinite play has exactly one winner, so one of the supposed winning strategies loses that play, a contradiction.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Recursively combine the two strategies into one infinite sequence and apply the mutually exclusive win condition. Arbitrary sets, function strategies and recursion on naturals fit undergraduate set-based reasoning.
