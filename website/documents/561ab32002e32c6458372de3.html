Statement

Let (\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P) carry a filtration (\mathcal F_n)_{n\in I}, where I=\{0,\ldots,N\} or I=\mathbb N_0. For K=\mathbb R or \mathbb C and d\ge1, every adapted process X_n\in K^d with \mathbb E\|X_n\|<\infty has an almost surely unique decomposition X=M+A into an integrable vector martingale and an integrable predictable vector process starting at zero. Vector conditional expectations and martingales are interpreted coordinatewise.

Proof 1: Decompose the finitely many real components — outline

The scalar Doob decomposition theorem states that every adapted integrable real process is almost surely uniquely a sum of a martingale and a predictable process starting at zero.

The theorem is valid word for word also for stochastic processes X taking values in the d-dimensional Euclidean space \mathbb R^d or the complex vector space \mathbb C^d. This follows from the one-dimensional version by considering the components individually (real and imaginary components in the complex case).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply scalar Doob decomposition to real and imaginary coordinates. The coordinate step is elementary, but filtrations, conditional expectations, martingales and predictability require measure-theoretic stochastic processes.
