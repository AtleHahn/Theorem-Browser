Statement

Let n\ge1,L\ge0 be integers, \alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n nonzero algebraic numbers with fixed complex logarithms, and p(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n) integer coefficients not all zero for 0\le\lambda_i\le L. Put \phi(z)=\sum_{\lambda_1=0}^L\cdots\sum_{\lambda_n=0}^L p(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n)\exp((\lambda_1\log\alpha_1+\cdots+\lambda_n\log\alpha_n)z). If \phi^{(k)}(0)=0 for k=0,\ldots,(L+1)^n-1, then \log\alpha_1,\ldots,\log\alpha_n are linearly dependent over \mathbb Q.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Undergraduate algebra supplies algebraic numbers and linear dependence, and calculus supplies exponentials and derivatives. Complex logarithms are choices of logarithms differing by integer multiples of 2pi i; multiplicative dependence is an integer-exponent product equal to one. Those small extensions make the finite equations and conclusion accessible.

Proof 1: Vandermonde determinant of logarithmic sums

The vanishing equations for the first (L+1)n derivatives give a homogeneous set of linear equations for the coefficients p, so the determinant is zero, and is a Vandermonde determinant, this time for the numbers λ1 log α1 + ⋯ + λn log αn. So two of these expressions must be the same which shows that log α1,...,log αn are linearly dependent over the rationals.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Differentiate the finite exponential sum to get powers of the fixed complex numbers sum lambda_i log(alpha_i), then use the Vandermonde determinant and subtract the two distinct integer index vectors. Undergraduate linear algebra and differentiation suffice, with the elementary complex-exponential derivative as a small extension. Logarithms are fixed constants, so no branch analysis is involved; algebraicity of the alpha_i is not needed for this local deduction.

Statement used:

For complex numbers z1,...,zm, the determinant of the matrix with entries z_j^k, k=0,...,m-1, is product_(i<j)(z_j-z_i). It vanishes exactly when two bases coincide.

Use a nonzero coefficient vector in the homogeneous system to force equal bases.
