Statement

Let B be standard Brownian motion with B_0=0, and let \mu,\sigma be real constants. If a strictly positive Itô process S satisfies dS_t=\sigma S_t\,dB_t+\mu S_t\,dt, then S_t=S_0\exp\left(\sigma B_t+(\mu-\sigma^2/2)t\right).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding an Ito process and its stochastic differential equation requires stochastic integration, beyond a short introduction to Brownian motion. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Logarithmic Itô transform and integration

Applying Itô's lemma with f(S_t)=\log(S_t), which is defined since S_t>0, gives

\begin{aligned} df&=f'(S_t)\,dS_t+\frac12 f''(S_t)(dS_t)^2\\ &=\frac1{S_t}\,dS_t+\frac12(-S_t^{-2})(S_t^2\sigma^2\,dt)\\ &=\frac1{S_t}(\sigma S_t\,dB_t+\mu S_t\,dt)-\frac{\sigma^2}{2}\,dt\\ &=\sigma\,dB_t+\left(\mu-\frac{\sigma^2}{2}\right)dt. \end{aligned}

It follows that

\log(S_t)=\log(S_0)+\sigma B_t+\left(\mu-\frac{\sigma^2}{2}\right)t,

and exponentiating gives the expression for S,

S_t=S_0\exp\left(\sigma B_t+\left(\mu-\frac{\sigma^2}{2}\right)t\right).

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply Ito’s formula to the logarithm and integrate the resulting stochastic differential. Brownian stochastic integration and quadratic variation are essential beyond-undergraduate prerequisites.
