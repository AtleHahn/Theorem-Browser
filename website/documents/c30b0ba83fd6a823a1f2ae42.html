Statement

Let \Omega\subset\mathbb R^n be connected and open, and let \Sigma\subset\Omega be a real-analytic hypersurface separating it into two nonempty connected open sets:

\Omega=\Omega_-\sqcup\Sigma\sqcup\Omega_+.

Let

P=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}A_\alpha(x)\partial^\alpha

be a linear differential operator of order m with real-analytic coefficients. Here \alpha\in\mathbb N_0^n, |\alpha|=\sum_j\alpha_j, and \partial^\alpha=\partial_{x_1}^{\alpha_1}\cdots\partial_{x_n}^{\alpha_n}.

Suppose \Sigma is noncharacteristic: for every x\in\Sigma and every nonzero covector \xi annihilating the tangent space T_x\Sigma,

\sum_{|\alpha|=m}A_\alpha(x)\xi^\alpha\ne0,

where \xi^\alpha=\prod_j\xi_j^{\alpha_j}.

If a distribution u\in\mathcal D'(\Omega) satisfies Pu=0 in \Omega and vanishes on \Omega_-, then it vanishes in an open neighborhood of \Sigma in \Omega. A distribution is a continuous linear functional on the space of smooth compactly supported test functions; vanishing on an open set means it gives zero on all test functions supported there.
