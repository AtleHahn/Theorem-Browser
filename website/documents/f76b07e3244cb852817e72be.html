Statement

For n\ge1, put \varphi(n)=\#\{1\le r\le n:\gcd(r,n)=1\}. For an integer a coprime to n and integers x,y, if x\equiv y\pmod{\varphi(n)}, then a^x\equiv a^y\pmod n. Negative powers mean powers of the inverse residue, with the trivial residue convention for n=1.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The stated objects and conditions use elementary integer arithmetic.

Proof 1: Reduce integer exponents modulo the totient

This follows from Euler's theorem, a^{\varphi(n)}\equiv1\pmod n, since, if x\equiv y\pmod{\varphi(n)}, then x=y+k\varphi(n) for some integer k, and one has

a^x=a^{y+\varphi(n)k} =a^y(a^{\varphi(n)})^k \equiv a^y1^k\equiv a^y\pmod n.

Integer-power laws are interpreted for the invertible residue of a.

Level justification

Write the exponent difference as a multiple of the totient and apply Euler with integer exponent laws for an invertible residue. Elementary modular arithmetic suffices.
