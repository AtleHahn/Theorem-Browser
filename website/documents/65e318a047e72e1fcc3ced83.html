Statement

For each integer n\ge5, neither A_n nor S_n is solvable. A finite group is solvable if it admits a finite series from the whole group to the trivial subgroup, with each subgroup normal in the preceding one and cyclic quotient groups.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory group/ring theory and linear algebra supply the objects. The extra Sylow, normalizer, invariant-polynomial or pseudoreflection condition is explicitly defined in subgroup, coordinate or polynomial terms; no specialist extension or representation theory is required.

Proof 1: Simple nonabelian alternating subgroup

For n ≥ 5, the symmetric group {\displaystyle {\mathcal {S}}_{n}} of degree n has only the alternating group {\displaystyle {\mathcal {A}}_{n}} as a nontrivial proper normal subgroup (see Symmetric group § Normal subgroups). For n ≥ 5, the alternating group {\displaystyle {\mathcal {A}}_{n}} is simple (that is, it does not have any nontrivial proper normal subgroup) and not abelian. This implies that both {\displaystyle {\mathcal {A}}_{n}} and {\displaystyle {\mathcal {S}}_{n}} are not solvable for n ≥ 5.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the stated normal-subgroup structure and nonabelian simplicity to exclude the explicitly defined cyclic-quotient series. Introductory finite group theory suffices.
