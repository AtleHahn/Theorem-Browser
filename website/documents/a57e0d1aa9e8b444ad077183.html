Undecidable problems need not form a single linear hierarchy: two effectively enumerable sets can each remain undecidable even with an oracle for the other. This refines the notion of computational limitation by distinguishing genuinely incomparable kinds of information.

Statement

There exist computably enumerable sets A,B\subseteq\mathbb N that are incomparable under Turing reducibility:

A\not\leq_T B\qquad\text{and}\qquad B\not\leq_T A.

A set is computably enumerable if a Turing machine without an oracle can enumerate its elements. The relation A\leq_T B means that a Turing machine supplied with an oracle answering membership questions about B decides membership in A, halting with the correct answer on every input.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The cumulative distinction between enumeration and decision, formal computation and relative oracle computation is not assumed in the undergraduate mathematics baseline; the complete reduction-theoretic assertion cannot confidently fit the short allowance.

Proof 1: Finite-injury priority with fresh witnesses and restraints (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Slides3–7: Construction and Verification).

Take the stage-s simulation to run for at most s steps and to allow oracle queries only below s. A requirement acts only with zero restraint and a witness outside its current enumerated set; impose restraint s and reset lower priorities. Thus each requirement acts at most once after its last injury. If it never acts after that stage, choose an unused witness above all stabilized higher restraints. A final oracle computation returning zero would eventually appear in the bounded simulations and force action, a contradiction.
