Statement

Let C be a smooth connected projective curve of genus g\ge2 over an algebraically closed field, with canonical line bundle \omega_C. Write h^0(C,L)=\dim_k H^0(C,L) over the base field k. For integers n\ge3, h^0(C,\omega_C^{\otimes n})=(2n-1)(g-1). In particular h^0(C,\omega_C^{\otimes3})=5g-5.

Proof 1: Use duality and negative-degree vanishing

Write h^j(C,L)=\dim_kH^j(C,L) and \chi(L)=h^0(C,L)-h^1(C,L); the superscript \vee denotes the dual line bundle. By Serre duality, the Euler characteristic reads as

\begin{aligned} \chi(\omega_C^{\otimes n}) &=h^0(C,\omega_C^{\otimes n}) -h^0\left(C,\omega_C\otimes(\omega_C^{\otimes n})^\vee\right)\\ &=h^0(C,\omega_C^{\otimes n}) -h^0\left(C,(\omega_C^{\otimes(n-1)})^\vee\right). \end{aligned}

Since \deg(\omega_C^{\otimes n})=n(2g-2),

h^0\left(C,(\omega_C^{\otimes(n-1)})^\vee\right)=0

for n\geq3, since its degree is negative for all g\geq2, implying it has no global sections. Riemann–Roch therefore gives

h^0(C,\omega_C^{\otimes n}) =\chi(\omega_C^{\otimes n}) =n(2g-2)-g+1=(2n-1)(g-1).

In particular, h^0(C,\omega_C^{\otimes3})=6g-6-g+1=5g-5.
