Statement

Let f(t,x) be a smooth complex-valued function near (0,0)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^n, and suppose that, for an integer k\ge1,

\partial_t^j f(0,0)=0\quad(0\le j<k),\qquad \partial_t^k f(0,0)\ne0.

Then, on a neighborhood of the origin, there are smooth complex-valued functions c(t,x) and a_0(x),\ldots,a_{k-1}(x), with c(0,0)\ne0, such that

f(t,x)=c(t,x)\left(t^k+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}a_j(x)t^j\right).

The division form holds as well: for every smooth complex-valued g(t,x) near the origin, there are smooth q(t,x) and r_0(x),\ldots,r_{k-1}(x) such that, near the origin,

g(t,x)=q(t,x)f(t,x)+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}t^j r_j(x).

The neighborhoods may be reduced as necessary. If f is real-valued, the preparation functions can be chosen real-valued; if both f and g are real-valued, so can the division functions. No uniqueness is asserted.

Level justification

The actual analytic formulation requires only multivariable calculus and polynomial algebra. Smooth parameter-dependent coefficients and local neighborhoods are a small extension of standard undergraduate analysis; no germ-module or singularity-theory prerequisites occur in this statement.

Proof 1: Real smooth division and a complex module reduction (external)

A supporting proof source can be found here (Golubitsky–Guillemin, Stable Mappings and Their Singularities, Chapter IV §2 Theorem 2.1 (real smooth division), followed by the finite-module/Nakayama bridge given below.). The additional argument below supplies the stated scope and is not attributed to that source.

The source proves division for a real smooth divisor. The following module argument gives the complex-divisor assertion. Let A be the local ring of complex smooth germs in x and B that in (t,x). The real function |f|^2 is t-regular of order 2k. Applying real division to real and imaginary parts shows that B/(|f|^2) is finite over A, generated by 1,t,\ldots,t^{2k-1}. Its quotient M=B/(f) is therefore finite over A.

By Hadamard's lemma, specialization at x=0 gives M/(x)M\cong\mathbb C[t]/(t^k), since f(t,0)=t^ku(t) with u(0)\ne0. Nakayama's lemma implies that 1,\ldots,t^{k-1} generate M, proving division for any g. Divide t^k by f. At x=0 the remainder is zero and the quotient has value 1/u(0) at the origin. Invert this quotient to obtain preparation. For real functions the source's real division gives real quotients, remainders and preparation functions.
