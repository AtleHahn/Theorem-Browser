Statement

Let f(t,x) be a smooth complex-valued function near (0,0)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^n, and suppose that, for an integer k\ge1,

\partial_t^j f(0,0)=0\quad(0\le j<k),\qquad \partial_t^k f(0,0)\ne0.

Then, on a neighborhood of the origin, there are smooth complex-valued functions c(t,x) and a_0(x),\ldots,a_{k-1}(x), with c(0,0)\ne0, such that

f(t,x)=c(t,x)\left(t^k+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}a_j(x)t^j\right).

The division form holds as well: for every smooth complex-valued g(t,x) near the origin, there are smooth q(t,x) and r_0(x),\ldots,r_{k-1}(x) such that, near the origin,

g(t,x)=q(t,x)f(t,x)+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}t^j r_j(x).

The neighborhoods may be reduced as necessary. If f is real-valued, the preparation functions can be chosen real-valued; if both f and g are real-valued, so can the division functions. No uniqueness is asserted.

Level justification

The actual analytic formulation requires only multivariable calculus and polynomial algebra. Smooth parameter-dependent coefficients and local neighborhoods are a small extension of standard undergraduate analysis; no germ-module or singularity-theory prerequisites occur in this statement.
