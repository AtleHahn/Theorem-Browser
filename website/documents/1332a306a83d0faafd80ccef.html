Statement

Let C be a compact Riemann surface of genus g, let \gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_{2g} generate H_1(C,\mathbb Z), and let \omega_1,\ldots,\omega_g be a basis of holomorphic one-forms. Let \Lambda be the period lattice generated by \Omega_j=(\int_{\gamma_j}\omega_1,\ldots,\int_{\gamma_j}\omega_g). Fix p_0\in C. The map

{\displaystyle {\begin{cases}u:C\to J(C)\\u(p)=\left(\int _{p_{0}}^{p}\omega _{1},\dots ,\int _{p_{0}}^{p}\omega _{g}\right){\bmod {\Lambda }}\end{cases}}}

where J(C)=\mathbb C^g/\Lambda, is independent of the integration path. Changing p_0 changes the map only by a translation of J(C).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves compact Riemann surfaces; holomorphic differential forms; homology and periods; quotients by a lattice. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Closed loops give lattice periods

Although this is seemingly dependent on a path from {\displaystyle p_{0}} to {\displaystyle p,} any two such paths define a closed loop in {\displaystyle C} and, therefore, an element of {\displaystyle H_{1}(C,\mathbb {Z} ),} so integration over it gives an element of {\displaystyle \Lambda .} Thus the difference is erased in the passage to the quotient by {\displaystyle \Lambda }. Changing base-point {\displaystyle p_{0}} does change the map, but only by a translation of the torus.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The final cancellation is elementary, but interpreting the argument requires a compact Riemann surface, holomorphic one-forms, integration on cycles, homology classes and the quotient by the period lattice. These interdependent objects are not a small extension of introductory topology and calculus. The proof is Beyond undergraduate even when homology invariance is accepted without its proof.

Kind:

Theorem

Treatment:

Implicitly assumed

Statement used:

On a smooth surface, integration of a closed one-form over closed curves depends only on their integral homology class; holomorphic one-forms on a Riemann surface are closed.

Application in proof:

Compare two paths by their difference loop. Its homology class is an integer combination of the chosen generators, so each difference of integrals is the corresponding combination of periods.

Application level:

Beyond undergraduate level

Level justification:

Understanding this application requires differential forms and homology together, including the distinction between a path and its homology class. These prerequisites exceed the specified baseline.

Proof 1 — Closed loops give lattice periods

Statement used:

The period lattice Lambda is the subgroup of C^g consisting of integer linear combinations of the vectors (integral_gamma_j omega_1,...,integral_gamma_j omega_g).

Application in proof:

The difference of path integrals lies in Lambda and therefore vanishes in C^g/Lambda; a base-point change supplies a fixed translation.
