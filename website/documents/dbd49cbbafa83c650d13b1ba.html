Statement

Let H be a normal subgroup of a group G and let c:G\to\operatorname{Aut}(H) be the conjugation homomorphism c(g)(h)=ghg^{-1}. Then \ker c=C_G(H)=\{g\in G:gh=hg\text{ for every }h\in H\}. In particular, for H=G this is the center Z(G).

Proof 1: Rewrite trivial conjugation as commutation

Given a group {\displaystyle G} and an element g\in G, the mapping {\displaystyle x\mapsto gxg^{-1}} is an automorphism - an isomorphism whose domain and image are the same group. This gives a homomorphism from {\displaystyle G} to its automorphism group {\displaystyle {\text{Aut}}(G)}, mapping each {\displaystyle g} to its respective inner automorphism as described, and the kernel of this homomorphism is the center {\displaystyle Z(G)} of {\displaystyle G}, consisting of {\displaystyle g\in G} where for every {\displaystyle x\in G}, we have {\displaystyle gxg^{-1}=x}, or equivalently {\displaystyle gx=xg}. More generally, for every normal subgroup {\displaystyle H} of {\displaystyle G} (i.e. subgroups invariant under conjugation by elements of G), this conjugation map is also an automorphism on {\displaystyle H}, giving another homomorphism {\displaystyle G} to {\displaystyle {\text{Aut}}(H)}, with the kernel being the centralizer {\displaystyle C_{G}(H)} of {\displaystyle H} in {\displaystyle G}, being the set of {\displaystyle g\in G} where for every {\displaystyle h\in H}, we have {\displaystyle ghg^{-1}=h}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Being in the kernel means conjugation fixes each element of H; multiplying by g converts this to commuting with every h. The special case H=G is the center. This is elementary group theory.
