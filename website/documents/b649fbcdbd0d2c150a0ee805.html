Statement

For an odd prime p and integers x,y, in the quotient ring \mathbb Z[i]/(p) one has (x+yi)^p=x+(-1)^{(p-1)/2}yi. In particular the Frobenius map \omega\mapsto\omega^p is the identity when p\equiv1\pmod4.

Level justification

Gaussian integers are concrete complex numbers with integer coordinates. Ring ideals, quotient cardinality, prime elements and congruences are introductory algebra; the norm and primary/quartic conditions are explicit finite arithmetic definitions. No field-extension norm or ideal-class theory is required.

Proof 1: Frobenius and the power of i

If p is an odd prime number, then we have {\displaystyle i^{p-1}=(-1)^{\frac {p-1}{2}}} in the Gaussian integers. Consequently, writing a Gaussian integer ω = x + iy with x,y ∈ Z and applying the Frobenius endomorphism in Z[i]/(p), one finds

{\displaystyle \omega ^{p}=(x+yi)^{p}\equiv x^{p}+y^{p}i^{p}\equiv x+(-1)^{\frac {p-1}{2}}yi{\pmod {p}},}

since the endomorphism fixes the elements of Z/(p). If {\displaystyle p=4n+1} for some integer n, then in the above expression for ωp, the exponent ⁠{\displaystyle (p-1)/2}⁠ of −1 is even. Hence the right hand side equals ω, so in this case the Frobenius endomorphism of Z[i]/(p) is the identity.
