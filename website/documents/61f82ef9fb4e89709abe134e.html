Statement

Let (\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P) carry a filtration (\mathcal F_n)_{n\in I}, where I=\{0,\ldots,N\} or I=\mathbb N_0. If an integrable real martingale Y satisfies Y_0=0 and each Y_n is \mathcal F_{n-1}-measurable for n\ge1, then Y_n=0 almost surely for every n.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement involves a martingale and one-step measurable random variables, beyond the rubric calculus baseline.

Proof 1: Compare the two conditional expectations

The process Y is a martingale, implying that

\mathbb E[Y_n\mid\mathcal F_{n-1}]=Y_{n-1}\quad\text{a.s.},

and also predictable, implying that

\mathbb E[Y_n\mid\mathcal F_{n-1}]=Y_n\quad\text{a.s.}

for any n\in I\setminus\{0\}. Since Y_0=0, this implies iteratively that Y_n=0 almost surely for all n\in I.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Compare the martingale conditional expectation with the measurable-variable identity and iterate almost surely. General conditional expectation on filtered probability spaces, martingales and predictability require graduate probability background.
