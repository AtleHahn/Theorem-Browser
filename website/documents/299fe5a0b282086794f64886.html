Statement

In a real or complex inner product space, if v_1,\ldots,v_n are pairwise orthogonal and \|v\|^2=\langle v,v\rangle, then \|\sum_{k=1}^n v_k\|^2=\sum_{k=1}^n\|v_k\|^2.

Proof 1: Expand the inner product and iterate over orthogonal sums

For two orthogonal vectors v and w, the Pythagorean theorem is a consequence of the properties of the inner product:

\begin{aligned} \|v+w\|^2&=\langle v+w,v+w\rangle\\ &=\langle v,v\rangle+\langle w,w\rangle+\langle v,w\rangle+\langle w,v\rangle\\ &=\|v\|^2+\|w\|^2, \end{aligned}

where \langle v,w\rangle=\langle w,v\rangle=0 because of orthogonality.

The Pythagorean identity can be extended to sums of more than two orthogonal vectors. If v_1,v_2,\ldots,v_n are pairwise-orthogonal vectors in an inner-product space, then application of the Pythagorean theorem to successive orthogonal sums (each partial sum being orthogonal to the next vector) results in the equation

\left\|\sum_{k=1}^n v_k\right\|^2=\sum_{k=1}^n\|v_k\|^2.
