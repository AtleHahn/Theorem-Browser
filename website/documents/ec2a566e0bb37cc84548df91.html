Statement

The function \sin z/z on \mathbb C\setminus\{0\} extends to an entire function by setting its value at zero to one.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex sine has its familiar power series/exponential definition, and entire means complex differentiable everywhere. The growth parameter is explicitly an infimum of exponential bounds. Calculus and real analysis suffice after these direct definitions.

Proof 1: Divide the sine power series

Taking a power series expansion for ⁠{\displaystyle \textstyle {\frac {\sin(z)}{z}}}⁠ around the singular point shows that

{\displaystyle {\text{sinc}}(z)={\frac {1}{z}}\left(\sum _{k=0}^{\infty }{\frac {(-1)^{k}z^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!}}\right)=\sum _{k=0}^{\infty }{\frac {(-1)^{k}z^{2k}}{(2k+1)!}}=1-{\frac {z^{2}}{3!}}+{\frac {z^{4}}{5!}}-{\frac {z^{6}}{7!}}+\cdots .}

The resulting power series converges on all of \mathbb C and has value 1 at z=0, giving the entire extension.

Level justification

Divide the sine series by z and use its infinite convergence radius and constant term. Undergraduate complex power-series theory suffices.
