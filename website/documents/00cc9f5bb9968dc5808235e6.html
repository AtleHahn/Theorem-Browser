Statement

For a positive integer n, put H_n=\sum_{i=1}^n1/i. A subset A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\} is primitive if no two distinct elements of A divide one another. Its logarithmic density in this interval is

\delta_n(A)=\frac{1}{H_n}\sum_{a\in A}\frac1a.

There exist constants C>0 and an integer N\geq3 such that, for every integer n\geq N and every primitive A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\},

\delta_n(A)\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{\log\log n}},

where \log denotes the natural logarithm. In particular, these densities tend uniformly to zero as n\to\infty.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The uniform bound is expressed with explicit constants and elementary divisibility, reciprocal sums and logarithms. Its decay interpretation uses elementary limits; no analytic-number-theory machinery is needed to understand it.
