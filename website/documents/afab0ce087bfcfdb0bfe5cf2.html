Statement

Let X,Y be real Banach spaces and F:A→Y be continuously Fréchet differentiable on an open A⊂X. Suppose a∈A and DF(a):X→Y is a bounded linear isomorphism. Then F has a C1 inverse G between neighborhoods of a and F(a), with DG(y)=(DF(G(y)))^{-1}.

Infinite-dimensional differentiability requires functional analysis.

Proof 1: Inverse function theorem in Banach spaces — outline

Write f=F and let g denote its local inverse. Identify the target with X using DF(a)^{-1} in the following affine normalization.

To prove existence, it can be assumed after an affine transformation that {\displaystyle f(0)=0} and {\displaystyle f^{\prime }(0)=I}, so that {\displaystyle a=0} and b=f(a)=0.

By the mean value theorem for vector-valued functions, for a differentiable function {\displaystyle u:[0,1]\to X}, {\textstyle \|u(1)-u(0)\|\leq \sup _{0\leq t\leq 1}\|u^{\prime }(t)\|}. Setting {\displaystyle u(t)=f(x+t(x^{\prime }-x))-x-t(x^{\prime }-x)}, it follows that

{\displaystyle \|f(x)-f(x^{\prime })-x+x^{\prime }\|\leq \|x-x^{\prime }\|\,\sup _{0\leq t\leq 1}\|f^{\prime }(x+t(x^{\prime }-x))-I\|.}

Choose the ball to lie in the domain of f. Now choose {\displaystyle \delta >0} so that {\textstyle \|f'(x)-I\|<{1 \over 2}} for {\displaystyle \|x\|<\delta }. Suppose that {\displaystyle \|y\|<\delta /2} and define {\displaystyle x_{n}} inductively by {\displaystyle x_{0}=0} and {\displaystyle x_{n+1}=x_{n}+y-f(x_{n})}. The assumptions show that if {\displaystyle \|x\|,\,\,\|x^{\prime }\|<\delta } then

{\displaystyle \|f(x)-f(x^{\prime })-x+x^{\prime }\|\leq \|x-x^{\prime }\|/2}.

In particular {\displaystyle f(x)=f(x^{\prime })} implies {\displaystyle x=x^{\prime }}. In the inductive scheme {\displaystyle \|x_{n}\|<\delta } and {\displaystyle \|x_{n+1}-x_{n}\|<\delta /2^{n}}. More precisely, \|x_{n+1}-x_n\|\le2^{-n}\|y\| and \|x_n\|\le2\|y\|<\delta. Thus {\displaystyle (x_{n})} is a Cauchy sequence tending to {\displaystyle x}. By construction {\displaystyle f(x)=y} as required, with \|x\|\le2\|y\|<\delta. Set V=B(0,\delta/2) and U=B(0,\delta)\cap f^{-1}(V); then f:U\to V is a bijection.

To check that {\displaystyle g=f^{-1}} is C1, write x=g(y) and, for k\ne0, {\displaystyle g(y+k)=x+h} so that {\displaystyle f(x+h)=f(x)+k}. By the inequalities above, {\displaystyle \|h-k\|\leq\|h\|/2} so that {\displaystyle \|h\|/2\leq\|k\|\leq2\|h\|}. On the other hand, if {\displaystyle A=f^{\prime }(x)}, then {\displaystyle \|A-I\|<1/2}. Using the geometric series for {\displaystyle B=I-A}, it follows that {\displaystyle \|A^{-1}\|<2}. But then

{\displaystyle {\|g(y+k)-g(y)-f^{\prime }(g(y))^{-1}k\| \over \|k\|}={\|h-f^{\prime }(x)^{-1}[f(x+h)-f(x)]\| \over \|k\|}\leq 4{\|f(x+h)-f(x)-f^{\prime }(x)h\| \over \|h\|}}

tends to 0 as {\displaystyle k} and {\displaystyle h} tend to 0, proving that {\displaystyle g} is C1 with {\displaystyle g^{\prime }(y)=f^{\prime }(g(y))^{-1}}.

The lower Lipschitz bound makes g continuous. Continuity of f' and of bounded-operator inversion makes g' continuous. Undoing the affine changes gives the stated neighborhoods and derivative formula.

Neumann series for ||I−A||<1 and continuity of bounded operator inversion
Kind:

Theorem

Treatment:

Cited without proof

Statement used:

If a bounded operator A on a Banach space satisfies ||I-A||<1, then A inverse=sum_(j>=0)(I-A)^j in operator norm and ||A inverse||<=1/(1-||I-A||). Inversion is continuous on invertible bounded operators.

Application in proof:

Invert the nearby derivatives, bound their inverses, and obtain continuity of the inverse derivative.
