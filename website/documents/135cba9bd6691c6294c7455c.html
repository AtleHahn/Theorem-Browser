Statement

Fix an integer m\geq1. For a nonzero real homogeneous polynomial F(X,Y,Z) of degree m, let

C_F=\{[X:Y:Z]\in\mathbb{RP}^2:F(X,Y,Z)=0\},

and let c(F) be the number of connected components of C_F. Then

\frac{1-(-1)^m}{2}\leq c(F)\leq\frac{(m-1)(m-2)}2+1.

Conversely, every integer in this interval is c(F) for some such polynomial F.

The real projective plane is the set of nonzero vectors in \mathbb R^3 modulo multiplication by a nonzero real scalar, equipped with the quotient topology. Homogeneous of degree m means that every monomial with nonzero coefficient has total degree m, so the equation defines a subset of projective space. Components are maximal connected subsets in this topology; the empty real zero set has zero components. The assertion concerns the real zero set and does not require nonsingularity or irreducibility.

Level

Level justification

Basic point-set topology and multivariable polynomials suffice. Projective space is explicitly the quotient by nonzero scaling, a small extension of familiar equivalence relations and quotient topology; no genus or algebraic-geometric scheme theory is needed.

Proof 1: Curve perturbations and the singular bound (external)

The following editorial argument completes the stated scope. A supporting source is Introduction to Topology of Real Algebraic Varieties (§1.3 bounds; §1.6.A–B construction; §1.8 conclusion. Mangolte, Real Algebraic Varieties, Remark 2.7.3 and Theorem 2.7.2, pp. 139–141, cover the singular bound.). Supporting sources: Mangolte, Real Algebraic Varieties, Remark 2.7.3.

For the upper bound remove repeated irreducible factors, preserving the real zero set and lowering degree to d<=m. Put B(d)=(d-1)(d-2)/2+1, which is nondecreasing for positive integral d. Apply the irreducible singular bound described by Mangolte, with Brusotti and the cited Bezout argument as dependencies. For reducible curves use component-count subadditivity and B(a+b)-B(a)-B(b)=ab-2. This is nonnegative except a=b=1; in that case two real projective lines intersect and their union is connected, giving B(2)=1 directly. This proves the bound by induction on factors, and B(d)<=B(m). Odd-degree F has a zero since its restriction to the sphere changes sign between antipodal points; this gives the lower bound. Viro sections 1.6–1.8 realize every permitted count by nonsingular curves.
