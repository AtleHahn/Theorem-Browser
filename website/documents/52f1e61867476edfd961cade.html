Statement

Let ABC be a nondegenerate Euclidean triangle with a=|BC|, b=|CA|, c=|AB| and opposite interior angles \alpha,\beta,\gamma. Then c=b\cos\alpha+a\cos\beta.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement and argument concern elementary Euclidean triangles, trigonometry and algebra.

Proof 1: Add the altitude projections

Drop the altitude from C to the line AB. The projections onto this line from the two adjacent sides have signed lengths b\cos\alpha and a\cos\beta, whose sum is AB=c. The same relation holds if the foot is outside the side because the obtuse-angle projection is negative.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Drop the altitude and add signed horizontal projections, including a negative projection for an obtuse angle. School coordinate geometry and cosine ratios suffice.
