Statement

Let (\Omega,\Sigma,\nu) be a measure space and f:\Omega\to\overline{\mathbb R} be measurable. Let g:\overline{\mathbb R}\to[0,\infty] be Borel measurable and nondecreasing. For a real t>0 with 0<g(t)<\infty, \nu\{f\ge t\}\le\frac1{g(t)}\int_\Omega g(f)\,d\nu. The integral may be infinite.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Arbitrary measure spaces and Lebesgue integration are outside the calculus baseline.

Proof 1: Apply Markov after the monotone transform

This statement follows from the Markov inequality, {\displaystyle \nu (\{x\in\Omega:|F(x)|\geq \varepsilon \})\leq {\frac {1}{\varepsilon }}\int _{\Omega}|F|d\nu }, with {\displaystyle F=g\circ f} and {\displaystyle \varepsilon =g(t)}, since in this case {\displaystyle \nu (\{x\in\Omega\,:\,\,g\circ f(x)\geq g(t)\})\geq \nu (\{x\in\Omega\,:\,\,f(x)\geq t\})}.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Unlike the elementary probability specializations, this statement allows an arbitrary measure space, extended-real measurable functions and possibly infinite Lebesgue integrals. Measurable composition and the measure form of Markov require the broader measure-theoretic framework.
