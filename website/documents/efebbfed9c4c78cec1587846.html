Statement

Let n\ge0 be an integer and let f be entire. Suppose there are positive radii R_k\to\infty such that

M_k=\max_{|z|=R_k}\operatorname{Re}f(z)=o(R_k^{n+1}).

Then f is a polynomial of degree at most n.

Level justification

Real analysis supplies derivatives, maxima, uniform convergence and completeness; complex arithmetic supplies the plane. Holomorphic means complex differentiable, and entire means so throughout the plane. With that short extension, the displayed bound or convergence assertion uses familiar analytic concepts without contour integrals or specialist function-space theory.

Proof 1: Derivative bound and a fixed-radius limit (outline)

Use the Borel–Carathéodory derivative estimate as an explicit prerequisite: if F is holomorphic near |z|\le R and \operatorname{Re}F\le M on the boundary, then for integers j\ge1 and 0<r<R,

\max_{|z|=r}|F^{(j)}(z)|\le \frac{2j!R}{(R-r)^{j+1}}(M-\operatorname{Re}F(0)).

Fix r>0. By the Borel–Carathéodory derivative estimate, for any 0<r<R_k,

\max_{|z|=r}|f^{(n+1)}(z)|\leq \frac{2(n+1)!}{(R_k-r)^{n+2}}R_k(M_k-\operatorname{Re}f(0)),

where M_k=\max_{|z|=R_k}\operatorname{Re}f(z)=o(R_k^{n+1}).

For sufficiently large k, r\leq R_k/2. Taking the k\to\infty limit gives

\max_{|z|=r}|f^{(n+1)}(z)|=0,

since (M_k-\operatorname{Re}f(0))/R_k^{n+1}\to0.

Thus f^{(n+1)} is zero on every such circle and, by continuity, on the entire plane. So f is a polynomial of degree at most n, by its Taylor expansion.

Level justification

The limit estimate itself is elementary, but the argument uses entire functions, bounds for all complex derivatives and global holomorphic Taylor representation. Under the conservative criterion, the combined complex-analysis background is retained beyond undergraduate rather than treating a holomorphic function as merely a real differentiable one.
