Statement

Let S be a nonempty set of points in the Euclidean plane with integer pairwise distances and finite diameter \delta. Then |S|\le4(\delta+1)^2.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement concerns points in the Euclidean plane, integer distances and an explicit quadratic bound on their number. Diameter is understood as the least upper bound of pairwise distances; this concrete geometric counting assertion is treated as elementary even though finiteness is a conclusion.

Proof 1: Substitute the diameter in the point bound

For a noncollinear set, the conic-intersection bound is 4(d(A,B)+1)(d(B,C)+1) for any triangle A,B,C in the set. Both side lengths are at most \delta, giving 4(\delta+1)^2. A collinear set has at most \delta+1 points, which also satisfies this bound.
