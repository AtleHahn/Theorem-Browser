Statement

Let C_1,C_2,C_3,C_4 be circles in the Euclidean plane passing through a point M. Assume each pair meets at a second point P_{ij}, that these six second intersection points are distinct and each belongs to exactly two of the circles, and that no three of them are collinear. For each three-element subset I\subset\{1,2,3,4\} let K_I be the circle through the three points P_{ij} with i,j\in I. Then all four circles K_I pass through a common point.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Finite circle and point constructions are elementary once the explicitly stated incidence conditions are understood.

Proof 1: Inversion and directed angles (external)

The following editorial argument completes the stated scope. A supporting source is An Extension of Clifford’s Circle Chain (Classical four-line construction described in Dougall; complete direct inversion/angle bridge supplied given below.).

Invert about M. The four original circles become four lines l_i, no two parallel and no three concurrent: their six pairwise intersections A_ij are finite and distinct. Let D_ijk be the circumcircle of the triangle formed by l_i,l_j,l_k. D_123 and D_124 meet at A_12 and a second point Q. Their tangent directions at A_12 differ by the angle between l_3 and l_4 (the inscribed-angle/tangent theorem), which is nonzero modulo pi; hence Q is distinct from A_12. It cannot be any other A_ij, since that would put three distinct points of one l_i on one of these circles. Directed angles modulo pi give angle(A_13,Q,A_14)=angle(A_13,Q,A_12)+angle(A_12,Q,A_14)=angle(l_3,l_2)+angle(l_2,l_4)=angle(l_3,l_4)=angle(A_13,A_34,A_14). Thus Q belongs to D_134; exchanging 1 and 2 gives Q in D_234. The inversion center cannot belong to any D_ijk, since its inverse would make P_ij,P_ik,P_jk collinear, excluded in the statement. Therefore inversion sends these four circles and Q to the four required K_I and a finite common point. Present this as an editorial direct argument based on the classical four-line/Miquel construction, not as a quotation of Dougall’s orthogonality extension.
