Statement

For a positive integer n, let F_n be the increasing sequence of reduced fractions a/b\in[0,1] with integers b\ge1, \gcd(a,b)=1 and b\le n, including 0/1 and 1/1. Write \varphi(m)=|\{1\le a\le m:\gcd(a,m)=1\}|, so \varphi(1)=1. For n\ge2, |F_n|=|F_{n-1}|+\varphi(n).

Proof 1: Farey length recurrence — source argument

The Farey sequence of order n contains all of the members of the Farey sequences of lower orders. In particular, for n\ge2, F_n contains all of the members of F_{n-1} and also contains an additional fraction for each number that is less than n and coprime to n. Thus F_6 consists of F_5 together with the fractions 1/6 and 5/6.

From this count, we can relate the lengths of F_n and F_{n-1} using Euler's totient function:

|F_n|=|F_{n-1}|+\varphi(n).

Level justification

New fractions at order n are exactly the reduced fractions with denominator n; count their coprime numerators. Elementary integer fractions and finite counting suffice.
