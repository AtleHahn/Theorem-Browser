Statement

Every finitely generated abelian group is a direct sum \mathbb Z^f\oplus\mathbb Z/d_1\mathbb Z\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb Z/d_r\mathbb Z, where d_i>1 and d_1\mid\cdots\mid d_r; the rank and factors are unique.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory group and ring theory supplies subgroups, quotients, commutators and homomorphisms. Any added terms such as perfect, nilpotent or virtually free describe explicit subgroup conditions; they are small algebraic extensions without representation or geometric-group theory.

Proof 1: Specialize invariant factors to the integers

Take R=\mathbb Z in the invariant-factor structure theorem. Abelian groups are modules over \mathbb Z; its proper nonzero ideals have unique positive generators d>1. The zero ideals give free copies of \mathbb Z, and the divisibility and uniqueness conclusions give the asserted form.

Specialize the supplied invariant-factor statement to the integers and translate modules to abelian groups. Integer scalar multiplication and principal ideals are a small extension of undergraduate group/ring theory, and the general module theorem is invoked rather than proved.
