Statement

On the open unit disc D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}, the polynomials f_n(z)=z-1+1/n (n\ge1) converge uniformly to f(z)=z-1. Each f_n has one zero in D, whereas f has none there.

Proof 1: Explicit linear-polynomial zeros

Consider the unit disk D and the sequence defined by

f_n(z)=z-1+\frac1n,\qquad z\in\mathbb C,

which converges uniformly to f(z)=z-1. The function f(z) contains no zeroes in D; however, each f_n has exactly one zero in the disk corresponding to the real value 1-1/n.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The difference from the limit is constantly 1/n in modulus, giving uniform convergence. Solving the linear equations puts each approximate zero at 1-1/n inside the disk and the limiting zero at the boundary point one. Elementary complex arithmetic and undergraduate uniform convergence suffice; Hurwitz’s theorem is not used.
