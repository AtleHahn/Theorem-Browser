Statement

Let (S,\Sigma,\mu) be a measure space and let f:S\to[-\infty,\infty] be measurable. For \varepsilon>0,

\mu\{x:|f(x)|\ge\varepsilon\}\le\frac1\varepsilon\int_S|f|\,d\mu.
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement concerns arbitrary measure spaces and extended Lebesgue integrals.

Proof 1: Integrate a simple-function minorant

We may assume that the function {\displaystyle f} is non-negative, since only its absolute value enters in the equation. Now, consider the real-valued function s on S given by

{\displaystyle s(x)={\begin{cases}\varepsilon ,&{\text{if }}f(x)\geq \varepsilon \\0,&{\text{if }}f(x)<\varepsilon \end{cases}}}

Then {\displaystyle 0\leq s(x)\leq f(x)}. By the definition of the Lebesgue integral

{\displaystyle \int _{S}f(x)\,d\mu \geq \int _{S}s(x)\,d\mu =\varepsilon \mu (\{x\in S:\,f(x)\geq \varepsilon \})}

and since {\displaystyle \varepsilon >0}, both sides can be divided by {\displaystyle \varepsilon }, obtaining

{\displaystyle \mu (\{x\in S:\,f(x)\geq \varepsilon \})\leq {1 \over \varepsilon }\int _{S}f\,d\mu .}
