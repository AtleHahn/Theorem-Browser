Statement

Let (X,\mathcal A,P) be a probability space and H a nonempty finite or countable family of measurable maps h:X\to\{0,1\}. For an m-sample r, write \widehat Q_r(h)=m^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^m h(r_i) and Q(h)=\int h\,dP. Define \Pi_H(k)=\max_{x\in X^k}|\{(h(x_1),\ldots,h(x_k)):h\in H\}|. Then for every integer m\ge1 and \varepsilon>0, P^m(\exists h\in H:|Q(h)-\widehat Q_r(h)|\ge\varepsilon)\le4\Pi_H(2m)e^{-m\varepsilon^2/8}.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement uses arbitrary probability measures and random samples.

Proof 1: Symmetrization, pair swapping and a finite union bound

Let

V=\{r\in X^m:\exists h\in H,\ |Q(h)-\widehat Q_r(h)|\geq\varepsilon\},

and

R=\{(r,s)\in X^m\times X^m:\exists h\in H,\ |\widehat Q_r(h)-\widehat Q_s(h)|\geq\varepsilon/2\}.

If m\varepsilon^2<2, the claimed right side exceeds 4e^{-1/4}>1, since \Pi_H(2m)\geq1, and the result follows from probability being at most one. Otherwise m\geq2/\varepsilon^2.

The proof transforms the original sample problem into the comparison of two independent samples, then considers permutations of their concatenation, and finally restricts H to the finite set of label patterns on a fixed joint sample.

Symmetrization

By the triangle inequality, if {\displaystyle |Q(h)-{\widehat {Q}}_{r}(h)|\geq \varepsilon } and {\displaystyle |Q(h)-{\widehat {Q}}_{s}(h)|\leq \varepsilon /2} then {\displaystyle |{\widehat {Q}}_{r}(h)-{\widehat {Q}}_{s}(h)|\geq \varepsilon /2}.

Therefore,

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}&P^{2m}(R)\\[5pt]\geq {}&P^{2m}\{\exists h\in H,|Q(h)-{\widehat {Q}}_{r}(h)|\geq \varepsilon {\text{ and }}|Q(h)-{\widehat {Q}}_{s}(h)|\leq \varepsilon /2\}\\[5pt]={}&\int _{V}P^{m}\{s:\exists h\in H,|Q(h)-{\widehat {Q}}_{r}(h)|\geq \varepsilon {\text{ and }}|Q(h)-{\widehat {Q}}_{s}(h)|\leq \varepsilon /2\}\,dP^{m}(r)\\[5pt]={}&A\end{aligned}}}

since {\displaystyle r} and {\displaystyle s} are independent.

Now for {\displaystyle r\in V} fix an {\displaystyle h\in H} such that {\displaystyle |Q(h)-{\widehat {Q}}_{r}(h)|\geq \varepsilon }. For this {\displaystyle h}, we shall show that

{\displaystyle P^{m}\left\{|Q(h)-{\widehat {Q}}_{s}(h)|\leq {\frac {\varepsilon }{2}}\right\}\geq {\frac {1}{2}}.}

Thus, integrating the pointwise bound over r\in V, {\displaystyle A\geq {\frac {P^{m}(V)}{2}}} and hence {\displaystyle P^{2m}(R)\geq {\frac {P^{m}(V)}{2}}}.

Notice, {\displaystyle m\cdot {\widehat {Q}}_{s}(h)} is a binomial random variable with expectation {\displaystyle m\cdot Q(h)} and variance {\displaystyle m\cdot Q(h)(1-Q(h))}. By Chebyshev's inequality we get

{\displaystyle P^{m}\left\{|Q(h)-{\widehat {Q_{s}(h)}}|>{\frac {\varepsilon }{2}}\right\}\leq {\frac {m\cdot Q(h)(1-Q(h))}{(\varepsilon m/2)^{2}}}\leq {\frac {1}{\varepsilon ^{2}m}}\leq {\frac {1}{2}}}

for the mentioned bound on {\displaystyle m}. Here we use the fact that {\displaystyle x(1-x)\leq 1/4} for 0\leq x\leq1.

All the events are measurable because H is finite or countable.

Pair-swap averaging

Let \Gamma_m be the set of all permutations of \{1,2,\ldots,2m\} that swap i and m+i for i in some subset of \{1,\ldots,m\}. For any \sigma\in\Gamma_m,

P^{2m}(R)=P^{2m}\{x:\sigma(x)\in R\},

since coordinate permutations preserve the product distribution P^{2m}. Therefore

\begin{aligned} P^{2m}(R) &=\int_{X^{2m}}1_R(x)\,dP^{2m}(x)\\ &=\frac1{|\Gamma_m|}\sum_{\sigma\in\Gamma_m} \int_{X^{2m}}1_R(\sigma(x))\,dP^{2m}(x)\\ &=\int_{X^{2m}}\frac1{|\Gamma_m|} \sum_{\sigma\in\Gamma_m}1_R(\sigma(x))\,dP^{2m}(x)\\ &=\int_{X^{2m}}\Pr_\sigma[\sigma(x)\in R]\,dP^{2m}(x)\\ &\leq\max_{x\in X^{2m}}\Pr_\sigma[\sigma(x)\in R]. \end{aligned}

The interchange is valid because |\Gamma_m| is finite. The penultimate integral is the expectation over x chosen according to P^{2m}, and the probability is over \sigma chosen uniformly from \Gamma_m. The maximum is guaranteed to exist since there is only a finite set of values that probability under a random permutation can take.

Finite label patterns

Fix x=(x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_{2m}) and let t be the number of distinct label patterns of H on x, which is at most \Pi_H(2m). This means there are functions h_1,h_2,\ldots,h_t\in H such that for any h\in H, there exists j between 1 and t with h_j(x_k)=h(x_k) for 1\leq k\leq2m.

Let \sigma be chosen uniformly from the 2^m permutations that swap i and m+i for a subset of i=1,\ldots,m, and write \sigma(x)=(x_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(2m)}). Define w_i^j=1 if h_j(x_i)=1 and w_i^j=0 otherwise. The two-sample event R occurs for \sigma(x) if and only if some j\in\{1,\ldots,t\} satisfies

\left|\frac1m\left(\sum_{i=1}^m w_{\sigma(i)}^j-\sum_{i=1}^m w_{\sigma(m+i)}^j\right)\right| \geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

By the union bound we get

\begin{aligned} \Pr_\sigma[\sigma(x)\in R] &\leq t\max_{1\leq j\leq t} \Pr_\sigma\left[ \left|\frac1m\left(\sum_{i=1}^m w_{\sigma(i)}^j-\sum_{i=1}^m w_{\sigma(m+i)}^j\right)\right| \geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right]\\ &\leq\Pi_H(2m)\max_{1\leq j\leq t} \Pr_\sigma\left[ \left|\frac1m\left(\sum_{i=1}^m w_{\sigma(i)}^j-\sum_{i=1}^m w_{\sigma(m+i)}^j\right)\right| \geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right]. \end{aligned}

Since the distribution over the permutations \sigma is uniform, for each i the difference w_{\sigma(i)}^j-w_{\sigma(m+i)}^j equals \pm|w_i^j-w_{m+i}^j|, with equal probability. Thus

\begin{aligned} &\Pr_\sigma\left[ \left|\frac1m\sum_{i=1}^m(w_{\sigma(i)}^j-w_{\sigma(m+i)}^j)\right| \geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right]\\ &\qquad=\Pr_\beta\left[ \left|\frac1m\sum_{i=1}^m|w_i^j-w_{m+i}^j|\beta_i\right| \geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right], \end{aligned}

where the \beta_i are independent random signs and both possibilities are equally likely. By Hoeffding's inequality, this is at most 2e^{-m\varepsilon^2/8}. Consequently

\Pr_\sigma[\sigma(x)\in R]\leq2\Pi_H(2m)e^{-m\varepsilon^2/8}.

Finally, combining all three parts of the proof we get

P^m(V)\leq2P^{2m}(R)\leq4\Pi_H(2m)e^{-m\varepsilon^2/8}.
