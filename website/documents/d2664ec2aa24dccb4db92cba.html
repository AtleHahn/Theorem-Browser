Statement

Let \Omega be a bounded Jordan domain, p\in\Omega, and \Omega_n bounded Jordan domains containing \Omega, with \overline{\Omega_{n+1}}\subset\Omega_n and Carathéodory kernel \Omega at p. Let f_n:\Omega_n\to\Omega be conformal bijections satisfying f_n(p)=p and f_n^{\prime}(p)>0. Then f_n^{\prime}\to1 uniformly on compact subsets of \Omega.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The conclusion is locally uniform convergence of complex derivatives under geometric domain hypotheses.

Proof 1: Derivatives of normalized outer Jordan maps converge to one — outline

By the Carathéodory kernel theorem, f_n(z) converges uniformly on compacta in \Omega to z. In fact, Carathéodory's theorem implies that the inverse maps tend uniformly on compacta to z. Given a subsequence of f_n, it has a subsequence convergent on compacta in \Omega, by Montel's theorem for the bounded family of holomorphic maps into \Omega. Since the inverse functions converge to z, it follows that the subsequence converges to z on compacta. Hence f_n converges to z on compacta in \Omega.

As a consequence, the derivative of f_n tends to 1 uniformly on compacta: locally uniform convergence of holomorphic functions implies locally uniform convergence of their derivatives.

Level justification

Use Caratheodory kernel convergence for inverse conformal maps, Montel subsequences and derivative convergence. Kernel convergence of varying Jordan domains and conformal normalization together go beyond a confidently small extension of ordinary undergraduate complex analysis.
