Statement

Given points A,B,C in the Euclidean plane with B\ne C, a straightedge and collapsing compass can construct the circle with center A and radius |BC|. A collapsing compass may draw a circle centered at one given point through another, but does not retain its aperture when lifted. Consequently every straightedge-and-fixed-compass construction can be performed with a straightedge and collapsing compass.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The tools and radius-transfer problem are elementary Euclidean geometry.

Proof 1: Transfer the radius through an equilateral triangle — outline

If A=B, draw the circle centered at A through C. Otherwise, write U(V) for the circle centered at U and passing through V.

  • Draw a circle centered at A and passing through B and vice versa. They will intersect at a point D and form the equilateral triangle ABD.
  • Extend DB past B and find the intersection of DB and the circle B(C), labeled E.
  • Create a circle centered at D and passing through E.
  • Extend DA past A and find the intersection of DA and the circle D(E), labeled F.
  • Construct a circle centered at A and passing through F.
  • Because ADB is an equilateral triangle, DA=DB.
  • Because E and F are on a circle around D, DE=DF.
  • Therefore, AF=BE.
  • Because E is on the circle B(C), BE=BC.
  • Therefore, AF=BC.

Transferring radii this way supplies the moves of a fixed compass.

Level justification

Construct an equilateral triangle and subtract equal initial segments from equal radii to transfer BC to A. This is classical elementary straightedge-and-compass geometry.

Routine omissions

The source omits the trivial A=B boundary, where the original circle already has the requested center; its equilateral construction is used only for A≠B. The positive radius BC ensures that the ray intersections lie beyond B and A. Replacing each fixed-compass radius transfer is the routine final simulation step.
