Statement

Let H consist of real-valued functions f on [0,\infty) that are locally absolutely continuous, vanish at zero, and have f'\in L^2(0,\infty), with \langle f,g\rangle=\int_0^\infty f' g'. Then K_y(x)=\min(x,y) reproduces evaluation at y, so H is an RKHS.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The function space uses absolute continuity, Lebesgue-square-integrable derivatives and the RKHS property.

Proof 1: Integrate the indicator of an initial interval — outline

To construct the reproducing kernel it suffices to consider a dense subspace, so let f\in H also satisfy {\displaystyle f\in C^{1}[0,\infty )} and {\displaystyle f(0)=0}. The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus then gives

{\displaystyle f(y)=\int _{0}^{y}f'(x)\,dx=\int _{0}^{\infty }G(x,y)f'(x)\,dx=\langle K_{y},f\rangle }

where

{\displaystyle G(x,y)={\begin{cases}1,&x<y\\0,&{\text{otherwise}}\end{cases}}}

and {\displaystyle K_{y}'(x)=G(x,y)\ \text{a.e.},\ K_{y}(0)=0} i.e.

{\displaystyle K(x,y)=K_{y}(x)=\int _{0}^{x}G(z,y)\,dz={\begin{cases}x,&0\leq x<y\\y,&{\text{otherwise.}}\end{cases}}=\min(x,y)}

This implies {\displaystyle K_{y}=K(\cdot ,y)} reproduces {\displaystyle f}.
