Statement

Up to similarity (isometry and uniform rescaling), at most countably many flat tori admit a finite triangulated circle packing. A flat torus is a quotient of the Euclidean plane by a lattice; a triangulated packing has circular-triangle gaps bounded by triples of tangent circles.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A flat torus is explicitly a lattice quotient, a limited geometric extension of undergraduate linear algebra/topology. Finite triangulations and countability use finite combinatorial encoding.

Proof 1: Count finite triangulations and use packing uniqueness — outline

In the case of flat tori (the quotient spaces of the Euclidean plane by a lattice, obtained by gluing opposite edges of a parallelogram), the geometry can vary continuously but, by the uniqueness of toroidal circle packings, only countably many distinct tori (up to scaling and isometry) have finite triangulated packings.

Here the uniqueness theorem is used without proof; the enumeration of finite triangulations is implicit.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Encode finite triangulations by finite combinatorial data and apply the exact uniqueness theorem to assign at most one similarity class per triangulation. Lattice quotients, basic countability and concrete circle configurations suffice; no proof of packing rigidity is required.
