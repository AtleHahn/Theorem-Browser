Statement

For bounded Lebesgue-measurable sets A_1,A_2\subset\mathbb R^2, a line bisects the area of both sets simultaneously.

Proof 1: Rotate a continuously chosen bisector through a half-turn — outline

Write m for planar Lebesgue measure. For each angle \alpha\in[0,180^\circ], a straight line (“knife”) of angle \alpha can bisect A_1. To see this, translate along its normal a straight line of angle \alpha from -\infty to \infty; the area of A_1 on one side changes continuously from 0 to m(A_1), so by the intermediate value theorem it must be equal to m(A_1)/2 somewhere along the way. It is possible that an entire range of translations of our line yield this area; in this case, it is a canonical choice to pick the middle one of all such translations.

For precision, for a unit normal v put H^+(v,c)=\{x:v\cdot x>c\}. If m(A_1)>0, the bisecting offsets form a nonempty compact interval I_v. The source uses the continuous midpoint selection c(v)=\tfrac12(\inf I_v+\sup I_v) for bounded A_1, leaving its continuity check implicit. Also I_{-v}=-I_v, so c(-v)=-c(v). If m(A_1)=0, choose c(v)=0 for every v.

When the knife is at angle 0, it also cuts A_2, but the pieces are probably unequal (if the pieces are equal, we are done). Define the positive side of the knife as the side in which the area of A_2 is larger, and carry this side continuously with the rotating normal. We now turn the knife, and translate it as described above. When the angle is \alpha, define p(\alpha) as the area of A_2 at the positive side of the knife. Initially p(0)>m(A_2)/2. The function p is continuous, since small changes in the angle lead to small changes in the position of the knife.

The area continuity follows by dominated convergence: when (v_j,c_j)\to(v,c), the indicators of A_2\cap H^+(v_j,c_j) converge to that of A_2\cap H^+(v,c) except on the line v\cdot x=c, which has measure zero. They are dominated by the integrable indicator of the bounded measurable set A_2. The same argument justifies the translation continuity for A_1 above.

When the knife is at angle 180^\circ, the knife is upside-down: the selected line is the same as at angle 0 but its positive side is reversed, so p(180^\circ)=m(A_2)-p(0)<m(A_2)/2. By the intermediate value theorem, there must be an angle at which p(\alpha)=m(A_2)/2. Cutting at that angle bisects both sets.
