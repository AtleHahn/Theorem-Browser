Statement

Let a regular simple closed continuously differentiable plane curve have length L and enclose area A. Then 4\pi A\le L^2, with equality exactly for a circle.

Level

Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Plane curves; Arc length; Area; Single-variable calculus. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Constant speed and the local periodic inequality

Local periodic inequality

Let L>0 and let y:[0,L]\to\mathbb R be continuously differentiable, with y(0)=y(L) and \int_0^L y(x)\,dx=0. Then

\int_0^L y(x)^2\,dx\le\frac{L^2}{4\pi^2}\int_0^L y'(x)^2\,dx.

Equality holds exactly when y(x)=c\sin(2\pi(x-\alpha)/L) for some real c,\alpha.

A linear change of variables reduces the problem to L=2\pi.

Take L=2\pi. Since Dirichlet's conditions are met, we can write

{\displaystyle y(x)={\frac {1}{2}}a_{0}+\sum _{n\geq 1}\left(a_{n}{\frac {\sin nx}{\sqrt {\pi }}}+b_{n}{\frac {\cos nx}{\sqrt {\pi }}}\right),}

and the fact that the average value of y is zero means that a0 = 0. By Parseval's identity,

{\displaystyle \int _{0}^{2\pi }y(x)^{2}\,\mathrm {d} x=\sum _{n=1}^{\infty }(a_{n}^{2}+b_{n}^{2})}

and

{\displaystyle \int _{0}^{2\pi }y'(x)^{2}\,\mathrm {d} x=\sum _{n=1}^{\infty }n^{2}(a_{n}^{2}+b_{n}^{2})}

and since the summands are all nonnegative, the Wirtinger inequality is proved. Furthermore, it is seen that equality holds if and only if an = bn = 0 for all n ≥ 2, which is to say that y(x)=(a_1\sin x+b_1\cos x)/\sqrt{\pi}. This is equivalent to the stated condition by use of the trigonometric addition formulas.

Plane curve

Let (x, y) be a positively oriented continuously differentiable parametrization of the given regular simple closed curve. Parametrizing the circle by [0, 2π] so that (x, y) has constant speed, the length L of the curve is given by

{\displaystyle \int _{0}^{2\pi }{\sqrt {x'(t)^{2}+y'(t)^{2}}}\,\mathrm {d} t}

and the area A enclosed by the curve is given (due to Stokes theorem) by

{\displaystyle -\int _{0}^{2\pi }y(t)x'(t)\,\mathrm {d} t.}

Since the integrand of the integral defining L is assumed constant, there is

{\displaystyle {\frac {L^{2}}{2\pi }}-2A=\int _{0}^{2\pi }{\big (}x'(t)^{2}+y'(t)^{2}+2y(t)x'(t){\big )}\,\mathrm {d} t}

which can be rewritten as

{\displaystyle \int _{0}^{2\pi }{\big (}x'(t)+y(t){\big )}^{2}\,\mathrm {d} t+\int _{0}^{2\pi }{\big (}y'(t)^{2}-y(t)^{2}{\big )}\,\mathrm {d} t.}

The first integral is clearly nonnegative. Without changing the area or length of the curve, (x, y) can be replaced by (x, y + z) for some number z, so as to make y have average value zero. Then the Wirtinger inequality can be applied to see that the second integral is also nonnegative, and therefore

{\displaystyle {\frac {L^{2}}{4\pi }}\geq A,}

which is the isoperimetric inequality. Furthermore, equality in the isoperimetric inequality implies both equality in the Wirtinger inequality and also the equality x′(t) + y(t) = 0, which amounts to y(t) = c1 sin(t – α) and then x(t) = c1 cos(t – α) + c2 for arbitrary numbers c1 and c2. These equations mean that the image of (x, y) is a round circle in the plane.
