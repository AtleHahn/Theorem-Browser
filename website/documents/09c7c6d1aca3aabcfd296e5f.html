Statement

Let p=(p_x) and q=(q_x) be probability vectors on a finite set U. Put A=\{x:p_x\ge q_x\}, a=\sum_{x\in A}p_x, and b=\sum_{x\in A}q_x. Then \sum_{x\in U}|p_x-q_x|=|a-b|+|(1-a)-(1-b)|. Thus the binary probability vectors (a,1-a) and (b,1-b) have the same \ell^1 distance as p,q.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Probability vectors are finite nonnegative lists summing to one; the asserted distance is an explicit sum of absolute differences.

Proof 1: Sum positive and negative differences separately

On A the differences p_x-q_x are nonnegative, and on its complement they are negative. Then

\begin{aligned} \sum_{x\in U}|p_x-q_x| &=\sum_{x\in A}(p_x-q_x)+\sum_{x\notin A}(q_x-p_x)\\ &=\left|\sum_{x\in A}p_x-\sum_{x\in A}q_x\right| +\left|\sum_{x\notin A}p_x-\sum_{x\notin A}q_x\right|\\ &=|a-b|+|(1-a)-(1-b)|. \end{aligned}

The last equality uses that each probability vector has total mass one.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Separate positive and negative differences and use the two total sums equal to one. School finite sums and absolute values suffice; no measure-theoretic total variation is used.
