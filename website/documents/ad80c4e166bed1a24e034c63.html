Statement

Let a<b be real. Let f be bounded and continuous on a\le\Re z\le b, holomorphic in the interior, and put M(x)=\sup_{y\in\mathbb R}|f(x+iy)|. For x=ta+(1-t)b with 0<t<1, one has M(x)\le M(a)^tM(b)^{1-t}. At t=0,1 the corresponding endpoint bound is equality. Wherever M is positive, \log M(x) is convex.

Proof 1: Boundary normalization and Gaussian damping (outline)

First suppose M(a),M(b)>0. Define F(z) by

F(z)=f(z)M(a)^{(z-b)/(b-a)}M(b)^{(z-a)/(a-b)},

using real logarithms for the positive bases. Then |F(z)|\leq1 on the edges of the strip. The result follows once it is shown that the inequality also holds in the interior of the strip. After an affine transformation in the coordinate z, it can be assumed that a=0 and b=1. The function

F_n(z)=F(z)e^{z^2/n}e^{-1/n}

tends to 0 as |z| tends to infinity in the strip and satisfies |F_n|\leq1 on the boundary of the strip. The maximum modulus principle can therefore be applied to F_n on sufficiently tall finite rectangles in the strip: boundedness of F and \Re(z^2)=x^2-y^2 make |F_n|\leq1 on their horizontal edges as well. So |F_n(z)|\leq1. Because F_n(z) tends to F(z) as n tends to infinity, it follows that |F(z)|\leq1.

Undoing the normalization gives M(x)\leq M(a)^tM(b)^{1-t}. If an endpoint maximum is zero, use the positive bounds M(a)+\varepsilon and M(b)+\varepsilon in the same construction and let \varepsilon\downarrow0. Applying the inequality to every substrip gives the stated logarithmic convexity.

Level justification

Normalize the strip bounds, damp by a Gaussian exponential, apply maximum modulus on expanding rectangles and remove the damping. Retain beyond undergraduate provisionally for the general holomorphic-strip maximum-modulus argument and boundary limiting framework.
