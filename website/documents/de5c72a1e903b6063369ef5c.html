Statement

If a nonnegative integer n is a sum of three integer squares, then n is not of the form 4^a(8b+7) with integers a,b\ge0.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement uses only integer arithmetic, squares and divisibility.

Proof 1: Square residues and repeated division by four (outline)

Squares modulo 8 are 0,1,4, and no sum of three of these residues is 7 modulo 8. To apply this observation to 4^a(8b+7), use the elementary reduction implicit in the source: if x^2+y^2+z^2 is divisible by 4, then each of x,y,z is even, since squares modulo 4 are 0 or 1. Dividing the three squares by 4 and repeating a times would therefore give a representation of 8b+7 by three squares, contrary to the modulo-8 observation.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Use square residues modulo four to force all three variables even and divide repeatedly, then use the modulo-eight obstruction. School modular arithmetic and finite descent suffice.
