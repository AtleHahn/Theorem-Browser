Statement

For 0\le p,q\le1, let D_2(p\Vert q)=p\log_2(p/q)+(1-p)\log_2((1-p)/(1-q)), with zero-mass terms zero and positive mass divided by zero giving +\infty. Then D_2(p\Vert q)\ge\frac{2(p-q)^2}{\ln2}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Relative entropy or exponential-family parametrization is a college probability/statistics concept; the explicit formula makes the statement accessible without graduate probability. A bright US college student should be able to understand the statement after about 30 minutes of focused background research; this rating concerns understanding the claim, not proving it.

Proof 1: Differentiate with respect to the second probability — outline

For interior probabilities 0<p,q<1, we assume {\displaystyle p\geq q} (other case is similar), and let

{\displaystyle f(p,q)=p\log_2 {\frac {p}{q}}+(1-p)\log_2 {\frac {1-p}{1-q}}-{\frac {1}{2\ln 2}}(2(p-q))^{2}.}

Since

{\displaystyle {\frac {\partial f}{\partial q}}=-{\frac {p-q}{\ln 2}}\left({\frac {1}{q(1-q)}}-4\right)\leq 0,}

and {\displaystyle f=0} when {\displaystyle q=p}, we conclude that {\displaystyle f(p,q)\geq 0} where {\displaystyle q\leq p.} For the two-point distributions P=(p,1-p) and Q=(q,1-q), with D(P\Vert Q)=D_2(p\Vert q) and 1-norm distance 2|p-q|, we have that {\displaystyle D(P||Q)\geq {\tfrac {1}{2\ln 2}}||P-Q||_{1}^{2}} for this special case.

Endpoint cases follow by limits and the extended-divergence convention in the statement.

Endpoint cases use elementary logarithmic limits and the stated infinity convention. Ordinary differential calculus suffices; no general information theory or measure theory is used.
