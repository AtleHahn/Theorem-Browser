Statement

Let ABC be a nondegenerate equilateral triangle in the Euclidean plane and P any point in that plane. The lengths PA,PB,PC are the side lengths of a possibly degenerate triangle.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement concerns lengths between points in an elementary Euclidean plane; rotations and congruence are proof methods.

Proof 1: Rotate the point through sixty degrees

Consider a rotation of 60^\circ about the point B. Assume A maps to C, and P maps to P'. Then PB=P'B, and, if P\ne B, \angle PBP'=60^\circ. Hence triangle PBP' is equilateral and PP'=PB. If P=B, the same equality holds with both sides zero. Then PA=P'C. Thus, triangle PCP' has sides equal to PA, PB, and PC and the proof by construction is complete, allowing collinearity or coincident vertices as degeneracies.
