Statement

Let F be continuous on \{z:\operatorname{Im}z\ge0\}, holomorphic on \{z:\operatorname{Im}z>0\}, and real valued on the real axis. The function equal to F(z) in the closed upper half-plane and to \overline{F(\overline z)} in the lower half-plane is holomorphic on \mathbb C.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex differentiability extends the calculus derivative; limits, compact sets, uniform approximation and zeros of functions are familiar analysis. Zero multiplicity is the order of the first nonzero Taylor coefficient, and circle integrals are parametrized ordinary integrals. These limited definitions suffice for the full claim.

Proof 1: Reflection and split contours via Morera — outline

The reflected definition agrees along the real axis because the boundary values are real. Apply Morera’s theorem to the extension: split contour integrals into their upper and lower parts using segments of the real axis.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Glue the conjugate functions continuously and split triangle contours across the real boundary before applying Morera. Undergraduate complex analysis suffices.
