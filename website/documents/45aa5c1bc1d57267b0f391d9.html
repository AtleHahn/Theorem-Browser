Statement

Let ABCD be a convex quadrilateral with consecutive side lengths a,b,c,d. Let m be the distance between the midpoints of sides a,c, let n be the distance between the midpoints of sides b,d, and let x be the distance between the two diagonal midpoints. Then m=\frac12\sqrt{2(b^2+d^2)-4x^2} and n=\frac12\sqrt{2(a^2+c^2)-4x^2}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All quantities are elementary Euclidean lengths and real square roots.

Proof 1: Substitute Euler’s quadrilateral theorem

In a convex quadrilateral with sides a, b, c and d, the length of the bimedian that connects the midpoints of the sides a and c is

{\displaystyle m={\tfrac {1}{2}}{\sqrt {-a^{2}+b^{2}-c^{2}+d^{2}+p^{2}+q^{2}}}}

where p and q are the length of the diagonals. The length of the bimedian that connects the midpoints of the sides b and d is

{\displaystyle n={\tfrac {1}{2}}{\sqrt {a^{2}-b^{2}+c^{2}-d^{2}+p^{2}+q^{2}}}.}

Use Euler’s quadrilateral theorem, a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2=p^2+q^2+4x^2.

The lengths of the bimedians can also be expressed in terms of two opposite sides and the distance x between the midpoints of the diagonals. This is possible when using Euler's quadrilateral theorem in the above formulas. Whence

{\displaystyle m={\tfrac {1}{2}}{\sqrt {2(b^{2}+d^{2})-4x^{2}}}}

and

{\displaystyle n={\tfrac {1}{2}}{\sqrt {2(a^{2}+c^{2})-4x^{2}}}.}

The two opposite sides in these formulas are not the two that the bimedian connects.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Both imported geometric identities are explicit equations among side, diagonal and midpoint distances. Substituting Euler’s quadrilateral identity into the two bimedian formulas uses only elementary geometry and square-root algebra.
