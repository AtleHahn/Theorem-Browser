Statement

Let q,v range over an open domain in \mathbb R^n. Let L(q,v) be smooth and independent of time, and let q(t) satisfy \frac d{dt}L_v=L_q. Then H=L_v\cdot\dot q-L is constant.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves multivariable calculus; Euler–Lagrange equations. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Time-translation specialization

Use the mechanical form of Noether's theorem: for an action symmetry t'=t+\varepsilon T, q'(t')=\varphi(q(t),\varepsilon) with \varphi(q,0)=q, the expression

\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q}\cdot\dot q-L\right)T -\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q}\cdot \left.\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial\varepsilon}\right|_{\varepsilon=0}

is conserved.

If the Lagrangian does not depend explicitly on time, time translation is such a symmetry with T=1 and \varphi(q,\varepsilon)=q. Thus the quantity

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q_i}\dot q_i-L

(called the Hamiltonian) is conserved.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The mechanical Noether formula is supplied. For this application, the action is the ordinary integral of L(q,qdot); translating time preserves it because L has no explicit time dependence. Set T=1 and phi(q,epsilon)=q, whose epsilon derivative is zero. Multivariable derivatives, ordinary integration and the displayed Euler–Lagrange equations suffice; no general Lie-group or field-theoretic Noether framework is needed.
