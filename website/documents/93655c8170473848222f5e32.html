Statement

Let G be a connected noncompact real semisimple Lie group with finite center and K a maximal compact subgroup. Let s=s_{\alpha_1}\cdots s_{\alpha_m} be a reduced word in simple reflections of the restricted Weyl group. At common regular parameters, c_s(\lambda)=\prod_{j=1}^m c_{s_{\alpha_j}}(s_{\alpha_{j+1}}\cdots s_{\alpha_m}\lambda). Thus the scalar for the longest element, Harish–Chandra’s c-function, is determined by the rank-one scalars. Here c_w(\lambda) is the scalar by which the standard spherical intertwiner A(w,\lambda) acts on the constant K-fixed vector in the compact realization of the spherical principal series; the final factor uses the empty product as the identity.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Proof 1: Rank-one reduction along a reduced Weyl word (outline)

The multiplicative property

c_{s_1s_2}(\lambda)=c_{s_1}(s_2\lambda)c_{s_2}(\lambda), \qquad \ell(s_1s_2)=\ell(s_1)+\ell(s_2),

reduces the computation of c_s to the case when s=s_\alpha, the reflection in a simple root \alpha, the so-called “rank-one reduction” of Gindikin and Karpelevich. For the reduced word in the statement, applying this property successively gives the displayed product.

In fact the integral involves only the closed connected subgroup G^\alpha corresponding to the Lie subalgebra generated by the restricted root spaces \mathfrak g_{\pm\alpha}. Then G^\alpha is a real semisimple Lie group with real rank one, and c_{s_\alpha} is just the Harish–Chandra c-function of G^\alpha. By Harish–Chandra’s integral formula, the unique Weyl element s_0 of greatest length corresponds to Harish–Chandra’s c-function:

c(\lambda)=c_{s_0}(\lambda).
