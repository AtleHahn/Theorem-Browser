Statement

A nondegenerate square in the Euclidean plane cannot be dissected into an odd finite number of triangles of equal area. A dissection is a finite collection of nondegenerate closed triangles with pairwise disjoint interiors whose union is the square; the triangles need not meet edge-to-edge, and their vertices may have arbitrary real coordinates.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The claim needs elementary Euclidean geometry, area and parity; the advanced valuation proof is not a statement prerequisite.

Proof 1: A valuation coloring and boundary parity (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Sections2–5, especially Theorems4.1–4.2, Lemma5.1 and Theorem5.2).

Normalize the square by a similarity, including a rotation if necessary. Two details complete the argument for the stated generality. In Theorem 4.1 localize the maximal pair (O,M) at M itself: (O_M,MO_M) dominates it, so maximality gives O=O_M. Chevalley’s theorem then supplies an ordered-group-valued valuation v on \mathbb R with v(2)>0 and v(q)=v_2(q)v(2) for rational q\ne0. No real-valued absolute-value extension is needed: use the equivalent colors v(x),v(y)>0; v(x)\le0,\ v(x)\le v(y); and v(y)\le0,\ v(y)<v(x). The determinant calculation gives v(A)\le-v(2)<0 for a tricolored triangle.

For a dissection with T-junctions, split edges at all meeting vertices only for the parity count. The same determinant argument shows that a line cannot contain three colors. Consequently the parity of 12-subedges on each original edge equals whether its original endpoints have colors1 and2. Interior subedges cancel in pairs, while the square boundary has odd parity. An original triangle is therefore tricolored, contradicting A=1/m for odd m. No subdivision into equal-area triangles is assumed.
