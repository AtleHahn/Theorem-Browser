Statement

Let A_1,\ldots,A_m be events in a probability space. Let N count those that occur, and put S_k=\sum_{J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\},\ |J|=k}\Pr(\cap_{j\in J}A_j), with S_0=1. For 0\le n\le m, \Pr(N=n)=\sum_{k=n}^m(-1)^{k-n}\binom kn S_k.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The formula counts which of finitely many events occur, using intersections, probabilities, binomial coefficients and finite sums. These are elementary finite probability and combinatorics. This rating concerns understanding the statement, independently of the difficulty of its proof.

Proof 1: Choose a single nonzero sequence entry

Use the probabilistic Schuette–Nesbitt formula

\mathbb E[c_N]=\sum_{k=0}^mS_k(\Delta^kc)_0.

Here E is the shift operator (Ec)_j=c_{j+1}, I is the identity operator, and \Delta=E-I. By the binomial theorem,

\Delta^k=(E-I)^k=\sum_{j=0}^k\binom kj(-1)^{k-j}E^j,\qquad k\in\mathbb N_0.

Applying this operator identity to the sequence c=(0,\ldots,0,1,0,0,\ldots) with n leading zeros and noting that (E^jc)_0=1 if j=n and (E^jc)_0=0 otherwise, the formula

\mathbb P(N=n)=\sum_{k=n}^m(-1)^{k-n}\binom knS_k

follows from the probabilistic Schuette–Nesbitt formula.
