Statement

Write \Gamma(t)=\int_0^\infty u^{t-1}e^{-u}\,du for real t>0, \zeta(t)=\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-t} for real t>1, and \gamma=\lim_{N\to\infty}(\sum_{n=1}^N1/n-\log N). For complex s with 0<\operatorname{Re}s<1, use the convergent Dirichlet series definition of \zeta(2-s) and the real logarithm in x^{s-1}. Then \int_0^\infty x^{s-1}\frac{\gamma x+\log\Gamma(1+x)}{x^2}\,dx=\frac\pi{\sin(\pi s)}\frac{\zeta(2-s)}{2-s}.

Proof 1: Weierstrass expansion and the master theorem — outline

Weierstrass's definition of the gamma function

{\displaystyle \Gamma (x)={\frac {\,e^{-\gamma \,x\,}}{x}}\,\prod _{n=1}^{\infty }\left(\,1+{\frac {x}{n}}\,\right)^{-1}e^{x/n}\!}

gives, for real |x|<1, the expression

{\displaystyle \log \Gamma (1+x)=-\gamma \,x+\sum _{k=2}^{\infty }{\frac {\,\zeta (k)\,}{k}}\,(-x)^{k}}

where {\textstyle \zeta (k)} is the Riemann zeta function.

Then applying Ramanujan master theorem we have:

{\displaystyle \int _{0}^{\infty }x^{s-1}{\frac {\,\gamma \,x+\log \Gamma (1+x)\,}{x^{2}}}\mathrm {d} x={\frac {\pi }{\sin(\pi s)}}{\frac {\zeta (2-s)}{2-s}}\!}

valid for {\textstyle 0<\operatorname {\mathcal {Re}} (s)<1}.

Here the product is used for x>0 and the series near 0. The source applies Ramanujan's master theorem without displaying the coefficient reindexing or verifying its analytic hypotheses.
