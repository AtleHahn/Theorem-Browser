Statement

For complex x_1,\ldots,x_n, put s_k=\sum_i x_i^k and write \prod_i(t-x_i)=\sum_{k=0}^n(-1)^k a_k t^{n-k}. Then a_0=1 and a_k=k^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^k(-1)^{i-1}a_{k-i}s_i for 1\le k\le n.

Proof 1: Identify the root coefficients and apply Newton’s recurrence

The polynomial with roots x_i may be expanded as

\prod_{i=1}^n(t-x_i)=\sum_{k=0}^n(-1)^k e_k t^{n-k},

where e_k=e_k(x_1,\ldots,x_n) is the elementary symmetric polynomial of degree k: the sum of all products of k distinct variables, with e_0=1. Thus a_k=e_k.

Given the power sums of the roots

s_k=\sum_{i=1}^n x_i^k,

the coefficients of the polynomial with roots x_1,\ldots,x_n may be expressed recursively in terms of the power sums by Newton's identities:

k a_k=\sum_{i=1}^k(-1)^{i-1}a_{k-i}s_i.

For the first values of k (up to n), this gives

\begin{aligned} a_0&=1,\\ -a_1&=-s_1,\\ a_2&=\frac12(a_1s_1-s_2),\\ -a_3&=-\frac13(a_2s_1-a_1s_2+s_3),\\ a_4&=\frac14(a_3s_1-a_2s_2+a_1s_3-s_4),\\ &\ \vdots \end{aligned}
