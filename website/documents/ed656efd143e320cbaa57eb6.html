Statement

For a non-increasing sequence a_n\ge0 of real numbers, \sum_{n\ge1}a_n converges if and only if \sum_{k\ge0}2^k a_{2^k} converges.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Nonnegative real series; Convergence and divergence; Monotone sequences; Powers of two. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Substitute powers of two in the general criterion

Schlömilch’s condensation criterion is used as an external theorem: if a_n\ge0 is non-increasing and u(0),u(1),\ldots are strictly increasing positive integers with \Delta u(k)=u(k+1)-u(k) and \Delta u(k)/\Delta u(k-1) bounded for k\ge1, then \sum_{n\ge1}a_n and \sum_{k\ge0}\Delta u(k)a_{u(k)} converge together. Take u(k)=2^k. Then \Delta u(k)=2^k and the successive difference ratio is 2, so the criterion gives exactly the asserted equivalence.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Substitute u(k)=2 to the k into the fully supplied real-series criterion and compute its consecutive difference ratio as two. Understanding nonnegative series convergence is introductory analysis; no further theory is needed.
