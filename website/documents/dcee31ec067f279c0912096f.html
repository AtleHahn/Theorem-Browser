Statement

Let G be a connected domain inside the unit circle, with boundary consisting of that circle and finitely many disjoint analytic slits and isolated points, and let a\in G. Suppose F_0,F_1 are meromorphic conformal bijections from G onto horizontal slit domains in the Riemann sphere (the complements of finitely many horizontal segments and points), both with their only pole at a, and with Laurent expansions F_j(w)=(w-a)^{-1}+O(w-a) there. Then F_0=F_1. Boundary traces are understood on the two sides of each slit; the standard boundary-extension theorem for these analytic slit domains supplies continuous traces, taking each boundary component into its corresponding horizontal segment or point.

Proof 1: Compare the boundary traces of the two normalized maps — outline

Write the two normalized uniformizers as

F_0(w)=(w-a)^{-1}+a_1(w-a)+a_2(w-a)^2+\cdots,F_1(w)=(w-a)^{-1}+b_1(w-a)+b_2(w-a)^2+\cdots.

Let C_i denote the boundary components of G, including the unit circle. The images under F_0 or F_1 of each C_i have a fixed y-coordinate, so are horizontal segments or points. On the other hand,

F_2(w)=F_0(w)-F_1(w)

is holomorphic in G, including at a. If it is constant, then it must be identically zero since F_2(a)=0.

Suppose F_2 is nonconstant. Then F_2(C_i) are all contained in horizontal lines. For every t not in one of these finitely many lines, Cauchy's argument principle shows that the number of solutions of F_2(w)=t in G is zero, using contours in G close to the boundary components. Thus the image is contained in finitely many horizontal lines. This contradicts the fact that the nonconstant holomorphic function F_2 is an open mapping.

Cancel Laurent principal parts, control separate slit-side boundary traces and exhaust the multiply connected domain to count zeros. Boundary extension for analytic slit domains and the associated contour limiting argument require advanced conformal mapping theory.
