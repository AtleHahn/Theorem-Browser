Statement

Let Q(x,y)=ax^2+bxy+cy^2 have integer coefficients. For integers \alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta with \alpha\delta-\beta\gamma=\pm1, define Q'(x',y')=Q(\alpha x'+\beta y',\gamma x'+\delta y'). Then Q' has the same discriminant as Q, where the discriminant is b^2-4ac, and the two forms represent exactly the same integers. Their middle coefficients also have the same parity.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The quadratic coefficients, discriminant and determinant-one substitution are explicitly finite integer polynomial formulas.

Proof 1: Substitution and its integral inverse — outline

Forms related by an integral substitution with \alpha\delta-\beta\gamma=\pm1 are called equivalent. Equivalent forms are readily seen to have the same discriminant, and hence also the same parity for the middle coefficient b, which coincides with the parity of the discriminant. Moreover, equivalent forms will represent exactly the same integers, because these kinds of substitutions can be reversed by substitutions of the same kind.

The source leaves the coefficient calculation implicit.

High school only
Level justification

Expand the substituted quadratic, use determinant squared one for its discriminant and reverse the explicit integer linear substitution. School two-variable algebra and parity suffice.
