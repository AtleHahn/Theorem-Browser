Statement

Let C\subset\mathbb P^2_{\mathbb C} be a smooth projective plane curve and p\in C. Let I_C and I_p be their homogeneous ideals. Then X=\operatorname{Proj}(\mathbb C[x,y,z]/(I_C I_p)) has an embedded point at p, and is not Cohen–Macaulay.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Smooth projective plane curves; Homogeneous ideals and Proj; Embedded points; Cohen–Macaulay curves. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Multiply the point and curve ideals — outline

Given a smooth projective plane curve {\displaystyle C\subset \mathbb {P} ^{2}}, a curve with an embedded point can be constructed by multiplying ideals: find the ideal {\displaystyle I_{p}} of a point {\displaystyle p\in C} and multiply it with the ideal {\displaystyle I_{C}} of {\displaystyle C}. Then

{\displaystyle X={\text{Proj}}\left({\frac {\mathbb {C} [x,y,z]}{I_{C}\cdot I_{p}}}\right)}

is a curve with an embedded point at {\displaystyle p}.

A locally Noetherian scheme of dimension at most one is Cohen–Macaulay if and only if it has no embedded primes, so this curve is not Cohen–Macaulay. The source supplies the ideal-product construction without expanding its embedded-point verification.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Form the projective scheme from the ideal product and use an embedded-prime Cohen-Macaulay criterion. Proj, embedded associated primes and local depth are substantial graduate algebraic geometry.
