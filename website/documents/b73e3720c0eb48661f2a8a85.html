Statement

Let X be a quasi-compact scheme with structure sheaf \mathcal O_X. Suppose that

H^1(X,\mathcal I)=0

for every quasi-coherent sheaf of ideals \mathcal I\subseteq\mathcal O_X. Then X is affine: it is isomorphic as a scheme to \operatorname{Spec}A for a commutative ring A.

Here quasi-compact means that every open cover has a finite subcover. A quasi-coherent ideal sheaf is an ideal subsheaf of \mathcal O_X that, on each affine open \operatorname{Spec}R, is the sheaf associated to an ideal of R. The group H^1(X,\mathcal I) is the first sheaf cohomology group, computed as the first right-derived functor of global sections.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Schemes, quasi-coherence and derived-functor cohomology are cumulative graduate prerequisites even with concise definitions; elementary point-set compactness alone is insufficient.
