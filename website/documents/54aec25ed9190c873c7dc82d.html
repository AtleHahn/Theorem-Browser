Statement

Let d\ge1 be an integer and S\subseteq\mathbb R^d. The conical hull is the set of all finite sums \sum_iw_iq_i with q_i\in S and w_i\ge0. Every point of the conical hull has such a representation with at most d nonzero terms (the empty sum is zero).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves finite-dimensional coordinate vectors; finite linear combinations; nonnegative coefficients. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Finite coefficient elimination — outline

Finite conical lemma

For q_1,\ldots,q_N\in\mathbb R^d and x=\sum_{i=1}^Nw_iq_i with w_i\ge0, there is such a representation with at most d nonzero coefficients.

This is trivial when {\displaystyle N\leq d}. If we can prove it for all {\displaystyle N=d+1}, then by induction we have proved it for all {\displaystyle N\geq d+1}. Thus it remains to prove it for {\displaystyle N=d+1}. This we prove by induction on {\displaystyle d}.

Base case: {\displaystyle d=1,N=2}, trivial.

Induction case. Represent {\displaystyle x=\sum _{n=1}^{d+1}w_{n}q_{n}}. If some {\displaystyle w_{n}=0}, then the proof is finished. So assume all {\displaystyle w_{n}>0}

If {\displaystyle \{q_{1},...,q_{d}\}} is linearly dependent, then we can use induction on its linear span to eliminate one nonzero term in {\displaystyle \sum _{n=1}^{d}{\frac {w_{n}}{w_{1}+\cdots +w_{d}}}q_{n}}, and thus eliminate it in {\displaystyle x=\sum _{n=1}^{d+1}w_{n}q_{n}} as well.

Else, there exists {\displaystyle (u_{1},...,u_{d})\in \mathbb {R} ^{d}}, such that {\displaystyle \sum _{n=1}^{d}u_{n}q_{n}=q_{d+1}}. Then we can interpolate a full interval of representations:

{\displaystyle x=\sum _{n=1}^{d}(w_{n}+\theta w_{d+1}u_{n})q_{n}+(1-\theta )w_{d+1}q_{d+1};\quad \theta \in [0,1]}

If {\displaystyle w_{n}+w_{d+1}u_{n}\geq 0} for all {\displaystyle n=1,...,d}, then set {\displaystyle \theta =1}. Otherwise, let {\displaystyle \theta } be the smallest {\displaystyle \theta } such that one of {\displaystyle w_{n}+\theta w_{d+1}u_{n}=0}. Then we obtain a representation of {\displaystyle x} as a linear combination with non-negative coefficients, with at most {\displaystyle d} nonzero terms.

Passage to the conical hull

Take any {\displaystyle x\in \mathrm {Cone} (S)}, represent {\displaystyle x=\sum _{n=1}^{N}w_{n}q_{n}} for some {\displaystyle q_{1},...,q_{N}\in S} and {\displaystyle w_{n}\geq 0}, then {\displaystyle x\in \mathrm {Cone} (\{q_{1},...,q_{N}\})}, and we use the lemma.
