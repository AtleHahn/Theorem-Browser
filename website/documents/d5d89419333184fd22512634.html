Statement

Let G be a connected solvable linear algebraic group over an algebraically closed field k, and let \rho:G\to GL(V) be a rational representation on a nonzero finite-dimensional k-vector space. There is a basis of V in which every \rho(g) is upper triangular.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves linear algebraic groups; connected solvable groups; rational representations; simultaneous triangularization. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Induct on the quotient by an invariant line — outline

Apply the invariant-line form of Lie–Kolchin to obtain L\subset V. The source proceeds by induction: apply the same statement to the quotient representation on V/L and induct on dimension to obtain a complete invariant flag. Lifting that flag and adjoining L gives a complete invariant flag of V. A basis adapted to it makes every matrix upper triangular.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Pass to the quotient rational representation, induct and lift the invariant flag. The induction is linear algebra, but rational representations of connected solvable algebraic groups are graduate prerequisites.
