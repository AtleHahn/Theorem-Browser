Statement

Let n\ge1, 1<p<\infty, u\in C_c^1(\mathbb R^n), and let t>0 be a regular value with nonempty level surface S=\{u=t\}. Set A=\mathcal H^{n-1}(S) and B=\int_S|\nabla u|^{-1}\,d\mathcal H^{n-1}. Then \int_S|\nabla u|^{p-1}\,d\mathcal H^{n-1}\ge A^p/B^{p-1}. Regular means \nabla u\ne0 on S.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The regular surface and its Hausdorff integrals require geometric measure theory.

Proof 1: A lower energy bound on a regular level set — proof

On the regular level surface in the statement, we have by Hölder's inequality,

{\displaystyle {\mathcal {H}}^{n-1}\left(u^{-1}(\{t\})\right)\leq \left(\int _{u^{-1}(\{t\})}|\nabla u|^{p-1}\right)^{\frac {1}{p}}\left(\int _{u^{-1}(\{t\})}{\frac {1}{|\nabla u|}}\right)^{1-{\frac {1}{p}}}.}

Therefore, we have

{\displaystyle \int _{u^{-1}(\{t\})}|\nabla u|^{p-1}\geq {\frac {{\mathcal {H}}^{n-1}\left(u^{-1}(\{t\})\right)^{p}}{\left(\int _{u^{-1}(\{t\})}{\frac {1}{|\nabla u|}}\right)^{p-1}}}.}

All surface integrals here are with respect to \mathcal H^{n-1}; the two quantities in the quotient are A and B as defined above.
