Statement

Let X be a countably infinite set and f:X\to\mathbb R. If for one bijection g:\mathbb Z^+\to X the series \sum_{n=1}^\infty f(g(n)) converges absolutely, its sum is the same for every such bijection.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Absolute convergence, enumerations and rearrangements are standard real-analysis and set concepts. The statement simply expresses enumeration invariance on a countable index set.

Proof 1: Bijections differ by rearrangement

We use rearrangement invariance of absolutely convergent real series: every permutation of such a series converges to the same sum.

Note that because the series is absolutely convergent, then every rearrangement is identical to a different choice of bijection {\displaystyle g.} Since all of these sums have the same value, then the sum of {\displaystyle f} over {\displaystyle X} is well-defined.

Level justification

Two enumerations differ by a permutation, and the supplied absolute-convergence rearrangement theorem preserves the sum. Basic series and bijections suffice.
