Statement

Let \Gamma be a rectifiable simple closed curve in \mathbb R^2, of length L, and let D be the bounded domain it encloses, of area A. Let r be the radius of a largest disk contained in \overline D, and let R be the radius of a smallest disk containing \overline D. Then

\pi^2(R-r)^2\le L^2-4\pi A.

A simple closed curve is the image of a continuous injective map of the circle; rectifiable means it has finite length, defined as the supremum of lengths of inscribed polygonal approximations. Area is planar Lebesgue measure. Equivalently, r is the supremum of radii of disks contained in \overline D, and R is the infimum of radii of disks containing \overline D.

Review notes

  • Classical quantitative strengthening of the isoperimetric inequality, controlling the difference of extremal radii.

Proof 1: Inner and outer radius inequalities (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (American Mathematical Monthly 86 (1979), 1–29; Part I.A, Theorems 2–4 and proofs, pp. 3–6; Part II.A, pp. 13–15, explains nonconvex scope.).

Theorems 2 and 3 apply to rectifiable Jordan domains, including nonconvex ones, and give rL\ge A+\pi r^2 and RL\ge A+\pi R^2. Both radii therefore lie between the real roots of \pi t^2-Lt+A. Their separation is \sqrt{L^2-4\pi A}/\pi, proving the bound. The nonconvex steps use inner parallel domains, convex hulls and polygonal approximation.
