Statement

For n\ge1, 1\le p<\infty, and x,y\in\mathbb C^n, (\sum_{k=1}^n|x_k+y_k|^p)^{1/p}\le(\sum_{k=1}^n|x_k|^p)^{1/p}+(\sum_{k=1}^n|y_k|^p)^{1/p}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The displayed finite numerical inequality uses complex absolute values, finite sums and real powers; no vector-space theory is needed to understand it. This rating concerns understanding the statement, independently of the difficulty of its proof.

Proof 1: Specialize to a finite counting measure

The L^p Minkowski inequality is \|f+g\|_p\leq\|f\|_p+\|g\|_p, where \|f\|_p=(\int|f|^p\,d\mu)^{1/p}.

The Minkowski inequality can be specialized to sequences and vectors by using the counting measure on S=\{1,\ldots,n\}:

\left(\sum_{k=1}^n|x_k+y_k|^p\right)^{1/p} \leq\left(\sum_{k=1}^n|x_k|^p\right)^{1/p} +\left(\sum_{k=1}^n|y_k|^p\right)^{1/p}.
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Interpret the supplied integral norm inequality on a finite counting space, where every integral is a finite sum. Undergraduate finite-dimensional norms and this elementary counting-measure interpretation suffice; general Lebesgue-space theory is unnecessary.
