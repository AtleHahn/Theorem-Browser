Statement

Let X,Y be independent real random variables. If X+Y and X-Y are independent, then X and Y have Gaussian distributions (allowing degenerate Gaussian laws).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Independence of finitely many scalar random variables and Gaussian laws, including constants, are a limited probability extension of undergraduate analysis and linear algebra. No stochastic convergence is required.

Proof 1: Specialize to the sum and difference — outline

The sum and difference of two independent variables form the Kac–Bernstein case of the Darmois–Skitovich theorem: they are the two linear forms in that theorem with nonzero coefficients 1,1 and 1,-1. The theorem therefore makes both variables Gaussian.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the supplied characterization with the two coefficient pairs (1,1) and (1,-1). Undergraduate probability, independence and Gaussian distributions suffice; the advanced proof of Darmois–Skitovich is not invoked.
