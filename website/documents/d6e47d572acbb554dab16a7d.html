Statement

Let f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb C be 2\pi-periodic and Lebesgue integrable over a period. Define

c_k=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(t)e^{-ikt}\,dt,\qquad S_nf(x)=\sum_{k=-n}^n c_ke^{ikx}.

If f has bounded variation in a neighborhood of x, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}S_nf(x)=\frac{f(x+)+f(x-)}2,

where f(x+) and f(x-) are the one-sided limits. In particular, if f is continuous at x, this limit is f(x).

If f has bounded variation over a period, the displayed convergence holds at every x. If it is also continuous everywhere, the convergence of S_nf to f is uniform.

Bounded variation on an interval [a,b] means that the supremum of

\sum_{j=1}^m|f(t_j)-f(t_{j-1})|

over all finite partitions a=t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_m=b is finite. One-sided limits at period endpoints are taken using the periodic extension.
