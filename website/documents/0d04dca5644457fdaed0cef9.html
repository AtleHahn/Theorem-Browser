Statement

A simple undirected graph has a vertex set V and edges that are unordered pairs of distinct vertices. A proper k-coloring is a map V\to\{1,\ldots,k\} assigning different values to adjacent vertices. Assume choice and fix a positive integer k. If every finite subgraph of G is k-colorable, then on the same vertex set there is a supergraph M with this property such that adding any missing edge destroys it.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Graphs, finite subset colorings and infinite sequences are elementary discrete mathematics. Matchings are disjoint edges and a ray is an infinite nonrepeating path; the conditions are explicitly given. No graph-minor or set-theoretic independence machinery is needed.

Proof 1: Apply Zorn to graphs ordered by adding edges — outline

Apply Zorn’s lemma to the graphs on the fixed vertex set extending G for which every finite subgraph is k-colorable, ordered by adding edges. This gives a maximal such graph M.

Level justification

Apply the supplied Zorn principle to edge sets ordered by inclusion; a finite subgraph of a chain union lies in a single member. Arbitrary sets, partial orders and this concrete maximality argument are standard undergraduate set-theoretic tools.
