A Platonic solid and its concentric dual have the same symmetry group

Statement

A Platonic solid is a convex polyhedron in Euclidean three-space whose faces are congruent regular p-gons and at each vertex of which q faces meet. The Euclidean isometries preserving it are exactly those preserving its concentric dual. Thus their full symmetry groups, regarded as sets of Euclidean isometries, coincide.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Symmetries are explicitly the distance-preserving spatial maps preserving each solid; equality as sets of maps needs no abstract group theory.

Proof 1: Transfer symmetries through the dual construction — outline

The dual is constructed by taking the centers of the original faces as vertices and connecting centers of adjacent faces. The symmetry group of a Platonic solid coincides with that of its concentric dual. This is easily seen by examining the construction of the dual polyhedron. Any symmetry of the original must be a symmetry of the dual and vice versa. For the reverse direction, dualizing again recovers the original up to uniform scale about their common center.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Symmetries preserve face centers and adjacency, and dualizing again recovers the solid up to a uniform central scale. School spatial symmetries suffice.
