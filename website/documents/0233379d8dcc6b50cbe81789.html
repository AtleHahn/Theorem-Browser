Statement

A partition of a nonnegative integer is a multiset of positive integers whose sum is that integer; order of parts is ignored. Let p(N,M;n) count partitions of n into at most M parts each at most N, where N,M\geq0 are integers, and put it equal to zero for n<0. For N,M\geq1 and n\geq0, p(N,M;n)=p(N,M-1;n)+p(N-1,M;n-M).

Level justification

Proof 1: Recurrence for partitions in a rectangle — proof

The difference p(N,M;n)-p(N,M-1;n) counts partitions having exactly M parts. Subtract 1 from each part and discard zeros. This gives partitions of n-M with at most M parts, each at most N-1. The operation is reversed by padding to M parts with zeros and adding 1 to each. Hence the difference is p(N-1,M;n-M).
