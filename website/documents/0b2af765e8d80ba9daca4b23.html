Statement

Let R be a real closed ordered field: every positive element is a square and every polynomial of odd degree over R has a root in R. Let F=\{f_1,\ldots,f_m\} and G=\{g_1,\ldots,g_r\} be finite sets in R[x_1,\ldots,x_n], and put

W=\{x\in R^n:f_j(x)\geq0\text{ for all }j,\ g_\ell(x)=0\text{ for all }\ell\}.

Define

P(F,G)=\left\{\sum_{\alpha\in\{0,1\}^m}\sigma_\alpha\prod_{j=1}^m f_j^{\alpha_j}+\sum_{\ell=1}^r h_\ell g_\ell\right\},

where each \sigma_\alpha is a finite sum of squares of polynomials and each h_\ell is an arbitrary polynomial over R. Empty sums are zero and empty products are one.

For every p\in R[x_1,\ldots,x_n], the condition p(x)\geq0 for every x\in W is equivalent to the existence of q_1,q_2\in P(F,G) and an integer s for which p^{2s} is defined in the polynomial ring and

q_1p=p^{2s}+q_2

is a polynomial identity.

Here nonnegative powers are ordinary polynomial powers, with p^0=1, including for the zero polynomial. Negative powers are interpreted using multiplicative inverses in the polynomial ring; thus s<0 is allowed only when p is a nonzero constant. This specifies the meaning of the integer exponent without using negative powers of a nonunit polynomial.

For the same R,F,G,W and every polynomial p, strict positivity has the companion certificate:

p(x)>0\text{ for every }x\in W \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \exists q_1,q_2\in P(F,G):\ q_1p=1+q_2.

The equality is an identity of polynomials, including when W is empty.
