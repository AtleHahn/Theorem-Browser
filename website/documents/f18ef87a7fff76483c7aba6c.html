Statement

Let G=(V,E) be a finite undirected simple graph, where edges join distinct vertices. A matching is a set of edges with no shared endpoints. Write \nu(G) for its largest possible size, and \operatorname{odd}(G-U) for the number of connected components of odd order after deleting a vertex subset U. For every U\subseteq V, \nu(G)\le\frac12\bigl(|V|+|U|-\operatorname{odd}(G-U)\bigr).

Level
High school only
Level justification

Matching, deleted-vertex components and their odd sizes are finite graph/counting notions supplied locally.

Proof 1: An unmatched vertex in each uncovered odd component

Intuitively, for any subset {\displaystyle U} of the vertices, the only way to completely cover an odd component of {\displaystyle G-U} by a matching is for one of the matched edges covering the component to be incident to {\displaystyle U}. If, instead, some odd component had no matched edge connecting it to {\displaystyle U}, then the part of the matching that covered the component would cover its vertices in pairs, but since the component has an odd number of vertices it would necessarily include at least one leftover and unmatched vertex. Therefore, if some choice of {\displaystyle U} has few vertices but its removal creates a large number of odd components, then there will be many unmatched vertices, implying that the matching itself will be small. This reasoning can be made precise by stating that the size of a maximum matching is at most equal to \frac12(|V|+|U|-\operatorname{odd}(G-U)).

Level
High school only
Level justification

Every odd component not matched into U leaves an unmatched vertex, and only |U| components can use such edges. Elementary parity and finite counting suffice.
