Statement

Let P be a polynomial with constant real coefficients, a\in\mathbb R, D=d/dx on an open real interval, and y a function differentiable to order at least \deg P. Then P(D)(e^{ax}y)=e^{ax}P(D+a)y, where D+a sends z to Dz+az.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves ordinary derivatives; exponential functions; constant-coefficient differential polynomials. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Induction on powers of differentiation

To prove the result, proceed by induction. Note that only the special case

{\displaystyle P(D)=D^{n}}

needs to be proved, since the general result then follows by linearity of D-operators.

The case n=0 is the identity. The result is clearly true for n = 1 since

{\displaystyle D(e^{ax}y)=e^{ax}(D+a)y.}

Now suppose the result true for n = k, that is,

{\displaystyle D^{k}(e^{ax}y)=e^{ax}(D+a)^{k}y.}

Then,

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}D^{k+1}(e^{ax}y)&\equiv {\frac {d}{dx}}\left\{e^{ax}\left(D+a\right)^{k}y\right\}\\&{}=e^{ax}{\frac {d}{dx}}\left\{\left(D+a\right)^{k}y\right\}+ae^{ax}\left\{\left(D+a\right)^{k}y\right\}\\&{}=e^{ax}\left\{\left({\frac {d}{dx}}+a\right)\left(D+a\right)^{k}y\right\}\\&{}=e^{ax}(D+a)^{k+1}y.\end{aligned}}}

This completes the proof.
