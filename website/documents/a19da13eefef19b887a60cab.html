Statement

Let I\subseteq\mathbb R be an interval and f:I\to\mathbb R continuous. If f' exists and is zero at every interior point of I, then f is constant on I.

Proof 1: Subtract the endpoint secant and apply Rolle

If a<b are two points of I, then the function

{\displaystyle g(x)=f(x)-f(a)-{\frac {f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}}(x-a)}

is continuous on [a,b], differentiable on (a,b), and satisfies the hypotheses of Rolle's theorem on the interval ⁠{\displaystyle [a,b]}⁠.

If the derivative of ⁠{\displaystyle f}⁠ is zero at every interior point of I, the derivative of ⁠{\displaystyle g}⁠ is

{\displaystyle g'(x)=-{\frac {f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}},}

and Rolle's theorem implies that there is ⁠{\displaystyle c\in (a,b)}⁠ such that

{\displaystyle 0=g'(c)=-{\frac {f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}}.}

Hence, ⁠{\displaystyle f(a)=f(b)}⁠ for every ⁠{\displaystyle a}⁠ and ⁠{\displaystyle b}⁠, and the function ⁠{\displaystyle f}⁠ is constant.
