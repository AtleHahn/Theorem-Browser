Statement

Let K\subset\mathbb C be compact with connected complement, and let f be holomorphic on a neighborhood of K. Then polynomials approximate f uniformly on K.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex differentiability extends the calculus derivative; limits, compact sets, uniform approximation and zeros of functions are familiar analysis. Zero multiplicity is the order of the first nonzero Taylor coefficient, and circle integrals are parametrized ordinary integrals. These limited definitions suffice for the full claim.

Proof 1: Place all allowed poles at infinity

Use the rational approximation theorem: a function holomorphic near a compact plane set can be uniformly approximated by rational functions whose poles lie in a chosen set meeting each component of its spherical complement. Since the complement of K is connected, choose only infinity. A rational function with no finite poles is a polynomial.
