Statement

If nonzero integers a,b satisfy a^2/b+b^2/a\in\mathbb Z, then both a^2/b and b^2/a are integers.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement uses two nonzero integers, their squared quotients and integrality of their sum.

Proof 1: Construct a monic integer quadratic

Consider the quadratic equation whose roots are {\displaystyle {\tfrac {a^{2}}{b}}} and {\displaystyle {\tfrac {b^{2}}{a}}}:

{\displaystyle (x-{\tfrac {a^{2}}{b}})(x-{\tfrac {b^{2}}{a}})=x^{2}-({\tfrac {a^{2}}{b}}+{\tfrac {b^{2}}{a}})x+{\tfrac {a^{2}}{b}}\cdot {\tfrac {b^{2}}{a}}.}

Simplify the coefficients:

  • The coefficient of {\displaystyle x} is {\displaystyle m=-({\tfrac {a^{2}}{b}}+{\tfrac {b^{2}}{a}}),}
  • The constant term is {\displaystyle n={\tfrac {a^{2}}{b}}\cdot {\tfrac {b^{2}}{a}}=ab.}

Thus, the equation becomes:

{\displaystyle x^{2}+mx+n=0,}

where:

  • {\displaystyle m=-({\tfrac {a^{2}}{b}}+{\tfrac {b^{2}}{a}})}, obviously integer, as negation of an integer,
  • {\displaystyle n=ab}, also integer, as the product of two integers.

Apply the rational root theorem:

{\displaystyle a,b} given to be integers ({\displaystyle a\neq 0,b\neq 0}), i.e. {\displaystyle {\tfrac {a^{2}}{b}}} and {\displaystyle {\tfrac {b^{2}}{a}}} are rational. If {\displaystyle {\tfrac {p}{q}}} is a rational root of the equation, written in lowest terms with positive denominator, then {\displaystyle q} is an integer factor of the {\displaystyle x^{2}} coefficient, i.e. of {\displaystyle 1}. Thus, {\displaystyle q=1}. Thus, the rational root is an integer. Thus, {\displaystyle {\tfrac {a^{2}}{b}}} and {\displaystyle {\tfrac {b^{2}}{a}}} are integers.
