Statement

Let X be a compact Hausdorff topological space. The following conditions are equivalent.

  1. There exist a totally ordered set L, compact in its order topology, and a continuous surjection f:L\to X. The order topology is generated by the open rays \{t\in L:t<a\} and \{t\in L:t>a\} for a\in L.
  2. X is monotonically normal: there is an assignment G sending every ordered pair (A,B) of disjoint closed subsets of X to an open subset G(A,B) of X such that
A\subseteq G(A,B)\subseteq\overline{G(A,B)}\subseteq X\setminus B,

and, for all such pairs (A,B),(A',B'),

A\subseteq A',\quad B'\subseteq B\quad\Longrightarrow\quad G(A,B)\subseteq G(A',B').

The closure is taken in X.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Undergraduate point-set topology supplies compactness, separation, continuity and closure. Order topology is explicitly generated by rays, and monotone normality adds one precisely stated neighborhood assignment to familiar normality. These are a small extension of that background within the thirty-minute reading allowance; no proof or set-theoretic topology machinery is needed.
