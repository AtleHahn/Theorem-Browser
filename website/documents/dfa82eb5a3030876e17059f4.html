Statement

Let p be a positive prime and m an integer with p\mid m^2+1. Then Q(x,y)=px^2+2mxy+((m^2+1)/p)y^2 is a positive definite integral binary quadratic form of discriminant -4 and represents p at (x,y)=(1,0). Positive definite means Q(x,y)>0 for every nonzero real pair (x,y), and the discriminant of ax^2+bxy+cy^2 is b^2-4ac.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The form and positivity condition are explicitly defined in elementary real polynomial terms.

Proof 1: Choose the leading and final coefficients — outline

One can use a form

px^2+2mxy+\frac{m^2+1}{p}y^2,

where the first coefficient a=p was chosen so that the form represents p by setting x=1, and y=0, the coefficient b=2m is an arbitrary even number (as it must be to get an even discriminant), and finally c=(m^2+1)/p is chosen so that the discriminant

b^2-4ac=4m^2-4pc=-4.

Of course the coefficient c must be an integer, so the problem is reduced to finding some integer m such that p divides m^2+1, as assumed here. Positive definiteness uses the usual criterion that the leading coefficient is positive and the discriminant negative.

High school only
Level justification

Compute the discriminant and evaluate at (1,0); use the elementary completed-square criterion for positivity. Quadratic algebra and integer divisibility suffice.
