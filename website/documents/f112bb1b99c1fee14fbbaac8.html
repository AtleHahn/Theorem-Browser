Statement

Let G be a group with identity e acting on a set X from the left, so ex=x and g(hx)=(gh)x. The relation x\sim y defined by y=gx for some g\in G is an equivalence relation; its classes are the orbits Gx=\{gx:g\in G\} and therefore partition X.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory group theory supplies subgroups, quotients, actions, products and homomorphisms. Perfect means generated by commutators, torsion means finite element orders, and the chain conditions are explicit sequences of subgroups. The relevant one of these closely related definitions is a small extension, without representation theory.

Proof 1: Orbits partition an acted-on set — outline

The defining properties of a group guarantee that the set of orbits of (points x in) X under the action of G form a partition of X. The associated equivalence relation is defined by saying x ~ y if and only if there exists a g in G with g⋅x = y. The orbits are then the equivalence classes under this relation; two elements x and y are equivalent if and only if their orbits are the same, that is, G⋅x = G⋅y.
