Statement

Let D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}, with planar area measure dS. Let L^2(D,dS) be the square-integrable complex functions, identified when equal almost everywhere, and let H_2 be its subspace of holomorphic functions.

For every F\in L^2(D,dS) there is a unique f\in H_2 minimizing

\iint_D|F(z)-f(z)|^2\,dS(z).

It is the orthogonal projection of F onto H_2 and is given by

f(z)=\frac1\pi\iint_D\frac{F(\zeta)}{(1-\overline\zeta z)^2}\,dS(\zeta),\qquad z\in D.

In particular every f\in H_2 satisfies the representation formula

f(z)=\frac1\pi\iint_D\frac{f(\zeta)}{(1-\overline\zeta z)^2}\,dS(\zeta).

Proof 1: Orthogonal projection onto the disk Bergman space (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Lemmas1.8–1.9, Proposition1.11, Theorem1.10, Lemma1.14 and Sections2.1–2.3, pp.3–9).

Apply Proposition 1.11 in L^2(D) to its closed subspace H_2 and write F=f+g with g\perp H_2. For each z, the source’s representing function is b_z(\zeta)=1/[\pi(1-\overline z\zeta)^2]\in H_2. Hence

f(z)=(f,b_z)=(F,b_z)=\frac1\pi\iint_D\frac{F(\zeta)}{(1-\overline\zeta z)^2}\,dS(\zeta).

The kernel is bounded as a function of \zeta for fixed z\in D, so the integral exists for every F\in L^2(D) and is independent of its almost-everywhere representative. Orthogonality gives \|F-h\|_2^2=\|g\|_2^2+\|f-h\|_2^2, proving unique minimality. Taking F\in H_2 gives the reproducing formula. In Lemma 1.9 the convex set must be nonempty and closed, as its proof uses; these hypotheses hold here.
