Statement

Graphs are finite and simple. A clique is a set of pairwise adjacent vertices; its maximum size is the clique number. A proper coloring assigns different colors to adjacent vertices; the least number of colors is the chromatic number. An induced subgraph keeps a vertex subset and all edges between its vertices. A graph is perfect if these two numbers agree for every induced subgraph. The complement joins exactly the distinct nonadjacent vertex pairs. A perfect graph has no induced odd cycle of length at least five and no induced subgraph complementary to such a cycle.

Proof 1: Compare the odd-cycle and complement color counts — outline

An odd hole means an induced odd-length cycle of length at least five; an odd antihole is its complement. An odd hole has clique number two but chromatic number three. An odd antihole on 2k+1 vertices, where k\ge2, has clique number k but chromatic number k+1. Neither can be an induced subgraph of a perfect graph, since perfection requires equality of these numbers for every induced subgraph.
