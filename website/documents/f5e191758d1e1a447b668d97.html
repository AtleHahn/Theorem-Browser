Statement

Fix R>0 and let z(\theta)=R^{-1}\cos\theta\,e^{i\theta} for -\pi/2<\theta<\pi/2. This parametrizes the nonzero points of the circle with diameter from 0 to 1/R. Then e^{1/z(\theta)}=e^R e^{-iR\tan\theta}; every value of modulus e^R occurs infinitely often as \theta\uparrow\pi/2, while z(\theta)\to0.

Proof 1: A tangent circle and unbounded angular winding — outline

Consider what happens when z takes values on the circle of diameter 1/R tangent to the imaginary axis at zero, where R>0. This circle is given in polar coordinates by r=(1/R)\cos\theta. Substituting in

f(z)=e^{1/z}=e^{\cos\theta/r}e^{-i\sin\theta/r},

we obtain

f(z)=e^R\left[\cos(R\tan\theta)-i\sin(R\tan\theta)\right]

and

|f(z)|=e^R.

As z\to0 on this circle from the upper half-plane, \theta\to\pi/2 with R fixed. So this part of the equation:

\left[\cos(R\tan\theta)-i\sin(R\tan\theta)\right]

takes on all values on the unit circle infinitely often, since R\tan\theta runs continuously without bound. Hence f(z) takes on every value of modulus e^R infinitely often as \theta\uparrow\pi/2.
