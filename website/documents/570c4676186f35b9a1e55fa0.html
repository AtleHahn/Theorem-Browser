Statement

For a bounded linear map T:X\to Y between real or complex Banach spaces, let T':Y'\to X' be T'y'=y'\circ T, where the primes denote continuous dual spaces with operator norms. Then T is surjective if and only if some \delta>0 satisfies \delta\|y'\|\le\|T'y'\| for every y'\in Y'.

Proof 1: Separation and the ball-image criterion — outline

Let B_X and B_Y denote the open unit balls. For \delta>0, consider the four statements:

  1. \delta\|y'\|\le\|T'y'\| for all y'\in Y';
  2. \delta B_Y\subset\overline{T(B_X)};
  3. \delta B_Y\subset T(B_X);
  4. T is surjective.

The idea of 1\Rightarrow2 is to show: y\notin\overline{T(B_X)}\Rightarrow\|y\|>\delta, and that follows from the Hahn–Banach theorem. 2\Rightarrow3 is exactly Schauder's near-openness lemma: for a continuous linear map with complete domain, B(0,\delta)\subset\overline{T(B(0,1))} implies B(0,\delta)\subset T(B(0,1)). Finally, 3\Rightarrow4 is trivial and 4\Rightarrow1 for some \delta>0 easily follows from the open mapping theorem.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The full proof needs continuous dual spaces with operator norms, the transpose, Hahn–Banach separation and the interaction between dual norm bounds and closures of images of balls. Although near openness and the final scaling can be understood from metric analysis, these combined duality and separation prerequisites exceed a short undergraduate extension.

Proof 2: Closed range theorem — outline

The lower bound implies that T' is injective and has closed image and then by the closed range theorem, that implies T has dense image and closed image, respectively; i.e., T is surjective.

The converse follows from the open mapping theorem: a surjective T satisfies \delta B_Y\subset T(B_X) for some \delta>0, where B_X,B_Y are the open unit balls. Evaluating y' on this inclusion gives \delta\|y'\|\le\|T'y'\|.
