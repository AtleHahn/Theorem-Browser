Statement

Let (X,A) and (Y,B) be CW pairs. Every continuous map f:X\to Y with f(A)\subseteq B is homotopic through maps of pairs to a cellular map (X,A)\to(Y,B), whose restriction A\to B is also cellular.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement is about maps and homotopies of CW pairs, requiring algebraic topology.

Proof 1: Cellular approximation for CW pairs — outline

Cellular approximation: a continuous map between CW complexes is homotopic to a cellular map, and the homotopy may be fixed on any subcomplex on which it is already cellular.

Restrict f to A and use cellular approximation to obtain a homotopy of f to a cellular map A\to B. Use the homotopy extension property to extend this homotopy to all of X, and apply cellular approximation again to obtain a map cellular on X, but fixed on A, so the homotopy remains a homotopy of pairs.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof uses CW pairs, cellular maps, relative cellular approximation and homotopy extension. These are several interdependent algebraic-topology concepts rather than a small extension of point-set topology.
