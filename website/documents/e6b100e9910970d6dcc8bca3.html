Statement

All graphs here are finite and simple. A clique is a set of pairwise adjacent vertices.

If a bipartite graph has n vertices and maximum matching size M, its minimum vertex clique-cover size is n-M. A matching is a set of disjoint edges; a clique cover covers all vertices by cliques.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All finite graph notions are defined and the local arguments use elementary counting and coloring.

Proof 1: Match edges and cover unmatched vertices

In n-vertex bipartite graphs, a minimum clique cover takes the form of a maximum matching together with an additional clique for every unmatched vertex, with size n-M, where M is the cardinality of the matching.

Level
High school only
Level justification

A bipartite clique has at most two vertices. Any overlapping cover can be made disjoint by assigning each vertex to one covering clique, leaving singleton or edge cliques. Its edge cliques then form a matching, so a cover has at least n-M members. Conversely a maximum matching plus unmatched singletons has n-M. These are elementary finite graph and counting arguments.
