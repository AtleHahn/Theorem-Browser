Statement

Let ABC be a nondegenerate Euclidean triangle. Its nine-point circle, the circle through the three side midpoints, is tangent to each of its three excircles. If the triangle is not equilateral, this circle is also internally tangent to its incircle, meeting it at exactly one point, called the Feuerbach point. In the equilateral case the nine-point circle and incircle coincide, so no unique Feuerbach point is defined.

The incircle lies inside the triangle and is tangent to its three sides. Each excircle is tangent to the three lines containing the sides, lying on the opposite side from the triangle of one of those lines and on the same side as the triangle of the other two. Internal tangency means that one circle lies inside the other and their circumferences meet at one point.
