Statement

Let R be a commutative ring with identity, let 0\le n<m, and let A\in R^{m\times n} and B\in R^{n\times m}. Then \det(AB)=0.

Level

Level justification

Introductory field/group theory and linear algebra supply all objects. Commutator, graph automorphism and 2-rank are explicit group or finite-coordinate definitions; the displayed identities and subgroup assertions require no specialist structural theory.

Proof 1: The sum has no admissible subsets

Let R be a commutative ring with identity, let m,n\ge0 be integers, and let A\in R^{m\times n} and B\in R^{n\times m}. Write [n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}. For an m-element subset S\subseteq[n], A_{[m],S} and B_{S,[m]} select the indicated columns and rows in increasing order. Empty determinants are 1 and empty sums are 0. The external Cauchy–Binet identity is \det(AB)=\sum_{\substack{S\subseteq[n]\\|S|=m}}\det(A_{[m],S})\det(B_{S,[m]}). If n<m, there is no m-element subset of [n], so the sum is empty and equals zero.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the fully stated Cauchy-Binet identity and note that its indexing family is empty. Linear algebra and introductory commutative-ring arithmetic suffice; no module rank theorem is needed.
