Statement

Let X be a real or complex Banach space. There exist a compact Hausdorff space K and a linear isometric embedding j:X\to C(K), where C(K) consists of continuous functions valued in the scalar field of X with the supremum norm. One can take K=\{x'\in X':\|x'\|\le1\}, the closed unit ball of the continuous dual with the weak-star topology, and j(x)(x')=x'(x).

Proof 1: Evaluate on the weak-star dual ball

The simplest choice is to let K be the unit ball of the continuous dual X ′, equipped with the w*-topology. This unit ball K is then compact by the Banach–Alaoglu theorem. The embedding j is introduced by saying that for every x ∈ X, the continuous function j(x) on K is defined by

{\displaystyle \forall x'\in K:\qquad j(x)(x')=x'(x).}

The mapping j is linear, and it is isometric by the Hahn–Banach theorem.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof simultaneously constructs a weak-star topological dual ball, evaluates vectors on it as continuous functions, invokes its compactness and obtains an isometry from Hahn–Banach. Continuous duals, dual norms, weak-star topology and the resulting function-space representation are a substantial functional-analysis package beyond the short undergraduate allowance.
