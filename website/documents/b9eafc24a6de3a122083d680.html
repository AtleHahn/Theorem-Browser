Statement

Every consistent theory T in classical first-order predicate logic has a complete consistent extension T' in the same language.

Here a theory is a set of sentences, consistency means that no contradiction is derivable from it, and an extension contains all its sentences. Completeness means that for every sentence \varphi of that language, either T'\vdash\varphi or T'\vdash\neg\varphi. The extension may be taken deductively closed. No computable enumerability of T' is asserted; the usual Boolean ultrafilter principle is assumed in the background.

Proof 1: Apply the Boolean ultrafilter lemma — source outline

Form the Lindenbaum Boolean algebra of sentences modulo provable equivalence over T. Thus [\varphi]=[\psi] when T\vdash\varphi\leftrightarrow\psi; conjunction and negation give meet and complement. Consistency makes this algebra nontrivial. Apply the Boolean ultrafilter lemma, as the source directs, to obtain an ultrafilter U.

Let T' be the sentences whose equivalence classes belong to U. Every sentence of T has class 1, so T\subseteq T'. Closure of a filter under finite meets and upward inclusion makes T' closed under logical consequence. Since 0\notin U, T' is consistent. An ultrafilter contains exactly one of each complementary pair [\varphi],[\neg\varphi], so T' is complete.
