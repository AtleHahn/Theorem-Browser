Statement

Let Z be a real random variable with 0<\mathbb E Z^2<\infty and \mathbb E Z^4<\infty. For 0\le\theta<1, \Pr\left(|Z|>\theta\sqrt{\mathbb E Z^2}\right)\ge\frac{(1-\theta^2)^2(\mathbb E Z^2)^2}{\mathbb E Z^4}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Scalar probability, expectation and second/fourth moments add closely related integral-average definitions to undergraduate analysis.

Proof 1: Apply Paley–Zygmund to the square — outline

Apply the Paley–Zygmund theorem to the nonnegative variable Y=Z^2 at its relative threshold \alpha=\theta^2: \Pr(Y>\alpha\mathbb E Y)\ge(1-\alpha)^2\frac{(\mathbb E Y)^2}{\mathbb E Y^2}. Substituting Y=Z^2 and taking the nonnegative square-root form of the event gives the displayed conclusion.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Substitute Y=Z squared and alpha=theta squared into the explicitly stated inequality, identifying the event and second/fourth moments. Undergraduate probability with expectations and moments suffices; no measure-theoretic limit theorem is used.
