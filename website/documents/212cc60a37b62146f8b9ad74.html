Statement

Let (X,\mathcal A) and (Y,\mathcal B) be measurable spaces with injective maps f:X\to Y and g:Y\to X. Assume each map is bimeasurable, meaning both preimages and images of measurable sets are measurable. Then there is a bijection h:X\to Y such that h and h^{-1} are measurable.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Sets, inverse images and countable operations are familiar from analysis and topology. A sigma-algebra is a family closed under complements and countable unions, and measurability is the corresponding inverse-image condition. Here no general integration, null-set quotient or stochastic process is required; the limited set-family definitions suffice.

Proof 1: The measurable partition in the chain construction — outline

First, one constructs a bijection h:X\to Y out of f and g exactly as in the alternating-chain proof of the Cantor–Bernstein–Schroeder theorem: use f on the chains starting in X, and g^{-1} on the other chains. The X-points in the former chains form

C=\bigcup_{n\ge0}(g\circ f)^n(X\setminus g(Y)).

This set is measurable by bimeasurability and closure under countable unions.

Second, h is measurable, since it coincides with f on a measurable set and with g^{-1} on its complement. Similarly, h^{-1} is measurable.
