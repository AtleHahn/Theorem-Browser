Statement

Let (X,\Sigma,\mu) be a measure space and 0<p\le\infty. The space L^p(\mu) of real or complex measurable functions modulo almost-everywhere equality is complete. For 1\le p<\infty use the distance \|f-g\|_p=(\int|f-g|^p\,d\mu)^{1/p}; for 0<p<1 use d(f,g)=\int|f-g|^p\,d\mu; for p=\infty use the essential-supremum norm distance.

Proof 1: Summable series and convergence theorems — outline

When {\displaystyle 1\leq p\leq \infty ,} the Minkowski inequality implies that the Lp space {\displaystyle L^{p}} is a normed space. In order to prove that {\displaystyle L^{p}} is complete, i.e. that {\displaystyle L^{p}} is a Banach space, it is enough (by the summable-series criterion for completeness of a normed space) to prove that every series {\displaystyle \sum u_{n}} of functions in {\displaystyle L^{p}(\mu )} such that

{\displaystyle \sum \|u_{n}\|_{p}<\infty }

converges in the {\displaystyle L^{p}}-norm to some function {\displaystyle f\in L^{p}(\mu ).} For {\displaystyle p<\infty ,} the Minkowski inequality and the monotone convergence theorem imply that

{\displaystyle \int \left(\sum _{n=0}^{\infty }|u_{n}|\right)^{p}\,\mathrm {d} \mu \leq \left(\sum _{n=0}^{\infty }\|u_{n}\|_{p}\right)^{p}<\infty ,\ \ {\text{ hence }}\ \ f=\sum _{n=0}^{\infty }u_{n}}

is defined {\displaystyle \mu }–almost everywhere and {\displaystyle f\in L^{p}(\mu ).} The dominated convergence theorem is then used to prove that the partial sums of the series converge to f in the {\displaystyle L^{p}}-norm,

{\displaystyle \int \left|f-\sum _{k=0}^{n}u_{k}\right|^{p}\,\mathrm {d} \mu \leq \int \left(\sum _{\ell >n}|u_{\ell }|\right)^{p}\,\mathrm {d} \mu \rightarrow 0{\text{ as }}n\rightarrow \infty .}

The case {\displaystyle 0<p<1} requires some modifications, because the p-norm is no longer subadditive. One starts with the stronger assumption that

{\displaystyle \sum \|u_{n}\|_{p}^{p}<\infty }

and uses repeatedly that

{\displaystyle \left|\sum _{k=0}^{n}u_{k}\right|^{p}\leq \sum _{k=0}^{n}|u_{k}|^{p}{\text{ when }}p<1}

The case {\displaystyle p=\infty } reduces to a simple question about uniform convergence outside a {\displaystyle \mu }-negligible set.
