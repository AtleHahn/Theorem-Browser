Statement

Let n\ge1 be an integer and let H be a closed subgroup of GL(n,\mathbb R). Then H, with its relative topology, is an embedded real-analytic submanifold and a real-analytic Lie group. Its tangent space at the identity is

\{X\in M_n(\mathbb R):\exp(tX)\in H\text{ for all }t\in\mathbb R\}.

Proof 1: Product-exponential slice and translated analytic charts

Let n\ge1 be an integer and H a closed subgroup of GL(n,\mathbb R). Put G=GL(n,\mathbb R) and \mathfrak g=M_n(\mathbb R) and

\mathfrak h=\{X\in\mathfrak g:\exp(tX)\in H\text{ for every }t\in\mathbb R\}.

Use the standard fact that \mathfrak h is a Lie subalgebra, hence a linear subspace. Give \mathfrak g the Hilbert–Schmidt inner product and set \mathfrak s=\mathfrak h^\perp. Thus \mathfrak g=\mathfrak s\oplus\mathfrak h.

Local slice

Use the decomposition \mathfrak g=\mathfrak s\oplus\mathfrak h above to identify S+T with (S,T). Define a map Φ : g → GL(n, R) by (S, T) ↦ eSeT. Expand the exponentials,

{\displaystyle \Phi (tS,tT)=e^{tS}e^{tT}=I+tS+tT+O(t^{2}),}

and the pushforward or differential at 0, Φ∗(S, T) = ⁠d/dt⁠Φ(tS, tT)|t = 0 is seen to be S + T, i.e. Φ∗ = Id, the identity. The hypothesis of the inverse function theorem is satisfied with Φ analytic, and thus there are open sets U1 ⊂ g, V1 ⊂ GL(n, R) with 0 ∈ U1 and I ∈ V1 such that Φ is a real-analytic bijection from U1 to V1 with analytic inverse. It remains to show that U1 and V1 contain open sets U and V such that \Phi^{-1}(H\cap V)=U\cap\mathfrak h.

Consider a countable open neighborhood basis Β at 0 ∈ g, linearly ordered by reverse inclusion with B1 ⊂ U1. Suppose for the purpose of obtaining a contradiction that for all i, Φ(Bi) ∩ H contains an element hi whose unique Φ-coordinate in B_i has nonzero \mathfrak s-component. Then, since Φ is a bijection on the Bi, there is a unique sequence Xi = Si + Ti, with 0 ≠ Si ∈ s and Ti ∈ h such that Xi ∈ Bi converging to 0 because Β is a neighborhood basis, with eSieTi = hi. Since eTi ∈ H and hi ∈ H, eSi ∈ H as well.

Normalize the sequence in s, Yi = ⁠Si/||Si||⁠. It takes its values in the unit sphere in s and since it is compact, there is a convergent subsequence converging to Y ∈ s. The index i henceforth refers to this subsequence. It will be shown that etY ∈ H, ∀t ∈ R. Fix t and choose a sequence mi of integers such that mi ||Si|| → t as i → ∞. For example, mi such that mi ||Si|| ≤ t ≤ (mi + 1) ||Si|| will do, as Si → 0. Then

{\displaystyle (e^{S_{i}})^{m_{i}}=e^{m_{i}S_{i}}=e^{m_{i}\|S_{i}\|Y_{i}}\rightarrow e^{tY}.}

Since H is a group, the left hand side is in H for all i. Since H is closed, etY ∈ H, ∀t, hence Y ∈ h. This is a contradiction, since Y\in\mathfrak s and \|Y\|=1. Hence, for some i the sets U = Βi and V = Φ(Βi) satisfy eU∩h = H ∩ V and the exponential restricted to the open set (U ∩ h) ⊂ h is in analytic bijection with the open set Φ(U) ∩ H ⊂ H. Thus \Phi^{-1}(H\cap V)=U\cap\mathfrak h, and the inverse on this slice is the restriction of \Phi^{-1}.

Translate the slice

For j ≥ i, the sets Φ(Bj) ∩ H form a neighborhood basis at I. This is, by the way it is constructed, a neighborhood basis both in the topology supplied by the slice coordinates and the relative topology. Since multiplication in G is analytic, the left and right translates of this neighborhood basis by a group element h ∈ H give a neighborhood basis at h in H. The topology generated by these bases is the relative topology. The conclusion is that the relative topology is the same as the topology supplied by the slice coordinates.

Next, construct coordinate charts on H. First define \varphi_1=\Phi^{-1}:V\to U\subset\mathfrak g. This is an analytic bijection with analytic inverse. Furthermore, if h\in H\cap V, then \varphi_1(h)\in\mathfrak h. By fixing a basis for g = h ⊕ s and identifying g with Rn², then in these coordinates φ1(h) = (x1(h), ..., xm(h), 0, ..., 0), where m is the dimension of h. This shows that (V, φ1) is a slice chart. By translating the charts obtained from the countable neighborhood basis used above one obtains slice charts around every point in H. This shows that H is an embedded submanifold of G.

Moreover, multiplication m, and inversion i in H are analytic since these operations are analytic in G and restriction to a submanifold (embedded or immersed) with the relative topology again yield analytic operations m : H × H → G and i : H × H → G. But since H is embedded, m : H × H → H and i : H × H → H are analytic as well.

At the identity the slice has tangent space \mathfrak h, since the differential of \Phi at zero is the identity.
