Statement

Let G be a finite simple undirected graph with n vertices and e edges. A plane drawing places vertices at distinct points and draws edges as arcs between their endpoints, with no vertex in an edge interior; crossings are intersections of edge interiors. Drawings may be taken with finitely many transverse crossings and no triple crossings. Let \operatorname{cr}(G) be the minimum number of crossings over these drawings. There is a crossing-minimal drawing in which edges with a common endpoint do not cross; thus every crossing involves four distinct vertices.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The drawing conventions and crossing count are locally explained finite graph and elementary plane geometry.

Proof 1: Interchange intersecting portions of adjacent edges — outline

Consider a diagram of G with \operatorname{cr}(G) crossings. We may assume that any two edges in this diagram with a common vertex are disjoint away from that vertex, otherwise we could interchange the intersecting parts of the two edges and reduce the crossing number by one. Thus every crossing in this diagram involves four distinct vertices of G.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Reroute two crossing edges sharing an endpoint by exchanging their portions up to the crossing. This decreases crossings, contradicting minimality. Only finite plane drawings are involved.
