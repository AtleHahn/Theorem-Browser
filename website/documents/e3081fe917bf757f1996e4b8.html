Statement

If positive integers a,b,c satisfy a^n+b^n=c^n for n\ge1 and g=\gcd(a,b,c), then (x,y,z)=(a/g,b/g,c/g) satisfies the same equation and is pairwise coprime.

Proof 1: Cancel the common factor and use prime divisibility

If two of the three numbers (a,b,c) can be divided by a prime d, then all three numbers are divisible by d. For example, if a and c are divisible by d=13, then b is also divisible by 13. This follows from the equation

b^n=c^n-a^n.

If the right-hand side of the equation is divisible by 13, then the left-hand side is also divisible by 13, and prime factorization implies that 13 divides b.

Let g represent the greatest common divisor of a,b,c. Then (a,b,c) may be written as a=gx, b=gy, and c=gz, where the three numbers (x,y,z) have common gcd 1. They are pairwise coprime: a prime dividing any two would, by the same argument, divide the third, contrary to their common gcd being 1. In other words,

\gcd(x,y)=\gcd(x,z)=\gcd(y,z)=1.

If (a,b,c) is a solution of Fermat's equation, then so is (x,y,z), since substituting gives

g^nx^n+g^ny^n=g^nz^n,

which implies the equation

x^n+y^n=z^n.

A pairwise coprime solution (x,y,z) is called a primitive solution. Thus every solution can be reduced to a primitive solution by dividing by the greatest common divisor g.
