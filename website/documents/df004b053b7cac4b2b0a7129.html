Statement

Let f be a nonzero real polynomial with nonzero constant coefficient a_0 and leading coefficient a_n. The number of positive roots counted with multiplicity is even if a_0a_n>0 and odd if a_0a_n<0.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The assertion compares the parity of a polynomial’s positive-root multiplicities with the signs of its two endpoint coefficients.

Mathematical Field
High School Mathematics
Required topics
real polynomialsroot multiplicitiessigns and parity
Review notes
none

Proof 1: Compare endpoint signs and crossing multiplicities — outline

Suppose first that f is nonconstant and a_0,a_n>0. Then f(x) starts at f(0)=a_0>0 and tends to +\infty as x\to+\infty, so it must cross the positive x-axis an even number of times (each of which contributes an odd number of roots), and each instance where it glances off (without crossing) the positive x-axis contributes an even number of roots.

The other endpoint-sign cases are similar. Multiplying f by -1 does not change its roots or their multiplicities; a nonzero constant polynomial has no roots.
