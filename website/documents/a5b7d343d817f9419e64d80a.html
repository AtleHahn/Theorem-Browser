Statement

There are no finite projective planes of orders 6 or 14.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A finite projective plane is an incidence system in which each pair of points determines a line and each pair of lines meets, with the stated uniform counts. This short finite combinatorial definition suffices for the counting/oval assertions, without coordinates or algebraic geometry.

Proof 1: Apply the two-square obstruction outline

Use the Bruck–Ryser theorem: if a finite projective plane of order q\equiv1 or 2\pmod4 exists, then q is a sum of two integer squares. This rules out projective planes of orders 6 and 14.
