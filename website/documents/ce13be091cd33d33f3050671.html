Statement

Fix an integer n\ge1. Let \mathcal K^n consist of all compact convex subsets of \mathbb R^n, including the empty set. A valuation is a function v:\mathcal K^n\to\mathbb R with v(\varnothing)=0 and v(S)+v(T)=v(S\cap T)+v(S\cup T) whenever S\cup T is convex. Suppose v is invariant under translations and rotations and continuous on nonempty sets for the Hausdorff distance d_H(S,T)=\max\{\sup_{s\in S}\inf_{t\in T}\|s-t\|,\sup_{t\in T}\inf_{s\in S}\|s-t\|\}. Let B be the closed Euclidean unit ball, and define W_j(K) by the polynomial identity \operatorname{Vol}_n(K+tB)=\sum_{j=0}^n\binom nj W_j(K)t^j\quad(t\ge0), where K+tB=\{x+ty:x\in K,y\in B\} and \operatorname{Vol}_n is Euclidean volume; set W_j(\varnothing)=0. There are real constants c_0,\ldots,c_n such that v(K)=\sum_{j=0}^n c_jW_j(K)\quad(K\in\mathcal K^n). In particular, if 0\le q\le n is an integer and also v(tK)=t^qv(K) for every t>0, then v is a constant multiple of W_{n-q}.

Proof 1: Dimension induction and rotational Minkowski means (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (§5, Theorem 5.1, proof pp.18–19; Proposition 5.2 proof §5.2 p.20; supporting §4.).

With the source’s intrinsic volumes, \binom nj W_j(K)=\kappa_jV_{n-j}(K), where \kappa_j is the volume of the unit j-ball. The proof uses rotations in SO(n) and covers lower-dimensional bodies. The empty set follows from normalization. If v is q-homogeneous, comparison of the distinct powers in v(tK) eliminates all terms except V_q, hence all except W_{n-q}.

The dimension induction uses finite simplex dissections, polynomial coefficient comparison, convex support functions and compactness. The needed polynomiality dependency has a proof by simplex decomposition and finite differences; degree-one Minkowski additivity then follows by a Vandermonde calculation. Rotational means can be established by uniform compactness and minimizing an ordinary integral. Together with the intrinsic-volume formulas, these use undergraduate linear algebra, real analysis and calculus; support functions and the dissection terminology form a small additional preparation requirement.
