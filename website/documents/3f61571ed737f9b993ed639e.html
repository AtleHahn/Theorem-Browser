Statement

Let ABC be a nondegenerate triangle and P a point strictly inside it. Let U,V,W be the intersections of the internal bisectors of \angle BPC,\angle CPA,\angle APB with BC,CA,AB, respectively. Then PA+PB+PC\ge2(PU+PV+PW).

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement concerns finite triangle geometry and distances, within high-school Euclidean geometry.

Proof 1: Triangle specialization of the polygon inequality

Polygon inequality (dependency)

For a convex polygon with vertices A_1,\ldots,A_n and an interior point P, let Q_i be the intersection of the internal bisector of \angle A_iPA_{i+1} with side A_iA_{i+1}, taking A_{n+1}=A_1. The generalized Barrow inequality states

\sum_{i=1}^n PA_i\ge\sec(\pi/n)\sum_{i=1}^n PQ_i.

Triangle specialization

For the triangle case n=3 the inequality becomes Barrow's inequality due to \sec\left(\tfrac{\pi}{3}\right)=2.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The supplied polygon inequality is stated using only vertices, distances and angle bisectors. Substituting n=3 and sec(pi/3)=2 gives exactly the three claimed bisector lengths. Plane geometry and elementary trigonometry suffice; proving the general polygon inequality is not part of this argument.
