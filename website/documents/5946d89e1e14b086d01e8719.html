Statement

Let G be a finite group and \operatorname{Char}(G) the integer span of its irreducible complex characters inside the complex-valued class functions, with pointwise addition and multiplication. Call a subgroup elementary if it is a direct product of a cyclic group and a group of prime-power order. Let I(G) be the integer span of characters induced from one-dimensional characters of elementary subgroups. Then I(G)=\operatorname{Char}(G) if and only if the trivial character 1_G belongs to I(G).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement is an ideal-membership criterion in a character ring.

Proof 1: Use the ideal and the ring identity — outline

The set of integer combinations of characters induced from linear characters of Brauer elementary subgroups is an ideal I(G) of \operatorname{Char}(G), so the proof that I(G)=\operatorname{Char}(G) reduces to showing that the trivial character is in I(G).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The final ideal argument is elementary ring theory, but the full claim uses virtual characters, character induction and elementary subgroups in finite-group representation theory. Learning these together exceeds a small extension of introductory groups and linear algebra.
