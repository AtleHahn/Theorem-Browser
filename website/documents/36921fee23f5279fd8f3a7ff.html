Statement

Let S be a complete Riemannian surface without boundary, with finite total Gaussian curvature and finite Euler characteristic. Write K for its Gaussian curvature and dA for the Riemannian area measure. Then

\int_S K\,dA\le 2\pi\chi(S).

Here finite total curvature means that K is integrable with respect to dA, and \chi(S) is the Euler characteristic. Completeness means completeness of the distance induced by the Riemannian metric. The surface may be noncompact; no orientability assumption is imposed.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement combines intrinsic curvature, metric completeness of a possibly noncompact surface, total curvature and Euler characteristic. Learning Riemannian surface geometry and its topological invariant cumulatively exceeds the conservative undergraduate allowance.

Proof 1: Distance-sphere variation and curvature exhaustion (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (§13, Theorem 13.6 and preceding argument, pp.81–87; supporting integrated inequality linked below).

Use the integrated length estimate in Bérard and Castillon, §2.1, (2.8) to complete the metric-ball argument at singular radii. For large a, \chi(B(t))\le\chi(S) for all t\ge a (their Lemma 2.1). If c=\int_S K>2\pi\chi(S), integrability gives G(t)=\int_{B(t)}K>2\pi\chi(S)+\delta eventually. Thus L(b)\le L(a)-\delta(b-a), contradicting nonnegative length. No convergence of L\prime is needed. The compact case is Gauss–Bonnet. For a nonorientable surface use its complete orientable double cover, on which both the integral and Euler characteristic double; apply the result componentwise if necessary.

No proof of the general inequality is included in the source article. It discusses the compact boundaryless case by citing Gauss–Bonnet and computes the Euclidean-plane example; these do not prove the general complete-surface inequality. The separate boundary discussion is not retained: its claim that the integrated boundary geodesic curvature must be positive is false in general and does not establish a boundary version of this theorem.
