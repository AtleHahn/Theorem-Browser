Statement

Let n\geq1 be an integer and let K\subseteq\mathbb R^n be nonempty and compact. Write

d=\max_{p,q\in K}\|p-q\|_2,

where \|x\|_2=(\sum_{j=1}^n x_j^2)^{1/2}. There exist a center c\in\mathbb R^n and a radius r\geq0 such that

K\subseteq\{x\in\mathbb R^n:\|x-c\|_2\leq r\},\qquad r\leq d\sqrt{\frac{n}{2(n+1)}}.

The constant is sharp: for the vertices of a regular n-simplex of diameter d>0—n+1 points whose pairwise distances all equal d—the smallest enclosing radius equals d\sqrt{n/[2(n+1)]}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Euclidean n-space and compactness are standard undergraduate analysis; the simplex is defined by pairwise distances. No convex-geometry theory is required to understand the bound.
