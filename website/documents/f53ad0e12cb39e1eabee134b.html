Statement

Let i\ge2 be an integer, Z_i an i-dimensional CW complex, X a topological space and f_i:Z_i\to X continuous. Suppose f_i induces a bijection on path components, isomorphisms on \pi_k for 1\le k<i at every basepoint, and a surjection on \pi_i. Form Z_{i+1} by attaching (i+1)-cells along based maps representing generators of each kernel \ker((f_i)_*: \pi_i(Z_i)\to\pi_i(X)), extending the map by the chosen nullhomotopies in X, and also attaching (i+1)-cells by constant boundary maps whose extensions represent generators of \pi_{i+1}(X) at every basepoint. Then the extended map f_{i+1}:Z_{i+1}\to X is an isomorphism on \pi_k for 1\le k\le i, remains bijective on path components, and is surjective on \pi_{i+1} at every basepoint.

Under the hypotheses of the statement, {\displaystyle Z_{i+1}} is built from {\displaystyle Z_{i}} by attaching (i+1)-cells that (for all basepoints)

  • are attached by the mappings {\displaystyle S^{i}\to Z_{i}} that generate the kernel of {\displaystyle \pi _{i}(Z_{i})\to \pi _{i}(X)} (and are mapped to X by the contraction of the corresponding spheroids)
  • are attached by constant mappings and are mapped to X to generate {\displaystyle \pi _{i+1}(X)}.

The cellular approximation theorem and the standard description of homotopy groups under cell attachment ensure then that adding (i+1)-cells doesn't affect {\displaystyle \pi _{k}(Z_{i}){\stackrel {\cong }{\to }}\pi _{k}(X)} for {\displaystyle k<i}, while {\displaystyle \pi _{i}(Z_{i})} gets factored by the classes of the attachment mappings {\displaystyle S^{i}\to Z_{i}} of these cells giving {\displaystyle \pi _{i}(Z_{i+1}){\stackrel {\cong }{\to }}\pi _{i}(X)}. Surjectivity of {\displaystyle \pi _{i+1}(Z_{i+1})\to \pi _{i+1}(X)} is evident from the second step of the construction.
