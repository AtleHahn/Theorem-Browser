Statement

On a finite product X=\prod_{i=1}^n X_i of finite totally ordered sets, let \mu(x)=\prod_i\mu_i(x_i) be a product probability mass function. If f,g:X\to\mathbb R are increasing in every coordinate, then \mathbb E_\mu[fg]\ge\mathbb E_\mu[f]\mathbb E_\mu[g]. Here each \mu_i is a nonnegative function on X_i summing to 1, and \mathbb E_\mu[h]=\sum_{x\in X}h(x)\mu(x).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The claim involves functions on product ordered probability spaces and expectations under a product measure, requiring undergraduate probability despite the finite setting. A bright US college student should be able to understand the statement after about 30 minutes of focused background research; this rating concerns understanding the claim, not proving it.

Proof 1: Verify the product lattice condition and apply FKG — outline

The product measure satisfies the FKG lattice condition: coordinatewise minima and maxima reorder the two arguments in each factor, giving \mu(x\wedge y)\mu(x\vee y)=\mu(x)\mu(y). Apply the FKG inequality $ (\sum fg\mu)(\sum\mu)\ge(\sum f\mu)(\sum g\mu)$ and use \sum\mu=1.
