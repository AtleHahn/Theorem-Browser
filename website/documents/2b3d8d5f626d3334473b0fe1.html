Statement

Let T be a satisfiable first-order theory in a language L with no finite models. Suppose for some infinite cardinal \kappa\ge|L| it is \kappa-categorical, meaning it has exactly one model of cardinality \kappa up to isomorphism. Then T is complete: for every L-sentence \varphi, either T\vdash\varphi or T\vdash\neg\varphi.

Proof 1: Comparing all models at one cardinal

By the downward and upward Löwenheim–Skolem theorems, every infinite model of T is elementarily equivalent to some model of cardinality \kappa. All models of T are infinite by assumption. The \kappa-sized models are isomorphic by categoricity, hence elementarily equivalent. Thus all models of T satisfy exactly the same sentences. By the completeness theorem, T is complete.
