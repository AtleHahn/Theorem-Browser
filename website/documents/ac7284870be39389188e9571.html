Statement

Assume choice. Form the graph with vertex set \mathbb R^2 and an edge between two points exactly one unit apart. There is a finite subgraph whose chromatic number equals that of the whole graph; the chromatic number is the least number of colors in a proper vertex coloring.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The graph is explicitly the plane with edges at unit distance. A proper coloring and its minimum number of colors are finite-label conditions on a set, and choice is a stated assumption. No graph compactness proof machinery is needed for comprehension.

Proof 1: Take the maximum finite-subgraph chromatic number

The whole infinite graph has a known coloring with seven colors based on a hexagonal tiling of the plane. The De Bruijn–Erdős theorem states that the chromatic number of an infinite graph with finite chromatic number equals the maximum of the chromatic numbers of its finite subgraphs. It shows that, for this problem, there exists a finite unit distance graph with the same chromatic number as the whole plane, so if the chromatic number is greater than five then this fact can be proved by a finite calculation.
