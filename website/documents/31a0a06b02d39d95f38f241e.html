Statement

For every small abelian category \mathcal A, there exist a ring R with identity (not necessarily commutative) and a full, faithful, exact functor

F:\mathcal A\longrightarrow R\text{-}\mathrm{Mod},

where R\text{-}\mathrm{Mod} is the category of unital left R-modules and R-linear maps. Thus F identifies \mathcal A, up to equivalence, with a full subcategory of modules, preserving kernels, cokernels, exact sequences and sums of morphisms.

Small means the objects and morphisms form sets. An abelian category is an additive category with kernels and cokernels in which every monomorphism is a kernel and every epimorphism is a cokernel. Additive means morphism sets are abelian groups with bilinear composition and finite biproducts exist. A functor preserves identities and composition; full and faithful mean it induces a bijection on each morphism set. Exact here means it is additive and carries short exact sequences to short exact sequences. Exactness of a sequence means that the image of each map is the kernel of the next. The theorem does not assert preservation of projective or injective objects as modules.

Proof 1: Yoneda sheaves and an enlarged projective generator (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (TheoremIII.3.1, pp.95–96; solutionsA.4.3 andA.4.4(d), pp.280–286).

Use the enlarged generator in ExerciseA.4.4(d), which gives fullness on the chosen small subcategory. In TheoremIII.3.1, the opposite of the sheaf category has the required small colimits, since the sheaf category has small limits. The Yoneda map induced by f:A\to B goes from h_A to h_B, correcting the reversed arrows inA.4.3. InA.4.4(d), with G=\operatorname{Hom}(P,-), the lifting identity is G(p_B)G(f)=uG(p_A); it implies G(p_Bfi)=0 for i:\ker p_A\to P. Right modules over S are left modules over S^{\mathrm{op}}; apply the construction to the opposite of the original category to obtain the stated covariant functor.
