Statement

Let M=(M_t)_{t\ge0} be a real-valued continuous local martingale with M_0=0 on a filtered probability space (\Omega,\mathcal F,(\mathcal F^0_t)_{t\ge0},\mathbb P). Use the usual augmentation of its given filtration and denote it by (\mathcal F_t): the filtration is completed by null sets and made right-continuous. A local martingale is an adapted process for which there are stopping times \sigma_n\uparrow\infty almost surely such that each stopped process M_{t\wedge\sigma_n} is a martingale. A martingale is integrable at each time and satisfies \mathbb E[M_t\mid\mathcal F_s]=M_s for s\le t; a stopping time S satisfies \{S\le t\}\in\mathcal F_t for every t. Write Q_t=\langle M\rangle_t for its continuous quadratic variation, the increasing process obtained as the limit in probability of sums of squared increments over deterministic partitions of [0,t] whose mesh tends to zero. Put Q_\infty=\lim_{t\to\infty}Q_t. Without assuming Q_\infty=\infty, there exists an extension of the probability space on which a standard real Brownian motion B can be defined so that, for the lifted processes M and Q,

M_t=B_{Q_t}\quad\text{for every }t\ge0\text{ almost surely}.

An extension means a probability space with a measure-preserving map to the original one; lifting a random variable means composing it with that map, so the original joint distributions are preserved. Standard Brownian motion starts at zero, has continuous paths, and has independent centered normal increments with variance equal to elapsed time. The assertion does not claim that B is Brownian for the original filtration indexed by the same time parameter.
