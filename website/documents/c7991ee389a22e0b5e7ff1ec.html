Statement

Let q,v range over an open domain in \mathbb R^n and time t over an interval. Let L(q,v,t) be smooth and let q(t) satisfy the Euler–Lagrange equations \frac d{dt}L_{v_i}=L_{q_i}. If L is independent of q_k, where 1\le k\le n, then p_k=L_{v_k}(q,\dot q,t) is constant.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves multivariable calculus; Euler–Lagrange equations. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Euler–Lagrange equation for the missing coordinate

By the Euler–Lagrange equation, if one of the coordinates, say qk, does not appear in the Lagrangian, the kth component of the right-hand side of the equation is zero, and the left-hand side requires that

{\displaystyle {\frac {d}{dt}}\left({\frac {\partial L}{\partial {\dot {q}}_{k}}}\right)={\frac {dp_{k}}{dt}}=0~,}

where the momentum

{\displaystyle p_{k}={\frac {\partial L}{\partial {\dot {q}}_{k}}}}

is conserved throughout the motion (on the physical path).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The assumed Euler-Lagrange equation makes the momentum derivative zero when the corresponding partial derivative vanishes. The result then follows from elementary constancy on an interval.

Proof 2: Specialize Noether’s theorem to a coordinate translation

Use the mechanical Noether theorem: an action symmetry with time generator T and coordinate generator Q conserves (p\cdot\dot q-L)T-p\cdot Q, where p=L_v. Here N denotes the number of symmetry parameters.

Consider a Lagrangian which does not depend on an ("ignorable") coordinate qk; so it is invariant (symmetric) under changes qk → qk + δqk. In that case, N = 1, T = 0, and Qk = 1 (all other components of Q being zero); the conserved quantity is, up to an overall minus sign, the corresponding momentum pk

{\displaystyle p_{k}={\frac {\partial L}{\partial {\dot {q_{k}}}}}.}
