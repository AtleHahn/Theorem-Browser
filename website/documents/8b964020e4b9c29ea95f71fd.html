Statement

For each integer n\ge1, let G_n be an abelian group and M_n=M(G_n,n) a connected pointed CW Moore space: its reduced integral homology is G_n in degree n and zero in every other degree. Put Y_n=\operatorname{SP}(M_n), the infinite symmetric product with its usual topology and empty-tuple basepoint. The natural inclusion \prod_{n\ge1}^{\mathrm{weak}}Y_n\longrightarrow\prod_{n\ge1}Y_n is a weak homotopy equivalence. Here the weak product is the union of finite products (all remaining coordinates at the basepoint), with its direct-limit topology, and the right-hand side has the Cartesian product topology.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves CW Moore spaces; infinite symmetric products; weak and Cartesian products; weak homotopy equivalence. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Use the single nonzero homotopy degree of each factor — outline

By the Dold–Thom theorem, each \operatorname{SP}(M(G_n,n)) is an Eilenberg–MacLane space K(G_n,n). This implies that the natural inclusion of their weak product into their Cartesian product is a weak homotopy equivalence.
