Statement

Let V be a finite-dimensional real vector space, \Lambda a full lattice, m a nonzero translation-invariant Lebesgue measure, and D a fundamental domain of volume v=m(D). For s\in L^1(V,m) put \mathbb Ps(x)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}s(x+\lambda) and S(\nu)=\int_Vs(x)e^{-2\pi i\langle\nu,x\rangle}\,dm(x). If \Lambda^*=\{\nu\in V^*: \langle\nu,\lambda\rangle\in\mathbb Z\ \forall\lambda\in\Lambda\}, then for \nu\in\Lambda^* the normalized Fourier coefficient of \mathbb Ps is v^{-1}S(\nu).

Proof 1: Unfold a fundamental-domain integral — outline

The periodization \mathbb Ps is integrable on V/\Lambda. The dual lattice \Lambda^* consists of covectors that evaluate to integers on \Lambda, so the character e^{-2\pi i\langle\nu,x\rangle} is unchanged by lattice translation.

For all \nu\in\Lambda^*, the Fourier transform \mathbb PS of the periodization \mathbb Ps as a function on V/\Lambda and the Fourier transform S of s on V itself are related by proper normalization:

\begin{aligned} \mathbb PS(\nu) &=\frac1v\int_D\mathbb Ps(x)e^{-2\pi i\langle\nu,x\rangle}\,dm(x)\\ &=\frac1v\int_Vs(x)e^{-2\pi i\langle\nu,x\rangle}\,dm(x)\\ &=\frac1vS(\nu). \end{aligned}

Here v=m(D)=m(V/\Lambda). The usual integrable-periodization and interchange facts justify unfolding the integral; the source leaves this general-lattice interchange implicit.
