Statement

For a bounded Lebesgue-measurable set A\subset\mathbb R^n, n\ge1, write m for Lebesgue measure and H^+(v,c)=\{x:v\cdot x>c\} for v\in S^{n-1}. There is a continuous odd function \alpha:S^{n-1}\to\mathbb R such that m(A\cap H^+(v,\alpha(v)))=m(A)/2. For positive m(A) it may be chosen as the midpoint of the interval of bisecting offsets; for zero m(A) take zero.

Proof 1: Choose the midpoint of the median interval — outline

Let {\displaystyle S=\{v=(v_{1},\ldots ,v_{n})\in \mathbb {R} ^{n}\colon v_{1}^{2}+\ldots +v_{n}^{2}=1\}} be the unit (n − 1)-sphere in {\displaystyle \mathbb {R} ^{n}}. For each point v on S, we can define a continuum {\displaystyle (E_{v,c})_{c\in \mathbb {R} }} of affine hyperplanes with normal vector v: {\displaystyle E_{v,c}=\{x\in \mathbb {R} ^{n}\colon x_{1}v_{1}+\ldots +x_{n}v_{n}=c\}} for {\displaystyle c\in \mathbb {R} }. For each {\displaystyle c\in \mathbb {R} }, we call the space {\displaystyle E_{v,c}^{+}=\{x\in \mathbb {R} ^{n}\colon x_{1}v_{1}+\ldots +x_{n}v_{n}>c\}} the "positive side" of {\displaystyle E_{v,c}}, which is the side pointed to by the vector v. By the intermediate value theorem, every family of such hyperplanes contains at least one hyperplane that bisects the bounded set A: at one extreme translation, no volume of A is on the positive side, and at the other extreme translation, all of A's volume is on the positive side, so in between there must be a closed interval Iv of possible values of {\displaystyle c\in \mathbb {R} }, for which {\displaystyle E_{v,c}} bisects the volume of A. If A has volume zero, we pick {\displaystyle c=0} for all {\displaystyle v\in S}. Otherwise, the interval Iv is compact and we can canonically pick {\displaystyle c={\frac {1}{2}}(\inf I_{v}+\sup I_{v})} as its midpoint for each {\displaystyle v\in S}. Thus we obtain a continuous function {\displaystyle \alpha \colon S\to \mathbb {R} } such that for each point v on the sphere S the hyperplane {\displaystyle E_{v,\alpha (v)}} bisects A. Note further that we have {\displaystyle I_{-v}=-I_{v}} and thus {\displaystyle \alpha (-v)=-\alpha (v)} for all {\displaystyle v\in S}.

Here A is the bounded measurable set in the statement. Continuity of the half-space volumes follows from dominated convergence; the source leaves the continuity check for the midpoint selection implicit.

Routine omissions

The source suppresses continuity of the median-interval endpoints. For bounded A, changing v moves every scalar projection uniformly by at most sup_A||x|| times ||v-w||, which bounds movement of both endpoints. This is the routine check behind the displayed midpoint recipe.
