Simpler proof assessment

Likely alternative prerequisite level: High school only; lowest recorded proof level: Undergraduate level. Bezout gives integer combinations of the denominations representing every residue modulo a_1. Negative coefficients of a_i (i>1) can be made nonnegative by adding multiples of a_1, which does not change the residue. Choose one nonnegative sum s_r for each of the finitely many residues. Every integer at least max_r s_r equals s_r plus a nonnegative multiple of a_1. This includes a_1=1 and needs elementary integer arithmetic, not asymptotic counting.

Statement

Let a_1,\ldots,a_n be positive integers with \gcd(a_1,\ldots,a_n)=1. Every sufficiently large integer is a sum c_1a_1+\cdots+c_na_n with nonnegative integers c_i.

Proof 1: Positivity of the asymptotic count — outline

Use the stated Schur asymptotic for the number of these representations: \frac{x^{n-1}}{(n-1)!a_1\cdots a_n}(1+o(1)), as the integer x tends to infinity. For the positive denominations in the coin application, the leading factor is positive, and the factor tending to one is eventually positive. Thus the representation count is eventually positive, giving at least one representation for every sufficiently large integer.
