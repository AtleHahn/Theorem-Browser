Statement

For real |x|<1, (1+x)^{-1/2}=\sum_{k=0}^\infty\binom{k-1/2}{k}(-1)^kx^k, where \binom rk=r(r-1)\cdots(r-k+1)/k! for k\ge1 and \binom r0=1.

Proof 1: Specialize the generalized binomial series — outline

Use (1+x)^r=\sum_{k=0}^\infty\binom rk x^k for real r and |x|<1, where \binom rk=r(r-1)\cdots(r-k+1)/k! and \binom r0=1.

With r = −s, we have for |x| < 1:

{\displaystyle {\frac {1}{(1+x)^{s}}}=\sum _{k=0}^{\infty }{\binom {-s}{k}}x^{k}=\sum _{k=0}^{\infty }{\binom {s+k-1}{k}}(-1)^{k}x^{k}.}

So, for instance, when s = 1/2,

{\displaystyle {\frac {1}{\sqrt {1+x}}}=1-{\frac {1}{2}}x+{\frac {3}{8}}x^{2}-{\frac {5}{16}}x^{3}+{\frac {35}{128}}x^{4}-{\frac {63}{256}}x^{5}+\cdots .}
