Statement

Let R be a circular rectangle, a region bounded by a cycle of four tangent circles. In Brooks’s alternating-chain process, pack a maximal chain tangent to the top and bottom sides, starting at the left side, then repeat in the remaining circular rectangle with the two side directions interchanged. Let r_1,r_2,\ldots be the successive chain counts and r(R)=[r_1;r_2,\ldots] the resulting simple continued fraction (finite if the process terminates, and otherwise the limit of its finite truncations). Then r(R) is rational if and only if this process terminates in a finite triangulated packing of R, meaning a packing whose remaining gaps are circular triangles.

Proof 1: Finite versus infinite simple continued fractions — outline

First pack a chain of circles tangent to the top and bottom sides of {\displaystyle R}, with the leftmost circle in the chain tangent to the left side of {\displaystyle R}; let {\displaystyle r_{1}} be the number of circles that can be packed in this way without overlapping the right side of {\displaystyle R}. Unless it completes the packing, this chain leaves a smaller circular rectangle between its rightmost circle and the right side of the starting rectangle; pack this rectangle recursively in the same way with another chain of circles, tangent to its left and right sides, and let {\displaystyle r_{2}} be the number of circles in this second chain. This packing process may continue forever, giving an infinite sequence of positive integers {\displaystyle r_{i}}, the number of circles in each chain, or it may terminate after a finite number of stages with a triangulated packing. Regardless, Brooks defines his invariant {\displaystyle r(R)} to be the simple continued fraction

{\displaystyle r(R)=r_{1}+{\frac {1}{r_{2}+{\frac {1}{r_{3}+\cdots }}}}.}

This is a rational number if and only if this packing process terminates with a finite triangulated packing.

Here a finite simple continued fraction is rational, whereas an infinite one with positive integer partial quotients after the first is irrational.
