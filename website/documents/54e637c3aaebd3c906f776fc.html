Statement

Let L_0=\{a^n b^m c^m:m,n\ge1\} and L_1=\{a^m b^m c^n:m,n\ge1\}. The context-free language L=L_0\cup L_1 is inherently ambiguous: every context-free grammar generating it has a word with two distinct parse trees.

Proof 1: Overlapping pumped parse-tree yields — outline

We use the following grammar form of Ogden's lemma as an external theorem. For every context-free grammar there is an integer p\ge1 such that a word with at least p marked positions has a derivation

S\Rightarrow^*uAv\Rightarrow^*uxAyv\Rightarrow^*uxzyv,

where z contains a marked position, xzy contains at most p marked positions, and either u,x both contain marked positions or y,v both do. The derivation includes the repeatable step A\Rightarrow^*xAy.

Fix any grammar generating L and put N=p!+p. Apply the lemma to a^N b^p c^p, marking the b and c positions. The source omits the routine checks of the lemma's conditions which force the form

u=a^N b^{p-s-k},\quad x=b^k,\quad z=b^s c^{s'},\quad y=c^k,\quad v=c^{p-s'-k},

with s,s'\ge0, s+s'\ge1, k\ge1 and s+s'+2k\le p.

Insert p!/k additional copies of A\Rightarrow^*xAy. This is an integer because 1\le k\le p. The resulting word is a^N b^N c^N, and one node of its parse tree has yield

x^{p!/k+1}zy^{p!/k+1}=b^{p!+k+s}c^{p!+k+s'}.

Symmetrically, starting from a^p b^p c^N with the a,b positions marked gives a parse of the same word containing a node whose yield is

a^{p!+k''+s''}b^{p!+k''+s^{\prime\prime\prime}}.

The two displayed yields overlap in the b block since

(p!+k+s)+(p!+k''+s^{\prime\prime\prime})>2p!+1>p!+p=N.

Neither yield contains the other: one extends into the a block and the other into the c block. Node yields in a single parse tree are nested or disjoint, so these are distinct parse trees. Since the grammar was arbitrary, L is inherently ambiguous.
