Statement

In the Gaussian integers \mathbb Z[i]=\{a+bi:a,b\in\mathbb Z\}, write N(a+bi)=a^2+b^2. A Gaussian integer is odd if its norm is odd, and primary if it is congruent to 1 modulo (1+i)^3. The units are 1,-1,i,-i.

For an odd Gaussian prime \pi and \alpha not divisible by \pi, define the quartic residue symbol [\alpha/\pi] to be the unique member of \{1,-1,i,-i\} satisfying

[\alpha/\pi]\equiv\alpha^{(N\pi-1)/4}\pmod\pi.

For an odd primary nonunit \beta=\prod_j\pi_j^{e_j} factored into primary primes and \alpha relatively prime to \beta, set [\alpha/\beta]=\prod_j[\alpha/\pi_j]^{e_j}.

If \alpha and \beta are relatively prime primary nonunits, then

[\alpha/\beta]\,[\beta/\alpha]^{-1}=(-1)^{((N\alpha-1)/4)((N\beta-1)/4)}.

The supplementary laws for a primary prime \pi=a+bi are

[i/\pi]=i^{-(a-1)/2},\qquad [\,(1+i)/\pi\,]=i^{(a-b-1-b^2)/4}.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Undergraduate elementary number theory and algebra, plus the explicitly given Gaussian symbol and primary normalization, suffice; class field theory is unnecessary.
