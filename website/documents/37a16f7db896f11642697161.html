Proof 1: Set one half-arc angle to a right angle

For positive half-arc angles \theta_1,\theta_2,\theta_3,\theta_4 of a cyclic quadrilateral, with \theta_1+\theta_2+\theta_3+\theta_4=\pi, Ptolemy's theorem has the trigonometric form

\sin\theta_1\sin\theta_3+\sin\theta_2\sin\theta_4 =\sin(\theta_1+\theta_2)\sin(\theta_1+\theta_4).

Let {\displaystyle \theta _{1}=90^{\circ }}. Then {\displaystyle \theta _{2}+(\theta _{3}+\theta _{4})=90^{\circ }}. Hence,

{\displaystyle \sin \theta _{1}\sin \theta _{3}+\sin \theta _{2}\sin \theta _{4}=\sin(\theta _{3}+\theta _{2})\sin(\theta _{3}+\theta _{4})}{\displaystyle \sin \theta _{3}+\sin \theta _{2}\cos(\theta _{2}+\theta _{3})=\sin(\theta _{3}+\theta _{2})\cos \theta _{2}}{\displaystyle \sin \theta _{3}=\sin(\theta _{3}+\theta _{2})\cos \theta _{2}-\cos(\theta _{2}+\theta _{3})\sin \theta _{2}}

Formula for compound angle sine (−).

Here take \theta_2=\beta, \theta_3=\alpha-\beta and \theta_4=\pi/2-\alpha.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Every hypothesis of the supplied four-angle identity is checked by the ordered-angle assumptions. The rest is a substitution, complementary-angle identities and rearrangement. No calculus or theorem about analytic trigonometric functions is required.

Statement used:

For positive theta_1,theta_2,theta_3,theta_4 summing to pi, sin(theta_1)sin(theta_3)+sin(theta_2)sin(theta_4)=sin(theta_1+theta_2)sin(theta_1+theta_4).

Application in proof:

Set theta_1=pi/2, theta_2=beta, theta_3=alpha-beta, theta_4=pi/2-alpha. The ordered acute-angle assumptions ensure all four are positive and their sum is pi; rearrange and replace complementary sines by cosines.

Application level:

Level justification:

The dependency is an explicit identity in sines of positive angles. Its use requires angle arithmetic and familiar trigonometric values, not a proof of Ptolemy’s theorem.
