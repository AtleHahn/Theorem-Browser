Statement

For an event determined by a sequence (X_n)_{n\ge1}, invariance under every finite permutation of the indices is equivalent to invariance under each transposition (i,j).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

An event of an infinite random sequence requires the measurable-event interpretation in addition to coordinate permutations. These are limited set/probability definitions; no probability limit theorem is used.

Proof 1: Decompose finite permutations into transpositions

Since any finite permutation can be written as a product of transpositions, if we wish to check whether or not an event A is symmetric (invariant under finite permutations), it is enough to check if its occurrence is unchanged by an arbitrary transposition (i,j), i,j\in\mathbb N.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Compose transpositions to obtain any finite permutation and preserve the event at each step. Undergraduate permutations and set invariance suffice; the probability law is not used.
