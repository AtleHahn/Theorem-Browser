Statement

The polynomial f(z)=z+z^2 is holomorphic on the open unit disk, has f(0)=0, f'(0)=1, and Taylor coefficients satisfying |a_n|\le n for every n\ge2, but is not injective on the disk.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Calculus and complex arithmetic suffice after learning that holomorphic means complex differentiable. The statements concern explicit constant or polynomial maps, or coefficients of a given convergent power series; no further complex-analytic machinery is needed to understand the claim.

Proof 1: Two equal values around the quadratic vertex

The coefficient condition |a_n|\le n is not sufficient to show the normalized holomorphic function is injective, as the function

{\displaystyle f(z)=z+z^{2}=(z+1/2)^{2}-1/4}

shows: it is holomorphic on the unit disc and satisfies {\displaystyle |a_{n}|\leq n} for all {\displaystyle n}, but it is not injective since {\displaystyle f(-1/2+w)=f(-1/2-w)}.

For sufficiently small nonzero w, both arguments lie in the unit disk and are distinct.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The noninjectivity is the elementary quadratic symmetry, for example f(-1/4)=f(-3/4). Verifying the full counterexample also requires that a complex polynomial is holomorphic and has the usual derivative and Taylor coefficients. These are a small extension of undergraduate calculus and complex-number algebra; no univalent-function theory or proof of Bieberbach’s theorem is used.
