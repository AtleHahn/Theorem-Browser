Statement

For any propositions P,Q in classical two-valued propositional logic,

(P\to Q)\ \leftrightarrow\ (\neg P\lor Q).

Thus either expression can replace the other in a classical logical proof. Here \neg negates the truth value, \lor is inclusive “or”, P\to Q is false exactly when P is true and Q false, and \leftrightarrow asserts equality of truth values.

Proof 1: Excluded middle and the converse

Assume P\to Q. By excluded middle either \neg P or P holds. In the first case \neg P\lor Q holds; in the second, the given implication yields Q, so again \neg P\lor Q holds.

Conversely assume \neg P\lor Q. If P is true, the first disjunct is ruled out, so Q is true. Hence P\to Q.
