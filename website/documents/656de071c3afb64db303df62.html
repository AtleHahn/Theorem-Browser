Statement

Let U\subset\mathbb C be open, and suppose f:U\to\mathbb C has a single-valued holomorphic antiderivative F on U, so F'=f. If two piecewise C^1 paths in U have the same initial point a and final point b, their integrals of f agree.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Calculus and complex arithmetic supply the formulas, curves and estimates. Complex differentiability, holomorphic logarithm branches or conformal bijections are closely related extensions; the contour integral can be understood by parametrization and the branch is explicitly fixed where needed. No Riemann surfaces, residues or differential forms are required by these statements.

Proof 1: Read the endpoints in the complex fundamental theorem

The fundamental theorem for complex line integrals states: if F(z) is a holomorphic function on an open region U, and \gamma is a piecewise C^1 curve in U from a to b, then

\int_\gamma F'(z)\,dz=F(b)-F(a).

Thus, when f(z) has a single-valued antiderivative F in an open region U, the path integral \int_\gamma f(z)\,dz is path independent: for either path from a to b, it equals F(b)-F(a).

Level justification

Apply the explicitly supplied endpoint formula to both paths. A complex derivative and complex-valued line integral can be understood from real vector calculus for this antiderivative application; no contour deformation, analytic continuation or existence theorem for primitives is used.
