Statement

Let k be a nonzero commutative ring with identity and let I=(z^2-xy-yx,zx-xz,zy-yz) in k\langle x,y,z\rangle. Use degree-lexicographic order with x<y<z and the corresponding reductions z^2\mapsto xy+yx, zx\mapsto xz, zy\mapsto yz. Then yx^2-x^2y\in I and y^2x-xy^2\in I.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Noncommuting word polynomials and their two-sided quotient ideal extend undergraduate ring algebra; the word order and reductions are explicit.

Proof 1: Subtract pairs of congruent word reductions

All the following equalities are in the quotient k\langle x,y,z\rangle/I.

Let us consider {\displaystyle z^{2}x}. If we resolve {\displaystyle z^{2}} first we get,

{\displaystyle z^{2}x=(xy+yx)x=xyx+yx^{2},}

which contains no leading words and is therefore reduced. Resolving {\displaystyle zx} first we obtain,

{\displaystyle z^{2}x=zxz=xz^{2}=x(xy+yx)=x^{2}y+xyx.}

This gives us {\displaystyle h_{1}=xyx+yx^{2}} and {\displaystyle h_{2}=x^{2}y+xyx}. The new relation is therefore {\displaystyle h_{1}-h_{2}=yx^{2}-x^{2}y\in I}.

Again, resolving {\displaystyle z^{2}} first we obtain,

{\displaystyle z^{2}y=(xy+yx)y=xy^{2}+yxy.}

On the other hand resolving {\displaystyle zy} twice first and then {\displaystyle z^{2}} we find,

{\displaystyle z^{2}y=zyz=yz^{2}=y(xy+yx)=yxy+y^{2}x.}

Thus we have {\displaystyle h_{3}=xy^{2}+yxy} and {\displaystyle h_{4}=yxy+y^{2}x} and the new relation is {\displaystyle h_{3}-h_{4}=xy^{2}-y^{2}x} with leading word {\displaystyle y^{2}x}. Since the coefficient of the leading word is -1 we scale the relation and then add {\displaystyle y^{2}x-xy^{2}} to the set of defining relations.
