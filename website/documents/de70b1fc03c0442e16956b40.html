Statement

Let f:\mathbb R\to[0,\infty) be measurable with \int_{\mathbb R}f(x)\,dx=1, let g be integrable for the probability measure f(x)\,dx, and let \varphi:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R be convex. Then \varphi\!\left(\int_{\mathbb R}g(x)f(x)\,dx\right)\le\int_{\mathbb R}\varphi(g(x))f(x)\,dx. In particular this applies to g(x)=x when the density has a finite first absolute moment.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Arbitrary measurable probability densities and integrability against their measures require Lebesgue integration.

Proof 1: Use the measure with the given density

The nonnegative normalized function f is a probability density. Apply measure-theoretic Jensen with this probability measure; its expectations are exactly the displayed weighted integrals. The identity choice g(x)=x gives the stated special case.
