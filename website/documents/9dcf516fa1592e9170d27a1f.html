Statement

Let X have finite mean \mu_x and standard deviation \sigma_x>0. For prescribed real \mu_y and \sigma_y\ge0, the variable Z=\sigma_y(X-\mu_x)/\sigma_x+\mu_y has mean \mu_y and standard deviation \sigma_y.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The claim uses mean and standard deviation and an affine change of variable. Their definitions as expectation and square-root variance can be connected directly to weighted averages and calculus; this is a small addition to the undergraduate baseline.

Mathematical Field
Probability theory
Required topics
Real random variablesExpectationVariance and standard deviation
Review notes
none

Proof 1: Center, scale and shift (outline)

If X has respective mean and standard deviation \mu_x,\sigma_x, the formula is

\widehat Y=\sigma_y\frac{X-\mu_x}{\sigma_x}+\mu_y,

which has mean \mu_y and standard deviation \sigma_y. Here \widehat Y=Z in the statement. The source states this moment-matching property without displaying the moment calculations.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The argument uses the definitions of mean, variance and standard deviation and their affine transformation formulas. These are ordinary undergraduate probability concepts; no measure-theoretic probability is needed to understand this computation. Finite positive input standard deviation permits division, and the nonnegative prescribed output standard deviation gives the claimed square root of the variance.

Kind:

Theorem

Treatment:

Implicitly assumed

Statement used:

For a real random variable X with finite second moment and real constants a,b, E[aX+b]=a E[X]+b and Var(aX+b)=a^2 Var(X).

Application in proof:

Set a=sigma_Y/sigma_X and b=mu_Y-a mu_X to obtain the two prescribed moments.

Application level:

Undergraduate level

Level justification:

These basic moment identities follow directly from linearity of expectation and the definition of variance.
