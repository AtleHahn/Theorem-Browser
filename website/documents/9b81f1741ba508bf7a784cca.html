Statement

For n≥1 and variables x_1,\ldots,x_n, let p_k=\sum_jx_j^k for k≥1, let e_0=1, and let e_k=\sum_{j_1<\cdots<j_k}x_{j_1}\cdots x_{j_k}, with e_k=0 for k>n. Let h_0=1 and h_k=\sum_{a_1+\cdots+a_n=k,\ a_j\ge0}x_1^{a_1}\cdots x_n^{a_n}. For k≥1 let M be k×k with M_{ij}=p_{i-j+1} for j≤i, M_{i,i+1}=−i, and other entries zero. Then h_k=det(M)/k!.

Proof 1: Symmetric-polynomial determinant: h from p — outline

Consider the first k complete homogeneous counterparts of Newton's identities,

j h_j=\sum_{i=1}^j h_{j-i}p_i,\qquad 1\le j\le k,

as linear equations in which the power sums are known and the complete homogeneous symmetric functions are unknown, and apply Cramer's rule to find the solution for the final unknown. The analogous computation gives

h_k=\frac1{k!} \begin{vmatrix} p_1&-1&0&\cdots\\ p_2&p_1&-2&0&\cdots\\ \vdots&&\ddots&\ddots\\ p_{k-1}&p_{k-2}&\cdots&p_1&1-k\\ p_k&p_{k-1}&\cdots&p_2&p_1 \end{vmatrix}.

The source leaves the intermediate computation implicit. Note that the use of determinants makes the formula for h_k have additional minus signs compared to the one for e_k.
