Statement

There is no constant B such that every finite simple planar graph with an edge has an edge uv with \deg(u)+\deg(v)\le B. The degree of a vertex is its number of neighbors; planar means that the graph can be drawn in the plane without crossing edges.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Counting neighbors in complete bipartite graphs is elementary finite combinatorics.

Proof 1: Use stars and two-vertex complete bipartite graphs

The complete bipartite graph K_{p,q} has two disjoint vertex classes of sizes p and q, all edges between classes, and none within a class. For n\ge4, the planar graphs K_{1,n-1} and K_{2,n-2} have every edge joining vertices of degrees 1,n-1 and 2,n-2, respectively. Thus each edge has endpoint-degree sum n. Since n is arbitrarily large, no universal bound exists.
