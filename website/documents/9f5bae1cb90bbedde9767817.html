Statement

Assume the axiom of choice. The unit square Q=[0,1]^2 can be partitioned into finitely many pairwise disjoint sets A_1,\ldots,A_r,B_1,\ldots,B_s such that there are area-preserving affine maps g_i,h_j:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R^2 for which

Q=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^r g_i(A_i)=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^s h_j(B_j).

Thus the two disjoint subcollections each reassemble into a full copy of Q; one copy may be translated to make the copies disjoint in the plane. The same paradoxical decomposition exists for the unit disk. An area-preserving affine map has the form x\mapsto Mx+b with a real invertible 2\times2 matrix M satisfying |\det M|=1. The pieces are arbitrary sets of points; measurability is not required.

Level justification

Finite equidecomposition is explicit; planar affine maps and area determinants are linear algebra. Choice is a stated set-theoretic assumption, not a proof prerequisite hidden in the result.
