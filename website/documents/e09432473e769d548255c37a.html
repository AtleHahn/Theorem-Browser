Statement

For B\subseteq\mathbb Z_{\geq0} and an integer h\geq2, let r_{B,h}(n) count representations n=b_1+\cdots+b_h with b_1\leq\cdots\leq b_h in B, allowing repetitions. For every fixed h\geq2 there exist B and constants c_1,c_2>0 such that c_1\log n\leq r_{B,h}(n)\leq c_2\log n for all sufficiently large integers n.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves integer representation counts; finite sums with repetitions; logarithmic growth and eventual inequalities. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Specialize the general growth theorem to the logarithm — outline

Use the stated growth theorem: for fixed h\geq2, if a positive locally integrable real function f satisfies

\frac1x\int_1^x f(t)\,dt\asymp f(x),\qquad \log x\ll f(x)\ll x^{1/(h-1)}(\log x)^{-\varepsilon}

for some \varepsilon>0, then there is a set B with r_{B,h}(n)\asymp f(n). Here u\asymp v means that the ratio is bounded above and below by positive constants for sufficiently large arguments, and u\ll v means an eventual constant upper bound.

The case f(x)=\log x recovers r_{B,h}(n)\asymp\log n, as required.

Level justification

Insert the ordinary function log x into the fully stated growth theorem. Its averaged integral and comparison to positive powers are standard calculus and asymptotic analysis; the representation-existence proof is not invoked.
