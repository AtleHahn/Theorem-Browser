Statement

For a nontrivial irreducible unitary complex representation \Gamma:G\to U(d) of a finite group, \sum_{g\in G}\Gamma(g)_{ij}=0 for every matrix entry.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A unitary representation is a group homomorphism to unitary matrices; irreducible means no common proper nonzero invariant subspace. These definitions connect introductory group theory directly to linear algebra, and the conclusion is an explicit finite matrix-entry sum.

Proof 1: Compare with the trivial representation — outline

Use the Schur orthogonality relations: for unitary representatives \Gamma^{(\lambda)} of the irreducible equivalence classes,

\sum_{g\in G}\overline{\Gamma^{(\lambda)}(g)_{ij}} \Gamma^{(\mu)}(g)_{kl} =\delta_{\lambda\mu}\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}\frac{|G|}{d_\lambda},

where d_\lambda is the dimension of \Gamma^{(\lambda)} and the Kronecker delta is 1 for equal labels and 0 otherwise.

Every group has an identity representation (all group elements mapped to 1). This is an irreducible representation. The orthogonality relations immediately imply that

\sum_{g\in G}\Gamma^{(\mu)}(g)_{ij}=0

for i,j=1,\dots,d_\mu and any irreducible representation \Gamma^{(\mu)} not equal to the identity representation.

Level justification

Insert the one-dimensional trivial representation into the explicitly supplied coefficient orthogonality formula. Undergraduate groups and complex linear algebra suffice with a representation as a matrix-valued homomorphism and irreducibility as absence of a common proper invariant subspace; no character-theory proof is required.
