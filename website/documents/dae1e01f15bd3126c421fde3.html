Statement

Let X,Y be independent \mathbb R^n-valued random variables, where n\ge1. Suppose X has a Lebesgue density f:\mathbb R^n\to[0,\infty) satisfying f(x)=f(-x) and such that \{x:f(x)\ge t\} is convex for every t\ge0. Then for every compact convex set K\subseteq\mathbb R^n with nonempty interior and K=-K,

\Pr(X\in K)\ge\Pr(X+Y\in K).
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding arbitrary random vectors, density-based probabilities and independence alongside the measurable multivariate formulation exceeds the standard undergraduate baseline; no specialist probability course is assumed.

  • A useful concentration consequence, rated low as a direct application of the general integral theorem.

Proof 1: Symmetric intersection volumes and independent averaging (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Sections12–13, Theorems12.1 and13.1, pp.23–26).

For the measurable level sets in the statement, first intersect L_t=\{f\ge t\} with a centered closed ball. A convex set with empty interior lies in a hyperplane and has zero volume; otherwise its closure is a convex body and differs from it only on its null boundary. Thus the symmetric intersection-volume inequality applies to these truncations. Increasing the ball radius gives |K\cap L_t|\ge |(K-y)\cap L_t|. Integrating in t>0 gives \int_K f(x)\,dx\ge\int_K f(x-y)\,dx. Finally integrate this inequality against the probability law \mu_Y: independence and Tonelli give \Pr(X+Y\in K)=\int\!\int_K f(x-y)\,dx\,d\mu_Y(y). No density assumption on Y is needed.
