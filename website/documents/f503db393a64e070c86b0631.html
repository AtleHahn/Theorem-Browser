Statement

For a,b>0, J(a,b):=\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{dx}{a\cos^2x+b\sin^2x}=\frac\pi{2\sqrt{ab}}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Real definite integrals; Trigonometric functions. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Substitute the tangent

We calculate:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}J&=\int _{0}^{\pi /2}{\frac {1}{a\cos ^{2}x+b\sin ^{2}x}}dx\\[6pt]&=\int _{0}^{\pi /2}{\frac {\frac {1}{\cos ^{2}x}}{a+b{\frac {\sin ^{2}x}{\cos ^{2}x}}}}dx\\[6pt]&=\int _{0}^{\pi /2}{\frac {\sec ^{2}x}{a+b\tan ^{2}x}}dx\\[6pt]&={\frac {1}{b}}\int _{0}^{\pi /2}{\frac {1}{\left({\sqrt {\frac {a}{b}}}\right)^{2}+\tan ^{2}x}}\,d(\tan x)\\[6pt]&=\left.{\frac {1}{\sqrt {ab}}}\arctan \left({\sqrt {\frac {b}{a}}}\tan x\right)\right|_{0}^{\pi /2}\\[6pt]&={\frac {\pi }{2{\sqrt {ab}}}}.\end{aligned}}}

The endpoint value at x=\pi/2 is understood as a one-sided limit.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use tangent substitution and an arctangent antiderivative, evaluating the upper endpoint by a limit. Undergraduate integral calculus suffices.
