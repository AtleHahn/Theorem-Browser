Statement

Let \Omega\subset\mathbb R^n be a bounded region with smooth boundary, and let F be a smooth vector field near \overline\Omega. With outward unit normal \nu, \int_\Omega\operatorname{div}F\,dV=\int_{\partial\Omega}F\cdot\nu\,dS.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Divergence; Volume and flux integrals. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Contract the vector field with the volume form (outline)

Contract F with the Euclidean volume form to obtain the (n-1)-form \omega=\iota_F(dV). The source prescribes this identification: its exterior derivative corresponds to (\operatorname{div}F)dV, and its boundary restriction integrates as outward flux. Apply the general Stokes formula \int_\Omega d\omega=\int_{\partial\Omega}\omega.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Contract the volume form, take its exterior derivative and apply Stokes-Cartan with induced boundary orientation. Differential forms and oriented-manifold integration are substantial extra prerequisites beyond the elementary divergence theorem.
