Statement

Let m\ge1 and let (\mu_n) be Borel probability measures on \mathbb R^m. Suppose they are tight: for every \varepsilon>0 there is a compact K\subset\mathbb R^m with \mu_n(K)\ge1-\varepsilon for all n. Then a subsequence converges weakly to a Borel probability measure \mu on \mathbb R^m, meaning convergence of integrals of every bounded continuous real function.

Proof 1: Euclidean specialization of Prokhorov’s theorem (outline)

Apply the stated Prokhorov theorem to the separable metric space \mathbb R^m and the collection \{\mu_n:n\ge1\}. Its tightness implies that its closure in the space of Borel probability measures is sequentially compact for weak convergence. The defining subsequence property of sequential compactness gives the required subsequence and probability-measure limit.
