Statement

For an integer n\ge1 and prime p, let R(n,p) be the exponent of p in \binom{2n}{n}. Then p^{R(n,p)}\le2n.

Proof 1: Bound the zero-one terms in Legendre’s formula

The exponent of {\displaystyle p} in {\displaystyle n!} is given by Legendre's formula

{\displaystyle \sum _{j=1}^{\infty }\left\lfloor {\frac {n}{p^{j}}}\right\rfloor \!,}

so

{\displaystyle R(n,p)=\sum _{j=1}^{\infty }\left\lfloor {\frac {2n}{p^{j}}}\right\rfloor -2\sum _{j=1}^{\infty }\left\lfloor {\frac {n}{p^{j}}}\right\rfloor =\sum _{j=1}^{\infty }\left(\left\lfloor {\frac {2n}{p^{j}}}\right\rfloor -2\!\left\lfloor {\frac {n}{p^{j}}}\right\rfloor \right)}

But each term of the last summation must be either zero (if {\displaystyle n/p^{j}{\bmod {1}}<1/2}) or one (if {\displaystyle n/p^{j}{\bmod {1}}\geq 1/2}), and all terms with {\displaystyle j>\log _{p}(2n)} are zero. Therefore,

{\displaystyle R(n,p)\leq \log _{p}(2n),}

and

{\displaystyle p^{R(n,p)}\leq p^{\log _{p}(2n)}=2n.}
Level
High school only
Level justification

Each floor difference is zero or one, and only exponents j<=log_p(2n) can contribute. Bounding their finite count and exponentiating yields the claim. This is integer arithmetic, floors and elementary logarithms.
