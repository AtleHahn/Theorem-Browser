Statement

Let k\ge0 be an integer, a\ne x real, and let f have continuous derivatives through order k on the closed interval joining a,x, with derivative f^{(k+1)} on its interior. Put P_k(x)=\sum_{j=0}^k f^{(j)}(a)(x-a)^j/j! and R_k(x)=f(x)-P_k(x). For some \xi strictly between a,x, R_k(x)=\frac{f^{(k+1)}(\xi)}{k!}(x-\xi)^k(x-a).

Proof 1: Use a linear test function

Choose G(t)=t-a.

Define, on the closed interval joining a and x,

F(t)=\sum_{j=0}^k\frac{f^{(j)}(t)}{j!}(x-t)^j=f(t)+f'(t)(x-t)+\frac{f''(t)}{2!}(x-t)^2+\cdots+ \frac{f^{(k)}(t)}{k!}(x-t)^k.

The expansion is truncated at order k (so F(t)=f(t) when k=0). By Cauchy's mean value theorem,

\frac{F'(\xi)}{G'(\xi)} =\frac{F(x)-F(a)}{G(x)-G(a)} \tag{*}

for some \xi on the open interval between a and x. The nonvanishing derivative of G ensures G(x)\ne G(a) by Rolle's theorem. Note that the numerator F(x)-F(a)=R_k(x) is exactly the remainder of the Taylor polynomial for y=f(x). Compute

\begin{aligned} F'(t) &=f'(t)+ \sum_{j=1}^{k}\left( \frac{f^{(j+1)}(t)}{j!}(x-t)^j -\frac{f^{(j)}(t)}{(j-1)!}(x-t)^{j-1} \right)\\ &=\frac{f^{(k+1)}(t)}{k!}(x-t)^k. \end{aligned}

(The sum is empty when k=0.) Plug this into (*) and rearrange terms to find that

R_k(x)=\frac{f^{(k+1)}(\xi)}{k!}(x-\xi)^k \frac{G(x)-G(a)}{G'(\xi)}.

Here G'(\xi)=1 and G(x)-G(a)=x-a, giving the Cauchy form of the remainder.
