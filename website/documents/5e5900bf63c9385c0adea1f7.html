Statement

Let X=\sum_{i=1}^nX_i for independent zero-one variables with success probabilities p_i, and put \mu=\sum_i p_i. For \delta\ge0,

\Pr(X\ge(1+\delta)\mu)\le\exp\left(-\frac{\delta^2\mu}{2+\delta}\right).
Level
High school only
Level justification

Only finite trials and explicit real functions occur in the statement.

Proof 1: Apply the positive logarithmic inequality — outline

For \delta>0, use the multiplicative Chernoff upper bound in logarithmic form,

\Pr(X\ge(1+\delta)\mu)\le\exp\{[\delta-(1+\delta)\log(1+\delta)]\mu\}.

Apply the stated logarithmic inequality \log(1+\delta)\ge2\delta/(2+\delta) to obtain the claimed weaker bound. For \delta=0, it is the trivial probability bound by one.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Insert the given logarithmic inequality into the explicit Chernoff exponent and handle delta zero separately. Undergraduate elementary probability and exponential estimates suffice.
