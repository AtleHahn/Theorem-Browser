Statement

Let (a_n)_{n\ge1} be a monotone real sequence tending to zero. Then \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^n a_n converges.

Level

Proof 1: Use bounded alternating partial sums

Use Dirichlet's test: if (a_n) is a monotonic sequence of real numbers tending to zero and (b_n) is a sequence of real or complex numbers with bounded partial sums, then \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_nb_n converges.

A particular case of Dirichlet's test is the more commonly used alternating series test for the case

{\displaystyle b_{n}=(-1)^{n}\Longrightarrow \left|\sum _{n=1}^{N}b_{n}\right|\leq 1.}
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The argument verifies that partial sums of (-1)^n have absolute value at most one, then applies the explicitly stated Dirichlet test to the given monotone sequence tending to zero. Undergraduate series and convergence notions suffice; the proof of Dirichlet’s test is not part of this argument.
