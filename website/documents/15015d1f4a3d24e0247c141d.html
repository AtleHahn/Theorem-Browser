Statement

Let a finite region of a square checkerboard admit a cyclic ordering of all its squares in which consecutive squares, including the last and first, share an edge. Delete one black and one white square. Then the remaining region can be tiled by grid-aligned dominoes.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The cycle is defined as a finite ordering of adjacent squares; only parity and pairing are needed.

Proof 1: Pairing along the remaining paths

The removal of any two oppositely colored squares splits the given cycle into one or two nonempty paths with an even number of squares each. These paths are easy to partition into dominoes by following them. If no squares remain, the empty tiling suffices.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Adjacent squares alternate colors. Between the two deleted opposite-color squares, each remaining segment of the cycle has an even number of squares, so consecutive pairs form dominoes. Adjacent deletions leave a single path, and deleting all squares permits the empty tiling. This uses finite counting and parity only.
