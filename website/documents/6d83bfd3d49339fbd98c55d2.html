Statement

Consider estimation of a real parameter \theta in a statistical model with finite positive Fisher information I(\theta). Let \mathcal U be a class of unbiased estimators with finite variances satisfying the scalar Cramér–Rao regularity hypotheses, so the unbiased bound applies to every member. If T\in\mathcal U is fully efficient, meaning \operatorname{Var}_\theta(T)=I(\theta)^{-1} for each parameter value, then for every S\in\mathcal U and every parameter value, E_\theta[(T-\theta)^2]\le E_\theta[(S-\theta)^2] and \operatorname{Var}_\theta(T)\le\operatorname{Var}_\theta(S). Thus T is minimum-variance unbiased within \mathcal U.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The complete statement requires a parameterized statistical model, unbiasedness, Fisher information, finite estimator variance, and simultaneous comparison over a class for every parameter value. The regularity assumption is explicit and restricts the estimator class; the claim does not assert that every arbitrary unbiased estimator qualifies. In a differentiable dominated model, the relevant score identities are E_theta[U_theta]=0 and E_theta[(S-theta)U_theta]=1, with finite positive E_theta[U_theta^2]=I(theta). Differentiation under the normalization and estimator-expectation integrals is a standard sufficient route to these identities. Different sufficient regularity packages do not change the conditional comparison being asserted. Proof 1: Compare the attained lower bound with every unbiased estimator

An unbiased estimator achieving the Cramér–Rao bound attains the lowest possible mean squared error among the unbiased estimators to which the bound applies. Indeed, their mean squared errors equal their variances, and the bound puts every such variance at least I(\theta)^{-1}, the value attained by T. It is therefore a minimum-variance unbiased estimator.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

For S and T in the stipulated regular unbiased class, MSE_theta(S)=Var_theta(S) and Var_theta(S)>=1/I(theta)=Var_theta(T)=MSE_theta(T). This proves exactly the two claimed inequalities and minimum variance within that class. No claim about biased or nonregular estimators is used. Understanding the statistical model, information and regularity-restricted quantifiers is required even though the numerical comparison is elementary.
