Statement

Let \omega=e^{2\pi i/3} and T=\mathbb C/(\mathbb Z+\mathbb Z\omega) with its induced Euclidean metric. Its systole (least length of a noncontractible loop) is 1, its area is \sqrt3/2, and hence \operatorname{sys}(T)^2=(2/\sqrt3)\operatorname{area}(T).

Proof 1: Hexagonal lattice minimum and fundamental area — outline

Use the flat-torus fact that the systole is the length of a shortest nonzero lattice vector, and the area is that of a fundamental parallelogram. A lattice vector a+b\omega, with a,b\in\mathbb Z, has squared length a^2-ab+b^2. For a nonzero vector this is a positive integer, hence at least 1, and the vector 1 attains 1. The parallelogram spanned by 1 and \omega has area |\operatorname{Im}\omega|=\sqrt3/2. These values give the asserted equality.
