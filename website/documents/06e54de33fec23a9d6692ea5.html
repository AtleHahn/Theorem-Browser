Statement

Let a chord AC of a circle with center M and radius r contain an interior point S. Set d=|MS|. Then |AS|\,|SC|=r^2-d^2.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement involves ordinary lengths and a quadratic identity.

Proof 1: Comparison with a diameter

By applying the chord theorem to a diameter going through the circle's center M and point S, the following relationship can be made:

|AS|\cdot|SC|=(r+d)\cdot(r-d)=r^2-d^2,

where r is the radius of the circle and d is the distance between the circle's center M and point S.

If the chord itself is a diameter, its two pieces already have lengths r+d and r-d, giving the same product directly. When S=M, one may choose any other diameter for the comparison.

Level

Level justification

Intersect with a diameter through S and use the chord-product equality. The diameter pieces have lengths r+d and r-d, whose product is r²-d²; the coincident-diameter and center cases are addressed. This is elementary circle geometry.
