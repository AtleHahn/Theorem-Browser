Statement

Fix a finitary algebraic signature: a set of operation symbols, each having a specified finite arity, possibly zero. An algebra of this signature is a nonempty set with interpretations of those operations. A subalgebra is a nonempty subset closed under all operations, including the constants. A homomorphism preserves all operations. A congruence \Phi on A is an equivalence relation compatible with every operation: applying an operation to componentwise \Phi-equivalent tuples gives \Phi-equivalent outputs. The quotient algebra A/\Phi has equivalence classes as elements and operations induced on representatives.

For an algebra A, let \operatorname{Con}(A) be its congruences ordered by inclusion. This is a complete lattice: every family has a greatest lower bound and a least upper bound. If \Phi\in\operatorname{Con}(A), then

\{\Psi\in\operatorname{Con}(A):\Phi\subseteq\Psi\}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Con}(A/\Phi),\qquad\Psi\longmapsto\Psi/\Phi

is a lattice isomorphism, where

\Psi/\Phi=\{([a]_\Phi,[b]_\Phi):(a,b)\in\Psi\}.

It is an order-preserving bijection whose inverse is also order-preserving; consequently it preserves all least upper and greatest lower bounds within these lattices.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The quantification over arbitrary algebraic signatures, compatible equivalence relations and quotient structures adds several interdependent abstractions beyond introductory group and ring theory. The complete formulation is conservatively beyond undergraduate despite its short analogies with groups.
