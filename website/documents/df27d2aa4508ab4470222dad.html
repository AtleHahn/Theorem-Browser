Statement

Let G be a finite group of even order n, and let g\in G have order k. Left multiplication by g is an odd permutation exactly when n/k is odd, equivalently when the cyclic subgroup generated by g has odd index.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory group theory supplies cosets, subgroup index and element order; linear algebra or permutation theory supplies permutation sign. The action is explicitly multiplication on cosets, requiring no representation theory.

Proof 1: Count the even-length orbits

In general, for any finite group G of order n, it is straightforward to determine the signature of the permutation πg made by left-multiplication by the element g of G. The permutation πg will be even, unless there are an odd number of orbits of even size. Assuming n even, therefore, the condition for πg to be an odd permutation, when g has order k, is that n/k should be odd, or that the subgroup <g> generated by g should have odd index.

Level justification

Partition left multiplication into n/k cycles of length k and multiply their signs. Undergraduate group theory supplies cyclic subgroup cosets and permutation parity.
