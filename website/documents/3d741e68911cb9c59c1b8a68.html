Statement

Let A be a real symmetric n\times n matrix, and let U\in\mathbb R^{n\times m} have orthonormal columns, 1\le m\le n. The compression is B=U^TAU. If the eigenvalues are ordered increasingly as \alpha_1\le\cdots\le\alpha_n and \beta_1\le\cdots\le\beta_m, then \alpha_j\le\beta_j\le\alpha_{n-m+j} for 1\le j\le m.

Proof 1: Compare two compressed eigenvector subspaces

This can be proven using the min-max principle. Choose corresponding eigenvectors bi for the eigenvalues βi to form an orthonormal basis and Sj be the j dimensional subspace Sj = span{b1, ..., bj}, then

{\displaystyle \beta _{j}=\max _{x\in S_{j},\|x\|=1}(Bx,x)=\max _{x\in S_{j},\|x\|=1}(U^TAUx,x)\geq \min _{\substack{V\subseteq\mathbb R^n\\\dim V=j}}\max _{y\in V,\|y\|=1}(Ay,y)=\alpha _{j}.}

According to the first part of the Courant–Fischer min-max theorem, αj ≤ βj. On the other hand, if we define Sm−j+1 = span{bj, ..., bm}, then

{\displaystyle \beta _{j}=\min _{x\in S_{m-j+1},\|x\|=1}(Bx,x)=\min _{x\in S_{m-j+1},\|x\|=1}(U^TAUx,x)=\min _{x\in S_{m-j+1},\|x\|=1}(A(Ux),Ux)\leq \alpha _{n-m+j},}

where US_{m-j+1} has dimension m-j+1, and the last inequality is given by the second part of the Courant–Fischer min-max theorem.

Here (x,y)=x^Ty is the Euclidean inner product, and U preserves lengths.

Level justification

Choose orthonormal eigenvectors of the symmetric compression and compare their Rayleigh quotients after the length-preserving embedding U. The supplied min-max formulas give both bounds. Finite-dimensional spectral theory and subspaces are standard undergraduate linear algebra.
