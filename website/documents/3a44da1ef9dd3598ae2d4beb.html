Statement

Every finite noncollinear point set in the real projective plane, and hence every such set in the Euclidean plane, determines at least three ordinary lines.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Real projective points/lines and their incidence duality are a limited homogeneous-coordinate extension of undergraduate linear algebra.

Proof 1: Dualize Melchior’s ordinary vertices

Dualize the points to a nontrivial line arrangement. Melchior’s inequality gives at least three vertices incident with exactly two lines. Projective duality turns those into three ordinary lines of the original point set. For Euclidean input points, connecting lines remain Euclidean lines.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Dualize points and lines in the real projective plane and apply the exact ordinary-vertex consequence of Melchior. Homogeneous coordinates from linear algebra plus elementary incidence duality are a small extension; no proof of the topological inequality is needed.
