Statement

Let d\geq1 and let X_1,X_2,\ldots be independent \mathbb R^d-valued random variables on one probability space. Put S_n=\sum_{j=1}^nX_j. There exists a random vector S on that probability space for which S_n\to S almost surely if and only if there is a Borel probability measure \mu on \mathbb R^d such that

\mathbb E e^{i\langle z,S_n\rangle}\longrightarrow\int_{\mathbb R^d}e^{i\langle z,x\rangle}\,\mu(dx)\qquad\text{for every }z\in\mathbb R^d.

No symmetry or identical-distribution hypothesis is required. Here \langle z,x\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^d z_jx_j, i^2=-1, and almost-sure convergence means Euclidean-norm convergence outside a probability-zero event. The integral is the characteristic function of the probability measure \mu.
