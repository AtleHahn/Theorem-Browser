Statement

Every infinite simple undirected graph G=(V,E) contains either a countably infinite independent set or a clique of cardinality |V|.

Here E consists of unordered pairs of distinct vertices. An independent set has no edge joining two of its vertices, and a clique has an edge joining every two distinct vertices. Countably infinite means in bijection with \mathbb N; a clique of cardinality |V| has a bijection with the entire vertex set V.

Equivalently, every infinite graph contains either a countably infinite clique or an independent set of cardinality |V|.

Level justification

Only elementary graph notions plus countability and arbitrary cardinal comparison are needed; the latter is standard undergraduate set theory. No ordinal partition calculus or forcing is needed.
