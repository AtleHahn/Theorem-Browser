Statement

Let X,Y be F-spaces (topological vector spaces whose topology comes from a complete translation-invariant metric), and let T:X\to Y be linear. Assume that whenever x_i\to0 and Tx_i\to y, one has y=0. Then T is continuous.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

F-spaces are explicitly complete translation-invariant metric vector spaces. The compatibility of vector operations with the topology is a short addition to undergraduate metric completeness and linear algebra; the hypothesis and conclusion use ordinary sequence convergence and continuity.

Proof 1: Translate to zero and apply the closed graph theorem

Suppose (x_i,Tx_i)\to(x,y) in X\times Y. Then x_i-x\to0 and, by linearity, T(x_i-x)=Tx_i-Tx\to y-Tx. The assumed condition gives y-Tx=0. Thus every limit of a sequence from the graph belongs to the graph. Since the product is metrizable, this proves that the graph is closed.

By the closed graph theorem for F-spaces, T is continuous.
