Statement

Let R be a field or an excellent discrete valuation ring. Let B be a finitely generated commutative R-algebra, \mathfrak p a prime ideal of B, and A=(B_{\mathfrak p})^h its henselization. Let \mathfrak m be any proper ideal of A and \widehat A=\varprojlim_{j\ge1}A/\mathfrak m^j its \mathfrak m-adic completion. Let F be a covariant functor from commutative A-algebras to sets which preserves filtered colimits: the canonical map \varinjlim_i F(C_i)\to F(\varinjlim_i C_i) is a bijection for each filtered system of A-algebras. For every positive integer c and every \bar\xi\in F(\widehat A), there exists \xi\in F(A) whose image in F(A/\mathfrak m^c) equals the image of \bar\xi under the completion projection. This is the meaning of \bar\xi\equiv\xi\pmod{\mathfrak m^c}. A henselian local ring is one in which coprime monic factorizations over the residue field lift to monic factorizations in the ring. The henselization of a local ring is its universal local map to a henselian local ring. Localization B_{\mathfrak p} inverts the elements outside \mathfrak p; a filtered system is a diagram in which every finite subdiagram admits a compatible cocone. A discrete valuation ring is a Noetherian local domain with a nonzero principal maximal ideal and every nonzero ideal a power of that ideal. Excellence means that the ring is Noetherian, its local completion maps have geometrically regular fibers, every finite-type algebra over it has open regular locus, and every finite-type algebra over it is catenary. Geometrically regular means remaining regular after every finite extension of the residue field; regular means all local rings have Krull dimension equal to the minimal number of generators of their maximal ideals. Catenary means that all saturated chains of prime ideals between two fixed comparable primes have the same finite length. With the convention \mathfrak m^c=A for c\le0, the congruence assertion also covers all integers c, since those congruences impose no additional restriction.
