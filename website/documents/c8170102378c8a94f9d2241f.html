Statement

Let M be a closed Riemannian manifold (compact and without boundary) with positive sectional curvature.

  • If M is even-dimensional and orientable, each connected component is simply connected.
  • If M is odd-dimensional, it is orientable.

Simply connected means path connected with every loop contractible.

Proof 1: Parallel normal field contradicts geodesic minimality (outline)

Prerequisites

Given a closed geodesic S^1\to M with a nonzero orthogonal and parallel vector field along the geodesic (i.e. a parallel section of the normal bundle to the geodesic), Synge's computation of the second variation formula for arclength shows that the geodesic may be deformed so as to shorten its length. The only tool used at this stage is the assumption on sectional curvature.

The construction of a parallel vector field along any path is automatic via parallel transport; the nontriviality in the case of a loop is whether the values at the endpoints coincide. This reduces to a problem of pure linear algebra: let V be a finite-dimensional real inner product space with T:V\to V an orthogonal linear map with an eigenvector v with eigenvalue one. If the determinant of T is positive and the dimension of V is even, or alternatively if the determinant of T is negative and the dimension of V is odd, then there is an eigenvector w of T with eigenvalue one which is orthogonal to v. In context, V is the tangent space to M at a point of a closed geodesic, T is the parallel transport map defined by the geodesic, and v is its tangent vector.

Argument

Given any noncontractible loop in a closed Riemannian manifold, there is a representative of its free homotopy class which has minimal possible arclength, and it is a closed geodesic. According to Synge's computation, this implies that there cannot be a nonzero parallel and orthogonal vector field along this geodesic. However:

Orientability implies that the parallel transport map along every loop has positive determinant. Even-dimensionality then implies the existence of a nonzero parallel vector field, orthogonal to the geodesic.

This contradiction establishes the nonexistence of noncontractible loops in each connected component.

Odd-dimensional case

Given any noncontractible loop in a closed Riemannian manifold, there is a representative of its free homotopy class which has minimal possible arclength, and it is a closed geodesic. According to Synge's computation, this implies that there cannot be a nonzero parallel and orthogonal vector field along this geodesic. However:

Nonorientability implies that the noncontractible loop can be chosen so that the parallel transport map has negative determinant. Odd-dimensionality then implies the existence of a nonzero parallel vector field, orthogonal to the geodesic.

This contradiction establishes the impossibility of nonorientability.

Orientability and the sign of loop parallel transport
Kind:

Theorem

Treatment:

Implicitly assumed

Statement used:

Parallel transport around a loop in an oriented Riemannian manifold has positive determinant. A nonorientable manifold has an orientation-reversing loop, whose parallel transport has negative determinant.

Application in proof:

Supply positive sign for the even orientable case and negative sign for an orientation-reversing loop in the odd case.
