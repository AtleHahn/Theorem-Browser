Statement

Let D\subset\mathbb R^2 be open and let f:D\to\mathbb R be continuous. For every (t_0,y_0)\in D, there exist an open interval I containing t_0 and a continuously differentiable function y:I\to\mathbb R such that

y(t_0)=y_0,\qquad (t,y(t))\in D,\qquad y'(t)=f(t,y(t))\quad(t\in I).

Uniqueness is not asserted.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The complete local-existence statement requires only undergraduate calculus and elementary topology; no existence-proof machinery is needed to understand it.

Proof availability

The source attempts a Lipschitz-approximation and compactness proof on a two-sided time interval. Its integral estimates use forward integration bounds also for negative times, where they have the wrong sign, and its maximal-difference interval is undefined in that direction. The supplied argument therefore does not establish the stated two-sided local existence without repair; no repaired or external proof is included.
