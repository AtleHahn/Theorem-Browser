Statement

Let X be a regular Hausdorff space: distinct points have disjoint open neighborhoods, as do a point and a closed set not containing it. Suppose there is a sequence of open covers \mathcal U_n such that for every point x and open neighborhood O of x, some n satisfies \bigcup\{U\in\mathcal U_n:x\in U\}\subseteq O. Such a space is called a Moore space. Suppose also that X is collectionwise normal: every discrete family (F_i)_{i\in I} of closed sets has pairwise disjoint open neighborhoods (V_i)_{i\in I} with F_i\subseteq V_i. A family of subsets is discrete if every point has an open neighborhood meeting at most one member of the family. Then a metric on X induces its given topology.
