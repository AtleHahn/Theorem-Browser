Statement

Let A act on L^2([0,1]) with Lebesgue measure by (Af)(t)=tf(t). Let \varepsilon>0 and 0\le a<a+\varepsilon\le1, and let V_{a,\varepsilon} consist of the functions vanishing almost everywhere outside [a,a+\varepsilon]. Then V_{a,\varepsilon} is invariant under A, and every f\in V_{a,\varepsilon} satisfies \|(A-aI)f\|_2\le\varepsilon\|f\|_2.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement concerns equivalence classes of square-integrable functions, almost-everywhere support, and their Hilbert-space norm.

Proof 1: Restrict functions to a short interval — outline

Consider the subspace of functions supported on the small interval [a,a+\varepsilon] inside [0,1]. Multiplication by t preserves this subspace. On it, Af is close to af, with error controlled by the width \varepsilon of the support interval.

Bound |t-a| by the interval width and integrate the squared pointwise estimate. The calculation is elementary, but arbitrary L2 functions, almost-everywhere support and invariant operator subspaces form a cumulative measure-theoretic prerequisite; retain conservatively.
