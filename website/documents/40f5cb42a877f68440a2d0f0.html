Statement

The Dedekind eta function \eta(\tau)=e^{\pi i\tau/12}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-e^{2\pi i n\tau}) for \operatorname{Im}\tau>0 satisfies \eta(i)=\tfrac12\pi^{-3/4}\Gamma(1/4). Here i^2=-1 and \Gamma(1/4)=\int_0^\infty t^{-3/4}e^{-t}\,dt. Equivalently,

e^{-\pi/12}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-e^{-2\pi n})=\frac{1}{2\pi^{3/4}}\int_0^\infty t^{-3/4}e^{-t}\,dt.

Proof 1: Lemniscatic elliptic integral and a Landen specialization (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Answer of22 July2018, equations(1)–(7) and final paragraph; DLMF19.8.11–12).

The answer uses q=e^{2\pi i\tau}, so its lemniscatic case is \tau=i/2. To obtain \tau=i, set k=k'=1/\sqrt2 and K=K(k). The Landen transformation gives

l=\frac{1-k'}{1+k'},\quad l'=\frac{2\sqrt{k'}}{1+k'},\quad L=\frac{1+k'}2K,\quad L'=(1+k')K,

so L'/L=2. Substitution into the answer’s equation (6) yields

\eta(i)=2^{-1/6}\sqrt{\frac{2L}{\pi}}\,l^{1/12}(l')^{1/3} =2^{1/6}(kk')^{1/6}\sqrt{\frac K\pi}=\sqrt{\frac K\pi}.

Its final integral calculation gives K=\Gamma(1/4)^2/(4\sqrt\pi). All roots are positive, so taking the square root gives the stated coefficient. The real product expression follows directly from the definition at \tau=i.
