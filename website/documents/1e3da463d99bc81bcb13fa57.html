Statement

For a bounded Lebesgue-measurable set A\subset\mathbb R^n, n\ge1, write m for Lebesgue measure and H^+(v,c)=\{x:v\cdot x>c\} for v\in S^{n-1}. For every v\in S^{n-1} there is c\in\mathbb R with m(A\cap H^+(v,c))=m(A)/2. If m(A)>0, all such offsets form a nonempty compact interval.

Proof 1: Translate the hyperplane and use the intermediate value theorem — outline

For fixed v, consider the family of hyperplanes v\cdot x=c, with positive side H^+(v,c). The half-space volume is continuous in c by the dominated convergence theorem: the indicators converge except on the limiting hyperplane, which has Lebesgue measure zero, and are dominated by the integrable indicator of A.

By the intermediate value theorem, every family of such hyperplanes contains at least one hyperplane that bisects the bounded set A: at one extreme translation, no volume of A is on the positive side, and at the other extreme translation, all of A's volume is on the positive side, so in between there must be a closed interval I_v of possible values of c\in\mathbb R for which the hyperplane bisects the volume of A. Here the interval property follows from monotonicity in c. If A has volume zero, we pick c=0. Otherwise, the interval I_v is compact.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The intermediate-value step is elementary, but continuity of the half-space volume is proved by dominated convergence of measurable indicators outside a null hyperplane. This requires Lebesgue integration and almost-everywhere convergence, beyond the ordinary undergraduate baseline.
