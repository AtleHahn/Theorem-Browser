Statement

Let H,V be groupoids with the same object set M, with source and target maps s,t. Use left-to-right arrow composition. Let S consist of quadruples (h,h',v,v') with h,h'\in H, v,v'\in V, s(h)=s(v), t(h)=s(v'), s(h')=t(v) and t(h')=t(v'). Regard h,h' as top/bottom and v,v' as left/right edges. These squares, with the indicated horizontal and vertical edge groupoids, form a double groupoid under

(h,h',v,v')\circ_1(h',h'',w,w')=(h,h'',vw,v'w'),(h,h',v,v')\circ_2(k,k',v',v'')=(hk,h'k',v,v'').

A double groupoid here has groupoid structures in the two directions satisfying the interchange law, equivalently it is an internal groupoid in groupoids.

Proof 1 — Double groupoid of boundary squares — outline

Given two groupoids H and V over a set M, there is a double groupoid \Box(H,V) with H,V as horizontal and vertical edge groupoids, and squares given by quadruples

\begin{pmatrix} &h&\\[-0.9ex]v&&v'\\[-0.9ex]&h'& \end{pmatrix}

where h,h' are in H and v,v' are in V, and the initial and final points of these edges match in M as suggested by the notation; that is,

s(h)=s(v),\quad t(h)=s(v'),\quad s(h')=t(v),\quad t(h')=t(v').

The compositions are to be inherited from those of H,V; that is:

\begin{pmatrix}&h&\\[-0.9ex]v&&v'\\[-0.9ex]&h'&\end{pmatrix} \circ_1 \begin{pmatrix}&h'&\\[-0.9ex]w&&w'\\[-0.9ex]&h''&\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}&h&\\[-0.9ex]vw&&v'w'\\[-0.9ex]&h''&\end{pmatrix}

and

\begin{pmatrix}&h&\\[-0.9ex]v&&v'\\[-0.9ex]&h'&\end{pmatrix} \circ_2 \begin{pmatrix}&k&\\[-0.9ex]v'&&v''\\[-0.9ex]&k'&\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}&hk&\\[-0.9ex]v&&v''\\[-0.9ex]&h'k'&\end{pmatrix}.
