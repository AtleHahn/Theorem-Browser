Even a complete first-order theory with an infinite model cannot fix its model's infinite cardinality. This exposes a limit of first-order axiomatic description: deciding every sentence does not select a unique structure or even a unique infinite size.

Statement

Let T be a consistent complete first-order theory in a countable language with at least one infinite model. For every infinite cardinal \kappa, T has a model of cardinality \kappa.

Proof 1: Downward and upward Löwenheim–Skolem — outline

For a consistent and complete theory T over a countable language, with at least one infinite model, the downward and upward Löwenheim–Skolem theorems imply that I(T,\kappa)\geq1 for all infinite \kappa. Here I(T,\kappa) is the number of non-isomorphic models of T of cardinality \kappa.

The downward theorem supplies a countably infinite model; the upward theorem supplies models of every larger infinite cardinality.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof invokes upward and downward Lowenheim-Skolem for formal theories and infinite cardinalities. These are several linked model-theoretic concepts beyond the baseline, despite the short deduction.
