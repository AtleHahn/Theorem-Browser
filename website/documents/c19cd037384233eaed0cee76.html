Statement

Let s be Schwartz on \mathbb R, with S(f)=\int_{\mathbb R}s(x)e^{-2\pi ixf}\,dx supported in [-f_0,f_0], where f_0>0. If T>0 and 1/T>2f_0, then the samples s(nT) determine S and hence s.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Calculus and introductory analysis supply smooth functions, improper integrals, series and complex exponentials. The additional Schwartz condition requires every derivative to decay faster than every inverse power; support in the displayed interval means the given integral transform vanishes outside it. These concrete conditions and uniqueness from sample values are a small extension within about 30 minutes. No Lebesgue-space equivalence classes, distribution theory or Fourier-series uniqueness theorem is needed merely to understand this formulation.

Proof 1: Separate the nonoverlapping spectral copies — outline

Use the Fourier-domain Poisson periodization formula:

\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}S(f+k/T) =T\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}s(nT)e^{-2\pi inTf}.

The periodic summation of the Fourier transform is completely defined by discrete samples of the original function.

For band-limited functions, choosing the sampling rate {\displaystyle {\tfrac {1}{T}}>2f_0} guarantees that no information is lost: since {\displaystyle S} can be reconstructed from these sampled values. Then, by Fourier inversion, so can {\displaystyle s.}

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof applies two supplied classical integral identities and separates translates of an interval. Undergraduate calculus, series and complex exponentials suffice for these formulas. The extra Schwartz condition means smoothness with every derivative decaying faster than any inverse power; it ensures convergence here, without requiring a topology on the Schwartz space or distributions. On the central support interval all shifted copies except k=0 vanish, so the samples determine S there and S is zero elsewhere. This concrete integral-and-series application is a small extension, unlike the separate L2-representative examples.

For Schwartz s with Fourier transform S in the 2pi convention and T>0, sum_k S(f+k/T)=T sum_n s(nT) exp(-2pi i n T f).

Application in proof:

Recover the periodic sum from the samples, then isolate its unshifted term on [-f0,f0].

Undergraduate level

Fourier inversion on Schwartz functions
Kind:

Theorem

Treatment:

Cited without proof

Statement used:

For a Schwartz function and its Fourier transform S(f)=integral s(x)exp(-2pi i x f)dx, s(x)=integral S(f)exp(2pi i x f)df.

Application in proof:

Recover s uniquely from the determined transform S.

Application level:

Undergraduate level

Level justification:

Applying the explicit integral identity needs calculus and complex exponentials, not L2 equivalence classes or distributional inversion.

Review notes:

The theorem is used in the classical Schwartz setting; the proof of Fourier inversion is not part of the argument.
