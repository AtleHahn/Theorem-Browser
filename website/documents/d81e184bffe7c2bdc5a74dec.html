Statement

Let n\ge2 and let K\subseteq\mathbb R^n be a convex body (a compact convex set with nonempty interior). Put K_x=\{z\in\mathbb R^{n-1}:(x,z)\in K\} and r(x)=\mu_{n-1}(K_x)^{1/(n-1)}, where \mu_{n-1} is Lebesgue measure in \mathbb R^{n-1}. On the interval I=\{x:K_x\ne\varnothing\}, r is concave: r(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y)\ge\lambda r(x)+(1-\lambda)r(y) for x,y\in I and 0\le\lambda\le1.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra and multivariable calculus supply Euclidean sets, convexity and volume. For finite box unions or convex-body slices, volume is the familiar geometric volume; the set sums or slices and concavity condition are explicit. No general measurable-function theory is needed to understand these formulations.

Proof 1: Use convexity of slices and Brunn–Minkowski

Convexity gives K_{\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y}\supseteq\lambda K_x+(1-\lambda)K_y. Applying the weighted Brunn–Minkowski inequality in \mathbb R^{n-1} gives r(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y)\ge\lambda r(x)+(1-\lambda)r(y) whenever both slices are nonempty. Thus r is concave on I.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Convexity puts every weighted combination of two slice points in the intermediate slice. Volume monotonicity and the stated weighted Brunn–Minkowski inequality then give concavity. Finite-dimensional convexity, slices and real volume roots are accessible from undergraduate geometry and analysis.
