Statement

For a set X, actions of the additive group \mathbb Z on X correspond bijectively to bijections T:X\to X: the action of 1 is T and n*x=T^n(x), with inverse iterates for negative n.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory group theory supplies subgroups, quotients, actions, products and homomorphisms. Perfect means generated by commutators, torsion means finite element orders, and the chain conditions are explicit sequences of subgroups. The relevant one of these closely related definitions is a small extension, without representation theory.

Proof 1: An integer action from a bijection — outline

The source states that an integer action is uniquely determined by its automorphism at 1, and conversely. Iteration supplies every positive action map, inverses supply the negative ones, and powers satisfy the additive action identity.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Compose the bijection and its inverse to define the integer action, then use the law of integer powers. Undergraduate groups and functions suffice.
