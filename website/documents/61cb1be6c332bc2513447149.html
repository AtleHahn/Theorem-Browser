Statement

For any group G, let \operatorname{Sym}(G) denote all bijections of its underlying set, under composition. Then T:G\to\operatorname{Sym}(G), T(g)=L_g with L_g(x)=gx, is an injective group homomorphism. Hence G is isomorphic to a subgroup of \operatorname{Sym}(G).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory abstract algebra supplies groups, ring operations, quotients and homomorphisms. The additional operations or central-element conditions are explicitly given; for the circle group, complex exponentials and continuity are familiar from calculus and topology. No further structural theory is needed to understand the claim.

Proof 1: Left translations, composition and evaluation at the identity

If g is any element of a group G with operation ∗, consider the function L_g : G → G, defined by L_g(x) = g ∗ x. By the existence of inverses, this function has also an inverse, {\displaystyle L_{g^{-1}}}. So multiplication by g acts as a bijective function. Thus, L_g is a permutation of G, and so is a member of Sym(G).

The set K = {L_g : g ∈ G} is a subgroup of Sym(G) that is isomorphic to G. The fastest way to establish this is to consider the function T : G → Sym(G) with T(g) = L_g for every g in G. T is a group homomorphism because (using · to denote composition in Sym(G)):

{\displaystyle (L_{g}\cdot L_{h})(x)=L_{g}(L_{h}(x))=L_{g}(h*x)=g*(h*x)=(g*h)*x=L_{g*h}(x),}

for all x in G, and hence:

{\displaystyle T(g)\cdot T(h)=L_{g}\cdot L_{h}=L_{g*h}=T(g*h).}

The homomorphism T is injective since T(g) = idG (the identity element of Sym(G)) implies that g ∗ x = x for all x in G, and taking x to be the identity element e of G yields g = g ∗ e = e, i.e. the kernel is trivial. Alternatively, T is also injective since g ∗ x = g′ ∗ x implies that g = g′ (because every group is cancellative).

Thus G is isomorphic to the image of T, which is the subgroup K.
