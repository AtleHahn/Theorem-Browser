Statement

For each n\in\{6,10,14\}, the equation a^n+b^n=c^n has no solution in positive integers.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The three specified exponent cases use only positive integer powers.

Proof 1: Square the coordinates and use the odd-prime cases

The known Fermat cases at exponents 3,5,7 imply the corresponding cases at 6,10,14: writing n=2p, any solution would give (a^2)^p+(b^2)^p=(c^2)^p, contrary to the case p.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Substitute a squared, b squared and c squared into the known odd-prime equations. The deep proofs of those imported cases are not used.
