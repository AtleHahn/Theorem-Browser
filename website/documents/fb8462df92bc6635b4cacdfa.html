Strongly continuous unitary evolution has a self-adjoint generator with a specified domain. This connects continuous temporal evolution with the Hamiltonian description of quantum dynamics, including the domain issues hidden by finite-matrix intuition.

Statement

Let H be a complex Hilbert space and (U_t)_{t\in\mathbb R} a strongly continuous one-parameter unitary group: U_{s+t}=U_sU_t and U_t\psi\to U_{t_0}\psi in norm for every \psi\in H as t\to t_0.

There is a unique densely defined self-adjoint operator A such that U_t=e^{itA} for all t\in\mathbb R, with the exponential defined by spectral functional calculus. Its domain and action are

D(A)=\left\{\psi\in H:\lim_{h\to0}\frac{U_h\psi-\psi}{h}\text{ exists in }H\right\}, \qquad A\psi=-i\lim_{h\to0}\frac{U_h\psi-\psi}{h}.

Conversely, every densely defined self-adjoint operator A on H, bounded or unbounded, gives a strongly continuous one-parameter unitary group by U_t=e^{itA}.

Proof 1: Orbit averaging and the self-adjoint generator (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Section5.1, Theorems5.1–5.2, printed pp.123–125; Lemmas2.3–2.7).

Teschl writes U_t=e^{-itB}; take A=-B. Theorem 5.1(ii) gives exactly the norm-difference-quotient domain, and Theorem 5.2 constructs the generator. In its last paragraph use the self-adjoint closure \overline B and \frac{d}{dt}\bigl((U_t-V_t)\psi\bigr)=-i\overline B(U_t-V_t)\psi; the norm is constant and initially zero. Equality of the groups, followed by Theorem 5.1(ii), identifies B with its closure on the full derivative domain. Although the book earlier adopts a separability convention, these arguments work on any Hilbert space: continuous orbit integrals are norm Riemann integrals, and the adjoint/range argument and scalar spectral integrals require no countable basis.
