Statement

Let n\ge1 be an integer and choose X,Y,Z,W independently and uniformly from \{1,\ldots,n\}. Then \Pr[\max(X,Y,Z)\le W]=\Pr[X\le Y\ \text{and}\ Z\le W].

Level
High school only
Level justification

Probability is explicitly uniform counting of n⁴ tuples, with no general probability theory needed.

Mathematical Field
Probability theory
Required topics
Finite countingUniform choices from a finite set
Review notes
none

Proof 1: Counting cubes and pairs

We use Nicomachus's identity \sum_{w=1}^n w^3=(1+\cdots+n)^2.

For any particular value W=w, the combinations of {\displaystyle X}, {\displaystyle Y}, and {\displaystyle Z} that make {\displaystyle W} largest form a cube {\displaystyle 1\leq X,Y,Z\leq w} so (adding the size of this cube over all choices of {\displaystyle W}) the number of combinations of {\displaystyle X,Y,Z,W} for which {\displaystyle W} is largest is a sum of cubes, the left hand side of the Nicomachus identity. The sets of pairs {\displaystyle (X,Y)} with {\displaystyle X\leq Y} and of pairs {\displaystyle (Z,W)} with {\displaystyle Z\leq W} form isosceles right triangles, and the set counted by the right hand side of the equation of probabilities is the Cartesian product of these two triangles, so its size is the square of a triangular number on the right hand side of the Nicomachus identity. The probabilities themselves are respectively the left and right sides of the Nicomachus identity, normalized to make probabilities by dividing both sides by {\displaystyle n^{4}}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Count the first event by cubes of possible lower values and the second by two triangular counts, then divide by n to the fourth. School finite probability and counting suffice.
