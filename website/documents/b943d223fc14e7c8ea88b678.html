Statement

Let (X,Σ,μ) be a measure space and 1\le p<\infty. If measurable real or complex functions u_n\in L^p(\mu) satisfy \sum_{n=0}^\infty\|u_n\|_p<\infty, then \sum_n|u_n| is finite almost everywhere, f=\sum_nu_n belongs to L^p, and the partial sums converge to f in L^p norm.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement concerns Lebesgue spaces and almost-everywhere convergence on arbitrary measure spaces.

Proof 1: Bound the absolute sum and apply dominated convergence

Minkowski's inequality and monotone convergence give

\int\left(\sum_{n=0}^\infty |u_n|\right)^p\,d\mu\le\left(\sum_{n=0}^\infty\|u_n\|_p\right)^p<\infty.

Thus f=\sum_nu_n exists almost everywhere and belongs to L^p. Dominated convergence gives

\int\left|f-\sum_{k=0}^nu_k\right|^p\,d\mu\le\int\left(\sum_{\ell>n}|u_\ell|\right)^p\,d\mu\longrightarrow0.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Control the absolute sum by Minkowski and monotone convergence, then use dominated convergence for the tails. General Lebesgue integration, almost-everywhere equivalence and Lp norms are essential.
