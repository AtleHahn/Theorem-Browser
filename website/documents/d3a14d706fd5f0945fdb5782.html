Statement

For integers g\ge0, k\ge1, define h_{g;k} as 1/k! times the number of tuples (\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_{k+2g-1},\sigma) of permutations of \{1,\ldots,k\}, where each \tau_i swaps two elements and fixes the others, \sigma is a single k-cycle, \tau_1\cdots\tau_{k+2g-1}\sigma=1, and the swaps generate an action transitive on the k elements. For any fixed k-cycle c, h_{g;k} is 1/k times the number of ordered factorizations of c into k+2g-1 transpositions.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The count uses permutation factorization, transpositions and transitive generated actions, requiring undergraduate algebra and combinatorics. A bright US college student should be able to understand the statement after about 30 minutes of focused background research; this rating concerns understanding the claim, not proving it.

Proof 1: Pass from all cycles to one fixed cycle — outline

For one numbered cycle, the product of the transpositions must be a k-cycle. Thus h_{g;k} is 1/k! times the number of transposition lists with such a product; equivalently it is 1/k times the number whose product is a fixed k-cycle.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Count k-cycles and transport their transposition factorizations by conjugation; the product cycle ensures transitivity. Introductory undergraduate permutation groups suffice; no Hurwitz geometry or branched-cover theory is used.
