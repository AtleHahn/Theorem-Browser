Statement

For n partners whose values on the cake [0,1] are given by continuous nonnegative value-density functions g_1,\ldots,g_n, the cake can be divided by n cuts into intervals assigned to two parts so that every partner assigns equal value to the two parts.

Proof 1: Group the positive and negative intervals

Suppose the interval [0,1] is a cake. There are n partners and each of the n functions is a value-density function of one partner. We want to divide the cake into two parts such that all partners agree that the parts have the same value.

The Hobby–Rice theorem supplies a signed partition

0=z_0<z_1<\dotsb<z_n<z_{n+1}=1,\qquad \delta_1,\dotsc,\delta_{n+1}\in\{+1,-1\},

such that

\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\delta_i\int_{z_{i-1}}^{z_i}g_j(z)\,dz=0 \quad\text{for }1\leq j\leq n.

In other words: for each of the n functions, its integral over the positive subintervals equals its integral over the negative subintervals. Taking these as the two parts, the Hobby–Rice theorem implies that this can be done with n cuts.

Level justification

The Hobby-Rice conclusion is explicitly a finite signed sum of integrals of continuous functions. Separate its positive and negative intervals to get equal values. Ordinary calculus suffices to understand and apply that supplied statement.
