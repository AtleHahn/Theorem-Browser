Statement

Let \Omega be an oval in a finite projective plane of even order n, and let N be its nucleus (all tangents pass through N). Put H=\Omega\cup\{N\}. For every R\in H, the set H\setminus\{R\} is an oval, that is, a set of n+1 points with no three collinear.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Finite projective incidence axioms and order n add a small set-and-incidence extension to undergraduate geometry. Oval and nucleus are explicitly described; no coordinate-field classification is required.

Proof 1: Adjoin the nucleus and delete one point — outline

The source constructs the hyperoval H=\Omega\cup\{N\} from the oval and its nucleus, then deletes any one of its points. The construction gives an (n+1)-point oval by the finite oval characterization.
