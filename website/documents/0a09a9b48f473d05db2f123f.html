Statement

Let n\ge1 and 0\le t\le n be integers. Let L and R be disjoint sets of n vertices each, and join every vertex of L to every vertex of R. Fix t pairwise disjoint edges. The family of perfect matchings containing these edges has (n-t)! members, with 0!=1, and every two members share at least t edges. A perfect matching pairs every vertex of L with exactly one vertex of R, using each vertex of R exactly once.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The finite pairing rule and all choices of vertices and edges are explicitly described; no group theory is needed.

Proof 1: Prescribe t pairs and choose the remaining matching — outline

Identify the permutations between the two n-element sets with perfect matchings. Take all matchings that contain the t chosen edges, equivalently all bijections with the corresponding t images prescribed. This is the fixed-image family of size (n-t)!; every pair of its members contains the chosen edges.

Level
High school only
Level justification

After fixing t disjoint pairs, pair the remaining n-t vertices on each side in (n-t)! ways. All resulting matchings contain the fixed pairs. This is elementary permutation counting, including the empty remaining set.
