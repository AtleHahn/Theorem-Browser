Statement

Write \mathrm{PA} for first-order Peano arithmetic and G for primitive recursive arithmetic plus quantifier-free transfinite induction up to \varepsilon_0, the least ordinal \alpha with \omega^\alpha=\alpha. Write \operatorname{Con}(T) for the standard arithmetical consistency statement of a theory T. Assume \mathrm{PA} is consistent. Then G interprets \mathrm{PA}, whereas \mathrm{PA} does not interpret G. Here “interprets” means a formal interpretation of theories.

Proof 1: Compose interpretations and apply Pudlák’s obstruction

Here Gentzen’s theory is G as defined in the statement.

A strong form of the second incompleteness theorem, proved by Pavel Pudlák, who was building on earlier work by Solomon Feferman, implies that, if PA is consistent, it cannot interpret Q plus Con(PA), where Q is Robinson arithmetic. By contrast, Q+Con(PA) does interpret PA, by a strong form of the arithmetized completeness theorem. So Q+Con(PA) is stronger (in one good sense) than PA is. But Gentzen's theory trivially interprets Q+Con(PA), since it contains Q and proves Con(PA), and so Gentzen's theory interprets PA. But, by Pudlák's result, PA cannot interpret Gentzen's theory, since Gentzen's theory (as just said) interprets Q+Con(PA), and interpretability is transitive. That is: If PA did interpret Gentzen's theory, then it would also interpret Q+Con(PA) and so would be inconsistent, by Pudlák's result. So, in the sense of consistency strength, as characterized by interpretability, Gentzen's theory is stronger than Peano arithmetic.
