Statement

A proper vertex coloring assigns distinct colors to the two ends of every edge. Graphs here are finite and simple, with no loops or repeated edges. Let G be a connected plane triangulation, meaning a graph drawn without crossings whose every face, including the outer face, has a triangular boundary. Write d(v) for the number of edges incident to v. Assign charge 6-d(v) to each vertex v. After any redistribution preserving the sum of all vertex charges, at least one vertex has strictly positive charge.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The finite graph, noncrossing drawing and color constraints are explicit elementary combinatorial objects.

Proof 1: Use conservation of the degree-deficit sum

The source degree-deficit identity gives total initial charge \sum_v(6-d(v))=12. Redistribution preserves this sum. If every final vertex charge were nonpositive, their finite sum would be nonpositive, contrary to the conserved value 12>0.

Use the supplied total charge twelve and the elementary fact that a finite sum of nonpositive numbers cannot be positive. School counting and inequalities suffice.
