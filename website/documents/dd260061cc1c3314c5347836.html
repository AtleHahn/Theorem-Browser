Statement

Let g,h be holomorphic near c, with h(c)=0 and h'(c)\ne0. Then \operatorname{Res}(g/h,c)=g(c)/h'(c), including the removable case when g(c)=0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Holomorphic differentiability and the residue coefficient add related local complex-function definitions to undergraduate differentiation and series.

Mathematical Field
Complex Analysis

Review notes

Proof 1: Evaluate the simple-pole limit

Put f=g/h. The at-most-simple-pole residue formula and the holomorphic derivative quotient limit give

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\operatorname {Res} (f,c)&=\lim _{z\to c}(z-c)f(z)=\lim _{z\to c}{\frac {zg(z)-cg(z)}{h(z)}}\\[4pt]&=\lim _{z\to c}{\frac {g(z)+zg'(z)-cg'(z)}{h'(z)}}={\frac {g(c)}{h'(c)}}.\end{aligned}}}

Apply the simple-pole limit and the derivative quotient limit at a simple zero. Undergraduate complex analysis suffices.
