Statement

Let G be a finite simple group whose 2-rank, the largest r such that (C_2)^r embeds in G, is two. Then G is isomorphic to one of \operatorname{PSL}_2(q), A_7, \operatorname{PSL}_3(q), \operatorname{PSU}_3(q), M_{11}, or \operatorname{PSU}_3(4), where in the families q is an odd prime power. Here A_7 is the alternating group and M_{11} the Mathieu group. The conclusion is a list of possible groups, not a claim that every parameter in the list gives a simple group of 2-rank two.

Proof 1: Combine the four Sylow-subgroup classifications

Alperin showed that a Sylow 2-subgroup of G must be dihedral, quasidihedral, wreathed, or isomorphic to a Sylow 2-subgroup of \operatorname{PSU}_3(4). In the dihedral case, the Gorenstein–Walter theorem gives \operatorname{PSL}_2(q) for odd q or A_7. The Alperin–Brauer–Gorenstein theorem handles the quasidihedral and wreathed cases, giving \operatorname{PSL}_3(q) or \operatorname{PSU}_3(q) for odd q, or M_{11}. Lyons handled the last case, giving only \operatorname{PSU}_3(4). These are all the cases.

Level justification

Exhaust the Sylow-subgroup alternatives using the four classification results. Understanding the alternatives and output families requires finite classical and unitary groups, special 2-groups and sporadic groups; this is not a small extension of introductory group theory.
