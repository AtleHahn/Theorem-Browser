Statement

For intuitionistic propositional formulas A,B, the expressions \neg(\neg A\land\neg B) and \neg\neg(A\lor B) are intuitionistically equivalent. In particular these give equivalent disjunction clauses when A=\phi^N and B=\theta^N in a negative translation.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Intuitionistic equivalence requires understanding constructive deduction, which is not specified locally. Its rules are a limited formal-logic extension of undergraduate proof reasoning; the negative-translation symbols are only substituted formulas.

Proof 1: A De Morgan variant of the translated disjunction

The constructive De Morgan equivalence \neg(A\lor B)\leftrightarrow(\neg A\land\neg B) allows the negated expression \neg(\neg A\land\neg B) to be rewritten as \neg\neg(A\lor B).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Negate the supplied constructive De Morgan equivalence. Undergraduate formal logic suffices with the small distinction between classical and intuitionistic validity; no general negative-translation theorem is used.
