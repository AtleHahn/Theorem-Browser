Statement

Let \mathcal M be a von Neumann algebra. For projections E,F\in\mathcal M, write E\sim F if a partial isometry U\in\mathcal M has U^*U=E, UU^*=F, and write E\precsim F if such a U has UU^*\le F. If E\precsim F and F\precsim E, then E\sim F.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Von Neumann algebras; Projections; Partial isometries; Murray–von Neumann equivalence. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Nested copies, common residual space and orthogonal shift — outline

Write M,N for the ranges of E,F, and write M\ll N when their projections satisfy P_M\precsim P_N. Equivalence of subspaces means equivalence of their projections in \mathcal M.

A proof, one that is similar to a set-theoretic argument, can be sketched as follows. Colloquially, N « M means that N can be isometrically embedded in M. So

{\displaystyle M=M_{0}\supset N_{0}}

where N0 is an isometric copy of N in M. By assumption, it is also true that, N, therefore N0, contains an isometric copy M1 of M. Therefore, one can write

{\displaystyle M=M_{0}\supset N_{0}\supset M_{1}.}

Iterating the fixed embedding partial isometries, by induction,

{\displaystyle M=M_{0}\supset N_{0}\supset M_{1}\supset N_{1}\supset M_{2}\supset N_{2}\supset \cdots .}

It is clear that

{\displaystyle R=\cap _{i\geq 0}M_{i}=\cap _{i\geq 0}N_{i}.}

Let

{\displaystyle M\ominus N{\stackrel {\mathrm {def} }{=}}M\cap (N)^{\perp }.}

So

{\displaystyle M=\oplus _{i\geq 0}(M_{i}\ominus N_{i})\quad \oplus \quad \oplus _{j\geq 0}(N_{j}\ominus M_{j+1})\quad \oplus R}

and

{\displaystyle N_{0}=\oplus _{i\geq 1}(M_{i}\ominus N_{i})\quad \oplus \quad \oplus _{j\geq 0}(N_{j}\ominus M_{j+1})\quad \oplus R.}

Notice

{\displaystyle M_{i}\ominus N_{i}\sim M_0\ominus N_0\quad {\mbox{for all}}\quad i.}

The theorem now follows from the countable additivity of ~.
