Statement

For an integrable real submartingale X_1,\ldots,X_n relative to a filtration (\mathcal F_i) and C>0, \Pr(\max_{1\le i\le n}X_i\ge C)\le\mathbb E[X_n^+]/C, where x^+=\max(x,0).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The conclusion concerns a maximal-event probability for an integrable submartingale.

Proof 1: First crossings and the terminal positive part — outline

  • The proof relies on the set-theoretic fact that the event E defined by {\displaystyle \max X_{i}>C} may be decomposed as the disjoint union of the events {\displaystyle E_{i}} defined by {\displaystyle X_{i}>C} and {\displaystyle X_{j}\leq C} for all {\displaystyle j<i}.
  • Then we can formulate the integral:
{\displaystyle CP(E_{i})=\int _{E_{i}}C\,dP\leq \int _{E_{i}}X_{i}\,dP\leq \int _{E_{i}}\operatorname {E} [X_{n}\mid {\mathcal {F}}_{i}]\,dP=\int _{E_{i}}X_{n}\,dP,}
  • This makes use of the submartingale property for the last inequality and the fact that {\displaystyle E_{i}\in {\mathcal {F}}_{i}} for the last equality.
  • Summing this result as {\displaystyle i} ranges from 1 to {\displaystyle n} results in the conclusion:
{\displaystyle CP(E)\leq \int _{E}X_{n}\,dP,}
  • This is sharper than the stated result. By using the elementary fact that {\displaystyle X_{n}\leq \max(X_{n},0)}, the bound follows for a strict crossing. The stated non-strict bound follows by the routine limit from positive thresholds below C.
