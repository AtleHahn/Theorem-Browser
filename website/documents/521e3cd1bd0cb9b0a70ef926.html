Statement

Let X_1,\ldots,X_n be independent normal random variables with common mean \mu and variance q>0, where n\ge1 is an integer. With known \mu, T=n^{-1}\sum_i(X_i-\mu)^2 is unbiased for q and attains the sample Cramér–Rao lower bound 2q^2/n. Thus it has minimum variance among unbiased estimators satisfying the scalar bound’s regularity hypotheses.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The concrete statistic is a mean of squared centered Gaussian observations, but the complete conclusion also asserts unbiasedness, attainment of reciprocal Fisher information and comparison with every unbiased estimator in the regular class. The class restriction is explicit; it does not purport to include arbitrary nonregular estimators. Gaussian moments, scores, information and the meaning of that restricted statistical comparison are interdependent prerequisites. Proof 1: Compare the computed variance with inverse information

The statistic

T=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\mu)^2}{n}

is unbiased for q, as E(T)=q. Its variance is

\begin{aligned} \operatorname{Var}(T) &=\operatorname{Var}\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\mu)^2}{n}\right)\\ &=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^n\operatorname{Var}((X_i-\mu)^2)}{n^2}\\ &=\frac{n\operatorname{Var}((X_1-\mu)^2)}{n^2}\\ &=\frac1n\left[E\{(X_1-\mu)^4\} -\left(E\{(X_1-\mu)^2\}\right)^2\right]. \end{aligned}

The second equality follows directly from the definition of variance and the fact that the X_i are independent. The first term in brackets is the fourth moment about the mean and has value 3q^2; the second is the square of the variance, or q^2. Thus

\operatorname{Var}(T)=\frac{2q^2}{n}.

Now, what is the Fisher information in the sample?

Recall that the score V for one observation is defined as

V=\frac{\partial}{\partial q}\log L(q,X),

where L is the likelihood function. Thus in this case,

\log L(q,X) =\log\left[\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi q}}e^{-(X-\mu)^2/(2q)}\right] =-\log\sqrt{2\pi q}-\frac{(X-\mu)^2}{2q},V=\frac{\partial}{\partial q}\log L(q,X) =\frac{\partial}{\partial q}\left[-\log\sqrt{2\pi q}-\frac{(X-\mu)^2}{2q}\right] =-\frac1{2q}+\frac{(X-\mu)^2}{2q^2},

where the second equality is from elementary calculus. By the Fisher information identity, the information in a single observation is just minus the expectation of the derivative of V, or

I_1(q)=-E\left(\frac{\partial V}{\partial q}\right) =-E\left(-\frac{(X-\mu)^2}{q^3}+\frac1{2q^2}\right) =\frac q{q^3}-\frac1{2q^2} =\frac1{2q^2}.

Thus the information in a sample of n independent observations is just n times this, or

I_n(q)=\frac n{2q^2}.

The scalar Cramér–Rao bound for unbiased estimators satisfying its regularity hypotheses states that

\operatorname{Var}(\widehat q)\geq\frac1{I_n(q)}=\frac{2q^2}{n}.

In this case, the inequality is saturated (equality is achieved), showing that the estimator T is efficient.

Level justification

The supplied moments give E(T)=q and Var(T)=(3q^2-q^2)/n=2q^2/n. The displayed score has mean zero, and its derivative gives I_1(q)=1/(2q^2); independence gives I_n(q)=n/(2q^2). For q>0 the normal scale density has common support, and on a compact positive-q neighborhood its derivatives times these polynomial statistics are integrably dominated, supporting the used information identities. Thus T attains the bound, and comparison is only with the expressly specified regular unbiased class. The calculation is sound without choosing a unique maximal regularity class.
