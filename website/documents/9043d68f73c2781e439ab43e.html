Statement

Let K be a field. Write \mathcal P(K)=\bigcup_{n\ge1}K((T^{1/n})), where K((T^{1/n})) consists of formal series \sum_{k\ge k_0}c_kT^{k/n} with k_0\in\mathbb Z and c_k\in K. Let 0\ne P(Y)=\sum_{a_i\ne0} a_iY^i\in\mathcal P(K)[Y] and let a nonzero root y\in\mathcal P(K) have initial term \gamma T^{v_0}. Set \mu=\min_i(v(a_i)+iv_0), I=\{i:v(a_i)+iv_0=\mu\}, and let c_i be the initial coefficient of a_i. Then \gamma\ne0 and \sum_{i\in I}c_i\gamma^i=0. Here v is the least exponent of a nonzero series, v(0)=\infty, and all minimum/index conditions run over nonzero coefficients.

Proof 1: Cancellation of the initial coefficient

Let c_i be the initial coefficient of a_i, that is, the coefficient of T^{v(a_i)} in a_i. Let \gamma T^{v_0} be the initial term of a corresponding Puiseux series solution of P(y)=0. If no cancellation occurred, then the coefficient of the lowest possible power T^\mu in P(y) would be

\sum_{i\in I}c_i\gamma^i,

where I is the set of indices satisfying v(a_i)+iv_0=\mu, on the Newton-polygon edge of slope -v_0. So, for having a root, the initial coefficient \gamma must be a nonzero root of the polynomial

\chi(X)=\sum_{i\in I}c_iX^i.

Level justification

Track lowest exponents in a polynomial whose coefficients and root are formal fractional Laurent series over an arbitrary field. Although the cancellation itself is elementary and definitions are provided, the nested polynomial/series arithmetic and valuation framework together are not confidently a small undergraduate extension. Retain beyond undergraduate in doubt.
