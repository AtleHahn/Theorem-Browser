Statement

Let E have n\ge0 elements and let \mathcal S be a family of subsets of E no one of which strictly contains another with |\mathcal S|=\binom n{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}. Then every member has size \lfloor n/2\rfloor or \lceil n/2\rceil. If n is even, \mathcal S is exactly the family of all n/2-element subsets; that family does attain equality.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The locally described finite-set family has maximum cardinality; its possible member sizes and even-size equality case require only finite counting and binomial coefficients.

Proof 1: Equality in the weighted inequalities

Let s_k be the number of k-element sets in \mathcal S, and put M=\binom n{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}. The Lubell–Yamamoto–Meshalkin inequality and the maximality of the middle binomial coefficient give

1=\frac{|\mathcal S|}{M}=\sum_{k=0}^n\frac{s_k}{M} \le\sum_{k=0}^n\frac{s_k}{\binom nk}\le1.

To have equality, all the inequalities in this comparison must be equalities. Since

{\displaystyle {n \choose \lfloor {n/2}\rfloor }={n \choose k}}

if and only if {\displaystyle k=\lfloor {n/2}\rfloor } or {\displaystyle \lceil {n/2}\rceil ,} equality implies that {\displaystyle \mathcal S} consists only of sets of sizes {\displaystyle \lfloor {n/2}\rfloor } or {\displaystyle \lceil {n/2}\rceil .} For even n, there is only one middle level, and the cardinality forces every set in that level to be included. Conversely, all sets of that size form an antichain attaining equality.
