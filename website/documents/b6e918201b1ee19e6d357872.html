Statement

Let C be a compact Riemann surface of genus g\geq2 and fix a base point P. In its Jacobian J, let W_k be the image of effective degree-k divisors under D\mapsto[D-kP]. For an effective divisor D of degree g-1, the multiplicity of W_{g-1} at [D-(g-1)P] is h^0(C,\mathcal O(D)), the dimension of meromorphic functions with poles bounded by D.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Jacobian varieties; effective divisors on compact Riemann surfaces; multiplicity of a divisor; global sections of line bundles. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Specialize the Riemann–Kempf formula

Use the external Riemann–Kempf multiplicity formula: for 1\leq k\leq g-1 and h^0(C,\mathcal O(D))=r+1, the multiplicity of W_k at [D-kP] is \binom{g-k+r}{r}. Substituting k=g-1 gives \binom{r+1}{r}=r+1=h^0(C,\mathcal O(D)), the claimed formula.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Substitute degree g-1 into the multiplicity formula on the Jacobian. Jacobian subvarieties, divisor classes, singular multiplicity and line-bundle sections are substantial algebraic-geometric prerequisites.
