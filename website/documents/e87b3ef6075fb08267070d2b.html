Statement

For any commutative ring R and any a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4,b_1,b_2,b_3,b_4\in R,

\begin{aligned} &(a_1^2+a_2^2+a_3^2+a_4^2)(b_1^2+b_2^2+b_3^2+b_4^2)\\ &=(a_1b_1-a_2b_2-a_3b_3-a_4b_4)^2\\ &\quad +(a_1b_2+a_2b_1+a_3b_4-a_4b_3)^2\\ &\quad +(a_1b_3-a_2b_4+a_3b_1+a_4b_2)^2\\ &\quad +(a_1b_4+a_2b_3-a_3b_2+a_4b_1)^2. \end{aligned}

Proof 1: Quaternion matrices and polynomial identity transfer (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Definition3.2 and Corollary3.3, pp.4–5).

Write M(a)=\begin{pmatrix}a_1+ia_2&a_3+ia_4\\-a_3+ia_4&a_1-ia_2\end{pmatrix}. Its determinant is \sum a_j^2, and M(a)M(b)=M(c) with exactly the four bilinear expressions displayed above. Determinant multiplicativity proves the identity over \mathbb R. Its difference is an integer-coefficient polynomial vanishing on every real tuple; induction on the number of variables using the univariate root bound shows every coefficient is zero. Evaluating that zero polynomial proves the identity in any commutative ring, including characteristic two.
