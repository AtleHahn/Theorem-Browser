Statement

For a positive integer n, let r_4(n) count ordered integer quadruples (x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4) with x_1^2+x_2^2+x_3^2+x_4^2=n; signs and order count separately. If n is even and m\ge0 is an integer, then r_4(2^m n)=r_4(n). Thus every value attained at an even input is attained infinitely often. In particular, r_4(2n)=r_4(8n) for every positive integer n.

Proof 1: The unchanged odd-divisor sum

By Jacobi’s formula, for even n the count is 24\sum_{d\mid n,\ d\text{ odd}}d. Multiplication by a power of 2 leaves the odd divisors unchanged, giving r_4(2^m n)=r_4(n). These inputs give the infinitely repeated value stated in the source; the displayed r_4(2n)=r_4(8n) is a special case.

Level justification

Multiplying an even integer by a power of two leaves its odd divisors unchanged, so the supplied divisor-sum formula has the same value. This is elementary prime divisibility and finite sums.
