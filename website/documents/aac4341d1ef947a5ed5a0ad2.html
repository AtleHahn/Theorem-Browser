Statement

Let A\subseteq X be path-connected spaces with basepoint a\in A, and let n\geq2. Suppose the pair (X,A) is (n-1)-connected, meaning \pi_j(X,A,a)=0 for 1\leq j<n (trivial pointed set when j=1). Then

H_j(X,A;\mathbb Z)=0\quad(0\leq j<n),

and the relative Hurewicz homomorphism induces an isomorphism

\pi_n(X,A,a)/N\ \cong\ H_n(X,A;\mathbb Z).

Here \pi_n(X,A,a) consists of based homotopy classes of maps of triples (D^n,S^{n-1},*)\to(X,A,a), with its usual concatenation group operation. The group \pi_1(A,a) acts on it by change of basepoint along loops in A. The subgroup N is the normal subgroup generated by

(\gamma\cdot u)u^{-1}\qquad(\gamma\in\pi_1(A,a),\ u\in\pi_n(X,A,a)).

For n\geq3 the group is abelian, so this is equivalently the quotient by all differences \gamma\cdot u-u. For n=2 the normal-subgroup formulation applies without assuming that the relative homotopy group is abelian.

Relative homology is the homology of the quotient of the singular chain complex of X by that of A, with integer coefficients. The Hurewicz map sends a relative disk class to the image of the generator of H_n(D^n,S^{n-1};\mathbb Z) under its representing map. Path-connected means any two points can be joined by a continuous path; D^n and S^{n-1} are the closed unit ball and its boundary.
