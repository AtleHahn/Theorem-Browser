Statement

Let B be a Boolean algebra and let F be a proper filter, meaning a nonempty upper subset closed under binary meets and different from B. Call F prime when a\vee b\in F implies a\in F or b\in F, and call it an ultrafilter when maximal among proper filters. Then F is prime if and only if it is an ultrafilter.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement defines proper filters, primeness and maximality. The only additional setting is a Boolean algebra, which an algebra student can interpret as an abstract system of meet, join and complement with the powerset example. These related definitions are a small extension within about 30 minutes; arbitrary rather than finite algebras do not by themselves require graduate set theory.

Proof 1: Reverse the order — outline

For a proper ideal I of a Boolean algebra B, the following are equivalent:

  • I is a prime ideal.
  • I is a maximal ideal, i.e. for any proper ideal J, if I is contained in J then I=J.

This theorem is a well-known fact for Boolean algebras. Its dual establishes the equivalence of prime filters and ultrafilters: the dual orders of Boolean algebras are exactly the Boolean algebras themselves.
