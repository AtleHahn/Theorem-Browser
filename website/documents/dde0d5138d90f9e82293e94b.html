Statement

Let n\geq1 and let f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R be real analytic. Let x_0 be an isolated critical point: \nabla f(x_0)=0, and some neighborhood of x_0 contains no other zero of \nabla f. Let \varepsilon>0. Suppose \gamma:(0,\varepsilon)\to\mathbb R^n\setminus\{x_0\} parametrizes a nonconstant trajectory of the gradient vector field and \gamma(t)\to x_0 as t\downarrow0. Then the secant lines \mathbb R(\gamma(t)-x_0) have a limit in real projective space \mathbb{RP}^{n-1}.

Here the trajectory is understood geometrically, allowing reparametrization of flow time: \gamma is continuously differentiable and \gamma'(t)=a(t)\nabla f(\gamma(t)) for a continuous nowhere-zero real function a. The endpoint parameter t=0 is not required to be a finite time for the differential equation x'=\nabla f(x). The gradient is the vector of first partial derivatives; real analytic means locally represented by a convergent real power series. Real projective space consists of unoriented lines through zero. The asserted limit is equivalently the existence of a unit vector v\in\mathbb R^n such that

\left|\left\langle\frac{\gamma(t)-x_0}{\|\gamma(t)-x_0\|},v\right\rangle\right|\longrightarrow1,

using the Euclidean norm and inner product.
