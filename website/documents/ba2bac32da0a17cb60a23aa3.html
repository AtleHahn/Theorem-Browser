Statement

Let n,r\ge1 be integers. Let G=(\{1,\ldots,n\},E) be a finite undirected simple graph with no set of r+1 pairwise adjacent vertices. For real weights x_i\ge0 with \sum_{i=1}^n x_i=1, define f(x)=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}x_ix_j, counting each unordered edge once. Then f(x)\le\tfrac12(1-1/r).

Level
High school only
Level justification

Every graph and weight condition is explicit and finite; the assertion uses only real arithmetic and inequalities.

Proof 1: Transfer weights to a clique — outline

Begin by considering a {\displaystyle K_{r+1}} free graph with vertices labelled {\displaystyle 1,2,\ldots ,n}, and considering maximizing the function

{\displaystyle f(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots ,x_{n})=\sum _{\{i,j\}\in E}x_{i}x_{j}}

counting each unordered edge once, over all nonnegative {\displaystyle x_{1},x_{2},\ldots ,x_{n}} with sum {\displaystyle 1}. This function is known as the Lagrangian of the graph and its edges.

The idea behind the proof is that if {\displaystyle x_{i},x_{j}} are both nonzero while {\displaystyle i,j} are not adjacent in the graph, the function

{\displaystyle f(x_{1},\ldots ,x_{i}-t,\ldots ,x_{j}+t,\ldots ,x_{n})}

is linear in {\displaystyle t}. Hence, one can either replace {\displaystyle (x_{i},x_{j})} with either {\displaystyle (x_{i}+x_{j},0)} or {\displaystyle (0,x_{i}+x_{j})} without decreasing the value of the function. Hence, there is a point with at most {\displaystyle r} nonzero variables where the function is maximized.

Now, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives that the maximal value is at most {\displaystyle {\frac {1}{2}}\left(1-{\frac {1}{r}}\right)}.

Level justification

Move the weights of a nonadjacent pair to one endpoint without decreasing the finite sum. Repeating leaves a clique of size at most r; expand the square of the sum and apply finite Cauchy-Schwarz. No calculus maximization theorem is needed.
