Statement

A finite hypergraph H=(X\sqcup Y,E) is bipartite here if every edge contains exactly one vertex of Y. A matching consists of pairwise disjoint edges and is Y-perfect if it covers Y. For S\subseteq Y, put N(S)=\{e\setminus Y:e\in E,\ e\cap S\ne\varnothing\}. For Y=\{1,\ldots,m\} and H_i=N(\{i\}), a Y-perfect matching exists exactly when there are pairwise disjoint choices R_i\in H_i for all i.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Hyperedges are finite subsets carrying exactly one distinguished Y vertex, and the statement defines matching, coverage and the neighbor set-families. The conclusion compares choosing disjoint tagged subsets with choosing their untagged parts. These are elementary finite-set definitions and quantifiers, not an assumed hypergraph course.

Proof 1: Remove or restore each distinguished tag

Consider a bipartite hypergraph H=(X\sqcup Y,E) where Y=\{1,\ldots,m\}. It can be represented as an indexed collection of families of sets (H_1,\ldots,H_m), where for each i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}, H_i:=N(\{i\}) is the set-family of neighbors of i. For every subset Y_0 of Y, the set-family N(Y_0) is the union of the set-families H_i for i\in Y_0. A Y-perfect matching in H corresponds to an indexed family of m representatives: for each i, the set-family H_i is represented by a set R_i\in H_i, and the representative sets R_i are pairwise disjoint.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Each edge has exactly one distinguished tag i. Removing these distinct tags turns a perfect matching into pairwise disjoint sets R_i; restoring them reverses the construction. This uses finite sets and the explicitly supplied hypergraph definition only.
