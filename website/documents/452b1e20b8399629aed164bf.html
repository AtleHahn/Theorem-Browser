Statement

Independently draw with replacement from n\geq1 coupon types, each with probability 1/n, and let T be the number of draws until every type has appeared. Here n is an integer. Let S(k,m) denote the number of partitions of a k-element set into m nonempty unlabeled blocks, with S(0,0)=1 and S(k,m)=0 when m>k or m=0<k. Then for integers k\geq0, \Pr(T\leq k)=n!S(k,n)/n^k, and for integers k\geq1, \Pr(T=k)=(n-1)!S(k-1,n-1)/n^{k-1}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The draw experiment, stopping count and partition numbers are defined locally. Exact and cumulative probabilities concern finite strings of draws, counted with factorials and set partitions. Understanding these formulas and boundary conventions needs elementary counting and probability ratios, not general stopping-time theory.

Proof 1: Count surjections and take a difference

Sequences of k draws are functions from the k draw positions to the n types, counted by n^k, while surjections (that land on each type at least once) are counted by n!S(k,n), so the probability that all types were drawn within the k-th draw is

P(T\leq k)=\frac{n!S(k,n)}{n^k}.

By the recurrence relation of the Stirling numbers, S(k,n)=nS(k-1,n)+S(k-1,n-1), the probability that exactly k draws are needed is

P(T=k)=\frac{n!S(k,n)}{n^k} -\frac{n!S(k-1,n)}{n^{k-1}} =\frac{(n-1)!S(k-1,n-1)}{n^{k-1}}.
Level
High school only
Level justification

Count equally likely draw strings, identify onto strings by partitions and labelings, and subtract consecutive cumulative probabilities using the supplied finite recurrence. Only finite counting and probability ratios are used.
