Statement

Let f be an entire function with no zeros and finite order \rho\ge0. Here, putting H(r)=\max\{e,\max_{|z|=r}|f(z)|\}, the order is

\rho=\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{\log\log H(r)}{\log r}.

Then f=e^p for a polynomial p of degree at most \rho (constant polynomials are included).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex differentiation and exponential functions extend calculus directly; real analysis supplies series, compact convergence and growth limits. All special functions or growth parameters used here are explicitly given by formulas, so no meromorphic continuation or abstract complex geometry is needed.

Proof 1: Apply polynomiality to an entire logarithm (outline)

Use the standard entire-logarithm theorem: a nowhere-zero entire function on the simply connected plane has an entire logarithm g, with e^g=f. Then \operatorname{Re}g=\log|f|. Use also the improved Liouville result: if an entire function h satisfies \max_{|z|=R_k}\operatorname{Re}h(z)=o(R_k^{n+1}) along radii tending to infinity, for an integer n\ge0, then h is a polynomial of degree at most n.

Apply the improved Liouville theorem to g=\log(f).

The source leaves the order-to-growth substitution implicit.
