Statement

Let a<b, let f:[a,b]\to\mathbb R be Lebesgue integrable, and let U_i be iid uniform random variables on [0,1]. Put X_i=a+(b-a)U_i. Then \frac{b-a}{n}\sum_{i=1}^nf(X_i)\longrightarrow\int_a^bf(x)\,dx almost surely.

Beyond undergraduate level

Proof 1: Uniform-density expectation and the strong law

Let X_i=a+(b-a)U_i for i=1,2,\ldots. Then X_1,X_2,\ldots are independent and identically distributed uniform random variables on [a,b].

Evaluate f(X_1),f(X_2),\ldots,f(X_n). Take their average and multiply by b-a, computing

(b-a)\frac{f(X_1)+f(X_2)+\cdots+f(X_n)}{n}.

Since f is integrable, the strong law of large numbers says that this converges almost surely to

(b-a)\operatorname E(f(X_1)) =(b-a)\int_a^b f(x)\frac1{b-a}\,dx =\int_a^b f(x)\,dx.
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the strong law to the sampled integrable function and compute its uniform-density expectation. The mechanics are straightforward, but the full arbitrary Lebesgue-integrable function and almost-sure convergence framework is a cumulative extension; conservatively retain beyond undergraduate.
