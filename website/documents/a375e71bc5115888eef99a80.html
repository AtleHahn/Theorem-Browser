Statement

A partition is a finite sum of positive integers with order disregarded, listed in nonincreasing order when adjacent parts are compared. For every integer m\ge0, the number of partitions of m with adjacent parts differing by at least two and smallest part at least two equals the number of partitions with every part congruent to 2 or 3 modulo five. The empty partition is included for m=0.

Proof 1: Compare coefficients in the supplied generating-function identity

Write (a;q)_n=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(1-aq^j), with the analogous infinite product.

Consider the following generating-function interpretations:

  • \frac{q^{n^2+n}}{(q;q)_n} is the generating function for partitions with exactly n parts such that adjacent parts have difference at least 2 and such that the smallest part is at least 2.
  • \frac{1}{(q^2;q^5)_\infty(q^3;q^5)_\infty} is the generating function for partitions such that each part is congruent to either 2 or 3 modulo 5.

The Rogers–Ramanujan identity

\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{q^{n^2+n}}{(q;q)_n}=\frac{1}{(q^2;q^5)_\infty(q^3;q^5)_\infty}

can now be interpreted by comparing coefficients of q^m, giving the stated equality of partition counts.
