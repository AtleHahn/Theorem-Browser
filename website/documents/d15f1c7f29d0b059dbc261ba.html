Statement

Let \sigma>0 be noninteger and let S\subset\mathbb Z be finite. There are no nonnegative real coefficients \gamma_m satisfying (j-\sigma)(j-\sigma-1)=\sum_{m\in S}\gamma_m(j+m)(j+m-1)\qquad\text{for every }j\in\mathbb Z. Every product (j+m)(j+m-1) is an integer-grid quadratic value.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The displayed assertion and contradiction use only elementary quadratic factorization and an integer immediately above a noninteger real number.

Proof 1: Use a negative shifted value against nonnegative grid values

For every integer j+m, the product (j+m)(j+m-1) is nonnegative. Hence the right side would be nonnegative for all j. Choose an integer j with j-1<\sigma<j. Then 0<j-\sigma<1, and (j-\sigma)(j-\sigma-1)<0, a contradiction.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All integer-grid products of consecutive integers are nonnegative, but the selected shifted argument lies strictly between zero and one and gives a negative product. School integer ordering and signs suffice.
