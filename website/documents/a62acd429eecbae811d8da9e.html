Statement

For every integer k\ge1 and \varepsilon>0 there is an integer N(k,\varepsilon) such that the following holds. If X is a real normed space of dimension N(k,\varepsilon), and K=\{x\in X:\|x\|\le1\} is its unit ball, there exist a k-dimensional subspace E and an ellipsoid D\subset E centered at 0 such that D\subset K\cap E\subset(1+\varepsilon)D. An ellipsoid here is the unit ball of a positive definite quadratic norm on E.

Proof 1: Pass from norm comparison to unit balls — outline

Use the external Dvoretzky theorem: on some k-dimensional subspace E, a Euclidean norm |\cdot| satisfies |x|\le\|x\|\le(1+\varepsilon)|x|. The unit ball of a norm is bounded, symmetric and convex; the unit ball of a Euclidean norm is an ellipsoid. These unit-ball descriptions give the ellipsoid-section formulation.

Level justification

Translate the supplied comparison of two finite-dimensional norms into inclusions of their unit balls, rescaling the Euclidean ball to obtain D. Undergraduate linear algebra and norm inequalities suffice.
