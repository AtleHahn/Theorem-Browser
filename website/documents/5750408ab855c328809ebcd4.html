Statement

For a nondegenerate Euclidean triangle ABC, with A^{\prime},B^{\prime},C^{\prime} the midpoints of BC,CA,AB, the Miquel point (the common point of the circumcircles of AB^{\prime}C^{\prime}, A^{\prime}BC^{\prime} and A^{\prime}B^{\prime}C) is the circumcenter of ABC. In trilinear coordinates relative to ABC it is (\cos A:\cos B:\cos C).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Substitute one-half in the supplied trilinears

If the fractional distances of A', B' and C' along sides BC (a), CA (b) and AB (c) are d_a, d_b and d_c, respectively, the Miquel point, in trilinear coordinates (x:y:z), is given by

x=a\left(-a^2d_ad_a'+b^2d_ad_b+c^2d_a'd_c'\right),y=b\left(a^2d_a'd_b'-b^2d_bd_b'+c^2d_bd_c\right),z=c\left(a^2d_ad_c+b^2d_b'd_c'-c^2d_cd_c'\right),

where d_a'=1-d_a, etc. These coordinate formulas are used without proof.

In the case d_a=d_b=d_c=\tfrac12 the Miquel point is the circumcenter (\cos A:\cos B:\cos C).
