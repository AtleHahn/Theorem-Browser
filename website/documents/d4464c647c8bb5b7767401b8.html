Statement

Let 1\to A\xrightarrow{q}B\xrightarrow{r}C\to1 be an exact sequence of groups. If a homomorphism t:B\to A satisfies tq=\operatorname{id}_A, then h=(t,r):B\to A\times C is an isomorphism compatible with the injection of A and projection onto C. In particular the sequence also has a right splitting.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Exactness means the first map is injective, the last is surjective and image equals kernel. These and a left inverse are familiar group-map conditions; the explicit product isomorphism adds no homological algebra.

Proof 1: Product map and section — outline

The map h=t\times r gives an isomorphism B\to A\times C. Inverting it and composing with the natural injection C\to A\times C, c\mapsto(1,c), gives a homomorphism u:C\to B splitting r.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use exactness and the retraction to identify B with A times C, then compose the inverse with the second-factor inclusion. Undergraduate homomorphisms and kernels suffice; exact-sequence notation adds only the kernel-image condition.
