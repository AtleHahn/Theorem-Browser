Statement

Let D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}. For a sequence of open sets U_n\subseteq\mathbb C containing zero, let V_n be the connected component containing zero of the interior of \bigcap_{j\ge n}U_j; take V_n=\varnothing if zero is not in that interior. Its kernel is \bigcup_n V_n if this union is nonempty, and \{0\} otherwise. The sequence converges to its kernel if every subsequence has the same kernel.

Let f_n:D\to\mathbb C be injective holomorphic functions with f_n(0)=0 and f_n'(0)>0 real, and set U_n=f_n(D). Then (f_n) converges uniformly on every compact subset of D to a function f if and only if (U_n) converges to a kernel U\ne\mathbb C.

If U=\{0\} then f=0. Otherwise U is a connected open set, f is injective and holomorphic, and f(D)=U.

Level justification

Real analysis supplies compact-uniform convergence and point-set topology supplies connected components. The additional notion of complex differentiability (holomorphic functions) is a small extension of differentiation; the specialized domain kernel is explicitly defined. Proof 1: Normal families and identification of the kernel (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Definition 3; Theorems 12–13 and proofs, printed pp.77–84.).

The V_n are increasing connected open sets. Every compact subset of their union lies in one V_N. A Jordan curve in the kernel therefore lies in V_N and in every simply connected U_j, j>=N. Its bounded interior lies in every U_j and, being open and attached to V_N along the curve, lies in the same component of the interior of their intersection. Thus the kernel contains the interior of every Jordan curve in it and is simply connected. The usual eventual-compact-containment kernel definition agrees: compact connected neighborhoods and paths to 0 are eventually contained in all U_j. Apply Theorems 12–13, including the degenerate kernel.
