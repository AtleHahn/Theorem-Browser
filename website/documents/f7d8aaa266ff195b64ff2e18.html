Statement

For a positive integer k and a prime p, let v_p(k!) be the exponent of p in the prime factorization of k!. Then v_p(k!)<k/(p-1)\le k.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The finite prime exponent is explicitly defined and compared with rational numbers.

Proof 1: Bound Legendre’s sum by a geometric series

Let k be any positive integer. Then according to Legendre's formula

k!=\prod_{p\text{ prime}}p^{v_p(k!)},

where

v_p(k!)=\left\lfloor\frac{k}{p}\right\rfloor+ \left\lfloor\frac{k}{p^2}\right\rfloor+\cdots.

Thus

v_p(k!)<\frac{k}{p}+\frac{k}{p^2}+\cdots=\frac{k}{p-1}\le k.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the supplied finite floor sum, bound it strictly by the infinite positive geometric series and evaluate that series. Elementary number theory supplies the terms, while convergence and the infinite tail require basic series calculus.
