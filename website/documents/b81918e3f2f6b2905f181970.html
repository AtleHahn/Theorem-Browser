Statement

Let n\geq3 be an integer. A simple planar polygon is the closed region bounded by a polygonal loop with no self-intersections and no holes. A set G of points in the polygon P guards P if, for every p\in P, there is a q\in G such that the entire line segment [p,q] lies in P.

Every simple planar polygon with n vertices can be guarded by at most \lfloor n/3\rfloor guards, which may all be chosen at vertices. This bound is sharp: for each n\geq3 there are simple polygons with n vertices for which at least \lfloor n/3\rfloor guards are necessary, even when guards may be placed anywhere in the polygon. Here \lfloor t\rfloor denotes the greatest integer not exceeding t.

Level
High school only
Level justification

After the local definition of guarding, both the universal upper bound and the existence of worst-case polygons use only elementary planar geometry, finite counting and integer rounding. No graph algorithms or proof-specific triangulation theory is required to understand the complete statement.
