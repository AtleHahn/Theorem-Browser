Statement

Let f\in L^1(\mathbb R) be complex-valued, of bounded variation in a neighborhood of x, and continuous at x. Then \frac1\pi\lim_{M\to\infty}\int_0^M\!du\int_{\mathbb R}f(t)\cos(u(x-t))\,dt=f(x).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The L1 real-line domain and local bounded-variation condition require measure-theoretic analysis.

Proof 1: Evaluate Jordan’s limiting mean using continuity

Jordan’s Fourier integral test gives the left-hand side as \lim_{\varepsilon\to0}(f(x+\varepsilon)+f(x-\varepsilon))/2. At a continuity point this limit is f(x).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the Fourier integral convergence theorem for an arbitrary L1 function with local bounded variation and simplify the one-sided limits. General Lebesgue Fourier integrals remain an advanced prerequisite despite the final continuity step.
