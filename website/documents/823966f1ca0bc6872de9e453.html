Statement

Every polynomial in \mathbb R[x] of degree greater than two has a degree-two divisor in \mathbb R[x].

Level

Level justification

Proof 1: Select a conjugate pair or two real linear factors — outline

By the fundamental theorem of algebra, choose a complex root and divide by its linear factor; repeat on the polynomial of degree one less. This gives a complex linear factorization. For real coefficients, nonreal roots occur with their conjugates and the same multiplicities. Pair their factors: (x-r)(x-\bar r)=x^2-2\operatorname{Re}(r)x+|r|^2 has real coefficients. Real roots already give real linear factors. If there is a nonreal root, its conjugate pair supplies the desired quadratic. If every root is real, use two linear factors (counted with multiplicity).

Level justification

Use the stated existence of complex roots to factor repeatedly, then select a conjugate pair or two real factors. Elementary polynomial division and complex conjugation suffice for this application.
