Statement

Let n\geq1 and let A:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb C^{n\times n} be measurable. Assume there are constants 0<\lambda\leq\Lambda<\infty such that for almost every x\in\mathbb R^n and every \xi\in\mathbb C^n,

\operatorname{Re}\langle A(x)\xi,\xi\rangle\geq\lambda|\xi|^2,\qquad |A(x)\xi|\leq\Lambda|\xi|.

Here \langle\xi,\eta\rangle=\sum_j\xi_j\overline{\eta_j} and |\xi|^2=\langle\xi,\xi\rangle.

Work in complex L^2(\mathbb R^n) with Lebesgue measure and norm \|u\|_2=(\int|u|^2)^{1/2}. Let H^1(\mathbb R^n) consist of the L^2 functions whose first distributional derivatives also belong to L^2, and write \nabla u=(\partial_1u,\ldots,\partial_nu). Define the divergence-form operator L=-\operatorname{div}(A\nabla) by

D(L)=\left\{u\in H^1:\ \text{there is }g\in L^2\text{ with }\int\langle A\nabla u,\nabla v\rangle=\int g\overline v\text{ for every }v\in H^1\right\},\qquad Lu=g.

All integrals here are over \mathbb R^n. Let L^{1/2} be its closed sectorial square root, obtained by functional calculus with the branch of z^{1/2} positive on the positive real axis. Equivalently, it is the closure of the operator initially defined for u\in D(L) by

\frac1\pi\int_0^\infty t^{-1/2}L(tI+L)^{-1}u\,dt,

where I is the identity operator and the integral is a norm-convergent improper integral in L^2.

Then

D(L^{1/2})=H^1(\mathbb R^n),

and there are constants c,C>0 such that for every u\in H^1(\mathbb R^n),

c\|\nabla u\|_2\leq\|L^{1/2}u\|_2\leq C\|\nabla u\|_2.

The constants are independent of u. No symmetry or real-valuedness of the coefficient matrix is required.
