Statement

Two polygonal regions in the Euclidean plane have the same area if and only if they can each be cut into finitely many triangles that can be paired so that corresponding triangles differ by a translation and a rotation.

A dissection covers the region with its pieces, whose interiors are pairwise disjoint; shared boundary edges are permitted. The pieces may be moved independently. Thus the conclusion requires only finitely many straight cuts and rigid motions, without stretching or rescaling.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The complete statement involves familiar plane figures, area, finite cutting, and moving pieces by rotations and translations. The locally specified dissection convention and paired pieces require only elementary reasoning; no topology or measure theory is needed.

Proof 1: Triangulation, rectangle reduction and common refinement (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Sections1–3, Lemma2 and the proof of the WBG theorem).

The following coordinate construction supplies a diagram-independent rectangle step. For a triangle with vertices (0,0),(b,0),(c,h), choose its base so that 0<c<b. Cut at height h/2, and divide the upper triangle by the vertical through its apex. Half-turns about the two side midpoints move these two pieces into the corners of the rectangle [0,b]\times[0,h/2].

For two rectangles of area A, let their longer side lengths be a,b\geq\sqrt A. A parallelogram with side lengths a,b and angle satisfying \sin\theta=A/(ab) has area A. With either side horizontal, cut this parallelogram into vertical strips whose width is that side length, and translate each strip by an integer multiple of that width into one fixed strip. Each horizontal section has exactly that width, so the translated pieces fill the corresponding rectangle with disjoint interiors. This proves the rectangle conversion using translations and rotations only. Converting each triangular piece to a unit-width rectangle, stacking, and taking common triangular refinements completes the argument. These coordinate details are an independent supplement to the linked construction.
