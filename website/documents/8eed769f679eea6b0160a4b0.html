Statement

For every z\in\mathbb C, \sum_{m=0}^\infty z^m/m! converges absolutely; equivalently, its radius of convergence is infinite.

Proof 1: Apply the ratio test — outline

For complex z, consider the series

1+\frac{z}{1!}+\frac{z^2}{2!}+\frac{z^3}{3!}+\cdots =\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^n}{n!}.

Using the ratio test, it is possible to show that this power series has an infinite radius of convergence and so defines e^z for all complex z.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

For fixed nonzero z, successive absolute terms have ratio |z|/(m+1) tending to zero; z=0 is immediate. The ratio test is standard calculus of numerical series.
