Strictly larger polynomial space allowances enable problems inaccessible at smaller exponents. This gives a rigorous resource hierarchy within effective computation, distinguishing computability in principle from bounded computational power.

Statement

For positive integers k_1<k_2, \mathrm{DSPACE}(n^{k_1})\subsetneq\mathrm{DSPACE}(n^{k_2}) and \mathrm{NSPACE}(n^{k_1})\subsetneq\mathrm{NSPACE}(n^{k_2}), where these classes allow constant factors in the space bound.

Proof 1: Substitute polynomial bounds into the hierarchy theorems

Positive integer powers n^k are space-constructible, and n^{k_1}=o(n^{k_2}). Apply the deterministic and nondeterministic space hierarchy theorems: for space-constructible f and g=o(f), the corresponding g-space class is a proper subset of the f-space class.

Level

Level justification

Insert polynomial bounds into deterministic and nondeterministic space hierarchy theorems. Turing space classes, nondeterminism and space constructibility are several concepts not developed locally; a small extension from the stated baseline cannot be justified confidently.
