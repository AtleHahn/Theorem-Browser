Statement

Let W be a Coxeter group with generators X, braid relations R and relations x^2=1 for x\in X. Let A=\langle X\mid R\rangle be the corresponding Artin group. A reduced word means a word in X of minimum length representing an element of W. Sending w\in W to the element of A represented by any reduced word for w defines a well-defined set map W\to A.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The two groups are described by generators and relations and minimum-length words are defined. Presentations and independence of representative are a short extension of introductory undergraduate group theory. No geometric realization or classification of Coxeter or Artin groups is needed.

Proof 1: Apply the reduced-word braid-relation theorem

If two reduced words represent the same element of a Coxeter group, then Matsumoto's theorem states that the first word can be transformed into the second by repeatedly transforming

xyxy\ldots to yxyx\ldots (or vice versa),

using the braid relations in R. In other words: if two reduced words are equivalent in the group, then they are equivalent under the sole Artin relations.

These are defining relations of A=\langle X\mid R\rangle. Thus Matsumoto's theorem implies that the map taking an element of the Coxeter group represented by some reduced word in the generators to the same word in the generators of the Artin group is well-defined.
