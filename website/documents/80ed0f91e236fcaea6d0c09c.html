Statement

Let n\geq1, let \mu be a finite Borel measure on \mathbb R^n, and let A\subseteq\mathbb R^n be measurable for the completion of \mu. Use the completed measure when evaluating \mu(A\cap B). For x\in\mathbb R^n and r>0, let B_r(x)=\{y:|y-x|<r\} be the open Euclidean ball.

For \mu-almost every x\in\mathbb R^n, all the denominators \mu(B_r(x)) are positive and

\lim_{r\downarrow0}\frac{\mu(A\cap B_r(x))}{\mu(B_r(x))}=\mathbf1_A(x),

where \mathbf1_A(x) is 1 on A and 0 off A. In particular, the density is one at \mu-almost every point of A and zero at \mu-almost every point of its complement. Finite means \mu(\mathbb R^n)<\infty; Borel sets form the sigma-algebra generated by open sets, and completion adjoins all subsets of Borel null sets. Almost every refers to \mu, not to Lebesgue measure.
