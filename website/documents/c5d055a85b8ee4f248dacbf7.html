Statement

Deleting the center point of a standard fundamental polygon from a closed orientable genus-n surface, n\ge1, gives a space homotopy equivalent to a bouquet of 2n circles. Its fundamental group is free on 2n generators.

Proof 1: Boundary retraction — outline

Let B be the genus-n surface with the center point of its standard fundamental polygon deleted. One can deformation retract B onto the edges labeled by

A_1B_1A_1^{-1}B_1^{-1}A_2B_2A_2^{-1}B_2^{-1}\cdots A_nB_nA_n^{-1}B_n^{-1}.

This space is known to be the wedge sum of 2n circles (also called a bouquet of circles), which further is known to have fundamental group isomorphic to the free group with 2n generators. In this case these can be represented by the edges themselves:

\{A_1,B_1,\ldots,A_n,B_n\}.

Homotopy invariance gives the same fundamental group for B.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Retract the punctured polygon onto its glued edges and identify the fundamental group of the resulting bouquet using homotopy invariance. Polygonal surfaces, deformation retraction, fundamental groups and free groups together are not confidently a small extension of point-set topology; retain beyond undergraduate provisionally.
