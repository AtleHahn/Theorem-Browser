Statement

Let G be a discrete subgroup of \mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb C) and suppose it contains a parabolic element fixing w\in\widehat{\mathbb C}. Let G_w=\{g\in G:g(w)=w\}. There exists an open horoball H based at w in hyperbolic three-space such that

gH=H\quad(g\in G_w),\qquad gH\cap H=\varnothing\quad(g\in G\setminus G_w).

Consequently the natural map H/G_w\to\mathbb H^3/G embeds this quotient horoball as a cusp neighborhood.

Use the upper-half-space model \mathbb H^3=\{(z,t):z\in\mathbb C,\ t>0\} with metric (|dz|^2+dt^2)/t^2 and ideal boundary \widehat{\mathbb C}=\mathbb C\cup\{\infty\}. The group \mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb C)=\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb C)/\{\pm I\} acts by the hyperbolic isometries extending its boundary transformations z\mapsto(az+b)/(cz+d). Here \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb C) consists of complex matrices \begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix} with ad-bc=1, and I is the identity. A parabolic transformation is nonidentity with exactly one fixed point on the boundary. An open horoball based at a finite w is the interior of a Euclidean ball tangent to t=0 at w; at w=\infty it has the form t>h for h>0. Discreteness uses the matrix-group topology. Quotients identify points in the same group orbit. No torsion-free or finite-volume assumption is made; the ambient quotient can be an orbifold.
