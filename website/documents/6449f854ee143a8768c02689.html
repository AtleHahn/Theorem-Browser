Statement

Let m,n\ge1 and c_i>0 with \sum_{i=1}^m c_i=1. Then, taking the infimum over real symmetric positive-definite n\times n matrices A_i, \inf_{A_1,\ldots,A_m}\frac{\det(\sum_i c_i A_i)}{\prod_i(\det A_i)^{c_i}}=1.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Positive-definite real matrices; Determinants; Infima; Positive weights. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Log-concavity of the determinant — outline

Let D denote the infimum in the statement. The log-concavity of the determinant on positive-definite matrices implies that the determinant constant D for identity maps is 1.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply log-determinant concavity to get a lower bound of one and choose identical matrices to attain it. Finite-dimensional positive-definite linear algebra and the stated concavity inequality suffice.
