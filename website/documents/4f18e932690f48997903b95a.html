Statement

The open set N=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^2:|xy|<1\} contains \{0\}\times\mathbb R, but no open neighborhood U of 0 satisfies U\times\mathbb R\subseteq N.

Proof 1: Force the proposed neighborhood into every shrinking interval

If such U existed, then for every t>0, considering the coordinate y=t would imply U\subseteq(-1/t,1/t). Their intersection is \{0\}, so U=\{0\} because it contains 0. This is not open in \mathbb R, a contradiction. Openness of N follows from continuity of (x,y)\mapsto|xy|.
