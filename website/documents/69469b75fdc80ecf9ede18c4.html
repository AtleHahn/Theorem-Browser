Statement

Let distinct chords AC and BD of a Euclidean circle intersect at an interior point S, with four distinct endpoints. Then |AS|\,|SC|=|BS|\,|SD|.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Only circles, interior intersections and products of segment lengths occur.

Proof 1: Inscribed angles and similar triangles

By the inscribed angle theorem, ⁠{\displaystyle \angle BCA=\angle ADB}⁠ and ⁠{\displaystyle \angle DAC=\angle CBD}⁠, as each pair intercepts a common chord. Opposite angles at the point ⁠{\displaystyle S}⁠ are also congruent, ⁠{\displaystyle \angle ASD=\angle BSC}⁠. Therefore ⁠{\displaystyle \triangle ASD}⁠ and ⁠{\displaystyle \triangle BSC}⁠ are similar triangles, with congruent corresponding angles.

The sides of one triangle are uniformly proportional to the corresponding sides of any similar triangle, so

{\displaystyle {\frac {|AS|}{|BS|}}={\frac {|SD|}{|SC|}},}

or, equivalently,

{\displaystyle |AS|\cdot |SC|=|BS|\cdot |SD|.}
Level
High school only
Level justification

Use equal inscribed angles to obtain similar triangles, then cross-multiply the side ratio. High-school Euclidean geometry suffices.
