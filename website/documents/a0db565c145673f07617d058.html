Statement

For every integer k\geq1 there is an integer L\geq1 such that every positive integer is a sum of at most L nonnegative integer kth powers.

Proof 1: Apply the positive-density basis theorem — outline

Let \mathfrak G^k=\{i^k:i\geq0\}, and let N\mathfrak G^k denote its N-fold sumset, equivalently sums of at most N nonnegative integer kth powers. Write

\sigma(A)=\inf_{n\geq1}\frac{|A\cap\{1,\ldots,n\}|}{n}

for Schnirelmann density.

Use the positive-density theorem: for all k there exists N for which \sigma(N\mathfrak G^k)>0. Also use Schnirelmann's theorem that a set containing zero with positive Schnirelmann density is an additive basis of finite order, meaning a fixed finite number of its summands suffice for every positive integer.

We have thus established the general solution to Waring's problem: for all k there exists a bound, depending only on k, such that every positive integer can be expressed as the sum of at most that many kth powers. The source leaves composition of the two finite bounds implicit.
