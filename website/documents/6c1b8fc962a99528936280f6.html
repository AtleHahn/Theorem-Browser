Statement

Let p be an odd prime. Suppose there is a prime q such that no two nonzero pth-power residues modulo q differ by 1, and p is not a pth-power residue modulo q. Explicitly, there are no integers u,v with q\nmid uv and u^p-v^p\equiv1\pmod q, and no integer w with w^p\equiv p\pmod q.

If positive integers x,y,z satisfy x^p+y^p=z^p, then at least one of x,y,z is divisible by p^2. In particular, no such solution has p\nmid xyz (the first case of Fermat's Last Theorem).
