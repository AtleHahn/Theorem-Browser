Statement

Let X be a scheme quasi-projective over an affine scheme S. Then some vector bundle E on X has a torsor W→X whose total scheme W is affine.

Level

Level justification

Vector-bundle torsors over quasi-projective schemes require substantial scheme-theoretic background. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Reduce through a projective closure and blow-up — outline

Jouanolou's proof used an explicit construction. Let S be an affine scheme and {\displaystyle X=\mathbf {P} _{S}^{r}}. Interpret the affine space {\displaystyle \mathbf {A} _{S}^{(r+1)^{2}}} as the space of (r + 1) × (r + 1) matrices over S. Within this affine space, there is a closed subscheme W consisting of idempotent matrices of rank 1. The image of such a matrix is therefore a point in X, and the map {\displaystyle W\to X} that sends a matrix to the point corresponding to its image is the map claimed in the statement of the theorem. To show that this map has the desired properties, Jouanolou notes that there is a short exact sequence of vector bundles:

{\displaystyle 0\to {\mathcal {O}}_{X}(-1)\to {\mathcal {O}}_{X}^{\oplus r+1}\to {\mathcal {F}}\to 0,}

where the first map is defined by multiplication by a basis of sections of {\displaystyle {\mathcal {O}}_{X}(1)} and the second map is the cokernel. The source supplies no verification of the torsor action: Jouanolou then asserts that W is a torsor for {\displaystyle {\mathcal {E}}=\operatorname {Hom} ({\mathcal {F}},{\mathcal {O}}_{X}(-1))}.

Jouanolou deduces the theorem in general by reducing to the above case. If X is projective over an affine scheme S, then it admits a closed immersion into some projective space {\displaystyle \mathbf {P} _{S}^{r}}. Pulling back the variety W constructed above for {\displaystyle \mathbf {P} _{S}^{r}} along this immersion yields the desired variety W for X. Finally, if X is quasi-projective, then it may be realized as an open subscheme of a projective S-scheme. Blow up the complement of X to get {\displaystyle {\bar {X}}}, and let {\displaystyle i\colon X\to {\bar {X}}} denote the inclusion morphism. The complement of X in {\displaystyle {\bar {X}}} is a Cartier divisor, and therefore i is an affine morphism. Now perform the previous construction for {\displaystyle {\bar {X}}} and pull back along i.

The pullback in the projective case is closed in the affine scheme W, and the last pullback is affine over the affine torsor for \bar X. Both are therefore affine; the torsor property is preserved by base change.
