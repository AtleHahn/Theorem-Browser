Statement

For f(x)=x³ on R, 0 is a degenerate critical point and every sublevel set {x:f(x)≤a} is a closed half-line. For ε>0, f_ε(x)=x³+εx has no critical point; for ε<0 it has exactly two, both nondegenerate.

Proof 1: Differentiate the cubic and its perturbation

Let {\displaystyle M=\mathbb {R} } and let {\displaystyle f(x)=x^{3}.} Then {\displaystyle 0} is a critical point of {\displaystyle f,} but writing M^a=\{x:f(x)\leq a\}=(-\infty,\sqrt[3]{a}], the topology of {\displaystyle M^{a}} does not change when {\displaystyle a} passes {\displaystyle 0.} The problem is that the second derivative is {\displaystyle f''(0)=0}—that is, the Hessian of {\displaystyle f} vanishes and the critical point is degenerate. This situation is unstable, since by slightly deforming {\displaystyle f} to {\displaystyle f_{\epsilon}(x)=x^{3}+\epsilon x}, the degenerate critical point is either removed ({\displaystyle \epsilon >0}) or breaks up into two non-degenerate critical points ({\displaystyle \epsilon <0}).

Compute 3x squared+epsilon and the second derivative 6x; inspect its real zeros and express each original sublevel set as a half-line. Degeneracy here is explicitly just a zero second derivative.
