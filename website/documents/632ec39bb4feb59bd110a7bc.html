Statement

Let a<b be real, let (x_n) be distinct points of (a,b), and let \lambda_n,\mu_n\geq0 have finite total sum, with \lambda_n+\mu_n>0 for every n. Define the normalized nonnegative jump function f:[a,b]\to\mathbb R by f(x)=\sum_{x_n\leq x}\lambda_n+\sum_{x_n<x}\mu_n. For c>0, let U_c(f)=\{x\in(a,b):\text{ there exist }a\leq s<x<t\leq b\text{ with }(f(t)-f(s))/(t-s)>c\}. Then U_c(f) is open and its Lebesgue length satisfies \ell(U_c(f))\leq4c^{-1}(f(b)-f(a)).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Real analysis supplies convergent nonnegative series, ordinary derivatives and secant slopes. Null sets mean interval covers of arbitrarily small length, and the length of an open set is the sum of its disjoint interval lengths. These limited additions suffice without general integration.

Proof 1: Thin a finite interval cover and sum the slope bounds — outline

By definition U_c(f) is an open subset of (a,b), so can be written as a disjoint union of at most countably many open intervals I_k=(a_k,b_k). Let J_k be an interval with closure in I_k and \ell(J_k)=\ell(I_k)/2. By compactness, there are finitely many open intervals of the form (s,t), with (f(t)-f(s))/(t-s)>c, covering the closure of J_k.

Use the elementary interval-cover thinning fact: the finite cover can be taken as adjacent open intervals (s_{k,1},t_{k,1}), (s_{k,2},t_{k,2}), … only intersecting at consecutive intervals. Hence

\ell(J_k)\leq\sum_m(t_{k,m}-s_{k,m}) \leq\sum_m c^{-1}(f(t_{k,m})-f(s_{k,m})) \leq2c^{-1}(f(b_k)-f(a_k)).

Finally sum both sides over k. Since \ell(I_k)=2\ell(J_k), monotonicity gives

\ell(U_c(f))\leq4c^{-1}(f(b)-f(a)).
