Statement

Let U be a finite set of variables indexed by a finite undirected graph, with a positive joint mass or density p. Write \partial x for the neighbors of vertex x. If the local Markov property holds, meaning that x is conditionally independent of U\setminus(\{x\}\cup\partial x) given \partial x, then p(U)=f_x(x,\partial x)f_{-x}(U\setminus\{x\}) for suitable factors. For densities, identities are understood almost everywhere, as in the usual conditional-density factorization.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Finite-dimensional mass/density functions, marginals and conditional independence are closely related ratio/integration definitions extending undergraduate multivariable calculus. Almost-everywhere equality means ignoring a set of measure zero; no abstract conditional expectation is constructed.

Proof 1: Separate one variable from the rest — outline

The local Markov property says the conditional law of x given all other variables depends only on its neighbors \partial x. Factoring the joint law into that conditional law and the marginal law of the remaining variables gives the displayed factors.

Level justification

Factor a positive joint mass or density into a conditional law and marginal, then use the supplied conditional-independence condition to drop nonneighbors. Undergraduate multivariate probability suffices; no abstract sigma-algebra conditional expectation is constructed.
