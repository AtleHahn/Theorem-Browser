Statement

Let ABC be a nondegenerate plane triangle with side lengths a=|BC|, b=|CA|, c=|AB| and incenter I. Extend both sides incident to A beyond A by length a, both sides incident to B beyond B by length b, and both sides incident to C beyond C by length c. The six extension endpoints lie on a circle centered at I. If r is the inradius and s=(a+b+c)/2 the semiperimeter, the radius of this circle is \sqrt{r^2+s^2}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All objects are defined by elementary triangle geometry and lengths.

Proof 1: Common right-triangle legs and Pythagoras — outline

Name the extension endpoints P_a,Q_a on AB,AC beyond A, respectively; P_b,Q_b on BC,BA beyond B; and P_c,Q_c on CA,CB beyond C.

Let I be the center of the incircle of triangle ABC, r its radius and F_a,F_b,F_c the three points where the incircle touches the triangle sides a,b,c. Since the (extended) triangle sides are tangents of the incircle it follows that IF_a,IF_b,IF_c are perpendicular to a,b,c. Furthermore, the following equalities for line segments hold:

|AF_c|=|AF_b|,\qquad |BF_c|=|BF_a|,\qquad |CF_a|=|CF_b|.

With that the six triangles IF_cP_a, IF_cQ_b, IF_aP_b, IF_aQ_c, IF_bQ_a and IF_bP_c all have a side of length |AF_c|+|BF_c|+|CF_a| and a side of length r with a right angle between them.

The common leg |AF_c|+|BF_c|+|CF_a| is the semiperimeter s. Thus, by the Pythagorean theorem, the radius of the Conway circle is

\sqrt{r^2+s^2},

where r and s are the inradius and semiperimeter of the triangle. The source states this radius formula after the common-right-triangle calculation, leaving the semiperimeter identification implicit.

Level justification

Equal tangent lengths identify the common horizontal leg of each contact-point right triangle as the semiperimeter. The other leg is the inradius; Pythagoras gives the common radius. Only elementary triangle geometry is used.
