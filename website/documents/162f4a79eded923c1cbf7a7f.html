Statement

Let P_1,\ldots,P_8 be distinct points of \mathbb P^2(K) over an algebraically closed field. Suppose P_1,\ldots,P_4 lie on a line L, and no seven of the eight lie on a conic. Multiplication by a nonzero linear equation of L identifies the vector space of quadratic forms vanishing at P_5,\ldots,P_8 with the cubic forms vanishing at all eight points. These spaces have dimension two.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use homogeneous coordinate triples modulo scale over an algebraically closed field, with lines and conics given by linear and quadratic homogeneous polynomials. The assertion identifies two polynomial vector spaces by multiplication and specifies their dimensions. These definitions and polynomial divisibility fit a short extension of undergraduate abstract and linear algebra.

Proof 1: Factor out the line and use four-point conic interpolation — outline

Assume P1, P2, P3, P4 are collinear and no seven points out of P1, ..., P8 are co-conic. Then no five points of P1, ..., P8 and no three points of P5, P6, P7, P8 are collinear. Since a cubic C through the eight points will always contain the whole line through P1, P2, P3, P4 on account of Bézout's theorem, the vector space of cubic homogeneous polynomials that vanish on (the affine cones of) P1, ..., P8 is isomorphic to the vector space of quadratic homogeneous polynomials that vanish (the affine cones of) P5, P6, P7, P8, which has dimension two. The isomorphism is multiplication by a nonzero linear equation of L.
