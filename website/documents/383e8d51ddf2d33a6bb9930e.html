Separate time continuity and a uniform Lipschitz bound imply joint continuity

Statement

Let D\subset\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^n be a rectangle. Suppose f:D\to\mathbb R^n is continuous in t for each fixed y and \|f(t,y)-f(t,z)\|\le L\|y-z\| with a constant L>0 independent of t. Then f is jointly continuous.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Multivariable continuity; Uniform Lipschitz bounds. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Split the difference in the two variables

Let L>0 be the Lipschitz constant of (t,y)\mapsto f(t,y) with respect to y. Since t\mapsto f(t,y) is a continuous function of t, for any point (t_0,y_0)\in D and \epsilon>0 there exists \delta>0 such that

\|f(t,y_0)-f(t_0,y_0)\|<\epsilon/2

when |t-t_0|<\delta. We have

\|f(t,y)-f(t_0,y_0)\| \leq\|f(t,y)-f(t,y_0)\| +\|f(t,y_0)-f(t_0,y_0)\|<\epsilon,

provided |t-t_0|<\delta and \|y-y_0\|<\epsilon/(2L), which shows that f is continuous at (t_0,y_0).

Level justification

Split the difference into variation in y and variation in t, bounding each by half epsilon. Undergraduate multivariable epsilon-delta continuity suffices.
