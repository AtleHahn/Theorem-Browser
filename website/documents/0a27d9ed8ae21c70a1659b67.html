Statement

Let A,B,C,D be points in Euclidean space and set E=(A+B)/2, F=(B+C)/2, G=(C+D)/2, H=(D+A)/2. Then E,F,G,H form a possibly degenerate planar parallelogram, even if the original quadrilateral is skew.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement says that successive side midpoints of a quadrilateral form a planar parallelogram, including when the original quadrilateral is skew. Understanding it requires elementary spatial geometry and midpoint coordinates, not the linear-algebra formulation of its proof.

Proof 1: Affine midpoint identity

Varignon's theorem can be proved as a theorem of affine geometry organized as linear algebra with the linear combinations restricted to coefficients summing to 1, also called affine or barycentric coordinates. The proof applies even to skew quadrilaterals in spaces of any dimension.

Any three points E,F,G are completed to a parallelogram by taking its fourth vertex to be E-F+G. Its affine span has dimension at most two, including the collinear case. In the construction of the Varignon parallelogram this is the point

(A+B)/2-(B+C)/2+(C+D)/2=(A+D)/2.

But this is the point H, whence EFGH forms a parallelogram.

Level justification

Compute the fourth affine vertex and use the span of two difference vectors for planarity in arbitrary dimension. Introductory linear algebra suffices.
