Statement

Let a point P lie at distance h>0 from a line. Two rays toward the line meet it at distinct points S,T, in increasing order, with signed angles -\pi/2<\gamma<\eta<\pi/2 relative to the perpendicular toward the line. If r is the inradius of PST, then (h-r)(\tan\eta-\tan\gamma)=r(\sec\gamma+\sec\eta).

Level
High school only
Level justification

The hypotheses and identity concern elementary Euclidean lengths and trigonometry.

Proof 1: Similar right triangles on the incenter parallel — outline

Let P have perpendicular foot R on the baseline, with PR=h. The rays PS,PT meet the baseline at S,T in increasing order and have signed angles \gamma<\eta to PR, both in (-\pi/2,\pi/2). Let O be the incenter of PST, with radius r. The line through O parallel to the baseline meets PR,PS,PT at Q,X,Y, respectively. The incircle touches the two rays at W,Z. Thus PQ=h-r and QR=r.

For nondegenerate auxiliary triangles, OWX is similar to PQX, and OZY to PQY. Using XY=XO+OY gives

(h-r)(\tan\eta-\tan\gamma)=r(\sec\gamma+\sec\eta).

Level justification

Use similar right triangles along the parallel through the incenter and add the two horizontal pieces. School similarity and tangent/secant ratios suffice.
