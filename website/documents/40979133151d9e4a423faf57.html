Statement

Let A,B\subseteq X in a topological space, and suppose a continuous f:X\to[0,1] is zero on A and one on B. Then the same function is zero on \overline A and one on \overline B; these closures have disjoint open neighborhoods.

Proof 1: Closed fibers and disjoint interval preimages

It follows that if two subsets A and B are separated by a function then so are their closures. Also it follows that if two subsets A and B are separated by a function then A and B are separated by neighbourhoods. Here separation by a function means separation by a continuous f:X\to[0,1] taking the values 0 and 1 on the respective sets.

Applying the second assertion to the closures gives the stated neighbourhood separation. The source asserts these consequences without supplying the closed-fiber or interval-preimage checks.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The closed fibers at zero and one contain the respective closures. Their inverse images of disjoint small interval neighborhoods give disjoint open neighborhoods. This is basic point-set topology.
