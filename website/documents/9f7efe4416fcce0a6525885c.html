Statement

For a commutative unital ring A, write \operatorname{Spec}A for its prime ideals. Set \mathbb V(S)=\{\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Spec}A:S\subseteq\mathfrak p\} and \mathbb I(X)=\bigcap_{\mathfrak p\in X}\mathfrak p. Then for every ideal J of A, \mathbb I(\mathbb V(J))=\sqrt J.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory ring theory supplies prime ideals. The spectrum is used only as their set, V and I are explicitly defined, and the radical consists of elements with a power in the ideal. These closely related definitions suffice without scheme theory.

Proof 1: Apply the prime-intersection description of the radical

By the definitions,

\mathbb I(\mathbb V(J)) =\bigcap_{\mathfrak p\in\mathbb V(J)}\mathfrak p =\bigcap_{\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Spec}A,\ \mathfrak p\supseteq J}\mathfrak p =\sqrt J,

where the last equality follows from the standard property that the radical of an ideal is the intersection of all prime ideals containing it.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Expand the two defined set operations and invoke the supplied prime-intersection description of a radical. Prime ideals and ideal radicals are a small extension of introductory commutative rings; no spectrum topology or scheme theory is used.
