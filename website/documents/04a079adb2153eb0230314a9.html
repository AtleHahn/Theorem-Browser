Statement

Let X,Y be independent centered real Gaussian vectors with covariance matrices M,N. Then \operatorname{Cov}(X_iY_i,X_jY_j)=M_{ij}N_{ij}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Finite Gaussian vectors can be understood as linear images of independent normal coordinates; covariance is the matrix of centered product expectations. These linked finite density/moment definitions extend undergraduate integration and linear algebra without stochastic processes.

Proof 1: Wick expansion and zero mixed covariances

Here \langle Z\rangle denotes \mathbb E Z.

Let {\displaystyle X} and {\displaystyle Y} be {\displaystyle n}-dimensional centered Gaussian random variables with covariances {\displaystyle \left\langle X_{i}X_{j}\right\rangle =M_{ij}}, {\displaystyle \left\langle Y_{i}Y_{j}\right\rangle =N_{ij}} and independent from each other so that we have

{\displaystyle \left\langle X_{i}Y_{j}\right\rangle =0} for any {\displaystyle i,j}

Then the covariance matrix of {\displaystyle X_{i}Y_{i}} and {\displaystyle X_{j}Y_{j}} is

{\displaystyle \operatorname {Cov} \left(X_{i}Y_{i},X_{j}Y_{j}\right)=\left\langle X_{i}Y_{i}X_{j}Y_{j}\right\rangle -\left\langle X_{i}Y_{i}\right\rangle \left\langle X_{j}Y_{j}\right\rangle }

Using Wick's theorem to develop

{\displaystyle \left\langle X_{i}Y_{i}X_{j}Y_{j}\right\rangle =\left\langle X_{i}X_{j}\right\rangle \left\langle Y_{i}Y_{j}\right\rangle +\left\langle X_{i}Y_{i}\right\rangle \left\langle X_{j}Y_{j}\right\rangle +\left\langle X_{i}Y_{j}\right\rangle \left\langle X_{j}Y_{i}\right\rangle }

and also using the independence of {\displaystyle X} and {\displaystyle Y}, we have

{\displaystyle \operatorname {Cov} \left(X_{i}Y_{i},X_{j}Y_{j}\right)=\left\langle X_{i}X_{j}\right\rangle \left\langle Y_{i}Y_{j}\right\rangle =M_{ij}N_{ij}}
