Statement

Let n\geq2 be an integer and let V_1,\ldots,V_n be nonatomic finite positive measures on [0,1]. Suppose A is a union of at most n-1 intervals and all V_i(A)=w. Then a subset of A formed from at most n-1 intervals has value w/2 for each measure.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The local halving assertion uses arbitrary nonatomic measures, rather than just integrals of elementary densities.

Proof 1: Bisect the moment-curve measures and count pieces — outline

Assume that A is a union of at most n-1 intervals and that all n measures value it as exactly w.

  • Define the following function on [0,1]:f(t)=(t,t^2,\ldots,t^n).
  • Define the following measures on \mathbb R^n:U_i(Y)=V_i(f^{-1}(Y)\cap A).
  • Note that f^{-1}(\mathbb R^n)=[0,1]. Hence, for every i, U_i(\mathbb R^n)=w.
  • By the Stone–Tukey theorem, there is a hyperplane that cuts \mathbb R^n into two half-spaces H,H' such that\forall i=1,\ldots,n:\quad U_i(H)=U_i(H')=w/2.The version used here applies to n finite measures on \mathbb R^n assigning zero mass to every affine hyperplane. Every such hyperplane intersects the moment curve in at most n points, so nonatomicity gives this condition.
  • Define M=f^{-1}(H)\cap A and M'=f^{-1}(H')\cap A. Then, by the definition of the U_i,\forall i=1,\ldots,n:\quad V_i(M)=V_i(M')=w/2.
  • The set A has at most n-1 connected components (intervals). Hence, its image f(A) also has at most n-1 connected components (one-dimensional curves in \mathbb R^n).
  • The hyperplane that forms the boundary between H and H' intersects f(A) in at most n points. Ignoring isolated cut points, which have zero mass, the total number of pieces in H\cap f(A) and H'\cap f(A) is therefore at most 2n-1. Hence, one of these must have at most n-1 components.
  • Suppose it is H that has at most n-1 components (curves). Hence, M has at most n-1 components (intervals), after the harmless removal of isolated cut points.
  • Hence, we can take the required subset of value w/2 to be M.

Level justification

Push nonatomic measures onto the moment curve, verify hyperplane-nullness, apply simultaneous bisection and count interval pieces. General nonatomic measures and their pushforwards are substantial measure-theoretic prerequisites even though the root count is elementary.
