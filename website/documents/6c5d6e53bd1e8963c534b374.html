Statement

In a field K, the element 16 is an eighth power if and only if at least one of 2,-2,-1 is a square in K, with integers interpreted through the prime subfield.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Fields, squares and powers are introductory algebra, and integer constants are interpreted in the field as stated. The equivalence needs no extension-field arithmetic.

Proof 1: Field criterion for sixteen to be an eighth power

Generally, 16 is an eighth power in a field K if and only if the polynomial X^8-16 has a root in K. Write

X^8-16=(X^4-4)(X^4+4) =(X^2-2)(X^2+2)(X^2-2X+2)(X^2+2X+2).

Thus, 16 is an eighth power in K if and only if 2, -2 or -1 is a square in K. Here the last two quadratic factors are (X-1)^2+1 and (X+1)^2+1.

Level justification

Factor the polynomial over an arbitrary field and complete two squares. Introductory undergraduate field algebra and the absence of zero divisors suffice; no field-extension theory is used.
