Statement

Fix a context-free grammar G with start symbol S and language L. There is an integer p\geq1 such that, for every word w\in L and every marking of at least p positions of w, one can write

w=uxzyv

as a concatenation of five terminal strings and choose a nonterminal A of G so that

S\Rightarrow^*uAv,\qquad A\Rightarrow^*xAy,\qquad A\Rightarrow^*z,

with these properties:

  • z contains at least one marked position.
  • xzy contains at most p marked positions.
  • Both u and x contain marked positions, or both y and v contain marked positions.

A context-free grammar has finite sets of terminal and nonterminal symbols and finitely many replacement rules, each replacing a single nonterminal by a string of symbols. Its language comprises the terminal strings derivable from S. The notation \Rightarrow^* means derivation in zero or more steps. Marking selects positions in the original word, and each substring inherits those marks.
