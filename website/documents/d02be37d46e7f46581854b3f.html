Statement

Let P be a probability measure on a nonempty measurable space X, let m\ge1 be an integer, and let R\subseteq X^{2m} be measurable. Let \Gamma_m consist of the 2^m permutations independently choosing whether to swap coordinates i and m+i for each 1\le i\le m. Then P^{2m}(R)=\mathbb E_{x\sim P^{2m}}\Pr_{\sigma\sim\mathrm{Unif}(\Gamma_m)}(\sigma x\in R)\le\max_{x\in X^{2m}}\Pr_\sigma(\sigma x\in R).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The equality concerns product probability measures on arbitrary measurable spaces and a finite permutation average.

Proof 1: Averaging the product-measure invariance

For any {\displaystyle \sigma \in \Gamma _{m},}

{\displaystyle P^{2m}(R)=P^{2m}\{x:\sigma (x)\in R\}}

(since coordinate permutations preserve the product distribution {\displaystyle P^{2m}}.)

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\therefore P^{2m}(R)={}&\int _{X^{2m}}1_{R}(x)\,dP^{2m}(x)\\[5pt]={}&{\frac {1}{|\Gamma _{m}|}}\sum _{\sigma \in \Gamma _{m}}\int _{X^{2m}}1_{R}(\sigma (x))\,dP^{2m}(x)\\[5pt]={}&\int _{X^{2m}}{\frac {1}{|\Gamma _{m}|}}\sum _{\sigma \in \Gamma _{m}}1_{R}(\sigma (x))\,dP^{2m}(x)\\[5pt]&{\text{(because }}|\Gamma _{m}|{\text{ is finite)}}\\[5pt]={}&\int _{X^{2m}}\Pr[\sigma (x)\in R]\,dP^{2m}(x)\quad {\text{(the expectation)}}\\[5pt]\leq {}&\max _{x\in X^{2m}}(\Pr[\sigma (x)\in R]).\end{aligned}}}

The maximum is guaranteed to exist since there is only a finite set of values that probability under a random permutation can take.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Average permutation invariance of a general product measure, interchange the finite sum and integral, and bound by the maximum. Arbitrary measurable product spaces and their integration require measure theory; retain conservatively.
