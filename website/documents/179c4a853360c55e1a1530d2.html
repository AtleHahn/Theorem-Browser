Statement

A drawing of a finite graph on a surface places vertices at distinct points and represents edges by arcs whose interiors avoid the vertices; crossings are finitely many transverse intersections of edge interiors, with no triple crossings. A graph embeds in the surface if it has a drawing without crossings. Two edges are independent if they have no common endpoint. Let S be either the real projective plane (the sphere with antipodal points identified) or the torus S^1\times S^1, where S^1=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|=1\}. A finite graph embeds in S if and only if it has a drawing on S in which every pair of independent edges crosses an even number of times.

Proof 1: Parity-preserving reductions on two surfaces (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorem 1.1; Sections 3–6, final argument Section 6.3, pp. 24–25.). Supporting sources: Colin de Verdière et al., A Direct Proof of the Strong Hanani–Tutte Theorem on the Projective Plane, §4.

Torus: Fulek–Pelsmajer–Schaefer, Theorem 1.1 and Section 6.3. Projective plane: Colin de Verdiere, Kaluza, Patak, Patakova and Tancer, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1608.07855, Theorem 1 and Section 4 (pp.14–19), with the auxiliary results proved in the same paper. A crossing-free embedding immediately supplies the converse for either surface.

Level justification

The two proofs use finite graph connectivity, induction, crossing parity and elementary linear algebra over the two-element field. For these particular surfaces, the curve information is one or two parity coordinates, and the cutting operations use explicit disk, cylinder and Möbius-strip models. Planar and weak surface Hanani–Tutte dependencies have combinatorial cutting proofs; the needed obstruction for K3,7 reduces to a matrix-rank inequality. The additional terminology and concrete surface facts fit the short preparation allowance from introductory algebra and topology; the long case analyses are reading of the proof itself.
