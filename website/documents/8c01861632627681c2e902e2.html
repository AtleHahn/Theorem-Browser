Statement

Fix an integer d\ge1, let E\subseteq\mathbb R^d, and let \lambda_d denote Lebesgue measure. A family \mathcal V is a Vitali cover of E if for every x\in E and \delta>0 some U\in\mathcal V contains x and has 0<\operatorname{diam}(U)<\delta. It is regular if a fixed finite constant C satisfies \operatorname{diam}(U)^d\leq C\lambda_d(U) for every U\in\mathcal V. If E is Lebesgue measurable with finite measure and \mathcal V is a regular Vitali cover by closed sets, there is a finite or countable disjoint selection (U_j) with \lambda_d(E\setminus\bigcup_jU_j)=0.

Proof 1: Excluding the divergent Hausdorff alternative

By outer regularity choose an open set B\supseteq E with finite Lebesgue measure, and restrict the family to sets contained in B. This remains a Vitali covering: sufficiently small sets containing a point of the open set B stay inside B.

Use the Hausdorff covering dichotomy: a Vitali class of closed sets for an H^d-measurable set has a finite or countable disjoint subcollection \{U_j\} such that either H^d(E\setminus\bigcup_jU_j)=0 or \sum_j\operatorname{diam}(U_j)^d=\infty. Here H^d denotes d-dimensional Hausdorff measure.

Indeed, the Hausdorff measure Hd on Rd coincides with a multiple of the d-dimensional Lebesgue measure. If a disjoint collection {\displaystyle \{U_{j}\}} is regular and contained in a measurable region B with finite Lebesgue measure, then

{\displaystyle \sum _{j}\operatorname {diam} (U_{j})^{d}\leq C\sum _{j}\lambda _{d}(U_{j})\leq C\,\lambda _{d}(B)<+\infty }

which excludes the divergent alternative of the Hausdorff covering dichotomy. It follows that E is covered, up to a Lebesgue-negligible set, by the selected disjoint subcollection.
