Statement

Let A,B\subseteq\mathbb R^n be disjoint nonempty closed convex sets, at least one compact. There are v\ne0 and c_A<c_B such that \langle v,a\rangle\le c_A<c_B\le\langle v,b\rangle for every a\in A,b\in B. Thus two parallel hyperplanes separated by a positive gap separate the sets.

Proof 1: Closest pair and a negative directional derivative

WLOG, {\displaystyle A} is compact. By the closest-pair lemma for a nonempty compact set and a nonempty closed set, there exist points {\displaystyle a_{0}\in A} and {\displaystyle b_{0}\in B} of minimum distance to each other. Since {\displaystyle A} and {\displaystyle B} are disjoint, we have {\displaystyle a_{0}\neq b_{0}}. Now, construct two hyperplanes {\displaystyle L_{A},L_{B}} perpendicular to line segment {\displaystyle [a_{0},b_{0}]}, with {\displaystyle L_{A}} across {\displaystyle a_{0}} and {\displaystyle L_{B}} across {\displaystyle b_{0}}. We claim that neither {\displaystyle A} nor {\displaystyle B} enters the space between {\displaystyle L_{A},L_{B}}, and thus the perpendicular hyperplanes to {\displaystyle (a_{0},b_{0})} satisfy the requirement of the theorem.

Algebraically, the hyperplanes {\displaystyle L_{A},L_{B}} are defined by the vector {\displaystyle v:=b_{0}-a_{0}}, and two constants {\displaystyle c_{A}:=\langle v,a_{0}\rangle <c_{B}:=\langle v,b_{0}\rangle }, such that {\displaystyle L_{A}=\{x:\langle v,x\rangle =c_{A}\},L_{B}=\{x:\langle v,x\rangle =c_{B}\}}. Our claim is that {\displaystyle \forall a\in A,\langle v,a\rangle \leq c_{A}} and {\displaystyle \forall b\in B,\langle v,b\rangle \geq c_{B}}.

Suppose for contradiction that there is some point {\displaystyle a\in A} such that {\displaystyle \langle v,a\rangle >c_{A}}. Then since the gradient of the function {\displaystyle f(z)=\|z-b_{0}\|^{2}} is given by {\displaystyle \nabla f(z)=2(z-b_{0})}, it follows that the directional derivative {\displaystyle \nabla _{a-a_{0}}f(a_{0})} has the same sign as {\displaystyle \langle a-a_{0},2(a_{0}-b_{0})\rangle =-2\langle a-a_{0},v\rangle }, which is negative. So for sufficiently small {\displaystyle \epsilon >0}, the point {\displaystyle a'=a_{0}+\epsilon (a-a_{0})} satisfies {\displaystyle f(a')<f(a_{0})}, which contradicts the choice of {\displaystyle a_{0}} since {\displaystyle a'\in A} by convexity. A similar argument shows {\displaystyle \forall b\in B,\langle v,b\rangle \geq c_{B}}.
