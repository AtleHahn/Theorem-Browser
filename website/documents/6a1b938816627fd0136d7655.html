Statement

A normal function is a class function f:\mathrm{Ord}\to\mathrm{Ord} that is strictly increasing and satisfies f(\lambda)=\sup_{\xi<\lambda}f(\xi) at each nonzero limit ordinal \lambda. For every nonempty set A of ordinals, f(\sup A)=\sup\{f(\alpha):\alpha\in A\}.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Ordinals and class functions; Ordinal suprema; Normal functions. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Use the largest element or ordinal continuity

If \sup A is 0 or a successor ordinal, then \sup A is an element of A and the equality

f(\sup A)=\sup f(A)=\sup\{f(\alpha):\alpha\in A\}

follows from the increasing property of f. If \sup A is a limit ordinal, then the equality follows from the continuous property of f.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Separate attained successor suprema from cofinal limit suprema and apply ordinal continuity. The full ordinal/class-function framework is beyond a small undergraduate extension despite the short order argument.
