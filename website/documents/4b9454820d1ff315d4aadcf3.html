Statement

For 1<p<\infty, H denotes the Hilbert transform on L^p(\mathbb R), with sign convention Hf(t)=\pi^{-1}\operatorname{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb R}f(\tau)/(t-\tau)\,d\tau. Let m\in L^2(\mathbb R) be real-valued with Fourier support in [-B,B], where 0\le B<|\omega|/(2\pi), and let \varphi\in\mathbb R, \omega\ne0. If \omega>0 and u(t)=m(t)\cos(\omega t+\varphi), then u(t)+iHu(t)=m(t)e^{i(\omega t+\varphi)}. Fourier support uses \widehat m(\xi)=\int m(t)e^{-2\pi it\xi}\,dt in the L^2 sense.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The function-space and harmonic-analysis prerequisites exceed undergraduate calculus and linear algebra.

Proof 1: Analytic representation of an amplitude-modulated carrier — proof

Write u_a=u+iHu. Applying Bedrosian’s theorem to the narrowband model, the analytic representation is:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}u_{a}(t)&=m(t)\cdot \cos(\omega t+\varphi )+i\cdot m(t)\cdot \sin(\omega t+\varphi ),\quad \omega >0\\&=m(t)\cdot \left[\cos(\omega t+\varphi )+i\cdot \sin(\omega t+\varphi )\right],\quad \omega >0\\&=m(t)\cdot e^{i(\omega t+\varphi )},\quad \omega >0.\,\end{aligned}}}

The last step is Euler’s formula.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Use Bedrosian and the carrier transform, then Euler’s formula. The final identity is elementary but L2 Fourier support and the Hilbert transform are substantial graduate analysis prerequisites.
