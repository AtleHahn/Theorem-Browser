Statement

Let X have mean \mu and finite positive variance \sigma^2. For every real k>0, \Pr(X-\mu\ge k\sigma)\le\frac1{1+k^2}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The assertion uses a single random quantity, its mean and finite positive variance, and the probability of exceeding a multiple of its standard deviation. These are related weighted-integral definitions extending undergraduate integration within about 30 minutes. No independent sequence, limiting distribution or advanced probability theory occurs.

Proof 1: Substitute the standardized threshold

Use Cantelli’s inequality \Pr(X-\mu\ge a)\le\sigma^2/(\sigma^2+a^2) for a>0. Substitute a=k\sigma and cancel \sigma^2>0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The argument substitutes a=k sigma into a supplied inequality for mean and variance, then cancels positive sigma². Its only background beyond algebra is ordinary undergraduate probability notation.
