Statement

If a fan Σ admits a subdivision Σ′ with each cone generated by part of a lattice basis and the same support, the induced toric morphism X(\Sigma′)\to X(\Sigma) is a resolution of singularities.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Toric varieties; Fans and lattice bases; Fan subdivisions; Proper birational morphisms. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Pass from a regular subdivision to its toric variety — outline

Subdivide each cone into cones generated by lattice-basis vectors and take the corresponding toric variety. The regular-cone criterion makes the new toric variety nonsingular; the fan refinement with unchanged support gives a proper birational morphism to the original toric variety.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply regular-cone smoothness and the proper birational morphism criterion for fan subdivisions. Understanding the variety-fan correspondence and resolution requires toric algebraic geometry.
