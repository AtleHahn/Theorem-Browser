Proof 1: Apply Pythagoras on a face and then in a perpendicular plane

In terms of solid geometry, Pythagoras's theorem can be applied to three dimensions as follows. Label successive edges of the cuboid AB,BC,CD, with lengths a,b,c, respectively, so that AC is a face diagonal and AD a body diagonal. The length of face diagonal AC is found from Pythagoras's theorem as:

{\displaystyle {\overline {AC}}^{\,2}={\overline {AB}}^{\,2}+{\overline {BC}}^{\,2},}

where these three sides form a right triangle. Using diagonal AC and the edge CD perpendicular to the face ABC, the length of body diagonal AD then is found by a second application of Pythagoras's theorem as:

{\displaystyle {\overline {AD}}^{\,2}={\overline {AC}}^{\,2}+{\overline {CD}}^{\,2},}

or, doing it all in one step:

{\displaystyle {\overline {AD}}^{\,2}={\overline {AB}}^{\,2}+{\overline {BC}}^{\,2}+{\overline {CD}}^{\,2}.}

Taking the positive square root gives \overline{AD}=\sqrt{a^2+b^2+c^2}.
