Statement

Let q_1,\ldots,q_n\geq0 sum to the positive integer h. Hamilton’s rule starts with a_i=\lfloor q_i\rfloor and awards one additional seat to each of the R=h-\sum_i\lfloor q_i\rfloor agents with largest remainders q_i-\lfloor q_i\rfloor, with arbitrary tie choices. Then \sum_i a_i=h and \lfloor q_i\rfloor\leq a_i\leq\lceil q_i\rceil for every i.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Quotas and allocations are finite real and integer arithmetic with explicitly defined rounding.

Proof 1: Inspect the floor allocation and positive remainders

Hamilton's largest-remainder method starts by computing the vector of quotas rounded down, that is, \lfloor q_1\rfloor,\ldots,\lfloor q_n\rfloor. If the sum of these rounded values is exactly h, then this vector is returned as the unique apportionment. If the sum is smaller than h, the remaining items are allocated among the agents according to their remainders q_i-\lfloor q_i\rfloor: the agent with the largest remainder receives one seat, then the agent with the second-largest remainder receives one seat, and so on, until all items are allocated.

Hamilton's largest-remainder method satisfies both lower quota and upper quota by construction.

The source gives this construction and assertion, without a separate proof of the quota bounds.

Level justification

Start with all floors and assign the integer remainder to the largest positive fractional parts, never more than once per agent. Elementary sums, floor/ceiling and ordering suffice.
