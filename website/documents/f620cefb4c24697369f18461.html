Statement

Let i\geq1 and N\geq1 be integers. Write the unique binomial expansion

N=\binom{n_i}{i}+\binom{n_{i-1}}{i-1}+\cdots+\binom{n_j}{j},\qquad n_i>n_{i-1}>\cdots>n_j\geq j\geq1.

For any family \mathcal A of N distinct i-element subsets of a set U, and any integer 0\leq r\leq i, let

\partial_r\mathcal A=\{B\subseteq U:|B|=i-r\text{ and }B\subseteq A\text{ for some }A\in\mathcal A\}.

Then

|\partial_r\mathcal A|\geq\sum_{t=j}^i\binom{n_t}{t-r},

where a binomial coefficient with negative lower index is interpreted as zero. The bound is attained by the first N i-element subsets of the positive integers in colexicographic order: for distinct finite sets A,B of the same size, A precedes B when the largest element of their symmetric difference belongs to B.

Equivalently, define D_k(N)=\sum_{t=j}^k\binom{n_t}{t-1} using the expansion of N with top lower index k. For an integer d\geq1, a vector (f_0,\ldots,f_{d-1}) of positive integers is the face-count vector of a finite simplicial complex of dimension d-1 if and only if

D_{s+1}(f_s)\leq f_{s-1}\qquad(1\leq s\leq d-1).

Here a finite simplicial complex is a finite family of finite sets closed under taking subsets, f_s counts its sets of size s+1, and dimension d-1 means its largest sets have size d.

High school only
Level justification

All objects are explicitly defined finite sets, counts and binomial sums. Colexicographic comparison and subset closure require elementary reasoning, not topology or algebraic combinatorics prerequisites.
