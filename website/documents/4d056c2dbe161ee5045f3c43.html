Statement

Let a,b be positive integers. If they have opposite parity and p is a positive divisor of a^2+b^2 with p^2<a^2+b^2, set c=(a^2+b^2-p^2)/(2p) and d=(a^2+b^2+p^2)/(2p). If a=2l,b=2m are even and n is a positive divisor of l^2+m^2 with n^2<l^2+m^2, set c=(l^2+m^2-n^2)/n, d=(l^2+m^2+n^2)/n. In each case (a,b,c,d) is a positive integer quadruple with a^2+b^2+c^2=d^2.

Proof 1: Use the two divisor recipes — outline

The source supplies the two divisor recipes. In the first, d-c=p and d+c=(a^2+b^2)/p; the odd divisor and odd quotient make both half-sums integral. In the second, d-c=2n and d+c=2(l^2+m^2)/n. Their products are respectively a^2+b^2 and 4(l^2+m^2)=a^2+b^2. The strict inequalities on the divisors ensure c>0. These routine factor and integrality checks are implicit in the recipes.

High school only
Level justification

Multiply d-c by d+c, use parity for half-sum integrality and the assumed strict bound for positivity. School difference-of-squares algebra and divisibility suffice.
