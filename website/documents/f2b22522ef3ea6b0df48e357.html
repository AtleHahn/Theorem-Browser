Statement

Let a<b and let F be a smooth real function on a neighborhood of [a,b]. Then \int_a^b F^{\prime}(x)\,dx=F(b)-F(a).

Proof 1: Evaluate the oriented interval boundary

Put f=F'. In the parlance of differential forms, f(x)\,dx is the exterior derivative of the 0-form, i.e. function, F: in other words, dF=f\,dx.

A closed interval [a,b] is a simple example of a one-dimensional manifold with boundary. Its boundary is the set consisting of the two points a and b. The boundary inherits a natural orientation from that of the interval. Intuitively, a inherits the opposite orientation as b, as they are at opposite ends of the interval. So, “integrating” F over two boundary points a,b is taking the difference F(b)-F(a).

Thus the general Stokes theorem \int_M d\omega=\int_{\partial M}\omega, applied to this compact oriented interval and the zero-form F, gives

\int_a^b F'(x)\,dx=\int_{[a,b]}dF=\int_{\partial[a,b]}F=F(b)-F(a).
