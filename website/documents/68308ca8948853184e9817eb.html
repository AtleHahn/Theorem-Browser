Statement

A ray in a graph is a one-way infinite simple path. Two rays are equivalent if there is a ray containing infinitely many vertices of each; an end is an equivalence class of rays. An end is thick if it contains infinitely many pairwise vertex-disjoint rays.

Let Q be the partial hexagonal grid with vertex set

\{(x,y)\in\mathbb Z^2:0\leq x\leq y\}.

Its edges join (x,y) to (x,y+1), and join (x,y) to (x+1,y) whenever both endpoints are in the vertex set and x+y is odd. A subdivision replaces edges by finite paths of positive length whose internal vertices are all distinct and are not original vertices.

For every infinite graph G and every thick end \omega of G, there is a subgraph of G isomorphic to a subdivision of Q, all of whose rays belong to \omega. Conversely, an infinite graph containing such a subdivision has a thick end. Thus having a thick end is equivalent to containing a subdivided partial hexagonal grid.

Level justification

Basic set theory and equivalence relations, together with finite graph concepts defined locally, suffice after a short extension to infinite paths and their equivalence. The explicit countable grid and finite edge subdivisions make the complete assertion accessible without infinite-graph theory machinery.
