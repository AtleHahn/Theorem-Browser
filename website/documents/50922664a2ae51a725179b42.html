Statement

Let \Phi be a finite reduced root system with simple roots \alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n, and set a_{ij}=2(\alpha_i,\alpha_j)/(\alpha_j,\alpha_j). Let \widetilde{\mathfrak g} be the complex Lie algebra with generators e_i,f_i,h_i and relations

[h_i,h_j]=0,\quad[e_i,f_j]=\delta_{ij}h_i,\quad[h_i,e_j]=a_{ij}e_j,\quad[h_i,f_j]=-a_{ij}f_j.

Let \mathfrak h be the span of the h_i, identify \alpha_j\in\mathfrak h^* by \alpha_j(h_i)=a_{ij}, and put Q_+=\sum_j\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\alpha_j. Write \widetilde{\mathfrak g}_\alpha=\{x:[h,x]=\alpha(h)x\text{ for all }h\in\mathfrak h\}. Let \widetilde{\mathfrak n}_+ and \widetilde{\mathfrak n}_- be the subalgebras generated by the e_i and f_i, respectively. Let \widetilde{\mathfrak g}=\widetilde{\mathfrak n}_+\oplus\mathfrak h\oplus\widetilde{\mathfrak n}_- be the preliminary Lie algebra with its triangular vector-space decomposition. Let \mathfrak r be the sum of its ideals meeting \mathfrak h trivially, and assume the properties \mathfrak r\cap\mathfrak h=0 and \mathfrak r=(\mathfrak r\cap\widetilde{\mathfrak n}_-)\oplus(\mathfrak r\cap\widetilde{\mathfrak n}_+). Then the quotient \mathfrak g=\widetilde{\mathfrak g}/\mathfrak r contains an isomorphic copy of \mathfrak h and has a vector-space decomposition \mathfrak g=\mathfrak n_+\oplus\mathfrak h\oplus\mathfrak n_-, where \mathfrak n_\pm are the images of \widetilde{\mathfrak n}_\pm.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Root-graded Lie algebras; Homogeneous ideals; Direct sums and quotient Lie algebras. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Take the quotient of the three summands — outline

Take the quotient \widetilde{\mathfrak g}/\mathfrak r. Because \mathfrak r meets \mathfrak h trivially, the image of \mathfrak h is a copy of \mathfrak h. The stated decomposition of \mathfrak r in the negative and positive summands gives the displayed quotient decomposition, with the images of those two subalgebras as \mathfrak n_- and \mathfrak n_+.

Level justification

The final direct-sum quotient step is elementary linear algebra, but the full assertion concerns a root-system presentation of a Lie algebra, its signed subalgebras and a distinguished ideal. Understanding these linked structures is not confidently a small extension of introductory algebra, so retain beyond undergraduate.
