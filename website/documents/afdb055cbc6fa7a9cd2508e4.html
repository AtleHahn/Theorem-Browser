Statement

For a finitely generated group H, let \operatorname{rank}(H) be the least size of a generating set. Start with a nontrivial finitely generated group G and repeatedly replace a factor by its free product decomposition into two nontrivial factors. This process terminates after at most \operatorname{rank}(G) splitting steps.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Group rank is defined as the minimum generator count. The only extra group construction is the free product, concretely reduced words alternating between factors, a short extension of introductory groups. The assertion is termination of repeated factorizations, not structural or algorithmic classification.

Proof 1: Finite termination of nontrivial free splittings — outline

Use Grushko's theorem: \operatorname{rank}(A*B)=\operatorname{rank}(A)+\operatorname{rank}(B) for finitely generated groups. Since the ranks of the nontrivial free factors are smaller than the rank of a free product, Grushko's theorem implies that the process of iterated nontrivial splitting of a finitely generated group G as a free product must terminate in a finite number of steps (more precisely, in at most \operatorname{rank}(G) steps).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the supplied rank-additivity formula to keep the total rank fixed while each split increases the number of positive-rank factors. Free products add the reduced-word group construction to introductory groups; no proof of Grushko theorem is required.
