Statement

Let V be a nonnegative finitely additive set function on all subsets of the positive integers with V(\mathbb N_{>0})=1 and V(F)=0 for every finite subset F. Then V is not countably additive.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The set function is explicitly only finitely additive and translation invariant or zero on finite subsets. Rational cosets and countable unions are undergraduate sets/algebra; no Lebesgue integral construction is required.

Proof 1: Singleton decomposition obstruction — outline

The normalized valuation vanishes on every finite subset of the positive integers; it is finitely additive but not countably additive.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Write the positive integers as a countable disjoint union of singletons; countable additivity would give one equal to the zero series. The explicit set-function definition and elementary series suffice, without general integration theory.
