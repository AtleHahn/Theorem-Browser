Statement

Let A be a commutative Noetherian ring. Call A normal if every localization at a prime ideal is an integrally closed domain. Let (R_1) mean that A_{\mathfrak p} is regular for every prime \mathfrak p of height at most one, and (S_2) mean \operatorname{depth}A_{\mathfrak p}\ge\min(2,\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak p) for every prime. Then A is normal if and only if (R_1) and (S_2) hold.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Depth, regular local rings and the interaction of localization with height require substantial local commutative algebra. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Primary decomposition and the determinant trick — outline

Sufficiency

Suppose A satisfies S2 and R1. Then A in particular satisfies S1 and R0; hence, it is reduced by the reducedness criterion R_0+S_1. If {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {p}}_{i},\,1\leq i\leq r} are the minimal prime ideals of A, then the total ring of fractions K of A is the direct product of the residue fields {\displaystyle \kappa ({\mathfrak {p}}_{i})=Q(A/{\mathfrak {p}}_{i})}, using the total-fraction-ring theorem for reduced Noetherian rings. That means we can write {\displaystyle 1=e_{1}+\dots +e_{r}} where {\displaystyle e_{i}} are the coordinate idempotents in K and such that {\displaystyle e_{i}e_{j}=0,\,i\neq j}. Now, if A is integrally closed in K, then each {\displaystyle e_{i}} is integral over A and so is in A; consequently, A is a direct product of integrally closed domains Aei's and we are done. Thus, it is enough to show that A is integrally closed in K.

For this end, suppose

{\displaystyle (f/g)^{n}+a_{1}(f/g)^{n-1}+\dots +a_{n}=0}

where all f, g, ai's are in A and g is moreover a non-zerodivisor. We want to show:

{\displaystyle f\in gA}.

Now, the condition S2 says that {\displaystyle gA} is unmixed of height one; i.e., each associated primes {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {p}}} of {\displaystyle A/gA} has height one. This is the standard associated-prime characterization of S_2 for reduced Noetherian rings. By the condition R1, the localization {\displaystyle A_{\mathfrak {p}}} is integrally closed and so {\displaystyle \phi (f)\in \phi (g)A_{\mathfrak {p}}}, where {\displaystyle \phi :A\to A_{\mathfrak {p}}} is the localization map, since the integral equation persists after localization. If {\displaystyle gA=\cap _{i}{\mathfrak {q}}_{i}} is an irredundant primary decomposition, then, for any i, the radical of {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {q}}_{i}} is an associated prime {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {p}}} of {\displaystyle A/gA} and so {\displaystyle f\in \phi ^{-1}({\mathfrak {q}}_{i}A_{\mathfrak {p}})={\mathfrak {q}}_{i}}; the equality here is because {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {q}}_{i}} is a {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {p}}}-primary ideal. Hence, the assertion holds.

Necessity

Suppose A is a normal ring. For S2, let {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {p}}} be an associated prime of {\displaystyle A/fA} for a non-zerodivisor f; by the same associated-prime characterization, we need to show it has height one. Replacing A by a localization, we can assume A is an integrally closed local domain with maximal ideal {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {p}}}. By definition, there is an element g in A such that {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {p}}=\{x\in A|xg\equiv 0{\text{ mod }}fA\}} and {\displaystyle g\not \in fA}. Put y = g/f in the total ring of fractions. Then y\mathfrak p\subseteq A. If {\displaystyle y{\mathfrak {p}}\subset {\mathfrak {p}}}, then {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {p}}} is a faithful {\displaystyle A[y]}-module and is a finitely generated A-module; consequently, by the determinant trick, {\displaystyle y} is integral over A and thus in A, a contradiction. Hence, {\displaystyle y{\mathfrak {p}}=A} or {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {p}}=(f/g)A}, which implies {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {p}}} has height one (Krull's principal ideal theorem).

For R1, we argue in the same way: let {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {p}}} be a prime ideal of height one. Localizing at {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {p}}} we assume {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {p}}} is a maximal ideal and the similar argument as above shows that {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {p}}} is in fact principal. Thus, A is a regular local ring. The choice of an associated-prime witness in this one-dimensional case is implicit in the source. At height zero the local normal domain is a field, so R_1 holds there as well.
