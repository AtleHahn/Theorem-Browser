Statement

Let G be a finite simple graph on n\ge3 vertices. If \deg v+\deg w\ge n for every distinct nonadjacent pair v,w, then G has a Hamiltonian cycle, a cycle passing through every vertex exactly once. The described reversal algorithm constructs such a cycle in O(n^2) time with constant-time adjacency tests. A finite simple graph here has a finite vertex set and edges that are unordered pairs of distinct vertices, with no repeated edges; the degree of a vertex is the number of its incident edges.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Graph edges, degrees and Hamiltonian cycles are explicitly finite definitions. The quadratic time bound counts scans and reversals of a finite ordering.

Proof 1: Cycle reversal with increasing edge count — outline

  1. Arrange the vertices arbitrarily into a cycle, ignoring adjacencies in the graph.
  2. While the cycle contains two consecutive vertices vi and vi + 1 that are not adjacent in the graph, perform the following two steps:
    • Search for an index j such that the four vertices vi, vi + 1, vj, and vj + 1 are all distinct and such that the graph contains edges from vi to vj and from vj + 1 to vi + 1
    • Reverse the part of the cycle between vi + 1 and vj (inclusive).

Each step increases the number of consecutive pairs in the cycle that are adjacent in the graph, by one or two pairs (depending on whether vj and vj + 1 are already adjacent), so the outer loop can only happen at most n times before the algorithm terminates, where n is the number of vertices in the given graph. By the following cyclic-order edge-counting argument, the desired index j must exist, or else the nonadjacent vertices vi and vi + 1 would have too small a total degree. Finding i and j, and reversing part of the cycle, can all be accomplished in time O(n). Therefore, the total time for the algorithm is O(n2), matching the number of edges in the input graph.

Degree-pair count in the cyclic order

Cut the cyclic ordering at the missing consecutive pair and label the resulting ordering u_1,\ldots,u_n, so u_1u_n is missing. For each k=2,\ldots,n, pair the possible edges u_1u_k and u_{k-1}u_n. These n-1 pairs account for the two endpoint degrees. If no pair contains both edges, their degree sum is at most n-1, contrary to the hypothesis. A pair containing both edges supplies the reversal index; its four vertices are distinct because u_1u_n is missing. This count uses the ordering only; the other consecutive pairs need not be edges.
