Statement

For a positive integer n and z\in\mathbb C, define \sigma_z(n)=\sum_{d\mid n,\ d>0}d^z, using d^z=\exp(z\log d) with the real logarithm of the positive integer d. Let n=\prod_{i=1}^r p_i^{a_i} be the prime factorization of a positive integer, with distinct primes p_i and positive integer exponents a_i; for n=1 use the empty product. Then \sigma_z(n)=\prod_{i=1}^r\left(\sum_{j=0}^{a_i}p_i^{jz}\right).

Proof 1: Expand the product by divisor exponents

The proof is combinatorial in nature. Distributing (expanding) the product

{\displaystyle \prod _{i=1}^{r}\left(1+p_{i}^{z}+p_{i}^{2z}+\cdots +p_{i}^{a_{i}z}\right)}

gives a sum in which each summand is a product. In the products there is one factor per parentheses, and each is one of the summands in the parentheses. Each such selection gives a summand labelled by a different divisor. Each such selection is equivalent to select, for each {\displaystyle i=1,2,\ldots ,r}, a value {\displaystyle 0\leq x_{i}\leq a_{i}} which will represent selecting the summand {\displaystyle p_{i}^{x_{i}z}} from the {\displaystyle i}-th parentheses. On the other hand, this is the same collection of choices needed to select a divisor of {\displaystyle n}, and write it as its prime factorization. This is because each divisor of {\displaystyle n} can be written uniquely in the form {\displaystyle \prod _{i=1}^{r}p_{i}^{x_{i}}}, for {\displaystyle 0\leq x_{i}\leq a_{i}}.
