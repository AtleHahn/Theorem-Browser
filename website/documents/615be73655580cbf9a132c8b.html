Statement

In the Beltrami–Klein unit-disk model, let h be a geodesic represented by a diameter and q an ultraparallel geodesic (their chords are disjoint and have four distinct ideal endpoints). Let P be the intersection of the Euclidean tangents to the boundary circle at the two endpoints of q. The Euclidean line through P perpendicular to the diameter cuts out the unique common hyperbolic perpendicular to h and q.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The Klein disk represents geodesics by chords; the supplied pole and perpendicular rules give the additional hyperbolic interpretation. This is a limited analytic-geometric extension of undergraduate geometry.

Proof 1: The pole line perpendicular to a diameter — outline

Use the Klein-model perpendicularity rules: for a nondiameter chord, perpendicular geodesics are chords whose extended lines pass through its pole, the intersection of its endpoint tangents; for a diameter, they are the Euclidean-perpendicular chords. Draw through P the line perpendicular to the diameter. The other chord projects orthogonally onto a section lying inside the diameter, and this line through its pole intersects both chords. Its disk segment is therefore the desired perpendicular. The line through P with the required Euclidean direction is unique.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the explicitly supplied Klein-disk perpendicularity rules to construct the Euclidean normal line through the pole and identify its unique chord. Undergraduate analytic geometry suffices with this concrete hyperbolic model as a small extension; no abstract hyperbolic-manifold theory is used.
