Statement

In a real or complex inner-product space, set \|u\|=\sqrt{\langle u,u\rangle}. Then \|u+v\|\le\|u\|+\|v\|. Equality holds exactly when one vector is zero or u=\alpha v for a positive real scalar \alpha.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra supplies norms, inner products and scalar-valued linear maps; analysis supplies suprema and norm closure. Seminorm or cyclic-vector conditions are explicitly given by norm inequalities or the closed span of iterates. These direct definitions suffice without spectral or weak-topology theory.

Proof 1: Expand the squared norm and inspect equality

The triangle inequality follows from the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality as follows. Given vectors u and v, and denoting the inner product as \langle u,v\rangle:

\begin{aligned} \|u+v\|^2 &=\langle u+v,u+v\rangle\\ &=\|u\|^2+\langle u,v\rangle+\langle v,u\rangle+\|v\|^2\\ &\le\|u\|^2+2|\langle u,v\rangle|+\|v\|^2\\ &\le\|u\|^2+2\|u\|\|v\|+\|v\|^2 \quad\text{(by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality)}\\ &=(\|u\|+\|v\|)^2. \end{aligned}

The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality turns into an equality if and only if u and v are linearly dependent. The inequality

\langle u,v\rangle+\langle v,u\rangle\le2|\langle u,v\rangle|

turns into an equality for linearly dependent u and v if and only if one of the vectors u or v is a nonnegative real scalar multiple of the other. Taking the square root of the final result gives the triangle inequality and the stated equality case.

Level justification

Expand the norm squared, bound its real mixed part, apply Cauchy–Schwarz and inspect both equality conditions. Undergraduate complex inner-product linear algebra suffices.
