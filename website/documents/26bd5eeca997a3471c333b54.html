Statement

Let X_1,\ldots,X_n be an integrable real submartingale relative to (\mathcal F_i), let C>0, and set E=\{\max_iX_i>C\}. Then C\Pr(E)\le\mathbb E[X_n1_E].

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement uses a submartingale, an event indicator and expectation on a filtered probability space.

Proof 1: Partition by the first strict crossing

The proof relies on the set-theoretic fact that the event E defined by \max_{1\le i\le n}X_i>C may be decomposed as the disjoint union of the events E_i defined by X_i>C and X_j\le C for all j<i.

Then we can formulate the integral:

C\Pr(E_i)=\int_{E_i}C\,d\Pr \le\int_{E_i}X_i\,d\Pr \le\int_{E_i}\mathbb E[X_n\mid\mathcal F_i]\,d\Pr =\int_{E_i}X_n\,d\Pr.

This makes use of the submartingale property for the last inequality and the fact that E_i\in\mathcal F_i for the last equality. Summing this result as i ranges from 1 to n results in the conclusion:

C\Pr(E)\le\int_E X_n\,d\Pr=\mathbb E[X_n1_E].

Level justification

Partition by the first crossing and integrate the conditional-expectation inequality on each filtration-measurable event. Submartingales and conditional expectation on filtered spaces require advanced probability.
