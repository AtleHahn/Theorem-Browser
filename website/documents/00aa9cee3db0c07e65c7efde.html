Statement

Let R be a ring with identity. Suppose 0\to K\to P\xrightarrow{\phi}M\to0 and 0\to K^\prime\to P^\prime\xrightarrow{\phi^\prime}M\to0 are short exact sequences of R-modules, with P,P^\prime projective. Then K\oplus P^\prime\cong K^\prime\oplus P.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The hypotheses and conclusion require module theory and projectivity.

Proof 1: Split the two projections of the pullback

Define the following submodule of {\displaystyle P\oplus P'}, where {\displaystyle \phi \colon P\to M} and {\displaystyle \phi '\colon P'\to M}:

{\displaystyle X=\{(p,q)\in P\oplus P':\phi (p)=\phi '(q)\}.}

The map {\displaystyle \pi \colon X\to P}, where {\displaystyle \pi } is defined as the projection of the first coordinate of {\displaystyle X} into {\displaystyle P}, is surjective. Since {\displaystyle \phi '} is surjective, for any {\displaystyle p\in P}, one may find a {\displaystyle q\in P'} such that {\displaystyle \phi (p)=\phi '(q)}. This gives {\displaystyle (p,q)\in X} with {\displaystyle \pi (p,q)=p}. Now examine the kernel of the map {\displaystyle \pi }:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\ker \pi &=\{(0,q):(0,q)\in X\}\\&=\{(0,q):\phi '(q)=0\}\\&\cong \ker \phi '\cong K'.\end{aligned}}}

We may conclude that there is a short exact sequence

{\displaystyle 0\rightarrow K'\rightarrow X\rightarrow P\rightarrow 0.}

Since {\displaystyle P} is projective this sequence splits, so {\displaystyle X\cong K'\oplus P}. Similarly, we can write another map {\displaystyle \pi '\colon X\to P'}, and the same argument as above shows that there is another short exact sequence

{\displaystyle 0\rightarrow K\rightarrow X\rightarrow P'\rightarrow 0,}

and so {\displaystyle X\cong P'\oplus K}. Combining the two equivalences for {\displaystyle X} gives the desired result.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The explicit kernel calculation is elementary, but the full argument combines arbitrary modules, short exact sequences, projectivity and splitting. Learning this linked module-theoretic framework from ordinary introductory algebra is not confidently a small extension, so retain beyond undergraduate.
