No first-order theory in the field signature singles out the real numbers up to isomorphism among all models. This is a direct limit on axiomatic characterization of the intended continuum, relevant to the distinction between a formal theory and its intended interpretation.

Statement

The structure (\mathbb R,+,\times,0,1) cannot be the unique model up to isomorphism of a first-order theory in its displayed signature.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

First-order satisfaction and cardinalities of models require model theory and set theory beyond the elementary baselines.

Proof 1: Apply the model-cardinality theorem

Use Löwenheim–Skolem: an infinite structure in signature \sigma has an elementarily equivalent structure of every infinite cardinality \kappa\ge|\sigma|, smaller ones as elementary substructures and larger ones as elementary extensions.

Apply the downward theorem to the real-field structure to obtain a countable elementary substructure. It satisfies every first-order axiom true in the original, but cannot be isomorphic to the uncountable original. Thus no first-order theory in the displayed signature uniquely characterizes the real-field structure.
