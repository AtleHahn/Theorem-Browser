A symmetry preserving the action produces a conserved quantity expressed through momentum and energy. This ties dynamical invariants to transformation principles and supports explanations of conservation in terms of the symmetry of physical laws.

Statement

In a smooth Hamilton–Jacobi setting let a principal function S(q,t) satisfy dS=p\cdot dq-Hdt. Suppose a symmetry (q,t)\mapsto(q+\varepsilon\phi_q,t+\varepsilon\phi_t) maps an extremal trajectory \mathcal C to an extremal \mathcal C^\prime with the same action on every corresponding trajectory segment to first order, and the principal-function differential applies on the local region connecting these trajectories. Then p\cdot\phi_q-H\phi_t is constant along \mathcal C.

Proof 1: Closed contour of the principal-function differential

Since dS=p\cdot dq-H\,dt is an exact differential on the region in question, its integral around a closed path \mathcal P is zero:

\int_{\mathcal P}dS=0.

Using the geometrical approach, the conserved quantity for a symmetry in Noether's sense can be derived. The symmetry is expressed as an infinitesimal transformation:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\mathbf {q'} &=&\mathbf {q} +\epsilon \phi _{\mathbf {q} }(\mathbf {q} ,t)\\t'&=&t+\epsilon \phi _{t}(\mathbf {q} ,t)\end{aligned}}}

Let {\textstyle {\cal {C}}} be an optimal trajectory and {\textstyle {\cal {C}}'} its image under the above transformation {\textstyle (\phi _{\mathbf {q} },\phi _{t})^{T}} (which is also an optimal trajectory). The closed path {\textstyle {\cal {P}}} of integration is chosen as {\textstyle ABB'A'}, where the branches {\textstyle AB} and {\textstyle A'B'} lie on {\textstyle {\cal {C}}} and {\textstyle {\cal {C}}'} . By the assumed equality of the actions on corresponding trajectory segments, to the first order in {\textstyle \epsilon },

{\displaystyle \int _{\cal {C}}dS=\int _{{\cal {C}}'}dS}

therefore,

{\displaystyle \int _{A}^{A'}dS=\int _{B}^{B'}dS}

By definition, on the {\textstyle AA'} branch we have {\textstyle d\mathbf {q} =\epsilon \phi _{\mathbf {q} }(\mathbf {q} ,t)} and {\textstyle dt=\epsilon \phi _{t}(\mathbf {q} ,t)}. Therefore, to the first order in {\textstyle \epsilon }, the quantity

{\displaystyle I=\mathbf {p} \phi _{\mathbf {q} }-H\phi _{t}}

is conserved along the trajectory.
