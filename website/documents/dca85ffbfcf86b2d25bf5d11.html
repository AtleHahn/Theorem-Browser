Statement

A Euclidean Hurwitz algebra is a nonzero finite-dimensional real unital algebra A (not assumed associative), with a positive-definite quadratic form q satisfying q(ab)=q(a)q(b). Write (a,b)=\frac12(q(a+b)-q(a)-q(b)), \|a\|^2=q(a), a^*=-a+2(a,1)1, L(a)b=ab, R(a)b=ba, and \operatorname{Re}a=(a,1)1. If B is a proper unital subalgebra of A and j is a unit vector orthogonal to B, then the subalgebra C generated by B and j is the orthogonal direct sum B\oplus Bj, with j^2=-1 and

(a+bj)^*=a^*-bj,\qquad(a+bj)(c+dj)=(ac-d^*b)+(bc^*+da)j\quad(a,b,c,d\in B).

Moreover B is associative.

The algebra axioms and all involution/multiplication formulas are explicitly specified. Understanding these finite algebraic conditions is a small extension without classification theory.

Proof 1: Orthogonal multiplication laws and norm comparison

Use the stated Hurwitz involution, adjoint and alternativity identities. Since (j,1)=0, one has j^*=-j and j^2=-1. The spaces B and Bj are orthogonal. For a\in B, orthogonality gives ja=a^*j, and therefore Bj=jB. Since Bj is orthogonal to 1, (bj)^*=-bj, giving the involution formula. The multiplication rules follow from these source inner-product calculations, for b,c\in B and arbitrary x\in A:

\begin{aligned}(b(cj),x)&=(b(jx),j(cj))=-(b(jx),c^*)\\&=-(cb,(jx)^*)=-((cb)j,x^*)=((cb)j,x). \end{aligned}

Thus b(cj)=(cb)j. Taking adjoints gives (jc)b=j(bc). Also, since (b,cj)=0,

((bj)(cj),x)=-((cj)x^*,bj)=(bx^*,(cj)j)=-(c^*b,x).

Hence (bj)(cj)=-c^*b. These rules show that B\oplus Bj is closed under multiplication and give the displayed product formula, so it is the subalgebra generated by B and j. Multiplicativity of the norm yields

(\|a\|^2+\|b\|^2)(\|c\|^2+\|d\|^2)=\|ac-d^*b\|^2+\|bc^*+da\|^2,

which reduces to (ac,d^*b)=(bc^*,da). By the adjoint identities this implies d(ac)=(da)c for all a,c,d\in B, so B is associative.
