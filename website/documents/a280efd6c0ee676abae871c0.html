There is a computable partition of \mathbb N=\{0,1,\ldots\} into countably many infinite sets, with each piece having a computable increasing enumeration.

Proof 1: Repeatedly bisect the remaining progression — outline

We computably partition \mathbb N into infinitely many infinite sets. For example, we can define them by repeated bisection:

\{0,2,4,6,\ldots\}\cup\{1,5,9,13,\ldots\}\cup \{3,11,19,27,\ldots\}\cup\cdots.

At each step, take alternate terms of the remaining arithmetic progression. Enumerate the pieces increasingly by r_{i0},r_{i1},\ldots, for i\in\mathbb N. The source leaves the disjointness, exhaustion and effective enumeration checks implicit.

The ith piece has increasing enumeration 2^i(2j+1)-1. Unique extraction of the power of two dividing n+1 gives disjointness and exhaustion, with explicit terminating algorithms. Basic computability terminology is a small extension of arithmetic.
