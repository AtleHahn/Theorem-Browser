Statement

Fix an algebraic closure of a number field K (a finite extension of \mathbb Q). For a finite extension L/K in this closure, let \mathcal O_K,\mathcal O_L be the rings of algebraic integers, and let P(L/K) be the set of nonzero prime ideals \mathfrak p of \mathcal O_K for which some prime \mathfrak P of \mathcal O_L above \mathfrak p has residue degree [\mathcal O_L/\mathfrak P:\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak p]=1.

Let F/\mathbb Q have degree at most 4. For every finite extension L/\mathbb Q, one has P(F/\mathbb Q)\supseteq P(L/\mathbb Q) if and only if L contains a subfield isomorphic to F by an isomorphism fixing \mathbb Q.

Proof 1: Frobenius fixed points in degree at most four (external)

A supporting source can be found here (Section 8, Frobenius correspondence and Theorem 8.31). The deduction below supplies the argument for the stored statement.

Take a finite common Galois closure E of F and L, G=Gal(E/Q), H=Gal(E/L), and S=Hom_Q(F,E), with |S|<=4. Use Chebotarev and the Frobenius orbit/residue-degree correspondence as named standard dependencies. For every h in H, choose a prime unramified in E with Frobenius conjugate to h, outside the finitely many primitive-element index primes if using polynomial factorizations. It has a degree-one prime in L, since h fixes the identity coset in G/H. The assumed prime-set inclusion implies that h fixes some point of S. The following small-permutation argument is supplied here: a group acting on at most four points, every element having a fixed point, has a common fixed point. Otherwise, if transitive on >=2 points, the orbit-counting average of fixed-point numbers is 1; the identity has more than 1 and all others have at least 1, a contradiction. The sole remaining orbit partition without a singleton is 2+2. The two actions on pairs have proper kernels; every element would have to lie in one of those two kernels, impossible because a group is not a union of two proper subgroups (choose elements outside opposite kernels and multiply). Thus H fixes an embedding sigma:F->E and sigma(F) lies in E^H=L. Conversely, if L contains a Q-copy of F, restricting any degree-one prime of L to that subfield gives degree one in F by multiplicativity of residue degrees. The last implication applies also to ramified primes, so it proves the exact prime-set inclusion.
