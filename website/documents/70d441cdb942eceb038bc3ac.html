Statement

Let K be a field of characteristic p>0. Write \mathcal P(K)=\bigcup_{n\ge1}K((T^{1/n})), where K((T^{1/n})) consists of formal series \sum_{k\ge k_0}c_kT^{k/n} with k_0\in\mathbb Z and c_k\in K. The polynomial X^p-X-T^{-1} has no root in \mathcal P(K), so this field is not algebraically closed.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Formal valued fields require algebra beyond calculus and linear algebra.

Proof 1: Successive forced fractional exponents

The example of the Artin–Schreier equation X^p-X=T^{-1} shows this. Write v for the smallest exponent of a nonzero series. Reasoning with valuations shows that X should have valuation -1/p: its valuation must be negative and pv(X)=-1. Its initial coefficient has pth power 1, hence is 1. If we rewrite it as X=T^{-1/p}+X_1, then

X^p=T^{-1}+X_1^p,\qquad\text{so}\qquad X_1^p-X_1=T^{-1/p},

and one shows similarly that X_1 should have valuation -1/p^2. Proceeding in that way one obtains the series

T^{-1/p}+T^{-1/p^2}+T^{-1/p^3}+\cdots.

Since this series makes no sense as a Puiseux series—because the exponents have unbounded denominators—the original equation has no solution.

Level justification

Force successive leading terms using characteristic-p series arithmetic and the least-exponent valuation, then contradict bounded denominators. Positive-characteristic formal Laurent fields and valuation arguments require several linked extensions of introductory algebra; retain beyond undergraduate provisionally.
