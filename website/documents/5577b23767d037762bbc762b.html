Statement

Let P,Q be probability measures on a measurable space (\mathcal X,\mathcal F). Write \mu=P+Q, p=dP/d\mu, q=dQ/d\mu, and v=d_{\rm TV}(P,Q)=\sup_{A\in\mathcal F}|P(A)-Q(A)|. Then v=1-\int\min(p,q)\,d\mu=\int\max(p,q)\,d\mu-1.

Proof 1: A maximizing event and complementary integrals — outline

The total-variation identities are

v=1-\int\min(p,q)\,d\mu=\int\max(p,q)\,d\mu-1.

To see this, take an event A^* maximizing |P(A)-Q(A)| over measurable events A, and without loss of generality assume P(A^*)\ge Q(A^*), so that v=P(A^*)-Q(A^*). Use the Hahn-decomposition sign property: p\ge q almost everywhere on A^* and p\le q almost everywhere on its complement. Then we can rewrite

v=\int_{A^*}\max(p,q)\,d\mu-\int_{A^*}\min(p,q)\,d\mu.

Then, adding and removing

\int_{\mathcal X\setminus A^*}\max(p,q)\,d\mu \quad\text{or}\quad \int_{\mathcal X\setminus A^*}\min(p,q)\,d\mu,

we obtain both identities.

Level justification

Use a Hahn maximizing event to split and recombine integrals of the two arbitrary measure densities. Radon-Nikodym and signed-measure concepts require measure theory.
