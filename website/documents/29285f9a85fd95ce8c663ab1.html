Unrestricted comprehension yields a contradiction

Russell's contradiction shows that unrestricted comprehension cannot consistently turn every condition into a set. It forces a distinction between meaningful predicates and admissible collections, placing a fundamental constraint on attempts to ground mathematics in an unrestricted notion of set.

Statement

An axiom system asserting that R=\{x:x\notin x\} is a set with x\in R\iff x\notin x for all sets x is inconsistent.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The comprehension condition is explicitly an elementary set-membership equivalence; inconsistency means it forces a contradiction.
