Statement

For every integer m\ge0, the following identity of rational functions in two indeterminates z,w over \mathbb Q holds:

\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}(w+m-k)^{m-k-1}(z+k)^k=\frac{(z+w+m)^m}{w}.

Here \binom{m}{k}=m!/(k!(m-k)!), 0!=1, and a zeroth power denotes the constant polynomial 1. Equivalently, the displayed equality holds for all complex numbers z,w with w\ne0, with zeroth powers interpreted as 1.

Level justification

The rational-function formulation and its complex evaluations fit standard undergraduate algebra. Binomial coefficients and finite sums are elementary; the formal-indeterminate formulation requires more than the high-school baseline.
