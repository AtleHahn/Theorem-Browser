Statement

The real number e=\exp(1) is transcendental: no nonzero polynomial with rational coefficients vanishes at e.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement identifies a familiar real constant as irrational or as satisfying no rational-coefficient polynomial. Understanding that assertion needs elementary numbers and polynomial algebra, not the advanced proof of irrationality or transcendence. This rating concerns understanding the statement, independently of the difficulty of its proof.

Proof 2: Evaluate an integer polynomial at e

Use the integer-independence lemma: if \gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_m are distinct complex algebraic numbers and b_1,\ldots,b_m are integers not all zero, then \sum_{j=1}^m b_j e^{\gamma_j}\ne0.

The integer-independence lemma is sufficient to prove that e is transcendental, since this lemma says that if a0, ..., an are integers not all of which are zero, then

{\displaystyle a_{n}e^{n}+\cdots +a_{0}e^{0}\neq 0.}

Clearing denominators covers polynomials with rational coefficients.
