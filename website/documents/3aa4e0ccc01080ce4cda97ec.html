Statement

Contexts Γ, Δ, Π, Λ are finite multisets of first-order formulas; A(x) is a formula, A(t) its capture-avoiding instance at term t, and ⊢ separates antecedent from succedent. In first-order LK, let D derive Γ ⊢ Δ,A(y), where y is the fresh eigenvariable used to infer Γ ⊢ Δ,∀x A(x) by the universal right rule. Let E derive A(t),Π ⊢ Λ, from which the universal left rule infers ∀x A(x),Π ⊢ Λ. Assume substitutions are capture-avoiding and eigenvariables have been renamed fresh for the term and contexts. The cut of these two quantified conclusions can be replaced by a cut on A(t) with the same endsequent Γ,Π ⊢ Δ,Λ.

Proof 1: Rename, substitute and cut on the instance — outline

Eigenvariables are always renamed before rewriting, to avoid accidental capture. A principal cut between the universal right rule \forall_R and the universal left rule \forall_L is reduced by substituting the term used in the left rule into the eigenvariable proof from the right rule, then cutting on the resulting instance. Thus the rewriting step preserves the endsequent while replacing a cut on \forall x\,A(x) by a cut on an instance A(t).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The local replacement is explicit, but understanding its validity requires LK derivation trees, eigenvariable conditions and a capture-avoiding substitution theorem on derivations. This combined proof-theoretic background is not confidently a small undergraduate extension.
