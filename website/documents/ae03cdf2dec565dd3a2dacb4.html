Statement

For n distinct points and m distinct lines in the Euclidean plane, their number of incidences is O(n\sqrt m+m). Here an incidence is a point lying on a line.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Euclidean points and lines; asymptotic growth bounds; Point-line incidences. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Exclude a four-cycle in the incidence graph

Use the Kővári–Sós–Turán bound: a bipartite graph on parts of sizes n,m with no K_{2,2} has O(n\sqrt m+m) edges. The point-line incidence graph has no K_{2,2}, since two distinct points cannot lie on two distinct common lines. Applying that bound gives the claimed incidence estimate.

Level justification

Build the finite incidence graph, exclude K2,2 by uniqueness of a line through two points and apply the explicit quantitative edge bound. Undergraduate discrete mathematics and asymptotic notation suffice.
