Statement

Write \mathbb N_0=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}. For an integer k\geq1, let \mathrm{FIN}_k be the set of functions f:\mathbb N_0\to\{0,1,\ldots,k\} with finite support \operatorname{supp}(f)=\{j:f(j)\ne0\} and maximum value k. A block sequence (f_j) satisfies \max\operatorname{supp}(f_j)<\min\operatorname{supp}(f_{j+1}). Sums of functions are pointwise sums; the block condition makes the supports disjoint.

For every integer r\geq1 and every coloring c:\mathrm{FIN}_k\to\{1,\ldots,r\}, there is an infinite block sequence (f_j)_{j\geq1} in \mathrm{FIN}_k such that all sums

F_1\circ f_{j_1}+\cdots+F_m\circ f_{j_m}

have one common color whenever m\geq1 and j_1<\cdots<j_m are positive integers, each F_i:\mathbb N_0\to\mathbb N_0 is nondecreasing and surjective, and \max_iF_i(k)=k. The last condition means precisely that the sum belongs to \mathrm{FIN}_k. Nondecreasing means F_i(a)\leq F_i(b) if a\leq b; surjective means every element of \mathbb N_0 is a value. Such a map has F_i(0)=0 and F_i(k)\leq k, so composition preserves finite support and never increases a height.

High school only
Level justification

Finite integer-valued functions, monotone height maps and explicitly quantified finite sums are understandable by elementary reasoning; no ultrafilter prerequisites enter.
