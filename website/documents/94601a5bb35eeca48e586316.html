Statement

A minor is obtained from a subgraph by contracting edges, up to isomorphism. Let G,H be finite graphs and \Sigma a surface. Suppose G embeds in \Sigma (vertices are distinct points and edges are arcs meeting only at common endpoints), whereas H does not. Then H is not a minor of G.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A surface is locally Euclidean, a limited extension of undergraduate topology; the embedding and graph-minor operations are explicitly described.

Proof 1: Use minor closure of surface embeddings

Every minor of a graph embedded in a surface embeds in that same surface. Thus if H were a minor of G, it would embed in \Sigma, contrary to the hypothesis.

Level justification

Apply the stated minor closure of surface embeddings and contradict the assumed nonembedding. Basic topology and finite graph operations suffice for the exact consequence; no surface classification is used.
