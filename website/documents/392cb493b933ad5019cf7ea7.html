Statement

Let p be an odd prime, k,l integers with p\nmid k. Then kx\equiv l\pmod p has exactly one residue class of solutions x.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Unique solvability of a linear congruence uses only integer divisibility and the finite residue classes.

Mathematical Field

Proof 1: Permute the finite residue classes

As x runs through all nonzero remainders modulo p without repetitions, so does kx: if, for distinct residues x_1,x_2, we have

kx_1\equiv kx_2\pmod p,

then p\mid k(x_1-x_2), hence p\mid(x_1-x_2), but x_1 and x_2 are not congruent modulo p. The zero class is also mapped to zero. Thus every residue l occurs exactly once.

Level justification

Use prime divisibility to show multiplication by k gives distinct remainders, hence permutes the finite residue set. School integer arithmetic and finite counting suffice.
