Statement

Fix an integer K\ge1, write L_1(x)=\log x and L_{j+1}(x)=\log L_j(x), and take n sufficiently large that all these quantities are positive. For a_n>0, define \rho_n by

\frac{a_n}{a_{n+1}}=1+\frac1n+\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{K-1}\frac1{\prod_{k=1}^{i}L_k(n)}+\frac{\rho_n}{n\prod_{k=1}^{K}L_k(n)}.

The series converges if \liminf\rho_n>1 and diverges if \limsup\rho_n<1. The empty sum is zero.

Proof 1: Iterated-logarithmic auxiliary sequence — outline

For Extended Bertrand's test, let \zeta_n=n\prod_{k=1}^K L_k(n). From the Taylor series expansion for large n we arrive at the approximation

L_k(n+1)=L_k(n)+\frac{1}{n\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}L_j(n)}+O\left(\frac1{n^2}\right),

where the empty product is assumed to be 1. Then the Kummer parameter is

\begin{aligned} \rho_{\mathrm{Kummer},n} &=n\prod_{k=1}^K L_k(n)\frac{a_n}{a_{n+1}} -(n+1)\left[\prod_{k=1}^K\left(L_k(n)+\frac1{n\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}L_j(n)}\right)\right]+o(1)\\ &=n\prod_{k=1}^K L_k(n)\left(\frac{a_n}{a_{n+1}}-1\right) -\sum_{j=1}^K\prod_{k=1}^j L_{K-k+1}(n)-1+o(1). \end{aligned}

Hence

\rho_{\mathrm{Kummer},n}=\rho_n-1+o(1).

The reciprocal series \sum 1/\zeta_n diverges. Kummer's test therefore gives convergence when \liminf\rho_n>1, and divergence when \limsup\rho_n<1: in the first case its parameter is bounded below by a positive constant eventually, and in the second it is negative eventually.
