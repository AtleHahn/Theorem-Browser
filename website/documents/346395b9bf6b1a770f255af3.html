Statement

Let f have a local inverse g near a real point a, and assume f is differentiable at a with f′(a)=0. Then g is not differentiable at b=f(a). This applies to f(x)=(x-a)^3, which is injective.

Proof 1: A zero derivative obstructs a differentiable inverse

A function {\displaystyle f} may be injective near a point {\displaystyle a} while {\displaystyle f'(a)=0}. An example is {\displaystyle f(x)=(x-a)^{3}}. But, for such a function, the inverse cannot be differentiable at {\displaystyle b=f(a)}, since if {\displaystyle f^{-1}} were differentiable at {\displaystyle b}, then, by the chain rule, {\displaystyle 1=(f^{-1}\circ f)'(a)=(f^{-1})'(b)f'(a)}, which implies {\displaystyle f'(a)\neq 0}.

Level justification

Apply the chain rule to the inverse composition and contradict one equal to zero. Elementary differential calculus suffices.
