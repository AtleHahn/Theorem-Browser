Statement

Let \mathbb N_0=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}. The only real polynomials P(x,y) of total degree two whose restrictions are bijections \mathbb N_0^2\to\mathbb N_0 are

P(x,y)=\frac{(x+y)^2+3x+y}{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad P(x,y)=\frac{(x+y)^2+x+3y}{2}.

Being a bijection here means that every nonnegative integer is the value of P at exactly one ordered pair of nonnegative integers. Both displayed polynomials have this property.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The full statement uses elementary polynomials and unique representation of nonnegative integers; bijection is explained directly. The transcendence methods mentioned for proofs are not statement prerequisites.
