Statement

On a measure space (S,\Sigma,\mu), write \|h\|_t=(\int_S|h|^t\,d\mu)^{1/t} for 0<t<\infty, and \|h\|_\infty=\operatorname{ess\,sup}|h|. Norms may be infinite; products with a zero factor are defined to be zero, including 0\cdot\infty. Let 0<p_0,p_1<\infty, 0<\theta<1, and p=(1-\theta)p_0+\theta p_1. Then

\int|f_0|^{p_0(1-\theta)}|f_1|^{p_1\theta}\,d\mu\le\|f_0\|_{p_0}^{p_0(1-\theta)}\|f_1\|_{p_1}^{p_1\theta}.

In particular \|f\|_p^p\le\|f\|_{p_0}^{p_0(1-\theta)}\|f\|_{p_1}^{p_1\theta}. All functions appearing here are measurable real- or complex-valued functions on S.

Proof 1: Lyapunov’s interpolation inequality — outline

Apply Hölder to |f_0|^{p_0(1-\theta)} and |f_1|^{p_1\theta} with conjugate exponents 1/(1-\theta) and 1/\theta. This gives the first bound. Set f_0=f_1=f to obtain the second.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply Holder to powers of two arbitrary measurable functions and specialize them to one function. General Lebesgue integration and Lp norms form the essential framework.
