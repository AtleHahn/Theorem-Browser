Statement

Let I be a nondegenerate real interval containing a, and let g,f,h:I\setminus\{a\}\to\mathbb R. Suppose g(x)\le f(x)\le h(x) and \lim_{x\to a}g(x)=\lim_{x\to a}h(x)=L\in\mathbb R, with limits taken within I. Then \lim_{x\to a}f(x)=L. Endpoints give one-sided limits.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Limits of real functions; Order inequalities. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Epsilon–delta bounds

A direct proof, using the (ε, δ)-definition of limit, would be to prove that for all real ε > 0 there exists a real δ > 0 such that for all x\in I with {\displaystyle 0<|x-a|<\delta ,} we have {\displaystyle |f(x)-L|<\varepsilon .} Symbolically,

{\displaystyle \forall \varepsilon >0,\exists \delta >0:\forall x\in I,(0<|x-a|<\delta \ \Rightarrow |f(x)-L|<\varepsilon ).}

As

{\displaystyle \lim _{x\to a}g(x)=L}

means that

{\displaystyle \forall \varepsilon >0,\exists \ \delta _{1}>0:\forall x\in I\ (0<|x-a|<\delta _{1}\ \Rightarrow \ |g(x)-L|<\varepsilon ).}

and

{\displaystyle \lim _{x\to a}h(x)=L}

means that

{\displaystyle \forall \varepsilon >0,\exists \ \delta _{2}>0:\forall x\in I\ (0<|x-a|<\delta _{2}\ \Rightarrow \ |h(x)-L|<\varepsilon ),}

then we have

{\displaystyle g(x)\leq f(x)\leq h(x)}{\displaystyle g(x)-L\leq f(x)-L\leq h(x)-L}

We can choose {\displaystyle \delta :=\min \left\{\delta _{1},\delta _{2}\right\}}. Then, if {\displaystyle 0<|x-a|<\delta }, combining the two limit estimates, we have

{\displaystyle -\varepsilon <g(x)-L\leq f(x)-L\leq h(x)-L\ <\varepsilon ,}{\displaystyle -\varepsilon <f(x)-L<\varepsilon ,}

which completes the proof. Q.E.D

Level justification

Take the minimum of the two delta bounds and squeeze f(x)-L between minus epsilon and epsilon. Undergraduate real analysis suffices.

Proof 2: Lower and upper limits

According to the hypotheses in the statement we have, taking the limit inferior and superior:

{\displaystyle L=\lim _{x\to a}g(x)\leq \liminf _{x\to a}f(x)\leq \limsup _{x\to a}f(x)\leq \lim _{x\to a}h(x)=L,}

so all the inequalities are indeed equalities, and the thesis immediately follows.

Here the order properties of lower and upper limits and the criterion that equality of their finite values implies convergence are used.
