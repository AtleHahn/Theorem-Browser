Statement

Let \Lambda\subset\mathbb C be a lattice of rank two and X=\mathbb C/\Lambda. The differential dz descends to a holomorphic one-form on X with no zeros or poles. Its divisor, and hence a representative of the canonical divisor, is zero.

Beyond undergraduate level

Proof 1: The standard differential gives the zero canonical divisor on a complex torus — outline

The standard complex coordinate z on \mathbb C yields a one-form \omega=dz on X that is everywhere holomorphic, i.e., has no poles at all, and is nowhere zero. Therefore, K, the divisor of \omega, is zero.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Descend dz through translation charts and read its zero divisor. Complex quotient manifolds, holomorphic differential forms and canonical divisors are substantial geometry.
