Statement

Let P,Q be propositions. In a proof system admitting conjunction elimination, disjunction introduction and disjunctive syllogism, P\land\neg P\vdash Q: from the contradictory premise one can derive any Q. Here disjunctive syllogism means that P\lor Q together with \neg P yields Q. Conjunction elimination takes either conjunct from an “and” statement; disjunction introduction takes P to the “or” statement P\lor Q.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Every inference rule is explicitly described and the conclusion is a finite propositional derivation.

Proof 1: Lewis disjunction argument

The Lewis argument is:

Step Proposition Derivation
1 {\displaystyle P\land \neg P} Premise
2 {\displaystyle P} Conjunction elimination (1)
3 {\displaystyle \neg P} Conjunction elimination (1)
4 {\displaystyle P\lor Q} Disjunction introduction (2)
5 {\displaystyle Q} Disjunctive syllogism (4,3)

For the informal version, take {\displaystyle P} standing for "all lemons are yellow" and {\displaystyle Q} standing for "Unicorns exist". We start out by assuming that (1) all lemons are yellow and that (2) not all lemons are yellow. From the proposition that all lemons are yellow, we infer that (3) either all lemons are yellow or unicorns exist. But then from this and the fact that not all lemons are yellow, we infer that (4) unicorns exist by disjunctive syllogism.
