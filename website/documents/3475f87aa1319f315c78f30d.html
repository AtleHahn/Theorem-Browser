Statement

Let (X_k,\mathcal F_k)_{k=0}^N be a real-valued submartingale, where N\ge1. Let c_1,\ldots,c_N be positive deterministic constants such that |X_k-X_{k-1}|\le c_k almost surely. Then for every \epsilon>0,

\mathbb P(X_N-X_0\le-\epsilon)\le\exp\left(-\frac{\epsilon^2}{2\sum_{k=1}^Nc_k^2}\right).
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The hypotheses require conditional expectation, filtrations and predictable processes in general probability theory.

Proof 1: Sign reversal

Symmetric upper-tail Azuma inequality (dependency)

Let (X_k,\mathcal F_k)_{k=0}^N be a real-valued supermartingale, where N\ge1. Let c_1,\ldots,c_N be positive deterministic constants such that |X_k-X_{k-1}|\le c_k almost surely. Then for every \epsilon>0,

\mathbb P(X_N-X_0\ge\epsilon)\le\exp\left(-\frac{\epsilon^2}{2\sum_{k=1}^Nc_k^2}\right).

Argument

Since a submartingale is a supermartingale with signs reversed, apply the upper-tail inequality to -X, with the same absolute increment bounds. Symmetrically,

\mathbb P(X_N-X_0\le-\epsilon)\le \exp\left(-\frac{\epsilon^2}{2\sum_{k=1}^Nc_k^2}\right).
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Sign reversal is elementary, but checking that -X is a supermartingale requires the conditional-expectation definition on a general filtered probability space. The statement makes no finite-space restriction. That measure-theoretic preparation remains beyond the short undergraduate allowance; deterministic increment bounds simplify the substitution but not the process framework.
