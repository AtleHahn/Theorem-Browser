Statement

For every even integer r\ge0 and every real x, (1+x)^r\ge1+rx, with 0^0=1.

Proof 1: Local integer inequality and the sign outside its range

Local integer inequality

For every integer r\ge0 and real x\ge-2, (1+x)^r\ge1+rx. Use the convention 0^0=1.

Bernoulli's inequality can be proved for the local integer case, in which {\displaystyle r} is a non-negative integer and {\displaystyle x\geq -2}, using mathematical induction in the following form:

  • we prove the inequality for {\displaystyle r\in \{0,1\}},
  • from validity for some r we deduce validity for {\displaystyle r+2}.

For {\displaystyle r=0},

{\displaystyle (1+x)^{0}\geq 1+0x}

is equivalent to {\displaystyle 1\geq 1} which is true.

Similarly, for {\displaystyle r=1} we have

{\displaystyle (1+x)^{r}=1+x\geq 1+rx.}

Now suppose the statement is true for {\displaystyle r=k}:

{\displaystyle (1+x)^{k}\geq 1+kx.}

Then it follows that

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}(1+x)^{k+2}&=(1+x)^{k}(1+x)^{2}\\&\geq (1+kx)\left(1+2x+x^{2}\right)\qquad \qquad \qquad {\text{ by hypothesis and }}(1+x)^{2}\geq 0\\&=1+2x+x^{2}+kx+2kx^{2}+kx^{3}\\&=1+(k+2)x+kx^{2}(x+2)+x^{2}\\&\geq 1+(k+2)x\end{aligned}}}

since {\displaystyle x^{2}\geq 0} as well as {\displaystyle x+2\geq 0}. By the modified induction we conclude the statement is true for every non-negative integer {\displaystyle r}.

Remaining negative bases

For r=0 both sides equal one. For even r\ge2, noting that if x<-2, then 1+rx is negative while (1+x)^r is nonnegative gives the remaining case.

Level justification

The local proof uses two starting exponents and induction in steps of two, multiplying by the nonnegative square (1+x)^2. Its remainder is kx^2(x+2)+x^2, nonnegative for x>=-2. For x<-2 and positive even r, the left side is nonnegative and the right side negative. These are elementary polynomial and sign arguments, with r=0 handled by the stated convention.
