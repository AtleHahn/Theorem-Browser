Statement

For a real or complex random variable X on a probability space and 1<r<s<\infty, \mathbb E|X|^r\le(\mathbb E|X|^s)^{r/s}. Thus a finite absolute s-moment implies a finite absolute r-moment.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Absolute moments are expectations of the displayed nonnegative powers, using complex modulus when needed. Learning probability as a normalized measure and expectation as integration adds a limited application of undergraduate analysis; the conclusion is a scalar integral comparison, not a theory of random processes or abstract Lp spaces.

Proof 1: Monotonicity of absolute moments

Put p=s/r>1 and q=p/(p-1). Hölder applied to |X|^r and the constant 1 gives the displayed inequality, since rp=s and \mathbb E1=1.

Level justification

Apply the supplied expectation form of Holder to |X|^r and one, then substitute p=s/r. Undergraduate random variables and moments suffice for this concrete inequality; no measure construction is used.
