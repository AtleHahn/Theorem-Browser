Statement

Let 0<\alpha\le1 and let F be uniformly bounded real-valued functions on [a,b] such that |f(x)-f(y)|\le M|x-y|^\alpha for one finite M and all f\in F, x,y\in[a,b]. Every sequence from F has a uniformly convergent subsequence. When \alpha=1, the limit is Lipschitz with constant M.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves real continuous functions; Hölder bounds; uniform convergence. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: A common Hölder modulus — outline

Dependency

For uniformly bounded and uniformly equicontinuous real continuous functions on [a,b], the interval Arzelà–Ascoli theorem gives a uniformly convergent subsequence from any sequence.

Common Hölder bound

The same equicontinuity argument applies when all functions satisfy |f(x)-f(y)|\le M|x-y|^\alpha with the same M and \alpha>0. Thus the family is relatively compact for uniform convergence. In the Lipschitz case \alpha=1, the limit has the same Lipschitz constant.

Level justification

The common Holder bound gives a common continuity modulus, so apply the explicit interval compactness theorem; pass the Lipschitz inequality to the limit. Undergraduate real analysis suffices.
