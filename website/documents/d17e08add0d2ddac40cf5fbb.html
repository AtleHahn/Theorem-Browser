Statement

For a Lie algebra V over a field, put ad_x(z)=[x,z]. Then ad_{[x,y]}=ad_x\circ ad_y-ad_y\circ ad_x, so x↦ad_x is a Lie algebra homomorphism into the endomorphisms with commutator bracket.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra supplies bilinear operations and linear endomorphisms. A Lie bracket adds antisymmetry and the Jacobi identity; a derivation or bracket homomorphism is an explicit compatibility identity. These are concrete algebraic conditions on familiar maps, learnable without a course in Lie theory.

Proof 1: Evaluate the rearranged identity on an arbitrary vector

Using the antisymmetry property {\displaystyle [x,y]=-[y,x]}, the Jacobi identity may be rewritten as a modification of the associative property:

{\displaystyle [[x,y],z]=[x,[y,z]]-[y,[x,z]]~.}

Another rearrangement shows that the Jacobi identity is equivalent to the following identity between the operators of the adjoint representation:

{\displaystyle \operatorname {ad} _{[x,y]}=[\operatorname {ad} _{x},\operatorname {ad} _{y}].}

There, the bracket on the left side is the operation of the original algebra, the bracket on the right is the commutator of the composition of operators, and the identity states that the {\displaystyle \mathrm {ad} } map sending each element to its adjoint action is a Lie algebra homomorphism.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Evaluate both operators on an arbitrary z and use the displayed rearranged Jacobi identity. Bilinearity makes x->ad_x linear, while the operator commutator is defined by ordinary composition. A Lie algebra here only adds a bilinear bracket and its identities to a familiar vector space. This is a small concrete extension of linear algebra, not a need for representation theory.
