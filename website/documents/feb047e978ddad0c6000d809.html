Statement

Every finite solvable group G is isomorphic to \operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q) for some finite Galois extension L/\mathbb Q. An extension is finite when L is finite-dimensional as a vector space over \mathbb Q; Galois means normal and separable. Its Galois group consists of field automorphisms of L fixing \mathbb Q, under composition. A group is solvable if its derived series, obtained by repeatedly replacing a group by the subgroup generated by its commutators aba^{-1}b^{-1}, eventually reaches the identity subgroup.

Proof 1: Nilpotent embedding problems and solvable-group induction (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (§5 printed pp.20–32: Theorems14–15 embedding problems; Propositions16–17 and final proof p.32. Preparatory §§2–4 pp.4–19.).

For nontrivial solvable G, Propositions 16–17 give a proper supplement U to its Fitting subgroup F(G). Realize U by induction over \mathbb Q, solve the split embedding problem with kernel F(G) by Theorem 14, and take the quotient F(G)\rtimes U\twoheadrightarrow G. The trivial group is realized by \mathbb Q itself.
