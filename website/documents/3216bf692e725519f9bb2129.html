Statement

Fix 0<p<1, put q=1-p, and define b_n(k)=\binom nkp^kq^{n-k} for integers 0\le k\le n, where \binom nk=n!/(k!(n-k)!) and 0!=1. Fix a real x, and choose an integer k_n nearest to np+x\sqrt{npq}. As n\to\infty, b_n(k_n)\big/\left[(2\pi npq)^{-1/2}\exp(-(k_n-np)^2/(2npq))\right]\to1.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Real sequences and limits; Factorials and binomial coefficients; Exponential function. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Stirling substitution and logarithmic cancellation — outline

Write k=k_n and x_n=(k-np)/\sqrt{npq}. Nearest-integer rounding gives |x_n-x|\le 1/(2\sqrt{npq}), so x_n\to x, k/n\to p, and both k and n-k tend to infinity.

The proof consists of transforming the binomial mass to the normal expression in the statement by three approximations.

First, according to Stirling's formula, the factorial of a large number n can be replaced with the approximation

{\displaystyle n!\simeq n^{n}e^{-n}{\sqrt {2\pi n}}\qquad {\text{as }}n\to \infty .}

Thus

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}{n \choose k}p^{k}q^{n-k}&={\frac {n!}{k!(n-k)!}}p^{k}q^{n-k}\\&\simeq {\frac {n^{n}e^{-n}{\sqrt {2\pi n}}}{k^{k}e^{-k}{\sqrt {2\pi k}}(n-k)^{n-k}e^{-(n-k)}{\sqrt {2\pi (n-k)}}}}p^{k}q^{n-k}\\&={\sqrt {\frac {n}{2\pi k\left(n-k\right)}}}{\frac {n^{n}}{k^{k}\left(n-k\right)^{n-k}}}p^{k}q^{n-k}\\&={\sqrt {\frac {n}{2\pi k\left(n-k\right)}}}\left({\frac {np}{k}}\right)^{k}\left({\frac {nq}{n-k}}\right)^{n-k}\end{aligned}}}

Next, the approximation {\displaystyle {\tfrac {k}{n}}\to p} is used to match the square root above to the desired square root on the right-hand side.

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}{n \choose k}p^{k}q^{n-k}&\simeq {\sqrt {\frac {1}{2\pi n{\frac {k}{n}}\left(1-{\frac {k}{n}}\right)}}}\left({\frac {np}{k}}\right)^{k}\left({\frac {nq}{n-k}}\right)^{n-k}\\&\simeq {\frac {1}{\sqrt {2\pi npq}}}\left({\frac {np}{k}}\right)^{k}\left({\frac {nq}{n-k}}\right)^{n-k}\qquad p+q=1\\\end{aligned}}}

Finally, the expression is rewritten as an exponential, {\displaystyle x_n={\frac {k-np}{\sqrt {npq}}}} (the standardized value for k) is introduced, and the Taylor Series approximation for ln(1+w) is used:

{\displaystyle \ln \left(1+w\right)\simeq w-{\frac {w^{2}}{2}}+{\frac {w^{3}}{3}}-\cdots }

Then

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}{n \choose k}p^{k}q^{n-k}&\simeq {\frac {1}{\sqrt {2\pi npq}}}\exp \left\{\ln \left(\left({\frac {np}{k}}\right)^{k}\right)+\ln \left(\left({\frac {nq}{n-k}}\right)^{n-k}\right)\right\}\\&={\frac {1}{\sqrt {2\pi npq}}}\exp \left\{-k\ln \left({\frac {k}{np}}\right)+(k-n)\ln \left({\frac {n-k}{nq}}\right)\right\}\\&={\frac {1}{\sqrt {2\pi npq}}}\exp \left\{-k\ln \left({\frac {np+x_n{\sqrt {npq}}}{np}}\right)+(k-n)\ln \left({\frac {n-np-x_n{\sqrt {npq}}}{nq}}\right)\right\}\\&={\frac {1}{\sqrt {2\pi npq}}}\exp \left\{-k\ln \left({1+x_n{\sqrt {\frac {q}{np}}}}\right)+(k-n)\ln \left({1-x_n{\sqrt {\frac {p}{nq}}}}\right)\right\}\qquad p+q=1\\&={\frac {1}{\sqrt {2\pi npq}}}\exp \left\{-k\left({x_n{\sqrt {\frac {q}{np}}}}-{\frac {x_n^{2}q}{2np}}+\cdots \right)+(k-n)\left({-x_n{\sqrt {\frac {p}{nq}}}-{\frac {x_n^{2}p}{2nq}}}-\cdots \right)\right\}\\&={\frac {1}{\sqrt {2\pi npq}}}\exp \left\{\left(-np-x_n{\sqrt {npq}}\right)\left({x_n{\sqrt {\frac {q}{np}}}}-{\frac {x_n^{2}q}{2np}}+\cdots \right)+\left(np+x_n{\sqrt {npq}}-n\right)\left(-x_n{\sqrt {\frac {p}{nq}}}-{\frac {x_n^{2}p}{2nq}}-\cdots \right)\right\}\\&={\frac {1}{\sqrt {2\pi npq}}}\exp \left\{\left(-np-x_n{\sqrt {npq}}\right)\left(x_n{\sqrt {\frac {q}{np}}}-{\frac {x_n^{2}q}{2np}}+\cdots \right)-\left(nq-x_n{\sqrt {npq}}\right)\left(-x_n{\sqrt {\frac {p}{nq}}}-{\frac {x_n^{2}p}{2nq}}-\cdots \right)\right\}\\&={\frac {1}{\sqrt {2\pi npq}}}\exp \left\{\left(-x_n{\sqrt {npq}}+{\frac {1}{2}}x_n^{2}q-x_n^{2}q+\cdots \right)+\left(x_n{\sqrt {npq}}+{\frac {1}{2}}x_n^{2}p-x_n^{2}p-\cdots \right)\right\}\\&={\frac {1}{\sqrt {2\pi npq}}}\exp \left\{-{\frac {1}{2}}x_n^{2}q-{\frac {1}{2}}x_n^{2}p-\cdots \right\}\\&={\frac {1}{\sqrt {2\pi npq}}}\exp \left\{-{\frac {1}{2}}x_n^{2}(p+q)-\cdots \right\}\\&\simeq {\frac {1}{\sqrt {2\pi npq}}}\exp \left\{-{\frac {1}{2}}x_n^{2}\right\}\\&={\frac {1}{\sqrt {2\pi npq}}}e^{\frac {-(k-np)^{2}}{2npq}}\\\end{aligned}}}

Each "{\displaystyle \simeq }" in the above argument is a statement that two quantities are asymptotically equivalent as n increases, in the same sense as in the original statement of the theorem—i.e., that the ratio of each pair of quantities approaches 1 as n → ∞.
