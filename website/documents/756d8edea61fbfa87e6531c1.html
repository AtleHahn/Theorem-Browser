Statement

There exist a field K of positive characteristic and a finite-dimensional Lie algebra \mathfrak g over K whose radical \mathfrak r has no Lie-subalgebra complement: there is no subalgebra \mathfrak s\subseteq\mathfrak g for which

\mathfrak g=\mathfrak r\oplus\mathfrak s

as vector spaces over K.

A Lie algebra is a vector space with a bilinear alternating bracket satisfying [x,[y,z]]+[y,[z,x]]+[z,[x,y]]=0. A subalgebra is a linear subspace closed under brackets; an ideal I satisfies [\mathfrak g,I]\subseteq I. The radical is the largest solvable ideal, where solvable means that the series I^{(0)}=I, I^{(j+1)}=[I^{(j)},I^{(j)}] reaches zero after finitely many steps; brackets of subspaces mean the linear span of their brackets. Positive characteristic means that for some prime p, p\cdot1_K=0. Direct sum means every element has a unique expression as a sum of elements from the two subspaces.

Level justification

Proof 1: A nonsplit characteristic-three extension (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Section4, example after Theorem4.2/Corollary4.3, pp.1447–1448.).

Specialize Block’s example to \mathbb F_3 with basis e,f,h,v,w. Besides [e,f]=h, use [h,e]=2e+w, [h,f]=-2f, and the standard two-dimensional module relations [h,v]=v, [h,w]=-w, [e,w]=v, [f,v]=w; all other independent brackets are zero. The abelian ideal V=\langle v,w\rangle is the radical since the quotient is simple \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb F_3). A complement would contain lifts H=h+av+bw and E=e+cv+dw satisfying [H,E]=2E. Comparing the w coefficients gives 1-d=2d, impossible in characteristic three. This replaces the source’s brief Cartan-subalgebra obstruction with a direct calculation.
