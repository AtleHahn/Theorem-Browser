Statement

Assume the intersection-angle assertion: when two lines cut by a transversal meet on one side of it, their two interior angles on that side have sum less than 180^\circ. If the interior angles on one side have sum 180^\circ, the two lines do not intersect, so are parallel (parallel means nonintersecting).

Level
High school only
Level justification

The hypotheses and deductions are explicit elementary angle and incidence statements.

Proof 1: Contradict the intersection-angle assertion

The angle sum on the other side is also 180^\circ, since angles on a straight line are supplementary. An intersection on either side would, by the assumed assertion, require the corresponding sum to be less than 180^\circ. This is impossible.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Both same-side angle sums are 180 degrees, so the assumed intersection criterion excludes an intersection on either side. School geometry suffices.
