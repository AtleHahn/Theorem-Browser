Statement

All graphs here are finite and simple. A clique is a set of pairwise adjacent vertices, an independent set a set of pairwise nonadjacent vertices, and a proper coloring partitions vertices into independent color classes. Write \omega(G) for the largest clique size, \chi(G) for the minimum number of colors and \alpha(G) for the largest independent-set size. A graph is perfect when \chi(H)=\omega(H) for every induced subgraph H, including the empty graph with both quantities zero. A graph G is perfect if and only if its complement \overline G is perfect, where two distinct vertices are adjacent in \overline G exactly when they are not adjacent in G.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Perfection, complement, clique and coloring are all defined for finite graphs; the equivalence needs only these finite notions.

Proof 1: Complement invariance of the forbidden induced subgraphs

By the strong perfect graph theorem, perfection is equivalent to absence of induced odd cycles of length at least five and their complements. Taking complements interchanges these two forbidden classes, so the characterization is unchanged. Thus G is perfect exactly when \overline G is perfect.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Apply the explicit forbidden-cycle characterization and observe that taking complements swaps its two forbidden classes. School finite graph reasoning suffices; the deep characterization is not proved.

Proof 2: Three-subgraph theorem specialization — outline

Use the Cameron–Edmonds–Lovász theorem: if the edges of a complete graph are partitioned into three spanning subgraphs so that every triple induces a connected graph in at least one, and two subgraphs are perfect, then the third is perfect. Take these subgraphs to be G, \overline G and the empty graph. On any triple, at least two of its three edges belong to the same one of G,\overline G, and those two edges connect the triple. The empty graph is perfect. Hence perfection of G implies that of \overline G, and applying this twice gives the equivalence.
