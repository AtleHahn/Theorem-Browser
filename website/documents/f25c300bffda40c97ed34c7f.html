Statement

Consider an axiomatic deductive system whose proofs are finite derivations from axioms using its inference rules. If every axiom is logically valid and every inference rule preserves logical validity, then every theorem derived with no premises is logically valid.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement explains finite derivations and requires only the distinction between valid axioms, validity-preserving rules and their conclusions. This is a small formalization of familiar undergraduate proof practice, with no model-theoretic constructions needed.

Proof 1: Propagate validity along a finite derivation — outline

In an axiomatic system, proof of soundness amounts to verifying the validity of the axioms and that the rules of inference preserve validity.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A proof is a finite list whose lines are axioms or follow from earlier lines. Induction on the line number propagates validity. Undergraduate experience with induction and mathematical logic suffices; learning the precise meanings of valid under every interpretation and validity-preserving rule is a small extension here, since no particular semantics or completeness theorem must be developed.
