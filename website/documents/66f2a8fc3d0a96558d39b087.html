Statement

Let d:M\times M\to[0,\infty) be symmetric, with d(x,y)=0 exactly when x=y. Then d(A,C)\le d(A,B)+d(B,C) for all triples if and only if |d(A,C)-d(B,C)|\le d(A,B) for all triples. In particular every metric satisfies the reverse triangle inequality.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All distance properties are explicitly inequalities for nonnegative real values. No metric topology is needed.

Proof 1: Interchange the points and rearrange

For metric spaces, the proof of the reverse triangle inequality is found by:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}d(A,B)+d(B,C)&\geq d(A,C)\\[4pt]\Rightarrow \quad d(A,B)&\geq d(A,C)-d(B,C)\\[10pt]d(C,A)+d(A,B)&\geq d(C,B)\\[4pt]\Rightarrow \quad d(A,B)&\geq d(B,C)-d(A,C)\end{aligned}}}

Putting these equations together we find:

{\displaystyle d(A,B)\geq |d(A,C)-d(B,C)|}

And in the converse, beginning from the reverse triangle inequality, we can again use two cases:

  • If {\displaystyle d(A,C)-d(B,C)\geq 0}, then

    {\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}&d(A,B)\geq |d(A,C)-d(B,C)|=d(A,C)-d(B,C)\\[4pt]&\Rightarrow \quad d(A,B)+d(B,C)\geq d(A,C)\end{aligned}}}

  • And if {\displaystyle d(A,C)-d(B,C)<0,} then

    {\displaystyle d(A,B)+d(B,C)\geq d(B,C)>d(A,C)}

    again by the nonnegativity of the metric.

Thus, in both cases, we find that

{\displaystyle d(A,B)+d(B,C)\geq d(A,C).}
