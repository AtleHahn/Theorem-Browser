Statement

For a complex Hermitian matrix M=M^*, let \operatorname{In}(M)=(\pi(M),\nu(M),\delta(M)) count its positive, negative and zero eigenvalues with multiplicity; * denotes conjugate transpose. Let H=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\B^*&D\end{pmatrix} be Hermitian, with A possibly singular. Define A^+ by unitary diagonalization: replace each nonzero eigenvalue of A by its reciprocal and each zero eigenvalue by zero. Put S=D-B^*A^+B. Then \pi(H)\ge\pi(A)+\pi(S),\qquad\nu(H)\ge\nu(A)+\nu(S).
