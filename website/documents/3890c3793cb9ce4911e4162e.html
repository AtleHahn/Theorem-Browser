Statement

Let D be a nonzero finite-dimensional associative unital division algebra over \mathbb R, with real scalars central, and put n=\dim_{\mathbb R}D. Write L_a for left multiplication by a\in D and \operatorname{tr}(a)=\operatorname{tr}(L_a). For each a\in D, either a\in\mathbb R1 or its minimal polynomial over \mathbb R is an irreducible quadratic Q_z(X)=X^2-2\operatorname{Re}(z)X+|z|^2 for some z\in\mathbb C\setminus\mathbb R; in the latter case the characteristic polynomial of L_a is a power of Q_z.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A finite-dimensional associative algebra is a vector space with bilinear associative multiplication and identity; division means nonzero elements are invertible. Central real scalars, multiplication matrices, trace and minimal polynomials are direct linear-algebra/ring notions. These finite-dimensional definitions suffice without classification theory.

Proof 1: Real characteristic-polynomial factorization

Identify real multiples of 1 with \mathbb R and an element with its left-multiplication endomorphism. Write

Q(z;x)=x^2-2\operatorname{Re}(z)x+|z|^2=(x-z)(x-\overline z),

so Q(z;x)=Q_z(x) in the statement; it is irreducible over \mathbb R for z\notin\mathbb R.

Pick a in D with characteristic polynomial p(x). By the fundamental theorem of algebra, we can write

{\displaystyle p(x)=(x-t_{1})\cdots (x-t_{r})(x-z_{1})(x-{\overline {z_{1}}})\cdots (x-z_{s})(x-{\overline {z_{s}}}),\qquad t_{i}\in \mathbf {R} ,\quad z_{j}\in \mathbf {C} \setminus \mathbf {R} .}

We can rewrite p(x) in terms of the polynomials Q(z; x):

{\displaystyle p(x)=(x-t_{1})\cdots (x-t_{r})Q(z_{1};x)\cdots Q(z_{s};x).}

Since zj ∈ C ∖ R, the polynomials Q(zj; x) are all irreducible over R. By the Cayley–Hamilton theorem, p(a) = 0 and because D is a division algebra, it follows that either a − ti = 0 for some i or that Q(zj; a) = 0 for some j. The first case implies that a is real. In the second case, it follows that Q(zj; x) is the minimal polynomial of a. Because p(x) has the same complex roots as the minimal polynomial and because it is real it follows that

{\displaystyle p(x)=Q(z_{j};x)^{k}=\left(x^{2}-2\operatorname {Re} (z_{j})x+|z_{j}|^{2}\right)^{k}}

for some k.
