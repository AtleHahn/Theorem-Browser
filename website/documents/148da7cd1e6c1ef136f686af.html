The canonical Weyl relations, together with regularity and irreducibility, force the full real line as the spectrum of both generators. In their position and momentum interpretation, the range of possible observable values is constrained by the representation of the relations rather than freely stipulated.

Statement

Let U(t)=e^{itQ} and V(s)=e^{isP} be jointly irreducible strongly continuous one-parameter unitary groups on a nonzero separable complex Hilbert space, with self-adjoint generators Q,P, satisfying U(t)V(s)=e^{-ist}V(s)U(t) for all real s,t. Then both Q and P have spectrum \mathbb R.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Unbounded self-adjoint generators and their spectra require substantial operator theory. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Use invariance of the spectrum under every real translation

P is unitarily equivalent to

e^{-itQ}Pe^{itQ}=P+tI,

and the spectrum of P must range along the entire real line. The analogous argument holds for Q.

The conjugation identity uses the Weyl relations and uniqueness of self-adjoint generators. The spectral prerequisites are that self-adjoint spectra on a nonzero Hilbert space are nonempty and real, invariant under unitary equivalence, and translated by adding a real scalar multiple of the identity.

Level justification
