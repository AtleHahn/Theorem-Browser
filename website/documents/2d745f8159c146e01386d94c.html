Statement

Let Q_1,\ldots,Q_N be nonempty bounded subsets of \mathbb R^D, where N,D\ge1 are integers, and put Q=\sum_nQ_n. Every x\in\operatorname{conv}Q can be written x=\sum_nq_n with q_n\in\operatorname{conv}Q_n for all n and q_n\in Q_n except for at most D indices. A convex hull consists of finite nonnegative weighted averages with total weight one; a Minkowski sum consists of sums choosing one element from each set.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Finite-dimensional real vector spaces; Convex combinations; Minkowski sums. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. Proof 1: Lift, sparsify and project

Use \operatorname{conv}(\sum_n Q_n)=\sum_n\operatorname{conv}Q_n to write x=\sum_n q_n with q_n\in\operatorname{conv}Q_n.

For each {\displaystyle n}, represent {\displaystyle q_{n}\in \operatorname {conv} Q_{n}} as {\displaystyle q_{n}=\sum _{k=1}^{K}w_{n,k}q_{n,k}}, where q_{n,k}\in Q_n, {\displaystyle K} is a large finite number, {\displaystyle w_{n,k}\geq 0}, and {\displaystyle \sum _{k}w_{n,k}=1}.

Now "lift" the representation {\displaystyle x=\sum _{n}\sum _{k}w_{n,k}q_{n,k}} from {\displaystyle \mathbb {R} ^{D}} to {\displaystyle \mathbb {R} ^{D+N}}. Define

{\displaystyle {\bar {x}}=(x,1,...,1);\quad {\bar {q}}_{n,k}=(q_{n,k},e_{n})}

where {\displaystyle e_{n}} is the vector in {\displaystyle \mathbb {R} ^{N}} that has 1 at coordinate {\displaystyle n}, and 0 at all other coordinates.

With this, we have a lifted representation

{\displaystyle {\bar {x}}=\sum _{n}\sum _{k}w_{n,k}{\bar {q}}_{n,k}.}

That is, {\displaystyle {\bar {x}}} is in the conic hull of {\displaystyle \{{\bar {q}}_{n,k}\}_{n\in 1:N,k\in 1:K}}.

By Carathéodory's theorem for conic hulls, we have an alternative representation

{\displaystyle {\bar {x}}=\sum _{n}\sum _{k}w'_{n,k}{\bar {q}}_{n,k}}

such that {\displaystyle w'_{n,k}\geq 0}, and at most {\displaystyle N+D} of them are nonzero. Since we defined

{\displaystyle {\bar {x}}=(x,1,...,1);\quad {\bar {q}}_{n,k}=(q_{n,k},e_{n})}

this alternative representation is also a representation for {\displaystyle x}.

We argue that for any {\displaystyle n_{0}\in 1:N}, there must be at least one value of {\displaystyle k} for which {\displaystyle w'_{n_{0},k}} is nonzero. Remember that we defined {\displaystyle ({\bar {x}})_{D+n_{0}}}, the {\displaystyle (D+n_{0})} entry of {\displaystyle {\bar {x}}}, to be {\displaystyle 1}. At the same time, from the lifted representation of {\displaystyle {\bar {x}}},

{\displaystyle ({\bar {x}})_{D+n_{0}}=\left(\sum _{n}\sum _{k}w'_{n,k}{\bar {q}}_{n,k}\right)_{D+n_{0}}}{\displaystyle =\sum _{n}\sum _{k}w'_{n,k}({\bar {q}}_{n,k})_{D+n_{0}}.}

We drop all terms on the r.h.s. for which {\displaystyle n\neq n_{0}} since they are zero. The remaining terms take the form {\displaystyle w'_{n_{0},k}({\bar {q}}_{n_{0},k})_{D+n_{0}}=w'_{n_{0},k}}, so we find the equation

{\displaystyle 1=\sum _{k}w'_{n_{0},k}.}

It follows that there is at least one element of the sum on the r.h.s. that is non-zero.

Combining the fact that for each value of {\displaystyle n} there is a non-zero {\displaystyle w_{n,k}'}, together with the fact that there are at most {\displaystyle N+D} of {\displaystyle w_{n,k}'} that are nonzero, we conclude that there can only be at most {\displaystyle D} elements of {\displaystyle n\in 1:N} for which there are at least two of {\displaystyle w_{n,k}'} that are nonzero.

Thus, projecting back to the first D coordinates, we obtain a representation

x=\sum_n\left(\sum_k w'_{n,k}q_{n,k}\right)

where each inner sum is in \operatorname{conv}Q_n, and for at most D of n it is not in Q_n. Indeed, when there is only one nonzero weight for an index n, the equation \sum_k w'_{n,k}=1 makes that weight equal to 1.
