Statement

For an integer n\ge1, the real affine space of n\times n matrices whose row and column sums all equal 1 has dimension (n-1)^2.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Real matrices; Affine spaces; Dimension and linear constraints. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Count the independent sum constraints — outline

Using the matrix entries as Cartesian coordinates, the matrices lie in an (n-1)^2-dimensional affine subspace of n^2-dimensional Euclidean space defined by 2n-1 independent linear constraints specifying that the row and column sums all equal 1. (There are 2n-1 constraints rather than 2n because one of these constraints is dependent, as the sum of the row sums must equal the sum of the column sums.)

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

There are 2n-1 independent row and column constraints: a linear dependence has row coefficients alpha_i and column coefficients beta_j satisfying alpha_i+beta_j=0 for every entry, hence only the common total-sum relation. Rank-nullity gives n^2-(2n-1). Standard linear algebra suffices.
