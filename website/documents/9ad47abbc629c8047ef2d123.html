Statement

Let (Y_n)_{n\ge1} be independent identically distributed real random variables with \mathbb E Y_n=0 and \operatorname{Var}(Y_n)=1, and put S_n=\sum_{j=1}^nY_j. Then S_n/\sqrt n does not converge in probability to any real random variable.

Proof 1: Independent consecutive blocks — outline

Set A_n=S_n/\sqrt n and B_n=(S_{2n}-S_n)/\sqrt n. They are independent and each converges in distribution to N(0,1) by the central limit theorem. Consequently their joint limit consists of two independent standard normal variables, and

\frac{S_{2n}}{\sqrt{2n}}-\frac{S_n}{\sqrt n}=\frac1{\sqrt2}B_n-\left(1-\frac1{\sqrt2}\right)A_n

converges in distribution to a nondegenerate centered normal variable: its variance is 1/2+(1-1/\sqrt2)^2>0. If S_n/\sqrt n converged in probability to a real random variable, this difference would converge in probability, hence in distribution, to zero. Uniqueness of distributional limits gives a contradiction.
