Statement

A Platonic solid is a convex polyhedron in Euclidean three-space whose faces are congruent regular p-gons and at each vertex of which q faces meet. If r is the inradius and A the total surface area, the volume is rA/3.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The solid is explicitly defined by regular congruent faces and equal vertex incidence; only finite counts and elementary geometry enter the statement.

Proof 1: Partition into pyramids from the center

Join the center to each face to partition the solid into pyramids with those faces as bases. Each pyramid has height r and volume one third of its base area times r. Summing over the faces gives rA/3.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Partition into pyramids of common height r and sum their elementary volumes. School spatial geometry suffices.
