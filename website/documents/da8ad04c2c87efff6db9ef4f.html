Statement

For a first-order theory T in a well-orderable language and a sentence s, in a usual sound classical first-order deductive system one has T\models s if and only if T\vdash s. Here the first relation means truth in all models of T, and the second means a finite formal deduction from T.

Proof 1: Combine completeness with soundness

The completeness theorem says that for any first-order theory T with a well-orderable language, and any sentence s in the language of T, if T\models s, then T\vdash s.

Since the converse (soundness) also holds, it follows that T\models s if and only if T\vdash s, and thus that syntactic and semantic consequence are equivalent for first-order logic.
