Statement

On a sphere of radius R>0, consider a nondegenerate triangle whose sides are minor great-circle arcs. Let their lengths a,b,c\in(0,\pi R) have the right interior angle between the sides a,b. Then \cos(c/R)=\cos(a/R)\cos(b/R); equivalently its central-angle side lengths \alpha=a/R, \beta=b/R, \gamma=c/R satisfy \cos\gamma=\cos\alpha\cos\beta.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The sphere, minor great-circle arcs and central-angle lengths are explicit spatial geometric definitions.

Proof 1: Set the included angle to a right angle in the spherical cosine law

The spherical cosine law gives \cos(c/R)=\cos(a/R)\cos(b/R)+\sin(a/R)\sin(b/R)\cos\theta, where \theta is opposite c. Put \theta=\pi/2 to remove the last term.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Set the included angle to pi/2 in the explicitly supplied cosine law. School sphere geometry and trigonometry suffice.
