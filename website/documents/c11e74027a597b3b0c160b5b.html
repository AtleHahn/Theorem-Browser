Statement

In a nondegenerate tetrahedron, join the midpoints of each pair of opposite edges. The resulting three segments have the same midpoint, namely the vertex centroid of the tetrahedron. In particular, they are concurrent there.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Opposite-edge midpoints and the common vertex centroid are elementary spatial geometry.

Proof 1: Group the vertices into the two opposite edges — outline

Use the weighted-centroid identity for two groups of two vertices. Their centroids are the midpoints of the two opposite edges, and the centroid of all four vertices bisects the segment joining them. A tetrahedron has three pairs of opposite edges, so the three such midlines have this common midpoint.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The average of two pairwise midpoint coordinates is the average of all four vertices, independent of the pairing. School coordinate means and midpoint geometry suffice.
