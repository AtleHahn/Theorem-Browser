Statement

Let D be a connected, simply connected smooth manifold of dimension n, and let g,h be smooth symmetric 2-tensors on D, with g positive definite. Suppose g,h satisfy the Euclidean hypersurface Gauss–Codazzi equations. Then there is a smooth immersion F:D\to\mathbb R^{n+1} whose first fundamental form is g and whose second fundamental form, for a choice of unit normal, is h. It is unique up to a rigid motion of \mathbb R^{n+1}.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves smooth manifolds; simple connectedness; symmetric tensors; Riemannian metric; Gauss–Codazzi equations; hypersurface immersions and fundamental forms. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Proof 1: Continue local realizations along paths — outline

Use the local hypersurface Bonnet theorem: compatible smooth tensors g,h with g positive definite have local realizations in Euclidean space, unique up to rigid motion.

Use this local existence theory to construct the immersion along arbitrary curves emanating from a single point. Simple connectedness says that any two such curves with a common endpoint are homotopic through paths fixing the endpoints. Uniqueness from the local formulation implies that the value of the immersion at the endpoint is fixed through the homotopy, so that an immersion results which is well-defined on the entire manifold.
