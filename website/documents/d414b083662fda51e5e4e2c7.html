Statement

Let Q_d be the graph on \{0,1\}^d, with two vertices adjacent exactly when one bit differs. For S\subseteq\{0,1\}^d, let \Gamma(S) be the vertices having a neighbor in S. For d\ge1, 0\le r<d and k=\sum_{i=0}^r\binom di, define \Phi_V(Q_d,k)=\min_{|S|=k}|\Gamma(S)\setminus S|. Then \Phi_V(Q_d,k)=\binom d{r+1}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Bit-string adjacency, the neighborhood and its minimum cardinality are explicitly finite constructions. Binomial layer sizes require elementary counting.

Proof 1: Subtracting the ball cardinality and attaining with the next layer

The closed-neighborhood bound at size k is \sum_{i=0}^{r+1}\binom di. Subtracting k=\sum_{i=0}^r\binom di gives the boundary lower bound \binom d{r+1}. A Hamming ball consisting of weights at most r has its boundary in the layer of weight r+1, attaining this value as in Harper’s theorem.

Level justification

Subtract the ball size from the supplied closed-neighborhood bound and count the next Hamming layer to attain it. Elementary bit strings, subsets and binomial counting suffice.
