Statement

Let I\subseteq\mathbb R be a nondegenerate interval and let f_k:I\to\mathbb R be functions differentiable on its interior. Suppose the series

s(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}f_k(x)

converges to a finite value for every x\in I, and the sequence of derivative partial sums \sum_{k=1}^n f_k'(x) converges uniformly on the interior of I to a function g. Then for almost every interior point x, s is differentiable and

s'(x)=g(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}f_k'(x).

Almost every means outside a set coverable, for every positive \varepsilon, by countably many open intervals of total length less than \varepsilon. Uniform convergence means that for every \varepsilon>0 there exists N such that |\sum_{k=1}^n f_k'(x)-g(x)|<\varepsilon for every n\ge N and every interior point x of I.
