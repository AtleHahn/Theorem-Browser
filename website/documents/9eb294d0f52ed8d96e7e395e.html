Statement

Let X be a totally ordered alphabet and L_K(X) the free Lie algebra over a field K. A Lyndon word is a nonempty word lexicographically smaller than each of its nonempty proper suffixes. For a one-letter word put \gamma(w)=w. For a longer Lyndon word write w=uv, with u,v Lyndon and v the longest proper Lyndon suffix, and put \gamma(w)=[\gamma(u),\gamma(v)]. The resulting elements form a basis of L_K(X).

Proof 1: Specialize the Hall basis construction to Lyndon factorization — outline

The Lyndon words are a special case of the Hall words, and so in particular there is a basis of the free Lie algebra corresponding to Lyndon words. There is a bijection γ from the Lyndon words in an ordered alphabet to a basis of the free Lie algebra on this alphabet defined as follows:

  • If a word w has length 1 then {\displaystyle \gamma (w)=w} (considered as a generator of the free Lie algebra).
  • If w has length at least 2, then write {\displaystyle w=uv} for Lyndon words u, v with v as long as possible (the "standard factorization"). Then {\displaystyle \gamma (w)=[\gamma (u),\gamma (v)]}.

The Hall-set basis theorem and the standard factorization are used here as prerequisites; the source supplies neither their proofs nor a separate independence or spanning argument.
