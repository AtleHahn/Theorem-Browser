Statement

Let E be a Banach space, F a normed space over the same real or complex field, and f:E\to F continuous and linear. If f is nearly open, meaning that \overline{f(U)} contains a neighborhood of zero for every zero neighborhood U, then f is open and surjective. More precisely, if B_F(0,\delta)\subseteq\overline{f(B_E(0,1))} for \delta>0, then B_F(0,\delta)\subseteq f(B_E(0,1)). Here B_E,B_F denote open norm balls.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Normed spaces combine linear algebra and a metric, and Banach means complete. Closed subspaces, unit balls and open maps are basic topology; near openness is explicitly a closure-neighborhood condition. No dual or spectral theory is required.

Proof 1: Summable corrections and completeness

The zero target has preimage zero. Let {\displaystyle y} be in {\displaystyle B(0,\delta )} and {\displaystyle c_{n}>0} some sequence. We have: {\displaystyle {\overline {B(0,\delta )}}\subset {\overline {f(B(0,1))}}}. Thus, for each {\displaystyle \epsilon >0} and nonzero {\displaystyle z} in {\displaystyle F}, we can find an {\displaystyle x} with {\displaystyle \|x\|<\delta ^{-1}\|z\|} and {\displaystyle z} in {\displaystyle B(f(x),\epsilon )}. Thus, taking {\displaystyle z=y}, we find an {\displaystyle x_{1}} such that

{\displaystyle \|y-f(x_{1})\|<c_{1},\,\|x_{1}\|<\delta ^{-1}\|y\|.}

Applying the same argument with {\displaystyle z=y-f(x_{1})}, we then find an {\displaystyle x_{2}} such that

{\displaystyle \|y-f(x_{1})-f(x_{2})\|<c_{2},\,\|x_{2}\|<\delta ^{-1}c_{1}}

where we observed {\displaystyle \|x_{2}\|<\delta ^{-1}\|z\|<\delta ^{-1}c_{1}}. Then so on; if a residual is zero, take all remaining terms to be zero. Thus, if {\displaystyle c:=\sum c_{n}<\infty }, we found a sequence {\displaystyle x_{n}} such that {\displaystyle x=\sum _{1}^{\infty }x_{n}} converges and {\displaystyle f(x)=y}. Also,

{\displaystyle \|x\|\leq \sum _{1}^{\infty }\|x_{n}\|\leq \delta ^{-1}\|y\|+\delta ^{-1}c.}

Since {\displaystyle \delta ^{-1}\|y\|<1}, by making {\displaystyle c} small enough, we can achieve {\displaystyle \|x\|<1}. {\displaystyle \square }

Scaling and translating this ball inclusion shows that f is open; scaling the included neighborhood of zero shows that it is surjective.
