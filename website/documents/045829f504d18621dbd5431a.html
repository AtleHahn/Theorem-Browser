Statement

For Gaussian integers \alpha=a+ib, \beta=c+id with integer coordinates, define N(\alpha)=a^2+b^2=|\alpha|^2. Then N(\alpha\beta)=N(\alpha)N(\beta).

Level
High school only
Level justification

This is an explicit assertion about complex numbers, sums of two squares and integer or rational coordinates. Its formulas can be understood with elementary complex-number algebra and divisibility. This rating concerns understanding the statement, independently of the difficulty of its proof.

Proof 1: Square the multiplicative complex modulus

The norm is the square of the complex absolute value. Multiplicativity of absolute value therefore gives N(\alpha\beta)=|\alpha\beta|^2=|\alpha|^2|\beta|^2=N(\alpha)N(\beta).

Level

Level justification

Square the supplied complex-modulus product identity. The Gaussian norm is explicitly the sum of two coordinate squares.
