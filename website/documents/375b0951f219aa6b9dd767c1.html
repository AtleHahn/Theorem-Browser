Statement

Among all open circular disks in \mathbb R^2, the family \{B((r,0),r):r>0\} is a maximal chain under inclusion.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The disks have explicit centers (r,0) and radii r. A chain under inclusion means each two disks are nested, and maximal means no further disk can be added while preserving that property. These definitions extend elementary plane geometry and set containment; distinguishing maximality from a largest disk requires no college order theory.

Proof 1: Axis-tangent disks form a maximal inclusion-chain — outline

Suppose that A is the collection of all circular regions (interiors of circles) in the plane. A maximal totally ordered sub-collection consists of all circular regions bounded by circles tangent from the right to the y-axis at the origin. These are precisely the disks B((r,0),r) for r>0.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The supplied outline concerns disks and inclusion only. Nestedness follows from the triangle inequality. To check maximality, any comparable disk contains arbitrarily small family disks and lies in the union x>0; its closure therefore contains the origin and it cannot cross the y-axis. If its center is (a,b) and radius s, these force sqrt(a^2+b^2)<=s<=a, hence b=0 and a=s. These are elementary distance inequalities and arbitrarily small positive quantities, not abstract order theory.
