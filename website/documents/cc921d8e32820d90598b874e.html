Statement

Let (X,\mathcal A,\mu) have a measurable set A with 0<\mu(A)<\infty. Let N be a nonmeasurable subset of a measurable null set Z in another measure space (Y,\mathcal B,\nu). In the completed product let f=\mathbf1_{A\times N}. Then \int_X f(x,y)\,d\mu(x) exists for every y but is not a measurable function on the original space Y.

Proof 1: An incomplete factor can produce a nonmeasurable partial integral — outline

If f is the characteristic function of a product of a measurable set and a non-measurable set contained in a measure 0 set, then its single integral is well defined everywhere but non-measurable.

Here A\times N\subseteq A\times Z and (\mu\times\nu)(A\times Z)=0, so f=\mathbf1_{A\times N} is measurable in the completed product. Its single integral is \mu(A)\mathbf1_N. The assumption 0<\mu(A)<\infty ensures that this is everywhere defined and nonmeasurable on the original space Y.

Level justification

Place the indicator in the completion of a null product set, then integrate sections to a nonmeasurable indicator on the original factor. Product measures, completion and nonmeasurable subsets require measure theory.
