Statement

Let n\ge2r\ge2 distinct elements be placed in a cyclic order. A cyclic interval of length r consists of r consecutive elements, allowing wraparound. A pairwise-intersecting family of such intervals contains at most r intervals.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement defines a finite circular arrangement and its length-r intervals, and asks for a bound on a finite family.

Proof 1: Pair intervals separating adjacent positions — outline

If the family is empty the bound is immediate. Otherwise fix one of its intervals (a_1,\ldots,a_r). Every other member separates some adjacent pair a_i,a_{i+1}, 1\le i<r, by containing exactly one of them. For each such pair the two length-r cyclic intervals that separate it are disjoint when n\ge2r, so at most one can belong to the intersecting family. Hence the total is at most 1+(r-1)=r.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Fix an interval and pair the two possible separating intervals for each adjacent pair. Pairwise intersection permits at most one from each disjoint pair. Elementary finite cyclic counting suffices.
