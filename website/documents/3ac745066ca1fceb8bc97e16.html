Statement

Let n,m\ge1, N=nm, and A_i,B_i\in\mathbb C^{m\times n} for 1\le i\le N. If \sum_i A_iXA_i^*=\sum_i B_iXB_i^* for every X\in M_n(\mathbb C), then there is a unitary matrix u\in M_N(\mathbb C) with A_i=\sum_j u_{ij}B_j for every i.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves complex matrices; matrix adjoints; unitary mixing. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Vectorize the operators and compare square matrix factors — outline

Use the stated matrix factor theorem: for square M,N, MM^*=NN^* if and only if M=NU for some unitary U.

View each Kraus operator as its column-stacked vector. The two lists therefore give square column matrices with the same product with their adjoints, namely the block matrix [\sum_i A_iE_{kl}A_i^*]_{k,l}, where E_{kl} are the matrix units in M_n(\mathbb C). The matrix factor theorem yields the claimed scalar unitary mixing.
