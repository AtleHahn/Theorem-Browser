Simpler proof assessment

Likely alternative prerequisite level: Undergraduate level; lowest recorded proof level: Beyond undergraduate level. Standardize the normal vector and apply an orthogonal coordinate change whose first row is (1,...,1)/sqrt(n). The joint density remains a product of n standard-normal densities because squared norm and the absolute Jacobian are preserved. The first coordinate determines the mean, and the squared norm of the other coordinates determines the sample variance. Product-density independence proves the result for every n>=2, mu and sigma>0 using multivariable calculus and elementary probability, not sufficiency or Basu.

Statement

Let n\ge2 and let X_1,\ldots,X_n be independent N(\mu,\sigma^2) real random variables, where \mu\in\mathbb R and \sigma^2>0. Then \bar X=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^nX_i and S^2=(n-1)^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\bar X)^2 are independent.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The probability model is the explicit normal distribution, whose density is a Gaussian calculus function. Independence means a product density, and mean/variance are its weighted integrals. The displayed sample statistics and probability limits require only these closely related elementary probability definitions, without general stochastic-process theory.

Proof 1: Basu theorem with fixed variance

Fix \sigma^2 and regard \mu as the parameter. Basu's theorem states that a boundedly complete sufficient statistic is independent of any ancillary statistic in that parameter family.

With respect to the parameter \mu, one can show that

\widehat\mu=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^nX_i}{n},

the sample mean, is a complete and sufficient statistic—it is all the information one can derive to estimate \mu, and no more—and

\widehat\sigma^2=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\bar X)^2}{n-1},

the sample variance, is an ancillary statistic: its distribution does not depend on \mu.

Therefore, from Basu's theorem it follows that these statistics are independent for each fixed \mu and \sigma^2.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply Basu using completeness, sufficiency and ancillarity in the fixed-variance normal family. These statistical model concepts and their interaction are not confidently a small extension of ordinary undergraduate probability; retain beyond undergraduate for this proof rather than substitute a different elementary normal calculation.
