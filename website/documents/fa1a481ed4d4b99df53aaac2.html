Statement

Let V be a vector space over a field with an antisymmetric bilinear bracket [x,y]=−[y,x], and put ad_x(y)=[x,y]. The Jacobi identity [x,[y,z]]+[y,[z,x]]+[z,[x,y]]=0 holds for all x,y,z if and only if each ad_x is a derivation: ad_x([y,z])=[ad_x(y),z]+[y,ad_x(z)].

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra supplies bilinear operations and linear endomorphisms. A Lie bracket adds antisymmetry and the Jacobi identity; a derivation or bracket homomorphism is an explicit compatibility identity. These are concrete algebraic conditions on familiar maps, learnable without a course in Lie theory.

Proof 1: Rearrange Jacobi using antisymmetry

Because the bracket multiplication is antisymmetric, the Jacobi identity admits the following equivalent reformulation. Defining the adjoint operator {\displaystyle \operatorname {ad} _{x}:y\mapsto [x,y]}, the identity becomes:

{\displaystyle \operatorname {ad} _{x}[y,z]=[\operatorname {ad} _{x}y,z]+[y,\operatorname {ad} _{x}z].}

Thus, the Jacobi identity for Lie algebras states that the action of any element on the algebra is a derivation.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The bracket and derivation rule are explicitly defined. Replace [z,x] by -[x,z] and [[x,y],z] by -[z,[x,y]] to rearrange Jacobi, then substitute ad_x(y)=[x,y]. Bilinear operations, vector spaces and linear maps are undergraduate linear algebra; no structural Lie theory is needed beyond the local definitions.
