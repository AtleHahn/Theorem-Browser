Statement

Let X be a compact convex subset of a normed linear space. Let f:X\times[0,1]\to\mathbb R and g:X\times[0,1]\to\mathbb R^K be continuous in x, concave in x (componentwise for g), with partial derivatives f_t,g_t continuous in (x,t). Suppose there is \hat x\in X with g(\hat x,t)>0 componentwise for every t. Set L(x,\lambda,t)=f(x,t)+\lambda\cdot g(x,t) and let (x^*(t),\lambda^*(t)) be a saddle point, maximizing in x and minimizing in \lambda\ge0. Let V(t)=\max\{f(x,t):x\in X,\ g(x,t)\ge0\}. In the special case K=1, f(x,t)=f(x) and g(x,t)=h(x)+t, one has V^\prime(t)=\lambda^*(t) for almost every t.

Proof 1: Differentiate the additive constraint

The constrained integral envelope identity is

V(t)=V(0)+\int_0^t L_t(x^*(s),\lambda^*(s),s)\,ds.

For the special case in which {\displaystyle f\left(x,t\right)} is independent of {\displaystyle t}, {\displaystyle K=1}, and {\displaystyle g\left(x,t\right)=h\left(x\right)+t}, this formula implies that {\displaystyle V^{\prime }(t)=L_{t}(x^{\ast }(t),\lambda ^{\ast }\left(t\right),t)=\lambda ^{\ast }\left(t\right)} for a.e. {\displaystyle t}. That is, the Lagrange multiplier {\displaystyle \lambda ^{\ast }\left(t\right)} on the constraint is its "shadow price" in the optimization program.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Differentiate the constrained integral envelope identity almost everywhere. Parameterized saddle duality and the Lebesgue fundamental theorem are essential to this claim beyond the elementary derivative of the additive constraint.
