Statement

Let M be a closed smooth n-manifold and f:M→R a Morse function. If C^k is the number of its critical points of index k, then χ(M)=∑_{k=0}^n(−1)^k C^k.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement relates Morse critical-point indices on a closed smooth manifold to its homological invariants.

Proof 1: Count cells using the Morse cell theorem

By the Morse cell theorem, M has the homotopy type of a finite CW complex with one \gamma-cell for each critical point of index \gamma of f:M\to\mathbb R. Using the fact that the alternating sum of the ranks of the homology groups of a topological space is equal to the alternating sum of the ranks of the chain groups from which the homology is computed, then by using the cellular chain groups it is clear that the Euler characteristic \chi(M) is equal to the sum

\sum_{\gamma=0}^n(-1)^\gamma C^\gamma=\chi(M),

where C^\gamma is the number of critical points of index \gamma.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the Morse CW model and the Euler–Poincare chain-count identity. Smooth Morse theory, CW homotopy type and cellular homology require advanced topology.
