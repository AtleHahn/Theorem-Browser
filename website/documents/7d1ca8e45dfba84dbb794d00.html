Statement

With e=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}1/i!, one has \lim_{k\to\infty}(1+1/k)^k=e, and the sequence on the left is nondecreasing.

Proof 1: Expand binomial coefficients into increasing products

Use monotone convergence of nonnegative sums: if a_{i,k}\ge0 and a_{i,k}\le a_{i,k+1}, then \sup_k\sum_i a_{i,k}=\sum_i\sup_k a_{i,k}.

Consider the expansion

{\displaystyle \left(1+{\frac {1}{k}}\right)^{k}=\sum _{i=0}^{k}{\binom {k}{i}}{\frac {1}{k^{i}}}}

For k\ge1, now set

{\displaystyle a_{i,k}={\binom {k}{i}}{\frac {1}{k^{i}}}={\frac {1}{i!}}\cdot {\frac {k}{k}}\cdot {\frac {k-1}{k}}\cdot \cdots {\frac {k-i+1}{k}}}

for 0\le i\le k, with the product interpreted as 1 when i=0, and {\displaystyle a_{i,k}=0} for {\displaystyle i>k}, then {\displaystyle 0\leq a_{i,k}\leq a_{i,k+1}} with {\displaystyle \sup _{k}a_{i,k}={\frac {1}{i!}}<\infty } and

{\displaystyle \left(1+{\frac {1}{k}}\right)^{k}=\sum _{i=0}^{\infty }a_{i,k}}.

The right hand side is a non-decreasing sequence in {\displaystyle k}, therefore

{\displaystyle \lim _{k\to \infty }\left(1+{\frac {1}{k}}\right)^{k}=\sup _{k}\sum _{i=0}^{\infty }a_{i,k}=\sum _{i=0}^{\infty }\sup _{k}a_{i,k}=\sum _{i=0}^{\infty }{\frac {1}{i!}}=e}.
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The finite binomial expansion gives nonnegative summands increasing with k and converging to 1/i!. The stated monotone-convergence rule for sums then interchanges the supremum and sum. This is introductory real analysis of sequences and series; no general measure-theoretic convergence theorem is required.
