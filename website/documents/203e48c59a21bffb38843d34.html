Statement

In ZF, let X be a set of nonempty sets and let < be a well-order of \bigcup X, meaning a total order in which every nonempty subset has a least element. Then X has a choice function: select the <-least element of each A\in X.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Undergraduate sets, functions, orders and infinite sequences suffice. Choice, well-ordering or maximal-chain principles are explicit set-selection/order assertions where invoked, not a demand to learn their independence proofs. The displayed quantifiers and constructions can be understood through familiar subsets and functions.

Proof 1: Least elements in the supplied well-order — outline

In general, if the union of all sets in ⁠{\displaystyle X}⁠ can be well-ordered, then a choice function for ⁠{\displaystyle X}⁠ can be constructed without using the axiom of choice. Note that it does not suffice that each ⁠{\displaystyle A\in X}⁠ can be well-ordered, since the axiom of choice may be needed to choose a canonical well-ordering for each ⁠{\displaystyle A}⁠ anyway.

Use the least-element strategy with respect to the supplied well-order.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A single given well-order on the union assigns a unique least element to every member set. This is introductory set/function reasoning with the well-order definition supplied; no ordinal recursion or choice of many orders is involved.
