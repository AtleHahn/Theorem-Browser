Statement

Let X be a finite nonempty set with a reflexive, antisymmetric and transitive relation \le, and let f:X\to X satisfy x\le f(x) for every x\in X. Then f(x)=x for some x\in X.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The finite set, order axioms and inequality x<=f(x) are stated explicitly. A fixed point is simply equality of input and output, accessible by elementary finite iteration.

Proof 1: Successive iterates stabilize

If the poset X is finite and nonempty, iterate an inflationary map f:X\to X. The sequence of successive iterates,

{\displaystyle x_{n+1}=f(x_{n}),n=0,1,2,\ldots ,}

where x0 is any element of X, is monotone increasing. By the finiteness of X, it stabilizes:

{\displaystyle x_{n}=x_{\infty },} for n sufficiently large.

It follows that x∞ is a fixed point of f.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Successive iterates can only increase; finiteness and antisymmetry force equality of consecutive iterates. The order axioms are supplied and only elementary finite reasoning is needed.
