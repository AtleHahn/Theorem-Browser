Statement

Let S be a semigroup. A congruence is an equivalence relation \rho on S such that x\rho y and u\rho v imply (xu)\rho(yv). Assuming Zorn’s lemma, the following are equivalent: every nonempty family of congruences on S has an inclusion-maximal member; there is no sequence \rho_1\subsetneq\rho_2\subsetneq\cdots of congruences.

Semigroups and monoids are direct variants of group axioms, and their definitions are supplied. Congruences, fibers and quotients use familiar equivalence relations and multiplication compatibility, so no specialist semigroup theory is required.

Proof 1: Specialize the ascending-chain criterion under Zorn’s lemma

Use the general ascending-chain/maximal-element criterion under Zorn’s lemma: for a partially ordered set P, every nonempty subset of P has a maximal element if and only if P has no infinite strictly increasing sequence. Apply it to the partially ordered set of semigroup congruences on S, ordered by inclusion. Its two conditions are exactly the stated maximal condition and ascending chain condition.
