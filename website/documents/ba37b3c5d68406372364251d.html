Statement

Let G be a finite simple perfect graph, meaning every induced subgraph has chromatic number equal to its largest clique size. Then the maximum size of an independent set of G equals the minimum number of cliques covering its vertices.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The claim compares finite subset sizes and clique covers, with perfection defined by finite induced subgraphs.

Proof 1: Complement graph correspondence

The complement of a perfect graph is perfect by the perfect graph theorem. A clique of the complement is an independent set of G, and a color class in the complement is a clique of G. Thus equality of clique number and chromatic number in the complement says exactly that the maximum independent-set size of G equals its minimum vertex clique-cover size.
