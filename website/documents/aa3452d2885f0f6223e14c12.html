Statement

Let P be a convex polyhedron. The angular defect at a vertex is 2\pi minus the sum of its incident face angles. Suppose no subset of the vertices has total defect equal to half the total defect of all vertices. Then the surface of P has no simple closed geodesic avoiding its vertices. Here a geodesic is locally a straight segment in each face and remains straight when neighboring faces are unfolded across an edge; simple means without self-intersections.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Closed geodesics on polyhedral surfaces and their relation to angular defect require undergraduate surface geometry despite the elementary unfolding description. A bright US college student should be able to understand the statement after about 30 minutes of focused background research; this rating concerns understanding the claim, not proving it.

Proof 1: Contradiction with defect bisection (outline)

A simple closed geodesic of a convex polyhedron would necessarily bisect the total angular defect of the vertices. This bisection property is used without proof in the source. A vertex-avoiding simple closed geodesic would therefore give a subset of the vertices with half the total defect, contrary to the hypothesis.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the supplied defect-bisection theorem and separation to obtain a forbidden subset of vertices. Undergraduate topology suffices with the explicitly defined unfolded straight geodesic; no differential-geometric geodesic equation is used.
