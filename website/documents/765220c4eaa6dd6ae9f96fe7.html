Statement

For every integer n>1 there is a prime p with n<p<2n.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement involves only primes and integer inequalities.

Proof 1: Apply Sylvester–Schur to n+1 through 2n

Use the Sylvester–Schur theorem: for every positive integer k, the product of k consecutive integers each greater than k has a prime divisor greater than k.

The stated conclusion follows from this theorem, by taking {\displaystyle k=n}, and considering the {\displaystyle k} numbers {\displaystyle n+1,n+2,\dots ,n+k=2n}, where {\displaystyle n>1}. According to Sylvester's generalization, one of these numbers has a prime factor greater than {\displaystyle k}. Since all these numbers are less than {\displaystyle 2(k+1)}, the number with a prime factor greater than {\displaystyle k} has only one prime factor, and thus is a prime. Since {\displaystyle 2n} is not prime, we know there exists a prime {\displaystyle p} with {\displaystyle n<p<2n}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Apply the supplied consecutive-integer theorem to n+1,...,2n. A prime divisor p>n cannot have another positive integer factor of at least two in any number at most 2n, so that number equals p. The endpoint 2n is composite for n>1. This uses elementary divisibility and inequalities; the proof of Sylvester–Schur is not invoked.
