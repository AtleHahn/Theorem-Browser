Statement

For a positive integer q, an integer a and a real x, let \pi(x;q,a) count primes p\le x with p\equiv a\pmod q, and let \varphi(q) count residue classes modulo q coprime to q. The logarithm is natural.

For every real x>q, \pi(x;q,a)\le2x/(\varphi(q)\log(x/q)).

Level
High school only
Level justification

All quantities involve elementary arithmetic and logarithms, with the counting function and totient defined locally.

Proof 1: Sharp large-sieve bound (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorem 2, equation (1.10), p.121; proof sections 3–6, pp.124–131.).

Use Theorem 2, equation (1.10), proved in sections 3–6. Let the lower endpoint tend to zero through positive values while holding the upper endpoint x fixed; the prime count is eventually pi(x;q,a) and the bound tends to 2x/(phi(q) log(x/q)). Reduce a to a positive coprime residue when gcd(a,q)=1. Otherwise any counted prime must equal gcd(a,q), so there is at most one. Writing t=x/q>1, the right side is at least 2t/log t>=2e>1, so that case also follows.
