Statement

Let n>1 be a nonsquare integer and x_1,y_1>0 integers with x_1^2-ny_1^2=-1. For odd k\geq1, let x_k+y_k\sqrt n=(x_1+y_1\sqrt n)^k. Then x_k^2-ny_k^2=-1 and, for odd k\geq3, x_k=(x_1^2+ny_1^2)x_{k-2}+2ny_1x_1y_{k-2}, y_k=(x_1^2+ny_1^2)y_{k-2}+2y_1x_1x_{k-2}. These give infinitely many solutions.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The integer constructions use finite algebra and explicitly stated recurrences.

Proof 1: Odd powers and two-step recurrence

Since

(x_1+y_1\sqrt n)(x_1-y_1\sqrt n)=-1,

odd powers x_k+y_k\sqrt n=(x_1+y_1\sqrt n)^k again have norm -1. Taking the positive sign for the solutions, the resulting recursion relation is

x_k=x_{k-2}x_1^2+nx_{k-2}y_1^2+2ny_{k-2}y_1x_1,y_k=y_{k-2}x_1^2+ny_{k-2}y_1^2+2x_{k-2}y_1x_1,

which gives an infinite tower of solutions to the negative Pell equation for n>1.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Multiply conjugate quadratic expressions, take odd powers, and expand the square to obtain the two-step recurrence. Elementary algebra and positivity establish infinitely many growing solutions; norm is only the displayed conjugate product.
