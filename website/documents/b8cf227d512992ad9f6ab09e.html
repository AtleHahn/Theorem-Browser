Statement

Let X be a set with an equivalence relation \sim. Form a category with objects the elements of X and exactly one arrow x\to y when x\sim y, and no such arrow otherwise. Composition and identities are the unique arrows with the required source and target. This defines a groupoid: every arrow is invertible.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The objects and arrows are explicitly constructed. Learning identities, associative composition and inverses in this unique-arrow case is a short extension of undergraduate equivalence relations and group operations, with no functors or higher category structures.

Proof 1: The thin groupoid of an equivalence relation — outline

Take the elements of X as objects. For x,y\in X, put a unique morphism from x to y if and only if x\sim y. This is the groupoid representing the equivalence relation.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The category has at most one arrow between any two objects, so composition laws follow from uniqueness and equivalence-relation transitivity; symmetry gives inverses. Category and groupoid definitions are a small extension of undergraduate sets and groups in this concrete thin case.
