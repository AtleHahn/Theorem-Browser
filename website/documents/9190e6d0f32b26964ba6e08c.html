Statement

Let X be a continuous real semimartingale, with quadratic variation [X]_t normalized by [X]_0=0. If a strictly positive continuous semimartingale Y satisfies dY_t=Y_t\,dX_t and Y_0=1, then Y_t=\exp\left(X_t-X_0-\tfrac12[X]_t\right).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves continuous semimartingales; quadratic variation; stochastic differential equations. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Logarithm and quadratic-variation correction

Applying Itô's lemma with f(Y)=\log(Y) gives

\begin{aligned} d\log(Y)&=\frac1Y\,dY-\frac1{2Y^2}\,d[Y]\\[6pt] &=dX-\tfrac12\,d[X]. \end{aligned}

The second line uses dY=Y\,dX and the stochastic-integral quadratic-variation rule d[Y]=Y^2\,d[X]. Integrating from 0 to t, with Y_0=1 and [X]_0=0, and exponentiating gives the solution

Y_t=\exp\left(X_t-X_0-\tfrac12[X]_t\right).
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply Ito to log Y, substitute the stochastic-integral quadratic variation, then integrate. Semimartingales, stochastic differentials and quadratic variation require graduate stochastic calculus.
