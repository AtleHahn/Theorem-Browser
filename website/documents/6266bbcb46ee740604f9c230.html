Statement

For every fixed nonnegative integer k and every fixed monadic second-order sentence \varphi in the incidence language of graphs (MSO2), there is an algorithm which, on input a finite graph G=(V,E) of treewidth at most k, decides whether G\models\varphi in time

O_{k,\varphi}(|V|+|E|+1).

The constants may depend on k and \varphi, but not on G. A tree decomposition need not be supplied with the input. The same assertion permits vertex and edge labels from any fixed finite alphabet, expressed by corresponding unary predicates in \varphi.

In MSO2, individual variables range over vertices or edges and set variables range over subsets of vertices or subsets of edges. Formulas are built from equality, membership and the vertex–edge incidence relation, using Boolean connectives and quantifiers over these individuals and sets. A sentence has no free variables; G\models\varphi means that the sentence is true in G. In particular the theorem applies to MSO1, which uses vertices, adjacency and vertex sets only.

A tree decomposition of G is a finite tree T with a bag B_t\subseteq V at each node t, such that every vertex belongs to a bag, every edge has both endpoints in some bag, and the nodes whose bags contain any fixed vertex form a connected subtree of T. Its width is \max_t|B_t|-1; treewidth is the minimum of these widths. Thus treewidth at most k means a decomposition exists with every bag of size at most k+1. The time bound uses the usual explicit vertex-and-edge input representation and counts elementary algorithmic operations.
