Statement

If four circles in a Descartes configuration have integer signed curvatures, replacing one of them by the alternative circle tangent to the other three also gives an integer signed curvature. A straight line, if one occurs in the configuration or replacement process, is assigned curvature zero.

Level justification

Proof 1: The two quadratic roots

For four mutually tangent generalized circles with distinct tangencies, signed curvatures satisfy (k_1+k_2+k_3+k_4)^2=2(k_1^2+k_2^2+k_3^2+k_4^2); a line has curvature zero, an enclosing circle has curvature -1/r, and the other circles have curvature 1/r.

k_4=k_1+k_2+k_3\pm2\sqrt{k_1k_2+k_2k_3+k_3k_1}.

When four tangent circles described by the fourth-curvature formula above all have integer curvatures, the alternative fourth circle described by the second solution to that formula must also have an integer curvature. This is because both solutions differ from an integer by the square root of an integer, and so either solution can only be an integer if this square root, and hence the other solution, is also an integer.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The two fourth-curvature roots sum to twice the sum of the other three integer curvatures. Thus the alternative root is an integer whenever the first is. Only the supplied quadratic identity and integer arithmetic are needed.
