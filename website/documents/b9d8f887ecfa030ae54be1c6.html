Statement

The two-sphere S^2 and the two-dimensional torus T^2 are not homeomorphic.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Basic topology supplies homeomorphisms, products and quotient spaces. The torus is S1 times S1, and the square identification is explicit. These standard concrete spaces need no homology or smooth-manifold theory.

Proof 1: Distinguish the fundamental groups

The surface of a ball (the two-dimensional sphere) has trivial fundamental group, meaning that any loop drawn on the surface can be continuously deformed to a single point. By contrast, the surface of a torus has nontrivial fundamental group, as there are loops on the surface which cannot be so deformed. Because the (non)triviality of the fundamental group is known to be invariant under homeomorphism, it follows that the two-dimensional sphere and torus are not homeomorphic.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the explicitly explained distinction that every sphere loop contracts while some torus loop does not. Undergraduate continuous maps and homotopies suffice for this exact consequence; no fundamental-group operation or calculation is required.
