Statement

Let n\geq1 be an integer and 1\leq p<\infty. Let B\subseteq L^p(\mathbb R^n), where functions are Lebesgue measurable and real- or complex-valued, identified when equal almost everywhere, with finite norm

\|f\|_p=\left(\int_{\mathbb R^n}|f(x)|^p\,dx\right)^{1/p}

for Lebesgue measure dx. Define translation by (\tau_h f)(x)=f(x-h) for h\in\mathbb R^n.

The set B is relatively compact in the L^p norm (its closure is compact) if and only if both conditions hold:

  1. For every \varepsilon>0 there is \delta>0 such that \|\tau_h f-f\|_p<\varepsilon for every f\in B and every h with |h|<\delta.
  2. For every \varepsilon>0 there is R>0 such that \int_{|x|>R}|f(x)|^p\,dx<\varepsilon for every f\in B.

Here |x| is Euclidean length. No separate boundedness assumption on B is required.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Although compactness and uniform quantifiers are undergraduate concepts, general Lebesgue Lp equivalence classes and their norm topology add a cumulative conceptual burden not confidently within the thirty-minute allowance. No PDE knowledge is required.
