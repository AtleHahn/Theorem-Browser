Statement

Let X be a Hausdorff locally convex topological vector space over \mathbb R or \mathbb C, and let K\subset X be nonempty, compact and convex. A map T:K\to K is affine if T(tx+(1-t)y)=tT(x)+(1-t)T(y) for 0\le t\le1. Every continuous affine map T:K\to K has a fixed point.

Proof 1: Orbit averages and separating linear functionals

Let T be a continuous affine self-mapping of K.

For x in K define a net \{x(N)\}_{N\in\mathbb N} in K by

x(N)=\frac{1}{N+1}\sum_{n=0}^N T^n(x).

Since K is compact, there is a convergent subnet in K:

x(N_i)\longrightarrow y.

To prove that y is a fixed point, it suffices to show that f(Ty)=f(y) for every f in the continuous dual of X. (The dual separates points by the Hahn–Banach theorem; this is where the assumption of local convexity is used.)

Since K is compact, |f| is bounded on K by a positive constant M. On the other hand

|f(Tx(N))-f(x(N))| =\frac{1}{N+1}|f(T^{N+1}x)-f(x)| \leq\frac{2M}{N+1}.

Taking N=N_i and passing to the limit along the subnet, it follows that

f(Ty)=f(y).

Hence

Ty=y.
