Statement

Let r,s,m\geq1 be integers, and let

f_1(\mathbf X,\mathbf Y),\ldots,f_m(\mathbf X,\mathbf Y)\in\mathbb Q(X_1,\ldots,X_r)[Y_1,\ldots,Y_s]

be irreducible polynomials. Here the coefficients are rational functions of \mathbf X=(X_1,\ldots,X_r), and irreducible means nonzero, not a unit, and not a product of two nonunits in this polynomial ring.

There is a tuple \mathbf a\in\mathbb Q^r at which every coefficient is defined such that all the specialized polynomials

f_1(\mathbf a,\mathbf Y),\ldots,f_m(\mathbf a,\mathbf Y)

are irreducible in \mathbb Q[Y_1,\ldots,Y_s].

In fact, there are infinitely many such tuples with all coordinates integers.

Level justification

Proof 1: Algebraic branches and integer specialization (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Section 69, pp.402–412; Proposition69.8, Corollaries69.9 and69.14 and their proofs.).

Clear denominators and take primitive polynomial representatives. Apply Corollary 69.14 to the union of Kronecker factor lists for all polynomials, excluding finitely many zeros of the obstruction product. Corollary 69.9 uses the union of all analytic obstruction functions; the proof of Proposition 69.8 explicitly selects arbitrarily large integers. Repeat one parameter at a time, retaining the other parameters among polynomial variables. This yields simultaneous irreducibility for arbitrary many Y variables and integer values of all X coordinates; choose the first coordinate in infinitely many ways. Track denominator and leading-coefficient nonvanishing at each step. The obstruction bound uses analytic algebraic branches and an integer divided-difference determinant.
