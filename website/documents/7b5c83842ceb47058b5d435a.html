Statement

Let \Omega\subseteq\mathbb C be open, let f be meromorphic on \Omega, and let C be a closed piecewise continuously differentiable curve in \Omega which is contractible to a point in \Omega and passes through no zero or pole of f. For w off the curve put

n(C,w)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_C\frac{dz}{z-w},

the winding number of C about w. Then

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_C\frac{f'(z)}{f(z)}\,dz =\sum_a m_a n(C,a)-\sum_b r_b n(C,b),

where a runs over the zeros of f in the component of \Omega containing C, m_a is the multiplicity of the zero, b runs over its poles in that component, and r_b is the order of the pole. Only terms of nonzero winding number contribute.

For a positively oriented simple closed contour with f meromorphic on a neighborhood of the contour and its interior, this is Z-P, the number of zeros minus the number of poles inside, counted with multiplicity and order.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Meromorphic functions, contour integration, multiplicity, winding numbers and contraction in an arbitrary open domain constitute cumulative complex-analysis and topological prerequisites beyond a confident 30-minute undergraduate extension.

Proof 1: Residues of the logarithmic derivative (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (general residue theorem, pp.17–19; argument principle, p.20).

Apply the proved residue theorem to f^\prime/f: locally f(z)=(z-a)^k u(z) with u holomorphic and nonzero, so f^\prime/f=k/(z-a)+u^\prime/u. Its residue is k, positive for zeros and negative for poles. Contractibility makes the winding number zero outside the domain. The curve together with the points of nonzero winding number is compactly contained in the domain, leaving finitely many contributing zeros and poles. For a positively oriented simple curve the interior winding numbers are one. These observations supply the local calculation and specialization of the linked theorem.
