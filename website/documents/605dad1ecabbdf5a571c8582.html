Statement

Let E be a real separable Banach space and let \mu be a Gaussian Borel probability measure on E: for every continuous linear functional \ell:E\to\mathbb R, the distribution of \ell under \mu is a real normal distribution, allowing variance zero. For h\in E, define

\|h\|_{H_\mu}=\sup\{\,|\ell(h)|:\ell\in E^*,\ \operatorname{Var}_\mu(\ell)\leq1\,\}\in[0,\infty],\qquad H_\mu=\{h\in E:\|h\|_{H_\mu}<\infty\},

where E^* is the continuous linear dual and

\operatorname{Var}_\mu(\ell)=\int_E\left(\ell(x)-\int_E\ell(y)\,d\mu(y)\right)^2d\mu(x).

The set H_\mu is the Cameron–Martin space, viewed here as a subspace of E.

Let h\in E and let \mu_h be the translated measure, defined on Borel sets by \mu_h(A)=\mu(A-h), where A-h=\{x:x+h\in A\}. If \mu_h and \mu are equivalent, meaning they have the same Borel null sets, then h\in H_\mu.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The conclusion requires Gaussian laws on a Banach space, the continuous dual, a variance-constrained dual supremum and equivalence of measures. Their cumulative infinite-dimensional probability background exceeds the conservative undergraduate reading allowance.
