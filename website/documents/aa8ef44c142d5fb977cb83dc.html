Statement

Let X be a smooth projective complex surface and K a canonical divisor. Write \chi(0)=\sum_{i=0}^2(-1)^i\dim_{\mathbb C}H^i(X,\mathcal O_X), and let e(X) be the topological Euler characteristic. With a dot denoting divisor intersection, \chi(0)=(K\cdot K+e(X))/12.

Proof 1: Specialize to the trivial line bundle

For each divisor D on the surface there is an invertible sheaf L=\mathcal O(D). Write h^i(L)=\dim_{\mathbb C}H^i(X,L) and \chi(D)=h^0(L)-h^1(L)+h^2(L). For surfaces the Todd class is

1+\frac{c_1(X)}2+\frac{c_1(X)^2+c_2(X)}{12},

and the Chern character of the sheaf L is just

1+c_1(L)+\frac{c_1(L)^2}2,

so the Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem states that

\begin{aligned} \chi(D)&=h^0(L)-h^1(L)+h^2(L)\\ &=\frac12c_1(L)^2+\frac12c_1(L)c_1(X) +\frac1{12}\bigl(c_1(X)^2+c_2(X)\bigr), \end{aligned}

where products of total cohomological degree four are evaluated on X, and c_i(X) denotes the Chern class of its tangent bundle.

Putting D=0, so that L is trivial and c_1(L)=0, shows that

\chi(0)=\frac1{12}\bigl(c_1(X)^2+c_2(X)\bigr).

Here c_1(X)^2=K\cdot K is the self-intersection number of the canonical class, and c_2(X)=e(X) is the topological Euler characteristic. This gives Noether's formula.
