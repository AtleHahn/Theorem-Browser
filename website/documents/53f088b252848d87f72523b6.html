Rotational symmetry of a Lagrangian forces conservation of the corresponding angular momentum. This gives a concrete instance of how invariance of physical laws explains a conserved quantity, conditional on the Lagrangian description and its equations of motion.

Statement

Let L(r,v,t) be a smooth Lagrangian on three-dimensional positions and velocities, invariant under rotations about a fixed unit axis n. Along a solution of \frac d{dt}L_v=L_r, put p=L_v. Then n\cdot(r\times p) is constant. If invariance holds for every axis, the vector r\times p is constant.

Proof 1: Rotation generator and scalar triple product

Write \mathbf L=\mathbf r\times\mathbf p for angular momentum. We use the mechanical Noether theorem: an invariant flow with generator (T,Q) conserves (p\cdot v-L)T-p\cdot Q; the negative of this quantity is conserved as well.

For concreteness, assume that the Lagrangian does not change under small rotations of an angle δθ about an axis n; such a rotation transforms the Cartesian coordinates by the equation

{\displaystyle \mathbf {r} \rightarrow \mathbf {r} +\delta \theta \,\mathbf {n} \times \mathbf {r} .}

Since time is not being transformed, the time component of the symmetry generator is T = 0, with one symmetry parameter. Taking δθ as the ε parameter and the Cartesian coordinates r as the generalized coordinates q, the corresponding Q variables are given by

{\displaystyle \mathbf {Q} =\mathbf {n} \times \mathbf {r} .}

Then Noether's theorem states that the following quantity is conserved,

{\displaystyle {\frac {\partial L}{\partial {\dot {\mathbf {q} }}}}\cdot \mathbf {Q} =\mathbf {p} \cdot \left(\mathbf {n} \times \mathbf {r} \right)=\mathbf {n} \cdot \left(\mathbf {r} \times \mathbf {p} \right)=\mathbf {n} \cdot \mathbf {L} .}

In other words, the component of the angular momentum L along the n axis is conserved. And if n is arbitrary, i.e., if the system is insensitive to any rotation, then every component of L is conserved; in short, angular momentum is conserved.
