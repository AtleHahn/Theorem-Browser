Statement

An equitable k-coloring of a finite simple undirected graph assigns one of k colors to every vertex, gives adjacent vertices different colors, and has any two color-class sizes differing by at most one; empty classes are allowed. For any fixed treewidth bound t_0, the smallest possible number of colors in an equitable coloring can be computed in polynomial time on finite graphs of treewidth at most t_0. In particular this holds for trees and outerplanar graphs.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Equitable coloring is defined. Treewidth adds finite vertex bags arranged in a tree with edge coverage and connected occurrence sets; outerplanarity means a plane drawing with all vertices on the outer face. Polynomial time means a fixed power of the input size. These concrete finite definitions extend undergraduate sets and elementary algorithms without requiring complexity classifications.

Proof 1: Fix the treewidth bound in the runtime exponent — outline

The equitable-coloring decision problem with a given number of colors has an algorithm running in O(N^{O(t)}) time for an N-vertex graph of treewidth t. In particular, equitable coloring can be solved optimally in polynomial time for trees and outerplanar graphs.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Fixing the treewidth bound makes the supplied runtime exponent constant, and trying at most N color counts remains polynomial. Treewidth and asymptotic runtime are a modest discrete-algorithm extension; the algorithm proof is not needed.
