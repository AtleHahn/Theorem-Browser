Statement

Let S be a nonempty bounded subset of an injective metric space M, and let d be its diameter. There is a point c∈M such that S⊂B̄(c,d/2), and its minimum enclosing radius equals d/2.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Metric injectivity means extension of every distance-nonincreasing map from a subspace. Together with diameter and enclosing balls this is a short extension of undergraduate metric topology; no categorical injective-object theory is needed.

Proof 1: Intersect the equal-radius balls centered on the set

Consider the closed balls B̄(s,d/2), s∈S. Distances between centers are at most d, the sum of the radii. Use the ball-intersection property of injective metric spaces: any family with center distances at most the sums of radii has nonempty intersection. Choose c in this intersection. Then d_M(c,s)≤d/2 for every s∈S, so B̄(c,d/2) contains S. The metric triangle-inequality lower bound shows that no smaller radius is possible.
