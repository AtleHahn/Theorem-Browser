Statement

Let \mathfrak g be a finite-dimensional Lie algebra over a field of characteristic zero. A Lie algebra is a vector space with an alternating bilinear bracket satisfying [x,[y,z]]+[y,[z,x]]+[z,[x,y]]=0 for all x,y,z. Brackets of subspaces denote the linear span of all brackets of their elements. An ideal I satisfies [\mathfrak g,I]\subseteq I. It is solvable if its derived series I^{(0)}=I, I^{(j+1)}=[I^{(j)},I^{(j)}] reaches zero. Write \mathfrak r for the radical, the largest solvable ideal. A Lie algebra is semisimple if its radical is zero. There is a semisimple subalgebra \mathfrak s\subseteq\mathfrak g such that

\mathfrak g=\mathfrak r\oplus\mathfrak s\quad\text{as vector spaces}.

Equivalently, \mathfrak g is the semidirect product \mathfrak r\rtimes\mathfrak s, where \mathfrak s acts on \mathfrak r by the bracket. Such a complement \mathfrak s is called a Levi subalgebra.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Although bracket and derived series have short definitions, the cumulative new algebraic structures and radical splitting are borderline beyond the conservative undergraduate allowance.
