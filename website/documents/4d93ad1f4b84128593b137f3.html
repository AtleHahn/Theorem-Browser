Statement

Assume the Boolean prime ideal theorem, in the equivalent form that every nontrivial Boolean algebra has an ultrafilter. Let B be any Boolean algebra: a bounded distributive lattice with operations \wedge,\vee, elements 0,1, and complements \neg b. Write b\leq c when b\wedge c=b.

A proper filter is a subset containing 1, not containing 0, closed under finite meets and upward closed for \leq. An ultrafilter is a maximal proper filter. Let S(B) be the set of ultrafilters on B, with the topology whose basis consists of

U_b=\{u\in S(B):b\in u\}\qquad(b\in B).

Then S(B) is compact, Hausdorff and totally disconnected, each U_b is clopen (both closed and open), and

B\longrightarrow\operatorname{Clop}(S(B)),\qquad b\longmapsto U_b

is an isomorphism of Boolean algebras. On the right the operations are intersection, union and complement relative to S(B), and \operatorname{Clop}(S(B)) denotes all its clopen subsets. Thus the map is bijective and preserves \wedge,\vee,\neg,0,1. Totally disconnected means that every connected component is a singleton. For the trivial Boolean algebra, S(B) is empty and its clopen algebra is also trivial.

Level justification

Although basic topology and algebra are undergraduate subjects, the cumulative combination of lattice operations, ultrafilters, a choice principle and canonical Stone-space construction is not confidently within the short reading allowance.
