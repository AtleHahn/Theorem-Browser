Statement

For a pointed space Y, write \operatorname{SP}(Y) for its infinite symmetric product: finite unordered tuples of points of Y, with basepoint entries ignored, with the usual direct-limit topology of finite symmetric products. Let H be the Hawaiian earring, based at its common circle point, let C H be its cone based at that same point of its base, and put W=C H\vee C H with those basepoints identified. The two abstract isomorphisms \pi_1\operatorname{SP}(C H)\cong H_1(C H;\mathbb Z) and \pi_1\operatorname{SP}(W)\cong H_1(W;\mathbb Z) cannot both hold.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Hawaiian earring and cones; Based wedge sums; Infinite symmetric products; First homology and fundamental groups. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Compare the cone and wedge groups

Let W=CH\vee CH be the wedge sum of two copies of the cone over the Hawaiian earring, with the common point as specified in the statement. On the one hand, H_1(W;\mathbb Z) is an infinite group while H_1(CH;\mathbb Z) is trivial. On the other hand,

\pi_1(\operatorname{SP}(W))\cong\pi_1(\operatorname{SP}(CH))\times\pi_1(\operatorname{SP}(CH))

holds since the map

\varphi:\operatorname{SP}(CH)\times\operatorname{SP}(CH)\longrightarrow\operatorname{SP}(CH\vee CH)

defined by

\varphi([x_1,\ldots,x_n],[y_1,\ldots,y_m])=[x_1,\ldots,x_n,y_1,\ldots,y_m]

is a homeomorphism for these compact spaces.

But this implies that either \pi_1(\operatorname{SP}(CH))\cong H_1(CH;\mathbb Z) or \pi_1(\operatorname{SP}(W))\cong H_1(W;\mathbb Z) does not hold.

Level justification

The proof compares homology and fundamental groups of infinite symmetric products of a pathological based wedge. Cones, direct-limit topologies and these algebraic-topology invariants together exceed the undergraduate baseline.
