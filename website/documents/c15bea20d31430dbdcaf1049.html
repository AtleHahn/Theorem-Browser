Statement

Let G be a finite connected simple undirected graph with at least one edge and with all vertex degrees even. Its line graph L(G) has one vertex for each edge of G, with two vertices adjacent when their edges share an endpoint. Then L(G) has a cycle visiting every vertex exactly once.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The line graph and the desired cycle are defined by finite vertices and shared endpoints. Connectedness and even degrees are elementary finite graph conditions, not prerequisites in linear algebra or topology.

Proof 1: Read the Euler tour as a cycle of edge-vertices — outline

By the stated Euler-tour theorem, G has a closed walk traversing each edge exactly once. List its edges in cyclic order. They are distinct and exhaust the vertices of L(G); consecutive edges, including the last and first, share the intervening vertex of the tour. Hence their list is a Hamiltonian cycle of L(G). The simple connected even-degree graph with an edge has at least three edges, so this is a cycle in the ordinary simple-graph sense.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Use the supplied Euler tour and regard each edge in its cyclic list as a vertex of the explicitly defined line graph. Distinctness, adjacency and exhaustiveness are finite combinatorial checks.
