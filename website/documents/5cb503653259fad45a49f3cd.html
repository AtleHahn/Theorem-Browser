Statement

Let H=\{z\in\mathbb C:\operatorname{Im}z>0\} and f:H\to H be holomorphic. For z_1,z_2\in H, \left|\frac{f(z_1)-f(z_2)}{\overline{f(z_1)}-f(z_2)}\right|\le\frac{|z_1-z_2|}{|\overline{z_1}-z_2|}. Also |f'(z)|/\operatorname{Im}f(z)\le1/\operatorname{Im}z.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex differentiability extends the calculus derivative; limits, compact sets, uniform approximation and zeros of functions are familiar analysis. Zero multiplicity is the order of the first nonzero Taylor coefficient, and circle integrals are parametrized ordinary integrals. These limited definitions suffice for the full claim.

Proof 1: Cayley conjugation — outline

Use the disk Schwarz–Pick inequalities: for a holomorphic h:D\to D,

\left|\frac{h(u)-h(v)}{1-\overline{h(u)}h(v)}\right|\le\left|\frac{u-v}{1-\overline uv}\right|,\qquad\frac{|h'(u)|}{1-|h(u)|^2}\le\frac1{1-|u|^2}.

This is an easy consequence of the disk Schwarz–Pick theorem: One just needs to remember that the Cayley transform {\displaystyle W(z)=(z-i)/(z+i)} maps the upper half-plane {\displaystyle \mathbf {H} } conformally onto the unit disc {\displaystyle \mathbf {D} }. Then, the map {\displaystyle W\circ f\circ W^{-1}} is a holomorphic map from {\displaystyle \mathbf {D} } into {\displaystyle \mathbf {D} }. Using the Schwarz–Pick theorem on this map, and finally simplifying the results by using the formula for {\displaystyle W}, we get the desired result.

The same substitution in the derivative inequality gives the stated derivative bound.
