Statement

Let P,Q:O\to\mathbb R^3 be C^2 vector fields on an open set O\subseteq\mathbb R^3. The vector Laplacian acts componentwise, and (P\cdot\nabla)Q is the directional derivative of Q. Then \Delta(P\cdot Q)=P\cdot\Delta Q-Q\cdot\Delta P+2\nabla\cdot[(Q\cdot\nabla)P+Q\times(\nabla\times P)].

Proof 1: Recombine the component gradient sums — outline

Write P=\sum_m p_m\widehat e_m and Q=\sum_m q_m\widehat e_m in Cartesian components. Summing the scalar Green identity for each component gives

P\cdot\Delta Q-Q\cdot\Delta P =\nabla\cdot\left(\sum_m p_m\nabla q_m-\sum_m q_m\nabla p_m\right).

Recall the vector identity for the gradient of a dot product,

\nabla(P\cdot Q) =(P\cdot\nabla)Q+(Q\cdot\nabla)P +P\times(\nabla\times Q)+Q\times(\nabla\times P),

which, written out in vector components is given by

\nabla(P\cdot Q)=\nabla\sum_m p_mq_m =\sum_m p_m\nabla q_m+\sum_m q_m\nabla p_m.

The contributions are

\sum_m p_m\nabla q_m=(P\cdot\nabla)Q+P\times(\nabla\times Q),\sum_m q_m\nabla p_m=(Q\cdot\nabla)P+Q\times(\nabla\times P).

These results can be rigorously proven to be correct through evaluation of the vector components. With a similar procedure, the Laplacian of the dot product can be expressed in terms of the Laplacians of the factors:

\Delta(P\cdot Q)=P\cdot\Delta Q-Q\cdot\Delta P +2\nabla\cdot[(Q\cdot\nabla)P+Q\times(\nabla\times P)].

The source leaves this final recombination implicit.
