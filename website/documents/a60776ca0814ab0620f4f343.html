Statement

Let E be an infinite-dimensional vector space over a field K. Then its algebraic dual E^*=\operatorname{Hom}_K(E,K) has \dim_K E^*>\dim_K E, so E and E^* are not isomorphic as vector spaces.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra supplies the algebraic dual as all scalar-valued linear maps. Infinite dimension means basis cardinality; the comparison is a set-cardinality assertion, with no continuous dual, norm or weak topology involved.

Proof 1: Combine the dual dimension formula with Cantor’s theorem — outline

Let I be a basis of E. By the Erdős–Kaplansky theorem, \dim_K E^*=|K^I|. Cantor’s theorem makes this cardinal strictly larger than |I|=\dim_K E. Hence the dual is not isomorphic to E.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the stated algebraic-dual dimension formula and embed subsets into two-valued functions before applying Cantor. Undergraduate linear algebra and elementary cardinal comparisons suffice; no proof of Erdos–Kaplansky or ordinal arithmetic is required.
