Statement

In the first-order sequent calculus specified below, every sequent A\vdash A is derivable using only atomic identity axioms P\vdash P, where P is an atomic formula. Consequently, replacing arbitrary-formula identity axioms by atomic ones does not change which sequents are derivable. Formulas are built from atomic predicates (equality, if present, is also atomic) using \land,\lor,\to,\neg,\forall,\exists. A sequent \Gamma\vdash\Delta has finite lists of formulas on each side. Derivable means the root of a finite proof tree whose leaves are axioms and whose other steps use these rules:

  • Structural rules allow permutation, insertion of a formula (weakening), and replacement of two identical occurrences by one (contraction), on either side.
  • Conjunction: from \Gamma,A\vdash\Delta or from \Gamma,B\vdash\Delta infer \Gamma,A\land B\vdash\Delta; from both \Gamma\vdash A,\Delta and \Gamma\vdash B,\Delta infer \Gamma\vdash A\land B,\Delta.
  • Disjunction: from both \Gamma,A\vdash\Delta and \Gamma,B\vdash\Delta infer \Gamma,A\lor B\vdash\Delta; from either \Gamma\vdash A,\Delta or \Gamma\vdash B,\Delta infer \Gamma\vdash A\lor B,\Delta.
  • Implication: from \Gamma\vdash A,\Delta and \Sigma,B\vdash\Pi infer \Gamma,\Sigma,A\to B\vdash\Delta,\Pi; from \Gamma,A\vdash B,\Delta infer \Gamma\vdash A\to B,\Delta.
  • Negation: from \Gamma\vdash A,\Delta infer \Gamma,\neg A\vdash\Delta; from \Gamma,A\vdash\Delta infer \Gamma\vdash\neg A,\Delta.
  • Quantifiers: from \Gamma,A[t/x]\vdash\Delta infer \Gamma,\forall xA\vdash\Delta; from \Gamma\vdash A[t/x],\Delta infer \Gamma\vdash\exists xA,\Delta. From \Gamma\vdash A[y/x],\Delta infer \Gamma\vdash\forall xA,\Delta; from \Gamma,A[y/x]\vdash\Delta infer \Gamma,\exists xA\vdash\Delta, with y not free in the conclusion in the latter two rules.
  • Cut: from \Gamma\vdash\Delta,A and A,\Sigma\vdash\Pi infer \Gamma,\Sigma\vdash\Delta,\Pi.

Here A[t/x] is substitution for free occurrences of x, with variables renamed to avoid capture. This is the ordinary classical Gentzen calculus; no nonlogical axioms or equality-specific rules are needed for the identity assertion.
