Statement

Let (a_n)_{n\ge0} be complex numbers and set

b_n=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk a_k,\qquad c_n=\sum_{k=0}^n(-1)^k\binom nk a_k.

Suppose \beta>1 and b_n=O(\beta^n), c_n=O(\beta^n). Put \alpha=\sqrt{\beta^2-1}. Then a_n=O(\alpha^n), and there is an absolute constant \kappa independent of the sequence and \beta for which

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{|a_n|}{\alpha^n}\le\kappa\left(\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{|b_n|}{\beta^n}\right)^{1/2}\left(\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{|c_n|}{\beta^n}\right)^{1/2}.

The least possible such universal constant satisfies 2/\sqrt3\le\kappa\le2. Here x_n=O(r^n) means |x_n|\le Cr^n for some finite C and all sufficiently large n, and \binom nk=n!/(k!(n-k)!).
