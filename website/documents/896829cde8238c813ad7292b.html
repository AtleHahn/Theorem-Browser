Statement

Let K,L be nonempty index sets and (C_k)_{k\in K},(D_l)_{l\in L} families of sets. Then (\bigcup_{k\in K}C_k)\cap(\bigcup_{l\in L}D_l)=\bigcup_{(k,l)\in K\times L}(C_k\cap D_l).

Proof 1: Identify two-coordinate choice functions with pairs

Apply the stated complete distributive identity \bigcap_{i\in I}\bigcup_{j\in J_i}T_{ij}=\bigcup_{f\in\prod_iJ_i}\bigcap_{i\in I}T_{i,f(i)} with I=\{1,2\}, J_1=K,J_2=L, T_{1,k}=C_k,T_{2,l}=D_l. A choice function is identified with (f(1),f(2))\in K\times L, and conversely each pair gives such a function. The left side is the intersection of the two displayed unions, and the right side is the union of C_k\cap D_l over all pairs.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The application uses only two outer coordinates: functions on {1,2} with values in K and L are pairs in K times L. Indexed unions, intersections and Cartesian products are introductory undergraduate set theory; the general notation does not impose an infinite choice principle in this finite specialization.

Statement used:

For two indexed families, intersecting their unions equals the union over two-coordinate selections of the corresponding intersections.

Application in proof:

Identify each selection with its pair (k,l) and substitute the two given families.
