Statement

Let A be self-adjoint on a nonzero finite-dimensional real or complex inner-product space. There exists a nonzero vector maximizing \|Au\|/\|u\|, and every such vector u is an eigenvector of A^2.

Level

Level justification

Understanding the statement involves finite-dimensional inner-product spaces; self-adjoint operators; eigenvectors; norm maximization. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. Proof 1: Compare maximality with the Cauchy–Schwarz equality bound

Let {\displaystyle A} be a self-adjoint operator on a finite-dimensional inner product space and {\displaystyle \mathbf {u} } be a non-zero vector which maximizes {\displaystyle {\frac {\|A\mathbf {u} \|}{\|\mathbf {u} \|}}}. The existence of {\displaystyle \mathbf {u} } is guaranteed by the Heine-Borel theorem. If {\displaystyle A\mathbf {u} =\mathbf {0} } then {\displaystyle \mathbf {u} } is an eigenvector of A^2 with eigenvalue zero. Otherwise the maximizing property of {\displaystyle \mathbf {u} } implies that

{\displaystyle {\frac {\|A\mathbf {u} \|}{\|\mathbf {u} \|}}\geq {\frac {\|A(A\mathbf {u} )\|}{\|A\mathbf {u} \|}}.}

In the other direction, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality implies that

{\displaystyle \|A\mathbf {u} \|^{2}=\langle A\mathbf {u} ,A\mathbf {u} \rangle =\langle \mathbf {u} ,A^{2}\mathbf {u} \rangle \leq \|\mathbf {u} \|\|A^{2}\mathbf {u} \|,}

hence equality holds. By the characterization of equality, {\displaystyle \mathbf {u} } and {\displaystyle A^{2}\mathbf {u} } are linearly dependent, therefore {\displaystyle \mathbf {u} } is an eigenvector of {\displaystyle A^{2}}.
