Statement

Fix 0<p<1, put q=1-p, and define b_n(k)=\binom nkp^kq^{n-k} for integers 0\le k\le n, where \binom nk=n!/(k!(n-k)!) and 0!=1. Let integers k_n satisfy k_n/n\to p. Then b_n(k_n)\sim(2\pi npq)^{-1/2}(np/k_n)^{k_n}(nq/(n-k_n))^{n-k_n}, where u_n\sim v_n means u_n/v_n\to1.

Proof 1: Cancel the three Stirling approximations

Write k=k_n; since k_n/n\to p\in(0,1), both k and n-k tend to infinity.

First, according to Stirling's formula, the factorial of a large number n can be replaced with the approximation

{\displaystyle n!\simeq n^{n}e^{-n}{\sqrt {2\pi n}}\qquad {\text{as }}n\to \infty .}

Thus

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}{n \choose k}p^{k}q^{n-k}&={\frac {n!}{k!(n-k)!}}p^{k}q^{n-k}\\&\simeq {\frac {n^{n}e^{-n}{\sqrt {2\pi n}}}{k^{k}e^{-k}{\sqrt {2\pi k}}(n-k)^{n-k}e^{-(n-k)}{\sqrt {2\pi (n-k)}}}}p^{k}q^{n-k}\\&={\sqrt {\frac {n}{2\pi k\left(n-k\right)}}}{\frac {n^{n}}{k^{k}\left(n-k\right)^{n-k}}}p^{k}q^{n-k}\\&={\sqrt {\frac {n}{2\pi k\left(n-k\right)}}}\left({\frac {np}{k}}\right)^{k}\left({\frac {nq}{n-k}}\right)^{n-k}\end{aligned}}}

Next, the approximation {\displaystyle {\tfrac {k}{n}}\to p} is used to match the square root above to the square root (2\pi npq)^{-1/2}.

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}{n \choose k}p^{k}q^{n-k}&\simeq {\sqrt {\frac {1}{2\pi n{\frac {k}{n}}\left(1-{\frac {k}{n}}\right)}}}\left({\frac {np}{k}}\right)^{k}\left({\frac {nq}{n-k}}\right)^{n-k}\\&\simeq {\frac {1}{\sqrt {2\pi npq}}}\left({\frac {np}{k}}\right)^{k}\left({\frac {nq}{n-k}}\right)^{n-k}\qquad p+q=1\\\end{aligned}}}

Here \simeq means asymptotic equivalence: the ratio of the two quantities approaches 1 as n\to\infty.

Level justification

Both factorial arguments k_n and n-k_n tend to infinity, so their Stirling ratios tend to one. Cancel the exponential factors and use k_n/n→p only in the square-root prefactor. Ordinary asymptotic arithmetic and continuity suffice.
