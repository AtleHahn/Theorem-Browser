Statement

If f is holomorphic and injective on \{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\} and has Taylor expansion f(z)=z+\sum_{m\ge2}a_mz^m, then |a_m|\le m for every integer m\ge2.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex differentiability and the explicitly defined Fourier sums extend calculus directly. For the Fourier assertions, null sets can be understood through arbitrarily small interval covers, and residual sets through countable intersections of dense open sets. These limited additions to real analysis and topology suffice without general measure integration.

Proof 1: Jacobi positivity and the coefficient criterion — theorem-reduction outline

For integers n\ge1 and 0\le k\le n-1, put

F_{n,k}(x)=\int_0^1t^{n-k-1/2}P_k^{(2n-2k,1)}(1-2tx)\,dt,

where {\displaystyle P_{k}^{(\alpha ,\beta )}} is the Jacobi polynomial of degree {\displaystyle k} with parameters {\displaystyle \alpha ,\beta }. {\displaystyle F_{n,k}} is a polynomial of degree k, and it is a hypergeometric series with rational coefficients (the precise coefficients can be derived using the hypergeometric series for Jacobi polynomials).

De Branges showed that if {\displaystyle F_{n,k}(x)>0} for {\displaystyle 0<x<1} and {\displaystyle k=0,1,\dots ,n-1}, then {\displaystyle |a_{n+1}|\leq n+1}.

Askey and Gasper (1976) proved the necessary inequalities. Applying this coefficient criterion for every n\ge1 proves the coefficient bound.

Level justification

The reduction concerns univalent holomorphic functions, Jacobi polynomial integrals and the de Branges analytic coefficient criterion. The complex-analysis setting and special-function criterion together exceed the baseline.
