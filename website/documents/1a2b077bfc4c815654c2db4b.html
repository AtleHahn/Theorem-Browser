Statement

Let f(n)=(n-1)/n for n\ge1. Then \sup_n|f(n)|=1, and for every real or complex sequence g with \sum|g(n)|\le1, |\sum f(n)g(n)|<1.

Proof 1: Strict pairing bound for a bounded sequence

For nonzero g choose the smallest m with g(m)\ne0. Then |\sum f(n)g(n)|\le((m-1)/m)|g(m)|+\sum_{n>m}|g(n)|=\|g\|_1-|g(m)|/m<1. If g=0 the conclusion is immediate. The values f(n) approach1 from below, so its supremum is1.

Level justification

Isolate the first nonzero coefficient to obtain a strict loss from the unit absolute-sum bound, and compute the supremum by a real limit. Undergraduate absolutely convergent series suffice; no Banach-dual representation theorem is used.
