Statement

Assume the intersection-angle assertion: when two lines cut by a transversal meet on one side of it, their two interior angles on that side have sum less than 180^\circ. Using the usual planar construction of a transversal separating two alleged intersections, two distinct lines cannot intersect twice.

Proof 1: Apply the angle assertion on both sides — outline

If two lines had two distinct intersection points, draw a transversal between these points as directed by the source. The intersection-angle assertion on each side says that each of the two interior-angle sums is less than 180^\circ. But the two sums together are 360^\circ, because at each transversal crossing the angles on its opposite sides are supplementary. This contradiction rules out two intersections.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Apply the assumed angle assertion on both sides of the separating transversal and contradict the supplementary-angle total. Elementary planar geometry and inequalities suffice.
