Statement

For any field K, the ring R=K[x,y]/(x^2,xy) is not Cohen–Macaulay.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Polynomial quotient rings over a field; Cohen–Macaulay rings. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Compare degrees of the fibers — outline

This follows, for example, by Miracle Flatness: R is finite over the polynomial ring A = K[y], with degree 1 over points of the affine line Spec A with y ≠ 0, but with degree 2 over the point y = 0 (because the K-vector space K[x]/(x2) has dimension 2).

Level justification

The argument uses finite algebras, affine scheme fibers, Cohen-Macaulayness and miracle flatness. These linked commutative-algebra and scheme concepts exceed introductory quotient-ring theory.
