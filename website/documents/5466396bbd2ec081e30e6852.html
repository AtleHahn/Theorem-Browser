Statement

Let K be a field of characteristic different from 2 and 3. Let a,b,c,d\in K, with a\ne0, and set f(x)=ax^3+bx^2+cx+d. Suppose \Delta=18abcd-4b^3d+b^2c^2-4ac^3-27a^2d^2=0. If b^2=3ac, then f(x)=a(x+b/(3a))^3. Otherwise set x_2=(9ad-bc)/(2(b^2-3ac)) and x_1=(4abc-9a^2d-b^3)/(a(b^2-3ac)); then x_1\ne x_2 and f(x)=a(x-x_1)(x-x_2)^2.

An algebraic closure only supplies all polynomial roots here, and a primitive cube root is a nonidentity root of X cubed minus one. The displayed finite formulas need no Galois or extension-degree theory.

Proof 1: Translate the depressed multiple-root factors — outline

Put p=(3ac-b^2)/(3a^2) and q=(2b^3-9abc+27a^2d)/(27a^3). The substitution x=t-b/(3a) gives f(t-b/(3a))=a(t^3+pt+q).

Use the depressed-cubic factorization: if 4p^3+27q^2=0, then for p=0 the polynomial is t^3, and for p\ne0 it is (t-3q/p)(t+3q/(2p))^2.

Substitution gives: If the discriminant of the cubic {\displaystyle ax^{3}+bx^{2}+cx+d} is zero, then

  • either, if {\displaystyle b^{2}=3ac,} the cubic has a triple root

    {\displaystyle x_{1}=x_{2}=x_{3}=-{\frac {b}{3a}},}

    and

    {\displaystyle ax^{3}+bx^{2}+cx+d=a\left(x+{\frac {b}{3a}}\right)^{3}}

  • or, if {\displaystyle b^{2}\neq 3ac,} the cubic has a double root

    {\displaystyle x_{2}=x_{3}={\frac {9ad-bc}{2(b^{2}-3ac)}},}

    and a simple root,

    {\displaystyle x_{1}={\frac {4abc-9a^{2}d-b^{3}}{a(b^{2}-3ac)}}.}

    and thus

    {\displaystyle ax^{3}+bx^{2}+cx+d=a(x-x_{1})(x-x_{2})^{2}.}
