Statement

Every positive Pythagorean triple, up to scaling and exchange of its legs, is proportional to (t,(t^2-1)/2,(t^2+1)/2) for a rational t>1.

Proof 1: Source argument

All Pythagorean triples can be obtained, with appropriate rescaling, from the basic sequence

\left(t,\frac{t^2-1}{2},\frac{t^2+1}{2}\right)

by allowing t to take non-integer rational values. If t is replaced with the fraction m/n in the sequence, the result is equal to the standard triple generator

(2mn,m^2-n^2,m^2+n^2)

after rescaling by 2n^2. Here Euclid's parameterization, with m>n>0 coprime and of opposite parity, supplies every primitive triple, up to exchange of its legs.

It follows that every triple has a corresponding rational t value which can be used to generate a similar triangle (one with the same three angles and with sides in the same proportions as the original). For example, the Platonic equivalent of (56,33,65) is generated by t=m/n=7/4 as

\left(t,\frac{t^2-1}{2},\frac{t^2+1}{2}\right) =\left(\frac{56}{32},\frac{33}{32},\frac{65}{32}\right).

High school only
Level justification

Write t=m/n and multiply the three rational coordinates by 2n squared to recover Euclid formula. School rational algebra and similarity suffice.
