Statement

For a closed connected oriented surface S, write \operatorname{Mod}(S) for orientation-preserving homeomorphisms modulo isotopy, and \operatorname{Mod}^{\pm}(S) when both orientations are allowed. If S has genus g, every finite subgroup F\le\operatorname{Mod}(S) embeds under the mod-three homology action in \operatorname{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z). In particular, for this fixed S only finitely many isomorphism types of finite subgroups occur.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The claim combines mapping class groups, isotopy, homology actions and symplectic groups. Understanding the complete action and embedding requires substantial geometric-topology background. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Restrict the finite quotient to a finite subgroup

Let \Phi_3 be the integral first-homology action followed by reduction modulo three. Use the stated fact that K=\ker\Phi_3 is torsion-free. Since every element of the finite group F has finite order, F\cap K=\{1\}. Therefore \Phi_3|_F is injective into the finite group \operatorname{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z).

A finite group has only finitely many subsets and hence only finitely many subgroups. Every such F is isomorphic to one of those subgroups, giving the claimed finiteness of isomorphism types.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The injective-kernel argument is basic group theory, but the full map is a surface mapping-class action on integral homology reduced to a symplectic group. Isotopy, homology and surface actions require substantial algebraic topology.
