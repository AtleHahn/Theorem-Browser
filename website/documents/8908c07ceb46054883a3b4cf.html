Statement

In a propositional calculus containing all instances of (Q\to R)\to((P\to Q)\to(P\to R)) and closed under modus ponens, if Q\to R and P\to Q are theorems, then P\to R is a theorem. Modus ponens means that U and U\to V yield V.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The assumed implication and modus ponens are fully displayed; theorem composition uses elementary finite symbolic deduction.

Proof 1: Apply the curried theorem twice

Whenever we have two theorems of the form {\displaystyle T_{1}=(Q\to R)} and {\displaystyle T_{2}=(P\to Q)}, we can prove {\displaystyle (P\to R)} by the following steps:

(1) {\displaystyle (Q\to R)\to ((P\to Q)\to (P\to R))}       (instance of the assumed curried hypothetical syllogism)

(2) {\displaystyle Q\to R}       (instance of (T1))

(3) {\displaystyle (P\to Q)\to (P\to R)}       (from (1) and (2) by modus ponens)

(4) {\displaystyle P\to Q}       (instance of (T2))

(5) {\displaystyle P\to R}       (from (3) and (4) by modus ponens)
