Statement

Let (\Omega,\Sigma,\nu) be a measure space and let f:\Omega\to\overline{\mathbb R} be measurable. For real t>0 and 0<p<\infty, \nu\{|f|\ge t\}\le t^{-p}\int_\Omega |f|^p\,d\nu, allowing an infinite integral.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement is about arbitrary measure spaces and nonnegative Lebesgue integrals.

Proof 1: Use the truncated power transform

The monotone-transform statement follows from the Markov inequality,

\nu(\{x\in\Omega:|F(x)|\geq\varepsilon\}) \leq\frac1\varepsilon\int_\Omega|F|\,d\nu,

with F=g\circ u and \varepsilon=g(t), since in this case

\nu(\{x\in\Omega:g\circ u(x)\geq g(t)\}) \geq\nu(\{x\in\Omega:u(x)\geq t\}).

For the absolute-value tail in the statement take u=|f|. The power bound then follows by defining g(s) as |s|^p if s\geq t and 0 otherwise. Here g(t)=t^p>0 and g(|f|)\leq|f|^p.
