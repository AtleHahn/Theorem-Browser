Statement

Let C be a rectifiable simple closed curve in the Euclidean plane, let L be its length, and let A be the area of the bounded region it encloses. Then

4\pi A\le L^2,

with equality if and only if C is a circle.

A simple closed curve has no self-intersections, apart from the coincidence of the starting and ending point of a parametrization. Rectifiable means that its length, defined as the supremum of lengths of inscribed polygonal paths, is finite; no smoothness is assumed.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The geometry is elementary, but the precise arbitrary-rectifiable-curve formulation uses a supremum definition of length and enclosed area for nonsmooth boundaries. Introductory real analysis and plane topology suffice, with rectifiability a small extension of polygonal approximation.

Proof 1: Wirtinger inequality and strict convexification (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (isoperimetric_kernel, isoperimetric_theorem_convex, isoperimetric_convexification_strict and isoperimetric_theorem).

The final theorem applies directly to rectifiable simple closed parametrizations, without a smoothness assumption, and proves that equality forces the image to be a circle. For the converse, a circle has positive radius r, length 2\pi r and enclosed area \pi r^2. Thus it attains equality. The kernel uses absolutely continuous arclength coordinates; strict area increase on convexification excludes every nonconvex interior from equality.

The full rectifiable-curve statement requires length and enclosed area for nonsmooth Jordan curves.
