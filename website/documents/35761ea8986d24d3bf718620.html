Statement

A Platonic solid is a convex polyhedron in Euclidean three-space whose faces are congruent regular p-gons and at each vertex of which q faces meet. If the edge length is a and there are F faces, the surface area is A=(a/2)^2Fp\cot(\pi/p).

Level
High school only
Level justification

Regular-polyhedron terminology, trigonometric functions and the displayed algebra lie within elementary geometry.

Proof 1: Sum the regular-polygon face areas

The surface area, A, of a Platonic solid \{p,q\} is easily computed as area of a regular p-gon times the number of faces F. This is:

A=\left(\frac a2\right)^2Fp\cot\left(\frac\pi p\right).
Level
High school only
Level justification

Multiply the regular-polygon face area by the face count. School trigonometry and area formulas suffice.
