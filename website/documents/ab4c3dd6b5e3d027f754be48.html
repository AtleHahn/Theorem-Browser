Quantum relative entropy is nonnegative for the stated density matrices. It supplies a precise measure of state mismatch, connecting quantum-state descriptions with informational discrimination.

Statement

Let n\ge1 be an integer. For a Hermitian matrix X=\sum_j\lambda_jP_j, scalar functions act spectrally: h(X)=\sum_j h(\lambda_j)P_j. Here P_j are the orthogonal eigenprojections and \operatorname{Tr} is the matrix trace. Let \rho,\sigma be positive-definite Hermitian n\times n density matrices, meaning \operatorname{Tr}\rho=\operatorname{Tr}\sigma=1. Then S(\rho\Vert\sigma)=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho\log\rho)-\operatorname{Tr}(\rho\log\sigma)\ge0.

Proof 1: The density-matrix specialization of Klein’s inequality — outline

Apply Klein’s inequality to f(t)=t\log t, whose derivative is 1+\log t, and to A=\rho,B=\sigma. It yields \operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\rho\log\rho-\sigma\log\sigma-(\rho-\sigma)(I+\log\sigma)\bigr)\ge0. The logarithmic sigma terms cancel and the linear terms have trace -\operatorname{Tr}\rho+\operatorname{Tr}\sigma=0. What remains is S(\rho\Vert\sigma)\ge0.
