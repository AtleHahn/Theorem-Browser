Statement

Let n\ge2, a<b, and for each i=1,\ldots,n let p_i,q_i be continuous real functions on [a,b]. Let x_i be a real C^1 function with p_ix_i' continuously differentiable, satisfying

(p_ix_i')'+q_ix_i=0,\qquad x_i(a)=1,\qquad x_i'(a)=R_i\in\mathbb R.

Assume x_i(t)\ne0 for a<t\le b. Define r_i=x_i'/x_i, W(x_i,x_j)=x_i'x_j-x_ix_j', and the forward difference \Delta u_i=u_{i+1}-u_i, iterated in the index i. Then

\begin{aligned} [x_{n-1}^2\Delta^{n-1}(p_1r_1)]_a^b ={}&\int_a^b(x_{n-1}')^2\Delta^{n-1}p_1\,dt-\int_a^bx_{n-1}^2\Delta^{n-1}q_1\,dt\\ &-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{k}(-1)^{n-k-1}\int_a^b\frac{p_{k+1}W(x_{k+1},x_{n-1})^2}{x_{k+1}^2}\,dt. \end{aligned}

Here [F]_a^b=F(b)-F(a); in particular \Delta^{n-1}u_1=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{n-1-k}\binom{n-1}{k}u_{k+1}. All functions in the integrands are evaluated at t.

Level justification

The displayed classical-solution identity uses calculus and explicitly defined finite differences and Wronskians. No oscillation theory is needed to understand it.
