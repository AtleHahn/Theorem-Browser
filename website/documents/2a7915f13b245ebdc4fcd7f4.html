Statement

If Y is compact, X is any topological space, x\in X, and N\subseteq X\times Y is open with \{x\}\times Y\subseteq N, then some open neighborhood U of x satisfies U\times Y\subseteq N.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Basic topology and real analysis supply normality, compactness, products, bases, continuity and uniform convergence. The maps and quantified conditions are explicit, with choice assumed where needed; no algebraic or differential topology is involved.

Proof 1: Specialize the finite-cover construction to a slice

We specialize the source's generalized tube-lemma construction to A=\{x\} and B=Y. If Y is empty, take U=X.

By the definition of the product topology, for each (x,b)\in\{x\}\times Y there are open sets U_{x,b}\subseteq X and V_{x,b}\subseteq Y such that

(x,b)\in U_{x,b}\times V_{x,b}\subseteq N.

The collection \{V_{x,b}:b\in Y\} is an open cover of the compact set Y so this cover has a finite subcover; namely, there is a finite set B_0(x)\subseteq Y such that

V_x:=\bigcup_{b\in B_0(x)}V_{x,b}

contains Y, where V_x is open in Y. Let

U_x:=\bigcap_{b\in B_0(x)}U_{x,b},

which is an open set in X since B_0(x) is finite. Moreover, the construction of U_x and V_x implies that

\{x\}\times Y\subseteq U_x\times V_x\subseteq N.

Here V_x=Y, so U=U_x gives the required tube.
