Statement

Given a nondegenerate circle and its center, a straightedge alone constructs a perpendicular to any prescribed line through any prescribed point.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Points, lines, circles, parallels and perpendiculars are elementary geometry.

Proof 1: Apply Thales and construct a parallel — outline

Local parallel-construction setup

Given an arbitrary line n with two marked points A and B, and their midpoint M, and an arbitrary point P in the plane (assumed not to be on line n), we wish to construct the parallel of line n through P:

  1. Construct a line AP.
  2. Construct a line BP.
  3. Choose a point R on line AP in general position so that the following lines and intersections are defined and distinct.
  4. Construct a line BR.
  5. Construct a line MR.
  6. Lines MR and BP intersect at point X.
  7. Construct a line AX.
  8. Lines BR and AX intersect at point Q.
  9. Construct a line PQ, the desired parallel.

This construction is a special case of the projective harmonic conjugate construction.

If the prescribed line m passes through the center of a circle, the segment defined by the diameter through the circle is bisected by the center. Otherwise, any other line in the plane may have a bisected segment constructed onto it. This construction makes use of the given circle, O(r), with center O.

Given a line, m, we wish to construct points A, B, and C on the line such that B is the midpoint:

  1. Draw an arbitrary line passing through the given circle's center, O, and the desired midpoint B (chosen arbitrarily) on the line m.
    • Notice that the line, OB, passes through the center of the circle and highlights a diameter, bisected by the circle center. Any parallel may be made from this line according to the bisected-segment parallel construction above.
  2. Choose an arbitrary point D on the given circle
    • The point D should be chosen such that line OD and line OB are neither parallel nor perpendicular.
  3. Construct a line, passing through D, that is parallel to the line OB.
    • This parallel meets the given circle again at E.
    • This parallel also intersects the line m at A, defining one end of the line segment.
  4. Create two lines, OD and OE, that each pass through the given circle's center.
    • These lines meet the given circle again at points F and G, respectively.
  5. Line FG intersects the line m at C, defining the other endpoint of the line segment.
    • Segment AC now lies on line m and has midpoint B.

This construction generalizes the parallel line construction to all possible lines, not just the ones with a collinear bisected line segment.

If the requested point lies on the given line, use that line. Otherwise, to construct a parallel line of any given line, through the requested point, we combine the two previous constructions:

  1. Any line from which a parallel is to be made must have a bisected segment constructed onto it, if one does not already exist.
  2. A parallel is then constructed according to the previous parallel construction involving the collinear bisected segment.

Perpendicular to the requested line

This construction makes use of the given circle O(r) by taking advantage of Thales's theorem.

Given a line m and a point A in the plane, we wish to construct a perpendicular to line m through A:

  1. If line m does not cut the given circle in two distinct points, or it passes through the given circle's center, then a new parallel line is constructed arbitrarily such that it cuts the given circle in two distinct points but not its center, and the perpendicular is to be made from this line instead.
  2. This auxiliary line which passes through the given circle but not its center, will intersect the given circle in two points, B and C.
  3. Draw a line BO, through the circle center.
    • This line meets the given circle again at point D.
  4. Draw a line DC.
    • By Thales's theorem, ∠BCD is a right angle, so this line is perpendicular to the parallel lines, BC and m.
  5. Construct a parallel of line DC through point A using the parallel construction above.
    • This line is perpendicular to line m and passes through point A.
