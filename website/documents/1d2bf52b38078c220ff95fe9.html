Statement

Let A\in\mathbb C^{n\times n} and W\in\mathbb C^{m\times m} be invertible, and let U,V\in\mathbb C^{n\times m}, with m,n\ge1. Then \det(A+UWV^T)=\det(W^{-1}+V^TA^{-1}U)\det(W)\det(A).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Finite complex matrices; Determinants; Matrix inverses; Transpose and matrix products. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Factor the weight from the generalized identity — outline

Use the generalized determinant identity:

Suppose A is an invertible n-by-n matrix and U, V are n-by-m matrices. Then

{\displaystyle \det(\mathbf {A} +\mathbf {UV} ^{\textsf {T}})=\det(\mathbf {I_{m}} +\mathbf {V} ^{\textsf {T}}\mathbf {A} ^{-1}\mathbf {U} )\det(\mathbf {A} ).}

Given additionally an invertible m-by-m matrix W, the relationship can also be expressed as

{\displaystyle \det(\mathbf {A} +\mathbf {UWV} ^{\textsf {T}})=\det(\mathbf {W} ^{-1}+\mathbf {V} ^{\textsf {T}}\mathbf {A} ^{-1}\mathbf {U} )\det(\mathbf {W} )\det(\mathbf {A} ).}

The source states the weighted form without expanding the substitution and determinant factorization.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Substitute UW for U in the displayed determinant update and factor W in the small matrix determinant. Undergraduate linear algebra suffices.
