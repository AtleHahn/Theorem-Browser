Statement

Let n\ge1 and let F=(f_1,\ldots,f_n):(\mathbb R^n,0)\to(\mathbb R^n,0) be a real analytic map germ whose complexification has an isolated zero at 0. Write \mathcal O_{\mathbb R^n,0} for the unital real algebra of real analytic function germs at 0, including constant germs, and put

B=\mathcal O_{\mathbb R^n,0}/(f_1,\ldots,f_n),\qquad J_F=\det DF.

This is a finite-dimensional real algebra. For any real linear functional \ell:B\to\mathbb R satisfying \ell([J_F])>0, the symmetric bilinear form

\beta(a,b)=\ell(ab)\quad(a,b\in B)

is nondegenerate, and

\operatorname{ind}_0\left(\sum_{j=1}^n f_j\frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\right) =\operatorname{signature}(\beta).

The brackets denote the class in B. The signature is the number of positive eigenvalues minus the number of negative eigenvalues of any real symmetric matrix representing \beta. The local index is the topological degree of x\mapsto F(x)/\|F(x)\| from a sufficiently small sphere about 0 to the unit sphere, with the usual boundary orientations. A germ identifies functions agreeing near 0; algebraic isolation here means isolation of the complexified zero, not merely of the real zero.

Proof availability

The article states the general signature formula and works out a particular planar cubic example. It does not prove the general formula or nondegeneracy assertion. The example’s polar-coordinate and matrix calculations are not a proof for arbitrary analytic germs.
