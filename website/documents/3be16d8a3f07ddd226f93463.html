Statement

Let \Omega\subset\mathbb R^n be bounded and open with piecewise smooth boundary \partial\Omega and outward unit normal \nu. For u\in C^1(\overline\Omega) and V\in C^1(\overline\Omega;\mathbb R^n), \int_\Omega u\,\operatorname{div}V=\int_{\partial\Omega}uV\cdot\nu-\int_\Omega\nabla u\cdot V, using volume and surface measure respectively.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Multivariable differentiation; Gradient and divergence; Volume and surface integrals; Outward unit normals. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. Proof 1: Integration by parts in several variables — proof

The product rule for divergence states

\nabla\cdot(uV)=u\,\nabla\cdot V+\nabla u\cdot V.

Let \Gamma=\partial\Omega. Integrating over \Omega with respect to the standard volume form d\Omega, and applying the divergence theorem, gives

\int_\Gamma uV\cdot\nu\,d\Gamma =\int_\Omega\nabla\cdot(uV)\,d\Omega =\int_\Omega u\,\nabla\cdot V\,d\Omega+\int_\Omega\nabla u\cdot V\,d\Omega,

where \nu is the outward unit normal vector to the boundary, integrated with respect to its standard surface measure d\Gamma. Rearranging gives

\int_\Omega u\,\nabla\cdot V\,d\Omega =\int_\Gamma uV\cdot\nu\,d\Gamma-\int_\Omega\nabla u\cdot V\,d\Omega,

or in other words

\int_\Omega u\,\operatorname{div}V\,d\Omega =\int_\Gamma uV\cdot\nu\,d\Gamma-\int_\Omega\operatorname{grad}u\cdot V\,d\Omega.
