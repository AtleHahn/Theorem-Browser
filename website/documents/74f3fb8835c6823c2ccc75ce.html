Statement

Let d,r be positive integers. For i=1,\ldots,d, let T_i be a rooted tree in which every node has a finite nonzero number of children and every node is at a finite level. A node's level is its distance from the root; write T_i(n) for the nodes at level n\geq0. Color every tuple in

\bigcup_{n\geq0}\prod_{i=1}^d T_i(n)

with one of r colors. There exist infinite rooted subtrees S_i\subseteq T_i, an increasing sequence of integers \ell_0<\ell_1<\cdots, and a color c, such that:

  • The ancestor relation on each S_i is inherited from T_i, and S_i(n)\subseteq T_i(\ell_n) for every n.
  • For every s\in S_i(n) and every child t of s in T_i, exactly one child of s in S_i lies at or below t in the descendant direction (that is, is a descendant of t, possibly t itself).
  • Every tuple in \bigcup_{n\geq0}\prod_{i=1}^d S_i(n) has color c.

Here a rooted tree has a unique finite path from its root to each node; children are the next nodes on such paths. A subtree's root need not be the original root. The first two conditions say that the subtrees are strongly embedded with a common level sequence.
