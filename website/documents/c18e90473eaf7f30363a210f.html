Statement

For pairwise coprime integers n_1,\ldots,n_k>1 and N=\prod_i n_i, the map \mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\to\prod_i\mathbb Z/n_i\mathbb Z sending [x]_N to ([x]_{n_i})_i is a ring isomorphism.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory group theory supplies subgroups, quotients, actions, products and homomorphisms. Perfect means generated by commutators, torsion means finite element orders, and the chain conditions are explicit sequences of subgroups. The relevant one of these closely related definitions is a small extension, without representation theory.

Proof 1: Residue operations and a bijective tuple map — outline

Suppose that x and y give the same remainder when divided by n_i, for every i. Their difference x-y is a multiple of each n_i. As the n_i are pairwise coprime, their product N also divides x-y, and thus x and y are congruent modulo N. If x and y are supposed to be non-negative and less than N, then their difference may be a multiple of N only if x=y.

The map

x\bmod N\;\mapsto\;(x\bmod n_1,\ldots,x\bmod n_k)

maps congruence classes modulo N to sequences of congruence classes modulo n_i; adding a multiple of N does not change any of the component classes. The proof of uniqueness just given shows that this map is injective. As the domain and the codomain of this map have the same number N of elements, the map is also surjective.

The map preserves addition and multiplication of residue classes, so it defines a ring isomorphism

\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\cong \mathbb Z/n_1\mathbb Z\times\cdots\times\mathbb Z/n_k\mathbb Z.
