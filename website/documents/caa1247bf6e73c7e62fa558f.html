Statement

Let \Phi be a computable set of pairs (A,n), where A\subseteq\mathbb N is finite and n\in\mathbb N, and write \Phi(X)=\{n:\exists\text{ finite }A\subseteq X\ (A,n)\in\Phi\}. Then \Phi has a least fixed set F under inclusion, and F is computably enumerable.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Sets of integers and finite implication rules are familiar discrete objects. Computably enumerable means that an algorithm can list the members; applying finite rules until no further consequences arise specifies the least closed set. This is a small algorithmic extension.

Proof 1: Iterate the finite-rule operator from the empty set — outline

The proof is obtained by iterating the enumeration operator Φ beginning with the empty set. First, a sequence Fk is constructed, for {\displaystyle k=0,1,\ldots }. Let F0 be the empty set. Proceeding inductively, for each k, let Fk + 1 be {\displaystyle F_{k}\cup \Phi (F_{k})}. Finally, F is taken to be {\textstyle \bigcup F_{k}}. The remainder of the proof consists of a verification that F is recursively enumerable and is the least fixed point of Φ. The sequence Fk used in this proof corresponds to the Kleene chain in the proof of the Kleene fixed-point theorem.

The verification of effective enumerability, fixedness and leastness is left implicit in this source sketch.
