Statement

Let c\in\mathbb R\cup\{-\infty,+\infty\} be an endpoint of an open interval I, and let f,g:I\to\mathbb R be differentiable with g^{\prime}(x)\ne0. Limits are taken within I. Assume either f,g\to0 or |g|\to\infty as x\to c. Then, with extended real inferior and superior limits, \liminf_{x\to c}f^{\prime}(x)/g^{\prime}(x)\le\liminf_{x\to c}f(x)/g(x)\le\limsup_{x\to c}f(x)/g(x)\le\limsup_{x\to c}f^{\prime}(x)/g^{\prime}(x). No limit of the derivative ratio is assumed.

Proof 1: Bound difference quotients and pass to shrinking tails — outline

Let {\displaystyle f} and {\displaystyle g} be functions satisfying the differentiability and endpoint assumptions of this statement; no limit of the derivative ratio is assumed. Let {\displaystyle I} be the open interval in the hypothesis with endpoint {\displaystyle c}. Considering that {\displaystyle g'(x)\neq 0} on this interval and {\displaystyle g} is continuous, {\displaystyle I} can be chosen smaller so that {\displaystyle g} is nonzero on {\displaystyle I}.

For each {\displaystyle x} in the interval, define {\displaystyle m(x)=\inf {\frac {f'(t)}{g'(t)}}} and {\displaystyle M(x)=\sup {\frac {f'(t)}{g'(t)}}} as {\displaystyle t} ranges over all values between {\displaystyle x} and {\displaystyle c}. (The symbols inf and sup denote the infimum and supremum.)

From the differentiability of {\displaystyle f} and {\displaystyle g} on {\displaystyle I}, Cauchy's mean value theorem ensures that for any two distinct points {\displaystyle x} and {\displaystyle y} in {\displaystyle I} there exists a {\displaystyle \xi } between {\displaystyle x} and {\displaystyle y} such that {\displaystyle {\frac {f(x)-f(y)}{g(x)-g(y)}}={\frac {f'(\xi )}{g'(\xi )}}}. Consequently, {\displaystyle m(x)\leq {\frac {f(x)-f(y)}{g(x)-g(y)}}\leq M(x)} whenever y lies strictly between x and c. The value {\displaystyle g(x)-g(y)} is always nonzero for distinct {\displaystyle x} and {\displaystyle y} in the interval, for if it was not, the mean value theorem would imply the existence of a {\displaystyle p} between {\displaystyle x} and {\displaystyle y} such that {\displaystyle g'(p)=0}.

The definition of {\displaystyle m(x)} and {\displaystyle M(x)} will result in an extended real number, and so it is possible for them to take on the values {\displaystyle \pm \infty }. In the following two cases, {\displaystyle m(x)} and {\displaystyle M(x)} will establish bounds on the ratio {\displaystyle {\frac {f}{g}}}.

Case 1: {\displaystyle \lim _{x\to c}f(x)=\lim _{x\to c}g(x)=0}

For any {\displaystyle x} in the interval {\displaystyle I}, and point {\displaystyle y} between {\displaystyle x} and {\displaystyle c},

{\displaystyle m(x)\leq {\frac {f(x)-f(y)}{g(x)-g(y)}}={\frac {{\frac {f(x)}{g(x)}}-{\frac {f(y)}{g(x)}}}{1-{\frac {g(y)}{g(x)}}}}\leq M(x)}

and therefore as {\displaystyle y} approaches {\displaystyle c}, {\displaystyle {\frac {f(y)}{g(x)}}} and {\displaystyle {\frac {g(y)}{g(x)}}} become zero, and so

{\displaystyle m(x)\leq {\frac {f(x)}{g(x)}}\leq M(x).}

Case 2: {\displaystyle \lim _{x\to c}|g(x)|=\infty }

For every {\displaystyle x} in the interval {\displaystyle I}, define {\displaystyle S_{x}=\{y\mid y} is between {\displaystyle x} and {\displaystyle c\}}. For every point {\displaystyle y} between {\displaystyle x} and {\displaystyle c},

{\displaystyle m(x)\leq {\frac {f(y)-f(x)}{g(y)-g(x)}}={\frac {{\frac {f(y)}{g(y)}}-{\frac {f(x)}{g(y)}}}{1-{\frac {g(x)}{g(y)}}}}\leq M(x).}

As {\displaystyle y} approaches {\displaystyle c}, both {\displaystyle {\frac {f(x)}{g(y)}}} and {\displaystyle {\frac {g(x)}{g(y)}}} become zero, and therefore

{\displaystyle m(x)\leq \liminf _{y\to c,\,y\in S_{x}}{\frac {f(y)}{g(y)}}\leq \limsup _{y\to c,\,y\in S_{x}}{\frac {f(y)}{g(y)}}\leq M(x).}

The limit superior and limit inferior are necessary since the existence of the limit of {\displaystyle {\frac {f}{g}}} has not yet been established.

By the shrinking-tail definitions,

\lim_{x\to c}m(x)=\liminf_{x\to c}\frac{f'(x)}{g'(x)},\qquad \lim_{x\to c}M(x)=\limsup_{x\to c}\frac{f'(x)}{g'(x)}.

Taking the lower and upper limits in the bounds above therefore gives

\liminf_{x\to c}\frac{f'(x)}{g'(x)} \le\liminf_{x\to c}\frac{f(x)}{g(x)} \le\limsup_{x\to c}\frac{f(x)}{g(x)} \le\limsup_{x\to c}\frac{f'(x)}{g'(x)}.

All limiting values here may be extended real numbers.
