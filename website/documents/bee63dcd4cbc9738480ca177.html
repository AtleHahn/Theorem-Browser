Statement

The circle constant \pi is irrational.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The assertion that the usual circle constant is not rational uses elementary real-number and geometry terminology.

Proof 1: Use conjugate roots of the rational-pi polynomial

Use the complete-root sum lemma: Let R_1,\ldots,R_r be pairwise disjoint nonempty finite sets of distinct complex roots, each the full root set of a nonconstant integer polynomial. The numbers \sum_{\alpha\in R_t}e^\alpha, 1\le t\le r, are linearly independent over \mathbb Q. If \pi=k/n for nonzero integers k,n, the numbers i\pi,-i\pi are precisely the roots of n^2x^2+k^2, and 0 is the root of x. These disjoint sets with coefficients 1,2 give e^{i\pi}+e^{-i\pi}+2e^0\ne0. Euler’s identity makes the left side -1-1+2=0, contradiction.

Apply the fully stated exponential-sum independence lemma to two explicit integer-polynomial root sets and evaluate their exponentials by Euler. Undergraduate complex exponentials, polynomial roots and rational linear independence suffice; the transcendence-theoretic proof of the lemma is not used.
