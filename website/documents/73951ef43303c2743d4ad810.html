Proof 1: Abel limit of the integrated geometric series

Abel limit theorem

Let \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_k=S converge, with real or complex coefficients, and put G(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_kz^k for |z|<1. For any fixed finite M>1, G(z)\to S as z\to1 within |1-z|\leq M(1-|z|). In particular, G(x)\to S as real x\to1^-.

Evaluation

Let G_a(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_kz^k, where

{\displaystyle a_{k}={\frac {(-1)^{k}}{k+1}}.}

The sum {\displaystyle \sum _{k=0}^{\infty }a_{k}} converges by the alternating series test, so we can apply Abel's theorem to the corresponding generating function {\displaystyle G_{a}(z)}. We obtain

{\displaystyle G_{a}(z)={\frac {\ln(1+z)}{z}},\qquad 0<z<1,}

by integrating the uniformly convergent geometric power series term by term on {\displaystyle [-z,0]}; thus the series

{\displaystyle \sum _{k=0}^{\infty }{\frac {(-1)^{k}}{k+1}}}

converges to {\displaystyle \lim \limits _{z\to 1^{-}}G_{a}(z)=\ln 2} by Abel's theorem.

Level justification

The proof checks alternating-series convergence, integrates a uniformly convergent geometric series on a compact interval and takes a real endpoint limit. All these steps are undergraduate calculus and analysis; the more general complex approach-region statement printed above the proof is not needed for this application.
