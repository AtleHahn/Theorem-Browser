Statement

On a measure space (S,\Sigma,\mu), write \|h\|_t=(\int_S|h|^t\,d\mu)^{1/t} for 0<t<\infty, and \|h\|_\infty=\operatorname{ess\,sup}|h|. Norms may be infinite; products with a zero factor are defined to be zero, including 0\cdot\infty. Assume \mu(S)>0, p>1, and g is nonzero almost everywhere. Put A=(\int|f|^{1/p}\,d\mu)^p and B=(\int|g|^{-1/(p-1)}\,d\mu)^{-(p-1)}, allowing B=0 if that integral is infinite. Then \int|fg|\,d\mu\ge AB with 0\cdot\infty=0. If the left side is finite and B>0, equality holds iff |f|=\alpha|g|^{-p/(p-1)} almost everywhere for some \alpha\ge0. All functions appearing here are measurable real- or complex-valued functions on S.

Proof 1: Reverse Hölder inequality — outline

If \int_S |g|^{-1/(p-1)}\,d\mu=\infty, then B=0 and the claimed lower bound is zero. Otherwise this integral is finite and strictly positive, since g is nonzero almost everywhere and \mu(S)>0.

Note that p and

{\displaystyle q:={\frac {p}{p-1}}\in (1,\infty )}

are Hölder conjugates. Application of Hölder's inequality gives

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\left\||f|^{\frac {1}{p}}\right\|_{1}&=\left\||fg|^{\frac {1}{p}}\,|g|^{-{\frac {1}{p}}}\right\|_{1}\\&\leqslant \left\||fg|^{\frac {1}{p}}\right\|_{p}\left\||g|^{-{\frac {1}{p}}}\right\|_{q}\\&=\|fg\|_{1}^{\frac {1}{p}}\left\||g|^{\frac {-1}{p-1}}\right\|_{1}^{\frac {p-1}{p}}\end{aligned}}}

Raising to the power p gives us:

{\displaystyle \left\||f|^{\frac {1}{p}}\right\|_{1}^{p}\leqslant \|fg\|_{1}\left\||g|^{\frac {-1}{p-1}}\right\|_{1}^{p-1}.}

Therefore:

{\displaystyle \left\||f|^{\frac {1}{p}}\right\|_{1}^{p}\left\||g|^{\frac {-1}{p-1}}\right\|_{1}^{-(p-1)}\leqslant \|fg\|_{1}.}

The left-hand side is AB, so this is the stated inequality.

Under the stated equality hypotheses, Hölder’s equality condition applies. Since g is nonzero almost everywhere, we can have equality if and only if there exists a constant α ≥ 0 such that |fg| = α |g|−q/p almost everywhere. Solving for the absolute value of f gives the claim.
