Statement

Let X_0,X_1,\ldots be independent integrable real random variables and \mathcal F_n=\sigma(X_0,\ldots,X_n). The unique Doob decomposition is A_n=\sum_{k=1}^n(\mathbb E[X_k]-X_{k-1}) and M_n=X_0+\sum_{k=1}^n(X_k-\mathbb E[X_k]), with empty sums zero. Here a Doob decomposition means a martingale M plus an integrable predictable process A starting at zero; predictability means A_n is measurable with respect to the preceding sigma algebra.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement requires independence, expectation, generated sigma algebras and the locally defined martingale/predictable decomposition.

Proof 1: Replace past-conditional expectations by ordinary means

Independence gives \mathbb E[X_k\mid\mathcal F_{k-1}]=\mathbb E[X_k]. Substitute this equality into the Doob formulas A_n=\sum_{k=1}^n(\mathbb E[X_k\mid\mathcal F_{k-1}]-X_{k-1}) and M_n=X_0+\sum_{k=1}^n(X_k-\mathbb E[X_k\mid\mathcal F_{k-1}]).

Level justification

Independence simplifies the formulas, but their meaning requires filtrations, conditional expectations, martingales and predictable processes. These linked probability prerequisites remain advanced.
