Statement

Let r>0 and let f be holomorphic on a neighborhood of the closed disk \{z:|z|\le r\}, with Taylor series f(z)=\sum_{k\ge0}a_kz^k about zero. Then \int_0^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^2\,d\theta=2\pi\sum_{k\ge0}|a_k|^2r^{2k}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex differentiability extends the calculus derivative. Power series, circle parametrization, bounds and convergence are familiar analysis; the hypotheses and formulas are explicit, with no abstract complex geometry or distribution theory.

Proof 1: Conjugate Taylor series and Cauchy coefficients

The Cauchy Integral Formula for coefficients states that under the hypotheses in the statement:

{\displaystyle a_{n}={\frac {1}{2\pi i}}\int _{\gamma }^{}{\frac {f(z)}{z^{n+1}}}\,\mathrm {d} z}

where γ is defined to be the positively oriented circular path around the origin of radius r. Also for {\displaystyle x\in \mathbb {C} ,} we have: {\displaystyle {\overline {x}}{x}=|x|^{2}.} Applying both of these facts to the problem starting with the second fact:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\int _{0}^{2\pi }\left|f\left(re^{i\theta }\right)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm {d} \theta &=\int _{0}^{2\pi }f\left(re^{i\theta }\right){\overline {f\left(re^{i\theta }\right)}}\,\mathrm {d} \theta \\[6pt]&=\int _{0}^{2\pi }f\left(re^{i\theta }\right)\left(\sum _{k=0}^{\infty }{\overline {a_{k}\left(re^{i\theta }\right)^{k}}}\right)\,\mathrm {d} \theta &&{\text{Using Taylor expansion on the conjugate}}\\[6pt]&=\int _{0}^{2\pi }f\left(re^{i\theta }\right)\left(\sum _{k=0}^{\infty }{\overline {a_{k}}}\left(re^{-i\theta }\right)^{k}\right)\,\mathrm {d} \theta \\[6pt]&=\sum _{k=0}^{\infty }\int _{0}^{2\pi }f\left(re^{i\theta }\right){\overline {a_{k}}}\left(re^{-i\theta }\right)^{k}\,\mathrm {d} \theta &&{\text{Uniform convergence of Taylor series}}\\[6pt]&=\sum _{k=0}^{\infty }\left(2\pi {\overline {a_{k}}}r^{2k}\right)\left({\frac {1}{2{\pi }i}}\int _{0}^{2\pi }{\frac {f\left(re^{i\theta }\right)}{(re^{i\theta })^{k+1}}}{rie^{i\theta }}\,\mathrm {d} \theta\right) \\&=\sum _{k=0}^{\infty }\left(2\pi {\overline {a_{k}}}r^{2k}\right)a_{k}&&{\text{Applying Cauchy Integral Formula}}\\&={2\pi }\sum _{k=0}^{\infty }{|a_{k}|^{2}r^{2k}}\end{aligned}}}
