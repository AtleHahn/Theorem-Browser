Statement

Let F/\mathbb Q be a finite abelian Galois extension and G=\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb Q). Let m\ge1 be its conductor, the least positive integer for which F\subseteq\mathbb Q(\zeta_m), with \zeta_m a primitive mth root of unity. Put

\theta_F=\frac1m\sum_{\substack{1\le a\le m\\(a,m)=1}}a\,\operatorname{res}_F(\sigma_a^{-1})\in\mathbb Q[G],\qquad\sigma_a(\zeta_m)=\zeta_m^a.

For every \alpha\in\mathbb Z[G] for which \alpha\theta_F=\sum_{\sigma\in G}a_\sigma\sigma has integer coefficients, and every nonzero fractional ideal J of F, the fractional ideal

\prod_{\sigma\in G}\sigma(J)^{a_\sigma}

is principal. Equivalently, \theta_F\mathbb Z[G]\cap\mathbb Z[G] annihilates the ideal class group of F. The group rings consist of finite formal sums of group elements with the indicated coefficients, with multiplication extending composition. A fractional ideal is a nonzero finitely generated module over the ring of integers contained in F; principal means generated by one nonzero field element. The ideal class group is fractional ideals modulo principal fractional ideals, and G acts by applying field automorphisms to ideals. Restriction is denoted by \operatorname{res}_F.

Proof 1: Gauss sums and cyclotomic descent (external)

The following editorial argument completes the stated scope. A supporting source is Stickelberger Ideal (Section 3.5, pp. 36–39, culminating in Theorem 3.19; Sections 3.2–3.4 supply Gauss-sum factorization.).

Apply Section 3.5 and Theorem 3.19. Lemma 3.11 permits representatives prime to m. Thus P in the proof is a squarefree product of primes not dividing m (retain multiplicities in the ideal factorization, but not in P). Lemma 3.18 is needed only for n=mP. Add the distinct prime divisors of P one at a time, so each new prime p is coprime to the current conductor and the ramification degree p-1 used in its induction is valid; omit the overgeneral statement for repeated conductor primes. In Proposition 3.17 replace the discriminant equivalence by this local argument: away from n, valuation(a) is divisible by n, so after removing an nth power the radicand is a unit u; T^n-u has invertible derivative over the local integers, hence defines an etale algebra and its field factors are unramified. This proves precisely the required assertion without supposing T^n-a is irreducible or that its polynomial discriminant equals the field discriminant. These two local replacements complete the source’s Gauss-sum descent argument. For m=1 the field is Q and its class group is trivial.
