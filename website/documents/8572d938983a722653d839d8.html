Statement

Let f be holomorphic on an open set U\subset\mathbb C. For any closed disk centered at c of radius r>0 contained in U, with positively oriented circle \gamma, all derivatives exist in its interior and f^{(k)}(z)=\frac{k!}{2\pi i}\int_\gamma\frac{f(w)}{(w-z)^{k+1}}\,dw\quad(|z-c|<r). In particular f is infinitely complex differentiable on U.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Real analysis and calculus supply limits, derivatives, series and integration. Complex differentiability, primitives and logarithms are direct extensions, and circle integrals are parametrized ordinary integrals. The full claims use no Riemann surfaces, residues or abstract differential forms.

Proof 1: Differentiate Cauchy’s integral formula — outline

Let r>0 such that the closed disk centered at c with radius r is contained in U. Then Cauchy’s integral formula, with the positive parametrization \gamma(t)=c+re^{it}, t\in[0,2\pi], gives for |z-c|<r

{\displaystyle f(z)={\frac {1}{2\pi i}}\int _{\gamma }{\frac {f(w)}{w-z}}\,dw,\quad f'(z)={\frac {1}{2\pi i}}\int _{\gamma }{\frac {f(w)}{(w-z)^{2}}}\,dw,\quad \ldots ,\quad f^{(k)}(z)={\frac {k!}{2\pi i}}\int _{\gamma }{\frac {f(w)}{(w-z)^{k+1}}}\,dw.}

Here the integrands are continuous on the circle. Keep this circle fixed while z varies in a smaller interior neighborhood; the denominators are bounded away from zero, so differentiation under the integral sign is justified. In particular, if f is once complex differentiable on the open set U, then it is actually infinitely many times complex differentiable on U.
