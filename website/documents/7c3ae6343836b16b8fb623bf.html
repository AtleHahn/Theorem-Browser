Statement

Let D be a circular disk of positive radius in the Euclidean plane, let p be an interior point of D, and let n\geq8 be an integer divisible by 4. Choose a line through p, and form n/2 lines through p by rotating it successively through angles 2\pi/n. These lines divide D into n pieces whose angles at p are all 2\pi/n.

Number the pieces D_1,\ldots,D_n consecutively around p, in either direction. Then

\sum_{j\text{ odd}}\operatorname{area}(D_j)=\sum_{j\text{ even}}\operatorname{area}(D_j).
Level
High school only
Level justification

Only elementary planar geometry, rotations and finite area sums are needed.

Proof 1: Polar area and cancellation of roots of unity (external)

The following editorial argument completes the stated scope. A supporting source is The Pizza Theorem (Theorem and proof pp. 1–2, through four-radius-square identity before Corollary.).

The following extension of the polar argument handles all stated slice counts: let the disk radius be R and its center have distance d from p and angular direction phi. The positive radial distance is r(theta)=d cos(theta-phi)+sqrt(R^2-d^2 sin(theta-phi)^2), so r(theta)^2+r(theta+pi)^2=2(R^2+d^2 cos(2(theta-phi))). Let s(theta) be the alternating +1/-1 sign of consecutive sectors. Since n is divisible by 4, s(theta+pi)=s(theta); the area difference is the integral over [0,pi] of s(theta)[R^2+d^2 cos(2(theta-phi))]. The sign has period h=4pi/n and zero mean on each period. There are m=n/4>=2 such periods in [0,pi]. The constant term integrates to zero. For the cosine term, summing the translated periods multiplies its complex exponential by sum_(j=0)^(m-1) exp(2ijh)=0. Thus the area difference is zero. This handles n congruent to 4 modulo 8 as well as multiples of 8.
