Statement

For integers n\ge0 and 0\le k\le n, write \binom nk=n!/(k!(n-k)!), with 0!=1. Then \binom nk=\binom n{n-k}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The identity compares two explicitly defined factorial ratios.

Proof 1: Compare coefficients after interchanging the variables

Use the binomial theorem:

{\displaystyle (x+y)^{n}=\sum _{k=0}^{n}{\binom {n}{k}}x^{n-k}y^{k}=\sum _{k=0}^{n}{\binom {n}{k}}x^{k}y^{n-k}.}

The final expression follows from the previous one by the symmetry of x and y in the first expression, and by comparison it follows that the sequence of binomial coefficients in the formula is symmetric, {\textstyle {\binom {n}{k}}={\binom {n}{n-k}}.}

Explicitly,

(x+y)^n=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk x^{n-k}y^k =\sum_{k'=0}^n\binom n{k'}x^{k'}y^{n-k'},

and the coefficient of the same monomial in the left and right-hand side expressions of the second equality must be the same; for x^{n-k}y^k=x^{k'}y^{n-k'}, so k'=n-k, \binom nk=\binom n{k'}=\binom n{n-k}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Interchanging the variables leaves (x+y)^n unchanged. Comparing the coefficient of the same monomial in the two binomial expansions gives the symmetry. Finite polynomial expansion and coefficient comparison are elementary algebra.
