Statement

Let \nabla be the Levi-Civita connection on a Riemannian or pseudo-Riemannian manifold. For vector fields define \nabla^2_{X,Y}Z=\nabla_X\nabla_YZ-\nabla_{\nabla_XY}Z and R(X,Y)Z=\nabla_X\nabla_YZ-\nabla_Y\nabla_XZ-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z. Then R(X,Y)Z=\nabla^2_{X,Y}Z-\nabla^2_{Y,X}Z.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Levi-Civita connections; Second covariant derivatives; Curvature. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Subtract the two second-derivative expressions — outline

Since the Levi-Civita connection is torsion-free, its curvature can also be expressed in terms of the second covariant derivative

{\textstyle \nabla _{X,Y}^{2}Z=\nabla _{X}\nabla _{Y}Z-\nabla _{\nabla _{X}Y}Z}

which depends only on the values of {\displaystyle X,Y} at a point. The curvature can then be written as

{\displaystyle R(X,Y)=\nabla _{X,Y}^{2}-\nabla _{Y,X}^{2}}

Thus, the curvature tensor measures the noncommutativity of the second covariant derivative.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Subtract the defined covariant derivatives and use vanishing torsion. Understanding connections on tangent bundles, vector-field brackets and curvature requires substantial differential geometry even though the manipulation is immediate.
