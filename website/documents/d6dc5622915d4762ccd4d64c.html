Statement

The notation \mathrm K_{j=1}^{\infty}a/b means the limit of the finite nested fractions a/(b+a/(b+\cdots+a/b)). Finite convergents are evaluated as rational functions after simplification; convergence here means that they are eventually finite and tend to a finite complex value. For z\in\mathbb C, the fraction y=1+\mathop{\mathrm K}_{j=1}^{\infty}z/1 converges exactly when z does not belong to the real interval (-\infty,-1/4). In the convergent case its limit is the root of w^2-w-z=0 with greater modulus, with the common root 1/2 selected at z=-1/4.

Proof 1: Specialize the periodic fixed-point criterion — outline

By applying the periodic continued-fraction convergence criterion, it can be shown that

y=1+\cfrac{z}{1+\cfrac{z}{1+\cfrac{z}{1+\ddots}}}

converges for every complex number z except when z is a negative real number and z<-1/4. Moreover, this continued fraction y converges to the particular value of

y=\frac12\left(1\pm\sqrt{4z+1}\right)

that has the larger absolute value. When z is real and z<-1/4, the two fixed points of the linear fractional transformation generating y have equal moduli and y diverges by oscillation. At z=-1/4 the roots coincide, a convergent case of the periodic criterion, and the limit is 1/2. The source does not expand the specialization of the criterion or the immediate zero-numerator case z=0.
