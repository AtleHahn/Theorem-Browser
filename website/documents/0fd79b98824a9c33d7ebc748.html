Statement

Let \theta=\sqrt[3]2 be real, \omega=(-1+i\sqrt3)/2, and K=\mathbb Q(\theta,\omega), the splitting field of X^3-2. Then [K:\mathbb Q]=6 and a rational basis is 1,\theta,\theta^2,\omega,\omega\theta,\omega\theta^2.

Proof 1: Multiply the cubic and quadratic tower degrees — outline

Consider the splitting field K of the irreducible polynomial {\displaystyle x^{3}-2} over {\displaystyle \mathbb {Q} }; that is, {\displaystyle K=\mathbb {Q} (\theta ,\omega )} where θ is a cube root of 2, and ω is a cube root of 1 (but not 1 itself). If we consider K inside the complex numbers, we may take {\displaystyle \theta ={\sqrt[{3}]{2}}}, the real cube root of 2, and {\displaystyle \omega =-{\tfrac {1}{2}}+i{\tfrac {\sqrt {3}}{2}}.} Since \mathbb Q(\theta) is real and \omega is nonreal, ω has minimal polynomial {\displaystyle x^{2}+x+1} over \mathbb Q(\theta), and the extension {\displaystyle \mathbb {Q} \subset K} has degree:

{\displaystyle [\,K:\mathbb {Q} \,]=[\,K:\mathbb {Q} (\,\theta \,)\,]\cdot [\,\mathbb {Q} (\,\theta \,):\mathbb {Q} \,]=2\cdot 3=6,}

with {\displaystyle \mathbb {Q} }-basis {\displaystyle \{1,\theta ,\theta ^{2},\omega ,\omega \theta ,\omega \theta ^{2}\}} obtained by multiplying the bases 1,\theta,\theta^2 and 1,\omega in the tower.
