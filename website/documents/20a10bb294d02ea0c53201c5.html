Statement

Fix a standard finite-alphabet proof calculus for Peano arithmetic (PA), and a positive integer N. Let B_N(m) be its standard arithmetical assertion that a PA-proof of the sentence with code m uses fewer than N symbols. Let G_N be the diagonal sentence with \mathrm{PA}\vdash G_N\leftrightarrow\neg B_N(\ulcorner G_N\urcorner). If PA is consistent, then PA proves G_N, but every PA-proof of G_N has at least N symbols.

Proof 1: Self-reference followed by exhaustive finite proof checking (outline)

The sentence G_N says:

This statement cannot be proved in Peano arithmetic in fewer than N symbols.

If PA is consistent, then it cannot prove the statement in fewer than N symbols, because the existence of such a proof would itself be a theorem of PA, a contradiction to G_N and its fixed-point equivalence. But simply enumerating all strings of length less than N and checking that each such string is not a proof (in PA) of the statement yields a proof of the statement, which necessarily has at least N symbols. These finite checks are carried out in PA and establish \neg B_N(\ulcorner G_N\urcorner); the fixed-point equivalence gives G_N.
