Statement

Let ABC be a nondegenerate Euclidean triangle, with A^{\prime},B^{\prime},C^{\prime} on the lines BC,CA,AB, respectively. Suppose the three circumcircles of AB^{\prime}C^{\prime}, A^{\prime}BC^{\prime} and A^{\prime}B^{\prime}C are defined, and the first and third share a point M distinct from A^{\prime},B^{\prime},C^{\prime}. Then M lies on the circle A^{\prime}BC^{\prime}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The conditional incidence statement uses only triangles, side lines and common points of circumcircles.

Proof 1: Add the two supplied cyclic-angle relations

Use directed angles modulo \pi to express the source's cyclic-quadrilateral calculation independently of the placement of the side points. Because A'B'CM and AB'C'M are cyclic,

\angle A'MB'=\angle A'CB',\qquad \angle B'MC'=\angle B'AC'.

Adding gives

\angle A'MC'=\angle BCA+\angle CAB=-\angle ABC=\angle A'BC'\pmod\pi.

Thus A',B,C',M are concyclic, as required. In the source's pictured placement the same calculation is written \angle A'MC'=2\pi-(\pi-C)-(\pi-A)=\pi-B.
