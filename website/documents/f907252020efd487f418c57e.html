Statement

Every finite group is isomorphic to the automorphism group of a finite distributive lattice, where lattice automorphisms preserve meet and join.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A finite distributive lattice is a finite ordered set with meet and join satisfying distributivity. Its automorphisms preserve those operations, directly analogous to familiar algebraic automorphisms. This limited definition makes the finite-group realization claim accessible.

Proof 1: Finite groups occur as automorphism groups of finite distributive lattices — outline

By Frucht's theorem, every finite group can be realized as the automorphism group of a finite simple graph. From the facts that every graph can be reconstructed from the containment partial order of its edges and vertices, and that every finite partial order is equivalent by Birkhoff's representation theorem to a finite distributive lattice, it follows that every finite group can be realized as the symmetries of a distributive lattice. These representations preserve automorphisms.

Lattice meet/join and lower sets are concrete finite order constructions, a small extension of undergraduate groups and partial orders.
