Statement

Let X be a real vector space, M\subseteq X a vector subspace, f:M\to\mathbb R linear, and p:X\to\mathbb R sublinear: p(u+v)\le p(u)+p(v) and p(tu)=tp(u) for t\ge0. Assume f\le p on M. For x\in X\setminus M, all linear extensions to M\oplus\mathbb Rx dominated by p are F_b(m+rx)=f(m)+rb, where a\le b\le c, a=\sup_{n\in M}[-p(-n-x)-f(n)] and c=\inf_{m\in M}[p(m+x)-f(m)]. The endpoints are finite and a\le c.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra supplies a one-dimensional extension of a subspace. Analysis supplies infima and suprema, while convexity/sublinearity is explicitly defined by inequalities. These direct conditions suffice without topological vector-space or dual theory.

Proof 1: All one-dimensional sublinear dominated extensions

Given any real number b, the map F_b:M\oplus\mathbb Rx\to\mathbb R defined by F_b(m+rx)=f(m)+rb is always a linear extension of f to M\oplus\mathbb Rx but it might not satisfy F_b\le p. It will be shown that b can always be chosen so as to guarantee that F_b\le p.

If m,n\in M then

f(m)-f(n)=f(m-n)\le p(m-n)=p(m+x-x-n)\le p(m+x)+p(-x-n)

which implies

-p(-n-x)-f(n)\le p(m+x)-f(m).

So define

a=\sup_{n\in M}[-p(-n-x)-f(n)] \qquad\text{and}\qquad c=\inf_{m\in M}[p(m+x)-f(m)]

where a\le c are real numbers. To guarantee F_b\le p, it suffices that a\le b\le c (in fact, this is also necessary) because then b satisfies “the decisive inequality”

-p(-n-x)-f(n)\le b\le p(m+x)-f(m) \qquad\text{for all }m,n\in M.

To see that f(m)+rb\le p(m+rx) follows, assume r\ne0 and substitute \frac1r m in for both m and n to obtain

-p\left(-\frac1r m-x\right)-\frac1r f(m) \le b\le p\left(\frac1r m+x\right)-\frac1r f(m).

If r>0 (respectively, if r<0) then the right (respectively, the left) hand side equals \frac1r[p(m+rx)-f(m)] so that multiplying by r gives rb\le p(m+rx)-f(m). For r=0 the inequality is the hypothesis f\le p on M.

Conversely, domination at m+x and -n-x forces a\le b\le c. If G:M\oplus\mathbb Rx\to\mathbb R is any linear extension of f to M\oplus\mathbb Rx then G=F_b for b:=G(x). In other words, every linear extension of f to M\oplus\mathbb Rx is of the form F_b for some unique b.

Level justification

Compare every candidate lower slope with every upper slope using subadditivity, choose between their real suprema and infima, and check positive and negative scalars separately. Undergraduate linear algebra and real analysis suffice; no topological duality theory is needed.
