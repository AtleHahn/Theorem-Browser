Statement

Let n\ge3 and let \theta:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R be a differentiable field for the energy E(\psi)=\int_{\mathbb R^n}(|\nabla\psi|^2+f(\psi))\,dx, where f is differentiable and f'(0)=0. Assume the displayed integrals are finite, I_1=\int|\nabla\theta|^2\,dx>0, and dilations \theta_\lambda(x)=\theta(\lambda x) for positive \lambda near one are admissible variations. If \theta is stationary for E, then \left.\frac{d^2}{d\lambda^2}E(\theta_\lambda)\right|_{\lambda=1}<0. Thus it cannot minimize energy among these nearby dilations.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The energy is an explicitly given multivariable integral and the admissible dilation family is specified. Stationarity means zero first derivative along admissible variations; this is a brief extension of the derivative test to a function-valued argument. Understanding the full assertion requires gradients and change of variables, but not Euler–Lagrange equations or general second-variation theory.

Proof 1: Differentiate the dilation energy — outline

First take dimension three and write E_\lambda=E(\theta_\lambda).

Define {\displaystyle \theta _{\lambda }(x)=\theta (\lambda x)\,} where {\displaystyle \lambda } is a positive constant, and write {\displaystyle I_{1}=\int (\nabla \theta )^{2}d^{3}x}, {\displaystyle I_{2}=\int f(\theta )d^{3}x}. Then

{\displaystyle E_{\lambda }=\int \left[(\nabla \theta _{\lambda })^{2}+f(\theta _{\lambda })\right]\,d^{3}x=I_{1}/\lambda +I_{2}/\lambda ^{3}.}

Stationarity along the admissible dilations gives {\displaystyle dE_{\lambda }/d\lambda \vert _{\lambda =1}=-I_{1}-3I_{2}=0.\,} and since {\displaystyle I_{1}>0\,},

{\displaystyle \left.{\frac {d^{2}E_{\lambda }}{d\lambda ^{2}}}\right|_{\lambda =1}=2I_{1}+12I_{2}=-2I_{1}\,<0.}

That is, {\displaystyle \delta ^{2}E<0\,} for a variation corresponding to a uniform stretching of the particle. Hence the energy is not a local minimum along this admissible dilation.

Derrick's argument works for {\displaystyle x\in \mathbb {R} ^{n}}, {\displaystyle n\geq 3\,}.

The source leaves the dimension-dependent scaling and derivative calculation for n>3 implicit.

Stationarity means its ordinary first derivative is zero; substitution into its second derivative gives -2(n-2)I1<0. This exact argument uses multivariable calculus and a one-parameter restriction, not general second-variation theory.
