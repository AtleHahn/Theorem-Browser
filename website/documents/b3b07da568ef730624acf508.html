Statement

Let K be a nonempty compact topological space and let f:K\to\mathbb R be continuous. Then f is bounded and there are p,q\in K such that f(p)=\sup f(K) and f(q)=\inf f(K).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Compactness and continuity are standard basic topology, and suprema and infima are standard real analysis. No additional concepts are required.

Mathematical Field
Point-set topology

none

Proof 1: Take the compact image in the real line — outline

The continuous image of a compact space is compact, so f(K) is compact in \mathbb R. By Heine–Borel it is closed and bounded. A nonempty closed bounded subset of \mathbb R contains its supremum and infimum. Those values belong to f(K) and are therefore attained by f.
