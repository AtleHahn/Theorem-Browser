Simpler proof assessment

Likely alternative prerequisite level: High school only; lowest recorded proof level: Undergraduate level. Replace the smallest layer of the antichain by its upper shadow, repeating until every set has size r. No new shadow set coincides with a larger existing member or lies beneath one, since that would violate the original antichain property; intersection is preserved by enlargement. Double counting incidences gives shadow size at least layer size for sizes below r<=n/2. Apply the finite cyclic-permutation proof of uniform EKR (or its explicitly stated finite-subset bound). All steps are finite counting; no symmetric-chain decomposition is required.

Statement

Let integers n,r satisfy n\ge2r\ge2. An intersecting family of nonempty subsets of an n-element set, each of size at most r and with no member contained in another, has at most \binom{n-1}{r-1} members.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement concerns finite families with local noncontainment and intersection conditions.

Mathematical Field
Combinatorics
Required topics

Review notes
none

Proof 1: Enlarge within symmetric chains — outline

Use a symmetric chain decomposition of all subsets: each chain contains nested sets of successive sizes k,k+1,\ldots,n-k. Choose the enlarged r-element set from the same chain as each member of the antichain. The enlarged family remains intersecting and has the same size. Apply the EKR bound for r-element families.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the supplied symmetric-chain decomposition to enlarge each antichain member to a distinct r-set, then apply EKR. Undergraduate discrete mathematics suffices with the explicitly described chain decomposition as a small finite-order extension.
