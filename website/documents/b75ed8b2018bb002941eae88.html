Statement

Let X\ge0 be a random variable on a probability space with 0<\mathbb E[X^2]<\infty. Then \mathbb P(X>0)\ge(\mathbb EX)^2/\mathbb E[X^2].

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Expectation and finite second moments are scalar integral-average definitions, a limited extension of undergraduate analysis.

Mathematical Field
Probability theory
Required topics

Proof 1: Cauchy–Schwarz with the positivity indicator

Since X=X\mathbf1_{\{X>0\}}, Cauchy–Schwarz yields

(\mathbb EX)^2=(\mathbb E[X\mathbf1_{\{X>0\}}])^2\le\mathbb E[X^2]\,\mathbb E[\mathbf1_{\{X>0\}}^2]=\mathbb E[X^2]\,\mathbb P(X>0).

Divide by the positive finite second moment.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the expectation form of Cauchy-Schwarz to X and a single indicator, then divide. Undergraduate probability with finite second moments suffices; no Hilbert-space construction or general integration theorem is used.
