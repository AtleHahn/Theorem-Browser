Statement

There exist an integer n\geq1, real random vectors X=(X_1,\ldots,X_n) and Y=(Y_1,\ldots,Y_n) with finite second moments, and real thresholds u_1,\ldots,u_n, such that

\mathbb E[X_i]=\mathbb E[Y_i]=0,\qquad \mathbb E[X_i^2]=\mathbb E[Y_i^2]\quad(1\leq i\leq n),\mathbb E[X_iX_j]\leq\mathbb E[Y_iY_j]\quad(i\ne j),

but

\Pr(X_i\leq u_i\text{ for all }i)>\Pr(Y_i\leq u_i\text{ for all }i).

Here \mathbb E denotes expectation and \Pr probability. Thus matching means and variances and ordering the off-diagonal covariances do not by themselves imply Slepian's lower-orthant probability comparison for arbitrary random vectors.
