Statement

Every finitely generated vector space over a field K is isomorphic to K^n for a finite n\geq0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory ring/group theory and linear algebra supply polynomial coefficients, ideals, determinants, bases and matrix products. Elementary transvections are explicitly given elementary row-operation matrices, and division ring means every nonzero element has an inverse. These direct definitions suffice without specialist theory.

Proof 1: Specialize invariant factors to a field

The invariant-factor theorem states that a finitely generated module over a principal ideal domain R is isomorphic to a finite direct sum of cyclic modules R/(d_i), where the ideals (d_i) are proper.

This includes the classification of finite-dimensional vector spaces as a special case, where R=K. Since fields have no non-trivial ideals, every finitely generated vector space is free: all occurring ideals must be zero, and one obtains a direct sum of one-dimensional subspaces.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Specialize the module invariant-factor theorem to a field. Although the final ideal observation is elementary, understanding the invoked general PID module classification and cyclic quotient decomposition is not confidently a small extension of introductory algebra.
