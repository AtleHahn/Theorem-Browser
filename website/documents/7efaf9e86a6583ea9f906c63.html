Statement

Let \Omega\subset\mathbb R^n be a compact measurable set. Let \xi:\Omega\to\mathbb R^n, let \varphi be a real function whose gradient is defined on \Omega, and let f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R. Suppose \xi=(\nabla\varphi)\circ\sigma and \sigma:\Omega\to\Omega preserves Lebesgue measure. Whenever the indicated compositions are integrable, \int_\Omega f(\xi(x))\,dx=\int_\Omega f(\nabla\varphi(x))\,dx.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The stated maps and integral identities use probability measures or Lebesgue measure theory, beyond the calculus/linear-algebra baseline.

none

Proof 1: Apply measure preservation to the composed test function

Substituting the factorization, the following holds:

\int_\Omega f(\xi(x))\,dx =\int_\Omega f((\nabla\varphi)\circ\sigma(x))\,dx =\int_\Omega f(\nabla\varphi(x))\,dx,

where the fact that \sigma preserves Lebesgue measure is used in the second equality, applied to the integrable function f\circ\nabla\varphi.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Substitute the factorization and apply measure-preserving integral invariance to an arbitrary integrable composition. General Lebesgue integration and measure-preserving transformations require graduate analysis.
