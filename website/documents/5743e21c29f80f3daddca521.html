Philosophical significance
Major

First-order sentences cannot enforce finiteness while permitting arbitrarily large finite models. This exposes a limitation of finite linguistic descriptions of structure and the passage from finite possibilities to infinite models.

Statement

If a first-order theory T has finite models of arbitrarily large cardinality, then it has an infinite model.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

First-order satisfaction and cardinalities of models require model theory and set theory beyond the elementary baselines.

Proof 1: Repeat the distinct-constant construction — outline

As in the source upward construction, adjoin constants c_0,c_1,\ldots and all inequalities c_i\ne c_j for i\ne j. Every finite subset involves finitely many such constants and is satisfiable in a sufficiently large finite model of T. By first-order compactness, the whole augmented theory has a model. Its infinitely many distinct constant interpretations make its reduct an infinite model of T.

Level justification

Retain Beyond undergraduate provisionally under the conservative criterion. The finite counting is easy, but following the argument in its full generality requires first-order structures and satisfaction of theories, language expansion by new constants, reducts and the model-existence meaning of logical compactness. Without an assumed logic elective, that combined semantic framework cannot confidently be fitted into the small additional-preparation allowance. Elementary substructures are not used.

Kind:

Theorem

Treatment:

Cited without proof

Statement used:

If every finite subset of a set of first-order sentences has a model, then the whole set has a model, also in a language expanded by additional constants.

Application in proof:

Apply to T together with c_i not equal c_j for all distinct nonnegative i,j; a sufficiently large finite model of T realizes each finite set of inequalities.

Application level:

Beyond undergraduate level

Level justification:

Checking this application requires the semantics of theories in an expanded first-order language, not topological compactness.

Review notes:

For a finite subset only finitely many constants need distinct interpretations. The resulting model of all inequalities has an infinite underlying set, and forgetting the constants preserves satisfaction of T.
