Statement

Let K be a field. Write \mathcal P(K)=\bigcup_{n\ge1}K((T^{1/n})), where K((T^{1/n})) consists of formal series \sum_{k\ge k_0}c_kT^{k/n} with k_0\in\mathbb Z and c_k\in K. For v(f) the smallest exponent of nonzero f and v(0)=\infty, put d(f,g)=e^{-v(f-g)}. The field \mathcal P(K) is not complete for this metric.

Proof 1: A Cauchy sequence with unbounded denominators — outline

The source gives the partial sums s_N=\sum_{k=1}^N T^{k+1/k}. The exponents strictly increase and tend to infinity, so v(s_M-s_N) tends to infinity as M>N\to\infty. Hence (s_N) is Cauchy. Its coefficientwise limit must contain every displayed term. The reduced denominator of k+1/k is k; these are unbounded. Thus no Puiseux series can be the limit.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the least-exponent valuation to make the partial sums Cauchy and exclude any limit with a common exponent denominator. Formal Laurent fields and their non-Archimedean valuation topology together are not confidently a small extension of the baseline, despite the explicit counterexample; retain beyond undergraduate provisionally.
