Statement

There is an absolute constant C>0 such that, for every finite planar simple undirected graph G=(V,E) with n=|V|, there is a partition into pairwise disjoint sets

V=A\sqcup S\sqcup B

with

|A|\leq\frac{2n}{3},\qquad |B|\leq\frac{2n}{3},\qquad |S|\leq C\sqrt n,

and with no edge having one endpoint in A and the other in B. Neither A nor B is required to induce a connected subgraph. The constant C is independent of the graph and n.

A graph is planar if it can be drawn in the plane with edge interiors disjoint from one another and all vertices. The set S is a vertex separator.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement uses finite vertex sets, adjacency, cardinalities and square roots. Big-O notation is replaced by an explicit universal constant, requiring no asymptotic-analysis course.

Proof 1: Circle packing, a random spherical cut and balance adjustment — outline

By the circle-packing theorem stated in the source, represent the n vertices by disks with disjoint interiors, with adjacency exactly tangency. The source’s spherical normalization step maps this packing by stereographic projection onto the unit sphere so that every plane through the sphere’s center leaves at most 3n/4 of the caps wholly on either side. Use this normalization as a geometric premise; its construction is not proved in the article.

Choose a uniformly random plane through the center. The source uses two geometric estimates: for suitable nonnegative radius parameters r_i, the crossing probability of cap i is at most a r_i, and \sum_i r_i^2\leq b, where a,b are absolute constants. The latter comes from the disjoint caps’ bounded total area. Thus the expected number N of crossed caps satisfies

\mathbb E N\leq a\sum_{i=1}^n r_i \leq a\sqrt{n\sum_{i=1}^n r_i^2}\leq a\sqrt{bn}.

The middle inequality is the elementary sum-of-squares inequality (the source phrases this step using Jensen’s inequality). Consequently some plane crosses at most c\sqrt n caps for an absolute constant c.

Delete the corresponding vertices, including any cap meeting the cut. The remaining caps lie wholly on its two sides, and disks on opposite sides cannot be tangent without meeting the cut. The two vertex sets therefore have no edges between them, and each has at most 3n/4 vertices. This gives the source’s three-quarters separator.

Use the source’s repeated splitting and regrouping to obtain the required two-thirds balance. Apply this construction to the induced subgraphs on both sides and repeat for four rounds, stopping a branch if its size is already at most n/3. There are at most 1+2+4+8=15 splits, so the union S of the deleted sets has size at most 15c\sqrt n. Each surviving piece has size at most (3/4)^4 n<n/3, unless it already met the stopping threshold, and distinct pieces have no edges between them.

If the total number of surviving vertices is below n/3, put them all in A and let B be empty. Otherwise accumulate entire pieces into A until its size first reaches n/3. Because every piece has size at most n/3, this gives |A|\leq2n/3. Put the remaining pieces in B; then |B|\leq n-|A|\leq2n/3. This proves the asserted partition with an absolute separator constant. The empty graph is immediate.
