Statement

Let \zeta denote the meromorphic continuation of \sum_{n\ge1}n^{-s} from \operatorname{Re}s>1. Every zero other than a negative even integer lies in 0<\operatorname{Re}s<1.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The conclusion classifies zeros of the meromorphic zeta function by their complex real parts.

Proof 1: Nontrivial zeta zeros lie in the open critical strip (outline)

Use the functional equation

\zeta(s)=2^s\pi^{s-1}\sin\left(\frac{\pi s}{2}\right)\Gamma(1-s)\zeta(1-s)

and the value \zeta(0)=-1/2.

The Hadamard–de la Vallée-Poussin theorem states that no zeros lie on the line \operatorname{Re}(s)=1. Together with the functional equation and the fact that there are no zeros with real part greater than 1, this shows that all nontrivial zeros must lie in the interior of the critical strip

0<\operatorname{Re}(s)<1.

Here the nontrivial zeros are those other than the negative even integers.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Combine two zero-free regions with the meromorphic functional equation and its gamma/sine factors. The outline requires analytic continuation and special-function zero/pole behavior in addition to ordinary complex analysis; this is not confidently a small extension, so retain beyond undergraduate.
