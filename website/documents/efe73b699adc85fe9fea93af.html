Statement

Let p be a prime, and suppose every element of a group G has finite order a power of p (including p^0=1 for the identity). Every finite subgroup H of G has order p^n for some integer n\ge0. In particular, this applies to G itself if it is finite.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory group and ring theory supplies subgroups, quotients, commutators and homomorphisms. Any added terms such as perfect, nilpotent or virtually free describe explicit subgroup conditions; they are small algebraic extensions without representation or geometric-group theory.

Proof 1: Exclude other prime divisors using Cauchy — outline

Cauchy’s theorem states that if a prime q divides the order of a finite group, that group has an element of order q. If a prime q divides |H|, Cauchy’s theorem applied to H produces an element of order q. The hypothesis forces this prime order to be a power of p, so q=p. Thus the finite order |H| has no prime divisors other than p and is a power of p, including |H|=1.

Level justification

Apply Cauchy to each prime divisor of the finite subgroup order and use the prescribed element orders. This is introductory finite group theory and elementary factorization.
