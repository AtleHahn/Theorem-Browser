Statement

Let C be a smooth projective geometrically integral curve over a field k, and let \mathfrak m be an effective divisor on C. Its generalized Jacobian J_{\mathfrak m} is a connected commutative algebraic group, and the natural map to the ordinary Jacobian fits into an exact sequence of algebraic groups

0\longrightarrow L_{\mathfrak m}\longrightarrow J_{\mathfrak m}\longrightarrow J_0\longrightarrow0,

where L_{\mathfrak m} is affine and J_0 is an abelian variety. Such sequences need not split: there are choices of curve and modulus for which no algebraic group homomorphism s:J_0\to J_{\mathfrak m} is a section of the displayed map. Here an effective divisor is a finite sum of closed points with nonnegative integer multiplicities, also viewed as the associated finite closed subscheme. For nonzero \mathfrak m, J_{\mathfrak m} is the degree-zero generalized Picard group scheme: it parameterizes degree-zero line bundles on C together with a trivialization on \mathfrak m, in families, up to isomorphism, with the usual faithfully-flat sheaf convention. Tensor product is the group operation; the map to J_0 forgets the trivialization. For \mathfrak m=0 use the ordinary degree-zero Picard scheme J_0=\operatorname{Pic}^0_{C/k}. A line bundle is a locally free sheaf of rank one, and a trivialization is an isomorphism with the structure sheaf on the indicated subscheme. Exactness is scheme-theoretic: the first group is the kernel and the last map is surjective as a faithfully-flat sheaf. An affine group has affine underlying scheme; an abelian variety is a smooth geometrically connected proper group variety.
