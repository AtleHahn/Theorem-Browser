Statement

Let F\subset\mathbb R^n be nonempty and closed, let x\in\mathbb R^n, and let z\in F minimize |x-z|. Call v an exterior normal at z if v=\nabla g(z) for some real C^1 function g defined near z and attaining a local maximum on F at z. Then 2(x-z) is an exterior normal at z.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Euclidean nearest points; continuously differentiable functions; gradients; constrained local maxima. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Differentiate a maximizing negative quadratic

The function g(y)=-|y-x|^2 has a local maximum on F at the closest point z. Its derivative at z is 2(x-z), so this vector is an exterior normal under the stated definition.
