Statement

Let k be a field, K\supseteq k an algebraically closed extension, and J a proper ideal of k[X_1,\ldots,X_n]. Then the polynomials in J have a common zero in K^n.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory ring theory supplies polynomial ideals and maximality. Algebraically closed means every nonconstant one-variable polynomial has a root; the extension here simply contains the coefficient field. The claims concern simultaneous polynomial zeros, requiring no Galois arithmetic or schemes.

Proof 1: Apply the strong theorem to the constant polynomial one

Use the strong Nullstellensatz in its base-field form: if p\in k[X_1,\ldots,X_n] vanishes on the common zero set V(J)\subseteq K^n, then p^r\in J for some r\geq1.

If the polynomials in J have no common zero in {\displaystyle K^{n},} then {\displaystyle \mathrm {V} (J)=\emptyset ,} and {\displaystyle p(a)=0} for all {\displaystyle a\in \mathrm {V} (J)} is vacuously true for any {\displaystyle p\in k[X_{1},\dots ,X_{n}],} including the constant polynomial 1, so by the strong Nullstellensatz, we have {\displaystyle 1^{r}\in J,} for some natural number r, but that means {\displaystyle 1\in J,} and so J must be the whole polynomial ring {\displaystyle k[X_{1},\dots ,X_{n}],} Q.E.D.
