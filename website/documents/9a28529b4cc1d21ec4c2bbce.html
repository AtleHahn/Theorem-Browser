Statement

An integer polynomial is primitive when the gcd of its coefficients is 1. If f,g\in\mathbb Z[x] are primitive, then fg is primitive.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The coefficient argument uses elementary integer divisibility and finite polynomial multiplication.

Proof 1: Product of primitive integer polynomials is primitive — proof

Clearly the product f(x)g(x) of two primitive polynomials has integer coefficients. Therefore, if it is not primitive, there must be a prime p which is a common divisor of all its coefficients. But p cannot divide all the coefficients of either f(x) or g(x) (otherwise they would not be primitive). Let arxr be the first term of f(x) not divisible by p and let bsxs be the first term of g(x) not divisible by p. Now consider the term xr+s in the product, whose coefficient is

{\displaystyle \cdots +a_{r+2}b_{s-2}+a_{r+1}b_{s-1}+a_{r}b_{s}+a_{r-1}b_{s+1}+a_{r-2}b_{s+2}+\cdots .}

The term arbs is not divisible by p (because p is prime), yet all the remaining ones are, so the entire sum cannot be divisible by p. By assumption all coefficients in the product are divisible by p, leading to a contradiction. Therefore, the coefficients of the product can have no common divisor greater than one and are thus primitive. {\displaystyle \square }

Choose the first coefficient of each factor not divisible by a proposed common prime; in their combined degree all other summands are divisible by that prime. Integer polynomial multiplication and prime divisibility suffice.
