Statement

Let G be a finite group of order n with identity e. For every a∈G, its order d, the least positive integer with a^d=e, divides n. Consequently a^n=e.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra and introductory abstract algebra supply determinants, matrices, field roots, ring operations and divisibility. The displayed assertion uses these directly; no graduate concepts are needed to understand it.

Proof 1: Apply subgroup divisibility to the cyclic subgroup

The cyclic subgroup ⟨a⟩ has cardinality equal to the order d of a. Applying Lagrange’s theorem to this subgroup gives d∣n. Writing n=rd, one obtains a^n=(a^d)^r=e.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply Lagrange to the cyclic subgroup and rewrite the exponent as a multiple of the element order. Introductory finite group theory suffices.
