Statement

Let n\ge t\ge1 be integers, let 0<p<1, and put [n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}. For a family \mathcal A of subsets of [n], define \mu_p(\mathcal A)=\sum_{A\in\mathcal A}p^{|A|}(1-p)^{n-|A|}. Suppose that \mathcal F is t-intersecting, meaning |A\cap B|\ge t for all A,B\in\mathcal F. For each integer r\ge0 with t+2r\le n, put

\mathcal G_{n,t,r}=\{A\subseteq[n]:|A\cap\{1,\ldots,t+2r\}|\ge t+r\}.

Then \mu_p(\mathcal F)\le\max_{r\ge0,\ t+2r\le n}\mu_p(\mathcal G_{n,t,r}). This bound is attained by a family \mathcal G_{n,t,r} of maximum \mu_p-weight.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The measure is explicitly a finite sum of elementary powers; understanding the statement does not require measure theory. Family intersections and comparison conditions are defined by counting elements.

Proof 1: Weighted shifting and extremal generating families (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorem 3.1; §§4–5, pp.3–12).

The families F_{t,r} in the paper are \mathcal G_{n,t,r} here. Their t-intersection follows from 2(t+r)-(t+2r)=t, so a maximizing one attains the bound. In Lemma 5.2’s proof the upper breakpoint is (r+1)/(t+2r+1); in its four-part decomposition the second distinguished coordinate is t+2s-1. In Lemma 4.13’s last case use S^{\prime\prime}=S^\prime\setminus\{s\} and T^{\prime\prime}=T^\prime\setminus\{s\}, with S\cap T\supseteq((S^{\prime\prime}\cap T^{\prime\prime})\setminus\{i,j\})\cup\{\ell+1,s\}. These corrections give the intersection estimate written there. The p>1/2 argument uses the generalized shifting lemma (Lemma 5.5, cited there to Ahlswede–Khachatrian) and the Erdős–Ko–Rado bound.
