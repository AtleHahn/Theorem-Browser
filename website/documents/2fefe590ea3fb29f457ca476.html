Proof 1: Scale a Whitney embedding and apply Nash–Kuiper

Use the Nash–Kuiper theorem: if (M,g) is an m-dimensional Riemannian manifold and f:M\to\mathbb R^n is a short smooth embedding, where n\ge m+1, then there is a sequence of continuously differentiable isometric embeddings converging uniformly to f. “Short” means that the pulled-back Euclidean metric is bounded above by g.

By the Whitney embedding theorem, a closed smooth m-manifold embeds smoothly in \mathbb R^{2m}. Any such embedding can be scaled by an arbitrarily small constant so as to become short, relative to the given Riemannian metric. Since m\ge1, the target dimension satisfies 2m\ge m+1. The Nash–Kuiper theorem therefore shows that any such closed m-dimensional Riemannian manifold admits a continuously differentiable isometric embedding into an arbitrarily small neighborhood in 2m-dimensional Euclidean space. A translation places the image in B(0,\varepsilon).

The source leaves the compact scaling and uniform-approximation estimates implicit.
