Statement

Let n,m\geq1 be integers and let \Omega\subset\mathbb R^n be a bounded domain of diameter d, satisfying the strong cone property: there is a finite open cover \{O_j\} of \partial\Omega and corresponding finite-height solid cones C_j of positive height and opening, with vertices at the origin such that x+C_j\subseteq\Omega whenever x\in\Omega\cap O_j.

There is a constant C=C(m,\Omega) such that, for every 1\leq p\leq\infty and every u\in W_p^m(\Omega), there is a polynomial v of total degree at most m-1 satisfying, simultaneously for k=0,\ldots,m,

|u-v|_{W_p^k(\Omega)}\leq C d^{m-k}|u|_{W_p^m(\Omega)}.

The constant is independent of p and u.

Here W_p^m(\Omega) consists of functions whose weak derivatives through order m belong to L^p(\Omega). For a multiindex \alpha=(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n) of nonnegative integers, write |\alpha|=\sum_j\alpha_j and D^\alpha for the corresponding weak partial derivative. The seminorms are

|u|_{W_p^k(\Omega)}=\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\|D^\alpha u\|_{L^p(\Omega)}^p\right)^{1/p}\quad(p<\infty),\qquad |u|_{W_\infty^k(\Omega)}=\max_{|\alpha|=k}\|D^\alpha u\|_{L^\infty(\Omega)}.

In particular, the order-zero seminorm is the L^p norm.

Proof 1: Moment polynomial and the infinity endpoint (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Sections 2–3, Lemmas 1–2 and Theorem 1, pp.113–114).

Bramble–Hilbert §2 uses the diameter-weighted Sobolev norm on the stated strong-cone domain. In §3, Lemma 1 chooses a unique polynomial v of degree at most m-1 making the integral of every D^alpha(u-v), |alpha|<m, zero. Lemma 2 gives the p-independent lower-derivative estimates; Theorem 1 combines them into the weighted quotient-norm bound. Apply the estimate to this same v, so all derivative orders are controlled simultaneously. For the sum and p-sum conventions on derivative seminorms, equivalence constants are bounded by the number of multiindices, independently of p. For the endpoint, the following completion is supplied here: for u in W^{m,infinity} on this bounded domain, u belongs to every finite-p space. The moment equations defining v are independent of p and uniquely determine it (solve the coefficients from highest degree downward). Therefore the same polynomial works for every finite p. For each derivative, the L^p norm converges to the essential supremum as p tends to infinity on the finite-measure domain; the finite p-sum over derivatives tends to the maximum. Taking the limit in every k estimate gives exactly the required infinity inequalities with the same constant. The order-m estimate is immediate because D^alpha v=0 there. The diameter powers are those of the source’s weighted norm.
