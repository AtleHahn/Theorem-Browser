Statement

Let I\subseteq\mathbb R be an interval and let f_k:I\to\mathbb R be nondecreasing for every integer k\ge1. Suppose s(x)=\sum_{k=1}^\infty f_k(x) converges to a finite real number for every x\in I. Then, outside a set of Lebesgue measure zero in the interior of I, all derivatives s^{\prime}(x),f_1^{\prime}(x),f_2^{\prime}(x),\ldots exist as finite real numbers and s^{\prime}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^\infty f_k^{\prime}(x). A set has Lebesgue measure zero if, for every \varepsilon>0, it is covered by countably many intervals whose total lengths are less than \varepsilon.

Level justification

The proof uses undergraduate real analysis with measure defined through interval covers and integration by simple functions. A greedy interval-selection argument proves monotone differentiation, and Fatou’s lemma bounds the derivative’s integral. Applying this bound to increasing remainders completes the series argument.
