Statement

For the conditional converse only, assume the rank form of the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture for the elliptic curves

E_m:\ y^2=x^3-m^2x\qquad(m\text{ a positive square-free integer}):\qquad \operatorname{rank}E_m(\mathbb Q)=\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E_m,s).

Here E_m(\mathbb Q) consists of the rational solutions together with the point at infinity O, with the chord-and-tangent group law: a line through two points (the tangent for a repeated point) meets the cubic a third time, and reflection in the x-axis gives their sum; O is the identity. Its rank is the integer r for which this finitely generated abelian group modulo its finite-order elements is isomorphic to \mathbb Z^r. The order on the right is the order of vanishing of the analytically continued Hasse–Weil L-function at 1 (zero if its value there is nonzero).

For clarity, the L-function convention is

L(E,s)=\prod_p P_p(p^{-s})^{-1}\qquad(\operatorname{Re}s>3/2),

continued analytically to s=1. To define the factors, reduce a Weierstrass equation for E that is integral and minimal at p modulo p; minimal means that the exponent of p in its discriminant is least among integral equations for the same curve. If the reduced projective cubic is nonsingular, put

P_p(T)=1-a_pT+pT^2,\qquad a_p=p+1-\#E(\mathbb F_p),

where the count includes the point at infinity. In the multiplicative case (a nodal cubic), put P_p(T)=1-T if the two tangent directions at the node are defined over \mathbb F_p, and P_p(T)=1+T otherwise. In the additive case (a cuspidal cubic), put P_p(T)=1.

For each positive square-free integer n, define the finite counts

\begin{aligned} A_n&=\#\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb Z^3:2x^2+y^2+32z^2=n\},\\ B_n&=\#\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb Z^3:2x^2+y^2+8z^2=n\},\\ C_n&=\#\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb Z^3:8x^2+2y^2+64z^2=n\},\\ D_n&=\#\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb Z^3:8x^2+2y^2+16z^2=n\}. \end{aligned}

Unconditionally, if n is a congruent number, then 2A_n=B_n for odd n, and 2C_n=D_n for even n.

Under the stated conjectural assumption, if n is odd and 2A_n=B_n, or n is even and 2C_n=D_n, then n is a congruent number: there exist positive rational numbers a,b,c with a^2+b^2=c^2 and ab/2=n.

The converse conclusion is conditional on the displayed BSD assumption; that assumption is not asserted here as a proved theorem.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The arithmetic conclusion is elementary, but understanding the actual conditional statement requires elliptic-curve groups, minimal reduction and local Euler factors, followed by analytic continuation and the conjectural equality of ranks. This cumulative preparation is substantially beyond the undergraduate reading allowance.
