Statement

A finite simple graph has finitely many vertices, at most one edge per vertex pair, and no loops. A proper coloring assigns distinct colors to adjacent vertices; a plane graph is drawn without crossings. Let G be a connected plane graph with at least three vertices and every face triangular. Write d(v) for the number of edges incident to vertex v. Then \sum_{v\in V(G)}(6-d(v))=12.

Level
High school only
Level justification

A graph is a finite set of vertices joined by edges; planarity means it can be drawn without crossings, and proper coloring assigns different colors across each edge. These finite combinatorial and planar notions are explicit.

Proof 1: Count face and vertex incidences

Suppose v,e,f are the number of vertices, edges, and regions (faces). Since each region is triangular and each edge is shared by two regions, we have 2e=3f. This together with Euler's formula, v-e+f=2, gives 6v-2e=12. Now, the degree of a vertex is the number of edges abutting it. If v_i is the number of vertices of degree i and D is the maximum degree of any vertex,

6v-2e =6\sum_{i=1}^D v_i-\sum_{i=1}^D i v_i =\sum_{i=1}^D(6-i)v_i =12.

This is \sum_{x\in V(G)}(6-d(x))=12, grouped by degree.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Count two face incidences per edge, three per triangle and two endpoint incidences per edge, then rearrange Euler. School finite counting and linear algebraic manipulation suffice.
