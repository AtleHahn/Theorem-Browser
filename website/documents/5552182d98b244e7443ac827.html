Statement

Let A be a nonzero Noetherian Cohen–Macaulay ring. Every associated prime of A has height zero, so A has no embedded associated primes.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Noetherian Cohen–Macaulay rings; Associated primes; Prime height; Embedded primes. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Proof 1: Apply unmixedness to the zero ideal

An ideal I of a Noetherian ring A is called unmixed in height if the height of I is equal to the height of every associated prime P of A/I.

The unmixedness theorem is said to hold for the ring A if every ideal I generated by a number of elements equal to its height is unmixed. A Noetherian ring is Cohen–Macaulay if and only if the unmixedness theorem holds for it.

The unmixed theorem applies in particular to the zero ideal (an ideal generated by zero elements, of height zero), and thus it says that all associated primes of A have height zero: there is no embedded component.
