Statement

Let X be a compact complex manifold and p,q\ge0 integers. Use the convention that forms with any negative bidegree component are zero. Write Z^{p,q}=\{\alpha\in\Omega^{p,q}(X):\partial\alpha=\bar\partial\alpha=0\} and H_{BC}^{p,q}(X)=Z^{p,q}/\operatorname{im}(\partial\bar\partial:\Omega^{p-1,q-1}\to\Omega^{p,q}). De Rham cohomology means d-closed complex forms modulo d-exact forms. Assume the ddbar property in this bidegree: every d-exact form in Z^{p,q} is \partial\bar\partial-exact. Then the natural map H_{BC}^{p,q}(X)\to H_{dR}^{p+q}(X,\mathbb C) is injective.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Compact complex manifolds; Complex differential forms; Bott–Chern quotient; De Rham cohomology; Injective maps. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Proof 1: Apply the ddbar property to the comparison kernel — outline

The comparison sends a representative closed form to its de Rham class. Under the stated ddbar property, a class that maps to zero has a \partial\bar\partial-exact representative, so is zero already in Bott–Chern cohomology.

Level justification

The kernel argument requires complex forms of bidegree, Dolbeault exactness, Bott-Chern cohomology and de Rham cohomology. Explicit quotient notation does not make this complex-geometric framework a small extension.
