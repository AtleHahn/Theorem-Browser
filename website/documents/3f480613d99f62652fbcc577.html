Statement

For each fixed positive integer k, some language in \Sigma_2^P does not belong to \mathsf{SIZE}(n^k). \mathsf{SIZE}(n^k) denotes languages with Boolean circuit families of size O(n^k).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement is a circuit lower bound for a quantified polynomial-time class.

Proof 1: Case split on whether SAT has polynomial-size circuits (outline)

There exists a language {\displaystyle L_{k}\in \Sigma _{4}^{P}-{\mathsf {SIZE}}(n^{k})} (this diagonalization result is taken as a prerequisite). Consider two cases:

  • If {\displaystyle {\mathsf {SAT}}\notin {\mathsf {P/poly}}} then {\displaystyle {\mathsf {SAT}}\notin {\mathsf {SIZE}}(n^{k})} and the theorem is proved, since \mathsf{SAT}\in\mathsf{NP}\subseteq\Sigma_2^P.
  • If {\displaystyle {\mathsf {SAT}}\in {\mathsf {P/poly}}}, then by Karp–Lipton theorem, {\displaystyle \Sigma _{4}^{P}=\Sigma _{2}^{P}} and therefore {\displaystyle L_{k}\in \Sigma _{2}^{P}-{\mathsf {SIZE}}(n^{k})}.
