Statement

Let \mathfrak A be a complex Hilbert algebra: an involutive algebra with inner product satisfying (a,b)=(b^*,a^*), bounded left multiplication, (xy,z)=(y,x^*z), and density of the span of products. Let H be its Hilbert completion, J the conjugate-linear isometric extension of the involution, and \lambda(a)x=ax, \rho(a)x=xa the bounded left and right actions. If M=\lambda(\mathfrak A)^{\prime\prime}, then JMJ=M^{\prime} and \lambda(\mathfrak A)^{\prime\prime}=\rho(\mathfrak A)^{\prime}.

Proof 1: Conjugate the algebra generated by bounded elements

The proof relies on the notion of “bounded elements” in the Hilbert space completion H.

An element x in H is said to be bounded (relative to \mathfrak A) if the map a\mapsto xa=\rho(a)x of \mathfrak A into H extends to a bounded operator on H, denoted by \lambda(x). Write \mathfrak B for the space of all bounded elements. We use the following bounded-element facts, stated in the source without proofs:

  • Jx is also a bounded element, denoted x^*, and \lambda(x^*)=\lambda(x)^*;
  • a\mapsto ax is given by the bounded operator \rho(x)=J\lambda(x^*)J on H;
  • M' is generated by the \rho(x)'s with x bounded;
  • \lambda(x) and \rho(y) commute for x,y bounded.

The commutation theorem follows immediately from the last assertion. In particular

M=\lambda(\mathfrak B)''.
