Statement

Let Q=[a,b]\times[c,d]\subset\mathbb R^2 with a<b and c<d. Suppose F:Q\to\mathbb R^3 is continuous and its horizontal projection \pi(x,y,z)=(x,y) satisfies \pi(F(Q))\subseteq Q. Then some q\in Q satisfies \pi(F(q))=q.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves continuous Euclidean maps; rectangles; coordinate projections. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Apply Brouwer to the projected sheet

This is a consequence of the n=2 case of Brouwer's fixed-point theorem applied to the continuous map that assigns to the coordinates of every point of the crumpled sheet the coordinates of the point of the flat sheet immediately beneath it. In the notation of the statement, this map is \pi\circ F:Q\to Q. Its fixed point is a point of the crumpled sheet that lies directly above its corresponding point (i.e. the point with the same coordinates) of the flat sheet.
