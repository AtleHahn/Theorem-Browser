Statement

Let A_1,\ldots,A_n be events in a probability space, where n\ge1 is an integer and \mathbb P denotes probability. No independence assumption is required. Then

\max\left(0,\sum_{k=1}^n\mathbb P(A_k)-(n-1)\right) \le\mathbb P\left(\bigcap_{k=1}^n A_k\right) \le\min_{1\le k\le n}\mathbb P(A_k),

and

\max_{1\le k\le n}\mathbb P(A_k) \le\mathbb P\left(\bigcup_{k=1}^n A_k\right) \le\min\left(1,\sum_{k=1}^n\mathbb P(A_k)\right).

Here the intersection means that all the events occur, and the union means that at least one occurs.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Events, unions and intersections are defined in elementary language; the complete finite bounds require only elementary probability and algebra.

Proof 1: Union bound and complements (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Properties of Probability Measure, Theorems 1–2).

Apply the union bound also to A_1^c,\ldots,A_n^c: since (\bigcap_k A_k)^c=\bigcup_k A_k^c, this gives \mathbb P(\bigcap_k A_k)\ge\sum_k\mathbb P(A_k)-(n-1). The other bounds follow from \bigcap_k A_k\subseteq A_j\subseteq\bigcup_k A_k and 0\le\mathbb P(B)\le1.

The union bound follows by induction from the two-event addition formula, whose proof splits events into disjoint pieces. Complementation and monotonicity follow from finite additivity.
