Statement

For a holomorphic map f:D\to D, where D=\{z:|z|<1\}, \left|\frac{f(z_1)-f(z_2)}{1-\overline{f(z_1)}f(z_2)}\right|\le\left|\frac{z_1-z_2}{1-\overline{z_1}z_2}\right| for all z_1,z_2\in D, and |f'(z)|/(1-|f(z)|^2)\le1/(1-|z|^2) for every z\in D.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex differentiability extends the calculus derivative; limits, compact sets, uniform approximation and zeros of functions are familiar analysis. Zero multiplicity is the order of the first nonzero Taylor coefficient, and circle integrals are parametrized ordinary integrals. These limited definitions suffice for the full claim.

Proof 1: Move both points to zero and take a difference quotient — outline

The proof of the Schwarz–Pick theorem follows from Schwarz's lemma and the fact that a Möbius transformation of the form

{\displaystyle {\frac {z-z_{0}}{{\overline {z_{0}}}z-1}},\qquad |z_{0}|<1,}

is an automorphism of the unit disk. Fix {\displaystyle z_{1}} and define the Möbius transformations

{\displaystyle M(z)={\frac {z_{1}-z}{1-{\overline {z_{1}}}z}},\qquad \varphi (z)={\frac {f(z_{1})-z}{1-{\overline {f(z_{1})}}z}}.}

Since {\displaystyle M(z_{1})=0} and the Möbius transformation is invertible, the composition {\displaystyle \varphi (f(M^{-1}(z)))} maps {\displaystyle 0} to {\displaystyle 0} and the unit disk is mapped into itself. Thus we can apply Schwarz's lemma, which is to say

{\displaystyle \left|\varphi \left(f(M^{-1}(z))\right)\right|=\left|{\frac {f(z_{1})-f(M^{-1}(z))}{1-{\overline {f(z_{1})}}f(M^{-1}(z))}}\right|\leq |z|.}

Now calling {\displaystyle z_{2}=M^{-1}(z)} (which will still be in the unit disk) yields the desired conclusion

{\displaystyle \left|{\frac {f(z_{1})-f(z_{2})}{1-{\overline {f(z_{1})}}f(z_{2})}}\right|\leq \left|{\frac {z_{1}-z_{2}}{1-{\overline {z_{1}}}z_{2}}}\right|.}

To prove the second part of the theorem, we rearrange the left-hand side into the difference quotient and let {\displaystyle z_{2}} tend to {\displaystyle z_{1}}.
