Statement

For a topological group G, a matrix coefficient means a function g\mapsto L(\pi(g)), where \pi:G\to GL(V) is a continuous representation on a finite-dimensional complex vector space and L is a linear functional on \operatorname{End}(V). If G is a compact subgroup of GL(N,\mathbb C), its matrix coefficients are uniformly dense in C(G).

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Proof 1: Peter–Weyl density for compact matrix groups — outline

The matrix coefficients form a unital algebra invariant under complex conjugation because the product of two matrix coefficients is a matrix coefficient of the tensor product representation, and the complex conjugate is a matrix coefficient of the dual representation. For the latter assertion, use the standard fact that a finite-dimensional representation of a compact group admits an invariant Hermitian inner product.

Hence the theorem follows directly from the complex Stone–Weierstrass theorem if the matrix coefficients separate points, which is obvious if G is a matrix group: the entries of the defining representation separate distinct matrices.

Level justification

The proof combines continuous compact-group representations, tensor and dual representations, invariant Hermitian forms and uniform function-algebra density.
