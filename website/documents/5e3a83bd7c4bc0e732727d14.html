Statement

Let T be a real interval and d\ge1. For every ordered tuple of distinct times (t_1,\ldots,t_k) let \nu_{t_1\ldots t_k} be a Borel probability measure on (\mathbb R^d)^k. Assume permutation consistency and marginal consistency: for Borel F_i\subseteq\mathbb R^d,

\nu_{t_{\pi(1)}\ldots t_{\pi(k)}}(F_{\pi(1)}\times\cdots\times F_{\pi(k)})=\nu_{t_1\ldots t_k}(F_1\times\cdots\times F_k),

and adding any further distinct times gives

\nu_{t_1\ldots t_{k+m}}(F_1\times\cdots\times F_k\times(\mathbb R^d)^m)=\nu_{t_1\ldots t_k}(F_1\times\cdots\times F_k).

Then there is a unique probability measure on the product sigma-algebra of (\mathbb R^d)^T with these finite-dimensional distributions. The coordinate process X_t(\omega)=\omega_t realizes them.

Proof 1: Specialize inner-regular extension to Euclidean spaces

Use the general extension theorem: compatible inner-regular probability measures on finite products of Hausdorff state spaces extend uniquely to a probability measure on the product sigma-algebra. Permutation consistency lets the tuple distributions define measures on products indexed by finite subsets, and marginal consistency gives compatibility under coordinate projections.

Note that the statement here is just a special case of the general extension theorem with {\displaystyle \Omega _{t}=\mathbb {R} ^{d}} for all {\displaystyle t\in T}, and {\displaystyle \mu _{\{t_{1},...,t_{k}\}}=\nu _{t_{1}\dots t_{k}}} for {\displaystyle t_{1},...,t_{k}\in T}. The stochastic process is simply the canonical process {\displaystyle (\pi _{t})_{t\in T}}, defined on {\displaystyle \Omega =(\mathbb {R} ^{d})^{T}} with probability measure {\displaystyle P=\mu }. The reason that the statement here does not mention inner regularity of the measures {\displaystyle \nu _{t_{1}\dots t_{k}}} is that this would automatically follow, since Borel probability measures on Polish spaces are automatically Radon.

Here \pi_t(\omega)=\omega_t is the coordinate projection, and finite-dimensional Euclidean spaces are Polish.
