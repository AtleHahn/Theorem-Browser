Statement

Let n>1 be an odd integer and a an integer with \gcd(a,n)=1. If the Jacobi symbol \left(\frac an\right) is not congruent to a^{(n-1)/2} modulo n, then n is composite.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Integer factorization and modular arithmetic suffice. The Jacobi symbol is the product of the quadratic-residue signs at prime factors, with multiplicities; this single finite arithmetic definition makes the congruence test understandable.

Proof 1: Contrapose the prime-modulus congruence

For every odd prime the Jacobi symbol equals the Legendre symbol, and Euler’s criterion gives the congruence to the half power. Thus comparing these two values can rule out primality when they disagree.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Assume primality and combine equality of Jacobi and Legendre symbols with Euler criterion, then contrapose. Undergraduate elementary number theory suffices with the symbols as a small modular-arithmetic extension.
