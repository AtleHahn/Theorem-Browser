Statement

For every positive integer k, every coloring c:\mathbb N_{>0}\to\{1,\ldots,k\}, and every positive integer m, there is a set S of m distinct positive integers such that every product of a nonempty subset of S has the same color. The elements of S can be chosen as powers of 2.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The assertion concerns coloring positive integers and multiplying members of finite subsets. These require only elementary arithmetic and finite sets. This rating concerns understanding the statement, independently of the difficulty of its proof.

Proof 1: Transfer additive sums to products of powers of two

Use the additive version of Folkman's theorem: every finite coloring of the positive integers admits, for every positive integer m, a set of m elements whose nonempty subset sums all have the same color. If one restricts S to consist only of powers of two, then the multiplicative result follows immediately from this additive version.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Pull the coloring back along exponentiation by two, apply the explicit additive assertion and turn sums into products. Undergraduate infinite-set coloring and elementary exponent laws suffice; no Ramsey-theoretic proof is needed.
