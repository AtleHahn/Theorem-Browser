Statement

Let M be a compact differentiable manifold of dimension n\ge1, possibly with boundary, and let v be a continuous tangent vector field all of whose zeros are isolated. If \partial M\ne\varnothing, assume that v is nonzero there and points in the outward normal direction along it. Then

\sum_{x:\,v(x)=0}\operatorname{index}_x(v)=\chi(M).

Here \chi(M) is the Euler characteristic. To define the local index, choose local coordinates about x and a small coordinate closed ball D centered at x containing no other zero. The index is the degree of the map

\partial D\longrightarrow S^{n-1},\qquad z\longmapsto\frac{v(z)}{\|v(z)\|},

using the coordinate representation of the vector field. No orientability of M is required.

Proof 1: Index invariance and Morse fields (proof sketch) (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Section 6, pp.35–41, especially Theorem 1 and Steps 1–3.). The linked exposition is a proof sketch with the indicated dependencies.

Apply Milnor section 6, including the tubular-neighborhood proof and the three concluding steps. Given a continuous field, choose disjoint small balls around its finitely many zeros. On their boundary spheres and on the complement the field has a positive lower norm bound; on the manifold boundary its outward component has a positive lower bound. Uniformly approximate by a smooth tangent field and perturb transversely, sufficiently little to preserve these bounds. No new zero occurs outside the balls, outwardness is preserved, and degree homotopy invariance makes the sum of new local indices in each ball equal to the old index. Apply the smooth theorem. No orientability assumption is introduced; alternatively the orientation double cover doubles both index sum and Euler characteristic.
