Statement

For real x\ge0 and integer r\ge0, (1+x)^r\ge1+rx.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement compares an integer power of a nonnegative increment with an affine expression.

Mathematical Field

Proof 1: Nonnegative binomial remainder

One can prove Bernoulli's inequality for x ≥ 0 using the binomial theorem. It is true trivially for r = 0 or r = 1, so suppose r is an integer at least 2. Then {\displaystyle (1+x)^{r}=1+rx+{\tbinom {r}{2}}x^{2}+...+{\tbinom {r}{r}}x^{r}.} Clearly {\displaystyle {\tbinom {r}{2}}x^{2}+...+{\tbinom {r}{r}}x^{r}\geq 0,} and hence {\displaystyle (1+x)^{r}\geq 1+rx} as required.

Level
High school only
Level justification

For r>=2 the binomial expansion leaves only nonnegative higher-order terms after subtracting 1+rx. The two smaller exponents give equality. This is elementary finite polynomial algebra.
