Statement

For integers n\ge2 and m\ge1, let every pair of distinct vertices in an n-vertex loopless multigraph be joined by exactly m edges. Its number of spanning trees, distinguishing parallel edges, is m^{n-1}n^{n-2}. A tree is a connected graph with no cycles; a spanning tree contains every vertex of the given graph.

Proof 1: Spanning trees of a complete uniform multigraph — outline

Kirchhoff's theorem holds for multigraphs as well: the spanning-tree count is any principal cofactor of the Laplacian matrix L. The matrix L is modified as follows:

  • The off-diagonal entry q_{i,j} equals -m, where m is the number of edges between i and j;
  • when counting the degree of a vertex for a diagonal entry, all loops are excluded.

For the complete simple graph K_n, the Laplacian matrix is

\begin{bmatrix} n-1&-1&\cdots&-1\\ -1&n-1&\cdots&-1\\ \vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\ -1&-1&\cdots&n-1 \end{bmatrix}.

Any principal cofactor of this matrix is n^{n-2}, the Cayley cofactor formula.

Cayley's formula for a complete multigraph is

m^{n-1}\bigl(n^{n-1}-(n-1)n^{n-2}\bigr)=m^{n-1}n^{n-2}

by the same cofactor method, since a simple graph is a multigraph with m=1. Here the Laplacian is multiplied by m, so its (n-1)-row cofactor is multiplied by m^{n-1}.
