Statement

Let \mu be a probability distribution on \mathbb R, with characteristic function \phi(t)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{itx}\,d\mu(x). Suppose on some open interval containing 0, the continuous logarithm of \phi with value 0 at 0 is a polynomial in t. Then \mu is Gaussian, allowing a point mass: its characteristic function is \exp(imt-\sigma^2t^2/2) for some m\in\mathbb R and \sigma^2\ge0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Undergraduate probability, integration and local complex logarithms suffice; no harmonic analysis on general groups is required.

Level justification

The statement uses characteristic functions and a local logarithm. The argument requires a combined development of measure-theoretic moment limits, Cauchy integral estimates, entire Fourier–Laplace transforms and analytic continuation; the polynomial obstruction then uses real and imaginary parts along complex rays.
