Statement

For a closed connected oriented surface S, write \operatorname{Mod}(S) for orientation-preserving homeomorphisms modulo isotopy, and \operatorname{Mod}^{\pm}(S) when both orientations are allowed. On \mathbb T^2=\mathbb R^2/\mathbb Z^2, the rule

\Phi:\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Mod}(\mathbb T^2),\qquad A\longmapsto[x+\mathbb Z^2\mapsto Ax+\mathbb Z^2]

is an injective group homomorphism.

Proof 1: Linear torus maps and a homology left inverse

It is easy to construct a morphism

\Phi:\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Mod}(\mathbb T^2):

every A\in\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z) induces an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism of \mathbb T^2 via

x+\mathbb Z^2\longmapsto Ax+\mathbb Z^2.

The action of diffeomorphisms on the first homology group of \mathbb T^2 gives a left-inverse \Pi to the morphism \Phi, proving in particular that \Phi is injective.

Here H_1(\mathbb T^2;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2 on the two coordinate-loop generators, and isotopic maps induce the same homology map. The source leaves the verification of the linear quotient construction and its homology action implicit.
