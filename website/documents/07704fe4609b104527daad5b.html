On an outcome belonging to exactly r of the events, the alternating sum of nonempty-intersection indicators is sum_(k=1)^r(-1)^(k+1) binom(r,k), equal to 1 for r>0 and 0 for r=0. Partition into the finitely many membership atoms and weight by their probabilities. Finite additivity gives the exact formula, including an empty family if allowed. Only elementary finite probability and the binomial theorem are needed.

Statement

Let A_1,\ldots,A_m be events in a probability space. Let N count those that occur, and put S_k=\sum_{J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\},\ |J|=k}\Pr(\cap_{j\in J}A_j), with S_0=1. Then \Pr(\bigcup_{j=1}^mA_j)=\sum_{k=1}^m(-1)^{k+1}S_k.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement involves finitely many events, their unions, intersections or complements and elementary probability identities; conditional probabilities, when used, are explicitly defined. This rating concerns understanding the statement, independently of the difficulty of its proof.

Proof 1: Choose the positive-count indicator sequence

Use the probabilistic Schuette–Nesbitt formula

\sum_{n=0}^m\Pr(N=n)c_n=\sum_{k=0}^m S_k(\Delta^k c)_0,

where \Delta c_j=c_{j+1}-c_j and

(\Delta^k c)_0=\sum_{j=0}^k(-1)^{k-j}\binom{k}{j}c_j.

The probabilistic version of the inclusion–exclusion principle can be derived from this formula by choosing the sequence c=(0,1,1,\ldots): the left-hand side reduces to the probability of the event \{N\geq1\}, which is the union of A_1,\ldots,A_m, and the right-hand side is

S_1-S_2+S_3-\cdots-(-1)^mS_m,

because (\Delta^0c)_0=0 and (\Delta^kc)_0=-(-1)^k for k\in\{1,\ldots,m\}.
