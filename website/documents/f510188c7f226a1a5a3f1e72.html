Statement

On a measure space let 1\le p_0<p_1\le\infty, 0<\theta<1, and 1/p_\theta=(1-\theta)/p_0+\theta/p_1, with 1/\infty=0. L^{p_0}\cap L^{p_1}\subseteq L^{p_\theta}\subseteq L^{p_0}+L^{p_1}.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The inclusions compare infinite-dimensional spaces of measurable functions.

Proof 1: Combine norm interpolation with a level-set split

Norm comparison

By splitting up |f| as the product |f|=|f|^{1-\theta}|f|^\theta and applying Hölder's inequality to its p_\theta power, we obtain: each f\in L^{p_0}\cap L^{p_1} satisfies

\|f\|_{p_\theta}\leq\|f\|_{p_0}^{1-\theta}\|f\|_{p_1}^{\theta}.

For p_1=\infty, the second factor is estimated by its essential supremum.

Inclusions

This result implies that L^{p_0}\cap L^{p_1}\subseteq L^{p_\theta}.

On the other hand, if we take the decomposition

f=f\mathbf1_{\{|f|>1\}}+f\mathbf1_{\{|f|\leq1\}},

then we see that f\mathbf1_{\{|f|>1\}}\in L^{p_0} and f\mathbf1_{\{|f|\leq1\}}\in L^{p_1}, whence each f in L^{p_\theta} can be written as a sum f=g+h, where g\in L^{p_0} and h\in L^{p_1}.

In particular, this implies that L^{p_\theta} is included in L^{p_0}+L^{p_1}, the sum of L^{p_0} and L^{p_1} in the space of all measurable functions. Therefore, we have the chain of inclusions

L^{p_0}\cap L^{p_1}\subseteq L^{p_\theta}\subseteq L^{p_0}+L^{p_1}.
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply measure-theoretic Holder to interpolated powers and split at level one for the reverse sum inclusion. General Lebesgue Lp spaces and essential suprema are advanced prerequisites.
