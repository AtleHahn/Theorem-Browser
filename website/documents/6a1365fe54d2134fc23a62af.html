Statement

Let n\ge1 and let \Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{v_1,\ldots,v_n\} be an (n-1)-simplex. Closed sets C_i\subseteq\Delta form a KKM covering if \operatorname{conv}\{v_i:i\in I\}\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in I}C_i for every nonempty I\subseteq[n]. Every KKM covering satisfies \bigcap_{i=1}^n C_i\ne\varnothing.

Proof 1: Take identical coverings in the rainbow theorem

The rainbow KKM theorem says that for n KKM coverings (C_i^j)_{i=1}^n, j=1,\ldots,n, there is a permutation \pi such that \bigcap_i C_i^{\pi(i)}\ne\varnothing. Choose C_i^j=C_i for every j. Then every such permuted intersection is \bigcap_i C_i, proving the claim.

Proof 2: Embed the covering in the singleton faces

A collection B of nonempty subsets of [n]=\{1,\ldots,n\} is balanced if there are weights w_J\ge0 such that \sum_{J\in B:i\in J}w_J=1 for every i\in[n]. The KKMS theorem states that a family of closed sets D_J, indexed by nonempty J\subseteq[n], with \operatorname{conv}\{v_i:i\in I\}\subseteq\bigcup_{\varnothing\ne J\subseteq I}D_J for every nonempty I, has a balanced subcollection B with \bigcap_{J\in B}D_J\ne\varnothing.

Suppose we have a KKM covering {\displaystyle C_{i}}, for {\displaystyle i=1,\ldots ,n}. Construct a KKMS covering {\displaystyle D_J} as follows:

  • {\displaystyle D_J=C_{i}} whenever {\displaystyle J=\{i\}} ({\displaystyle J} is a singleton that contains only element {\displaystyle i}).
  • {\displaystyle D_J=\emptyset } otherwise.

The KKM condition on the original covering {\displaystyle C_{i}} implies the KKMS condition on the new covering {\displaystyle D_J}. Therefore, there exists a balanced collection such that the corresponding sets in the new covering have nonempty intersection. Nonempty intersection excludes all nonsingleton indices. Thus the only possible balanced collection is the collection of all singletons; hence, the original covering has nonempty intersection.
