Statement

The fields between \mathbb Q and K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3) are exactly \mathbb Q,K,\mathbb Q(\sqrt2),\mathbb Q(\sqrt3),\mathbb Q(\sqrt6).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement specifies all intermediate fields of a concrete extension of the rationals, requiring field-theoretic concepts beyond the stipulated calculus and linear-algebra background.

Proof 1: Match the five subgroups with their fixed fields

Consider the field

{\displaystyle K=\mathbb {Q} \left({\sqrt {2}},{\sqrt {3}}\right)=\left[\mathbb {Q} ({\sqrt {2}})\right]\!({\sqrt {3}}).}

Since K is constructed from the base field {\displaystyle \mathbb {Q} } by adjoining √2, then √3, each element of K can be written as:

{\displaystyle (a+b{\sqrt {2}})+(c+d{\sqrt {2}}){\sqrt {3}},\qquad a,b,c,d\in \mathbb {Q} .}

Its Galois group {\displaystyle G={\text{Gal}}(K/\mathbb {Q} )} comprises the automorphisms of K which fix every rational number. Such automorphisms must send √2 to √2 or –√2, and send √3 to √3 or –√3, since they permute the roots of any irreducible polynomial. Suppose that f exchanges √2 and –√2 and fixes √3, so

{\displaystyle f\left((a+b{\sqrt {2}})+(c+d{\sqrt {2}}){\sqrt {3}}\right)=(a-b{\sqrt {2}})+(c-d{\sqrt {2}}){\sqrt {3}}=a-b{\sqrt {2}}+c{\sqrt {3}}-d{\sqrt {6}},}

and g exchanges √3 and –√3 and fixes √2, so

{\displaystyle g\left((a+b{\sqrt {2}})+(c+d{\sqrt {2}}){\sqrt {3}}\right)=(a+b{\sqrt {2}})-(c+d{\sqrt {2}}){\sqrt {3}}=a+b{\sqrt {2}}-c{\sqrt {3}}-d{\sqrt {6}}.}

These are clearly automorphisms of K, respecting its addition and multiplication. There is also the identity automorphism e which fixes each element, and the composition of f and g which changes the signs on both radicals:

{\displaystyle (fg)\left((a+b{\sqrt {2}})+(c+d{\sqrt {2}}){\sqrt {3}}\right)=(a-b{\sqrt {2}})-(c-d{\sqrt {2}}){\sqrt {3}}=a-b{\sqrt {2}}-c{\sqrt {3}}+d{\sqrt {6}}.}

Since the order of the Galois group is equal to the degree of the field extension, {\displaystyle |G|=[K:\mathbb {Q} ]=4}, there can be no further automorphisms:

{\displaystyle G=\left\{1,f,g,fg\right\},}

which is isomorphic to the Klein four-group. By the finite Galois correspondence, its five subgroups correspond to the fields intermediate between the base {\displaystyle \mathbb {Q} } and the extension K.

  • The trivial subgroup {1} corresponds to the entire extension field K.
  • The entire group G corresponds to the base field {\displaystyle \mathbb {Q} .}
  • The subgroup {1, f} corresponds to the subfield {\displaystyle \mathbb {Q} ({\sqrt {3}}),} since f fixes √3.
  • The subgroup {1, g} corresponds to the subfield {\displaystyle \mathbb {Q} ({\sqrt {2}}),} since g fixes √2.
  • The subgroup {1, fg} corresponds to the subfield {\displaystyle \mathbb {Q} ({\sqrt {6}}),} since fg fixes √6.

For each of the three order-two subgroups, the fixed field has degree two over \mathbb Q, by the Galois degree formula. Thus the fixed radical displayed in each case generates the whole fixed field.
