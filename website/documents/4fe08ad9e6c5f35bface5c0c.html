Statement

For topological spaces X,Y and a commutative ring R with identity, there is a first-quadrant homological spectral sequence

E^2_{p,q}=\bigoplus_{i+j=q}\operatorname{Tor}^R_p(H_i(X;R),H_j(Y;R))\Longrightarrow H_{p+q}(X\times Y;R).
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Singular homology; Commutative rings; Tor functors; Spectral sequences. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Apply Eilenberg–Zilber and the algebraic spectral formula — outline

Write C_*(X;R) for the singular chain complex of X with coefficients in R. The chain complex of the space X × Y is related to the chain complexes of X and Y by a natural quasi-isomorphism

{\displaystyle C_{*}(X\times Y;R)\simeq C_{*}(X;R)\otimes_R C_{*}(Y;R).}

For singular chains this is the theorem of Eilenberg and Zilber. Then the homology of the tensor product on the right is given by the spectral Künneth formula of homological algebra, taken as a prerequisite for these free nonnegative chain complexes:

E^2_{p,q}=\bigoplus_{i+j=q}\operatorname{Tor}^R_p(H_i(X;R),H_j(Y;R)) \Longrightarrow H_{p+q}(C_*(X;R)\otimes_R C_*(Y;R)).

The quasi-isomorphism identifies this target with H_{p+q}(X\times Y;R).

The freeness of the chain modules means that in this geometric case it is not necessary to use any hyperhomology or total derived tensor product.
