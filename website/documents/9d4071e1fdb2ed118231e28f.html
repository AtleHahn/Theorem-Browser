Statement

Let n,m\ge1 be integers and \Phi:M_n(\mathbb C)\to M_m(\mathbb C) complex-linear. Write E_{kl} for the matrix units, * for conjugate transpose, and C_\Phi=[\Phi(E_{kl})]_{k,l=1}^n. A map is r-positive if applying it entrywise to an r\times r block matrix preserves positive semidefiniteness, and completely positive if it is r-positive for every integer r\ge1. Suppose B\in M_{nm}(\mathbb C) satisfies C_\Phi=B^*B. Write B^*=[b_1,\ldots,b_{nm}] and define V_i e_k=P_kb_i, where P_k selects the kth block of m coordinates. Then \Phi(A)=\sum_iV_iAV_i^*. Here e_k is the kth standard basis vector of \mathbb C^n, and P_k:\mathbb C^{nm}\to\mathbb C^m selects the kth block of m coordinates.

Proof 1: Expand a square factor into column outer products

Let

{\displaystyle B^{*}=[b_{1},\ldots ,b_{nm}],}

where bi*'s are the row vectors of B, then

{\displaystyle C_{\Phi }=\sum _{i=1}^{nm}b_{i}b_{i}^{*}.}

The corresponding Kraus operators are obtained by the following block computation.

The vector space Cnm can be viewed as the direct sum {\displaystyle \textstyle \oplus _{i=1}^{n}\mathbb {C} ^{m}} compatibly with the identification {\displaystyle \textstyle \mathbb {C} ^{nm}\cong \mathbb {C} ^{n}\otimes \mathbb {C} ^{m}} and the standard basis of Cn.

If Pk ∈ Cm × nm is projection onto the k-th copy of Cm, then Pk* ∈ Cnm×m is the inclusion of Cm as the k-th summand of the direct sum and

{\displaystyle \;\Phi (E_{kl})=P_{k}\cdot C_{\Phi }\cdot P_{l}^{*}=\sum _{i=1}^{nm}P_{k}b_{i}(P_{l}b_{i})^{*}.}

Now if the operators Vi ∈ Cm×n are defined on the k-th standard basis vector ek of Cn by

{\displaystyle \;V_{i}e_{k}=P_{k}b_{i},}

then

{\displaystyle \Phi (E_{kl})=\sum _{i=1}^{nm}P_{k}b_{i}(P_{l}b_{i})^{*}=\sum _{i=1}^{nm}V_{i}e_{k}e_{l}^{*}V_{i}^{*}=\sum _{i=1}^{nm}V_{i}E_{kl}V_{i}^{*}.}

Extending by linearity gives us

{\displaystyle \Phi (A)=\sum _{i=1}^{nm}V_{i}AV_{i}^{*}}

for any A ∈ Cn×n.
