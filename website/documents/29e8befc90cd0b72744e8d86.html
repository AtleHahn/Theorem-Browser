Statement

For a sigma-finite measure space (S,\mu), real f_1,f_2\in L^p(S) and 1\le p<\infty, \|f_1+f_2\|_p\le\|f_1\|_p+\|f_2\|_p. Here \|h\|_p=(\int_S|h|^p\,d\mu)^{1/p}.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The arbitrary-measure-space statement requires measure theory beyond undergraduate calculus.

Proof 1: Use a two-point counting measure in the integral inequality

Use Minkowski's integral inequality

\left\|\int_{S_1}F(x,\cdot)\,d\mu_1(x)\right\|_p \leq\int_{S_1}\|F(x,\cdot)\|_p\,d\mu_1(x).

In the following take (S_2,\mu_2)=(S,\mu).

If {\textstyle \mu _{1}} is the counting measure on a two-point set {\textstyle S_{1}=\{1,2\},} then Minkowski's integral inequality gives the usual Minkowski inequality as a special case: for putting {\textstyle f_{i}(y)=F(i,y)} for {\textstyle i=1,2,} the integral inequality gives

{\displaystyle \|f_{1}+f_{2}\|_{p}=\left(\int _{S_{2}}\left|\int _{S_{1}}F(x,y)\,\mu _{1}(\mathrm {d} x)\right|^{p}\mu _{2}(\mathrm {d} y)\right)^{\frac {1}{p}}\leq \int _{S_{1}}\left(\int _{S_{2}}|F(x,y)|^{p}\,\mu _{2}(\mathrm {d} y)\right)^{\frac {1}{p}}\mu _{1}(\mathrm {d} x)=\|f_{1}\|_{p}+\|f_{2}\|_{p}.}

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Use counting measure in the outer variable of Minkowski integral inequality while retaining the arbitrary measure space in the inner norm. General Lp functions and nested Lebesgue integration remain essential.
