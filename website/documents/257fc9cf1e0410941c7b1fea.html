Statement

Let G be a finite simple undirected graph (edges join distinct vertices with at most one edge per pair). A Hamiltonian path visits every vertex once, and a Hamiltonian cycle returns to its start after visiting every vertex once. If every vertex has degree three, then the number of Hamiltonian cycles through any fixed edge is even.

Proof 1: Hamiltonian cycles through an edge of a cubic graph — outline

Fix an edge uv. The argument applies to graphs in which all vertices have odd degree; in particular it applies to a cubic graph.

Define an exchange graph {\displaystyle H}, the vertices of which are in one-to-one correspondence with the Hamiltonian paths in {\displaystyle G} beginning at {\displaystyle u} and continuing through edge {\displaystyle uv}. Two such paths {\displaystyle p_{1}} and {\displaystyle p_{2}} are defined as being connected by an edge in {\displaystyle H} if one may obtain {\displaystyle p_{2}} by adding a new edge to the end of {\displaystyle p_{1}} and removing another edge from the resulting cycle in {\displaystyle p_{1}}. The resulting path must have the same beginning u,uv. This operation is reversible, forming a symmetric relation, so {\displaystyle H} is an undirected graph. If path {\displaystyle p} ends at vertex {\displaystyle w}, then the vertex corresponding to {\displaystyle p} in {\displaystyle H} has degree equal to the number of ways that {\displaystyle p} may be extended by an edge that does not connect back to {\displaystyle u}; that is, the degree of this vertex in {\displaystyle H} is either {\displaystyle \deg(w)-1} (an even number) if {\displaystyle p} does not form part of a Hamiltonian cycle through {\displaystyle uv}, or {\displaystyle \deg(w)-2} (an odd number) if {\displaystyle p} is part of a Hamiltonian cycle through {\displaystyle uv}. By the handshaking lemma, since {\displaystyle H} has an even number of odd vertices, {\displaystyle G} must have an even number of Hamiltonian cycles through {\displaystyle uv}.
