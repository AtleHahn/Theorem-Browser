Statement

Let G be a finite nonabelian simple group of odd order whose proper subgroups are all solvable. If E\le G is an elementary abelian subgroup of rank three, then there is exactly one maximal proper subgroup of G containing E. Here simple means having no normal subgroups other than 1 and G; solvable means that the sequence of successive commutator subgroups eventually becomes trivial. Elementary abelian of rank three means isomorphic to (\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^3 for some prime p. A maximal proper subgroup is proper and is contained in no larger proper subgroup.

Level justification

The uniqueness argument depends on coprime action on chief factors, Fitting and p-core series, self-centralizing Sylow subgroups, and the interaction of transitivity with ZJ normalizer control. Its prerequisite chain requires coordinating solvable-group structure and characteristic subgroup theory well beyond introductory abstract algebra plus the reading allowance. Individual subgroup calculations are elementary, but they do not account for the preparation needed for the complete argument.
