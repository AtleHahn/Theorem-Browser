Statement

Let p be a prime and let K be a finite field extension of \mathbb Q_p, the completion of \mathbb Q for the p-adic absolute value |p|_p=p^{-1}. A nonsingular quadratic form on K^n, n\geq1, is a polynomial q(x)=x^{\mathsf T}Ax with A an invertible symmetric matrix over K. Two forms are equivalent if they have the same dimension and one becomes the other under an invertible K-linear change of variables.

Choose a diagonalization q\simeq\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_i x_i^2, with a_i\in K^{\times}. Define its determinant square class and Hasse invariant by

d(q)=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}a_i\right)(K^{\times})^2\in K^{\times}/(K^{\times})^2, \qquad h(q)=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(a_i,a_j)_K.

Here (K^{\times})^2 is the subgroup of nonzero squares and the quadratic Hilbert symbol is

(a,b)_K=\begin{cases}1,&z^2=ax^2+by^2\text{ has a solution }(x,y,z)\in K^3\setminus\{(0,0,0)\},\\-1,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

An empty product is 1. The displayed invariants are independent of the chosen diagonalization.

Two nonsingular quadratic forms over K are equivalent if and only if their dimensions, determinant square classes and Hasse invariants agree.
