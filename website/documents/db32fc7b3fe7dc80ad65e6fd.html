Statement

In a finite triangulation of triangle ABC, label A,B,C by 1,2,3 and use only endpoint labels on each boundary side. Label all vertices by 1,2,3. Traverse the boundary A\to B\to C\to A and let its degree be the number of 1\to2 transitions minus the number of 2\to1 transitions. This degree is 1; consequently at least one small triangle has all three labels.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The signed transition count and coloring conditions are finite, explicitly defined planar notions.

Proof 1: Count boundary transitions and apply the degree bound

If a labeling satisfies the Sperner condition, then its degree is exactly 1: there are 1–2 and 2–1 switches only in the side between vertices 1 and 2, and the number of 1–2 switches must be one more than the number of 2–1 switches (when walking from vertex 1 to vertex 2).

Musin’s theorem states that the number of fully labeled triangles is at least the degree of the labeling. Therefore there is at least one fully labeled triangle.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Count alternating boundary-label changes and apply the explicitly stated lower bound. The degree here is defined as an integer count, with no topological degree theory required.
