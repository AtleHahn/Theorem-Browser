Statement

Consider an open Jackson network of m queues in equilibrium, with equilibrium queue-length distribution \pi on \mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m. At each node there is one first-come-first-served server with independent exponential service times of positive rate \mu_i; external arrivals are independent Poisson processes, and a service completion at node i is routed to node j with probability p_{ij} or leaves the network with the remaining probability. Routing choices, service times and external arrival processes are independent. Queues have unlimited capacity. For any node receiving arrivals at positive equilibrium rate, the vector of queue lengths seen by an arriving customer has distribution \pi. The observation is taken before that customer joins its destination queue and with that customer excluded from all queue counts; for an internal transfer this means after removing it from the origin queue. Queue length otherwise counts both waiting and in-service customers. Thus, for every \mathbf n\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m, the probability of seeing \mathbf n at an arrival equals \pi(\mathbf n). The arrival distribution is the stationary distribution sampled at arrivals to the chosen node, rather than at uniformly sampled times.

Proof 1: Product-form arrival-rate conditioning (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Slide 12: Arrival theorem of open networks; supplemental IIT Delhi ELL785 Lecture 3 slides 156–158, PDF pp.39–40.).

Write \lambda_i for total node input and \alpha_j for external input. For an arrival routed i\to j, the vector with the customer removed is n precisely when the pre-completion state is n+e_i. The product law gives \pi(n+e_i)\mu_i p_{ij}=\pi(n)\lambda_i p_{ij}. External arrivals contribute \pi(n)\alpha_j. Summing and using the traffic equation gives \pi(n)\lambda_j; divide by the positive arrival rate \lambda_j. This includes feedback i=j and zero-rate origins, whose contribution is zero.
