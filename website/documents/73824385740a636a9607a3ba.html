Statement

Orient the edges of a finite simple cycle of odd length at least three. Then the resulting directed graph contains a path on three distinct vertices.

Proof 1: Odd cyclic alternation is impossible

If two consecutive cycle edges never formed a directed path, their directions would have to alternate around the cycle. Alternation cannot close consistently around an odd number of edges. Thus two consecutive edges form the required path.

Level
High school only
Level justification

If adjacent edges never form a directed path, directions must alternate, which cannot close on an odd cycle. Elementary finite parity reasoning suffices.
