Statement

For a prime p and a positive integer n, let A_{p,n}=\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}(-1)^m\binom{p-1}{m}m^{2n}. Then A_{p,n}\equiv-1\pmod p if p-1\mid2n, and A_{p,n}\equiv0\pmod p otherwise.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Congruence expresses equality of integer remainders; the statement otherwise uses finite sums and divisibility.

Proof 1: Fermat reduction and vanishing finite differences

Let S(r,j) denote the number of partitions of an r-element set into j nonempty unlabeled subsets. Use the finite-difference identity

\sum_{m=0}^{j}(-1)^m\binom jm m^r=(-1)^j j!S(r,j),

and S(r,j)=0 when j>r.

One has from Fermat's little theorem,

{\displaystyle m^{p-1}\equiv 1{\pmod {p}}}

for m = 1, 2, ..., p – 1.

If p – 1 divides 2n**, then one has

{\displaystyle m^{2n}\equiv 1{\pmod {p}}}

for m = 1, 2, ..., p – 1. Thereafter, one has

{\displaystyle \sum _{m=1}^{p-1}(-1)^{m}{\binom {p-1}{m}}m^{2n}\equiv \sum _{m=1}^{p-1}(-1)^{m}{\binom {p-1}{m}}{\pmod {p}},}

from which the first case follows immediately.

If p – 1 does not divide 2n**, apply Fermat's theorem as follows. If one lets ℘ = ⌊ 2n / (p – 1) ⌋, then after iteration one has

{\displaystyle m^{2n}\equiv m^{2n-\wp (p-1)}{\pmod {p}}}

for m = 1, 2, ..., p – 1 and 0 < 2n – ℘(p – 1) < p – 1.

Thereafter, one has

{\displaystyle \sum _{m=0}^{p-1}(-1)^{m}{\binom {p-1}{m}}m^{2n}\equiv \sum _{m=0}^{p-1}(-1)^{m}{\binom {p-1}{m}}m^{2n-\wp (p-1)}{\pmod {p}}.}

The second case follows from this congruence and the fact that S(n,j) = 0 for j > n.
