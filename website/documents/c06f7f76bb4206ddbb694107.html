Statement

Let (X,d) be a nonempty compact metric space and T:X\to X satisfy d(Tx,Ty)<d(x,y) whenever x\ne y. Then T has a unique fixed point.

Proof 1: Minimum displacement outline

In a non-empty compact metric space, any function {\displaystyle T} satisfying {\displaystyle d(T(x),T(y))<d(x,y)} for all distinct {\displaystyle x,y}, has a unique fixed point. The proof is simpler than the Banach theorem, because the function {\displaystyle d(T(x),x)} is continuous, and therefore assumes a minimum, which is easily shown to be zero.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The strict contraction is continuous. Minimize its continuous displacement on compact X; a positive minimum would decrease after applying T. Two distinct fixed points violate the strict inequality. This is standard metric analysis.
