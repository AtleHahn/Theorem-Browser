Statement

Let f be a nonzero entire function of finite order \rho, with nonzero zeros (a_n) repeated with multiplicity. If p=\lfloor\rho\rfloor, then \sum_n|a_n|^{-p-1}<\infty. Here the finite order \rho\ge0 means \rho=\limsup_{r\to\infty}\log\log\max(e,M_f(r))/\log r, where M_f(r)=\max_{|z|=r}|f(z)|.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Entire means complex differentiable everywhere, and finite order is a growth condition given by a limsup of logarithms. Real analysis supplies zero counts, series, growth estimates and limits; no general entire-function factorization theory is needed to understand these claims.

Proof 1: Reciprocal powers of finite-order zeros are summable — source argument

If there are only finitely many nonzero zeros, the sum is finite. Otherwise, let f be an entire function of finite order \rho. If f has a zero of order m at the origin, write

f(z)=z^m f_0(z),

where f_0 is entire and f_0(0)\ne0. Factoring off z^m and multiplying by a nonzero constant do not affect the order or the nonzero zeros, so it is enough to work under the normalization f(0)=1.

Let a_1,a_2,\ldots be the nonzero zeros of f, repeated according to multiplicity and ordered so that

|a_1|\leq |a_2|\leq\cdots.

Let p=\lfloor\rho\rfloor. We show that

\sum_{n=1}^\infty |a_n|^{-p-1}<\infty.

Indeed, let n(r) denote the number of zeros of f in |z|<r, counted with multiplicity. By Jensen's formula, one obtains an estimate of the form

n(r)\log2\leq\log M(2r),

where

M(r)=\max_{|z|=r}|f(z)|.

Since f has order \rho, for every \varepsilon>0 and all sufficiently large r,

\log M(r)\leq r^{\rho+\varepsilon}.

Choosing \varepsilon>0 so small that \rho+\varepsilon<p+1, it follows that

n(r)=O(r^{\rho+\varepsilon}).

Since k\leq n(2|a_k|), this gives

|a_k|\geq Ck^{1/(\rho+\varepsilon)}

for all sufficiently large k and some constant C>0. Therefore

|a_k|^{-p-1}\leq C'k^{-(p+1)/(\rho+\varepsilon)},

and the exponent on the right is greater than 1. Hence

\sum_{n=1}^\infty |a_n|^{-p-1}<\infty.
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Jensen zero counting and entire-function order first control the moduli of zeros, after factoring the origin with its multiplicity. The final p-series comparison is undergraduate, but the preceding analytic-zero framework is substantial complex analysis.
