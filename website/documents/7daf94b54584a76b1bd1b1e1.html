Statement

If H is an abelian normal subgroup of a finite group G with \gcd(|H|,[G:H])=1, any two complements K,L (subgroups with G=HK=HL and H\cap K=H\cap L=\{1\}) are conjugate in G.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Finite groups, subgroups, normality, conjugation and permutations belong to introductory abstract algebra. The complement condition is supplied explicitly where needed; understanding the assertion requires no new theory.

Proof 1: First-cohomology vanishing

If H is abelian, the fact that all complements are conjugate follows from the vanishing of H^1(G/H,H) (as H and G/H have coprime orders). Here one uses the standard cohomological classification of complements up to conjugacy.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement uses elementary finite-group notions, but the supplied proof is solely a citation to H^1 vanishing and its classification of complements. To understand those citations one must introduce the quotient action on H, crossed homomorphisms and equivalence modulo principal crossed homomorphisms. It is not clear that the combined cohomological interpretation fits the small extension allowance. Retain Beyond undergraduate provisionally rather than replacing the actual proof with a different elementary argument.

Treatment:

Cited without proof

Statement used:

If a finite group Q acts on a finite abelian group H and gcd(|Q|,|H|)=1, then H^1(Q,H)=0.

Application in proof:

Take Q=G/H with the conjugation action, which is well-defined because H is abelian. The normal Hall condition gives coprime orders.

Application level:

Uncertain

Level justification:

The coprime condition is elementary, but understanding the first-cohomology group requires crossed homomorphisms and their equivalence classes. The prerequisite burden is not confidently within the allowance.

H¹ classifies complements up to conjugacy in an abelian extension
Kind:

Theorem

Treatment:

Cited without proof

Statement used:

For a split extension with abelian normal subgroup H and quotient Q, H-conjugacy classes of complements are parametrized by H^1(Q,H) for the quotient conjugation action.

Application in proof:

A given complement provides the splitting; vanishing gives a single H-conjugacy class, hence the two complements are conjugate in G.

Application level:

Uncertain

Level justification:

One must connect complements to crossed homomorphisms and understand the quotient by principal ones. A mere name for H^1 does not establish understanding of this classification.
