Statement

If H is an abelian normal subgroup of a finite group G with \gcd(|H|,[G:H])=1, any two complements K,L (subgroups with G=HK=HL and H\cap K=H\cap L=\{1\}) are conjugate in G.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Finite groups, subgroups, normality, conjugation and permutations belong to introductory abstract algebra. The complement condition is supplied explicitly where needed; understanding the assertion requires no new theory.

Proof 1: First-cohomology vanishing

If H is abelian, the fact that all complements are conjugate follows from the vanishing of H^1(G/H,H) (as H and G/H have coprime orders). Here one uses the standard cohomological classification of complements up to conjugacy.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The two first-cohomology dependencies reduce to explicit finite-group calculations. Crossed homomorphisms form an abelian group of functions, principal crossed homomorphisms form a subgroup, and their quotient records conjugacy classes of complement graphs. Summing the crossed-homomorphism equation and using coprimality makes that quotient zero. Introductory group theory supplies the background; the function-group definitions and their interpretation fit the short preparation allowance.

1. Coprime-order vanishing of H¹
Kind:

Theorem

Treatment:

Cited without proof

Statement used:

If a finite group Q acts on a finite abelian group H and gcd(|Q|,|H|)=1, then H^1(Q,H)=0.

Application in proof:

Take Q=G/H with the conjugation action, which is well-defined because H is abelian. The normal Hall condition gives coprime orders.

Application level:

Undergraduate level

Level justification:

Summing the crossed-homomorphism identity over the finite quotient gives n c(q)=S−q·S. Bezout and coprime orders allow division by n in H, making every crossed homomorphism principal.

2. H¹ classifies complements up to conjugacy in an abelian extension
Kind:

Theorem

Treatment:

Cited without proof

Statement used:

For a split extension with abelian normal subgroup H and quotient Q, H-conjugacy classes of complements are parametrized by H^1(Q,H) for the quotient conjugation action.

Application in proof:

A given complement provides the splitting; vanishing gives a single H-conjugacy class, hence the two complements are conjugate in G.

Application level:

Undergraduate level

Level justification:

Using a given complement, write the group as pairs with the conjugation action. Other complements are graphs of crossed homomorphisms; conjugation by H changes the function by a principal crossed homomorphism. This proves the required classification with elementary group calculations.
