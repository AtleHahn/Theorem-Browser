Statement

A simple undirected graph has a vertex set V and edges that are unordered pairs of distinct vertices. A proper k-coloring is a map V\to\{1,\ldots,k\} assigning different values to adjacent vertices. Let G be finite. If each edge can be assigned a direction so that at most k edges point out of each vertex, where k\ge0 is an integer, then G has a proper (2k+1)-coloring.

High school only

Proof 1: Remove a low-degree vertex recursively — outline

For a nonempty finite graph this follows because such graphs always have a vertex of degree at most 2k, which can be colored with one of 2k+1 colors after all the remaining vertices are colored recursively.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The outdegree bound gives at most k times the number of vertices edges, so average degree is at most 2k. Every induced subgraph inherits the same orientation bound. Remove a low-degree vertex, color the rest by induction, and restore it using one of 2k+1 colors. This is elementary counting and induction.
