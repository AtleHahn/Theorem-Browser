Use the finite Schreier coset graph as a combinatorial graph: e vertices and ne positively labeled directed edges. Choose a spanning tree. Reduced-word rewriting shows the non-tree edges form a free basis of H, as in the algebraic Schreier argument, without a topological fundamental group. There are ne-(e-1)=1+e(n-1) such edges. The trivial rank-zero case has e=1 and is separate. Ordinary groups and finite graph counting suffice.

Statement

If G is a free group of finite rank n and H has finite index e in G, then H is free of rank 1+e(n−1).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Group theory supplies subgroup index. A free group is the reduced-word group on generators, and its rank is the number of free generators. Those closely related definitions suffice for the explicit index formula.

Proof 1: Count Euler characteristic in the finite cover

A short proof of the Nielsen–Schreier theorem uses the algebraic topology of fundamental groups and covering spaces. A free group G on a set of generators is the fundamental group of a bouquet of circles, a topological graph X with a single vertex and with a loop-edge for each generator. Any subgroup H of the fundamental group is itself the fundamental group of a connected covering space Y → X. The space Y is a finite topological graph, the Schreier coset graph having one vertex for each coset in G/H. In a connected finite topological graph, it is possible to shrink the edges of a spanning tree of the graph, producing a bouquet of circles that has the same fundamental group H. Since H is the fundamental group of a bouquet of circles, it is itself free.

The rank of H can be computed using two properties of Euler characteristic of a graph (the number of vertices minus the number of edges), which remains invariant under contraction of edges (homotopy). The first property is that the Euler characteristic of a bouquet of s circles is 1 - s. The second property is multiplicativity in covering spaces: If Y is a degree-d cover of X, then

{\displaystyle \chi (Y)=d\cdot \chi (X)}.

Now suppose H is a subgroup of the free group G, with index [G : H] = e. As above, consider the covering graph Y corresponding to H, and contract edges to make it a bouquet of r circles, so that H is a free group of rank r. Applying the two properties of Euler characteristic gives:

{\displaystyle \chi (Y)=1-r}

and

{\displaystyle \chi (Y)=e\cdot \chi (X)=e(1-n).}

Combining these equations, we obtain {\displaystyle r=1-e(1-n)=1+e(n-1).}
