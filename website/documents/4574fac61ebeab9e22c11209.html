Statement

Fix \varepsilon>0. For Lebesgue-almost every real number x, infinitely many distinct rational numbers p/q, p\in\mathbb Z, q\in\mathbb Z_{>0}, satisfy |x-p/q|<\varepsilon/q^2.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Real analysis supplies rational approximation and infinitely many solutions of explicit inequalities. A null subset of the line can be defined by interval covers of arbitrarily small total length; this limited addition suffices to understand the almost-everywhere claim.

Proof 1: Apply the divergent harmonic-series case — outline

Apply Khinchin’s theorem with \psi_\varepsilon(q)=\varepsilon q^{-1}. The relevant series diverges, so almost every real number is \psi_\varepsilon-approximable. Its defining inequality is exactly |x-p/q|<\varepsilon/q^2.
