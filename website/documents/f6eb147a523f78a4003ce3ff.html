Statement

Let r>1 be irrational and m\in\mathcal B_r=\{\lfloor nr\rfloor:n\ge1\}. Then

m=\left\lfloor\left(\left\lfloor\frac mr\right\rfloor+1\right)r\right\rfloor.

Thus the index of m in the strictly increasing sequence (\lfloor nr\rfloor)_{n\ge1} is \lfloor m/r\rfloor+1.

Proof 1: Identify the next integer above m/r

First, the fractional-part membership criterion follows from

\begin{aligned} m\in\mathcal B_r &\Longleftrightarrow \exists n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}:\ m=\lfloor nr\rfloor\\ &\Longleftrightarrow \exists n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}:\ m<nr<m+1\\ &\Longleftrightarrow \exists n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}:\ \frac mr<n<\frac mr+\frac1r\\ &\Longleftrightarrow \exists n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}:\ n-\frac1r<\frac mr<n\\ &\Longleftrightarrow 1-\frac1r<\left\{\frac mr\right\}. \end{aligned}

Here \{x\}=x-\lfloor x\rfloor denotes the fractional part of x.

Furthermore, for a Beatty term m,

\begin{aligned} m=\left\lfloor(\lfloor m/r\rfloor+1)r\right\rfloor &\iff m<(\lfloor m/r\rfloor+1)r<m+1\\ &\iff m/r<\lfloor m/r\rfloor+1<(m+1)/r\\ &\iff \lfloor m/r\rfloor+1-1/r<m/r<\lfloor m/r\rfloor+1\\ &\iff 1-1/r<m/r-\lfloor m/r\rfloor=\{m/r\}. \end{aligned}

The last condition holds by membership. Since r>1, the Beatty sequence is strictly increasing, so this gives its index.

Level justification

The proof first derives the membership criterion by elementary floor inequalities, then substitutes the only possible integer index floor(m/r)+1. Since increasing n by one increases nr by more than one, the floor sequence is strictly increasing and this index is unique. No analysis of infinite-sequence limits is needed.
