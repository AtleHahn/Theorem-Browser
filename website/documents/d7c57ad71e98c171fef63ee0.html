Statement

Let A\subseteq B=A[x_1,\ldots,x_r] be integral domains, and assume each x_i is algebraic over the fraction field of A. There exists 0\ne a\in A such that B[a^{-1}] is integral over A[a^{-1}].

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

From introductory ring algebra, domains, fraction fields, polynomial relations and adjoining finitely many elements are familiar. The extra definitions are concrete: A[a^-1] allows denominators that are powers of a, and integrality means satisfying a monic polynomial over the base ring, for every element of the extension. These closely related extensions of fraction and polynomial arithmetic fit about 30 minutes of preparation and give the complete meaning of the existence claim, without general localization theory.

Proof 1: Invert the product of leading coefficients

Each generator x_i satisfies a relation with coefficients in A and nonzero leading coefficient:

{\displaystyle a_{i0}x_{i}^{n}+a_{i1}x_{i}^{n-1}+\dots +a_{in}=0,\,\,a_{ij}\in A}

where n depends on i and {\displaystyle a_{i0}\neq 0}. Set {\displaystyle a=a_{10}a_{20}\dots a_{r0}}. Then {\displaystyle B[a^{-1}]} is integral over {\displaystyle A[a^{-1}]}.

Level justification

Introductory ring algebra supplies domains, polynomial rings and fraction fields. The extra notions here are concrete: A[a^-1] consists of fractions with denominators powers of a, and an integral element satisfies a monic polynomial over the base ring. After clearing coefficient denominators, inverting the product makes every leading coefficient a unit; the stated closure theorem then handles every element of the generated algebra. These closely related extensions of polynomial and fraction arithmetic fit the small additional-preparation allowance; no general localization or integral-extension structure theory is used.

An algebra generated by integral elements is integral
Kind:

Theorem

Treatment:

Implicitly assumed

Statement used:

If finitely many elements of a commutative extension ring are integral over R, then every element of the algebra they generate is integral over R.

Application in proof:

Take R=A[a^-1]; dividing each relation by its now-invertible leading coefficient makes each generator integral, and B[a^-1]=R[x_1,...,x_r].

Application level:

Undergraduate level

Level justification:

To apply the result one only needs the monic-polynomial definition and generated-ring notation; proving the closure theorem is not required.

Review notes:

For each leading coefficient a_i, its inverse in A[a^-1] is the product of the other leading coefficients divided by a. If there are no generators, take a=1.
