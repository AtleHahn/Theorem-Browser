Statement

For a nonempty finite word s of length \ell and a nonnegative rational number r=m/\ell, let s^r be the first m symbols of the infinite repetition sss\cdots. For a real number e>0, an infinite word is e-power-free if no finite contiguous substring is a power s^r with r\geq e.

For an integer k\geq2, define the repetition threshold

\operatorname{RT}(k)=\inf\{e>0:\text{there is an infinite }e\text{-power-free word on a }k\text{-symbol alphabet}\}.

Then

\operatorname{RT}(k)=\begin{cases} 7/4,&k=3,\\ 7/5,&k=4,\\ k/(k-1),&k=2\text{ or }k\geq5. \end{cases}

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Word repetitions and the piecewise formula are elementary, but the exact infimum threshold for infinite sequences uses basic undergraduate real-analysis language. No automata or morphic-sequence theory is required.
