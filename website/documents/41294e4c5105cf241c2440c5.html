Statement

In a nondegenerate Euclidean triangle let a,b,c>0 be opposite \alpha,\beta,\gamma\in(0,\pi). Given a,b and their included angle \gamma, set \Delta=2\arctan\left(\frac{a-b}{a+b}\cot\frac\gamma2\right), where \arctan has values in (-\pi/2,\pi/2). Then \beta=\frac{\pi-\gamma-\Delta}{2},\qquad\alpha=\beta+\Delta,\qquad c=\frac{a\sin\gamma}{\sin\alpha}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The explicit reconstruction uses elementary triangle geometry and inverse trigonometric functions.

Proof 1: Half-angle difference and angle-sum solution

The law of tangents can be used to compute the angles of a triangle in which two sides a and b and the enclosed angle \gamma are given. From

\tan\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2} =\frac{a-b}{a+b}\tan\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2} =\frac{a-b}{a+b}\cot\frac\gamma2,

compute the angle difference \alpha-\beta=\Delta; use that to calculate

\beta=\frac{\pi-\gamma-\Delta}{2} \quad\text{and then}\quad \alpha=\beta+\Delta.

Here (\alpha-\beta)/2\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2), so the principal inverse tangent gives exactly the \Delta specified in the statement.

Once an angle opposite a known side is computed, the remaining side c can be computed using the law of sines:

c=\frac{a\sin\gamma}{\sin\alpha}.

High school only
Level justification

Use the tangent-law angle difference, choose the principal arctangent by the known angle range, solve the angle sum and apply the sine law. School trigonometry suffices.
