Statement

Let x,y,a,b be positive integers with a>1 and b>1. Then

x^a-y^b=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad (x,y,a,b)=(3,2,2,3).

Thus 8=2^3 and 9=3^2 are the only consecutive positive integers both expressible as powers with positive integer base and integer exponent greater than one.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All objects and hypotheses use positive integers and powers; the uniqueness assertion is understandable with standard high-school algebra. No transcendence theory or cyclotomic-field proof background is needed.

Proof 1: Cyclotomic units and the prime-exponent reduction (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Bourbaki exposé 909, pp.1–26: reduction in Section 1; Theorem 1.3, Section 6 and supporting Sections 2–9.).

If a base is 1, the equation has no solution under a,b>1. Otherwise choose prime divisors of the exponents and apply the source’s reduction. The exceptional reduced powers are 9 and 8; their prime factorizations force (x,a)=(3,2) and (y,b)=(2,3).
