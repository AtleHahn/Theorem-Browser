Statement

Work with polynomials whose coefficients are integers modulo 8: coefficients are added and multiplied modulo 8, and two polynomials are equal when corresponding coefficients have the same remainder. In (\mathbb Z/8\mathbb Z)[x], P(x)=x^2-1 has roots 1,3,5,7 and factorizations (x-1)(x-7)=(x-3)(x-5)=P(x), but P(x)\ne(x-1)(x-3). Thus choosing roots does not by itself supply a factorization to which Vieta’s formulas apply over a ring with zero divisors. A zero divisor is a nonzero residue whose product with another nonzero residue is zero.

High school only
Level justification

Proof 1: Check the modulo-eight factors outline

Over a ring that is not an integral domain, some care is needed. For example, in the ring of the integers modulo 8, the quadratic polynomial P(x)=x^2-1 has four roots (1, 3, 5, and 7) and two different factorizations:

x^2-1=(x-1)(x-7)=(x-3)(x-5).

As P(x)\ne(x-1)(x-3), one cannot apply Vieta's formulas with the roots 1 and 3. The source gives these roots and factorizations without the finite arithmetic checks.

Level justification

Square four residues and expand the three proposed quadratic products modulo eight. The explicitly defined remainder arithmetic requires only integer calculations.
