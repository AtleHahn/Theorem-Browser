Statement

Let X be a real or complex Hausdorff locally convex topological vector space and let K\subseteq X be compact, not necessarily convex. Define its extreme points by

\operatorname{ext}(K)=\{p\in K:\text{there are no distinct }x,y\in K\text{ and }0<t<1\text{ with }p=tx+(1-t)y\}.

Then

\overline{\operatorname{co}}(K)=\overline{\operatorname{co}}(\operatorname{ext}(K)).

In particular, when K is also convex, K=\overline{\operatorname{co}}(\operatorname{ext}(K)).

A topological vector space has continuous vector addition and scalar multiplication; Hausdorff means distinct points have disjoint neighborhoods, and locally convex means that zero has a neighborhood basis of convex sets. Convexity uses real coefficients, including in a complex space. The convex hull \operatorname{co}(S) is the set of finite convex combinations \sum_jt_js_j with s_j\in S, t_j\ge0, and \sum_jt_j=1. The bar denotes closure in X, and the convex hull of the empty set is empty.
