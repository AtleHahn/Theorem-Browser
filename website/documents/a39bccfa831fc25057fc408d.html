Statement

For a fixed integer n\ge3, the following are equivalent: x^n+y^n=z^n has a solution in positive integers; it has a solution in nonzero integers; it has a solution in nonzero rational numbers; and u^n+v^n=1 has a rational solution with uv\ne0.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The equivalence concerns integer powers, signs, rational numbers and nonzero solutions.

Proof 1: Change signs and clear denominators

Write \mathbb N=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}, \mathbb Z for the integers, and \mathbb Q for the rationals. A non-trivial solution means one whose coordinates are all nonzero.

The equivalence between positive and nonzero integer solutions is clear if n is even (replace each integer by its absolute value). If n is odd and all three of x, y, z are negative, then we can replace x, y, z with −x, −y, −z to obtain a solution in N. If two of them are negative, it must be x and z or y and z. If x, z are negative and y is positive, then we can rearrange to get (−z)n + yn = (−x)n resulting in a solution in N; the other case is dealt with analogously. Now if just one is negative, it must be x or y. If x is negative, and y and z are positive, then it can be rearranged to get (−x)n + zn = yn again resulting in a solution in N; if y is negative, the result follows symmetrically. Thus in all cases a nontrivial solution in Z would also mean a solution exists in N, the original formulation of the problem.

The exponents of x, y, and z are equal (to n), so if there is a solution in Q, then it can be multiplied through by an appropriate common denominator to get a solution in Z, and hence in N.

A non-trivial solution a, b, c ∈ Z to xn + yn = zn yields the non-trivial solution a/c, b/c ∈ Q for vn + wn = 1. Conversely, a non-trivial solution a/b, c/d ∈ Q to vn + wn = 1 yields the non-trivial solution ad, cb, bd for xn + yn = zn.

The reverse inclusions of positive integers into nonzero integers and of integers into rationals are immediate.

Handle signs according to parity, clear common denominators and divide by the nonzero third coordinate. Elementary integer and rational powers suffice.
