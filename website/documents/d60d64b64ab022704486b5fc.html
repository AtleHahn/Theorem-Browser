Statement

Let G be a finite graph of treewidth at most an integer k\ge0, such that adding any missing edge raises its treewidth above k. Then G is chordal, so it is its own chordal completion.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Treewidth requires learning tree decompositions: vertex bags cover edges and bags containing a fixed vertex form a connected subtree. Chordality excludes induced cycles of length at least four, and completion adds edges. These are concrete finite graph definitions that fit brief preparation from sets and trees; no algorithmic graph theory is needed.

Proof 1: Edge-maximal graphs of bounded treewidth are their own chordal completions — outline

Use the source characterization of treewidth by a chordal completion with smallest maximum-clique size. It provides a chordal completion of G with treewidth at most k. By the source edge-maximality condition, no edge can be added while keeping treewidth at most k, so this completion must already be G. Hence G is chordal.
