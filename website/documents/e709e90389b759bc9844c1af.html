Statement

For a nondegenerate triangle with sides a,b,c and semiperimeter s=(a+b+c)/2, the altitude to side a is h_a=\frac{2\sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}}a.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement concerns elementary plane geometry and real arithmetic.

Proof 1: Triangle altitude in terms of side lengths — source argument

This follows from combining Heron's formula for the area of a triangle in terms of the sides with the area formula {\displaystyle {\tfrac {1}{2}}\times {\text{base}}\times {\text{height}},} where the base is taken as side a and the height is the altitude from the vertex A (opposite side a).

Level
High school only
Level justification

Equate Heron area with half base times altitude and solve for the altitude. School algebra and geometry suffice.
