Statement

Let S\subset\mathbb Z be finite, let \gamma_m\in\mathbb R for m\in S, and define (Tu)_j=\sum_{m\in S}\gamma_m u_{j+m} for every real sequence (u_j)_{j\in\mathbb Z}. The operator T sends every nondecreasing sequence to a nondecreasing sequence if and only if every \gamma_m\ge0.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The operator is explicitly a finite weighted sum of shifted real-sequence terms. Bi-infinite indexing adds integer labels, not infinite summation or functional-analysis prerequisites; monotonicity is an elementary inequality.

Proof 1: Compare adjacent differences and test a step sequence

Case 1: sufficient condition

Assume that \gamma_m\ge0 for all m\in S and that u_j is nondecreasing with j. Then

(Tu)_j-(Tu)_{j-1} =\sum_{m\in S}\gamma_m\bigl(u_{j+m}-u_{j+m-1}\bigr)\ge0.

This means that monotonicity is preserved for this case.

Case 2: necessary condition

We prove the necessary condition by contradiction. Assume that \gamma_p<0 for some p\in S and choose the following nondecreasing sequence, for an integer k:

u_i=0,\quad i<k;\qquad u_i=1,\quad i\ge k.

Then from the definition of T we get

(Tu)_j-(Tu)_{j-1} =\sum_{m\in S}\gamma_m\bigl(u_{j+m}-u_{j+m-1}\bigr),

where

u_{j+m}-u_{j+m-1} =\begin{cases}0,&j+m\ne k,\\1,&j+m=k.\end{cases}

Now choose j=k-p, to give

(Tu)_{k-p}-(Tu)_{k-p-1} =\gamma_p(u_k-u_{k-1})<0,

which implies that Tu is not nondecreasing, and we have a contradiction. Thus, monotonicity is not preserved for \gamma_p<0, which completes the proof.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Nonnegative coefficients preserve the sign of adjacent differences; a step sequence isolates any negative coefficient. All sums are finite and the sequence notation needs only elementary real inequalities.
