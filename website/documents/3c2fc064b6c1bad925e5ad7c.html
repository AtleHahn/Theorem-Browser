Statement

A partition of a nonnegative integer is a multiset of positive integers with that sum; zero has the empty partition. For every n\ge0, the number of partitions of n into odd parts equals the number into pairwise distinct parts.

Level
High school only
Level justification

A partition is explicitly a finite sum of positive integers considered without order; divisibility and repetitions are elementary.

Proof 1: Specialize Glaisher’s theorem to two

Use Glaisher’s partition theorem: for d\ge2, excluding parts divisible by d gives the same count as restricting each multiplicity to less than d. Set d=2. Parts not divisible by 2 are odd, and multiplicity less than 2 means that no part repeats.
