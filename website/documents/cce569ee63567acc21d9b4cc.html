Statement

For a variety X/\mathbb F_q, write N_m=|X(\mathbb F_{q^m})| and Z(X,T)=\exp(\sum_{m\geq1}N_m T^m/m) as a formal power series. For a smooth projective geometrically connected n-dimensional variety X/\mathbb F_q, choose a prime \ell\ne\operatorname{char}\mathbb F_q and put E=\sum_i(-1)^i\dim H^i_{\mathrm{et}}(X_{\overline{\mathbb F}_q},\mathbb Q_\ell). Then Z(X,q^{-n}T^{-1})=\pm q^{nE/2}T^E Z(X,T).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves smooth projective varieties over finite fields; etale cohomology; Euler characteristic; local zeta functions. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Pair reciprocal Frobenius eigenvalues outline

Use Z(X,T)=\prod_i P_i(T)^{(-1)^{i+1}}, where P_i(T)=\det(1-TF\mid H^i_{\mathrm{et}}(X_{\overline{\mathbb F}_q},\mathbb Q_\ell)) and \ell\ne\operatorname{char}\mathbb F_q. The functional equation for the zeta function follows from Poincaré duality for \ell-adic cohomology: the inverse roots in degrees i and 2n-i pair as \alpha and q^n/\alpha. Applying this pairing to the alternating determinant product gives the stated functional equation. The determinant rearrangement is not expanded in the source.
