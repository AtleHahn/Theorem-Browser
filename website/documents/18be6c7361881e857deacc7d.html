Statement

Let a<b be real and let K:[a,b]^2\to\mathbb R be continuous and symmetric. Suppose it is positive definite in the non-strict sense: \sum_{i,j}K(x_i,x_j)c_ic_j\ge0 for all finite point lists and real coefficients. On real L^2[a,b], put (T_Kf)(t)=\int_a^bK(t,s)f(s)\,ds. Every eigenfunction with nonzero eigenvalue has a continuous representative.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Eigenfunctions here are equivalence classes in Lebesgue L2, and the conclusion concerns continuous representatives.

Proof 1: Integral continuity and the nonzero eigenvalue equation

For an eigenfunction e_i with eigenvalue \lambda_i,

\lambda_i e_i(t)=[T_K e_i](t)=\int_a^b K(t,s)e_i(s)\,ds.

If \lambda_i\ne0, the eigenfunction e_i is seen to have a continuous representative on [a,b].

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Use uniform continuity of the kernel and L2 Cauchy–Schwarz to obtain a continuous integral image, then divide by the nonzero eigenvalue. General L2 functions and continuous representatives of almost-everywhere classes require measure-theoretic functional analysis.
