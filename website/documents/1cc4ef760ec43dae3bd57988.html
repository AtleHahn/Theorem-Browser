Likely alternative prerequisite level: Undergraduate level; lowest recorded proof level: Beyond undergraduate level. Prove closedness of the graph directly: if x_j->x and Ax_j->y, symmetry gives <y,z>=lim<x_j,Az>=<x,Az>=<Ax,z> for every z, hence y=Ax. Apply the stated closed graph theorem for an everywhere-defined linear map between Banach spaces. The application needs only norm convergence, Hilbert inner products and completeness, a small extension of undergraduate analysis; it avoids adjoint-domain and unbounded-self-adjoint-operator theory.

Statement

Let H be a Hilbert space and let A:H\to H be a linear operator defined everywhere such that \langle Ax,y\rangle=\langle x,Ay\rangle for all x,y\in H. Then A is bounded.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Basic topology and real analysis supply compactness, completeness, continuity and sequence convergence; linear algebra supplies inner products and orthogonality. Hilbert completeness and the explicitly specified bounds or maps are direct extensions, with no spectral or weak-topology theory needed.

Mathematical Field
Functional analysis
Required topics

Proof 1: Self-adjointness and the closed graph theorem

An everywhere-defined symmetric operator is self-adjoint. Self-adjoint operators are closed, so the everywhere-defined operator A has a closed graph. The closed graph theorem therefore makes A bounded.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Pass from everywhere-defined symmetry to self-adjointness and closedness, then apply the closed graph theorem. Hilbert adjoint domains and closed unbounded operators are graduate functional analysis.
