Statement

Let P,Q be propositions and let \bot mean falsehood, with \neg A abbreviating A\to\bot. Using implication elimination (from A and A\to B infer B) and implication introduction (a derivation of B under assumption A yields A\to B), one can derive (P\to Q)\to(\neg Q\to\neg P) without excluded middle or double-negation elimination.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Negation and both implication rules are supplied. The result is a finite derivation rather than a prerequisite in intuitionistic metatheory.

Proof 1: Compose the implication with a derivation of falsehood — outline

Assume P\to Q. Assume also Q\to\bot. Composing these implications gives P\to\bot. Discharging the second assumption yields (Q\to\bot)\to(P\to\bot), namely \neg Q\to\neg P. Discharging the first assumption proves the displayed formula.
