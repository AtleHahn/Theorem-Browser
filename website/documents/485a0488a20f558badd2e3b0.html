Statement

Let F be a field and A=\begin{pmatrix}a_1&b_1&c_1\\a_2&b_2&c_2\\a_3&b_3&c_3\end{pmatrix} be a matrix over F with \det A\ne0, and let d=(d_1,d_2,d_3)^T\in F^3. The unique solution (x,y,z)^T of A(x,y,z)^T=d is x=\det[d,b,c]/\det[a,b,c], y=\det[a,d,c]/\det[a,b,c], z=\det[a,b,d]/\det[a,b,c], where a,b,c are the columns of A and brackets denote a matrix assembled from its columns.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Matrices, determinants, kernels, linear independence and bases are standard undergraduate linear algebra. Over an algebraically closed field, the extra condition simply guarantees polynomial roots; infinite dimensions mean cardinalities of bases. The specific matrix or dimension claim adds no specialist construction.

Proof 1: Three explicit column substitutions

Given

{\displaystyle \left\{{\begin{matrix}a_{1}x+b_{1}y+c_{1}z&={d_{1}}\\a_{2}x+b_{2}y+c_{2}z&={d_{2}}\\a_{3}x+b_{3}y+c_{3}z&={d_{3}}\end{matrix}}\right.}

which in matrix format is

{\displaystyle {\begin{bmatrix}a_{1}&b_{1}&c_{1}\\a_{2}&b_{2}&c_{2}\\a_{3}&b_{3}&c_{3}\end{bmatrix}}{\begin{bmatrix}x\\y\\z\end{bmatrix}}={\begin{bmatrix}{d_{1}}\\{d_{2}}\\{d_{3}}\end{bmatrix}}.}

Since \det A\ne0, Cramer's rule gives the unique solution. The values of x, y and z can be found as follows:

{\displaystyle x={\frac {\begin{vmatrix}{d_{1}}&b_{1}&c_{1}\\{d_{2}}&b_{2}&c_{2}\\{d_{3}}&b_{3}&c_{3}\end{vmatrix}}{\begin{vmatrix}a_{1}&b_{1}&c_{1}\\a_{2}&b_{2}&c_{2}\\a_{3}&b_{3}&c_{3}\end{vmatrix}}},\quad y={\frac {\begin{vmatrix}a_{1}&{d_{1}}&c_{1}\\a_{2}&{d_{2}}&c_{2}\\a_{3}&{d_{3}}&c_{3}\end{vmatrix}}{\begin{vmatrix}a_{1}&b_{1}&c_{1}\\a_{2}&b_{2}&c_{2}\\a_{3}&b_{3}&c_{3}\end{vmatrix}}},\quad z={\frac {\begin{vmatrix}a_{1}&b_{1}&{d_{1}}\\a_{2}&b_{2}&{d_{2}}\\a_{3}&b_{3}&{d_{3}}\end{vmatrix}}{\begin{vmatrix}a_{1}&b_{1}&c_{1}\\a_{2}&b_{2}&c_{2}\\a_{3}&b_{3}&c_{3}\end{vmatrix}}}.}
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply Cramer rule to the three columns and write the replacements explicitly. Matrices and determinants over a field are standard introductory algebra.
