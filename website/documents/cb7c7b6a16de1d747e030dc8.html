Statement

Let f\in\mathbb R^N have \sum_{n=0}^{N-1}f[n]=0, let P be uniform on \{0,\ldots,N-1\}, and put Y[n]=f[(n-P)\bmod N]. Then \mathbb EY[n]=0 and R[n,k]=\mathbb E(Y[n]Y[k]) depends only on n-k modulo N. The covariance matrix is diagonalized by g_m[n]=N^{-1/2}e^{2\pi imn/N} with eigenvalues |\widehat f[m]|^2/N, where \widehat f[m]=\sum_n f[n]e^{-2\pi imn/N}.

Proof 1: Average cyclic shifts and diagonalize circular convolution

All indices are interpreted modulo N. Write \bar f[n]=f[-n] and let \Theta denote circular convolution. Use the discrete Fourier transform normalization in the statement.

Clearly

{\displaystyle \mathbf {E} \{Y[n]\}={\frac {1}{N}}\sum _{p=0}^{N-1}f[(n-p){\bmod {N}}]=0}

and

{\displaystyle R[n,k]=\mathbf {E} \{Y[n]Y[k]\}={\frac {1}{N}}\sum _{p=0}^{N-1}f[(n-p){\bmod {N}}]f[(k-p){\bmod {N}}]={\frac {1}{N}}f\Theta {\bar {f}}[n-k],\quad {\bar {f}}[n]=f[-n]}

Hence

{\displaystyle R[n,k]=R_{Y}[n-k],\qquad R_{Y}[k]={\frac {1}{N}}f\Theta {\bar {f}}[k]}

Since RY is N periodic, Y is a circular stationary random vector. The covariance operator is a circular convolution with RY and is therefore diagonalized in the discrete Fourier Karhunen–Loève basis

{\displaystyle \left\{{\frac {1}{\sqrt {N}}}e^{i2\pi mn/N}\right\}_{0\leq m<N}.}

The power spectrum is Fourier transform of RY:

{\displaystyle P_{Y}[m]={\hat {R}}_{Y}[m]={\frac {1}{N}}\left|{\hat {f}}[m]\right|^{2}}
