Statement

Let A_1,\ldots,A_m\subseteq\Omega, m\ge0, and put N(\omega)=\sum_{j=1}^m1_{A_j}(\omega) and N_k(\omega)=\sum_{J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\},\ |J|=k}1_{\cap_{j\in J}A_j}(\omega). An empty intersection is \Omega, so N_0=1. Let V be a module over a unital ring, and work on V^{\mathbb N_0}. For a sequence c=(c_0,c_1,\ldots), define (Ec)_j=c_{j+1} and \Delta=E-I; thus (\Delta c)_j=c_{j+1}-c_j. Pointwise in \omega, E^{N(\omega)}=\sum_{k=0}^mN_k(\omega)\Delta^k.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The sequence module uses coordinatewise ring-module operations, and shift/difference are explicitly defined. This is a limited extension of undergraduate algebra without operator topology.

Proof 1: Evaluate the polynomial identity at the shift

Use the polynomial identity x^{N(\omega)}=\sum_{k=0}^mN_k(\omega)(x-1)^k and substitute x=E. Since I commutes with E, polynomial evaluation gives E^N=\sum_kN_k(E-I)^k=\sum_kN_k\Delta^k.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Substitute the explicitly defined shift in a polynomial identity. Undergraduate algebra suffices after the module definition; only addition, integer multiples and composition are used, without module structure theory.

Proof 2: Expand the product of indicator-selected operators

Fix \omega\in\Omega; indicators below are evaluated at \omega. To prove the operator identity, we first want to verify the equation

{\displaystyle \sum _{n=0}^{m}1_{\{N=n\}}E^{n}=\prod _{j=1}^{m}(1_{A_{j}^{\mathrm {c} }}I+1_{A_{j}}E)}

involving indicator functions of the sets A1, ..., Am and their complements with respect to Ω. Suppose an ω from Ω belongs to exactly k sets out of A1, ..., Am, where k ∈ {0, ..., m}, for simplicity of notation say that ω only belongs to A1, ..., Ak. Then the left-hand side of the displayed product identity is Ek. On the right-hand side of the displayed product identity, the first k factors equal E, the remaining ones equal I, their product is also Ek, hence the displayed product identity is true.

Note that

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}1_{A_{j}^{\mathrm {c} }}I+1_{A_{j}}E&=I-1_{A_{j}}I+1_{A_{j}}E\\&=I+1_{A_{j}}(E-I)=I+1_{A_{j}}\Delta ,\qquad j\in \{1,\ldots ,m\}.\end{aligned}}}

Inserting this result into the displayed product identity and expanding the product gives

{\displaystyle \sum _{n=0}^{m}1_{\{N=n\}}E^{n}=\sum _{k=0}^{m}\sum _{\scriptstyle J\subset \{1,\ldots ,m\} \atop \scriptstyle |J|=k}1_{\cap _{j\in J}A_{j}}\Delta ^{k},}

because the product of indicator functions is the indicator function of the intersection. Using the definition of N_k, the operator identity follows.
