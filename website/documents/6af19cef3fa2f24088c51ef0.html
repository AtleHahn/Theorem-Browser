Statement

Let \mathbb D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}, and let H^\infty(\mathbb D) be the algebra of bounded holomorphic functions on \mathbb D. For every integer n\geq1 and every f_1,\ldots,f_n\in H^\infty(\mathbb D), the following are equivalent:

  1. There exist g_1,\ldots,g_n\in H^\infty(\mathbb D) such that\sum_{j=1}^n f_j(z)g_j(z)=1\qquad\text{for every }z\in\mathbb D.
  2. There is a real number \delta>0 such that\sum_{j=1}^n|f_j(z)|\geq\delta\qquad\text{for every }z\in\mathbb D.

A holomorphic function is complex differentiable at every point of its open domain; bounded means that its absolute value has a finite upper bound on that domain.
