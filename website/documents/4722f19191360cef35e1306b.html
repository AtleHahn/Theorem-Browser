Statement

Assume the axiom of dependent choice. Every nonempty partially ordered set in which every chain is finite has a maximal element.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Partial orders, chains, antichains and countability are undergraduate set/order concepts. A chain means pairwise comparable and an antichain pairwise incomparable; selection principles, where assumed, are explicit hypotheses. These short definitions suffice for the quantified existence or partition claim.

Proof 1: Strictly increasing sequence contradiction — outline

A weakened form of Zorn's lemma can be proven from ZF + DC (Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory with the axiom of choice replaced by the axiom of dependent choice). For a nonempty partially ordered set, having no maximal element is equivalent to stating that its strict ordering relation is entire: for every x there is a y with x<y. This allows us to apply the axiom of dependent choice to construct a strictly increasing countably infinite chain. As a result, any nonempty partially ordered set with exclusively finite chains must have a maximal element.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

With no maximal element, every point has a strictly larger successor; the explicitly stated dependent-choice principle gives an infinite ascending chain. Partial orders and this single sequence-selection axiom are a small extension of undergraduate set theory.
