Statement

For a subset A of a complete metric space (X,d), define \alpha(A)=\inf\{r\geq0:A\text{ is covered by finitely many subsets of diameter at most }r\}, with infimum of the empty admissible family equal to infinity. Let β denote either the ball measure or the separation measure of noncompactness, using their usual Lipschitz-equivalent conventions: there are fixed c,C>0 with c\alpha(A)\leq\beta(A)\leq C\alpha(A) for all subsets A. If A_n are nonempty closed subsets, A_{n+1}⊆A_n, and β(A_n)→0, then \bigcap_{n\geq1}A_n is nonempty and compact.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Completeness, compactness and finite diameter or ball covers belong to undergraduate metric topology. The separation alternative measures how far apart an infinite sequence can remain; these related noncompactness quantities add limited definitions, and their comparison is assumed.

Proof 1: Transfer convergence through Lipschitz equivalence

The source states that the ball, separation and Kuratowski measures are mutually Lipschitz equivalent. In particular, 0\leq\alpha(A_n)\leq\beta(A_n)/c, so β(A_n)→0 implies α(A_n)→0. Apply Kuratowski’s intersection theorem in its α formulation to the given decreasing sequence of nonempty closed sets. Its intersection is nonempty and compact.
