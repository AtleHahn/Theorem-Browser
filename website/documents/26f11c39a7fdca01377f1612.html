Statement

Write [n]^H for the words of length H over \{1,\ldots,n\}. A combinatorial line is \{w(1),\ldots,w(n)\}, where the length-H word w(x) has at least one variable position and all variable positions receive the same letter. The partition of [3]^2 into A=\{11,22,23,31\} and B=\{12,13,21,32,33\} has no monochromatic combinatorial line.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The words, variable positions and finite colorings are defined locally; the proof uses elementary finite counting.

Proof 1: A two-coloring of the ternary square without a combinatorial line — outline

Use the displayed partition. Each of the three rows, three columns and the variable word xx has points in both parts. These are exactly the combinatorial lines of [3]^2.

Level

Level justification

Check the three rows, three columns and main diagonal against the explicit two-color partition. Elementary finite enumeration suffices.
