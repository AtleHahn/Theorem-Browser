Statement

Let n\ge1 be an integer and let u,v\in L^1(\mathbb R^n) be complex-valued, with Lebesgue measure. Write \|w\|_1=\int_{\mathbb R^n}|w(x)|\,dx and r(x)=\int_{\mathbb R^n}u(t)v(x-t)\,dt wherever this integral exists. For w\in L^1(\mathbb R^n) define \widehat w(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb R^n}w(x)e^{-2\pi i\xi\cdot x}\,dx, where \xi\cdot x=\sum_{j=1}^n\xi_jx_j. Then r\in L^1(\mathbb R^n) and \widehat r(\xi)=\widehat u(\xi)\widehat v(\xi) for every \xi\in\mathbb R^n.

Proof 1: Interchanging the absolutely integrable double integral

Put U=\widehat u, V=\widehat v. The convolution of u and v is defined almost everywhere by

r(x)=\int_{\mathbb R^n}u(\tau)v(x-\tau)\,d\tau.

Also, by Tonelli's theorem and translation invariance,

\begin{aligned} \iint|u(\tau)v(x-\tau)|\,dx\,d\tau &=\int\left(|u(\tau)|\int|v(x-\tau)|\,dx\right)d\tau\\ &=\int|u(\tau)|\,\|v\|_1\,d\tau =\|u\|_1\|v\|_1. \end{aligned}

Hence by Fubini's theorem we have that r is defined almost everywhere and r\in L^1(\mathbb R^n), so its Fourier transform R is defined by the integral formula:

\begin{aligned} R(\xi)=\widehat r(\xi) &=\int_{\mathbb R^n}r(x)e^{-2\pi i\xi\cdot x}\,dx\\ &=\int_{\mathbb R^n}\left(\int_{\mathbb R^n}u(\tau)v(x-\tau)\,d\tau\right)e^{-2\pi i\xi\cdot x}\,dx. \end{aligned}

Note that |u(\tau)v(x-\tau)e^{-2\pi i\xi\cdot x}|=|u(\tau)v(x-\tau)|. Hence by the argument above we may apply Fubini's theorem again (i.e. interchange the order of integration):

\begin{aligned} R(\xi) &=\int_{\mathbb R^n}u(\tau) \underbrace{\left(\int_{\mathbb R^n}v(x-\tau)e^{-2\pi i\xi\cdot x}\,dx\right)}_{V(\xi)e^{-2\pi i\xi\cdot\tau}}\,d\tau\\ &=\underbrace{\left(\int_{\mathbb R^n}u(\tau)e^{-2\pi i\xi\cdot\tau}\,d\tau\right)}_{U(\xi)}V(\xi). \end{aligned}
