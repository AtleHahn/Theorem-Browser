Statement

A finite term is a finite rooted ordered tree: variables and constants are leaves, and a function symbol with its specified arity labels each internal node. Equality means identical trees. A substitution simultaneously replaces each occurrence of a variable by its assigned finite term, leaving all other symbols fixed. If a variable x occurs as a strict subterm of a term t, there is no substitution \sigma by finite terms with x\sigma=t\sigma.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Finite terms, proper subterms and simultaneous substitution are explicitly finite tree constructions.

Proof 1: A finite tree is not a proper subtree of itself

An attempt to unify a variable x with a term containing x as a strict subterm, x=f(\ldots,x,\ldots), would lead to an infinite term as solution for x, since the substituted term would occur as a proper subterm of itself. In the set of finite first-order terms, the equation x=f(\ldots,x,\ldots) has no solution.

Level
High school only
Level justification

A substituted strict occurrence would make a finite tree equal to a proper subtree of itself, contradicting its node count. Elementary finite trees and counting suffice.
