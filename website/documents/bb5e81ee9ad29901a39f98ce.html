Statement

Let R\subset\mathbb R^2 be an open simply connected region and let f,g,\varphi be continuously differentiable real functions on R. Suppose

h=\frac{\partial(\varphi f)}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial(\varphi g)}{\partial y}

is strictly positive almost everywhere on R, or strictly negative almost everywhere there, with respect to plane area measure. Then the autonomous system \dot x=f(x,y), \dot y=g(x,y) has no nonconstant periodic solution lying entirely in R.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Calculus and basic plane topology supply partial derivatives, differentiable curves and open regions. The extra conditions say that loops contract in R and that the continuous divergence-like function has the indicated strict sign except on a zero-area set. A periodic solution is a closed parametrized curve satisfying the two displayed differential equations. These concrete extensions fit about 30 minutes; general flow theory and measure-theoretic integration are not required to understand the claim.

Proof 1: Green’s theorem and vanishing trajectory flux

Without loss of generality take h>0 almost everywhere. Suppose C is a nonconstant closed trajectory, and let D be its interior. Use the standard facts that a periodic orbit of a continuously differentiable planar vector field is a simple closed curve, and that its interior lies in the simply connected region R. By Green’s theorem,

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}&\iint _{D}\left({\frac {\partial (\varphi f)}{\partial x}}+{\frac {\partial (\varphi g)}{\partial y}}\right)\,dx\,dy=\iint _{D}\left({\frac {\partial (\varphi {\dot {x}})}{\partial x}}+{\frac {\partial (\varphi {\dot {y}})}{\partial y}}\right)\,dx\,dy\\[6pt]={}&\oint _{C}\varphi \left(-{\dot {y}}\,dx+{\dot {x}}\,dy\right)=\oint _{C}\varphi \left(-{\dot {y}}{\dot {x}}+{\dot {x}}{\dot {y}}\right)\,dt=0\end{aligned}}}

with {\displaystyle {\dot {x}}=dx/dt} and {\displaystyle {\dot {y}}=dy/dt} being Newton's notation and the first equality following from the plane autonomous system. Because of the constant sign, the left-hand integral in the previous line must evaluate to a positive number. But on {\displaystyle C}, {\displaystyle dx={\dot {x}}\,dt} and {\displaystyle dy={\dot {y}}\,dt}, so the bottom integrand is in fact 0 everywhere and for this reason the right-hand integral evaluates to 0. This is a contradiction, so there can be no such closed trajectory {\displaystyle C}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof substitutes the two differential equations into a planar Green formula, making the boundary integrand vanish. Undergraduate multivariable calculus and basic topology suffice to understand the supplied simple-orbit and enclosed-region facts. A periodic solution is a differentiable closed trajectory of the given vector field; no qualitative dynamical-systems theory must be learned. The almost-everywhere sign condition adds ignoring zero-area sets, and continuity makes the positivity argument elementary.

The integral of a continuous function strictly positive almost everywhere over a nonempty region is positive
Kind:

Theorem

Treatment:

Implicitly assumed

Statement used:

A continuous function strictly positive almost everywhere on a nonempty open region has positive integral over that region whenever the integral exists.

Application in proof:

Contradict the zero boundary flux.

Application level:

Undergraduate level

Level justification:

Continuity rules out any negative point and a positive point supplies a small disk with positive lower bound. Only elementary integration and zero-area exceptional sets are involved.

Review notes:

The enclosed region has positive area. Since its closure lies in R, the continuous integrand is bounded there and the integral is finite.
