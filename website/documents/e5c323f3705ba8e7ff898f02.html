Statement

Let X be Banach and Y normed, and suppose bounded linear maps T_n:X\to Y converge pointwise to T:X\to Y. Then for every compact K\subseteq X, \sup_{x\in K}\|T_nx-Tx\|\longrightarrow0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A Banach space is a complete normed vector space. Linear algebra and metric analysis supply bounded linear maps, pointwise/uniform convergence and compact sets; the operator norm is a unit-ball supremum. These related definitions suffice without dual or spectral theory.

Proof 1: A finite cover and a three-term estimate

Use the corollary of the uniform boundedness principle that the pointwise limit T is bounded linear. By the uniform boundedness principle, let

M=\max\{1,\sup_n\|T_n\|,\|T\|\}

be a positive uniform upper bound on the operator norms.

Fix any compact K\subset X. Then for any \epsilon>0, finitely cover (use compactness) K by a finite set of open balls \{B(x_i,r)\}_{i=1,\ldots,N} of radius r=\epsilon/M.

Since T_n\to T pointwise on each of x_1,\ldots,x_N, for all large n,

\|T_n(x_i)-T(x_i)\|\leq\epsilon \qquad\text{for all }i=1,\ldots,N.

Then by the triangle inequality, for x\in B(x_i,r),

\|T_n(x)-T(x)\| \leq\|T_n(x-x_i)\|+\|T_n(x_i)-T(x_i)\|+\|T(x_i-x)\| \leq3\epsilon.

Thus for all large n this bound holds for every x\in K, proving uniform convergence.

Level justification

Obtain a common operator bound, cover the compact set by finitely many small balls and control three norm terms at each center. Undergraduate metric compactness and normed linear algebra suffice with uniform boundedness as one explicit extension.
