For classical first-order logic, failure of proof always has a semantic witness: a model of the premises where the conclusion fails. This makes precise the completeness connection between formal derivability and truth in all models, without identifying any one model as the intended reality.

Let T be a classical first-order theory and s a sentence with T\nvdash s. Then some model of T makes s false.

Proof 1: Take the contrapositive of completeness

If all models of T made s true, completeness would give T\vdash s, contrary to the hypothesis. Therefore there is a model of T in which s is false.

Level
