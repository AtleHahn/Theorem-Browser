Statement

Let f:S^1\to S^1 be a smooth orientation-preserving diffeomorphism, with f(e^{i\theta})=e^{ig(\theta)} and g(\theta+2\pi)=g(\theta)+2\pi. On L^2(S^1) let P be the orthogonal projection onto the closed span of e^{in\theta} for n\geq0, let T=2P-I, and let (V(f)h)(\zeta)=h(f^{-1}(\zeta)). Set K_f=V(f)PV(f)^{-1}-P. Write D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\} and D^*=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|>1\}. Suppose holomorphic functions f_-(z)=\sum_{n\geq0}a_nz^n on D and f_+(z)=z+\sum_{n\geq1}b_nz^{-n} on D^* extend smoothly to S^1 and satisfy f_-(\zeta)=f_+(f(\zeta)). For F=f_+|_{S^1} one has (I-K_f)F=e_1, where e_1(e^{i\theta})=e^{i\theta}.

Proof 1: Fourier projections and substitution

Let F be the restriction of f_+ to the unit circle. Then

TF=-F+2e^{i\theta}

and

T(F\circ f)=F\circ f.

Hence

T(F\circ f)\circ f^{-1}-T(F)=2F-2e^{i\theta}.

With V(f) and K_f=V(f)PV(f)^{-1}-P as defined in the statement, the equation above reduces to

(I-K_f)F=e^{i\theta}.
