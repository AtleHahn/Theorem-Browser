Statement

For -2\le\alpha\le2, the improper integral I(\alpha)=\int_0^{\infty}\log(1+\alpha e^{-x}+e^{-2x})\,dx equals \pi^2/6-\tfrac12(\arccos(\alpha/2))^2.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves improper integrals; logarithms; inverse trigonometric functions. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Parameter derivative and endpoint relation

{\displaystyle I(\alpha )=\int _{0}^{\infty }\ln \left(1+\alpha e^{-x}+e^{-2x}\right)dx.}

While the primitive function of the integrand cannot be expressed in terms of elementary functions, by differentiating with respect to {\displaystyle \alpha } we arrive at

{\displaystyle {\frac {dI}{d\alpha }}=\int _{0}^{\infty }{\frac {e^{-x}}{1+\alpha e^{-x}+e^{-2x}}}dx,}

which can be integrated by substituting {\displaystyle u=e^{-x}} and completing the square. In the range {\displaystyle -2<\alpha <2} the definite integral reduces to

{\displaystyle {\frac {dI}{d\alpha }}={\frac {2}{\sqrt {4-\alpha ^{2}}}}\left[\arctan \left({\frac {\alpha +2}{\sqrt {4-\alpha ^{2}}}}\right)-\arctan \left({\frac {\alpha }{\sqrt {4-\alpha ^{2}}}}\right)\right].}

The expression can be simplified using the arctangent addition formula and integrated with respect to {\displaystyle \alpha } by means of trigonometric substitution, resulting in

{\displaystyle I(\alpha )=-{\frac {1}{2}}\arccos \left({\frac {\alpha }{2}}\right)^{2}+c.}

The integration constant {\displaystyle c} can be determined by noticing that two distinct values of {\displaystyle I(\alpha )} are related by

{\displaystyle I(2)=4I(0),}

because when calculating {\displaystyle I(2)} we can factor {\displaystyle 1+2e^{-x}+e^{-2x}=(1+e^{-x})^{2}} and express it in terms of {\displaystyle I(0)} using the logarithm of a power identity and the substitution {\displaystyle u=x/2}. This makes it possible to determine {\displaystyle c={\frac {\pi ^{2}}{6}}}.
