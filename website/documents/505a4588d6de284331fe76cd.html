An infinite set can have the same cardinality as a proper subset, contrary to the finite intuition that the whole must be larger than a part. This elementary bijection makes a defining feature of infinite size explicit without treating equality of cardinality as equality of sets.

Statement

With \mathbb N=\{0,1,2,\ldots\} and 2\mathbb N=\{2n:n\in\mathbb N\}, the map f:\mathbb N\to2\mathbb N given by f(n)=2n is a bijection.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The sets and doubling map are explicit integer formulas; bijection means each target has exactly one preimage.
