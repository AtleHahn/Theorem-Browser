Statement

On [0,1] with Lebesgue measure define f_n(x)=n1_{(0,1/n]}(x) for positive integers n. Then f_n\to0 pointwise and \int_0^1f_n\,dx=1 for all n, while no nonnegative Lebesgue-integrable g can dominate every f_n almost everywhere.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement specifies Lebesgue integrability and almost-everywhere domination, as well as a concrete convergent sequence.

Proof 1: Harmonic lower bound for the supremum of the spikes

Any g which dominates the sequence must also dominate the pointwise supremum h = supn fn. Observe that

{\displaystyle \int _{0}^{1}h(x)\,dx\geq \int _{\frac {1}{m}}^{1}{h(x)\,dx}=\sum _{n=1}^{m-1}\int _{\left({\frac {1}{n+1}},{\frac {1}{n}}\right]}{h(x)\,dx}\geq \sum _{n=1}^{m-1}\int _{\left({\frac {1}{n+1}},{\frac {1}{n}}\right]}{n\,dx}=\sum _{n=1}^{m-1}{\frac {1}{n+1}}\to \infty \qquad {\text{as }}m\to \infty }

by the divergence of the harmonic series. Hence, the monotonicity of the Lebesgue integral tells us that there exists no integrable function which dominates the sequence on [0,1]. A direct calculation shows that integration and pointwise limit do not commute for this sequence:

{\displaystyle \int _{0}^{1}\lim _{n\to \infty }f_{n}(x)\,dx=0\neq 1=\lim _{n\to \infty }\int _{0}^{1}f_{n}(x)\,dx,}

because the pointwise limit of the sequence is the zero function.

For almost-everywhere domination, take the union of the exceptional null sets for all n; outside this null set g\geq h, and the same integral bound applies.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Bound an arbitrary almost-everywhere dominating Lebesgue-integrable function by the supremum of the spikes, then use null-set unions and integral monotonicity. The numerical harmonic estimate is elementary, but the general Lebesgue domination framework is not confidently a small extension of ordinary Riemann calculus; retain beyond undergraduate.
