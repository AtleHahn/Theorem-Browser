Statement

Let C be a smooth projective curve of genus g\ge2 over a number field K, embedded in its Jacobian J over \overline K. Let L be a symmetric ample line bundle on J and \widehat h its Néron–Tate height. Then only finitely many points of C(\overline K) are torsion points of J.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves number fields; smooth projective curves; Jacobian varieties; ample line bundles; Néron–Tate heights; torsion points. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Height-zero torsion points

Let {\displaystyle C} be a curve of genus {\displaystyle g\geq 2} that is defined over a number field {\displaystyle K}. Let {\displaystyle L} be a symmetric ample line bundle for the Jacobian {\displaystyle J} and let {\displaystyle {\widehat {h}}} denote the associated Néron-Tate height on {\displaystyle J}. Use the Bogomolov theorem: there exists {\displaystyle \epsilon >0} such that the set

{\displaystyle \{x\in C({\overline {K}}):{\widehat {h}}(x)<\epsilon \}}

is finite. Since {\displaystyle {\widehat {h}}(x)=0} if and only if {\displaystyle x} is a torsion point, this recovers the Manin-Mumford conjecture as a special case.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The finite-set inclusion is elementary, but the full application uses projective curves over number fields, Jacobians, symmetric ample line bundles and canonical heights. This is a substantial arithmetic-geometric background.
