Statement

Let A be a unital associative algebra over a field F of characteristic different from 2, and let \sigma:A\to A be an F-linear involution satisfying \sigma(xy)=\sigma(y)\sigma(x). With x\circ y=(xy+yx)/2, the fixed subspace H(A,\sigma)=\{x\in A:\sigma(x)=x\} is a Jordan subalgebra of A^+. Here A^+ denotes A with this Jordan product; a Jordan algebra has a commutative product satisfying (x\circ x)\circ(x\circ y)=x\circ((x\circ x)\circ y).

Level justification

Undergraduate algebra and analysis supply the objects. Hermite polynomials have a single defining recurrence, Jordan products and graph-end conditions are explicitly specified, and well-quasi-order is defined by a sequence comparison. These concrete additions do not require their advanced proof theories.

Proof 1: Closure under the symmetrized product

Use the fact that the symmetrized product on an associative algebra of characteristic different from 2 defines the Jordan algebra A^+.

If (A, σ) is an associative algebra with an involution σ, then if σ(x) = x and σ(y) = y it follows that {\textstyle \sigma (xy+yx)=xy+yx.} Thus the set of all elements fixed by the involution (sometimes called the hermitian elements) form a subalgebra of A+, which is sometimes denoted H(A,σ).
