Statement

On \mathbb R^d, d\ge1, for 1\le p\le2 and 1/p+1/q=1, the Euclidean Fourier transform normalized to have L^1\to L^\infty and L^2\to L^2 norms one satisfies, for every f\in L^p(\mathbb R^d), \|\mathcal Ff\|_q\le\|f\|_p. On the circle with measure dx/(2\pi), for every f\in L^p(\mathbb T) the coefficients \widehat f(n)=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(x)e^{-inx}\,dx satisfy \|\widehat f\|_{\ell^q}\le\|f\|_{L^p}. In the first assertion the transform on Lp is its compatible bounded extension.

Level justification

The inequalities concern Fourier maps between Lebesgue and sequence spaces, not finite Fourier sums.

Proof 1: Interpolate the Fourier endpoint bounds

Use Riesz–Thorin interpolation: a complex-linear operator with endpoint bounds L^{p_j}\to L^{q_j} of norms M_j has intermediate bound M_0^{1-\theta}M_1^\theta at reciprocal exponents interpolated with weights 1-\theta,\theta.

The integral estimate and Plancherel theorem show that the Fourier transform {\displaystyle {\mathcal {F}}} maps L1(Rd) boundedly into L∞(Rd) and L2(Rd) into itself. A similar argument shows that the Fourier series operator, which transforms periodic functions  f  : T → C into functions {\displaystyle {\hat {f}}:\mathbf {Z} \to \mathbf {C} } whose values are the Fourier coefficients

{\displaystyle {\hat {f}}(n)={\frac {1}{2\pi }}\int _{-\pi }^{\pi }f(x)e^{-inx}\,dx,}

maps L1(T) boundedly into ℓ∞(Z) and L2(T) into ℓ2(Z). With the normalizations in the statement, these endpoint bounds have norm one. The Riesz–Thorin interpolation theorem now implies the following:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\left\|{\mathcal {F}}f\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbf {R} ^{d})}&\leq \|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbf {R} ^{d})}\\\left\|{\hat {f}}\right\|_{\ell ^{q}(\mathbf {Z} )}&\leq \|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbf {T} )}\end{aligned}}}

where 1 ≤ p ≤ 2 and ⁠1/p⁠ + ⁠1/q⁠ = 1. This is the Hausdorff–Young inequality.
