The Stirling correction series diverges at every nonzero argument despite furnishing asymptotic approximations. This distinguishes useful approximation from convergent infinite summation and clarifies why adding indefinitely many terms need not improve a model's numerical prediction.

Statement

The formal correction series \sum_{k\ge0}A_k z^k in n!\sim\sqrt{2\pi n}(n/e)^n\sum_{k\ge0}A_k n^{-k} has radius of convergence zero.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Factorials; Asymptotic expansions; Power series; Radius of convergence. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Superexponential growth of the odd coefficients — outline

Use the coefficient asymptotic A_{2j+1}\sim(-1)^j2(2j)!/(2\pi)^{2(j+1)}. These coefficients grow superexponentially. The ratio test on this odd-index subsequence shows zero convergence radius.

Level justification

Use the explicitly supplied odd-coefficient asymptotic to show superexponential growth and apply the power-series convergence criterion. Undergraduate real analysis and factorial growth suffice.
