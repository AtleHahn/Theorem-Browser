Statement

There exists a compact convex set K\subset\mathbb R^2 of minimum area among all compact convex sets that can accommodate every planar curve of length 1 by rotation and translation. Precisely, for every continuous rectifiable curve \gamma:[0,1]\to\mathbb R^2 with length 1, there are R\in SO(2) and t\in\mathbb R^2 such that R\gamma([0,1])+t\subset K. Curve length is the supremum, over partitions 0=t_0<\cdots<t_m=1, of \sum_{j=1}^m\|\gamma(t_j)-\gamma(t_{j-1})\|. Rectifiable means this supremum is finite; curves need not be simple or closed. Area is two-dimensional Lebesgue measure, and K has area no greater than any other compact convex set with the stated property.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The explicit partition-supremum definition of length is a small extension of undergraduate real analysis; the remaining quantifiers use elementary plane geometry.
