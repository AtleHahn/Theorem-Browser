Statement

For four mutually tangent generalized circles in a Descartes configuration (six distinct tangencies, with parallel lines interpreted as tangent at infinity), let k_i be their signed curvatures, with a line assigned zero. Then k_4=k_1+k_2+k_3\pm2\sqrt{k_1k_2+k_2k_3+k_3k_1}. An enclosing circle has negative reciprocal radius; the others have positive reciprocal radius.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Signed curvature is explicitly reciprocal radius with the stated sign, and lines have value zero. Parallel lines being tangent at infinity is the supplied convention for admitting that configuration, not a requirement to construct inversive geometry. The asserted formula uses elementary circle tangency and square roots.

Proof 1: Solving the curvature quadratic — outline

For four mutually tangent generalized circles with distinct tangencies, signed curvatures satisfy (k_1+k_2+k_3+k_4)^2=2(k_1^2+k_2^2+k_3^2+k_4^2); a line has curvature zero, an enclosing circle has curvature -1/r, and the other circles have curvature 1/r.

If one of the four curvatures is considered to be a variable, and the rest to be constants, this is a quadratic equation. To find the radius of a fourth circle tangent to three given kissing circles, the quadratic equation can be solved as

{\displaystyle k_{4}=k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}\pm 2{\sqrt {k_{1}k_{2}+k_{2}k_{3}+k_{3}k_{1}}}.}

High school only
Level justification

Treat the supplied Descartes relation as a quadratic in k4 and apply the quadratic formula. Signed reciprocal radii and the assigned zero for a line are explicitly defined.
