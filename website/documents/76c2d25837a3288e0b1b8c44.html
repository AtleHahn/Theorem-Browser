Statement

A partition of a nonnegative integer is a multiset of positive integers whose sum is that integer; order of parts is ignored. If p(n) counts the partitions of n and p(0)=1, then as formal power series \sum_{n\geq0}p(n)q^n=\prod_{j\geq1}\sum_{i\geq0}q^{ji}=\prod_{j\geq1}(1-q^j)^{-1}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Formal series use polynomial-style coefficient arithmetic without a convergence requirement. Coefficient extraction and partitions/compositions are explicitly described finite counts for each degree. This is a small extension of familiar power series and polynomial algebra.

Proof 1: Generating function for integer partitions — outline

The source writes a partition by multiplicities m_j of each part j and displays the product of sums \prod_j\sum_{i\geq0}q^{ji}. Selecting q^{jm_j} in each factor encodes those multiplicities, with total exponent \sum_j jm_j. Thus the coefficient of q^n counts partitions of n. Each factor is the geometric series (1-q^j)^{-1}. For each coefficient only finitely many positive part sizes contribute.
