Statement

Let T be a classical first-order theory of arithmetic and G a sentence independent of T but true in the standard natural numbers. Then T has a model in which G is false, and every such model is nonstandard, meaning not isomorphic to the standard arithmetic structure.

Proof 1: A false model of a true independent arithmetic sentence is nonstandard — source argument

By first-order completeness, independence gives a model of T+\neg G. Since G is true in the standard natural numbers, a model falsifying it cannot be the standard structure (or an isomorphic copy).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Use completeness to obtain a model falsifying the independent sentence and distinguish it from standard arithmetic by truth. Independence, formal theories and arbitrary arithmetic models together are not confidently a small addition to ordinary undergraduate logic.
