Statement

The coefficients of ((1+x)^2(1-x))^{-1} give a power series asymptotic to 1/(4(1-x)) as x\uparrow1, whose partial sums are not asymptotic to any nonzero linear function of the index.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Power series; Asymptotic equivalence; Partial sums. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Alternating coefficient partial sums

{\displaystyle {\frac {1}{(1+x)^{2}(1-x)}}=1-x+2x^{2}-2x^{3}+3x^{4}-3x^{5}+\cdots }

is asymptotic to {\displaystyle 1/(4(1-x))} as {\displaystyle x\to 1}, but the partial sums of its coefficients are 1, 0, 2, 0, 3, 0, 4, ... and are not asymptotic to any linear function.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Read the alternating coefficients and compare the even and odd partial sums with a linear sequence, while taking the rational-function ratio limit. Undergraduate power series and asymptotics suffice.
