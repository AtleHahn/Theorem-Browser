Statement

If F\subseteq E are finite fields and \alpha generates the multiplicative group E^\times, then E=F(\alpha). Such a generator exists, so every extension of finite fields is simple.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Fields and cyclic groups are introductory abstract algebra. F(alpha) means the smallest subfield containing F and alpha, and the assertion uses only containment and powers of a multiplicative generator. Unlike norm-surjectivity questions, no extension degree, determinant norm or finite-field arithmetic construction is needed.

Proof 1: Use a generator of the multiplicative group

For the case where F is finite, we simply take \alpha to be a primitive root of the finite extension field E, that is, a generator of the cyclic multiplicative group E^\times. Every nonzero element of E is then a power of \alpha, so E=F(\alpha).

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Choose the supplied multiplicative generator; every nonzero field element is one of its powers and zero belongs to the generated subfield. Finite fields and cyclic groups are introductory algebra, with generated subfield a small added definition.
