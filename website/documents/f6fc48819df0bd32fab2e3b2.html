Statement

Let P>0, let k\ge1 be an integer, and let s\in C^k(\mathbb R) be P-periodic, with \widehat s[n]=P^{-1}\int_0^P s(x)e^{-2\pi inx/P}\,dx. Then |n|^k\widehat s[n]\to0 as |n|\to\infty.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Complex Fourier coefficients defined by integrals; Ck periodic functions; Limits of sequences. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Apply Riemann–Lebesgue to the derivative coefficients

If {\displaystyle s\in C^{k}(\mathbb {R} )}, then the Fourier coefficients of the {\displaystyle k^{\text{th}}} derivative of {\displaystyle s} can be expressed in terms of the Fourier coefficients {\displaystyle {\widehat {s}}[n]} of {\displaystyle s}, via the formula

{\displaystyle {\widehat {s^{(k)}}}[n]=\left(i{\frac {2\pi n}{P}}\right)^{k}{\widehat {s}}[n].}

In particular, by the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma, for any fixed {\displaystyle k\geq 1} we have {\displaystyle {\widehat {s^{(k)}}}[n]\to 0} as {\displaystyle |n|\to \infty }, it follows that {\displaystyle |n|^{k}{\widehat {s}}[n]} tends to zero, i.e., the Fourier coefficients converge to zero faster than the reciprocal of the {\displaystyle k^{\text{th}}} power of {\displaystyle |n|}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the derivative-coefficient relation and Riemann-Lebesgue only to a continuous periodic derivative. Undergraduate integration by parts, Fourier coefficients and real-series limits suffice; no general L1 Fourier framework is needed for the used case.
