Statement

Let X_1,X_2,\ldots be independent identically distributed real random variables with \mathbb E X_1=0, and let S_n=\sum_{j=1}^nX_j. Then \mathbb E(X_1^2)<\infty\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\text{for every }\varepsilon>0,\ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb P(|S_n|>\varepsilon n)<\infty.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Undergraduate probability and infinite series suffice; the precise summability assertion needs no Borel–Cantelli prerequisite.

Proof 1: Truncation and independent large jumps (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (1949 article pp. 286–291, sufficiency pp. 286–288 and variance necessity pp. 289–290; appended 1950 remark p. 138).

Apply the sufficiency argument to X_j/\varepsilon for each \varepsilon>0. For necessity put q_n=\mathbb P(|X_1|>2n) and R_k=\{|X_k|>2n\}. The events R_k\cap\{|S_n-X_k|<n\} imply |S_n|>n. Independence and the union lower bound give \mathbb P(|S_n|>n)\ge n q_n(p-nq_n) for a fixed p>0 and large n. Here nq_n\to0 follows from integrability. Summing forces \sum n q_n<\infty, equivalently a finite second moment.
