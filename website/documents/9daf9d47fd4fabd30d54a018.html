Statement

Let X be a smooth projective surface over a field, and let K_X be its canonical divisor. Assume the following Reider criterion holds on X: for every nef divisor D with D^2>4, the system |K_X+D| has no base points unless a nonzero effective divisor E satisfies (D\cdot E,E^2)=(0,-1) or (1,0). Then for every ample line bundle L and integer m\ge3, the system |K_X+mL| is base-point-free.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Smooth projective surfaces; Ample line bundles; Canonical divisors and linear systems; Nef and effective divisors; Intersection numbers; Conditional criterion. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Exclude the exceptional intersection numbers

Set D=mL. Ampleness gives L^2\ge1 and L\cdot E\ge1 for every nonzero effective divisor E. Hence D^2\ge m^2>4 and D\cdot E\ge m>2. The assumed base-point criterion requires an exceptional divisor with D\cdot E=0 or 1; both are impossible. Thus the system is base-point-free.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Use ample intersection positivity to exclude the exceptional divisors in the stated Reider criterion. Projective surfaces, divisor intersections and adjoint linear systems are advanced algebraic-geometric objects even though the inequalities are elementary.
