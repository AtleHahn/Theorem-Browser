Statement

Let U,V\subseteq\mathbb C be nonempty simply connected open domains and f:U\to V a biholomorphism. If F is a holomorphic primitive of f on U, then all primitives of f^{-1} on V are G(z)=zf^{-1}(z)-F(f^{-1}(z))+C, C\in\mathbb C.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex differentiability extends derivative limits; biholomorphic means a bijection with holomorphic inverse, and primitive means derivative equal to the given function. Simply connected means loops contract in the domain. These are related small extensions of undergraduate calculus and basic topology for this explicit composition formula, without a contour-integration theory.

Proof 1: Antiderivative of a biholomorphic inverse — outline

The source uses existence of primitives on simply connected domains. Since the inverse is holomorphic, complex product and chain rules give G'(z)=f^{-1}(z)+z(f^{-1})'(z)-f(f^{-1}(z))(f^{-1})'(z)=f^{-1}(z). On the connected domain all primitives differ by a constant.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Differentiate the displayed composition and cancel f(f inverse(z))=z; connectedness gives the constant ambiguity. Undergraduate calculus and basic topology suffice with complex differentiation, holomorphic inverse and primitive defined in direct analogy with real calculus.
