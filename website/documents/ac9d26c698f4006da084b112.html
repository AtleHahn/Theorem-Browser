Statement

Let n\ge1 and let 2n bihomogeneous polynomials over \mathbb C, each of bidegree (1,1) in two blocks of n+1 variables, have a finite common zero set in \mathbb P^n\times\mathbb P^n. Count those zeros with intersection multiplicities. Their number is \binom{2n}{n}.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Complex projective spaces; Bihomogeneous polynomials; Intersection multiplicities. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Extract the central coefficient (outline)

Use the multi-homogeneous Bézout theorem: for n_1+\cdots+n_k equations of multidegrees d_{j1},\ldots,d_{jk} with finite common zero set in \prod_i\mathbb P^{n_i}, its multiplicity count is the coefficient of t_1^{n_1}\cdots t_k^{n_k} in \prod_j(d_{j1}t_1+\cdots+d_{jk}t_k). In the two-block bidegree-(1,1) case, the multi-homogeneous Bézout theorem gives the coefficient

\binom{2n}{n}=\frac{(2n)!}{(n!)^2}

of t_1^nt_2^n in (t_1+t_2)^{2n}.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Extracting a binomial coefficient is elementary, but the imported theorem counts zeros in a product of projective spaces with intersection multiplicities. Understanding bihomogeneous equations, multiprojective geometry and these multiplicities requires substantial algebraic geometry beyond the short undergraduate extension. The coefficient formula does not define away the geometric meaning of the counted objects.
