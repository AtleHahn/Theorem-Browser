Statement

Let k be a field, R=k[x_1,\ldots,x_n], and g_1,\ldots,g_t a homogeneous regular sequence of positive-degree polynomials, with 1\le t\le n. Then \operatorname{pd}_R(R/(g_1,\ldots,g_t))=t. In particular, \operatorname{pd}_R k=n for the module on which every x_i acts as zero.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Proof 1: The last Koszul map cannot split

Let R=k[x_1,\ldots,x_n] and I=(g_1,\ldots,g_t). Use the Koszul resolution of R/I, which is exact for a homogeneous regular sequence. Its term in degree j is the free module on G_{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge G_{i_j}, and its differential is

d(G_{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge G_{i_j})=\sum_{a=1}^j(-1)^{a+1}g_{i_a}G_{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat G_{i_a}\wedge\cdots\wedge G_{i_j}.

In particular, the sequence {\displaystyle x_{1},\ldots ,x_{n}} is regular, and the Koszul complex is thus a projective resolution of {\displaystyle k=R/\langle x_{1},\ldots ,x_{n}\rangle .} In this case, the nth syzygy module is free of dimension one (generated by the product of all {\displaystyle G_{i}}); the (n − 1)th syzygy module is thus the quotient of a free module of dimension n by the submodule generated by {\displaystyle (x_{1},-x_{2},\ldots ,\pm x_{n}).} This quotient cannot be a projective module, as otherwise, there would exist polynomials {\displaystyle p_{i}} such that {\displaystyle p_{1}x_{1}+\cdots +p_{n}x_{n}=1,} which is impossible (substituting 0 for the {\displaystyle x_{i}} in the latter equality provides 1 = 0). This proves that the projective dimension of {\displaystyle k=R/\langle x_{1},\ldots ,x_{n}\rangle } is exactly n.

The same proof applies for proving that the projective dimension of {\displaystyle k[x_{1},\ldots ,x_{n}]/\langle g_{1},\ldots ,g_{t}\rangle } is exactly t if the {\displaystyle g_{i}} form a regular sequence of homogeneous polynomials.

For the general sequence, the same splitting would give \sum_i p_i g_i=1, impossible on evaluating at the origin since the g_i are homogeneous of positive degree.
