Proof 1: Differentiate vectors fixed in a rotating frame — outline

The Jacobi identity for the cross product arises from the fact that the cross product of two vectors which stay fixed relative to a rotating frame of reference also rotates with that frame of reference. Specifically, let {\displaystyle \mathbf {a} } and {\displaystyle \mathbf {b} } be two vectors which rotate with the frame of reference, {\displaystyle D} be the relative differential operator of the frame, and {\displaystyle {\boldsymbol {\omega }}} be the angular velocity vector. For a vector \mathbf u, the relative derivative is D\mathbf u=d\mathbf u/dt-\boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf u. We have

{\displaystyle D(\mathbf {a} \times \mathbf {b} )={\frac {d}{dt}}(\mathbf {a} \times \mathbf {b} )-{\boldsymbol {\omega }}\times (\mathbf {a} \times \mathbf {b} )}

We also have

{\displaystyle D\mathbf {a} \times \mathbf {b} +\mathbf {a} \times D\mathbf {b} =\left({\frac {d\mathbf {a} }{dt}}\times \mathbf {b} -({\boldsymbol {\omega }}\times \mathbf {a} )\times \mathbf {b} \right)+\left(\mathbf {a} \times {\frac {d\mathbf {b} }{dt}}-\mathbf {a} \times ({\boldsymbol {\omega }}\times \mathbf {b} )\right)}

Both of these equations yield the zero vector because {\displaystyle D(\mathbf {a} \times \mathbf {b} )=D\mathbf {a} =D\mathbf {b} =\mathbf {0} }. Using the product rule, bilinearity and anticommutativity of the cross product, we arrive at

{\displaystyle {\boldsymbol {\omega }}\times (\mathbf {a} \times \mathbf {b} )+\mathbf {a} \times (\mathbf {b} \times {\boldsymbol {\omega }})+\mathbf {b} \times ({\boldsymbol {\omega }}\times \mathbf {a} )=\mathbf {0} }

The instantaneous vectors \mathbf a,\mathbf b and angular velocity \boldsymbol\omega are arbitrary.
