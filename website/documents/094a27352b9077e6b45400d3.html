Statement

Let a<b be real and let F be bounded and continuous on the closed strip a\le\Re z\le b, and holomorphic in its interior. If |F|\le1 on both boundary lines, then |F|\le1 throughout the strip.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Real analysis supplies derivatives, maxima, uniform convergence and completeness; complex arithmetic supplies the plane. Holomorphic means complex differentiable, and entire means so throughout the plane. With that short extension, the displayed bound or convergence assertion uses familiar analytic concepts without contour integrals or specialist function-space theory.

Proof 1: Boundary modulus controls a bounded holomorphic strip function — source argument

After an affine transformation in the coordinate z, it can be assumed that a=0 and b=1. The function

F_n(z)=F(z)e^{z^2/n}e^{-1/n}

tends to 0 as |z| tends to infinity in the strip and satisfies |F_n|\leq1 on the boundary of the strip. The maximum modulus principle can therefore be applied to F_n: take sufficiently tall finite rectangles, whose horizontal edges also have modulus at most one by the stated decay. So |F_n(z)|\leq1. Because F_n(z) tends to F(z) as n tends to infinity, it follows that |F(z)|\leq1.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The damping factor has modulus exp((x^2-y^2-1)/n), giving decay on tall rectangles and preserving the vertical boundary bound. The proof then applies the holomorphic maximum-modulus principle and takes a pointwise limit. The complex analytic framework is beyond a small extension of ordinary calculus.
