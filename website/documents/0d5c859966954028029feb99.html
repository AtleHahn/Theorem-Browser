Statement

Let A,B,C be noncollinear points in the Euclidean plane and let O lie on none of the three side lines. Suppose AO,BO,CO meet the opposite side lines at finite points D,E,F, respectively. Ratios of collinear segments are directed: AF/FB is positive when F lies between A,B and negative otherwise. Then \dfrac{AF}{FB}\dfrac{BD}{DC}\dfrac{CE}{EA}=1.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The incidence conditions and real directed length ratios are elementary Euclidean geometry.

Proof 1: Cancel triangle-area ratios — outline

Write |\triangle XYZ| for the unsigned area of triangle XYZ.

First, the sign of the left-hand side is positive since either all three of the ratios are positive, the case where O is inside the triangle, or one is positive and the other two are negative, the case O is outside the triangle.

To check the magnitude, note that the area of a triangle of a given height is proportional to its base. So

{\displaystyle {\frac {|\triangle BOD|}{|\triangle COD|}}=\left|{\frac {\overline {BD}}{\overline {DC}}}\right|={\frac {|\triangle BAD|}{|\triangle CAD|}}.}

Therefore,

{\displaystyle \left|{\frac {\overline {BD}}{\overline {DC}}}\right|={\frac {|\triangle BAD|-|\triangle BOD|}{|\triangle CAD|-|\triangle COD|}}={\frac {|\triangle ABO|}{|\triangle CAO|}}.}

(Replace the minus with a plus if A and O are on opposite sides of BC.) Similarly,

{\displaystyle \left|{\frac {\overline {CE}}{\overline {EA}}}\right|={\frac {|\triangle BCO|}{|\triangle ABO|}},}

and

{\displaystyle \left|{\frac {\overline {AF}}{\overline {FB}}}\right|={\frac {|\triangle CAO|}{|\triangle BCO|}}.}

Multiplying these three equations gives

{\displaystyle \left|{\frac {\overline {AF}}{\overline {FB}}}\cdot {\frac {\overline {BD}}{\overline {DC}}}\cdot {\frac {\overline {CE}}{\overline {EA}}}\right|=1,}

as required.

Level justification

Equal-height area ratios identify each side ratio with a ratio of two triangles through O. Their magnitudes telescope, and the geometric sign cases make the product positive. This is high-school Euclidean area geometry.

Proof 2: Divide two Menelaus equations

The theorem can also be proven easily using Menelaus's theorem. From the transversal BOE of triangle △ACF,

{\displaystyle {\frac {\overline {AB}}{\overline {BF}}}\cdot {\frac {\overline {FO}}{\overline {OC}}}\cdot {\frac {\overline {CE}}{\overline {EA}}}=-1}

and from the transversal AOD of triangle △BCF,

{\displaystyle {\frac {\overline {BA}}{\overline {AF}}}\cdot {\frac {\overline {FO}}{\overline {OC}}}\cdot {\frac {\overline {CD}}{\overline {DB}}}=-1.}

The theorem follows by dividing these two equations.

Level justification

Proof 3: Cancel barycentric-coordinate ratios

Given three points A, B, C that are not collinear, and a point O, that belongs to the same plane, the barycentric coordinates of O with respect of A, B, C are the unique three numbers {\displaystyle \lambda _{A},\lambda _{B},\lambda _{C}} such that

{\displaystyle \lambda _{A}+\lambda _{B}+\lambda _{C}=1,}

and

{\displaystyle {\overrightarrow {XO}}=\lambda _{A}{\overrightarrow {XA}}+\lambda _{B}{\overrightarrow {XB}}+\lambda _{C}{\overrightarrow {XC}},}

for every point X (the arrow denotes the displacement vector between its endpoints).

For Ceva's theorem, the point O is supposed to not belong to any line passing through two vertices of the triangle. This implies that {\displaystyle \lambda _{A}\lambda _{B}\lambda _{C}\neq 0.}

If one takes for X the intersection F of the lines AB and OC, the last equation may be rearranged into

{\displaystyle {\overrightarrow {FO}}-\lambda _{C}{\overrightarrow {FC}}=\lambda _{A}{\overrightarrow {FA}}+\lambda _{B}{\overrightarrow {FB}}.}

The left-hand side of this equation is a vector that has the same direction as the line CF, and the right-hand side has the same direction as the line AB. These lines have different directions since A, B, C are not collinear. It follows that the two members of the equation equal the zero vector, and

{\displaystyle \lambda _{A}{\overrightarrow {FA}}+\lambda _{B}{\overrightarrow {FB}}=0.}

It follows that

{\displaystyle {\frac {\overline {AF}}{\overline {FB}}}={\frac {\lambda _{B}}{\lambda _{A}}},}

where the left-hand-side fraction is the signed ratio of the lengths of the collinear line segments AF and FB.

The same reasoning shows

{\displaystyle {\frac {\overline {BD}}{\overline {DC}}}={\frac {\lambda _{C}}{\lambda _{B}}}\quad {\text{and}}\quad {\frac {\overline {CE}}{\overline {EA}}}={\frac {\lambda _{A}}{\lambda _{C}}}.}

Ceva's theorem results immediately by taking the product of the three last equations.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof uses real affine coordinates and independence of two plane directions to obtain the ratio of barycentric coordinates for each side. These are elementary undergraduate linear-algebra concepts; the subsequent cancellation is scalar arithmetic.
