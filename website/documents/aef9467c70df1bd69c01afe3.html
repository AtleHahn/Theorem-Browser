Statement

Let M be a finite representable matroid on E=\{1,\ldots,n\}, represented by columns v_1,\ldots,v_n over a field. Its rank is \rho(X)=\dim\operatorname{span}\{v_j:j\in X\}. For X_1,X_2,X_3,X_4\subseteq E, write \rho_{i_1\cdots i_k}=\rho(X_{i_1}\cup\cdots\cup X_{i_k}). Then \rho_1+\rho_2+\rho_{123}+\rho_{124}+\rho_{34}\le\rho_{12}+\rho_{13}+\rho_{14}+\rho_{23}+\rho_{24}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The representable matroid is explicitly given by columns of a matrix and its rank by dimensions of their spans. Thus every quantity is ordinary finite-dimensional linear algebra, requiring no abstract matroid theory.

Proof 1: Represent rank by dimensions of column spans

Suppose that M is a representable matroid and let A=[v_1\ v_2\ \cdots\ v_n] be a matrix such that M=M(A). For X,Y\subseteq E(M)=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}, define

U=\operatorname{span}\{v_i:i\in X\}, \qquad W=\operatorname{span}\{v_j:j\in Y\}.

If we suppose that U=\operatorname{span}\{u_1,u_2,\ldots,u_m\} and W=\operatorname{span}\{w_1,w_2,\ldots,w_r\}, then clearly we have

\operatorname{span}\{u_1,u_2,\ldots,u_m,w_1,w_2,\ldots,w_r\}=U+W.

Hence

\rho(X\cup Y) =\dim\operatorname{span}\bigl(\{v_i:i\in X\}\cup\{v_j:j\in Y\}\bigr) =\dim(U+W).

Finally, if we define V_i=\operatorname{span}\{v_j:j\in X_i\} for i=1,2,3,4, then by the last equality and Ingleton's inequality for four finite-dimensional subspaces,

\begin{aligned} &\dim V_1+\dim V_2+\dim(V_1+V_2+V_3)+\dim(V_1+V_2+V_4)+\dim(V_3+V_4)\\ &\qquad\le\dim(V_1+V_2)+\dim(V_1+V_3)+\dim(V_1+V_4) +\dim(V_2+V_3)+\dim(V_2+V_4), \end{aligned}

we get the result.
