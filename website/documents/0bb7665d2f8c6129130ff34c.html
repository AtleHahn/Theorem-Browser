Statement

Let p,c_0,c_1,\ldots\in\mathbb C and L=\limsup_{n\to\infty}|c_n|^{1/n}\in[0,\infty]. The radius of convergence of \sum_{n=0}^\infty c_n(z-p)^n is R=1/L, with 1/0=\infty and 1/\infty=0. The series converges absolutely for |z-p|<R and diverges for |z-p|>R; no conclusion at the boundary is asserted.

Level justification

Proof 1: Substitute the power-series terms into the root test — outline

Put a_n=c_n(z-p)^n and apply the root test to these terms. Their absolute nth roots have the factorization |a_n|^{1/n}=|c_n|^{1/n}|z-p|. This root-test specialization gives the radius 1/\limsup_{n\to\infty}|c_n|^{1/n}, with convergence on the boundary requiring a separate check.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof applies the scalar root test to the moduli of complex-series terms and factors out the fixed distance from the center. This is ordinary undergraduate series analysis, not contour or holomorphic-function theory. The center is handled directly when the scaling factor is zero.
