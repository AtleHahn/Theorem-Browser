Statement

Let E be a real separable Banach space and let X_1,X_2,\ldots be independent Borel-measurable E-valued random variables on one probability space. No symmetry assumption is made. Put S_n=\sum_{j=1}^nX_j. The following are equivalent:

  1. For some E-valued random variable S on this probability space, \|S_n-S\|\to0 almost surely.
  2. For some such S, \|S_n-S\|\to0 in probability.
  3. The laws of S_n converge weakly to a Borel probability measure on E.

A separable Banach space is a complete normed real vector space with a countable dense subset. Almost surely means outside an event of probability zero. Convergence in probability means \mathbb P(\|S_n-S\|>\varepsilon)\to0 for every \varepsilon>0. Weak convergence of laws means convergence of integrals of every bounded continuous real function on E. The limit in the first two conditions is existential, not a random variable fixed in advance.
