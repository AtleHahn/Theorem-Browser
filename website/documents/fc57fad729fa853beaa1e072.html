Statement

For a_n>0 and n\ge2, put \rho_n=n\log n(a_n/a_{n+1}-1)-\log n. The series \sum a_n converges if \liminf\rho_n>1 and diverges if \limsup\rho_n<1.

Proof 1: Logarithmic auxiliary sequence — outline

Let {\displaystyle \zeta _{n}} be an auxiliary sequence of positive constants. Define

{\displaystyle K_n\equiv \left(\zeta _{n}{\frac {a_{n}}{a_{n+1}}}-\zeta _{n+1}\right)}

Kummer's test states that the series will:

  • Converge if there exists a {\displaystyle c>0} such that {\displaystyle K_n\geq c} for all {\displaystyle n>N}. (Note this is not the same as saying {\displaystyle K_n>0})
  • Diverge if {\displaystyle K_n\leq 0} for all {\displaystyle n>N} and {\displaystyle \sum _{n=N+1}^{\infty }1/\zeta _{n}} diverges.

If K_n\ge c>0 eventually, fix a positive number 0<\delta<c. There exists a natural number {\displaystyle N} such that for every {\displaystyle n>N},

{\displaystyle \delta \leq \zeta _{n}{\frac {a_{n}}{a_{n+1}}}-\zeta _{n+1}}.

Since {\displaystyle a_{n+1}>0}, for every {\displaystyle n>N},

{\displaystyle 0\leq \delta a_{n+1}\leq \zeta _{n}a_{n}-\zeta _{n+1}a_{n+1}}.

In particular {\displaystyle \zeta _{n+1}a_{n+1}\leq \zeta _{n}a_{n}} for all {\displaystyle n>N} which means that starting from the index {\displaystyle N+1} the sequence {\displaystyle \zeta _{n}a_{n}>0} is monotonically decreasing and positive which in particular implies that it is bounded below by 0. Therefore, the limit

{\displaystyle \lim _{n\to \infty }\zeta _{n}a_{n}=L} exists.

This implies that the positive telescoping series

{\displaystyle \sum _{n=N+1}^{\infty }\left(\zeta _{n}a_{n}-\zeta _{n+1}a_{n+1}\right)} is convergent,

and since for all {\displaystyle n>N},

{\displaystyle \delta a_{n+1}\leq \zeta _{n}a_{n}-\zeta _{n+1}a_{n+1}}

by the direct comparison test for positive series, the series {\displaystyle \sum _{n=N+1}^{\infty }\delta a_{n+1}} is convergent.

On the other hand, if K_n\le0 eventually and \sum_n1/\zeta_n diverges, then there is an N such that {\displaystyle \zeta _{n}a_{n}} is nondecreasing for {\displaystyle n>N}. In particular, there exists an {\displaystyle \epsilon >0} for which {\displaystyle \zeta _{n}a_{n}>\epsilon } for all {\displaystyle n>N}, and so {\displaystyle \sum _{n}a_{n}=\sum _{n}{\frac {a_{n}\zeta _{n}}{\zeta _{n}}}} diverges by comparison with {\displaystyle \sum _{n}{\frac {\epsilon }{\zeta _{n}}}}.

For Bertrand's test, let {\displaystyle \zeta _{n}=n\ln(n)}. Then:

{\displaystyle K_n=n\ln(n)\left({\frac {a_{n}}{a_{n+1}}}\right)-(n+1)\ln(n+1)}

Using {\displaystyle \ln(n+1)=\ln(n)+\ln(1+1/n)} and \ln(1+1/n)=1/n+O(n^{-2}) for large n, {\displaystyle K_n} may be written:

{\displaystyle K_n=n\ln(n)\left({\frac {a_{n}}{a_{n+1}}}-1\right)-\ln(n)-1+o(1)=\rho_n-1+o(1)}

The reciprocal series \sum_{n\ge2}1/(n\log n) diverges. The strict liminf or limsup condition therefore gives the corresponding eventual bound in Kummer’s test.

Level justification

The proof develops Kummer’s comparison through telescoping positive series, then uses a logarithmic Taylor estimate and strict liminf/limsup conditions. These are undergraduate real-analysis tools. The distinction between an eventually positive expression and a uniform positive lower bound is correctly retained, and the strict limit hypotheses supply the needed margin.
