Statement

The complex projective plane \mathbb P^2(\mathbb C) consists of nonzero triples (x,y,z)\in\mathbb C^3 modulo multiplication by a nonzero complex scalar; write its points as [x:y:z]. A rational self-map is represented by

[x:y:z]\dashmapsto[f_0(x,y,z):f_1(x,y,z):f_2(x,y,z)],

where the f_i are homogeneous polynomials of the same degree, not all zero, and the map is defined wherever their values are not all zero. Two representations define the same rational map when they agree on a nonempty Zariski-open subset (the complement of an algebraic subset defined by homogeneous polynomial equations). A rational self-map is birational if it has a rational inverse, with both compositions equal to the identity as rational maps.

Every birational self-map of \mathbb P^2(\mathbb C) is a finite composition of projective linear automorphisms

[v]\longmapsto[Av],\qquad A\in\mathrm{GL}_3(\mathbb C),

and the standard quadratic transformation

\sigma:[x:y:z]\dashmapsto[yz:zx:xy].

Equivalently, the group of birational self-maps under composition, called the plane Cremona group, is generated by \mathrm{PGL}_3(\mathbb C)=\mathrm{GL}_3(\mathbb C)/\{cI:c\in\mathbb C^\times\} together with \sigma.
