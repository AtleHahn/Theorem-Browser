Statement

Let ABC be a nondegenerate Euclidean triangle, with opposite side lengths a,b,c, respective interior angles \alpha,\beta,\gamma, semiperimeter s=(a+b+c)/2, and inradius r>0. Then \frac{a+b}{c}=\frac{\cos((\alpha-\beta)/2)}{\sin(\gamma/2)}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement equates an elementary ratio of side lengths with a ratio of sines and cosines of half-angle sums or differences.

Proof 1: Cotangent product-to-sum and cosine addition

Let the incircle touch the three sides. The two tangent segments from each vertex have equal length. If their lengths from A,B,C are x,y,z, respectively, then a=y+z, b=z+x, c=x+y, and s=x+y+z. Thus x=s-a, y=s-b, and z=s-c. The radius to a tangency point is perpendicular to the side, and the segment joining the incenter to a vertex bisects that vertex angle. The resulting right triangles therefore give \cot(\alpha/2)=(s-a)/r, \cot(\beta/2)=(s-b)/r, and \cot(\gamma/2)=(s-c)/r.

We also use the general addition formula

\cot(u+v+w)= \frac{\cot u+\cot v+\cot w-\cot u\cot v\cot w} {1-\cot u\cot v-\cot v\cot w-\cot w\cot u}.

Applying it to \cot(\tfrac12\alpha+\tfrac12\beta+\tfrac12\gamma)=\cot\tfrac\pi2=0, we obtain

\cot\frac\alpha2\cot\frac\beta2\cot\frac\gamma2 =\cot\frac\alpha2+\cot\frac\beta2+\cot\frac\gamma2.

This is the triple cotangent identity.

From the addition formula and the law of cotangents we have

\begin{aligned} \frac{\cos\frac12(\alpha-\beta)}{\cos\frac12(\alpha+\beta)} &=\frac{\cot\frac12\alpha\,\cot\frac12\beta+1} {\cot\frac12\alpha\,\cot\frac12\beta-1}\\[4pt] &=\frac{\cot\frac12\alpha+\cot\frac12\beta+2\cot\frac12\gamma} {\cot\frac12\alpha+\cot\frac12\beta}\\[4pt] &=\frac{4s-a-b-2c}{2s-a-b}. \end{aligned}

Here, an extra step is required to transform a product into a sum, according to the triple cotangent identity above. This gives the result

\frac{b+a}{c}=\frac{\cos\frac12(\alpha-\beta)}{\sin\frac12\gamma}

as required.
