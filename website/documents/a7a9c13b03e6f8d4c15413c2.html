Statement

For -1<x<1, with \arcsin x\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2], (\arcsin x)'=1/\sqrt{1-x^2}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Inverse sine; Real derivatives; Principal real inverse branches. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Differentiate sine of the principal inverse

We let

{\displaystyle y=\arcsin x\,\!}

Where

{\displaystyle -{\frac {\pi }{2}}\leq y\leq {\frac {\pi }{2}}}

Then

{\displaystyle \sin y=x\,\!}

Taking the derivative with respect to {\displaystyle x} on both sides and solving for dy/dx:

{\displaystyle {d \over dx}\sin y={d \over dx}x}{\displaystyle \cos y\cdot {dy \over dx}=1\,\!}

Substituting {\displaystyle \cos y={\sqrt {1-\sin ^{2}y}}} in from above,

{\displaystyle {\sqrt {1-\sin ^{2}y}}\cdot {dy \over dx}=1}

Substituting {\displaystyle x=\sin y} in from above,

{\displaystyle {\sqrt {1-x^{2}}}\cdot {dy \over dx}=1}{\displaystyle {dy \over dx}={\frac {1}{\sqrt {1-x^{2}}}}}
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The principal inverse lies in (-pi/2,pi/2), where cosine is positive and sine has nonzero derivative. Differentiate sin y=x and replace cos y by the positive square root sqrt(1-x^2). This is standard inverse-function calculus.
