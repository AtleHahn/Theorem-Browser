Statement

Let k be algebraically closed, n\geq0, and I a proper ideal of k[t_1,\ldots,t_n]. Then some x\in k^n satisfies f(x)=0 for every f\in I.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory ring theory supplies polynomial ideals and maximality. Algebraically closed means every nonconstant one-variable polynomial has a root; the extension here simply contains the coefficient field. The claims concern simultaneous polynomial zeros, requiring no Galois arithmetic or schemes.

Proof 1: Finite residue field and evaluation

Local lemma

If a field K is finitely generated as an algebra over a subfield k, then [K:k]<\infty.

The lemma is a consequence of the Noether normalization lemma. Indeed, by the normalization lemma, K is a finite module over the polynomial ring {\displaystyle k[x_{1},\ldots ,x_{d}]} where {\displaystyle x_{1},\ldots ,x_{d}} are elements of K that are algebraically independent over k. But since K has Krull dimension zero and since an integral ring extension (e.g., a finite ring extension) preserves Krull dimensions, the polynomial ring must have dimension zero; i.e., {\displaystyle d=0}.

Evaluate in the residue field

Replacing I by a maximal ideal {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {m}}}, we can assume {\displaystyle I={\mathfrak {m}}} is maximal. Let {\displaystyle A=k[t_{1},...,t_{n}]} and {\displaystyle \phi :A\to A/{\mathfrak {m}}} be the natural surjection. By the lemma {\displaystyle A/{\mathfrak {m}}} is a finite extension. Since k is algebraically closed that extension must be k. Then for any {\displaystyle f\in {\mathfrak {m}}},

{\displaystyle f(\phi (t_{1}),\cdots ,\phi (t_{n}))=\phi (f(t_{1},\cdots ,t_{n}))=0};

that is to say, {\displaystyle x=(\phi (t_{1}),\cdots ,\phi (t_{n}))} is a zero of {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {m}}}.
