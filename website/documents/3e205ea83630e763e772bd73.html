Statement

Consider a nondegenerate spherical triangle on a unit sphere, with minor great-circle sides. Let a,b,c\in(0,\pi) be the central angles subtended by its sides, where a joins vertices \mathbf u,\mathbf v, b joins \mathbf u,\mathbf w and c joins \mathbf v,\mathbf w. Let C be its interior angle at \mathbf u, measured between the tangent directions of the two sides there. As a,b,c\to0, c^2=a^2+b^2-2ab\cos C+O(a^4+b^4+c^4).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Spherical triangles; Small-angle limits; Big O error terms. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Insert the small-angle Taylor expansions

To prove this, we will use the small-angle approximation obtained from the Maclaurin series for the cosine and sine functions:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\cos a&=1-{\frac {a^{2}}{2}}+O\left(a^{4}\right)\\\sin a&=a+O\left(a^{3}\right)\end{aligned}}}

Substituting these expressions into the spherical law of cosines nets:

{\displaystyle 1-{\frac {c^{2}}{2}}+O\left(c^{4}\right)=1-{\frac {a^{2}}{2}}-{\frac {b^{2}}{2}}+{\frac {a^{2}b^{2}}{4}}+O\left(a^{4}\right)+O\left(b^{4}\right)+\cos(C)\left(ab+O\left(a^{3}b\right)+O\left(ab^{3}\right)+O\left(a^{3}b^{3}\right)\right)}

or after simplifying:

{\displaystyle c^{2}=a^{2}+b^{2}-2ab\cos C+O\left(c^{4}\right)+O\left(a^{4}\right)+O\left(b^{4}\right)+O\left(a^{2}b^{2}\right)+O\left(a^{3}b\right)+O\left(ab^{3}\right)+O\left(a^{3}b^{3}\right).}

The big O terms for a and b are dominated by O(a4) + O(b4) as a and b get small, so we can write this last expression as:

{\displaystyle c^{2}=a^{2}+b^{2}-2ab\cos C+O\left(a^{4}\right)+O\left(b^{4}\right)+O\left(c^{4}\right).} Since |\cos C|\le1, the bounds are uniform in the included angle.
