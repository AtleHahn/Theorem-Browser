Statement

Let W:S^2\to\mathbb R^3 be continuous. There exists p\in S^2 at which the component of W(p) tangent to the sphere is zero; equivalently, W(p) is a scalar multiple of p.

Proof 1: A continuous vector field on a sphere is radial somewhere — outline

The component of W tangent to each spherical shell is a continuous tangent field. The hairy ball theorem forces that field to vanish at some point. This is the source’s horizontal-wind conclusion for a spherical shell.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Project W orthogonally onto p-perpendicular vectors and apply the stated hairy-ball consequence. Continuous vector-valued functions and orthogonal projection suffice; tangent vectors on this sphere are explicitly perpendicular vectors, without general bundles.
