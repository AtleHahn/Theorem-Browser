Statement

Let 1<p\leq\infty, and let f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb C be a measurable 2\pi-periodic function whose restriction to [0,2\pi] belongs to L^p. For each integer n, define

\widehat f(n)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} f(t)e^{-int}\,dt.

Then the symmetric Fourier partial sums converge to f at Lebesgue-almost every point:

\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{|n|\leq N}\widehat f(n)e^{inx}=f(x)\qquad\text{for almost every }x\in\mathbb R.

For finite p, membership in L^p means \int_0^{2\pi}|f(t)|^p\,dt<\infty; for p=\infty, it means essential boundedness.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Fourier partial sums and complex exponentials are accessible from calculus, but general Lebesgue Lp classes, essential boundedness and almost-everywhere convergence add a cumulative measure-theoretic burden not confidently covered by introductory real analysis plus thirty minutes. Conservatively beyond undergraduate; this is not a rating of the difficult proof.
