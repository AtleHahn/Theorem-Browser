Statement

Let G be a finite directed acyclic graph and k\ge1. Let T_k have vertices 1,\ldots,k and arrows i\to j exactly when i<j, and let P_{k+1} be the directed path on k+1 vertices. A graph homomorphism maps vertices while preserving arrows. Then a homomorphism G\to T_k exists if and only if no homomorphism P_{k+1}\to G exists.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The target graph and arrow-preserving maps are explicitly defined. The claim concerns finite directed paths, absence of cycles and integer labels from 1 to k, all understandable using elementary finite reasoning.

Proof 1: Map longest-path ranks to the transitive tournament — outline

Use reachability to order the vertices of G. In an acyclic graph a directed walk cannot repeat a vertex, and chains in the reachability order can be joined along directed paths; hence the longest chain size is the largest number of vertices in a directed path. If no P_{k+1} maps to G, this size is at most k. The rank N(v) of a longest chain ending at v, from the source's Mirsky construction, strictly increases along every arrow. Thus v\mapsto N(v) is a homomorphism to T_k. Conversely a homomorphism from P_{k+1} to G followed by one to T_k would require k+1 strictly increasing labels in \{1,\ldots,k\}, impossible.
