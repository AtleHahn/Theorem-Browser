Statement

Let N_t be a centered real Gaussian process on [0,T] with continuous covariance R(t,s). Suppose deterministic k,S\in L^2([0,T]) satisfy \int_0^T R(t,s)k(s)\,ds=S(t) almost everywhere, and let \rho=\int_0^T k(t)S(t)\,dt>0. Put G=\int_0^T k(t)X_t\,dt, with X=N under H and X=N+S under K. Then G\sim N(0,\rho) under H and G\sim N(\rho,\rho) under K. The threshold G_0=\sqrt\rho\,\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha) gives false-alarm probability \alpha\in(0,1) and detection probability \Phi(\sqrt\rho-\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha)).

Proof 1: Use the covariance equation in the second moment

Let \Phi denote the standard normal distribution function.

Writing x(t)=X_t and R_N=R, since X(t) is a Gaussian process,

{\displaystyle G=\int _{0}^{T}k(t)x(t)\,dt,}

is a Gaussian random variable that can be characterized by its mean and variance.

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\mathbf {E} [G\mid H]&=\int _{0}^{T}k(t)\mathbf {E} [x(t)\mid H]\,dt=0\\\mathbf {E} [G\mid K]&=\int _{0}^{T}k(t)\mathbf {E} [x(t)\mid K]\,dt=\int _{0}^{T}k(t)S(t)\,dt\equiv \rho \\\mathbf {E} [G^{2}\mid H]&=\int _{0}^{T}\int _{0}^{T}k(t)k(s)R_{N}(t,s)\,dt\,ds=\int _{0}^{T}k(t)\left(\int _{0}^{T}k(s)R_{N}(t,s)\,ds\right)\,dt=\int _{0}^{T}k(t)S(t)\,dt=\rho \\\operatorname {var} [G\mid H]&=\mathbf {E} [G^{2}\mid H]-(\mathbf {E} [G\mid H])^{2}=\rho \\\mathbf {E} [G^{2}\mid K]&=\int _{0}^{T}\int _{0}^{T}k(t)k(s)\mathbf {E} [x(t)x(s)\mid K]\,dt\,ds=\int _{0}^{T}\int _{0}^{T}k(t)k(s)(R_{N}(t,s)+S(t)S(s))\,dt\,ds=\rho +\rho ^{2}\\\operatorname {var} [G\mid K]&=\mathbf {E} [G^{2}|K]-(\mathbf {E} [G|K])^{2}=\rho +\rho ^{2}-\rho ^{2}=\rho \end{aligned}}}

Hence, we obtain the distributions under H and K:

{\displaystyle H:G\sim N(0,\rho )}{\displaystyle K:G\sim N(\rho ,\rho )}

In the integrals below, N(m,v) denotes the normal density with mean m and variance v, evaluated at the integration variable G.

The false alarm error is

{\displaystyle \alpha =\int _{G_{0}}^{\infty }N(0,\rho )\,dG=1-\Phi \left({\frac {G_{0}}{\sqrt {\rho }}}\right).}

So the test threshold is

{\displaystyle G_{0}={\sqrt {\rho }}\Phi ^{-1}(1-\alpha ).}

Its power of detection is

{\displaystyle \beta =\int _{G_{0}}^{\infty }N(\rho ,\rho )\,dG=\Phi \left({\sqrt {\rho }}-\Phi ^{-1}(1-\alpha )\right)}
