Statement

For every finite field F and integer n\ge1, the n polynomials f_j(X_1,\ldots,X_n)=X_j have degree sum n and exactly one common zero in F^n, the origin. Thus the strict degree-sum inequality in the divisibility and nontrivial-zero theorems cannot be replaced by a non-strict one.

Level justification

Introductory algebra supplies fields, characteristic and multivariate polynomial evaluation. A finite field is a field with finitely many elements; the statements only count its polynomial solutions and compare degrees, without field extensions or norm maps. This is a direct use of the baseline.

Proof 1: Set every coordinate equal to zero

The Chevalley–Warning divisibility theorem and Chevalley's nontrivial-zero theorem are best possible in the sense that, given any n, the list f_j=X_j, j=1,\dots,n, has degree sum n and only the trivial solution.

Level justification

The coordinate equations force every coordinate to zero, and each polynomial has degree one. The reasoning is elementary, but the full statement uses arbitrary finite fields and polynomial systems, standard undergraduate algebra.
