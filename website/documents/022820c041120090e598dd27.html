Statement

Let X be a paracompact Hausdorff space and Y a real topological vector space. Suppose F assigns to each x\in X a nonempty convex subset F(x)\subset Y, and for each y\in Y the set \{x\in X:y\in F(x)\} is open in X. Then there is a continuous function f:X\to Y such that f(x)\in F(x) for every x\in X. A topological vector space has continuous addition and scalar multiplication. Convexity means closure under tu+(1-t)v for 0\le t\le1. Paracompact means every open cover has a locally finite open refinement: every member of the refinement lies in a member of the original cover, and each point has a neighborhood meeting only finitely many refinement members. Hausdorff means distinct points have disjoint neighborhoods. The result also applies to complex topological vector spaces regarded as real ones.
