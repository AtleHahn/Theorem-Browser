Statement

Let a bounded region V\subset\mathbb R^3 be partitioned into finitely many subregions V_1,\ldots,V_m with piecewise smooth boundaries. Assume their outer boundary pieces partition \partial V and each internal interface occurs twice with opposite outward normals, up to surface-null sets. For a continuous vector field F on the boundaries, put \Phi(W)=\int_{\partial W}F\cdot n_W\,dS. Then \Phi(V)=\sum_{j=1}^m\Phi(V_j).

Proof 1: Flux is additive across a finite partition

First suppose that V is divided into two volumes V_1 and V_2 by a surface S_3, and let S_1,S_2 be their respective external boundary parts. The flux \Phi(V_i) out of each component region V_i is equal to the sum of the flux through its two boundary parts, so the sum of the flux out of the two parts is

\Phi(V_1)+\Phi(V_2)=\Phi_1+\Phi_{31}+\Phi_2+\Phi_{32},

where \Phi_1 and \Phi_2 are the flux out of surfaces S_1 and S_2, \Phi_{31} is the flux through S_3 out of volume 1, and \Phi_{32} is the flux through S_3 out of volume 2. The point is that surface S_3 is part of the surface of both volumes. The outward direction of the normal vector \hat n is opposite for each volume, so the flux out of one through S_3 is equal to the negative of the flux out of the other, so these two fluxes cancel in the sum:

\Phi_{31}=\int_{S_3}F\cdot\hat n\,dS =-\int_{S_3}F\cdot(-\hat n)\,dS=-\Phi_{32}.

Therefore:

\Phi(V_1)+\Phi(V_2)=\Phi_1+\Phi_2.

Since the union of surfaces S_1 and S_2 is \partial V,

\Phi(V_1)+\Phi(V_2)=\Phi(V).

This principle applies to a volume divided into any finite number of parts. Since the integral over each internal partition appears with opposite signs in the flux of the two adjacent volumes, they cancel out, and the only contribution to the flux is the integral over the external surfaces. Since the external surfaces of all the component volumes equal the original surface,

\Phi(V)=\sum_{i=1}^m\Phi(V_i).
