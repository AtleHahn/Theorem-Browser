Statement

For n\ge1 and real sequences a_1\le\cdots\le a_n and b_1\le\cdots\le b_n, \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n a_i b_i\ge\left(\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n a_i\right)\left(\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n b_i\right).

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement uses only finite real sums and order.

Proof 1: Specialize FKG to a uniform chain — outline

Apply the FKG inequality to the chain X=\{1,\ldots,n\}, with uniform weights and the increasing functions f(i)=a_i and g(i)=b_i.

If the lattice {\displaystyle X} is totally ordered, then the lattice condition is satisfied trivially for any measure μ. In case the measure μ is uniform, the FKG inequality is Chebyshev's sum inequality: if the two increasing functions take on values {\displaystyle a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq \cdots \leq a_{n}} and {\displaystyle b_{1}\leq b_{2}\leq \cdots \leq b_{n}}, then

{\displaystyle {\frac {a_{1}b_{1}+\cdots +a_{n}b_{n}}{n}}\geq {\frac {a_{1}+\cdots +a_{n}}{n}}\;{\frac {b_{1}+\cdots +b_{n}}{n}}.}
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the explicitly stated finite FKG theorem to the totally ordered index set. Meet and join are min and max, distributivity is immediate, and constant weights satisfy the product condition with equality. These finite order definitions are a small extension of undergraduate discrete mathematics; no statistical-mechanics or measure-theory proof is used.
