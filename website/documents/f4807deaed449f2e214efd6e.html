Statement

For each integer d\ge1, there is an integer m>d and a map f:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^m that preserves every unit distance but does not preserve all Euclidean distances.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves finite-dimensional Euclidean spaces; distance-preserving functions. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Map colors to simplex vertices — outline

Use the known fact that for each finite d, the points of \mathbb R^d can be colored with finitely many colors so that points at unit distance have different colors.

Every Euclidean space can be mapped to a space of sufficiently higher dimension in a way that preserves unit distances but is not an isometry. To do so, following known results on the Hadwiger–Nelson problem, color the points of the given space with a finite number of colors so that no two points at unit distance have the same color. Then, map each color to a vertex of a higher-dimensional regular simplex with unit edge lengths.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the exact finite coloring result and send colors to distinct unit-simplex vertices. Euclidean linear algebra and elementary coloring suffice; no proof of the coloring bound is needed, and finite image excludes an isometry.

Routine omissions

A finite image cannot preserve all distances in an unbounded domain. Padding target coordinates allows a strictly larger dimension in the general case.
