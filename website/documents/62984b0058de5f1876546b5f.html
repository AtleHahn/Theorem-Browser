Statement

Let L be a fixed holomorphic logarithm branch on a slit plane avoiding zero, and let c\in\mathbb C. For f(z)=\exp(cL(z)), one has f'(z)=(c/z)\exp(cL(z)).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Calculus and complex arithmetic supply the formulas, curves and estimates. Complex differentiability, holomorphic logarithm branches or conformal bijections are closely related extensions; the contour integral can be understood by parametrization and the branch is explicitly fixed where needed. No Riemann surfaces, residues or differential forms are required by these statements.

Proof 1: Differentiate on a fixed logarithm branch

Consider f(z)=z^c, where c is any complex number and z is in the given slit complex plane excluding 0. On the fixed logarithm branch L, define

z^c=\exp(cL(z)).

Applying the chain rule, as in the real power-rule argument, and using L'(z)=1/z and the derivative of the complex exponential, yields

f'(z)=\frac cz\exp(cL(z)).

Due to the multivalued nature of complex power functions for non-integer exponents, one must be careful to specify the branch of the complex logarithm being used.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The differentiation uses a holomorphic logarithm branch on a slit plane, including its derivative and compatibility with a complex power. Analytic branches require complex-analysis background beyond an elementary complex difference quotient.
