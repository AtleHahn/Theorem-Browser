Statement

On a measure space (S,\Sigma,\mu), write \|h\|_t=(\int_S|h|^t\,d\mu)^{1/t} for 0<t<\infty, and \|h\|_\infty=\operatorname{ess\,sup}|h|. Norms may be infinite; products with a zero factor are defined to be zero, including 0\cdot\infty. Let 1<p,q<\infty, 1/p+1/q=1, f\in L^p(\mu) and g\in L^q(\mu). Then \|fg\|_1=\|f\|_p\|g\|_q iff there are \alpha,\beta\ge0, not both zero, with \alpha|f|^p=\beta|g|^q almost everywhere.

Proof 1: Equality criterion in Hölder’s inequality — outline

For positive norms normalize a=|f|/\|f\|_p and b=|g|/\|g\|_q. Young’s nonnegative difference a^p/p+b^q/q-ab integrates to zero exactly when it vanishes almost everywhere. Young’s equality condition is a^p=b^q, equivalently

\|g\|_q^q|f|^p=\|f\|_p^p|g|^q.

Conversely a nonzero nonnegative proportionality of the two integrable powers gives this normalized equality after integration, hence equality in Hölder. If f=0 almost everywhere, take \alpha=1,\beta=0; if g=0, take \alpha=0,\beta=1. These are exactly the zero-norm cases.
