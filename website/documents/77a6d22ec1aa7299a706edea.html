Statement

Let A:H\to Z be a bounded linear map between real or complex Hilbert spaces, with brackets [z,x] conjugate-linear in the first variable. There is a unique bounded linear map A^*:Z\to H such that [z,Ah]_Z=[A^*z,h]_H for every z,h.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra supplies inner products, adjoints and scalar-valued linear maps; analysis supplies completeness and norm bounds. The statement explicitly characterizes the representing map or adjoint, so understanding the existence claim adds only the complete inner-product setting, without weak topology or spectral theory.

Proof 1: Represent each composed inner-product functional — outline

In the source’s bracket notation, \langle z\mid x\rangle=[z,x]; also write \langle x,z\rangle=[z,x].

For every {\displaystyle z\in Z,} the scalar-valued map {\displaystyle \langle z\mid A(\cdot )\rangle _{Z}} on {\displaystyle H} defined by

{\displaystyle h\mapsto \langle z\mid Ah\rangle _{Z}=\langle Ah,z\rangle _{Z}}

is a continuous linear functional on {\displaystyle H} and so by the Riesz representation theorem, there exists a unique vector in {\displaystyle H,} denoted by {\displaystyle A^{*}z,} such that {\displaystyle \langle z\mid A(\cdot )\rangle _{Z}=\left\langle A^{*}z\mid \cdot \,\right\rangle _{H},} or equivalently, such that

{\displaystyle \langle z\mid Ah\rangle _{Z}=\left\langle A^{*}z\mid h\right\rangle _{H}\quad {\text{ for all }}h\in H.}

The assignment {\displaystyle z\mapsto A^{*}z} thus induces a function {\displaystyle A^{*}:Z\to H} called the adjoint of {\displaystyle A:H\to Z} whose defining condition is

{\displaystyle \langle z\mid Ah\rangle _{Z}=\left\langle A^{*}z\mid h\right\rangle _{H}\quad {\text{ for all }}h\in H{\text{ and all }}z\in Z.}

The adjoint {\displaystyle A^{*}:Z\to H} is necessarily a continuous (equivalently, a bounded) linear operator.
