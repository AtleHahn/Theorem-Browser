Statement

Let k\ge1 and m\ge0 be integers. For real x, let B_m(x) be the Bernoulli polynomial, conventionally defined by te^{xt}/(e^t-1)=\sum_{m\ge0}B_m(x)t^m/m!. Define [\mathcal L_kf](x)=k^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}f((x+j)/k). Then \mathcal L_kB_m=k^{-m}B_m.

Proof 1: Substitute into the polynomial multiplication identity

For the Bernoulli polynomials, the multiplication theorem is

{\displaystyle k^{1-m}B_{m}(kx)=\sum _{n=0}^{k-1}B_{m}\left(x+{\frac {n}{k}}\right)}

The eigenvectors of \mathcal L_k are given by the Bernoulli polynomials. That is, one has that

{\displaystyle {\mathcal {L}}_{k}B_{m}={\frac {1}{k^{m}}}B_{m}}
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Substitute x/k into the supplied multiplication formula and divide by k to get the displayed eigenidentity. Understanding the full setting uses the exponential Taylor-series definition of the Bernoulli polynomials and a linear operator on functions, both accessible from undergraduate calculus and linear algebra. The operator is explicitly a finite average; no transfer-operator spectral theory is needed.
