Statement

Let m,n\ge1 and r_1,\ldots,r_m be positive integers. Let X be a nonnegative real m\times n matrix with row sums r_i and all column sums 1 (hence \sum_i r_i=n). Then X is a finite convex combination of 0–1 matrices with the same row and column sums. Here a convex combination means \sum_{j=1}^k\theta_jY_j, with \theta_j\ge0 and \sum_j\theta_j=1.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Convex decompositions of arbitrary matrices require undergraduate linear algebra and the notion of a convex combination. A bright US college student should be able to understand the statement after about 30 minutes of focused background research; this rating concerns understanding the claim, not proving it.

Proof 1: Split rows, decompose, and recombine — outline

Replace row i by r_i rows, each equal to the original row divided by r_i. The resulting square matrix is doubly stochastic. Apply the Birkhoff–von Neumann theorem, which expresses it as a convex combination of permutation matrices, then additively recombine each group of r_i rows. This gives the required convex combination of 0–1 matrices.

Split rows into equal unit-sum rows, apply the supplied permutation decomposition and recombine. Undergraduate finite matrix algebra and convex combinations suffice; each recombined column still has exactly one selected entry.
