Statement

Let N=2^n for an integer n\ge0. On a circle of circumference 2N, take N equally spaced points P_1,\ldots,P_N, and let Q be the midpoint of an arc between consecutive points (the antipodal point for N=1). Then \sum_{j=1}^{N}|QP_j|^{-2}=\pi^2/4.

Level
High school only
Level justification

This is a finite geometric identity using lengths and induction.

Proof 1: Doubling circles and inverse Pythagoras outline

For a circle of circumference 2N, let P_1,...,P_N be equally spaced, and choose Q halfway along an arc between adjacent points (for N=1, take the opposite point). Set α=1/2, the fraction of the arc at which Q lies. Let sisc denote the sum of 1/|QP_j|². Compare the two circles by placing Q and the larger center R at opposite ends of a diameter of the smaller circle, as in the source’s stated QR-diameter configuration.

The independence of sisc from N is proved here when {\displaystyle N} is a power of 2, i.e. {\displaystyle N=2^{n}}. The proof proceeds by induction on {\displaystyle n}, and uses the Inverse Pythagorean Theorem, which states that:

{\displaystyle {\frac {1}{a^{2}}}+{\frac {1}{b^{2}}}={\frac {1}{h^{2}}}}

where {\displaystyle a} and {\displaystyle b} are the legs and {\displaystyle h} is the height of a right triangle.

  • In the base case of {\displaystyle n=0}, there is only 1 chord. In the case of {\displaystyle \alpha =1/2}, it corresponds to the diameter and the {\displaystyle sisc} is {\displaystyle \pi ^{2}/4} .

  • For the nondegenerate doubling step, take n\ge1. Assume that you have {\displaystyle 2^{n}} points on a circle with radius {\displaystyle 2^{n}/\pi } and center {\displaystyle O}, and {\displaystyle 2^{n+1}} points on a circle with radius {\displaystyle 2^{n+1}/\pi } and center {\displaystyle R}. The induction step consists in showing that these 2 circles have the same {\displaystyle sisc} for a given {\displaystyle \alpha }.

  • Start by drawing the circles so that they share point {\displaystyle Q}. Note that {\displaystyle R} lies on the smaller circle. Then, note that {\displaystyle 2^{n+1}} is always even, and a simple geometric argument shows that you can pick pairs of opposite points {\displaystyle P_{1}} and {\displaystyle P_{2}} on the larger circle by joining each pair with a diameter. Furthermore, for each pair, one of the points will be in the "lower" half of the circle (closer to {\displaystyle Q}) and the other in the "upper" half.

  • The diameter of the bigger circle {\displaystyle P_{1}P_{2}} cuts the smaller circle at {\displaystyle R} and at another point {\displaystyle P}. You can then make the following considerations:

    • {\displaystyle P_{1}{\widehat {Q}}P_{2}} is a right angle, since {\displaystyle P_{1}P_{2}} is a diameter.
    • {\displaystyle Q{\widehat {P}}R} is a right angle, since {\displaystyle QR} is a diameter.
    • {\displaystyle Q{\widehat {R}}P_{2}=Q{\widehat {R}}P} is half of {\displaystyle Q{\widehat {O}}P} for the Inscribed Angle Theorem.
    • Hence, the arc {\displaystyle QP} is equal to the arc {\displaystyle QP_{2}}, again because the radius is half.
    • The chord {\displaystyle QP} is the height of the right triangle {\displaystyle QP_{1}P_{2}}, hence for the Inverse Pythagorean Theorem:
{\displaystyle {\frac {1}{{\overline {QP}}^{2}}}={\frac {1}{{\overline {QP_{1}}}^{2}}}+{\frac {1}{{\overline {QP_{2}}}^{2}}}}
  • Hence for half of the points on the bigger circle (the ones in the lower half) there is a corresponding point on the smaller circle with the same arc distance from {\displaystyle Q} (since the circumference of the smaller circle is half that of the bigger circle, the last two points closer to {\displaystyle R} must have arc distance 2 as well). Vice versa, for each of the {\displaystyle 2^{n}} points on the smaller circle, we can build a pair of points on the bigger circle, and all of these points are equidistant and have the same arc distance from {\displaystyle Q}.
  • Furthermore, the total {\displaystyle sisc} for the bigger circle is the same as the {\displaystyle sisc} for the smaller circle, since each pair of points on the bigger circle has the same inverse square sum as the corresponding point on the smaller circle.

Routine omissions

The source abbreviates the correspondence between equally spaced small-circle points and opposite pairs on the large circle. The QR-diameter configuration is stated explicitly from the later source paragraph. The initial n=0 to n=1 doubling is a degenerate case omitted by the source: the larger diameter is tangent to the smaller circle at R, so its second intersection is not distinct and the strict nearer/farther description fails. The displayed inverse-Pythagorean relation applies with altitude QR; verification of this boundary case is left implicit, not supplied as a new proof. Subsequent n>=1 midpoint configurations are nondegenerate.
