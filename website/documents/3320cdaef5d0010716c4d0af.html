Statement

Let K be algebraically closed and f_1,\ldots,f_n\in K[x_1,\ldots,x_n] have positive degrees d_1,\ldots,d_n. If their common affine zero set is finite, the sum of its local intersection multiplicities is at most d_1\cdots d_n.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Local intersection multiplicity in arbitrary-dimensional affine algebraic geometry requires local algebra beyond merely understanding common polynomial zeros; a short lookup is insufficient for the full assertion. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Affine Bézout bound from the isolated projective bound (outline)

This theorem is a corollary, not explicitly stated, of a more general statement proved by Masser and Wüstholz.

For stating the general result, one has to recall that the intersection points form an algebraic set, and that there is a finite number of intersection points if and only if all components of the intersection have a zero dimension (an algebraic set of positive dimension has an infinity of points over an algebraically closed field). An intersection point is said isolated if it does not belong to a component of positive dimension of the intersection; the terminology makes sense, since an isolated intersection point has neighborhoods (for Zariski topology or for the usual topology in the case of complex hypersurfaces) that do not contain any other intersection point.

Consider n projective hypersurfaces that are defined over an algebraically closed field by n homogeneous polynomials in {\displaystyle n+1} variables, of degrees {\displaystyle d_{1},\ldots ,d_{n}.} Then, the sum of the multiplicities of their isolated intersection points is at most the product {\displaystyle d_{1}\cdots d_{n}.}

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

To apply the projective bound to the affine problem, the reader must homogenize the polynomials, distinguish isolated projective intersections from positive-dimensional components, and know that local intersection multiplicities agree on an affine chart. In particular multiplicity is a local algebraic-geometric invariant, not merely root counting for one polynomial. The full prerequisite burden remains beyond undergraduate even with the projective theorem accepted.

Affine-chart preservation of isolatedness and local intersection multiplicity
Kind:

Lemma

Treatment:

Implicitly assumed

Statement used:

Restricting projective intersections to an affine chart preserves their local intersection multiplicities, and a finite affine intersection consists of points isolated in that chart and in the projective intersection.

Application in proof:

The affine points contribute their unchanged multiplicities to the projective isolated-point sum, so their total is no larger.

Application level:

Beyond undergraduate level

Level justification:

This uses locality of algebraic intersection multiplicity and component dimension. An informal projective-completion picture does not supply these prerequisites.
