Statement

Let n\ge1 be an integer, written uniquely as

n=(2a+1)2^{4d+c},\qquad a,d\ge0,\quad0\le c<4,

and set \rho(n)=8d+2^c. On the round sphere S^{n-1}=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:\|x\|=1\}, the maximum number of continuous tangent vector fields linearly independent at every point is exactly \rho(n)-1. The same maximum holds for smooth fields.

Explicitly, there exist \rho(n)-1 continuous (and smooth) maps v_j:S^{n-1}\to\mathbb R^n such that x\cdot v_j(x)=0 and the vectors v_1(x),\ldots,v_{\rho(n)-1}(x) are linearly independent for every x. No family of \rho(n) continuous tangent fields has this property. A family of zero fields means the empty family.

Level justification

Tangency is explicitly perpendicularity in Euclidean space. Multivariable calculus and linear algebra suffice for the exact count; no bundles or K-theory are needed to understand it.
