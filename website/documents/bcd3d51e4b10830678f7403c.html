Statement

A global field is a finite extension of \mathbb Q or of \mathbb F_q(T), where \mathbb F_q is a finite field and T an indeterminate. An absolute value on K is a map |\cdot|:K\to[0,\infty) that vanishes exactly at zero, is multiplicative, and satisfies the triangle inequality; it is nontrivial if it is not identically 1 on K\setminus\{0\}. A place v means an equivalence class of nontrivial absolute values (equivalent when they induce the same topology), and K_v is the metric completion of K for that place. A quadratic form in n variables is a homogeneous degree-two polynomial over the field. Let Q be a quadratic form in n\ge1 variables over a global field K. There is a nonzero x\in K^n with Q(x)=0 if and only if for every place v there is a nonzero x_v\in K_v^n with Q(x_v)=0.

Proof 1: Local invariants and characteristic-two reduction (external)

A supporting proof source can be found here (Theorem 2.1 and proof, pp.10–11; Wu arXiv:1904.02355, definitions pp.1–2 and cyclic-algebra step p.5; Schulze-Pillot Theorem 6.1 pp.73–75.). The additional argument below supplies the stated scope and is not attributed to that source.

Supporting sources: Schulze-Pillot, Quadratic Forms, Theorems 6.1–6.2; Stalder, Proposition 16.3: separable completions.

In characteristic different from two, use Schulze-Pillot's Theorems 6.1–6.2 after splitting off the zero radical. The following characteristic-two argument is supplied here using the linked classification theorem and the named arithmetic dependencies.

For a global function field K of characteristic two, quaternion reduced norms are surjective, so Berhuy–Frings–Tignol Theorem 2.1 classifies regular forms by dimension, discriminant and Clifford invariant. The discriminant is an Artin–Schreier class in even dimension and a square class in odd dimension. Brauer classes are detected by all completions by Albert–Brauer–Hasse–Noether; Artin–Schreier classes by Chebotarev; square classes by separability of completion.

A regular form of dimension at least five is isotropic by the C_2 theorem for global function fields. Dimension one is anisotropic. In dimension two, isotropy is equivalent to vanishing of the Arf class, hence is detected locally. In dimension three, local isotropy makes the Clifford invariant zero; classification compares the form with \langle d\rangle\perp H.

In dimension four let D be the Arf class and C the Clifford class. If D=0, locally isotropic forms are hyperbolic, so C=0 globally and classification gives H\perp H. Otherwise let E/K be the Artin–Schreier extension defined by D. Locally an isotropic form is H plus a binary form with Arf class D, so its Clifford class is split by E. Brauer local-global injectivity over E gives C_E=0. The cyclic relative Brauer-group theorem writes C=(D,a) with a\ne0. The binary form aX^2+XY+(D/a)Y^2 has these invariants; classification identifies q with its orthogonal sum with H. Thus local isotropy implies global isotropy; the converse is scalar extension.

For singular forms write q=q_{\rm ns}\perp r\perp0^z, where r is anisotropic quasilinear. Since [K:K^2]=2, \dim r\le2. Separability of completion preserves the independence of its coefficients over squares, so this dimension and z are detected locally. If z>0, isotropy is immediate. When \dim r is zero or one, removing 0^z leaves a regular form covered above. If \dim r=2, its coefficients form a K^2-basis of K, so it represents every scalar and is isometric to \langle1,t\rangle for a fixed 2-basis element t. Adding suitable radical vectors to each vector of a symplectic basis kills the diagonal coefficients of q_{\rm ns} without changing polar pairings. Thus

q\simeq H^s\perp\langle1,t\rangle\perp0^z,

where H is the hyperbolic plane. This form is classified by s,z and is isotropic precisely when s>0 or z>0.
