Statement

Let g:J\to\mathbb R be continuous and u,v:I\to J, h:I\to\mathbb R differentiable, with I,J open real intervals. Then \frac d{dx}\int_{u(x)}^{v(x)}h(x)g(t)\,dt=h'(x)\int_{u(x)}^{v(x)}g(t)\,dt+h(x)[g(v(x))v'(x)-g(u(x))u'(x)].

Proof 1: Factor out the parameter function and use the product rule

The moving-endpoint formula follows from the chain rule and the First Fundamental Theorem of Calculus. Define

{\displaystyle G(x)=\int _{u(x)}^{v(x)}g(t)\,dt,}

and

{\displaystyle \Gamma (x)=\int _{c}^{x}g(t)\,dt.}

Here c is any fixed point of J.

Then, {\displaystyle G(x)} can be written as a composition: {\displaystyle G(x)=(\Gamma \circ v)(x)-(\Gamma \circ u)(x)}. The Chain Rule then implies that

{\displaystyle G'(x)=\Gamma '\left(v(x)\right)v'(x)-\Gamma '\left(u(x)\right)u'(x).}

By the First Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, {\displaystyle \Gamma '(x)=g(x)}. Therefore, substituting this result above, we get the desired equation:

{\displaystyle G'(x)=g\left(v(x)\right){v'(x)}-g\left(u(x)\right){u'(x)}.}

Because {\displaystyle h(x)} does not depend on the integration variable, it may be moved out from under the integral sign, and the moving-endpoint formula may be used with the Product rule, i.e.,

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}{\frac {d}{dx}}\left(\int _{u(x)}^{v(x)}h(x)g(t)\,dt\right)&={\frac {d}{dx}}\left(h(x)\int _{u(x)}^{v(x)}g(t)\,dt\right)\\&=h'(x)\int _{u(x)}^{v(x)}g(t)\,dt+h(x){\frac {d}{dx}}\left(\int _{u(x)}^{v(x)}g(t)\,dt\right)\end{aligned}}}
