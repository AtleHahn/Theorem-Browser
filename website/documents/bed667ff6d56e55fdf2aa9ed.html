Statement

For every integer k\geq1 and every permutation \beta=(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_k) of \{1,\ldots,k\}, there is a real constant C>0 such that for every integer n\geq1, the number of permutations of \{1,\ldots,n\} avoiding \beta is at most C^n.

A permutation \pi=(\pi_1,\ldots,\pi_n) contains \beta if there exist indices 1\leq i_1<\cdots<i_k\leq n such that, for every a,b\in\{1,\ldots,k\},

\pi_{i_a}<\pi_{i_b}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\beta_a<\beta_b.

It avoids \beta if no such indices exist. The constant C may depend on \beta, but not on n.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Finite arrangements, order comparisons and exponentiation suffice; the equivalent limiting formulation is not needed.
