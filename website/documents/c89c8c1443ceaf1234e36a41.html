Statement

For a nondegenerate Euclidean triangle with side lengths a,b,c and semiperimeter s=(a+b+c)/2, its circumdiameter is 2R=\frac{abc}{2\sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}}=\frac{2abc}{\sqrt{(a+b+c)(-a+b+c)(a-b+c)(a+b-c)}}.

High school only

Proof 1: Substitute Heron’s area formula

The diameter of the circumcircle can be expressed as

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}{\text{diameter}}&{}={\frac {abc}{2\cdot {\text{area}}}}={\frac {|AB||BC||CA|}{2|\Delta ABC|}}\\[5pt]&{}={\frac {abc}{2{\sqrt {s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}}}}\\[5pt]&{}={\frac {2abc}{\sqrt {(a+b+c)(-a+b+c)(a-b+c)(a+b-c)}}}\end{aligned}}}

Here \Delta ABC denotes the triangle, and |\Delta ABC| its area; a, b, c are the lengths of the sides of the triangle and {\displaystyle s={\tfrac {a+b+c}{2}}} is the semiperimeter. The expression {\displaystyle \scriptstyle {\sqrt {s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}}} above is the area of the triangle, by Heron's formula.
