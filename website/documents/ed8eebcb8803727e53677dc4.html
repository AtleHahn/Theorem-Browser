Statement

For d\ge1 and locally integrable f:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb C, define Mf(x)=\sup_{r>0}|B(x,r)|^{-1}\int_{B(x,r)}|f(y)|dy, where B(x,r) is the ball centered at x of radius r and |\cdot| denotes Lebesgue measure. For 1<p\le\infty and f\in L^p(\mathbb R^d), \|Mf\|_p\le C_{p,d}\|f\|_p. For finite p, one may take C_{p,d}=(2^p5^dp/(p-1))^{1/p}, and for p=\infty one may take C_{\infty,d}=1.

Proof 1: Truncation and distribution-function integration

For p=\infty, every average is bounded by the essential supremum. For finite p>1, use the weak bound: for f\in L^1(\mathbb R^d) and t>0, |\{Mf>t\}|\le5^d\|f\|_1/t.

The Lp bounds for p > 1 can be deduced from the weak {\displaystyle L^{1}} bound by the Marcinkiewicz interpolation theorem. Here is how the argument goes in this particular case. Define the function {\displaystyle f_{t}\colon \mathbb {R} ^{d}\to \mathbb {C} } by {\displaystyle f_{t}(x)=f(x)} if {\displaystyle |f(x)|>t/2} and 0 otherwise. We have then

{\displaystyle |f(x)|\leq |f_{t}(x)|+{\frac {t}{2}}}

and, by the definition of maximal function {\displaystyle Mf}

{\displaystyle Mf(x)\leq Mf_{t}(x)+{\frac {t}{2}}}

By the weak-type estimate applied to {\displaystyle f_{t}}, we have:

{\displaystyle |\{Mf>t\}|\leq |\{Mf_{t}>t/2\}|\leq {\frac {2\cdot 5^{d}}{t}}\int _{\mathbb {R} ^{d}}|f_{t}(x)|dx={\frac {2\cdot 5^{d}}{t}}\int _{|f(x)|>{\frac {t}{2}}}|f(x)|dx.}

Then

{\displaystyle \|Mf\|_{p}^{p}=\int _{\mathbb {R} ^{d}}\vert Mf(x)\vert ^{p}dx=\int _{\mathbb {R} ^{d}}\int _{0}^{Mf(x)}pt^{p-1}dtdx=p\int _{0}^{\infty }t^{p-1}|\{Mf>t\}|dt}

By the estimate above we have:

{\displaystyle \|Mf\|_{p}^{p}\leq p\int _{0}^{\infty }t^{p-1}\left({2\cdot 5^{d} \over t}\int _{|f(x)|>{\frac {t}{2}}}|f(x)|dx\right)dt=2\cdot 5^{d}p\int _{\mathbb {R} ^{d}}\int _{0}^{2|f(x)|}t^{p-2}dt|f(x)|dx={\frac {2^{p}\cdot 5^{d}p}{p-1}}\|f\|_{p}^{p}.}
