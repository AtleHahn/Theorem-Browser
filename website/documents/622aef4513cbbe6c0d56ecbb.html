Averaging independent Cauchy observations does not concentrate around any constant. Repetition alone therefore does not guarantee stable empirical averages; moment assumptions in laws of large numbers have substantive force.

Statement

Let X_1,X_2,\ldots be iid standard Cauchy random variables. Then their sample means do not converge in probability to any real constant.

Proof 1: An unchanged nondegenerate distribution excludes a constant limit

For the standard Cauchy distribution, the median is zero, but the expected value does not exist, and indeed the average of n such variables have the same distribution as one such variable. It does not converge in probability toward zero (or any other value) as n goes to infinity.

Level justification

The imported stability assertion makes each sample mean standard Cauchy. Its probability outside a fixed finite neighborhood of any proposed constant is positive and independent of n, contradicting convergence in probability. Proving stability is unnecessary, but understanding the Cauchy law, iid sampling and convergence in probability is required for the complete statement and application.
