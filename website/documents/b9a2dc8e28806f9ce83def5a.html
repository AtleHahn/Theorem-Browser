Statement

For a positive integer n, let \varphi(n) count the integers 1\le k\le n with \gcd(k,n)=1. For every positive integer n, \sum_{d\mid n}\varphi(d)=n, where d ranges over the positive divisors of n.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement involves only a finite sum of explicitly defined integer counts.

Proof 1: Partition a cyclic group by its generated subgroups

One proof is to note that φ(d) is also equal to the number of possible generators of the cyclic group Cd ; specifically, if Cd = ⟨g⟩ of order d, then gk is a generator for every k coprime to d. A cyclic group of order n has exactly one subgroup of order d for each divisor d of n. Since every element of Cn generates a cyclic subgroup, and each subgroup Cd ⊆ Cn is generated by precisely φ(d) elements of Cn, the formula follows.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Partition elements of a finite cyclic group by the subgroup they generate and count its generators. Introductory group theory suffices.

Proof 2: Reduce the finite fractions and group by denominator — outline

The formula can also be derived from elementary arithmetic. For example, let n = 20 and consider the positive fractions up to 1 with denominator 20:

Put them into lowest terms:

{\displaystyle {\tfrac {1}{20}},\,{\tfrac {1}{10}},\,{\tfrac {3}{20}},\,{\tfrac {1}{5}},\,{\tfrac {1}{4}},\,{\tfrac {3}{10}},\,{\tfrac {7}{20}},\,{\tfrac {2}{5}},\,{\tfrac {9}{20}},\,{\tfrac {1}{2}},\,{\tfrac {11}{20}},\,{\tfrac {3}{5}},\,{\tfrac {13}{20}},\,{\tfrac {7}{10}},\,{\tfrac {3}{4}},\,{\tfrac {4}{5}},\,{\tfrac {17}{20}},\,{\tfrac {9}{10}},\,{\tfrac {19}{20}},\,{\tfrac {1}{1}}}

These twenty fractions are all the positive reduced fractions k/d\le1 whose denominators are the divisors d = 1, 2, 4, 5, 10, 20. The fractions with 20 as denominator are those with numerators relatively prime to 20, namely ⁠1/20⁠, ⁠3/20⁠, ⁠7/20⁠, ⁠9/20⁠, ⁠11/20⁠, ⁠13/20⁠, ⁠17/20⁠, ⁠19/20⁠; by definition this is φ(20) fractions. Similarly, there are φ(10) fractions with denominator 10, and φ(5) fractions with denominator 5, etc. Thus the set of twenty fractions is split into subsets of size φ(d) for each d dividing 20. A similar argument applies for any n.
