Statement

Let T be a classical theory with standard consistency sentence \operatorname{Con}(T) and provability predicate \operatorname{Prov}_T. Suppose a sentence G satisfies T\nvdash G, T\vdash G\leftrightarrow\neg\operatorname{Prov}_T(\ulcorner G\urcorner), and T\vdash\operatorname{Con}(T)\to\neg\operatorname{Prov}_T(\ulcorner G\urcorner). Then T\nvdash\operatorname{Con}(T). Here T\vdash A denotes provability and \ulcorner A\urcorner the natural-number code of A.

Proof 1: A formalized consistency implication obstructs self-consistency — source argument

If T proved its consistency, modus ponens with the assumed formalized implication would give T\vdash\neg\operatorname{Prov}_T(\ulcorner G\urcorner). The fixed-point equivalence would then yield T\vdash G, contrary to the assumed unprovability. Therefore T cannot prove its consistency.
