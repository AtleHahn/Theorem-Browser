Statement

Let P>0, s\in L^2([0,P]), S[n]=P^{-1}\int_0^P s(x)e^{-2\pi inx/P}\,dx and s_N(x)=\sum_{|n|\le N}S[n]e^{2\pi inx/P} for an integer N\ge0. With \|g\|_2=(P^{-1}\int_0^P|g|^2)^{1/2}, every trigonometric polynomial p_N(x)=\sum_{|n|\le N}p[n]e^{2\pi inx/P} different from s_N satisfies \|s_N-s\|_2<\|p_N-s\|_2.

Proof 1: Minimize the coefficient error using Parseval — outline

The partial sum

s_N(x)=\sum_{n=-N}^{N}S[n]e^{i2\pi nx/P}

is a trigonometric polynomial of degree at most N. A general such polynomial can be expressed as

p_N(x)=\sum_{n=-N}^{N}p[n]e^{i2\pi nx/P}.

For g\in L^2([0,P]), write \widehat g[n]=P^{-1}\int_0^P g(x)e^{-2\pi inx/P}\,dx. Parseval's theorem,

\frac1P\int_0^P|g(x)|^2\,dx=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}|\widehat g[n]|^2,

implies that s_N is the unique best trigonometric polynomial of degree at most N approximating s(x), in the sense stated. The source leaves this implication unexpanded.

Level justification

Apply Parseval to the L2 error and minimize the retained coefficient squares. The minimization is elementary but the argument explicitly uses arbitrary Lebesgue L2 functions and the infinite Fourier Parseval identity.
