Statement

Let 0\le p\le1 and let X take value 1 with probability p and value 0 with probability 1-p. For t\in\mathbb R,

\mathbb E e^{tX}=(1-p)+pe^t\le\exp(p(e^t-1)),

where the expectation is the finite weighted sum (1-p)e^0+pe^t.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Expectation is explicitly defined as a two-term weighted sum, with no general probability machinery.

Proof 1: Sum the two possible values

The two possible values give

\mathbb E e^{tX}=(1-p)e^0+pe^t=1+p(e^t-1)\le e^{p(e^t-1)},

using 1+u\le e^u for real u.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Evaluate the explicitly defined two-term weighted sum, rewrite it as 1+u with u=p(exp(t)-1), and apply the supplied scalar exponential inequality. Only elementary exponentials, algebra and finite probability are used; differentiating to prove the imported inequality is not required.
