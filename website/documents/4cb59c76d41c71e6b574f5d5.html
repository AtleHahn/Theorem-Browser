Statement

The nonnegative numbers that are both of the form 2^b-1 for an integer b\ge0 and triangular, of the form y(y+1)/2 for an integer y\ge0, are exactly 0,1,3,15,4095.

Proof 1: Transfer the known solution list through the square substitution

We use the known classification of the Ramanujan–Nagell equation 2^n-7=x^2: its positive integer solutions have n=3,4,5,7,15, corresponding to x=1,3,5,11,181. This classification is not proved here.

The problem of finding all numbers of the form 2^b-1 (Mersenne numbers) which are triangular is equivalent:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}&\ 2^{b}-1={\frac {y(y+1)}{2}}\\[2pt]\Longleftrightarrow &\ 8(2^{b}-1)=4y(y+1)\\\Longleftrightarrow &\ 2^{b+3}-8=4y^{2}+4y\\\Longleftrightarrow &\ 2^{b+3}-7=4y^{2}+4y+1\\\Longleftrightarrow &\ 2^{b+3}-7=(2y+1)^{2}\end{aligned}}}

The values of b are just those of n − 3, and the corresponding triangular Mersenne numbers (also known as Ramanujan–Nagell numbers) are:

{\displaystyle {\frac {y(y+1)}{2}}={\frac {(x-1)(x+1)}{8}}}

for x = 1, 3, 5, 11 and 181, giving 0, 1, 3, 15, 4095 and no more (sequence A076046 in the OEIS).
