Statement

Every red-blue coloring of the edges of a complete graph on six vertices has at least two monochromatic triangles. A triangle is monochromatic when its three edges have the same color.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Finite graph terminology is defined by pairs of vertices; the statements require only finite counting and elementary logic.

Proof 1: Count mixed-color triples

Count the number of ordered triples of vertices, x, y, z, such that the edge, (xy), is red and the edge, (yz), is blue. Firstly, any given vertex will be the middle of either 0 × 5 = 0 (all edges from the vertex are the same colour), 1 × 4 = 4 (four are the same colour, one is the other colour), or 2 × 3 = 6 (three are the same colour, two are the other colour) such triples. Therefore, there are at most 6 × 6 = 36 such triples. Secondly, for any non-monochromatic triangle (xyz), there exist precisely two such triples. Therefore, there are at most 18 non-monochromatic triangles. Therefore, at least 2 of the 20 triangles in the K6 are monochromatic.

Level justification

Count red-blue wedges by their middle vertex and then by their nonmonochromatic triangle, subtracting from twenty. Elementary finite counting and multiplication suffice.
