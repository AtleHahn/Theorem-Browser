Canonical choice from subsets of the natural numbers

Choosing from arbitrarily many nonempty subsets of the natural numbers needs no axiom of choice because taking the least element supplies a uniform rule. This distinguishes explicit selection procedures from the unrestricted nonconstructive selection asserted by choice.

Statement

In ZF, every set X of nonempty subsets of \mathbb N has a choice function, namely f(A)=\min A for A\in X.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Undergraduate sets, functions, orders and infinite sequences suffice. Choice, well-ordering or maximal-chain principles are explicit set-selection/order assertions where invoked, not a demand to learn their independence proofs. The displayed quantifiers and constructions can be understood through familiar subsets and functions.

Proof 1: Select the least natural number

If each member of the collection ⁠{\displaystyle X}⁠ is a nonempty subset of the natural numbers, then an explicit rule is to choose the smallest element of each ⁠{\displaystyle A\in X}⁠. The canonical choice function that maps each ⁠{\displaystyle A\in X}⁠ to its smallest element can be constructed in ZF without the axiom of choice.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Every nonempty subset has a unique least natural number, so the explicit assignment defines a function on the whole family. Introductory sets and functions suffice; invoking ZF here does not require a model-theoretic argument or arbitrary choices.
