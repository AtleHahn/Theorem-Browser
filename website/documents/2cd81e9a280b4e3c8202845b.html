Statement

Let p(z)=\sum_{j=0}^n a_jz^j\in\mathbb C[z] have degree n\ge1. Suppose there is \omega\in\mathbb C with |\omega|=1 such that p=\omega p^*, where p^*(z)=\sum_{j=0}^n\overline{a_{n-j}}z^j. Then p and q^*(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(n-j)\overline{a_{n-j}}z^j have the same number of zeros in \{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}, counted with multiplicity. Thus q^* is the conjugate-reciprocal polynomial of q=p^{\prime}. Bars denote complex conjugation.

Proof 1: Rouché comparison with boundary factors (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Ancochea §§2–4, pp.900–902; repaired scaling polynomial and boundary-factor detail given below.).

For clarity, the scaled-polynomial and boundary-factor steps are written explicitly here. Write A=np-zp′=omega q*. For 0<t<1 close to one, p(tz) has exactly k interior roots. Self-inversiveness gives |p(tz)|=t^n|p(z/t)| on the circle, so H_t=p(tz)-t^(2n)p(z/t) also has k interior roots. H_t/(1-t) tends to 2A, hence A has at most k interior roots by zero continuity. Conversely |A|=|zp′| on the circle. Divide both by their common unit-circle-zero factor D; then Rouche shows A+t zp′ has the same number of interior zeros as A. As t tends to one this polynomial tends to np, giving the reverse inequality. D has only boundary zeros and does not affect interior counts. In each limit, zeros already strictly inside the disk persist, giving the needed lower-semicontinuity inequality; boundary zeros need not persist inside.
