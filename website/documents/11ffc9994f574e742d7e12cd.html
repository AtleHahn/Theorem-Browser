Statement

For integers n\ge1 and k\ge0, let V_k be the homogeneous degree-k complex polynomials in x_1,\ldots,x_n, with \mathfrak{gl}_n action E_{ij}=x_i\partial/\partial x_j. The vector x_1^k is a highest-weight vector of weight (k,0,\ldots,0): E_{ij}x_1^k=0 for i<j and E_{ii}x_1^k=k\delta_{i1}x_1^k. Here the term highest-weight vector with this weight denotes exactly these annihilation and eigenvalue conditions for the specified operators.

Proof 1: Differentiate the pure first-coordinate monomial

Use E_{ij}=x_i\partial/\partial x_j on homogeneous polynomials.

One can also easily identify the highest weight structure of these representations. The monomial {\displaystyle x_{1}^{k}} is a highest weight vector, indeed: {\displaystyle E_{ij}x_{1}^{k}=0} for i < j. Its highest weight equals to (k, 0, ... ,0), indeed: {\displaystyle E_{ii}x_{1}^{k}=k\delta _{i1}x_{1}^{k}}.
