Statement

A Pythagorean triple means positive integers (a,b,c) with a^2+b^2=c^2; it is primitive if \gcd(a,b,c)=1. Put s=(a+b+c)/2. The integers (u,v,w,z)=(s,s-a,s-b,-(s-c)) satisfy (u+v+w+z)^2=2(u^2+v^2+w^2+z^2).

Proof 1: Source calculation — outline

For a right triangle of area K=ab/2 and semiperimeter s, the inradius and exradii are r=s-c, r_a=s-b, r_b=s-a, r_c=s, and

K=s(s-c)=(s-a)(s-b).

These radii are positive integers for a Pythagorean triple; integrality follows from primitive-triple parity and integer scaling.

The incircle and excircles of a right triangle may be translated to produce four mutually tangent circles, which is the configuration treated by Descartes' theorem. To obtain this configuration, the centers of the incircle and of the excircles tangent to the sides of lengths {\displaystyle a} and {\displaystyle b} move to the triangle vertices, {\displaystyle (0,0),} {\displaystyle (a,0),} and {\displaystyle (0,b),} while the excircle of the hypotenuse, of radius {\displaystyle s,} becomes the outer Soddy circle, with center at {\displaystyle (a,b).} Descartes' theorem states that the bends of the four circles,

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}k&={\frac {1}{r}}={\frac {1}{s-c}},&k_{a}&={\frac {1}{r_{a}}}={\frac {1}{s-b}},\\k_{b}&={\frac {1}{r_{b}}}={\frac {1}{s-a}},&k_{c}&=-{\frac {1}{r_{c}}}=-{\frac {1}{s}},\end{aligned}}}

which are the curvatures of the circles, apart from a minus sign factor for the outer circle, satisfy the equation

{\displaystyle \left(k+k_{a}+k_{b}+k_{c}\right)^{2}=2\left(k^{2}+k_{a}^{2}+k_{b}^{2}+k_{c}^{2}\right).}

Multiplying each of the bends by the area {\displaystyle K} gives an integer solution to this equation:

{\displaystyle K\left(k,k_{a},k_{b},k_{c}\right)=(s,s-a,s-b,-(s-c)).}
