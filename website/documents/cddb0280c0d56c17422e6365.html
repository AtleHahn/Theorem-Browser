Statement

Let A be an infinite subset of the nonnegative integers, and let s_A(n)=|\{(a,b)\in A^2:a+b\le n\}|, counting ordered pairs. For every real c>0, the error s_A(n)-cn is not bounded as n\to\infty.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The relevant statement concepts are Infinite sets of integers; Ordered-pair counts; Bounded asymptotic error. Counting pairs is elementary, but the assertion that the error is unbounded for every positive slope requires undergraduate understanding of asymptotic quantifiers. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Apply Erdős–Fuchs to the bounded-error case — outline

The Erdős–Fuchs theorem states that, for any given c>0, the relation

s_A(n)=cn+o\left(n^{1/4}\log(n)^{-1/2}\right)

cannot be satisfied. This is even stronger than the assertion that the relation

s_A(n)=cn+O(1)

could not hold: a bounded error is o(n^{1/4}\log(n)^{-1/2}).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The Erdős–Fuchs statement is supplied explicitly for the same pair-counting function. Understanding its use requires only the definitions of bounded error and little-o and the calculus fact that n^(1/4)/sqrt(log n) tends to infinity. Dividing a bounded error by that diverging scale gives zero, contradicting the supplied theorem. The proof of Erdős–Fuchs and any analytic-number-theory machinery behind it are not prerequisites.
