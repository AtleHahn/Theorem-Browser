Statement

Let H=\ell^2(\mathbb R) be the space of real sequences x=(x_j)_{j\ge1} with \sum_jx_j^2<\infty, with distance d(x,y)=(\sum_j(x_j-y_j)^2)^{1/2}. There is f:H\to H preserving every unit distance but not all distances.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Normed-vector geometry and convergent series are undergraduate concepts. The sequence space and distance are explicitly defined by the sum of squares; the claim only concerns distance preservation or an infimum to a subspace, with no spectral or dual structure.

Proof 1: Nearby countable images followed by an infinite simplex

Use separability of H: it contains a countable dense subset. The maps in the following construction have domain or range in H.

  • The first function maps every point of the Hilbert space onto a nearby point in a countable dense subset. As long as this transformation moves each point by a distance less than {\displaystyle {\tfrac {1}{2}}}, it will map points at unit distance from each other to distinct images.
  • The second function maps this dense set onto a countable unit simplex, an infinite set of points all at unit distance from each other. One example of a countable simplex in this space consists of the sequences of real numbers that take the value {\displaystyle 1/{\sqrt {2}}} in a single position and are zero everywhere else. There are infinitely many sequences of this form, and the distance between any two such sequences is one. This second function must be one-to-one but can otherwise be chosen arbitrarily.

When these two transformations are combined, they map any two points at unit distance from each other to two different points in the dense subset, and from there map them to two different points of the simplex, which are necessarily at unit distance apart. Therefore, their composition preserves unit distances. However, it is not an isometry, because it maps every pair of points, no matter their original distance, either to the same point or to a unit distance.
