The identity of a non-Hausdorff space has a nonclosed graph

Statement

If X is a non-Hausdorff topological space, the identity map \operatorname{id}_X:X\to X is continuous but its graph is not closed in X\times X.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Hausdorffness, product topology, metrizability and local Euclidean neighborhoods belong to basic topology or are direct definitions within it. No smooth-manifold structure is asserted.

Proof 1: Identify the graph with the diagonal

The graph of the identity is the diagonal \{(x,x):x\in X\}. A space has closed diagonal if and only if it is Hausdorff. Thus the identity, which is continuous, has nonclosed graph when X is not Hausdorff.

Level justification

Identify the graph with the diagonal and apply the closed-diagonal criterion. Standard undergraduate point-set topology suffices.
