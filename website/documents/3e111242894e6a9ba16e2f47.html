Symplectic transformations cannot squeeze a phase-space ball through a thinner cylinder in a conjugate coordinate plane, even when volume alone would allow it. This exposes constraints on classical Hamiltonian dynamics beyond conservation of total phase-space volume; it is a classical geometric result, not itself quantum uncertainty.

Statement

Fix an integer n\ge1 and real numbers r,R>0. In coordinates (x_1,\ldots,x_n,y_1,\ldots,y_n) on \mathbb R^{2n}, set

B(r)=\{z:\|z\|<r\},\qquad Z(R)=\{z:x_1^2+y_1^2<R^2\},\qquad J=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_n\\-I_n&0\end{pmatrix}.

If there is a smooth embedding f:B(r)\to Z(R) satisfying

Df(z)^T J Df(z)=J\quad\text{for every }z\in B(r),

then r\le R. A smooth embedding is an injective smooth map, with injective derivative, that is a homeomorphism onto its image. The matrix identity says that f preserves the standard symplectic form \sum_i dx_i\wedge dy_i.
