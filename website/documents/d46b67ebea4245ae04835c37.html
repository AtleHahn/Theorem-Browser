Statement

For integers k\ge1, n\ge0 and n times differentiable real functions f_1,\ldots,f_k on an interval,

\left(\prod_{i=1}^k f_i\right)^{(n)}=\sum_{j_1+\cdots+j_k=n}\frac{n!}{j_1!\cdots j_k!}\prod_{i=1}^k f_i^{(j_i)},

where all j_i are nonnegative integers and f_i^{(0)}=f_i.

Proof 1: Count differentiation choices by factor (outline)

Symbolic expansion of repeated differentiation according to the binomial theorem gives the general Leibniz rule for two factors. For the nth derivative of an arbitrary number of factors, one has a similar formula with multinomial coefficients:

\left(\prod_{i=1}^k f_i\right)^{(n)} =\sum_{j_1+\cdots+j_k=n} \binom{n}{j_1,j_2,\ldots,j_k}\prod_{i=1}^k f_i^{(j_i)}, \qquad \binom{n}{j_1,\ldots,j_k}=\frac{n!}{j_1!\cdots j_k!}.
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Repeatedly apply the product rule and count how many derivative choices hit each factor. Undergraduate calculus and multinomial counting suffice.
