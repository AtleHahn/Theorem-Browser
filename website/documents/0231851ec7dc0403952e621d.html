Statement

Let N\in\mathbb Z and f:[N,\infty)\to\mathbb R be nonincreasing. Then \sum_{n=N}^{\infty}f(n) converges to a finite real number if and only if I=\int_N^{\infty}f(x)\,dx is finite. When finite, I\leq\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}f(n)\leq f(N)+I.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves real series; monotone functions; improper Riemann integrals. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Integral test for convergence — proof

The proof uses the comparison test, comparing the term f(n) with the integral of f over the intervals [n-1,n) and [n,n+1) respectively. We use the standard fact that a monotone function is Riemann integrable on every compact subinterval.

Since f is a monotone decreasing function, we know that

{\displaystyle f(x)\leq f(n)\quad {\text{for all }}x\in [n,\infty )}

and

{\displaystyle f(n)\leq f(x)\quad {\text{for all }}x\in [N,n].}

Hence, for every integer n ≥ N,

{\displaystyle \int _{n}^{n+1}f(x)\,dx\leq \int _{n}^{n+1}f(n)\,dx=f(n)}\tag{2}

and, for every integer n ≥ N + 1,

{\displaystyle f(n)=\int _{n-1}^{n}f(n)\,dx\leq \int _{n-1}^{n}f(x)\,dx.}\tag{3}

By summation over all n from N to some larger integer M, we get from (2)

{\displaystyle \int _{N}^{M+1}f(x)\,dx=\sum _{n=N}^{M}\underbrace {\int _{n}^{n+1}f(x)\,dx} _{\leq \,f(n)}\leq \sum _{n=N}^{M}f(n)}

and from (3)

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\sum _{n=N}^{M}f(n)&=f(N)+\sum _{n=N+1}^{M}f(n)\\&\leq f(N)+\sum _{n=N+1}^{M}\underbrace {\int _{n-1}^{n}f(x)\,dx} _{\geq \,f(n)}\\&=f(N)+\int _{N}^{M}f(x)\,dx.\end{aligned}}}

Combining these two estimates yields

{\displaystyle \int _{N}^{M+1}f(x)\,dx\leq \sum _{n=N}^{M}f(n)\leq f(N)+\int _{N}^{M}f(x)\,dx.}

If f has a negative value, its decreasing tail is bounded above by a fixed negative number, so both the series and the improper integral diverge to -\infty. Otherwise f\geq0, and the partial sums and integrals are nondecreasing. The two estimates show that either is bounded precisely when the other is. Letting M tend to infinity, bounded monotone convergence gives the bounds in the statement and the result.
