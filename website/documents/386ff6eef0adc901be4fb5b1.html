Statement

For a positive integer n, let \varphi(n) count the integers 1\le k\le n with \gcd(k,n)=1. For positive integers m,n with \gcd(m,n)=1, one has \varphi(mn)=\varphi(m)\varphi(n).

Level
High school only
Level justification

All terms are locally defined finite integer counts.

Proof 1: Use the Chinese-remainder bijection of coprime residues — outline

If m,n>1, let A,B,C be the positive integers less than m,n,mn, respectively, and coprime to the corresponding bound. The Chinese remainder theorem gives a bijection A\times B\to C, so \varphi(m)\varphi(n)=\varphi(mn). If one modulus is 1, use \varphi(1)=1: the sole allowed integer is 1, with \gcd(1,1)=1.
