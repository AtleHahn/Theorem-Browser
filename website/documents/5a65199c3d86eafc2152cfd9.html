Statement

Let G be a finite simple undirected graph with n\ge2 vertices and edge set E. If its Laplacian eigenvalues are 0,\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_{n-1}, then \sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\lambda_i=2|E|. Here the Laplacian is L=D-A, where D is the diagonal vertex-degree matrix and A is the adjacency matrix.

Proof 1: The Laplacian eigenvalue sum counts twice the edges

The weighted forest theorem identifies the coefficient q_{n-1} of x^{n-1} in x\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(x+\lambda_i) with the sum of forest weights, where each weight is the product of component sizes. Here q_{n-1}=\sum_i\lambda_i. A spanning forest with n-1 components consists of exactly one edge, so each such forest has weight 2. There is one for each edge, giving q_{n-1}=2|E|.
