Constructing a Sylow subgroup by adjoining normalizing elements

Statement

Let G be a finite group, p a prime, and H_0 any subgroup of p-power order. While p divides [G:H_i], choose an element x_i\in N_G(H_i)\setminus H_i of p-power order and put H_{i+1}=\langle H_i,x_i\rangle. Such choices exist, every H_i is a p-subgroup, and the procedure terminates at a Sylow p-subgroup containing H_0. Here N_G(H)=\{g\in G:gHg^{-1}=H\}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory group/ring theory and linear algebra supply the objects. The extra Sylow, normalizer, invariant-polynomial or pseudoreflection condition is explicitly defined in subgroup, coordinate or polynomial terms; no specialist extension or representation theory is required.

Proof 1: Normalizer growth and finite adjoining — outline

Use the normalizer growth lemma: if H is a p-subgroup of a finite group G and p\mid[G:H], then p\mid[N_G(H):H]. Starting with H_0 (which may be the identity subgroup), successively adjoin elements of p-power order lying in its normalizer but outside it. This yields a generating system for a Sylow p-subgroup.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the normalizer index divisibility and Cauchy in its quotient, extract a p-power element and grow the p-subgroup until finite termination. Introductory finite group theory, quotients and element orders suffice.

Routine omissions

Existence of an eligible p-power element follows from Cauchy in N_G(H)/H and taking the p-primary part of a lift; these routine finite-group checks are not expanded in the source. Normality of H_i in the generated subgroup gives a p-group extension, and strict increase of subgroup orders forces termination. The terminal index is prime to p. No efficient search or complexity bound is supplied.
