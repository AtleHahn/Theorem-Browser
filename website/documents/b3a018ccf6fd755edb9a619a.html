Statement

Two runners start together on a circle, move continuously in opposite directions with strictly increasing unbounded distances traveled, and cross a fixed mark which is never a meeting point. At each mark crossing the runner reports the number of meetings so far, counting the initial meeting as number 1. Then every positive integer is reported exactly once between the two runners.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement uses explicitly described motion, integer-coordinate rays or finite polynomial products. Its quantities and conditions can be understood using elementary arithmetic, algebra and coordinate geometry. Proof 1: Count one mark crossing between consecutive meetings

Consider two athletes running in opposite directions, at varying speeds, around a circular racetrack, with the continuous strictly increasing unbounded distances in the statement. Each time one of the athletes crosses a fixed mark on the track, they report the number of times they have met the other athlete (counting the start of the race as a meeting). Because they cross paths a countable number of times, the mark can be placed at a point on the track where they never meet. Then, between each two consecutive times the athletes meet, they will together cover each point on the track once, so during this time exactly one of them will cross the mark and report their number of meetings. Therefore, the sets of numbers reported by each athlete form a partition of the positive integers.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Track the increasing continuous total distance between meetings, use one full relative circuit per meeting interval and the countability argument for choosing a nonmeeting mark. Undergraduate real analysis of continuous monotone functions and countable sets suffices; these infinite-motion and mark-existence details go beyond finite race arithmetic.
