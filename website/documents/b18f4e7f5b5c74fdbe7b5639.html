Statement

Let K be an algebraically closed field, n\ge1 an integer, and A\in K^{n\times n}. If v\in K^n\setminus\{0\} and Av=\lambda v, then \det(A-\lambda I)=0, where I is the identity matrix.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra and introductory abstract algebra supply determinants, matrices, field roots, ring operations and divisibility. The displayed assertion uses these directly; no graduate concepts are needed to understand it.

Proof 1: Use a nonzero null vector

The eigenvector equation gives (A-\lambda I)v=0 with v\ne0. Therefore \det(A-\lambda I)=0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The eigenvector is a nonzero null vector of A-lambda I, so that matrix is singular. Standard linear algebra over a field suffices; algebraic closure is not used in this implication.
