Simpler proof assessment

Likely alternative prerequisite level: Undergraduate level; lowest recorded proof level: Beyond undergraduate level. Form the real vector space of finite formal combinations of symbols e_x and define their bilinear form by K. Positivity gives Cauchy-Schwarz by the quadratic discriminant, so zero-norm vectors form the radical. Quotient by that subspace and take its metric completion to obtain a Hilbert space. The images of e_x give exactly K(x,y), including singular kernels and arbitrary sets X. Algebraic quotients and completion are a small undergraduate extension, without RKHS evaluation machinery.

Statement

Every symmetric positive semidefinite real-valued kernel K on a set X can be written K(x,y)=\langle\varphi(x),\varphi(y)\rangle_F for a real Hilbert space F and a map \varphi:X\to F.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Positive semidefiniteness of a kernel means every finite matrix of its values has nonnegative quadratic form, a direct linear-algebra definition. Hilbert means a complete inner-product space. The claim is just realization of the given pairwise values as inner products, without reproducing evaluations or duality.

Proof 1: Take kernel sections as features

Use the Moore–Aronszajn theorem to obtain the RKHS H of K. Set F=H and \varphi(x)=K_x. The reproducing identity gives \langle K_x,K_y\rangle=K(x,y), as required.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the Moore-Aronszajn reproducing-kernel Hilbert space and its kernel-section identity. The RKHS construction and evaluation representation are not defined locally and exceed a confidently small undergraduate extension; retain beyond undergraduate provisionally.
