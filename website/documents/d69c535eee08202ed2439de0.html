Statement

For continuous real functions f_0,\ldots,f_2 on an open interval I=(a,b), the identity \int_a^b\sum_{j=0}^{2}f_jh^{(j)}=0 for every smooth compactly supported h holds if and only if f_2\in C^1(I), v=f_1-f_2'\in C^1(I), and v'=f_0.

Proof 1: Eliminate the chain variables in nested order

Use the source-stated higher fundamental lemma: the variational identity is equivalent to existence of C^1 functions u_0,\ldots,u_{n-1} with f_0=u_0', f_j=u_{j-1}+u_j' for 1\le j<n, and f_n=u_{n-1}. For n=2, the chain is f_2=u_1, f_1=u_0+u_1', f_0=u_0'. Thus u_1=f_2 and u_0=f_1-f_2', giving the stated nested derivative condition. Conversely these choices give the chain.

In contrast to the first-order case, this does not lead to the relation f_0-f_1'+f_2''=0, since the function f_2=u_1 need not be differentiable twice. The sufficient condition f_0-f_1'+f_2''=0 is not necessary. Rather, the necessary and sufficient condition may be written as f_0-(f_1-f_2')'=0; the brackets cannot in general be opened because of non-differentiability.
