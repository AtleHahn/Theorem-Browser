Statement

Let P be outside a Euclidean circle, let PT be tangent at T, and let a secant ray from P meet the circle first at G_1 and then at G_2. Then |PT|^2=|PG_1|\,|PG_2|.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement uses ordinary Euclidean geometry.

Proof 1: Use the alternate segment angle and triangle similarity

Beginning with the alternate segment theorem,

\begin{array}{cl} &\angle PG_2T=\angle PTG_1\\[4pt] \implies&\triangle PTG_2\sim\triangle PG_1T\\[4pt] \implies&\dfrac{|PT|}{|PG_2|}=\dfrac{|PG_1|}{|PT|}\\[4pt] \implies&|PT|^2=|PG_1|\cdot|PG_2|. \end{array}

For the similarity, the triangles also share the angle at P.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Use the tangent-chord angle and the shared angle to obtain similar triangles, then multiply the length ratio. School Euclidean geometry suffices.
