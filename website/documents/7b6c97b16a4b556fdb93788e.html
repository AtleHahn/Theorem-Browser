Statement

Let U\subset\mathbb R^3 be a bounded domain with piecewise smooth boundary and outward unit normal n. Let \psi be C^2 on a neighborhood of \overline U and harmonic on U. Fix \eta\in\mathbb R^3\setminus\overline U, and put G(y,\eta)=-1/(4\pi\|y-\eta\|). With \partial_n= n\cdot\nabla_y, \int_{\partial U}\bigl(\psi(y)\partial_nG(y,\eta)-G(y,\eta)\partial_n\psi(y)\bigr)\,dS_y=0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Multivariable derivatives; Harmonic functions; Smooth Newton kernel away from its pole; Surface and volume integrals. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Use the exterior branch and harmonicity

Green’s second identity gives the boundary integral as the volume integral of \psi\Delta_yG-G\Delta\psi. The pole lies outside \overline U, so the Newton kernel G is smooth and harmonic on a neighborhood of \overline U. Since \Delta\psi=0 as well, both volume terms vanish. This is the source exterior zero branch, where its delta term has no support in U.
