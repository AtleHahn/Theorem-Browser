Statement

Let X be a smooth n-dimensional variety over a finite field k, and let \ell\ne\operatorname{char}(k) be prime. For each finite extension E/k, with geometric Frobenius F_E,

\#X(E)=\sum_{i=0}^{2n}(-1)^i\operatorname{tr}(F_E\mid H_c^i(X_{\bar k},\mathbb Q_\ell)).
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Smooth varieties over finite fields; Constructible ℓ-adic sheaves; Compactly supported étale cohomology; Geometric Frobenius. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Specialize to the constant sheaf

Use the Grothendieck sheaf trace identity

\sum_{x\in X(E)}\operatorname{tr}(F_E\mid\mathcal F_x)=\sum_{i=0}^{2n}(-1)^i\operatorname{tr}(F_E\mid H_c^i(X_{\bar k},\mathcal F))

For a constant sheaf {\displaystyle \mathbb {Q} _{\ell}} (viewed as {\displaystyle (\varprojlim \mathbb {Z} /\ell^{n}\mathbb {Z} )\otimes \mathbb {Q} } to qualify as an \ell-adic sheaf) the left hand side of this formula is the number of E-points of X.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Specialize the sheaf trace formula to the constant ell-adic sheaf, whose stalk trace is one. Etale cohomology, geometric Frobenius and ell-adic sheaves require graduate algebraic geometry.
