Statement

Let G be a loopless graph, with an arbitrary possibly uncountable vertex set. If every finite subgraph of G is planar, then G has a proper vertex coloring with four colors.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Graphs, finite subset colorings and infinite sequences are elementary discrete mathematics. Matchings are disjoint edges and a ray is an infinite nonrepeating path; the conditions are explicitly given. No graph-minor or set-theoretic independence machinery is needed.

Proof 1: Apply the finite-character coloring theorem

Every finite subgraph of G has a four-coloring by the finite four-color theorem. Apply the de Bruijn–Erdős theorem: if every finite subgraph of a graph is k-colorable for a fixed finite k, then the entire graph is k-colorable. Taking k=4 gives the conclusion.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the finite four-color theorem to each finite subgraph and then the supplied finite-character coloring theorem. Undergraduate sets and graphs suffice for arbitrary vertex sets; no logical compactness proof is required.
