Statement

Let X be a real or complex normed space, I\ne\varnothing, x_i\in X and c_i\in\mathbb K. There is a bounded linear f:X\to\mathbb K with f(x_i)=c_i for every i if and only if there exists K>0 such that |\sum_i s_ic_i|\le K\|\sum_i s_ix_i\| for every finitely supported scalar family (s_i).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra supplies linear functionals and bilinear forms; real analysis supplies norm bounds and completeness. The interpolation or variational equations are explicit. These direct normed/Hilbert-space extensions require no dual-space topology or PDE background.

Proof 1: Criterion for bounded linear interpolation of prescribed values

The Hahn–Banach Theorem implies the above statement. In order to see it, simply observe that the existence of the continuous linear functional {\displaystyle f:X\longrightarrow {\mathbb {K} }}, where {\displaystyle {\mathbb {K} }} is either {\displaystyle {\mathbb {R} }} or {\displaystyle {\mathbb {C} }}, implies the thesis, simply taking as {\displaystyle K} any positive upper bound for the (finite) norm of {\displaystyle f}, which is a non-negative real number. For the converse, let {\displaystyle M} be the vector subspace of {\displaystyle X} generated by the family {\displaystyle \left(x_{i}\right)_{i\in I}}. Then, the inequality implies that there is a well-defined linear functional {\displaystyle g:M\longrightarrow {\mathbb {K} }}, such that {\displaystyle g\left(x_{i}\right)=c_{i}}, for all {\displaystyle i\in I}. Moreover, the inequality, as stated, also implies that this linear functional {\displaystyle g} is also continuous and its norm {\displaystyle \vert \vert g\vert \vert } is bounded by {\displaystyle K} as continuous linear operator. Anyway, taking {\displaystyle p:X\longrightarrow {\mathbb {R} }}, defined as {\displaystyle p(v):=K\vert \vert v\vert \vert }, we immediately conclude that {\displaystyle p} is a semi-norm and that {\displaystyle \vert g(w)\vert \leq p(w)}, for all {\displaystyle w\in M}. Hence, Hahn-Banach Theorem in its real or complex seminorm form implies the existence of {\displaystyle f:X\longrightarrow {\mathbb {K} }} extending {\displaystyle g}, having norm, as linear operator, bounded above by {\displaystyle K} and, hence, continuous. As {\displaystyle f} extends g, then {\displaystyle f} satisfies the first claim. In other words, Hahn-Banach implies this theorem as stated.
