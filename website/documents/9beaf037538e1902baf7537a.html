Statement

Every continuous complex-linear functional A on the complex continuous functions C([0,1]) with the supremum norm has the form A(f)=\int_0^1 f\,d\alpha for a complex-valued function \alpha of bounded variation. No uniqueness of the unnormalized integrator \alpha is asserted.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The theorem concerns functional-analytic duality and Stieltjes integration beyond the baseline calculus.

Proof 1: Specialize to the interval and use Stieltjes correspondence

Apply the regular-measure representation theorem on the compact interval: A(f)=\int_{[0,1]}f\,d\mu for a finite regular complex Borel measure \mu. Use the interval measure–Stieltjes correspondence to represent this integration functional by a bounded-variation integrator \alpha. For continuous f, its Lebesgue–Stieltjes integral agrees with the Riemann–Stieltjes integral, giving the stated expression.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Represent the functional by a regular complex Borel measure and convert it to a bounded-variation Stieltjes integrator. Complex measures, functional representation and Stieltjes correspondences require advanced analysis.
