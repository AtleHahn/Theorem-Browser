Statement

Let D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}, and let f_n:D\to\mathbb C be injective holomorphic functions with f_n(0)=0 and positive real f_n'(0). Put U_n=f_n(D). For any sequence of open sets containing zero, define its kernel as the union of the components containing zero of the interiors of its tail intersections; a component is empty if zero is not in that interior, and an empty union is replaced by \{0\}. Convergence to a kernel means that every subsequence has that same kernel. Suppose (U_n) converges to a kernel U\ne\mathbb C. Then \sup_n f_n'(0)<\infty, and (f_n) is uniformly bounded on each compact subset of D.

Proof 1: Proper kernel convergence bounds normalized univalent maps — outline

By Koebe's quarter theorem, U_n contains the disk about zero of radius f_n'(0)/4. Kernel convergence to a proper subset of \mathbb C implies that these radii are uniformly bounded. Koebe's distortion theorem gives

|f_n(z)|\le f_n'(0)\frac{|z|}{(1-|z|)^2}.

Hence (f_n) is uniformly bounded on compact subsets of D.

Level justification

Use Koebe disks to bound derivatives under the specified subsequence-stable kernel convergence and apply distortion on compact sets. Kernel convergence through interiors, components and every subsequence is a substantial specialized addition to complex analysis; its cumulative burden is not confidently small, so retain beyond undergraduate provisionally.
