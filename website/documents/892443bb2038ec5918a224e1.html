Statement

Let S be a topological space and (C_k)_{k\ge0} a sequence of nonempty compact subsets closed in S, with C_{k+1}\subseteq C_k for every k\ge0. Then \bigcap_{k\ge0}C_k\ne\varnothing.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Basic point-set topology and real analysis supply compactness, closed sets, continuity, metrics and completeness. The Cantor-set construction or cofinite topology, where present, is a direct set-based example learnable briefly. The complete assertion needs no algebraic or differential topology.

Proof 1: Extract a finite subcover from the complements

Assume, by way of contradiction, that {\displaystyle {\textstyle \bigcap _{k=0}^{\infty }C_{k}}=\emptyset }. For each {\displaystyle k}, let {\displaystyle U_{k}=C_{0}\setminus C_{k}}. Since {\displaystyle {\textstyle \bigcup _{k=0}^{\infty }U_{k}}=C_{0}\setminus {\textstyle \bigcap _{k=0}^{\infty }C_{k}}} and {\displaystyle {\textstyle \bigcap _{k=0}^{\infty }C_{k}}=\emptyset }, we have {\displaystyle {\textstyle \bigcup _{k=0}^{\infty }U_{k}}=C_{0}}. Since the {\displaystyle C_{k}} are closed relative to {\displaystyle S} and therefore, also closed relative to {\displaystyle C_{0}}, the {\displaystyle U_{k}}, their set complements in {\displaystyle C_{0}}, are open relative to {\displaystyle C_{0}}.

Since {\displaystyle C_{0}\subseteq S} is compact and {\displaystyle \{U_{k}\vert k\geq 0\}} is an open cover (on {\displaystyle C_{0}}) of {\displaystyle C_{0}}, a finite cover {\displaystyle \{U_{k_{1}},U_{k_{2}},\ldots ,U_{k_{m}}\}} can be extracted. Let {\displaystyle M=\max _{1\leq i\leq m}{k_{i}}}. Then {\displaystyle {\textstyle \bigcup _{i=1}^{m}U_{k_{i}}}=U_{M}} because {\displaystyle U_{0}\subseteq U_{1}\subseteq U_{2}\subseteq \cdots \subseteq U_{n}\subseteq U_{n+1}\cdots }, by the nesting hypothesis for the collection {\displaystyle (C_{k})_{k\geq 0}}. Consequently, {\displaystyle C_{0}={\textstyle \bigcup _{i=1}^{m}U_{k_{i}}}=U_{M}}. But then {\displaystyle C_{M}=C_{0}\setminus U_{M}=\emptyset }, a contradiction.

Proof 2: Finite intersection property in the first compact set (outline)

Use the finite-intersection characterization of compactness: any family of closed subsets of a compact space whose every finite subfamily has nonempty intersection has nonempty total intersection. Regard the C_k as closed subsets of the compact subspace C_0. Each finite intersection of the nested C_k equals the member with largest index, and so is nonempty. The characterization gives \bigcap_{k\ge0}C_k\ne\varnothing.
