Statement

Let n,r be nonnegative integers and let R=\{0,1\}^r be the set of binary strings of length r, so |R|=2^r. Bitwise exclusive-or u\oplus t adds each pair of bits modulo 2. For A\subseteq R, write A\oplus t=\{u\oplus t:u\in A\}. Empty unions are empty and empty products equal 1; R has one empty string when r=0. Suppose |A|/|R|\ge1-2^{-n}. Choose t_1,\ldots,t_r independently and uniformly from R and put S=\bigcup_{i=1}^r(A\oplus t_i). Then \Pr[S\ne R]\le2^{r-nr}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Binary strings, exclusive-or, translations and the empty cases are explicitly defined. Uniform independent choices from a finite set and the chance that their union misses a string use finite counting and elementary probability. The bound 2^(r-nr) requires integer powers; the quantified family introduces no measure-theoretic probability or college-level construction.

Proof 1: Multiply missed-point probabilities and sum over the binary cube — outline

Randomly pick t_1,t_2,\ldots,t_r, independently and uniformly from R. Let S=\bigcup_i A\oplus t_i (the union of all transforms of A).

So, for all u in R,

\Pr[u\notin S]=\prod_{i=1}^r\Pr[u\notin A\oplus t_i]\le\frac{1}{2^{nr}}.

The probability that there will exist at least one element in R not in S is

\Pr\Bigl[\bigvee_{u\in R}(u\notin S)\Bigr]\le\sum_{u\in R}\frac{1}{2^{nr}}=\frac{2^r}{2^{nr}}=2^{r-nr}.
Level
High school only
Level justification

For a fixed string u, exclusive-or with u permutes the possible translations, so its miss probability is at most 2^{-n}. Independent choices multiply these bounds, and summing over 2^r strings bounds the probability of any miss. These are finite counting and elementary probability rules. With r=0 the empty union misses the unique empty string and both sides are one.
