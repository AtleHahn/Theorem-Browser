Statement

Let X=(X_1,\ldots,X_n) be a real random vector, with finite means \mu_i and variances \sigma_i^2, and assume \sum_i\sigma_i^2>0. Put \mu=(\mu_i) and s=(\sigma_i), where \sigma_i\ge0. For k>0, \Pr(\|X-\mu\|_2\ge k\|s\|_2)\le k^{-2}.

Proof 1: Rewrite sums of squares as norms

Use the Birnbaum–Raymond–Zuckerman inequality:

\Pr\left(\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\mu_i)^2\geq k^2\sum_{i=1}^n\sigma_i^2\right)\leq\frac1{k^2}.

This result can be rewritten in terms of vectors X=(X_1,\ldots,X_n) with mean \mu=(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_n), standard deviation s=(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_n), in the Euclidean norm:

\Pr(\|X-\mu\|_2\geq k\|s\|_2)\leq\frac1{k^2}.
