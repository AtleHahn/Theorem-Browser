Statement

Let F be a field in which -1 has no square root, and I an infinite set of positive odd integers. If every polynomial over F of degree in I has a root in F, then every odd-degree polynomial over F has a root in F.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory algebra supplies fields, polynomial roots and Gaussian integers. A Gaussian prime is an irreducible in Z[i], and associates differ by a unit. No field norms beyond the explicitly given conjugate product or extension arithmetic are needed.

Proof 1: Multiply by a power of the root-free quadratic

For f of odd degree d, choose d+2k\in I with k\ge0, possible because an infinite set of positive integers is unbounded. The polynomial (x^2+1)^kf(x) has a root by hypothesis. Since x^2+1 has no root in F, this is a root of f.

Raise the degree by multiplying by powers of the root-free quadratic, then use the no-zero-divisor property to recover a root of f. Undergraduate fields and polynomial degrees suffice.
