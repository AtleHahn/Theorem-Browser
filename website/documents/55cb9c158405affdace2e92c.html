Statement

Let \mu be standard Gaussian measure on \mathbb R^n, and let A be Lebesgue measurable with \mu(A)>0. With d(x,A)=\inf_{y\in A}\|x-y\|, \int_{\mathbb R^n}e^{d(x,A)^2/4}\,d\mu(x)\le\frac1{\mu(A)}.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The precise measurable-function and probability-density statements require measure theory.

Proof 1: Insert Gaussian weights and compare squared distances

Write \gamma(x)=(2\pi)^{-n/2}e^{-\|x\|^2/2}. In Prékopa–Leindler take \lambda=1/2 and

h(x)=\gamma(x),\quad f(x)=e^{d(x,A)^2/4}\gamma(x),\quad g(x)=1_A(x)\gamma(x).

It suffices to check the pointwise condition for y\in A; then d(x,A)\le\|x-y\|. Expanding these defined functions gives

(2\pi)^n f(x)g(y)=e^{d(x,A)^2/4-\|x\|^2/2-\|y\|^2/2}\le e^{\|x-y\|^2/4-\|x\|^2/2-\|y\|^2/2}=e^{-\|(x+y)/2\|^2}=(2\pi)^n h((x+y)/2)^2.

Since \int h=1 and \int g=\mu(A)>0, Prékopa–Leindler yields 1\ge(\int f\,\mu(A))^{1/2}. Rearranging gives the bound.
