Statement

Let f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R be bounded and 2\pi-periodic, with a finite partition of each period into intervals on which it is monotone. Write c_k=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(t)e^{-ikt}\,dt and S_nf(x)=\sum_{k=-n}^{n}c_ke^{ikx}. Then for every real x, S_nf(x)\to(f(x+)+f(x-))/2, where f(x+) and f(x-) are the right and left limits.

Proof 1: Pass from finite piecewise monotonicity to Jordan’s test — outline

A bounded function with finitely many monotonic intervals in a period is of bounded variation on that period. Apply the Dirichlet–Jordan theorem: for an integrable periodic function of bounded variation, its Fourier partial sums converge at each point to the arithmetic mean of its one-sided limits.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Bounded variation is the small additional definition beyond introductory real analysis: total sums of absolute increments are uniformly bounded. Finite monotone pieces give this directly, and the supplied convergence theorem is expressed in explicit Fourier integrals and one-sided limits. No proof of the Fourier convergence theorem is required.
