Statement

Let R be a ring of subsets of X (containing \varnothing and closed under finite unions and differences), and let \mu:R\to[0,\infty] satisfy \mu(\varnothing)=0 and countable additivity whenever the disjoint union remains in R. Then \mu extends to a measure on the sigma-algebra \sigma(R) generated by R. If X is a countable union of sets in R of finite \mu-measure, the extension is unique and sigma-finite.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The assertion concerns premeasure extension and uniqueness on a generated sigma-algebra, requiring measure theory; it does not require the proof’s outer-measure construction or transfinite induction.

Proof 1: Carathéodory extension of a premeasure — outline

First extend {\displaystyle \mu } to an outer measure {\displaystyle \mu ^{*}} on the power set {\displaystyle 2^{X}} of {\displaystyle X} by

{\displaystyle \mu ^{*}(T)=\inf \left\{\sum _{n}\mu \left(S_{n}\right):T\subseteq \cup _{n}S_{n}{\text{ with }}S_{1},S_{2},\ldots \in R\right\}}

with the infimum of an empty collection taken as infinity, and then restrict it to the set {\displaystyle {\mathcal {B}}} of {\displaystyle \mu ^{*}}-measurable sets (that is, Carathéodory-measurable sets), which is the set of all {\displaystyle M\subseteq X} such that {\displaystyle \mu ^{*}(S)=\mu ^{*}(S\cap M)+\mu ^{*}(S\cap M^{\mathrm {c} })} where M^{\mathrm c}=X\setminus M, for every {\displaystyle S\subseteq X.} {\displaystyle {\mathcal {B}}} is a {\displaystyle \sigma }-algebra, and {\displaystyle \mu ^{*}} is {\displaystyle \sigma }-additive on it, by the Caratheodory lemma.

It remains to check that {\displaystyle {\mathcal {B}}} contains {\displaystyle R.} That is, to verify that every set in {\displaystyle R} is {\displaystyle \mu ^{*}}-measurable. This is done by basic measure theory techniques of dividing and adding up sets.

Restricting \mu^* to \sigma(R) gives the required extension. Under the stated finite-measure covering hypothesis, the extension is sigma-finite.

For uniqueness, take any other extension {\displaystyle \nu } so it remains to show that {\displaystyle \nu =\mu ^{*}.} By {\displaystyle \sigma }-additivity, uniqueness can be reduced to the finite-total-mass case, which will now be assumed.

Now we could concretely prove {\displaystyle \nu =\mu ^{*}} on {\displaystyle \sigma (R)} by using the Borel hierarchy of {\displaystyle R,} and since {\displaystyle \nu =\mu ^{*}} at the base level, we can use well-ordered induction to reach all the countable ordinal stages below {\displaystyle \omega _{1},} whose union generates {\displaystyle \sigma (R).}
