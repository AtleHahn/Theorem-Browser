Statement

For s\in\mathbb C and real |x|<1, (1+x)^{-s}=\sum_{k=0}^\infty\binom{s+k-1}{k}(-1)^kx^k. Powers of the positive real base use its real logarithm, and \binom rk=r(r-1)\cdots(r-k+1)/k! with \binom r0=1.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex exponentials and complex differentiability extend familiar definitions, while all generalized coefficients are supplied by formulas. No abstract analytic continuation or Riemann surfaces are needed.

Proof 1: Substitute the negative exponent

Use the generalized binomial series (1+x)^r=\sum_{k\ge0}\binom rk x^k for complex r and real |x|<1.

With r = −s, we have for |x| < 1:

{\displaystyle {\frac {1}{(1+x)^{s}}}=\sum _{k=0}^{\infty }{\binom {-s}{k}}x^{k}=\sum _{k=0}^{\infty }{\binom {s+k-1}{k}}(-1)^{k}x^{k}.}
