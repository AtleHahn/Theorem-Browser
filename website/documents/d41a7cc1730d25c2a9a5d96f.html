Statement

There is an absolute constant C>0 such that, for every real sequence (s_j)_{j\geq1} and positive integers m,n, the discrepancy

D(m)=\sup_{0\leq a\leq b\leq1}\left|\frac1m\#\{1\leq j\leq m:a\leq\{s_j\}\leq b\}-(b-a)\right|

satisfies

D(m)\leq C\left(\frac1n+\frac1m\sum_{k=1}^n\frac1k\left|\sum_{j=1}^m e^{2\pi i k s_j}\right|\right).

Here \{s\}\in[0,1) is the fractional part of s, i^2=-1, and \# denotes the finite number of elements.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves finite interval counts and fractional parts; suprema over real intervals; finite complex exponential sums. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Apply the circle inequality to the empirical measure — outline

Use the probability measure on \mathbb R/\mathbb Z given by

\mu_m(S)=\frac1m\#\{1\leq j\leq m:\{s_j\}\in S\}.

For a probability measure \mu on this circle, the Erdős–Turán inequality states

\sup_A|\mu(A)-\operatorname{mes}(A)|\leq C\left(\frac1n+\sum_{k=1}^n\frac{|\widehat\mu(k)|}{k}\right),\qquad \widehat\mu(k)=\int e^{2\pi i k\theta}\,d\mu(\theta),

where A ranges over arcs and \operatorname{mes} is normalized Lebesgue measure. Applying this inequality to \mu_m yields the discrepancy bound in the statement.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The measure used is an explicitly finite empirical distribution, so its integrals are finite averages and arc measure is ordinary length. Applying the supplied circle inequality is a small finite-probability and Fourier-sum extension; no general measure construction is required.
