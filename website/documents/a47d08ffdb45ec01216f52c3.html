Statement

Let K be a field of characteristic different from 2 and 3. Let a,b,c,d\in K, with a\ne0, and set f(x)=ax^3+bx^2+cx+d. In an algebraic closure let r_1,r_2,r_3 be its roots, counted with multiplicity. Then a^4\prod_{i<j}(r_i-r_j)^2=18abcd-4b^3d+b^2c^2-4ac^3-27a^2d^2. Writing this common value as \Delta, it also equals [4(b^2-3ac)^3-(2b^3-9abc+27a^2d)^2]/(27a^2). For t^3+pt+q the corresponding value is -4p^3-27q^2.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory algebra supplies polynomial factorization, multiplicity and field characteristic. An algebraic closure only supplies all polynomial roots here, and a primitive cube root is a nonidentity root of X cubed minus one. The displayed finite formulas need no Galois or extension-degree theory.

Proof 1: Expand using Vieta and translate the roots — outline

Use Vieta’s formulas b=-a(r_1+r_2+r_3), c=a(r_1r_2+r_1r_3+r_2r_3), d=-ar_1r_2r_3. To prove the stated discriminant identities, one can use Vieta's formulas to express everything as polynomials in r1, r2, r3, and a. The remaining verification is equality of the two resulting finite polynomials.

Put p=(3ac-b^2)/(3a^2) and q=(2b^3-9abc+27a^2d)/(27a^3). The substitution x=t-b/(3a) gives f(t-b/(3a))=a(t^3+pt+q).

The general cubic discriminant is the product of {\displaystyle a^{4}} and the discriminant of the corresponding depressed cubic. Using the formula relating the general cubic and the associated depressed cubic, this implies that the discriminant of the general cubic can be written as

{\displaystyle {\frac {4(b^{2}-3ac)^{3}-(2b^{3}-9abc+27a^{2}d)^{2}}{27a^{2}}}.}
