Statement

Let A,B be Hermitian n\times n matrices and let 1\le p\le\infty. List each eigenvalue vector in descending order. For a Hermitian matrix C, set \|C\|_{S^r}=(\sum_i|\lambda_i(C)|^r)^{1/r} for 1\le r<\infty, and \|C\|_{S^\infty}=\max_i|\lambda_i(C)|. For real vectors the corresponding \ell^r norms have the same formulas. Then \|\lambda(A+B)-\lambda(A)\|_{\ell^p}\le\|B\|_{S^p}.

Proof 1: Selected-coordinate majorization and Schur convexity

Use the selected-index Lidskii inequality: for 1\leq i_1<\cdots<i_k\leq n,

\lambda_{i_1}(A+B)+\cdots+\lambda_{i_k}(A+B) \leq\lambda_{i_1}(A)+\cdots+\lambda_{i_k}(A)+\lambda_1(B)+\cdots+\lambda_k(B).

Note that

\sum_i\lambda_i(A+B)=\operatorname{tr}(A+B)=\sum_i\bigl(\lambda_i(A)+\lambda_i(B)\bigr).

In other words,

\lambda(A+B)-\lambda(A)\preceq\lambda(B),

where \preceq means majorization: the leading sums after decreasing rearrangement are bounded by the corresponding sums on the right, with equal total sums. By the Schur convexity theorem, we then have

\|\lambda(A+B)-\lambda(A)\|_{\ell^p}\leq\|B\|_{S^p}.

Here Schur convexity is used in the form that a symmetric convex function, in particular an \ell^p norm for 1\leq p\leq\infty, does not increase under majorization.
