Statement

Let d\ge1 be an integer and N a point process on \mathbb R^d, viewed as a random locally finite counting measure, not necessarily simple. Its intensity measure is \Lambda(B)=\mathbb E[N(B)] for Borel sets B. Sums over N count multiplicities. Suppose \Lambda(dx)=\lambda(x)\,dx, with measurable \lambda\ge0. For measurable real f with \int |f(x)|\lambda(x)\,dx<\infty, \mathbb E\!\left[\sum_{x\in N}f(x)\right]=\int_{\mathbb R^d}f(x)\lambda(x)\,dx.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Random measures and their densities are beyond the stipulated calculus background.

Proof 1: Integrate against the intensity density

Use Campbell’s mean formula: if \int |f|\,d\Lambda<\infty, then \mathbb E\sum_{x\in N}f(x)=\int f\,d\Lambda. If the intensity measure \Lambda of a point process N has a density \lambda(x), then Campbell's formula becomes:

\mathbb E\left[\sum_{x\in N}f(x)\right]=\int_{\mathbb R^d}f(x)\lambda(x)\,dx.
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The substitution of a density is short, but the full application requires random counting measures, their intensity measure, measurable random sums and Lebesgue integrability. These are measure-theoretic probability prerequisites.
