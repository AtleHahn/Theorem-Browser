Statement

For every irrational real number \alpha, infinitely many distinct rational numbers p/q, with integers p and q\ge1, satisfy |\alpha-p/q|<1/q^2.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement quantifies over integer numerators and denominators and elementary real-number error inequalities; no limit or general infinite-set operation is needed to understand arbitrarily many distinct rational approximants.

Proof 1: Pass from shrinking errors to infinitely many approximants — outline

Use the finite-N Dirichlet bound: for every positive integer N there are integers p,q, 1\le q\le N, with |\alpha-p/q|<1/(qN)\le1/q^2. For irrational \alpha, it follows that the quadratic-error inequality is satisfied by infinitely many rational approximants.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Apply the explicit finite-N inequality. If only finitely many rational values occurred, their positive errors would have a positive minimum, contradicted by choosing N sufficiently large. Elementary inequalities and finite minima suffice.
