Statement

For a positive integer n, let r_4(n) count ordered integer quadruples (x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4) with x_1^2+x_2^2+x_3^2+x_4^2=n; signs and order count separately. Let \sigma(k) be the sum of positive divisors of a positive integer k. Then r_4(n)=8\sigma(n)-32\sigma(n/4), with the second term interpreted as 0 when 4\nmid n.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The result involves finite integer counting and the explicitly defined divisor-sum function.

Proof 1: Removing the divisors divisible by four

Jacobi’s formula gives r_4(n)=8\sum_{d\mid n,\ 4\nmid d}d. Rewriting this restricted divisor sum by removing the divisors divisible by 4 gives 8\sigma(n)-32\sigma(n/4), with no subtraction when 4\nmid n.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Use the supplied integer counting formula and remove divisors divisible by four by writing them as four times divisors of n/4. Finite divisor sums and multiplication suffice.
