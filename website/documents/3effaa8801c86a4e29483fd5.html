Statement

For a Lipschitz function u:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R and a fixed unit vector v, the directional derivative D_vu(x)=\lim_{h\to0}(u(x+hv)-u(x))/h exists for Lebesgue-almost every x.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Multivariable calculus supplies directional derivatives and the Lipschitz inequality. Almost everywhere here means outside a set coverable by boxes of arbitrarily small total volume; this limited null-set definition suffices without general measure integration.

Proof 1: Apply the one-dimensional theorem on parallel lines — outline

For a Lipschitz-continuous function u on \mathbb R^n, for any fixed unit vector v, the v-directional derivative of u exists almost everywhere. This is a consequence of a special case of the Fubini theorem: a measurable set in Rn has Lebesgue measure zero if its restriction to every line parallel to v has (one-dimensional) Lebesgue measure zero. Considering in particular the set in Rn where the v-directional derivative of u fails to exist (whose measurability is required here but is not proved in the source), the latter condition is met due to the one-dimensional case of Rademacher's theorem.

Level justification
