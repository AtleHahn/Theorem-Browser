Statement

For every open set U\subset\mathbb R^n, n\ge1, the space \mathcal D'(U) of distributions, with its strong dual topology, is Souslin: there are a separable completely metrizable space P and a continuous surjection P\to\mathcal D'(U). Here \mathcal D(U)=C_c^\infty(U) is the space of smooth compactly supported real or complex functions. For compact K\subset U, let \mathcal D_K be the functions supported in K, with seminorms

p_m(\varphi)=\max_{|\alpha|\le m}\sup_{x\in K}|\partial^\alpha\varphi(x)|,\qquad m=0,1,2,\ldots,

where \alpha is a tuple of nonnegative integers, |\alpha| its sum and \partial^\alpha the corresponding partial derivative (for K=\varnothing, set all these seminorms to zero). Equip \mathcal D(U) with the finest locally convex vector topology making every inclusion \mathcal D_K\hookrightarrow\mathcal D(U) continuous. Distributions are continuous linear functionals on this space. The strong dual topology is generated by the seminorms T\mapsto\sup_{\varphi\in B}|T(\varphi)|, where B ranges over bounded subsets of \mathcal D(U). Such a subset is bounded precisely when its functions have support in a common compact K\subset U and \sup_{\varphi\in B}p_m(\varphi)<\infty for every m.
