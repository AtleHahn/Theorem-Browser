Naming a witness whose existence is already provable need not add consequences in the old vocabulary. This separates a useful extension of expressive resources from an increase in substantive mathematical commitments.

Statement

Let T be a first-order theory in a language L, and suppose T\vdash\exists y\,\varphi(y), where \exists y\,\varphi(y) is a closed L-formula. If a is a fresh constant, the theory T_1=T\cup\{\varphi(a)\} in L\cup\{a\} is conservative over T: it proves exactly the same L-sentences as T.

Proof 1: Treat a constant as a zero-argument function — outline

Suppose that a closed formula {\displaystyle \forall {\vec {x}}\,\exists y\,\!\,\varphi (y,{\vec {x}})} is a theorem of a first-order theory {\displaystyle T}, where we denote {\displaystyle {\vec {x}}:=(x_{1},\ldots ,x_{n})}. Let {\displaystyle T_{1}} be a theory obtained from {\displaystyle T} by extending its language with a new functional symbol {\displaystyle f} (of arity {\displaystyle n}) and adding a new axiom {\displaystyle \forall {\vec {x}}\,\varphi (f({\vec {x}}),{\vec {x}})}. Then {\displaystyle T_{1}} is a conservative extension of {\displaystyle T}, i.e. the theories {\displaystyle T} and {\displaystyle T_{1}} prove the same theorems not involving the functional symbol {\displaystyle f}).

Shoenfield states the theorem in the form for a new function name, and constants are the same as functions of zero arguments. Apply this function-name theorem with zero arguments: the new function name is the constant a, and the added axiom is \varphi(a).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Specialize the function-name extension theorem to arity zero. The full setting requires formal first-order theories, language expansion and conservativity; those combined logical notions are not confidently a small extension. Retain beyond undergraduate provisionally.
