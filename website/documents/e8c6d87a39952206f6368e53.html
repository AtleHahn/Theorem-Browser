A scheme can be recovered up to isomorphism from its category of quasi-coherent modules, without separately supplying tensor structure. This is a precise case in which a geometric object's identity is encoded by the organization of associated objects and their morphisms, supporting structural approaches to mathematical ontology.

Statement

Let X and Y be quasi-separated schemes. If the categories \operatorname{QCoh}(X) and \operatorname{QCoh}(Y) of quasi-coherent sheaves of modules are equivalent as categories, then X and Y are isomorphic as schemes. Thus a quasi-separated scheme is determined up to isomorphism by its quasi-coherent module category, without being supplied a tensor structure on that category.

A scheme is a locally ringed space locally isomorphic to an affine scheme \operatorname{Spec}A for a commutative ring A with identity. The spectrum has prime ideals as points, Zariski-closed subsets V(I)=\{\mathfrak p:I\subseteq\mathfrak p\}, and structure sheaf given on distinguished opens D(a)=\{\mathfrak p:a\notin\mathfrak p\} by the localization A_a. A locally ringed space is a topological space with a sheaf of commutative rings whose stalks are local rings. Quasi-separated means that the intersection of any two affine open subsets is quasi-compact, that is, every open cover has a finite subcover.

A sheaf \mathcal F of \mathcal O_X-modules is quasi-coherent if on every affine open \operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq X it is isomorphic to the sheaf associated to some A-module M, whose sections on D(a) are M_a. The morphisms in \operatorname{QCoh}(X) are sheaf-module homomorphisms. An equivalence of categories is a functor admitting an inverse up to natural isomorphism. An isomorphism of schemes is an invertible morphism of locally ringed spaces. No claim that an arbitrary given equivalence is itself a pullback functor is required.
