Statement

For a real symmetric n\times n matrix A, there exists an orthonormal eigenbasis u_1,\ldots,u_n with real eigenvalues \lambda_i. If S=[u_1\ \cdots\ u_n] and c=S^{\mathsf T}x, then Q(x)=x^{\mathsf T}Ax=\sum_i\lambda_i c_i^2. The coordinate principal-axis lines are \operatorname{span}(u_i) and are mutually perpendicular.

Proof 1: Orthogonal coordinate calculation

The orthonormal eigenbasis follows from the following statements guaranteed by the spectral theorem:

  • The eigenvalues of A are real.
  • A is diagonalizable, and the eigenspaces of A are mutually orthogonal.

In particular, A is orthogonally diagonalizable, since one may take a basis of each eigenspace and apply the Gram-Schmidt process separately within the eigenspace to obtain an orthonormal eigenbasis.

Suppose that the eigenvalues of A are λ1, ..., λn (possibly repeated according to their algebraic multiplicities) and the corresponding orthonormal eigenbasis is u1, ..., un. Then,

{\displaystyle \mathbf {c} =[\mathbf {u} _{1},\ldots ,\mathbf {u} _{n}]^{\textsf {T}}\mathbf {x} ,}

and

{\displaystyle Q(\mathbf {x} )=\lambda _{1}c_{1}^{2}+\lambda _{2}c_{2}^{2}+\dots +\lambda _{n}c_{n}^{2},}

where ci is the i-th entry of c. This follows from A=SDS^{\mathsf T}, with D=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n), and

x^{\mathsf T}Ax=(S^{\mathsf T}x)^{\mathsf T}D(S^{\mathsf T}x).

Furthermore,

The i-th principal axis is the line determined by equating cj = 0 for all j = 1, ..., i − 1, i + 1, ..., n. The i-th principal axis is the span of the vector ui.

Since the vectors u_i are orthonormal, these axis lines are mutually perpendicular.
