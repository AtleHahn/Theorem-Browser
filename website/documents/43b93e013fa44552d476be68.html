Statement

A single-vertex crease pattern consists of distinct rays from an interior point of a flat sheet. Flat-foldable means that all prescribed creases are folded, each as a mountain or valley, with no other creases, to a flat configuration with compatible layer order and no paper crossing. Let n\ge1 be an integer. Suppose there are 2n creases with positive successive angles \alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_{2n} totaling 360^\circ and alternating sum zero. Then at least 2^n distinct assignments of mountain or valley to the prescribed creases admit a flat folding.

High school only

Proof 1: Two choices at every smallest-sector reduction — outline

Consider the smallest angle αi and the two creases on either side of it. Mountain-fold one of these two creases and valley-fold the other, choosing arbitrarily which fold to use for which crease. Then, glue the resulting flap of paper onto the remaining part of the crease pattern. The result of this gluing will be a crease pattern with two fewer creases, on a conical sheet of paper, that still satisfies Kawasaki's condition (the alternating sum of the successive angles is zero). Therefore, by mathematical induction, repeating this process will eventually lead to a flat folding. The base case of the induction is a cone with only two creases and two equal-angle wedges, which can obviously be flat-folded by using a mountain fold for both creases.

Undoing the flap identifications gives flat foldings of the original pattern. In the two-crease base case, reversing all mountain and valley folds gives a second assignment, with both folds valleys.

There are two ways to choose which folds to use in each step of this method, and each step eliminates two creases. Therefore, any crease pattern with 2n creases that satisfies Kawasaki's condition has at least 2n different choices of mountain and valley folds that all lead to valid flat foldings.

There are n-1 removal steps and the two base assignments, giving 2^{n-1}\cdot2=2^n choices. Different choices differ on prescribed original creases.
