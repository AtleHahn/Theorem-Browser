Statement

For 1<p<\infty, H denotes the Hilbert transform on L^p(\mathbb R), with sign convention Hf(t)=\pi^{-1}\operatorname{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb R}f(\tau)/(t-\tau)\,d\tau. Let \mathcal D_{L^p}=\bigcap_{n\ge0}W^{n,p}(\mathbb R) with its projective-limit topology given by the Sobolev seminorms. Then H:\mathcal D_{L^p}\to\mathcal D_{L^p} is continuous.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Sobolev spaces; Projective-limit topology; Hilbert transform; Continuity of linear maps. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Proof 1: Continuity of the Hilbert transform on smooth Lp functions — proof

The Hilbert transform is bounded on L^p and commutes with differentiation. Consequently each derivative of Hu is the Hilbert transform of the corresponding derivative of u, with its L^p norm bounded by the same operator bound. This gives continuity for every Sobolev seminorm and hence for the projective-limit topology.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Control every weak derivative by the same Lp bound and use the Sobolev seminorm topology. Sobolev spaces and locally convex projective limits require graduate analysis.
