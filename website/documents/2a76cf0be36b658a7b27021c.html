Statement

If positive integers a,b,c,d satisfy a^2+b^2+c^2=d^2, then x=d^2-a^2, y=d^2-b^2, z=d^2-c^2 are sides of a triangle with semiperimeter s=d^2 and integer area abcd. Its inradius is abc/d, exradii opposite x,y,z are bcd/a,acd/b,abd/c, and circumradius is xyz/(4abcd).

Level
High school only
Level justification

Only elementary arithmetic, algebra and Euclidean geometry are needed.

Proof 1: Construct sides from complementary squares — outline

If a, b, c, d is a Pythagorean quadruple with {\textstyle a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}=d^{2}} it will generate a Heronian triangle with sides x, y, z as follows:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}x&=d^{2}-a^{2}\\y&=d^{2}-b^{2}\\z&=d^{2}-c^{2}\end{aligned}}}

It will have a semiperimeter {\textstyle s=d^{2}}, an area {\textstyle A=abcd} and an inradius {\textstyle r=abc/d}.

The exradii will be:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}r_{x}&=bcd/a,\\r_{y}&=acd/b,\\r_{z}&=abd/c.\end{aligned}}}

The circumradius will be:

{\displaystyle R={\frac {(d^{2}-a^{2})(d^{2}-b^{2})(d^{2}-c^{2})}{4abcd}}={\frac {abcd(1/a^{2}+1/b^{2}+1/c^{2}-1/d^{2})}{4}}}

The source gives this construction and the resulting formulas without expanding their verification by Heron's formula and the standard triangle-radius identities.
