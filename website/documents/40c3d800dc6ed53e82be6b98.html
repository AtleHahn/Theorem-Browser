Statement

For real x, \cos(3x)=\cos^3x-3\cos x\sin^2x and \sin(3x)=3\cos^2x\sin x-\sin^3x.

Level

Proof 1: Separate real and imaginary parts

For the complex numbers the binomial theorem can be combined with de Moivre's formula to yield multiple-angle formulas for the sine and cosine. According to De Moivre's formula,

{\displaystyle \cos \left(3x\right)+i\sin \left(3x\right)=\left(\cos x+i\sin x\right)^{3}.}

Using the binomial theorem, the expression on the right can be expanded, and then the real and imaginary parts can be taken to yield formulas for cos(3x) and sin(3x). Since

{\displaystyle \left(\cos x+i\sin x\right)^{3}=\cos ^{3}x+3i\cos ^{2}x\sin x-3\cos x\sin ^{2}x-i\sin ^{3}x,}

De Moivre's formula yields

{\displaystyle \cos(3x)=\cos ^{3}x-3\cos x\sin ^{2}x\quad {\text{and}}\quad \sin(3x)=3\cos ^{2}x\sin x-\sin ^{3}x.}
Level
High school only
Level justification

Expand the cube with i^2=-1 and i^3=-i, then compare real and imaginary parts using the stated de Moivre formula. Elementary complex arithmetic and trigonometry suffice.
