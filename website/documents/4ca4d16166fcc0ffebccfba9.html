Statement

Let n\geq1 and let f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R be infinitely differentiable and symmetric: for every permutation \sigma of \{1,\ldots,n\},

f(x_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(n)})=f(x_1,\ldots,x_n).

There is an infinitely differentiable function F:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R such that

f(x)=F(e_1(x),\ldots,e_n(x))\qquad(x\in\mathbb R^n),

where

e_j(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=\sum_{1\leq i_1<\cdots<i_j\leq n}x_{i_1}\cdots x_{i_j}\qquad(1\leq j\leq n)

are the elementary symmetric polynomials. Infinitely differentiable means that all partial derivatives of every order exist and are continuous on the indicated Euclidean space.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Multivariable calculus and finite polynomial sums suffice; symmetry and the coordinate polynomials are explicitly defined. No invariant-theory proof prerequisites are required.
