Statement

The real number e=\exp(1) is irrational.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement identifies a familiar real constant as irrational or as satisfying no rational-coefficient polynomial. Understanding that assertion needs elementary numbers and polynomial algebra, not the advanced proof of irrationality or transcendence. This rating concerns understanding the statement, independently of the difficulty of its proof.

Proof 1: Apply root-sum independence to zero and one

Use the complete-root sum lemma: Let R_1,\ldots,R_r be pairwise disjoint nonempty finite sets of distinct complex roots, each the full root set of a nonconstant integer polynomial. The numbers \sum_{\alpha\in R_t}e^\alpha, 1\le t\le r, are linearly independent over \mathbb Q. If e=p/q for nonzero integers p,q, apply it to root sets \{1\} and \{0\} of x-1 and x, with coefficients q,-p. It gives qe-p\ne0, contradiction.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the fully stated finite-root exponential-sum independence theorem on the singleton root sets of x and x-1. Polynomial roots and rational linear independence are undergraduate algebra; only its explicit numerical consequence is used, not transcendence-theoretic methods.
