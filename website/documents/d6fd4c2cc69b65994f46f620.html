Statement

Let V be a complex vector space of dimension n, and \Lambda\subset V a lattice of rank 2n. The group of homomorphisms \Lambda\to U(1)=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|=1\} is isomorphic to the real torus \mathbb R^{2n}/\mathbb Z^{2n}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra supplies bases, integer combinations and determinants; group theory supplies homomorphisms and quotients. A lattice is the integer span of a basis, and a character here is a homomorphism into the unit circle. Coordinates reduce the statements to these familiar objects.

Proof 1: Exponentiating linear functionals

For a lattice {\displaystyle \Lambda \cong \mathbb {Z} ^{2n}}, any such character factors through {\displaystyle \mathbb {R} } composed with the exponential map. That is, a character is a map of the form

{\displaystyle {\text{exp}}(2\pi i\langle l^{*},-\rangle )}

for some covector l^*\in V_{\mathbb R}^*. The periodicity of {\displaystyle {\text{exp}}(2\pi if(x))} for a linear {\displaystyle f(x)} gives the isomorphism of the character group with \mathbb R^{2n}/\mathbb Z^{2n}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Choose arguments for the character values on a lattice basis, extend linearly and quotient by integer-valued functionals. Undergraduate linear algebra, group homomorphisms and complex exponential suffice; lattice characters are simply homomorphisms from a free abelian group.
