Statement

Let R be a commutative ring and \mathfrak p a prime ideal. If \mathfrak p^n for n\ge1 has a primary decomposition, its \mathfrak p-primary component is the symbolic power \mathfrak p^{(n)}=R\cap\mathfrak p^n R_{\mathfrak p}, where the intersection denotes inverse image under localization.

Beyond undergraduate level

Primary decomposition and associated-prime module theory exceed calculus and linear algebra.

Proof 1: Apply minimal-component localization to a prime power

The prime \mathfrak p is the unique minimal prime over \mathfrak p^n. The stated minimal-component localization theorem identifies that component with the inverse image of \mathfrak p^n R_{\mathfrak p}. This is exactly the definition of \mathfrak p^{(n)}.
