Statement

Consider impartial two-player games of perfect information under normal play: both players have the same legal moves at each position, the player unable to move loses, and there is no infinite sequence of legal moves. Positions and their options are sets; infinite branching is allowed.

Every position G is equivalent to a unique ordinal nimber *\alpha. The ordinal Nim position *\alpha has as options all *\beta with \beta<\alpha. Equivalence means that for every impartial game H satisfying the same ending and normal-play conditions, the disjunctive sums G+H and *\alpha+H have the same winner under optimal play. In a disjunctive sum, each move changes exactly one component. Thus the equivalent Nim heap may have a transfinite ordinal size; for finite game trees its size is a nonnegative integer.
