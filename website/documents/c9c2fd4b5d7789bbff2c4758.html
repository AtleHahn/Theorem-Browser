Statement

Let X be a real random variable with \Pr(m\le X\le M)=1, where m\le M are finite real numbers. Set \mu=\mathbb E[X] and \operatorname{Var}(X)=\mathbb E[(X-\mu)^2]. Then

\operatorname{Var}(X)\le\frac{(M-m)^2}{4}.

Equality holds exactly when the probability distribution of X is \tfrac12\delta_m+\tfrac12\delta_M, where \delta_a denotes unit mass at a. For m<M this assigns probability 1/2 to each endpoint; for m=M it is the point mass at their common value.

Proof 1: Nonnegative quadratic and arithmetic–geometric mean — outline

Writing \sigma^2=\operatorname{Var}(X), the source uses

0\le\mathbb E[(M-X)(X-m)] =-\mathbb E[X^2]-mM+(m+M)\mu.

Thus

\sigma^2=\mathbb E[X^2]-\mu^2 \le (M-\mu)(\mu-m) \le\frac{(M-m)^2}{4}.

The last step is the two-number arithmetic–geometric mean inequality, applied to M-\mu and \mu-m. The source leaves checking the equality cases in these two inequalities implicit; these routine checks are not expanded into an additional proof here.
