Statement

Assume Peano arithmetic is consistent. There exists a consistent computably enumerable first-order theory with no complete consistent computably enumerable extension; Peano arithmetic itself is such a theory.

Proof 1: Apply incompleteness to a proposed complete extension of arithmetic

Peano arithmetic is computably enumerable and is consistent by hypothesis. Gödel’s incompleteness theorem says that a consistent computably enumerable theory extending Peano arithmetic cannot be complete. Thus Peano arithmetic has no extension possessing all three of these properties, and is a counterexample to the proposed effective extension principle.
