Statement

For a cyclic group G of finite order n\ge1, every subgroup is cyclic and has order dividing n, and for each positive divisor d of n there is exactly one subgroup of order d.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory group and ring theory supplies subgroups, quotients, commutators and homomorphisms. Any added terms such as perfect, nilpotent or virtually free describe explicit subgroup conditions; they are small algebraic extensions without representation or geometric-group theory.

Proof 1: Passing from integer subgroups to the cyclic quotient (outline)

Use the classification of subgroups of the infinite cyclic group: they are k\mathbb Z. View G as a quotient \mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z. The subgroup correspondence identifies its subgroups with k\mathbb Z containing n\mathbb Z, that is, with positive divisors k of n. Their images are cyclic of order n/k, giving exactly one for each divisor order.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the integer subgroup classification and the quotient correspondence to identify subgroups by divisors. Cyclic groups, quotient groups and subgroup order are standard introductory abstract algebra.
