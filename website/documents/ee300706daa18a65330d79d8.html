For real normed spaces, the full distance structure determines the affine linear structure up to translation. This gives a precise instance of reconstructing algebraic relations from geometrical ones, rather than assuming that linear coordinates are independent primitive data.

Statement

Let V and W be real normed vector spaces, and let f:V\to W be a surjective isometry, meaning that f is onto and

\|f(u)-f(v)\|_W=\|u-v\|_V\qquad(u,v\in V).

Then f is affine: there are a real-linear map L:V\to W and a vector b\in W such that f(v)=L(v)+b for every v\in V.

Proof 1: Doubling the midpoint defect by reflection (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Main theorem and proof, p.1).

A surjective isometry is injective because zero distance forces equality. For the final affine step set L(x)=f(x)-f(0). Midpoint preservation gives L(2x)=2L(x) and hence L(x+y)=L(x)+L(y). Thus L(qx)=qL(x) for rational q, and continuity of the isometry extends this identity to every real scalar. No completeness or strict convexity is required.
