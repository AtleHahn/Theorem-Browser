Statement

Let D\subset\mathbb R^2 be the closed bounded region enclosed by a positively oriented piecewise smooth Jordan curve \gamma:[a,b]\to\mathbb R^2. Let \psi be a continuous, piecewise smooth map from a neighborhood of D into \mathbb R^3, and let F:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R^3 be smooth. Put \Gamma=\psi\circ\gamma. Then

\int_a^b F(\Gamma(t))\cdot\Gamma'(t)\,dt =\iint_D(\nabla\times F)(\psi(u,v))\cdot(\psi_u\times\psi_v)\,du\,dv.

The derivatives and integrals are taken piece by piece across the finite smooth pieces. The right side is the oriented surface integral with the stated parametrization, including multiplicity; the boundary on the left is the parametrized path \Gamma, which need not be simple.

Proof 1: Additive Stokes with cancellation and smooth approximation (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Section 1.8, Theorem 1.8.3, printed pp.20–21; Section 1.6).

The source’s additive proof cancels oppositely oriented internal edges. For a shared parameter edge c, continuity gives the same trace \psi\circ c on both sides, so the tangential pullbacks agree. On a smooth piece, with P=F(\psi)\cdot\psi_u and Q=F(\psi)\cdot\psi_v, the chain rule gives Q_u-P_v=(\nabla\times F)(\psi)\cdot(\psi_u\times\psi_v).

For the curved Jordan boundary, the following smooth-approximation argument also avoids assuming a finite transverse intersection of every seam with the boundary. Smooth the continuous finite-piece parametrization on its neighborhood. The approximations converge uniformly, their first derivatives are uniformly bounded, and their derivatives converge off the seams. Green’s theorem for each smooth approximation and dominated convergence give convergence of the area integrals. On the boundary, the image curves converge uniformly with uniformly bounded speeds; integration by parts against the absolutely continuous function F(\Gamma) gives convergence of their line integrals. This is an additional adaptation of the additive argument. Everything is computed in the parameter domain, retaining multiplicity and allowing a nonsimple image boundary.

The finite-piece extension uses Lipschitz bounds, smoothing by averages and dominated convergence, obtained from real-variable approximation and the fundamental theorem of calculus.
