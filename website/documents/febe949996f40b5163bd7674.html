Statement

Let k be a field of characteristic zero. Every algebraic variety X over k has a resolution of singularities: there is a nonsingular variety \widetilde X and a proper birational morphism \pi:\widetilde X\to X that is an isomorphism over the nonsingular locus of X.

The embedded form is as follows. If X is a closed subvariety of a nonsingular variety W, there is a finite sequence of blowups of W along nonsingular closed centers, each having normal crossings with the exceptional divisors already created, such that the resulting strict transform \widetilde X\subset\widetilde W is nonsingular and has normal crossings with the exceptional divisor. Its map to X is a resolution and is an isomorphism away from the singular locus of X.

A variety here is a reduced separated scheme of finite type over k; birationality is understood componentwise. Nonsingular means that all local rings are regular. A proper morphism is separated, of finite type and universally closed; birational means that it is an isomorphism on dense open subsets. Blowing up a center with ideal sheaf I means forming \operatorname{Proj}(\bigoplus_{j\geq0}I^j). The strict transform is the closure of the inverse image away from the centers. The exceptional divisor records the divisors introduced by blowup. Normal crossings means that locally, in regular coordinates, these divisors are coordinate hyperplanes and the center or final strict transform meets them in coordinate-subspace form.

Proof availability

Source proof outline and limitations

The source replaces a variety by local ideal-sheaf data, exceptional divisors and multiplicity/order data. It proposes choosing smooth blowup centers from a suitable local invariant, with compatibility ensuring that the local centers glue. The induction passes from lower-dimensional marked ideals to maximal-order marked ideals and then to general marked ideals; the Hilbert–Samuel function is one ingredient in stronger constructions.

The invariant, the two induction steps, global compatibility and termination are not proved in the article. Its descriptions of marked-ideal cosupport and maximal order are also too compressed to serve as precise definitions for a proof. This section records the strategy, without turning those assertions into a completed resolution algorithm.
