Statement

A finite simple undirected graph G=(V,E) has vertices V and edges consisting of unordered pairs of distinct vertices. A proper vertex coloring assigns different colors to the ends of every edge. A proper edge coloring gives different colors to edges sharing an endpoint. The line graph L(G) has one vertex for each edge of G, with adjacency precisely when the two edges share an endpoint. Proper edge colorings of G correspond bijectively to proper vertex colorings of L(G) using the same labeled colors. In particular, \chi'(G)=\chi(L(G)), where \chi' denotes the minimum number of edge colors.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Graphs, both kinds of coloring and the line graph are defined explicitly using finite vertices and edges. The statement asks for the same labeled assignments under a relabeling of edges as vertices.

Proof 1: Edge colorings are vertex colorings of the line graph

View each edge color in G as the color of its corresponding vertex of L(G). The conflict conditions are identical, and the same correspondence in reverse recovers the edge coloring.
