Statement

A convex body K\subseteq\mathbb R^n, n\ge1, is a compact convex set with nonempty interior. An ellipsoid is a+TB, where B=\{x:\|x\|\le1\}, a\in\mathbb R^n and T is an invertible real linear map; volume is Euclidean n-dimensional volume. There is a unique minimum-volume ellipsoid E=a+TB containing K, and a+\frac1n(E-a)\subseteq K\subseteq E. If K is balanced, meaning tK\subseteq K for every real |t|\le1, the factor 1/n can be replaced by 1/\sqrt n.

Proof 1: Contact-point decompositions and polarity (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (§4.2.1 Propositions 4.2–4.7, printed pp.84–88; Exercises 4.20–4.21 and solutions printed p.341 (PDF p.358).).

Normalize the outer ellipsoid to the unit ball. Its contact points satisfy \sum c_i u_i u_i^T=I, \sum c_i u_i=0 and \sum c_i=n. For each unit v, these imply \max_i\langle v,u_i\rangle\ge1/n, so the contact-point hull, hence K, contains B/n by separation. In the symmetric case use both signs and \max_i|\langle v,u_i\rangle|\ge1/\sqrt n. Uniqueness and symmetry force the center to be zero. Undoing the affine normalization yields the stated centered containments, also for n=1.

Level justification

The proof combines positive definite matrices, determinant inequalities and compactness with convex polarity, a contact-point decomposition and an affine lift. The symmetric contact dependency follows from separation in the finite-dimensional space of symmetric matrices and a determinant perturbation. Polars and contact resolutions are short extensions of linear algebra and introductory real analysis; the complete chain fits the undergraduate allowance.
