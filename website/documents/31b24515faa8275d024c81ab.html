Statement

If Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory \mathrm{ZF} is consistent, then the theory

\mathrm{ZF}+\mathrm{BPI}+\neg\mathrm{AC}

is consistent. Here \mathrm{BPI} is the Boolean prime ideal theorem and \mathrm{AC} is the axiom of choice, with the formulations below.

  • \mathrm{BPI} says that every nontrivial Boolean algebra has a prime ideal. A Boolean algebra is a bounded distributive lattice (B,\wedge,\vee,0,1) with complements, with 0\ne1 in the nontrivial case. A lattice has a greatest lower bound a\wedge b and least upper bound a\vee b for every pair; bounded means it has least and greatest elements 0,1. Complements satisfy a\wedge\neg a=0 and a\vee\neg a=1. An ideal I\subset B contains 0, is closed under joins and downward closed; it is prime if 1\notin I and a\wedge b\in I implies a\in I or b\in I.
  • \mathrm{AC} says that for every indexed family (A_i)_{i\in J} of nonempty sets there is a function f with domain J such that f(i)\in A_i for all i.

Consistency means that no contradiction has a finite formal proof from the axioms in classical first-order logic. \mathrm{ZF} is the usual set theory with extensionality, empty set, pairing, union, power set, infinity, separation and replacement schemes, and foundation, without the axiom of choice. All algebras and families in the added axioms are sets of the model.
