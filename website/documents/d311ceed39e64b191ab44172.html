Statement

Let 0<\lambda_1<\lambda_2<\cdots be a strictly increasing sequence of real numbers. For any 0<a<b, the complex linear span of x^{\lambda_1},x^{\lambda_2},\ldots is dense in C([a,b];\mathbb C) in the uniform norm if and only if

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac1{\lambda_j}=\infty.

For b>0, the same condition is necessary and sufficient for the complex linear span of 1,x^{\lambda_1},x^{\lambda_2},\ldots to be dense in C([0,b];\mathbb C). Here 0^{\lambda_j}=0. Density means that for every continuous complex-valued f and every \varepsilon>0 a finite linear combination of the indicated functions differs from f by less than \varepsilon everywhere on the interval.
