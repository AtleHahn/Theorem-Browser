Statement

Let S be a nonempty compact convex subset of \mathbb R^d, and let \varphi:S\to2^S have nonempty convex values. Then \varphi has a closed graph if and only if it is upper hemicontinuous and every \varphi(x) is closed. Upper hemicontinuity means that for each relatively open W\subseteq S, the set \{x\in S:\varphi(x)\subseteq W\} is relatively open in S. Thus the closed-graph and upper-hemicontinuous formulations of Kakutani’s hypotheses are equivalent.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Compact convex subsets of Euclidean space, closed graphs and open sets are undergraduate analysis/topology. The set-valued upper-hemicontinuity condition is explicitly defined by open inverse containment sets, a direct extension of familiar continuity.

The source invokes the set-valued closed-graph theorem: with compact Hausdorff range Y, a correspondence into Y has a closed graph exactly when it is upper hemicontinuous and closed-valued.
