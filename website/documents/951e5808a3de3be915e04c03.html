Statement

Let X be a compact complex manifold of complex dimension n and L an ample holomorphic line bundle. Its Dolbeault cohomology groups satisfy H^{p,q}(X,L)=0 for integers 0\le p,q\le n with p+q>n.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Compact complex manifolds; Ample holomorphic line bundles; Dolbeault cohomology. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Proof 1: Set the bundle rank equal to one

Le Potier's theorem states: Let X be an n-dimensional compact complex manifold and E a holomorphic vector bundle of rank r over X. If E is ample, then

H^{p,q}(X,E)=0\qquad\text{for }p+q\geq n+r.

In case of r=1, with E=L an ample line bundle on X, this gives p+q\geq n+1, or p+q>n.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Setting r=1 is elementary, but understanding the theorem as applied requires compact complex manifolds, holomorphic vector and line bundles, ampleness and bundle-valued Dolbeault cohomology. This sequence of interdependent structures clearly exceeds the undergraduate allowance, even though no proof of Le Potier vanishing is required.
