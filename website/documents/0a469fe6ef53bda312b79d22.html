Statement

Let \mathcal P be the linear isometric Plancherel extension on L^2(\mathbb R) of the transform \widehat f(\xi)=\int f(x)e^{-2\pi ix\xi}\,dx on L^1\cap L^2. For f,g\in L^2(\mathbb R), \int f\overline g=\int(\mathcal Pf)\overline{\mathcal Pg}. If both functions also lie in L^1, the right side is \int\widehat f\overline{\widehat g}.

Proof 1: Plancherel inner-product identity — source argument

Due to the complex polarization identity, one can apply the Plancherel norm isometry to the L^2(\mathbb R) inner product of two functions. The identity expresses the inner product in terms of the squared norms of f+i^kg, k=0,1,2,3, which are preserved by the linear isometry \mathcal P. Thus

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x)\overline{g(x)}\,dx =\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(\mathcal Pf)(\xi) \overline{(\mathcal Pg)(\xi)}\,d\xi.

If f and g are furthermore L^1(\mathbb R) functions, then

(\mathcal Pf)(\xi)=\widehat f(\xi) =\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x)e^{-2\pi i\xi x}\,dx

and

(\mathcal Pg)(\xi)=\widehat g(\xi) =\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g(x)e^{-2\pi i\xi x}\,dx,

so

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x)\overline{g(x)}\,dx =\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\widehat f(\xi)\overline{\widehat g(\xi)}\,d\xi.

Level justification

Use polarization of the norm isometry, but interpret the full Plancherel extension on Lebesgue L2 and its compatibility with L1 transforms. Those measure-theoretic Hilbert-space objects require graduate analysis.
