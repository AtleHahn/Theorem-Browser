Statement

Let f:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R be continuously differentiable, with an attained global minimum f^*=f(x^*). For \mu>0, the \mu-PL condition means \frac12\|\nabla f(x)\|^2\geq\mu(f(x)-f^*) for all x. Assume that f satisfies this PL condition. Let f=\mathbb E_i f_i, with continuously differentiable f_i having L-Lipschitz gradients for L>0, and assume the gradient sampling is unbiased: \mathbb E_i\nabla f_i(x)=\nabla f(x). Let the displayed second moments be finite. Given the current iterate x_k, sample a fresh index i_k and put x_{k+1}=x_k-\eta_k\nabla f_{i_k}(x_k) with \eta_k>0. Expectations in the one-step bound are conditional on x_k. Fix \eta_k=1/L and suppose \mathbb E_i\|\nabla f_i(x_k)-\nabla f(x_k)\|^2\leq C at every step. Then \mathbb E[f(x_k)-f^*]\leq(1-\mu/L)^k(f(x_0)-f^*)+C/(2\mu). Here x_0 is fixed, and the expectation in this final bound is over all sampled indices.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The update and PL condition are explicit, and the probabilistic additions are sampling averages and their second moments. Iterated averaging over finitely many fresh samples is a limited extension of undergraduate multivariable calculus.

Proof 1: Variance identity and geometric iteration

Local upper-bound lemma

If f:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R is continuously differentiable and its gradient is L-Lipschitz, then f(y)\leq f(x)+\langle\nabla f(x),y-x\rangle+\frac L2\|y-x\|^2 for all x,y\in\mathbb R^d.

Set {\textstyle g(t)=f(x+t(y-x))} for {\textstyle t\in [0,1]} and use the {\textstyle L} -Lipschitz continuity to show that {\textstyle f(y)-f(x)=g(1)-g(0)=\int _{0}^{1}g'(t)=\langle \int _{0}^{1}\nabla f(x+t(y-x))dt,y-x\rangle \leq \langle \nabla f(x),y-x\rangle +{\frac {L}{2}}\|y-x\|^{2}}.

Conditional expectation and variance

Because all {\textstyle \nabla f_{i}} are {\textstyle L} -Lipschitz, so is {\textstyle \nabla f}. We thus have

{\displaystyle f(x_{k+1})\leq f(x_{k})-\eta _{k}\langle \nabla f(x_{k}),\nabla f_{i_{k}}(x_{k})\rangle +{\frac {L\eta _{k}^{2}}{2}}\|\nabla f_{i_{k}}(x_{k})\|^{2}}

Take expectation over the fresh index, conditional on x_k, and use unbiasedness. Use the variance identity

{\displaystyle \mathbb {E} _{i}[\|\nabla f_{i}(x_{k})\|^{2}]=\mathbb {E} _{i}[\|\nabla f_{i}(x_{k})-\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}]+\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}}

At \eta_k=1/L, apply the PL inequality to the resulting negative gradient-square term and use the variance bound C. For constant learning rate schedule, with {\textstyle \eta _{k}=\eta =1/L}, we have

{\displaystyle \mathbb {E} \left[f\left(x_{k+1}\right)-f^{*}\mid x_k\right]\leq \left(1-\mu /L\right)\left[f\left(x_{k}\right)-f^{*}\right]+{\frac {C}{2L}}}

Taking total expectations and iterating this inequality, we have

{\displaystyle \mathbb {E} \left[f\left(x_{k}\right)-f^{*}\right]\leq \left(1-\mu /L\right)^{k}\left[f\left(x_{0}\right)-f^{*}\right]+{\frac {C}{2\mu }}}

Level justification

Average the descent estimate over a fresh index, expand its second moment into variance plus squared mean and iterate total expected errors. Undergraduate calculus and probability suffice; conditioning is the concrete independent-sampling update, not a general filtration theorem.
