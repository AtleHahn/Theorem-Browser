Statement

Let A be a finite alphabet, X=A^{\mathbb Z} the set of bi-infinite configurations, with the product of the discrete topologies on A. A basic open cylinder specifies symbols at finitely many integer positions. Write (sx)_i=x_{i-1} for the shift. A cellular-automaton map has a finite set N\subset\mathbb Z and a rule \mu:A^N\to A such that f(x)_i=\mu((x_{i+n})_{n\in N}) for all i. If a cellular-automaton map f:X\to X is bijective, then f^{-1} is also a cellular-automaton map, possibly with a different finite neighborhood.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Basic topology supplies a product of finite discrete spaces and bijections. The cellular-automaton rule is explicitly a finite-neighborhood update on a sequence, a concrete discrete definition. The inverse claim needs no computability or dynamical-system theory for comprehension.

Proof 1: Continuity of the inverse and the local-rule characterization — outline

The local rule makes f continuous and shift-equivariant. Since the finite-alphabet shift space is compact Hausdorff, the compact-to-Hausdorff continuous-bijection theorem makes f^{-1} continuous. Its inverse map also commutes with the shift. The Curtis–Hedlund–Lyndon theorem then expresses f^{-1} by a finite local rule.
