Likely alternative prerequisite level: Undergraduate level; lowest recorded proof level: Beyond undergraduate level. Polarization of the multiplicative quadratic norm gives operators J_i for an orthonormal basis of 1-perp with J_i^2=-I and J_i J_j=-J_j J_i. If N>1, J_1 makes N even. On the complexification, pair generators: i J_(2k-1)J_(2k) are commuting involutions for k=1,...,(N-2)/2. Their simultaneous sign eigenspaces have equal dimensions, since a paired generator flips just its own sign. Thus 2^((N-2)/2) divides N, leaving N=2,4,8 (and separately N=1). Finite-dimensional matrix algebra replaces Clifford classification.

Statement

A Euclidean Hurwitz algebra is a nonzero finite-dimensional real unital algebra A (not assumed associative), with a positive-definite quadratic form q satisfying q(ab)=q(a)q(b). Write (a,b)=\frac12(q(a+b)-q(a)-q(b)), \|a\|^2=q(a), a^*=-a+2(a,1)1, L(a)b=ab, R(a)b=ba, and \operatorname{Re}a=(a,1)1. Then N=\dim_{\mathbb R}A belongs to \{1,2,4,8\}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Finite-dimensional linear algebra supplies bilinear multiplication, quadratic forms, adjoints and orthogonal sums. The algebra axioms and all involution/multiplication formulas are explicitly specified. Understanding these finite algebraic conditions is a small extension without classification theory.

Proof 1: Clifford-module dimension divisibility — outline

Use the Hurwitz identities L(a^*)=L(a)^* and L(a)^2=L(a^2), with a^2=-\|a\|^2 1 for a\perp1.

In fact the operators L(a) with (a, 1) = 0 satisfy L(a)2 = −‖a‖2I and so define a representation of a real Clifford algebra. If a is a unit vector, then L(a) is skew-adjoint with square −I. So N must be either even or 1 (in which case A contains no unit vectors orthogonal to 1). The real Clifford algebra and its complexification act on the complexification of A, an N-dimensional complex space. If N is even, N − 1 is odd, so the Clifford algebra has exactly two complex irreducible representations of dimension 2N/2 − 1. By complete reducibility of these Clifford representations, this power of 2 must divide N. It is easy to see that this implies N can only be 1, 2, 4 or 8.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Turn left multiplications into a Clifford action and use irreducible dimensions and complete reducibility to restrict N. Clifford algebras and their complex representation classification form a substantial additional representation-theoretic framework.
