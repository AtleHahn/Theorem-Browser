Statement

Every continuous linear functional f:M\to\mathbb K on a vector subspace M of a locally convex topological vector space X over \mathbb K\in\{\mathbb R,\mathbb C\} has a continuous linear extension to X.

Proof 1: Continuous extension on a locally convex space

Let {\displaystyle f} be a continuous linear functional defined on a vector subspace {\displaystyle M} of a locally convex topological vector space {\displaystyle X.} Because {\displaystyle X} is locally convex, there exists a continuous seminorm {\displaystyle p:X\to \mathbb {R} } on {\displaystyle X} that dominates {\displaystyle f} (meaning that {\displaystyle |f(m)|\leq p(m)} for all {\displaystyle m\in M}). By the Hahn–Banach theorem, there exists a linear extension of {\displaystyle f} to {\displaystyle X,} call it {\displaystyle F,} that satisfies {\displaystyle |F|\leq p} on {\displaystyle X.} This linear functional {\displaystyle F} is continuous since {\displaystyle |F|\leq p} and {\displaystyle p} is a continuous seminorm.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Dominate the subspace functional by an ambient continuous seminorm, extend it and use that seminorm for continuity. General locally convex topological vector spaces and their seminorm-generated topologies require graduate functional analysis.
