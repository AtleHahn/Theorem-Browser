Arithmetic can encode a bitmap depicting the very formula used to display it. This is self-depiction through numerical encoding, not semantic self-reference or a theorem about the formula asserting its own truth.

Statement

Define \operatorname{mod}(t,q)=t-q\lfloor t/q\rfloor for real t and q>0. Consider the inequality

\frac12<\left\lfloor\operatorname{mod}\left(\left\lfloor\frac y{17}\right\rfloor 2^{-17\lfloor x\rfloor-\operatorname{mod}(\lfloor y\rfloor,17)},2\right)\right\rfloor.

Proof 1: Binary digit extraction and the specified bitmap (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Section3, Lemma1 and proof, pp.5–6; Höhle, R Implementation).

The paper proves the general bit-extraction lemma; its example uses a different vertical convention. The Höhle implementation contains exactly the 543 digits printed here. They give k=17N with 0\le N<2^{1802}. On the cell indexed by (i,j), \lfloor y/17\rfloor=N and \operatorname{mod}(\lfloor y\rfloor,17)=j, so the inequality selects precisely bit 17i+j of N. The decoded bitmap, arranged with i=105,\ldots,0 from left to right and j=0,\ldots,16 from top to bottom, displays the stated inequality. The exact pixel rows retain the finite verification.

Finite integer arithmetic suffices. Division by a power of two shifts the binary digits, reduction modulo two removes higher bits, and the floor removes the remaining fractional tail. The specified finite bitmap then verifies the particular constant.
