Statement

If B is a nonzero simple unital ring, then its center Z(B) is a field.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Polynomial substitution, ring centers, simple rings and rational partial fractions are introductory algebra. Invariance is explicitly a polynomial equality, and simple means no nontrivial two-sided ideals; no further structure is needed.

Proof 1: Central principal ideal — outline

For 0\ne x\in Z(B), the nonzero two-sided ideal Bx=xB equals B by simplicity. Thus x is a unit. Its inverse is central as well, so every nonzero element of the commutative ring Z(B) is invertible.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use simplicity of the central principal ideal to obtain an inverse, then commute it with arbitrary elements. Introductory undergraduate ring theory suffices after the simple-ring definition; no structure theorem is used.
