Statement

Let f be holomorphic on a neighborhood of the closed disk |z-c|\le r, with r>0, and let M_r=\max_{|w-c|=r}|f(w)|. For every integer k\ge0, |f^{(k)}(c)|\le k!M_r/r^k.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex differentiability and entire functions add one direct extension of calculus; power-series coefficients, derivatives, absolute values and growth bounds are familiar analysis. The explicit inequalities require no specialist complex-analytic constructions to understand.

Proof 1: Bound the contour integrand and length

Let U be an open neighborhood of the closed disk on which f is holomorphic, and write B(c,r)=\{w:|w-c|<r\} and S(c,r)=\{w:|w-c|=r\}. Let r > 0 such that the closed disk B(c, r) ∪ S(c, r) is contained in U. Then Cauchy's integral formula with a positive parametrization γ(t) = c + reit of the circle S(c, r) with {\displaystyle t\in [0,2\pi ]} gives

{\displaystyle f(c)={\frac {1}{2\pi i}}\int _{\gamma }{\frac {f(w)}{w-c}}\,dw,\quad f'(c)={\frac {1}{2\pi i}}\int _{\gamma }{\frac {f(w)}{(w-c)^{2}}}\,dw,\quad \ldots ,\quad f^{(k)}(c)={\frac {k!}{2\pi i}}\int _{\gamma }{\frac {f(w)}{(w-c)^{k+1}}}\,dw.}

Here the differentiated kernels are uniformly bounded on the circle as the evaluation point varies in a smaller disk around its center, which justifies differentiation under the integral sign. In particular, if f is once complex differentiable on the open set U, then it is actually infinitely many times complex differentiable on U. One also obtains Cauchy's estimate

{\displaystyle |f^{(k)}(c)|\leq {\frac {k!}{2\pi }}\int _{\gamma }{\frac {M_{r}}{|w-c|^{k+1}}}\,|dw|={\frac {k!M_{r}}{r^{k}}},\quad M_{r}=\max _{|w-c|=r}|f(w)|}

for any c ∈ U and r > 0 such that B(c, r) ∪ S(c, r) ⊂ U.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The bound uses the higher-derivative Cauchy formula and complex differentiation of a contour kernel. Understanding holomorphy and its integral representation is a substantial complex-analysis prerequisite; the final modulus estimate does not eliminate it.
