Statement

Every finite product of compact topological spaces is compact in the product topology, including the empty product, a one-point space.

Level

Level justification

Finite products and compactness are standard basic topology; no additional background is required to understand the assertion.

Proof 1: Cover slices, cover the base, and induct

Local tube lemma

If Y is compact, X is any topological space, x\in X, and N\subseteq X\times Y is open with \{x\}\times Y\subseteq N, then some open neighborhood U of x satisfies U\times Y\subseteq N.

For each y\in Y, choose open U_y\subseteq X, V_y\subseteq Y with (x,y)\in U_y\times V_y\subseteq N. Select finitely many V_{y_1},\ldots,V_{y_m} covering Y and put U=\bigcap_{j=1}^m U_{y_j}. Then U is a neighborhood of x and U\times Y\subseteq N. If Y is empty take U=X.

Two factors and induction

Let X and Y be compact spaces. Let \{G_{a}\} be an open cover of X\times Y. For each x\in X, cover the slice \{x\}\times Y by finitely many elements of \{G_{a}\} (this is possible since \{x\}\times Y is compact, being homeomorphic to Y). Call the union of these finitely many elements N_{x}. By the tube lemma, there is an open set of the form W_{x}\times Y containing \{x\}\times Y and contained in N_{x}. The collection of all W_{x} for x\in X is an open cover of X and hence has a finite subcover \{W_{x_{1}},\dots ,W_{x_{n}}\}. Thus the finite collection \{W_{x_{1}}\times Y,\dots ,W_{x_{n}}\times Y\} covers X\times Y. Using the fact that each W_{x_{i}}\times Y is contained in N_{x_{i}} and each N_{x_{i}} is the finite union of elements of \{G_{a}\}, one gets a finite subcollection of \{G_{a}\} that covers X\times Y.

By the two-factor result and induction, one can show that the finite product of compact spaces is compact. The empty product is a singleton and is compact.

Proof 2: Reduce to properness of the projection — outline

Use the stated projection theorem: if X is compact and Y is any topological space, then p:X\times Y\to Y has compact preimages of compact subsets. For compact Y, its preimage p^{-1}(Y)=X\times Y is compact. Applying this finite-product reduction successively gives the result. The product of zero factors is a singleton, which is compact.
