Statement

Let H be a complex Hilbert space, let A\subseteq B(H) be a self-adjoint algebra of bounded operators, and let \overline A^{\mathrm s} denote its strong-operator closure. Every a\in\overline A^{\mathrm s} with \|a\|\leq1 is the strong limit of a net a_\alpha\in A with \|a_\alpha\|\leq1. Strong convergence means a_\alpha\xi\to a\xi for every \xi\in H.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement concerns strong operator approximation in an infinite-dimensional operator algebra.

Proof 1: Reduction to self-adjoint density using a two-by-two matrix (outline)

Use the self-adjoint case of density as an explicit dependency: for any self-adjoint operator algebra, self-adjoint contractions in its strong closure are strongly approximable by self-adjoint contractions from that algebra. For a\in\overline A^{\mathrm s} with \|a\|\leq1, the source considers, on H\oplus H, the self-adjoint matrix

X=\begin{pmatrix}0&a\\a^*&0\end{pmatrix}.

Its norm is \|a\|, and it belongs to the strong closure of M_2(A). Apply the self-adjoint density case to M_2(A) to approximate X strongly by self-adjoint contractions in M_2(A). Their upper-right entries belong to A, have norm at most one, and converge strongly to a. This proves the assertion.
