Statement

Write V for n-dimensional volume and S for boundary surface area. For an n-dimensional convex body K\subset\mathbb R^n, n\ge2, and unit ball B, [V(K)/V(B)]^{1/n}\le[S(K)/S(B)]^{1/(n-1)}, with equality exactly when K is a ball.

Proof 1: Substitute the unit ball into the mixed-volume inequality

Let K and L be two n-dimensional convex bodies in \mathbb R^n, meaning compact convex sets with nonempty interior. Define a quantity V_1(K,L) by

nV_1(K,L)=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0} \frac{V(K+\varepsilon L)-V(K)}{\varepsilon},

where V denotes the n-dimensional Lebesgue measure and + denotes the Minkowski sum, K+\varepsilon L=\{x+\varepsilon y:x\in K,\ y\in L\}. Minkowski's first inequality for convex bodies states that

V_1(K,L)\geq V(K)^{(n-1)/n}V(L)^{1/n},

with equality if and only if K and L are homothetic, i.e. are equal up to translation and dilation.

If L is the n-dimensional unit ball B, then nV_1(K,B) is the (n-1)-dimensional surface measure of K, denoted S(K). By taking L=B in Minkowski's first inequality for convex bodies, one obtains the isoperimetric inequality for convex bodies in \mathbb R^n:

\left(\frac{V(K)}{V(B)}\right)^{1/n} \leq\left(\frac{S(K)}{S(B)}\right)^{1/(n-1)},

with equality if and only if K is a ball of some radius. The normalization uses S(B)=nV(B); equality means that K is homothetic to B.
