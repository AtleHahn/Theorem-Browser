Statement

Let X be a real random variable with mean \mu=\mathbb E X and variance 0<\sigma^2=\mathbb E[(X-\mu)^2]<\infty. For every real k>0, \Pr(|X-\mu|\ge k\sigma)\le k^{-2}. Equivalently, for every a>0, \Pr(|X-\mu|\ge a)\le\sigma^2/a^2.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

From undergraduate integration, add a probability law and its weighted average, then use the displayed mean and variance formulas. The threshold event and equivalence of the two parametrizations are ordinary inequalities. These closely related single-variable definitions fit about 30 minutes without stochastic convergence or construction of measures.

Proof 1: Apply Markov to the squared deviation

Markov's inequality states that for any non-negative real-valued random variable {\displaystyle Y} and any positive number {\displaystyle a}, we have {\displaystyle \Pr(|Y|\geq a)\leq \mathbb {E} [|Y|]/a}. One way to prove Chebyshev's inequality is to apply Markov's inequality to the random variable {\displaystyle Y=(X-\mu )^{2}} with {\displaystyle a=(k\sigma )^{2}}:

{\displaystyle \Pr(|X-\mu |\geq k\sigma )=\Pr((X-\mu )^{2}\geq k^{2}\sigma ^{2})\leq {\frac {\mathbb {E} [(X-\mu )^{2}]}{k^{2}\sigma ^{2}}}={\frac {\sigma ^{2}}{k^{2}\sigma ^{2}}}={\frac {1}{k^{2}}}.}

Setting k=a/\sigma gives the equivalent threshold form.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply Markov to the squared deviation at threshold k²sigma² and cancel its expectation sigma². This is ordinary undergraduate mean, variance and probability algebra.

Proof 2: Split the variance over the tail event — outline

Chebyshev’s inequality can also be proved directly using conditional expectation. A null event contributes zero to the decomposition below; its conditional expectation need not be defined separately:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\sigma ^{2}&=\mathbb {E} {\bigl [}(X-\mu )^{2}{\bigr ]}\\[5pt]&=\mathbb {E} {\Bigl [}(X-\mu )^{2}\;{\Big |}\;k\sigma \leq |X-\mu |{\Bigr ]}\Pr {\bigl [}k\sigma \leq |X-\mu |{\bigr ]}+\mathbb {E} {\Bigl [}(X-\mu )^{2}\;{\Big |}\;k\sigma >|X-\mu |{\Bigr ]}\Pr {\bigl [}k\sigma >|X-\mu |{\bigr ]}\\[5pt]&\geq (k\sigma )^{2}\Pr {\bigl [}k\sigma \leq |X-\mu |{\bigr ]}+0\cdot \Pr {\bigl [}k\sigma >|X-\mu |{\bigr ]}\\[5pt]&=k^{2}\sigma ^{2}\Pr {\bigl [}k\sigma \leq |X-\mu |{\bigr ]}\end{aligned}}}

Chebyshev's inequality then follows by dividing by {\displaystyle k^{2}\sigma ^{2}}.

Setting k=a/\sigma gives the equivalent threshold form.
