Statement

Let w(t) be standard real Brownian motion starting at zero, and let T>0. Then

\mathbb E\exp\!\left(-\frac12\int_0^T w(t)^2\,dt\right)=\frac1{\sqrt{\cosh T}}.

Proof 1: Laplace transform of the Brownian square integral (outline)

Use the stated Riccati expectation formula in its scalar case: for continuous H(t)\ge0 on [0,T],

\mathbb E\exp\!\left(-\int_0^T w(t)^2H(t)\,dt\right)=\exp\!\left(\frac12\int_0^T G(t)\,dt\right),

where G solves G'=2H-G^2, G(T)=0. In the special case of a one-dimensional Brownian motion where H(t)=1/2, the unique solution is G(t)=\tanh(t-T), and we have the formula

\mathbb E\left[\exp\left(-\tfrac12\int_0^T w(t)^2\,dt\right)\right]=\frac1{\sqrt{\cosh T}}.
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Solving the scalar Riccati equation and integrating tanh are ordinary calculus, but the full application concerns expectations of exponential functionals of Brownian paths. Understanding the path integral and stochastic expectation formula requires substantial probability background.
