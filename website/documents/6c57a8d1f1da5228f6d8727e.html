Statement

Let f be entire with f(0)\ne0. Let (a_n) list all its zeros with multiplicity, and suppose \sum_n|a_n|^{-p-1}<\infty for an integer p\ge0. With E_p(w)=(1-w)\exp(\sum_{j=1}^p w^j/j), with the sum empty for p=0, there is an entire function g such that f(z)=e^{g(z)}\prod_n E_p(z/a_n).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Real analysis supplies infinite products, series and derivatives, and complex arithmetic supplies complex exponentials. Holomorphic or entire functions and zero multiplicity are limited extensions; the canonical factors are defined explicitly, so understanding the product identity requires no general factorization theory.

Proof 1: Removing a canonical zero product leaves an entire exponential — source argument

By the canonical product convergence theorem, the canonical product

{\displaystyle P(z)=\prod _{n=1}^{\infty }E_{p}(z/a_{n})}

converges locally uniformly and defines an entire function whose zeros are precisely the zeros {\displaystyle a_{n}}, with the same multiplicities. Thus

{\displaystyle F(z)={\frac {f(z)}{P(z)}}}

is a zero-free entire function. Since the complex plane is simply connected, there is an entire function {\displaystyle g} such that

{\displaystyle F(z)=e^{g(z)}.}

Hence

{\displaystyle f(z)=e^{g(z)}P(z).}
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

This proof uses infinite canonical products with controlled zero multiplicities, holomorphic cancellation at zeros and existence of a global holomorphic logarithm on the simply connected plane. These are linked complex-analysis prerequisites beyond the baseline.
