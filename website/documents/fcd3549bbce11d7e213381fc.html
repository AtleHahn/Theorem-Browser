Statement

Let \mu_n and \mu be Borel probability measures on \mathbb R, and define their characteristic functions by \varphi_n(t)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{itx}\,d\mu_n(x) and \varphi(t)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{itx}\,d\mu(x) for t\in\mathbb R. If \varphi_n(t)\to\varphi(t) for Lebesgue-almost every t, then \mu_n converges weakly to \mu: for every bounded continuous h:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R, \int h\,d\mu_n\to\int h\,d\mu.

Proof 1: Fourier tail bounds and tightness (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorem 1 proof and Lemma 3, through its concluding square; alternatively Corollary 4 proof.).

The dominated-convergence step in Theorem 1 needs only almost-everywhere convergence of the characteristic functions. Lemma 3 then gives tightness; finitely many initial measures can be included by enlarging the compact interval. Every weak subsequential limit has characteristic function equal to \varphi almost everywhere, hence everywhere by continuity, and so equals \mu. The subsequence criterion gives convergence of the whole sequence. Replace t by -t for the source’s Fourier-sign convention.
