Statement

Let X be a real random variable and M(t)=\mathbb E e^{tX}\in(0,\infty]. For every a\in\mathbb R,

\Pr(X\ge a)\le\inf_{t>0}M(t)e^{-ta},\qquad\Pr(X\le a)\le\inf_{t<0}M(t)e^{-ta}.

Each bound M(t)e^{-ta} is valid individually with the indicated sign of t; infinite expectations give a trivial bound.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Undergraduate analysis supplies exponentials, monotonicity and infima. Add expectation of a nonnegative function as its probability-weighted integral, allowing infinity; the two tail events are ordinary inequalities. This coherent single-variable addition fits about 30 minutes. Moment-generating-function structure theory is not needed because M is defined explicitly.

Proof 1: Apply Markov to the exponential

The generic Chernoff bound for a random variable {\displaystyle X} is attained by applying Markov's inequality to {\displaystyle e^{tX}} (which is why it is sometimes called the exponential Markov or exponential moments bound). For positive {\displaystyle t} this gives a bound on the right tail of {\displaystyle X} in terms of its moment-generating function {\displaystyle M(t)=\operatorname {E} (e^{tX})}:

{\displaystyle \operatorname {P} \left(X\geq a\right)=\operatorname {P} \left(e^{tX}\geq e^{ta}\right)\leq M(t)e^{-ta}\qquad (t>0)}

Since this bound holds for every positive {\displaystyle t}, we may take the infimum:

{\displaystyle \operatorname {P} \left(X\geq a\right)\leq \inf _{t>0}M(t)e^{-ta}}

Performing the same analysis with negative {\displaystyle t} we get a similar bound on the left tail:

{\displaystyle \operatorname {P} \left(X\leq a\right)=\operatorname {P} \left(e^{tX}\geq e^{ta}\right)\leq M(t)e^{-ta}\qquad (t<0)}

and

{\displaystyle \operatorname {P} \left(X\leq a\right)\leq \inf _{t<0}M(t)e^{-ta}}
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Monotonicity of exp(tX) identifies the upper-tail event for t>0 and lower-tail event for t<0. Apply Markov and take infima over admissible parameters. This is undergraduate probability and elementary real analysis.
