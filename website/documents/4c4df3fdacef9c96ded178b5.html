Statement

Let U,V\subseteq\mathbb C be disjoint nonempty open sets and D=U\cup V. Define f=0,g=0 on U and f=1,g=2 on V. Then f,g are holomorphic on D and agree on the nonempty open set U, but do not agree throughout D.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Real analysis supplies differentiability and power series; complex linear coordinates supply the plane. Holomorphic means complex differentiable on an open set, with derivatives and series interpreted over the complex numbers. The zero-set or coefficient assertion then uses familiar limits, connectedness and series; no contour-integration theory is needed to understand it.

Proof 1: Failure of analytic identity on a disconnected domain — outline

The connectedness assumption on the domain D in the identity theorem is necessary. For example, if D consists of two disjoint nonempty open sets U,V, f can be 0 on U, and 1 on V, while g is 0 on U, and 2 on V.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Each point has a neighborhood contained in U or V, where both functions are constant. Their complex difference quotients are therefore zero, proving holomorphicity directly; agreement and disagreement follow from the values. From real analysis, elementary complex arithmetic and basic topology, the only new prerequisite is complex differentiability, a small extension. The identity theorem is contextual motivation and is not used to verify the example.
