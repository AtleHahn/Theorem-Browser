Statement

Let A,B be topological spaces, A=X\cup Y, and suppose X,Y are both closed or both open in A. If f:A\to B restricts continuously to X and Y with their subspace topologies, then f is continuous.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Basic topology and real analysis supply covers, continuity, compactness and uniform convergence. Local finiteness and the uniform norm are explicitly described; relative compactness means compact closure in that familiar metric function space.

Mathematical Field
Point-set topology

none

Proof 1: Preimages and unions

Suppose first that X and Y are closed. If U is a closed subset of B, then f^{-1}(U)\cap X and f^{-1}(U)\cap Y are both closed, since each is the preimage under f restricted to X and Y respectively, which by assumption are continuous. These sets are closed in A because they are relatively closed in the closed subsets X and Y. Then their union, f^{-1}(U), is also closed, being a finite union of closed sets.

A similar argument applies when X and Y are both open, using open subsets U of B and the fact that a relatively open subset of an open set is open in A.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use closed-set preimages and finite closed unions, or open preimages and open unions, with the subspace topology. Undergraduate introductory topology suffices.
