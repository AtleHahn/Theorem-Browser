Statement

For a real Lie algebra \mathfrak g, an involutive automorphism \sigma gives the eigenspace decomposition \mathfrak g=\mathfrak h\oplus\mathfrak m, with eigenvalues +1 on \mathfrak h and -1 on \mathfrak m, satisfying

[\mathfrak h,\mathfrak h]\subseteq\mathfrak h,\quad[\mathfrak h,\mathfrak m]\subseteq\mathfrak m,\quad[\mathfrak m,\mathfrak m]\subseteq\mathfrak h.

Conversely, any real vector-space direct-sum decomposition with these bracket properties defines an involutive Lie automorphism by these two signs.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra supplies endomorphisms, common kernels, eigenspaces and bilinear operations. A Lie bracket and its ideal/automorphism conditions are short algebraic definitions; for operators the bracket is the commutator. No representation classification or Lie groups are needed for these elementary invariance claims.

Proof 1: Eigenspace signs and bracket preservation

Since \sigma^2=1, the eigenspaces for the possible eigenvalues \pm1 form a direct sum. The automorphism identity \sigma[X,Y]=[\sigma X,\sigma Y] gives the three bracket containments according to the products of the two signs.

Conversely define \sigma as +1 on \mathfrak h and -1 on \mathfrak m. The three bracket containments imply that it preserves brackets on the three types of pairs, hence on arbitrary pairs by bilinearity. Its square is the identity.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Diagonalize the involution using its square-free polynomial and multiply eigenspace signs through the bilinear bracket. Undergraduate linear algebra suffices with a Lie algebra treated as a vector space with bracket and a bracket-preserving map, a small algebraic extension.
