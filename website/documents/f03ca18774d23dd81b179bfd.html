Statement

Let (X_i)_{i\ge1} be independent identically distributed real random variables with \mathbb E X_i=\mu and 0<\operatorname{Var}(X_i)=\sigma^2<\infty. Put \bar X_n=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n X_i. Let \Phi(z)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}\int_{-\infty}^z e^{-u^2/2}\,du. Then \sup_{z\in\mathbb R}|\mathbb P(\sqrt n(\bar X_n-\mu)\le z)-\Phi(z/\sigma)|\to0.

Proof 1: Use continuity of the limiting distribution function

By the classical central limit theorem, the distribution functions of \sqrt n(\bar X_n-\mu) converge to z\mapsto\Phi(z/\sigma).

The convergence in the central limit theorem is uniform because the limiting cumulative distribution function is continuous. Here we use the theorem that pointwise convergence of distribution functions to a continuous distribution function is uniform.
