Statement

Let X^1,X^2 be real Itô processes driven by the same standard Brownian motion B, with dX^i_t=\mu^i_t\,dt+\sigma^i_t\,dB_t\quad(i=1,2). Then d(X^1_tX^2_t)=(X^2_t\mu^1_t+X^1_t\mu^2_t+\sigma^1_t\sigma^2_t)\,dt+(X^2_t\sigma^1_t+X^1_t\sigma^2_t)\,dB_t.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Itô processes; common Brownian driver; stochastic differentials. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Gradient, Hessian and diffusion-matrix substitution

Let {\displaystyle \mathbf {X} _{t}} be a two-dimensional Ito process with SDE:

{\displaystyle d\mathbf {X} _{t}=d{\begin{pmatrix}X_{t}^{1}\\X_{t}^{2}\end{pmatrix}}={\begin{pmatrix}\mu _{t}^{1}\\\mu _{t}^{2}\end{pmatrix}}dt+{\begin{pmatrix}\sigma _{t}^{1}\\\sigma _{t}^{2}\end{pmatrix}}\,dB_{t}}

Then we can use the multi-dimensional form of Ito's lemma to find an expression for {\displaystyle d(X_{t}^{1}X_{t}^{2})}.

We have {\displaystyle \mu _{t}={\begin{pmatrix}\mu _{t}^{1}\\\mu _{t}^{2}\end{pmatrix}}} and {\displaystyle \mathbf {G} ={\begin{pmatrix}\sigma _{t}^{1}\\\sigma _{t}^{2}\end{pmatrix}}}.

We set {\displaystyle f(t,\mathbf {X} _{t})=X_{t}^{1}X_{t}^{2}} and observe that {\displaystyle {\frac {\partial f}{\partial t}}=0}, {\displaystyle (\nabla _{\mathbf {X} }f)^{T}={\begin{pmatrix}X_{t}^{2}&X_{t}^{1}\end{pmatrix}}}, and {\displaystyle H_{\mathbf {X} }f={\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}}}

Here dX_t^1\,dX_t^2 denotes the quadratic-covariation differential \sigma_t^1\sigma_t^2\,dt.

Substituting these values in the multi-dimensional Itô formula gives us:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}d(X_{t}^{1}X_{t}^{2})&=df(t,\mathbf {X} _{t})\\&=0\cdot dt+{\begin{pmatrix}X_{t}^{2}&X_{t}^{1}\end{pmatrix}}\,d\mathbf {X} _{t}+{\frac {1}{2}}{\begin{pmatrix}dX_{t}^{1}&dX_{t}^{2}\end{pmatrix}}{\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}}{\begin{pmatrix}dX_{t}^{1}\\dX_{t}^{2}\end{pmatrix}}\\[1ex]&=X_{t}^{2}\,dX_{t}^{1}+X_{t}^{1}\,dX_{t}^{2}+dX_{t}^{1}\,dX_{t}^{2}\end{aligned}}}

This is a generalisation of Leibniz's product rule to Ito processes, which need not be differentiable.

Further, using the drift-and-diffusion form of the multidimensional Itô formula gives us

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}d(X_{t}^{1}X_{t}^{2})&=\left\{0+{\begin{pmatrix}X_{t}^{2}&X_{t}^{1}\end{pmatrix}}{\begin{pmatrix}\mu _{t}^{1}\\\mu _{t}^{2}\end{pmatrix}}+{\frac {1}{2}}\operatorname {Tr} \left[{\begin{pmatrix}\sigma _{t}^{1}&\sigma _{t}^{2}\end{pmatrix}}{\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}}{\begin{pmatrix}\sigma _{t}^{1}\\\sigma _{t}^{2}\end{pmatrix}}\right]\right\}dt\\[1ex]&\qquad +\left(X_{t}^{2}\sigma _{t}^{1}+X_{t}^{1}\sigma _{t}^{2}\right)dB_{t}\\[2ex]&=\left(X_{t}^{2}\mu _{t}^{1}+X_{t}^{1}\mu _{t}^{2}+\sigma _{t}^{1}\sigma _{t}^{2}\right)dt+\left(X_{t}^{2}\sigma _{t}^{1}+X_{t}^{1}\sigma _{t}^{2}\right)dB_{t}\end{aligned}}}

so we see that the product {\displaystyle X_{t}^{1}X_{t}^{2}} is itself an Itô drift-diffusion process.
