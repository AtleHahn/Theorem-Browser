Statement

Let f:X\to Y be continuous between CW complexes and let e be an open cell of X. Then f(\overline e), and hence f(e), meets only finitely many open cells of Y.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement concerns continuous maps and closures in general CW topology; its compactness mechanism belongs to the proof.

Proof 1: A continuous cell image meets finitely many cells

Let e=e^n be the cell in the statement. The closure of e^n is compact in X, being the image of the characteristic map of the cell, and hence the image of the closure of e^n under f is also compact in Y. Then it is a general result of CW-complexes that any compact subspace of a CW-complex meets (that is, intersects non-trivially) only finitely many cells of the complex. Thus f(\overline{e^n}), and hence f(e^n), meets at most finitely many cells of Y.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Compactness alone is elementary, but the proof relies on characteristic maps and the finite-cell compactness property of general CW complexes. Retain beyond undergraduate provisionally because the CW topology and cell-closure framework is not a confidently small extension here.
