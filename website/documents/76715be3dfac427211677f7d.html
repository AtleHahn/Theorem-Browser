Statement

Let K be a real closed field with an o-minimal expansion. Let f:A⊂Kn→Kn be a definable C1 map on an open definable set, and assume Df(a) is invertible. Then f has a definable C1 local inverse near a. This includes semialgebraic maps over K.

Level justification

Proof 1: Inverse function theorem over a real closed field — outline

We use the definable mean value inequality and the definable extreme value theorem on closed bounded sets over K. These replace the use of Cauchy completeness in establishing the local image inclusion.

After an affine transformation, assume that the base point and its image are 0 and that f'(0)=I. Choose r>0 such that the closed ball \overline B(0,r) lies in the domain and \|f'(x)-I\|<1/2 on a neighborhood of it. The mean value inequality gives

\|f(x)-f(x')-x+x'\|\leq\frac12\|x-x'\|.

In particular f is injective on B(0,r) and \frac12\|x\|\leq\|f(x)\|.

We shall directly show B(0,r/4)\subset f(B(0,r)). Given a point y in B(0,r/4), consider the function

P(x)=\|f(x)-y\|^2

defined on a neighborhood of \overline B(0,r). If P'(x)=0, then

0=P'(x)=2\bigl[f_1(x)-y_1,\ldots,f_n(x)-y_n\bigr]f'(x),

and so f(x)=y, since f'(x) is invertible.

Now, by the extreme value theorem, P attains a minimum at some point x_0 on the closed ball \overline B(0,r). This point lies in B(0,r): on the boundary,

\|f(x)-y\|\geq r/2-\|y\|>\|y\|,

whereas P(0)=\|y\|^2. Since P'(x_0)=0, f(x_0)=y, which proves the claimed inclusion. Therefore

f:U=B(0,r)\cap f^{-1}(B(0,r/4))\longrightarrow V=B(0,r/4)

is bijective.

We show the inverse g=f^{-1} is continuously differentiable. Let A=f'(x). For x=g(y), write g(y+k)=x+h, or y+k=f(x+h). By the earlier estimate, we have

\|h-k\|=\|f(x+h)-f(x)-h\|\leq\|h\|/2,

and so \|h\|/2\leq\|k\|. Since \|A-I\|<1/2, we have \|Ah\|\geq\|h\|/2. Thus the finite-dimensional square matrix A is invertible and \|A^{-1}\|\leq2. Now

\|g(y+k)-g(y)-A^{-1}k\| =\|h-A^{-1}(f(x+h)-f(x))\| \leq\|A^{-1}\|\,\|Ah-f(x+h)+f(x)\|.

As k\to0, we have h\to0 and \|h\|/\|k\| is bounded. Hence g is differentiable at y with derivative g'(y)=f'(g(y))^{-1}. Also, g' is the composition \iota\circ f'\circ g, where \iota:T\mapsto T^{-1}, so g' is continuous. Undo the initial affine transformation.

The inverse is definable, since its graph is obtained from the definable graph of f|_U by interchanging the two coordinates.
