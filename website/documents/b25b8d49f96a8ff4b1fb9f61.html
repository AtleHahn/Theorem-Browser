Countable nonstandard models of PA cannot have computable arithmetic operations under any numbering of their elements. This adds an effective distinction between the standard natural numbers and their nonstandard first-order models, while not removing the latter's model-theoretic existence.

Statement

Let M be a countable nonstandard model of first-order Peano arithmetic: M satisfies PA but is not isomorphic to the usual natural-number structure. For every bijection b:\mathbb N\to M, neither of the operations

m\oplus n=b^{-1}(b(m)+_M b(n)),\qquad m\otimes n=b^{-1}(b(m)\cdot_M b(n))

is a recursive (computable) function \mathbb N^2\to\mathbb N. Consequently, no countable nonstandard model of PA has a recursive presentation of its arithmetic structure.

Proof 1: Inseparable sets and two arithmetic codings (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Chapters4–5, pp.61–68; Lemma2.8 and Observations2.9–2.13 pp.42–45).

Transport the full structure along any given b:\mathbb N\to M; the resulting structure on \mathbb N is still a nonstandard PA model. The source produces a nonrecursive set X and codes a,c for X and \mathbb N\setminus X. If addition were recursive, searching all labels z for a sum of p(n) copies of z equal to a or c would enumerate both sets. For multiplication, use the codes 2^a,2^c: the equivalence p(n)\mid a\iff\exists z\,z^{p(n)}=2^a follows from Observations2.10–2.13. Each test uses only a finite product of p(n) copies of z. Alternating the two searches decides X, a contradiction in either case. All code labels are fixed constants; neither procedure needs to compute the bijection or the internal exponentiation function.
