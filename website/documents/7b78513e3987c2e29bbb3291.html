Statement

An equitable k-coloring of a finite simple undirected graph assigns one of k colors to every vertex, gives adjacent vertices different colors, and has any two color-class sizes differing by at most one; empty classes are allowed. For every fixed integer k\geq3, deciding whether a finite simple graph has an equitable k-coloring is NP-complete.

Proof 1: Pad a coloring instance with isolated vertices — outline

Reduce ordinary graph k-coloring to equitable k-coloring by adding sufficiently many isolated vertices, that is, vertices incident with no edges. These can fill the color classes to equal sizes. An equitable coloring of the enlarged graph restricts to a proper coloring of the original graph. This reduction gives NP-completeness.
