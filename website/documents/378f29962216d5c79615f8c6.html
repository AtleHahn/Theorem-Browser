Statement

Let A\in F^{n\times n} for a field F and integer n\ge1, with \det A\ne0. Then A^{-1}=(\det A)^{-1}\operatorname{adj}(A), where the adjugate is the transpose of the cofactor matrix.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Undergraduate linear algebra supplies determinants, cofactors, matrices and permutation signs; introductory algebra supplies commutative rings and polynomial coefficients. The displayed formula uses these directly, so moving the entries from a field to a commutative ring is a small extension, with no additional structural theory required.

Proof 1: Column-cofactor left inverse

Fix the index j of a column, and consider that the entries of the other columns have fixed values. This makes the determinant a function of the entries of the jth column. Linearity with respect to this column means that this function has the form

{\displaystyle D_{j}(a_{1,j},\ldots ,a_{n,j})=C_{1,j}a_{1,j}+\cdots +C_{n,j}a_{n,j},}

where the {\displaystyle C_{i,j}} are coefficients that depend on the entries of A that are not in column j. So, one has

{\displaystyle \det(A)=D_{j}(a_{1,j},\ldots ,a_{n,j})=C_{1,j}a_{1,j}+\cdots +C_{n,j}a_{n,j}}

(Laplace expansion provides a formula for computing the {\displaystyle C_{i,j}} but their expression is not important here.)

If the function {\displaystyle D_{j}} is applied to any other column k of A, then the result is the determinant of the matrix obtained from A by replacing column j by a copy of column k, so the resulting determinant is 0 (the case of two equal columns).

Let M be the n × n matrix that has the coefficients of {\displaystyle D_{j}} as jth row, for {\displaystyle j=1,\ldots ,n} (this is the adjugate matrix for A).

The above properties of the functions {\displaystyle D_{j}} show that one has MA = det(A)In, and therefore,

{\displaystyle \left({\frac {1}{\det(A)}}M\right)\,A=I_{n}.}

Thus (\det A)^{-1}M is the inverse of A, since a left inverse of a square matrix is also a right-inverse (see Invertible matrix theorem).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Column multilinearity and repeated-column vanishing give the cofactor matrix product MA=det(A)I. Divide by the nonzero determinant and apply the finite-dimensional fact that a square matrix’s left inverse is also its right inverse. This is ordinary linear algebra over a field; the general field does not introduce any extra structure beyond field arithmetic.
