Statement

Let A,U,C,V be matrices over a unital ring, of sizes n×n,n×k,k×k,k×n respectively. Assume A,C,A+UCV and C^{-1}+VA^{-1}U are invertible. Then (A+UCV)^{-1}=A^{-1}-A^{-1}U(C^{-1}+VA^{-1}U)^{-1}VA^{-1}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory ring/group theory and linear algebra supply polynomial coefficients, ideals, determinants, bases and matrix products. Elementary transvections are explicitly given elementary row-operation matrices, and division ring means every nonzero element has an inverse. These direct definitions suffice without specialist theory.

Proof 1: Multiply by the proposed inverse

The formula can be proven by checking that {\displaystyle (A+UCV)} times its alleged inverse on the right side of the Woodbury identity gives the identity matrix:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}&\left(A+UCV\right)\left[A^{-1}-A^{-1}U\left(C^{-1}+VA^{-1}U\right)^{-1}VA^{-1}\right]\\={}&\left\{I-U\left(C^{-1}+VA^{-1}U\right)^{-1}VA^{-1}\right\}+\left\{UCVA^{-1}-UCVA^{-1}U\left(C^{-1}+VA^{-1}U\right)^{-1}VA^{-1}\right\}\\={}&\left\{I+UCVA^{-1}\right\}-\left\{U\left(C^{-1}+VA^{-1}U\right)^{-1}VA^{-1}+UCVA^{-1}U\left(C^{-1}+VA^{-1}U\right)^{-1}VA^{-1}\right\}\\={}&I+UCVA^{-1}-\left(U+UCVA^{-1}U\right)\left(C^{-1}+VA^{-1}U\right)^{-1}VA^{-1}\\={}&I+UCVA^{-1}-UC\left(C^{-1}+VA^{-1}U\right)\left(C^{-1}+VA^{-1}U\right)^{-1}VA^{-1}\\={}&I+UCVA^{-1}-UCVA^{-1}\\={}&I.\end{aligned}}}
Level

Level justification

Multiply the proposed inverse by A+UCV and collect factors without changing their order. Matrix algebra and elementary ring operations suffice.

Proof 2: Reduced identity and push-through relation

For the discussion below U,V are conformable rectangular matrices and I denotes the identity of the required size; assume all displayed inverses exist.

To derive (I+UV)^{-1}=I-U(I+VU)^{-1}V, combine two identities. For a square matrix P with I+P invertible, the first follows from

{\displaystyle I=(I+P)^{-1}(I+P)=(I+P)^{-1}+(I+P)^{-1}P,}

thus,

{\displaystyle (I+P)^{-1}=I-(I+P)^{-1}P,}

and similarly

{\displaystyle (I+P)^{-1}=I-P(I+P)^{-1}.}

The second identity is the so-called push-through identity

{\displaystyle (I+UV)^{-1}U=U(I+VU)^{-1}}

that we obtain from

{\displaystyle U(I+VU)=(I+UV)U}

after multiplying by {\displaystyle (I+VU)^{-1}} on the right and by {\displaystyle (I+UV)^{-1}} on the left.

Putting all together,

{\displaystyle \left(I+UV\right)^{-1}=I-UV\left(I+UV\right)^{-1}=I-U\left(I+VU\right)^{-1}V.}

where the first and second equality come from the first and second identity, respectively.

To recover the stated formula, replace U by A^{-1}U and V by CV in the reduced identity , and factor A+UCV=A(I+A^{-1}UCV).

Proof 3: Solve a block linear system

Solve the following block matrix inversion problem.

{\displaystyle {\begin{bmatrix}A&U\\V&-C^{-1}\end{bmatrix}}{\begin{bmatrix}X\\Y\end{bmatrix}}={\begin{bmatrix}I\\0\end{bmatrix}}.}

Expanding, we can see that the above reduces to

{\displaystyle {\begin{cases}AX+UY=I\\VX-C^{-1}Y=0\end{cases}}}

which is equivalent to {\displaystyle (A+UCV)X=I}. Eliminating the first equation, we find that {\displaystyle X=A^{-1}(I-UY)}, which can be substituted into the second to find {\displaystyle VA^{-1}(I-UY)=C^{-1}Y}. Expanding and rearranging, we have {\displaystyle VA^{-1}=\left(C^{-1}+VA^{-1}U\right)Y}, or {\displaystyle \left(C^{-1}+VA^{-1}U\right)^{-1}VA^{-1}=Y}. Finally, we substitute into our {\displaystyle AX+UY=I}, and we have {\displaystyle AX+U\left(C^{-1}+VA^{-1}U\right)^{-1}VA^{-1}=I}. Thus, {\displaystyle (A+UCV)^{-1}=X=A^{-1}-A^{-1}U\left(C^{-1}+VA^{-1}U\right)^{-1}VA^{-1}.} We have derived the Woodbury matrix identity.

Proof 4: Compare two block LDU inversions

In this block derivation use D=-C^{-1}, where C is the matrix in the statement.

Start with the block matrix

{\displaystyle {\begin{bmatrix}A&U\\V&D\end{bmatrix}}}

By eliminating the entry under the A (given that A is invertible) we get

{\displaystyle {\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\-VA^{-1}&I\end{bmatrix}}{\begin{bmatrix}A&U\\V&D\end{bmatrix}}={\begin{bmatrix}A&U\\0&D-VA^{-1}U\end{bmatrix}}}

Likewise, eliminating the entry above D gives

{\displaystyle {\begin{bmatrix}A&U\\V&D\end{bmatrix}}{\begin{bmatrix}I&-A^{-1}U\\0&I\end{bmatrix}}={\begin{bmatrix}A&0\\V&D-VA^{-1}U\end{bmatrix}}}

Now combining the above two, we get

{\displaystyle {\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\-VA^{-1}&I\end{bmatrix}}{\begin{bmatrix}A&U\\V&D\end{bmatrix}}{\begin{bmatrix}I&-A^{-1}U\\0&I\end{bmatrix}}={\begin{bmatrix}A&0\\0&D-VA^{-1}U\end{bmatrix}}}

Moving to the right side gives

{\displaystyle {\begin{bmatrix}A&U\\V&D\end{bmatrix}}={\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\VA^{-1}&I\end{bmatrix}}{\begin{bmatrix}A&0\\0&D-VA^{-1}U\end{bmatrix}}{\begin{bmatrix}I&A^{-1}U\\0&I\end{bmatrix}}}

which is the LDU decomposition of the block matrix into lower triangular, diagonal, and upper triangular matrices. Now inverting both sides gives

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}{\begin{bmatrix}A&U\\V&D\end{bmatrix}}^{-1}&={\begin{bmatrix}I&A^{-1}U\\0&I\end{bmatrix}}^{-1}{\begin{bmatrix}A&0\\0&D-VA^{-1}U\end{bmatrix}}^{-1}{\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\VA^{-1}&I\end{bmatrix}}^{-1}\\[8pt]&={\begin{bmatrix}I&-A^{-1}U\\0&I\end{bmatrix}}{\begin{bmatrix}A^{-1}&0\\0&\left(D-VA^{-1}U\right)^{-1}\end{bmatrix}}{\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\-VA^{-1}&I\end{bmatrix}}\\[8pt]&={\begin{bmatrix}A^{-1}+A^{-1}U\left(D-VA^{-1}U\right)^{-1}VA^{-1}&-A^{-1}U\left(D-VA^{-1}U\right)^{-1}\\-\left(D-VA^{-1}U\right)^{-1}VA^{-1}&\left(D-VA^{-1}U\right)^{-1}\end{bmatrix}}\qquad \mathrm {(1)} \end{aligned}}}

We could equally well have done it the other way (provided that D is invertible) i.e.

{\displaystyle {\begin{bmatrix}A&U\\V&D\end{bmatrix}}={\begin{bmatrix}I&UD^{-1}\\0&I\end{bmatrix}}{\begin{bmatrix}A-UD^{-1}V&0\\0&D\end{bmatrix}}{\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\D^{-1}V&I\end{bmatrix}}}

Now again inverting both sides,

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}{\begin{bmatrix}A&U\\V&D\end{bmatrix}}^{-1}&={\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\D^{-1}V&I\end{bmatrix}}^{-1}{\begin{bmatrix}A-UD^{-1}V&0\\0&D\end{bmatrix}}^{-1}{\begin{bmatrix}I&UD^{-1}\\0&I\end{bmatrix}}^{-1}\\[8pt]&={\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\-D^{-1}V&I\end{bmatrix}}{\begin{bmatrix}\left(A-UD^{-1}V\right)^{-1}&0\\0&D^{-1}\end{bmatrix}}{\begin{bmatrix}I&-UD^{-1}\\0&I\end{bmatrix}}\\[8pt]&={\begin{bmatrix}\left(A-UD^{-1}V\right)^{-1}&-\left(A-UD^{-1}V\right)^{-1}UD^{-1}\\-D^{-1}V\left(A-UD^{-1}V\right)^{-1}&D^{-1}+D^{-1}V\left(A-UD^{-1}V\right)^{-1}UD^{-1}\end{bmatrix}}\qquad \mathrm {(2)} \end{aligned}}}

Now comparing elements (1, 1) of the RHS of (1) and (2) above gives the Woodbury formula

{\displaystyle \left(A-UD^{-1}V\right)^{-1}=A^{-1}+A^{-1}U\left(D-VA^{-1}U\right)^{-1}VA^{-1}.}

Substituting D=-C^{-1} in the last identity gives the displayed Woodbury identity.
