Statement

Let V,W be strictly convex real normed spaces: their unit spheres contain no nontrivial line segment. Suppose f:V\to W is an isometry, meaning \|f(u)-f(v)\|_W=\|u-v\|_V for all u,v\in V; surjectivity is not assumed. Then f(tu+(1-t)v)=tf(u)+(1-t)f(v) for every u,v\in V and t\in[0,1].

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Normed spaces and isometries combine linear algebra with metric geometry. Strict convexity is explicitly no nontrivial unit-sphere segment; the conclusion is preservation of ordinary convex combinations.

Proof 1: Uniqueness in the intersection of tangent balls — outline

For strictly convex spaces the result is true, and easy, even for isometries which are not necessarily surjective. In this case, for any u and v in V, and for any t in [0,1], write

r=\|u-v\|_V=\|f(u)-f(v)\|_W

and denote the closed ball of radius R around v by \bar B(v,R). Then tu+(1-t)v is the unique element of \bar B(v,tr)\cap\bar B(u,(1-t)r), so, since f is injective, f(tu+(1-t)v) is the unique element of

f\bigl(\bar B(v,tr)\cap\bar B(u,(1-t)r)\bigr) =f\bigl(\bar B(v,tr)\bigr)\cap f\bigl(\bar B(u,(1-t)r)\bigr) \subseteq\bar B(f(v),tr)\cap\bar B(f(u),(1-t)r).

The inclusion follows from preservation of distances. The last intersection also has a unique element, namely tf(u)+(1-t)f(v), and therefore f(tu+(1-t)v) is equal to it. The cases u=v or t\in\{0,1\} give the identity directly.
