Statement

Let Y be a Hausdorff locally convex topological vector space over \mathbb R or \mathbb C. Suppose every continuous linear map from a vector subspace M of any locally convex space X into Y extends continuously and linearly to X. Then Y is complete.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Extension properties and completion for arbitrary locally convex spaces are functional analysis prerequisites.

Proof 1: Extend the identity to the completion outline

Let Z be the Hausdorff completion of Y, in which Y is a dense topological vector subspace. The completion of a locally convex space is locally convex. Apply the extension property to the identity map on Y to obtain a continuous linear map F:Z\to Y that restricts to that identity. Compose F with the inclusion j:Y\hookrightarrow Z. The continuous maps jF and \mathrm{id}_Z agree on the dense subspace Y and have Hausdorff target, so they agree on all of Z. Hence every point of Z lies in Y, and Y=Z is complete.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof uses the Hausdorff completion of a general locally convex topological vector space. Nonmetrizable completeness and its extension structure exceed the metric/normed-space undergraduate setting.
