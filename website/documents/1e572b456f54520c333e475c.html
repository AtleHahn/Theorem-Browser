Statement

For A\subseteq\mathbb N_{>0} put A(n)=|A\cap\{1,\ldots,n\}| and \sigma(A)=\inf_{n\geq1}A(n)/n. For subsets of positive integers, write A\oplus B for the positive elements of (A\cup\{0\})+(B\cup\{0\}); repeated sums likewise allow zero summands. For l\geq1 and A_1,\ldots,A_l\subseteq\mathbb N_{>0}, \sigma\!\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^l A_i\right)\geq1-\prod_{i=1}^l(1-\sigma(A_i)).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Counts of positive integers; Infima of bounded real sequences; finite sumsets allowing zero summands; finite products. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Iterate the complementary-density product — outline

The two-set theorem gives \sigma(A\oplus B)\geq\sigma(A)+\sigma(B)-\sigma(A)\sigma(B)=1-(1-\sigma(A))(1-\sigma(B)). Iterating this identity by induction gives the displayed finite-product bound.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Iterate the explicitly stated two-set inequality by induction and multiply complementary densities. Undergraduate real infima and elementary finite sumsets suffice; no proof of the density theorem is invoked.
