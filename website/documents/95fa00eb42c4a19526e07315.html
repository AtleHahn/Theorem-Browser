Statement

Let A=(\forall x_1\cdots x_k)(\exists y_1\cdots y_m)\theta(x_1,\ldots,x_k,y_1,\ldots,y_m), with k,m\ge1 and quantifier-free \theta in a relational language without equality, functions or constants. Enumerate tuples of positive integers by increasing sum, then lexicographically within each sum, writing the nth tuple as (a_1^n,\ldots,a_k^n). For fresh variables z_1,z_2,\ldots, put

B_n=\theta(z_{a_1^n},\ldots,z_{a_k^n},z_{(n-1)m+2},\ldots,z_{nm+1}),\quad D_n=(\exists z_1\cdots z_{nm+1})\bigwedge_{j=1}^n B_j.

For every positive integer n, the implication A\to D_n is provable in classical first-order logic.

Proof 1: Induction introducing fresh witnesses — outline

By induction on n. For n\ge2, the ordering of the tuples is such that

a_i^n<(n-1)m+2\qquad(1\le i\le k).

Consequently, the universal arguments of B_n use only the previously available variables, and variable substitution in A gives

\begin{aligned} D_{n-1}\wedge A &\ \longrightarrow\ D_{n-1}\wedge (\forall z_1\cdots z_{(n-1)m+1}) (\exists z_{(n-1)m+2}\cdots z_{nm+1})B_n\\ &\ \longrightarrow\ D_n. \end{aligned}

The last implication chooses the new existential witnesses after the witnesses in D_{n-1} have been chosen.

For the base case,

\begin{aligned} D_1 &=(\exists z_1\cdots z_{m+1}) \theta(z_{a_1^1},\ldots,z_{a_k^1},z_2,\ldots,z_{m+1})\\ &=(\exists z_1\cdots z_{m+1}) \theta(z_1,\ldots,z_1,z_2,\ldots,z_{m+1}) \end{aligned}

is a corollary of A as well. So A\to D_n is provable for every n.

The elementary tuple-index bound is left implicit in the source.
