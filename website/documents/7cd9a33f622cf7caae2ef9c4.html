Statement

For a metric space (M,d) and fixed z\in M, the map x\mapsto d(z,x) is 1-Lipschitz and uniformly continuous. In particular the norm map on a normed vector space is 1-Lipschitz for the metric d(u,v)=\|u-v\|.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra supplies norms, inner products and scalar-valued linear maps; analysis supplies suprema and norm closure. Seminorm or cyclic-vector conditions are explicitly given by norm inequalities or the closed span of iterates. These direct definitions suffice without spectral or weak-topology theory.

Proof 1: Apply the reverse bound at a fixed point

For metric spaces, the proof of the reverse triangle inequality is found similarly by:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}d(A,B)+d(B,C)&\geq d(A,C)\\[4pt]\Rightarrow \quad d(A,B)&\geq d(A,C)-d(B,C)\\[10pt]d(C,A)+d(A,B)&\geq d(C,B)\\[4pt]\Rightarrow \quad d(A,B)&\geq d(B,C)-d(A,C)\end{aligned}}}

Putting these equations together we find:

{\displaystyle d(A,B)\geq |d(A,C)-d(B,C)|}

This implies that the norm {\displaystyle \|\cdot \|} as well as the distance-from-z function d(z, ·) are Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constant 1, and therefore are in particular uniformly continuous.

Here A=x, B=y, C=z gives the distance-from-z bound. Given \varepsilon>0, choose \delta=\varepsilon. In a normed space \|x\|=d(0,x) for the metric d(u,v)=\|u-v\|.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the metric triangle inequality in two orders, combine the resulting bounds, and take delta equal to epsilon. Metric spaces, normed spaces and uniform continuity are introductory analysis concepts.
