Statement

For a real symmetric n\times n matrix A, let n_+(A),n_-(A),n_0(A) be its numbers of positive, negative and zero eigenvalues, counted with multiplicity. For any two real symmetric matrices A,B of the same size, there exists an invertible real matrix P such that

B=PAP^{\mathsf T}

if and only if

(n_+(A),n_-(A),n_0(A))=(n_+(B),n_-(B),n_0(B)).

Equivalently, every real symmetric matrix is congruent to a diagonal matrix whose diagonal consists of n_+(A) entries 1, n_-(A) entries -1, and n_0(A) entries 0, and these three counts are independent of the diagonalizing change of basis. Here P^{\mathsf T} denotes transpose and congruence means a transformation of the displayed form. Singular matrices are included.

Proof 1: Signed diagonalization and subspace dimensions (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Sylvester’s law of inertia, pp.13–15; Proposition3.4 pp.12–13).

In an orthogonal eigenbasis, rescale the ith coordinate by |\lambda_i|^{-1/2} when \lambda_i\ne0, and by 1 when \lambda_i=0. The resulting matrix R is invertible and R^{\mathsf T}AR is the signed diagonal form. If R_A^{\mathsf T}AR_A=D=R_B^{\mathsf T}BR_B, then P=R_B^{-\mathsf T}R_A^{\mathsf T} satisfies B=PAP^{\mathsf T}. Uniqueness follows from the source’s positive-subspace intersection argument and rank invariance, so singular matrices are included.
