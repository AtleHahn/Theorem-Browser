Statement

A chain is a set of pairwise comparable elements, and an antichain has no distinct comparable elements. For an integer N\ge1, order \{1,\ldots,N\} by divisibility. Its largest chain size and minimum antichain-partition size are both 1+\lfloor\log_2N\rfloor.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Comparability, chains and antichains are defined for a finite list of integers. Divisibility, powers of two, logarithms and floor suffice to understand both finite counts.

Proof 1: Powers-of-two chain and dyadic antichain blocks

In the set of positive integers from 1 to N, ordered by divisibility, one of the largest chains consists of the powers of two that lie within that range, from which it follows that the height of this partial order is 1+\lfloor\log_2N\rfloor.

The numbers can be partitioned into the antichains \{1\}, \{2,3\}, \{4,5,6,7\}, etc., with the last block cut off at N. There are 1+\lfloor\log_2N\rfloor sets in this partition, and within each of these sets, the ratio of the larger of two distinct numbers to the smaller is less than two, so no two distinct numbers within one of these sets can be divisible.

In such a partition, every two elements of the longest chain must go into two different antichains, so the number of antichains is always greater than or equal to the height. The displayed chain and partition therefore attain both bounds.

High school only
Level justification

The powers-of-two chain and the dyadic antichain blocks achieve matching counts. Distinct numbers in one block have ratio below two, precluding divisibility. Elementary integers, logarithms and finite counting suffice.
