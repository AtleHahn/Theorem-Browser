Statement

Every finite simple graph on n\ge3 vertices with degree at least n/2 at every vertex has a Hamiltonian cycle. A finite simple graph here has a finite vertex set and edges that are unordered pairs of distinct vertices, with no repeated edges; the degree of a vertex is the number of its incident edges. A Hamiltonian cycle visits every vertex exactly once and returns to the initial vertex.

Proof 1: Degree-sum comparison

Ore’s theorem states that a finite simple graph on n\ge3 vertices with degree sum at least n for every nonadjacent pair has a Hamiltonian cycle.

Ore's theorem is a generalization of Dirac's theorem that, when each vertex has degree at least n/2, the graph is Hamiltonian. For, if a graph meets Dirac's condition, then clearly each pair of vertices has degrees adding to at least n.

Proof 2: Bound each missing pair’s degree sum — outline

For a finite simple undirected graph G on n\ge3 vertices, its closure is obtained by repeatedly adding an edge between nonadjacent vertices whose current degrees sum to at least n, until no such pair remains. Every nonadjacent pair has degree sum at least n, so every missing edge is eligible in the closure procedure, and degrees only increase. Thus the closure is complete. It is Hamiltonian, and the Bondy–Chvátal equivalence gives Hamiltonicity of G.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The finite edge-addition procedure is defined locally. Every missing edge qualifies, yielding a complete graph; apply the stated closure equivalence and an elementary cycle through all vertices.
