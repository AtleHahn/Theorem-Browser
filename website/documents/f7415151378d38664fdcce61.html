Statement

Assume the axiom of choice. Let S be the unit circle and G the group of rotations through rational multiples of \pi. There is an uncountable set X\subseteq S containing exactly one point of each G-orbit, and the sets gX for g\in G form a countable partition of S into pairwise congruent sets.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Group actions, countability and partitions are familiar undergraduate concepts. A transversal chooses one point per orbit, and choice is an explicit hypothesis. The rotations are concrete, so no measurable selection or topology of quotient groups is involved.

Proof 1: Select one representative per rational-rotation orbit — outline

Let ⁠{\displaystyle S}⁠ be the unit circle, and ⁠{\displaystyle G}⁠ be the group consisting of all rational rotations (i.e., rotations by angles which are rational multiples of ⁠{\displaystyle \pi }⁠). Since ⁠{\displaystyle G}⁠ is countable while ⁠{\displaystyle S}⁠ is uncountable, ⁠{\displaystyle S}⁠ must break up into uncountably many orbits under the action of ⁠{\displaystyle G}⁠.

Using the axiom of choice, we could pick a single point from each orbit, obtaining an uncountable subset ⁠{\displaystyle X}⁠ of ⁠{\displaystyle S}⁠. The set of translates gX, for g\in G, partitions the circle into a countable collection of pairwise disjoint sets, which are all pairwise congruent.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use countability of rotation orbits and choice of one representative per orbit, then check disjoint translates. Undergraduate group actions and countability suffice; the choice principle is used as an explicit selection rule, not through axiomatic set-theoretic machinery.
