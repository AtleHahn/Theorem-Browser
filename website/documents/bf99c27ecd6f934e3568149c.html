Statement

A positive integer is perfect if the sum of its positive proper divisors equals itself. An even positive integer N is perfect if and only if N=2^{p-1}(2^p-1) for an integer p\ge2 for which 2^p-1 is prime.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Perfectness is defined by a finite sum of positive divisors; the formula uses only integer powers and primality.

Proof 1: Multiply coprime divisor sums and exhaust the odd factor divisors

Euler's proof is short and depends on the fact that the sum of divisors function σ is multiplicative; that is, if a and b are any two relatively prime integers, then σ(ab) = σ(a)σ(b). For this formula to be valid, the sum of divisors of a number must include the number itself, not just the proper divisors. A number is perfect if and only if its sum of divisors is twice its value.

Sufficiency.

One direction of the theorem (the part already proved by Euclid) immediately follows from the multiplicative property: every Mersenne prime gives rise to an even perfect number. When 2p − 1 is prime, {\displaystyle \sigma (2^{p-1}(2^{p}-1))=\sigma (2^{p-1})\sigma (2^{p}-1).} The divisors of 2p−1 are 1, 2, 4, 8, ..., 2p−1. The sum of these divisors is a geometric series whose sum is 2p − 1. Next, since 2p − 1 is prime, its only divisors are 1 and itself, so the sum of its divisors is 2p.

Combining these,

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\sigma (2^{p-1}(2^{p}-1))&=\sigma (2^{p-1})\sigma (2^{p}-1)\\&=(2^{p}-1)(2^{p})\\&=2(2^{p-1})(2^{p}-1).\end{aligned}}}

Therefore, 2p−1(2p − 1) is perfect.

Necessity.

In the other direction, suppose that an even perfect number has been given, and partially factor it as 2kx, where k\ge1 and x is odd. For 2kx to be perfect, the sum of its divisors must be twice its value:

2^{k+1}x=\sigma(2^kx)=(2^{k+1}-1)\sigma(x).

The odd factor 2k+1 − 1 on the right side of the displayed identity is at least 3, and it must divide x, the only odd factor on the left side, so x/(2k+1 − 1) is a proper divisor of x. Dividing both sides of the displayed identity by the common factor 2k+1 − 1 and taking into account the known divisors x and x/(2k+1 − 1) of x gives

{\displaystyle 2^{k+1}x/(2^{k+1}-1)=\sigma (x)=x+x/(2^{k+1}-1)+{}}other divisors{\displaystyle {}=2^{k+1}x/(2^{k+1}-1)+{}}other divisors.

For this equality to be true, there can be no other divisors. Therefore, x/(2k+1 − 1) must be 1, and x must be a prime of the form 2k+1 − 1.
