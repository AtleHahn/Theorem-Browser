Statement

Work in the real projective geometric algebra \mathbb R_{3,0,1} of three-dimensional Euclidean space. Let B_1,B_2 be commuting bivectors with B_1^2=0, B_2^2=-1, and B_1B_2 purely of grade four. For real \alpha,\beta, put T=1+\alpha B_1, R=\cos\beta+B_2\sin\beta, and S=TR. Write \langle S\rangle_j for the grade-j component. If V=\langle S\rangle_2 is invertible and Q=\langle S\rangle_4, then T=1+QV^{-1} and R=ST^{-1}=T^{-1}S.

Proof 1: Use the nilpotent factor in the bivector component — outline

We simply write out {\displaystyle S} and consider the result grade-by-grade:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}S&=TR\\&=(1+\alpha B_1)(\cos\beta+B_2\sin\beta)\\&=\underbrace {\cos \beta } _{\text{scalar}}+\underbrace {\sin \beta B_{2}+\alpha \cos \beta B_{1}} _{\text{bivector}}+\underbrace {\alpha \sin \beta B_{1}B_{2}} _{\text{quadrivector}}\end{aligned}}}

Because the quadrivector part \langle S\rangle_4=\langle T\rangle_2\langle R\rangle_2 and B_1^2=0, T is directly found to be

T=1+QV^{-1},

where V=\langle S\rangle_2 is invertible and Q=\langle S\rangle_4. Indeed, multiplication of the grade-two formula gives \alpha B_1V=Q. Thus

R=ST^{-1}=T^{-1}S,

with T^{-1}=1-\alpha B_1.
