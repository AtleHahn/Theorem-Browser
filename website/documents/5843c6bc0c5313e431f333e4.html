Statement

Let P be a set of n\ge3 distinct points in a projective plane, not all on one line. Let \mathcal L(P) be the set of distinct lines containing at least two points of P, and put t=|\mathcal L(P)|. Then t\ge n.

If t=n, every two distinct lines in \mathcal L(P) meet at a point of P. Moreover, either P, together with the lines \ell\cap P for \ell\in\mathcal L(P) and their inherited incidences, forms a finite projective plane, or P is a near-pencil: exactly n-1 of its points lie on one line.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding an abstract projective plane and its induced incidence configuration is a small extension of undergraduate sets and elementary geometry: unique joining lines and intersections suffice to interpret the count and equality alternatives. The needed incidence definitions and finite examples fit the conservative thirty-minute allowance; no coordinate fields or proof of the classification are prerequisites.
