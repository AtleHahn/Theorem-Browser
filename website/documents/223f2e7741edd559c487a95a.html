Statement

Let q be a prime power and let \mathbb F_q be the field with q elements. A polynomial f\in\mathbb F_q[x] is exceptional if the map x\mapsto f(x) is a bijection of \mathbb F_{q^m} for infinitely many positive integers m.

If f has degree d\ge1 and q>d^4, then f is exceptional if and only if the map x\mapsto f(x) is a bijection of \mathbb F_q.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the conclusion requires extension fields and the infinite family of permutation conditions, not just polynomial evaluation on one finite set.

Proof 1: Collision curves and the Weil bound (external)

The following editorial argument completes the stated scope. A supporting source is Exceptional polynomials (Handbook of Finite Fields, Section 8.4), with the collision-curve argument of Chahal–Ghorpade (Theorems 8.4.4 and 8.4.19, Remark 8.4.20, printed pp.229,231; conservative one-component proof supplied given below.).

Zieve, https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/zieve/wp-content/uploads/sites/822/2026/03/epfacts.pdf, Theorem 8.4.19 and Remark 8.4.20, confirms the exact threshold and identifies the erroneous stronger claims. The following additional argument uses his factor criterion 8.4.4 as a standard dependency. Remove a Frobenius power f=g^(p^a); this preserves bijectivity on every finite extension and reduces degree. If g is not exceptional, its collision polynomial has an absolutely irreducible Fq component other than the diagonal, of degree e<=d-1. On its smooth projective normalization, genus <=(e-1)(e-2)/2. Hasse–Weil gives at least q+1-(e-1)(e-2)sqrt(q) rational points. Discard at most (e-1)(e-2) branches above singular points (sum of branch counts <=2 times arithmetic genus), at most e points above infinity, and at most e intersections with the diagonal. Since sqrt(q)>d squared, q+1-(d-2)(d-3)sqrt(q)-(d-2)(d-3)-2(d-1)>0 for d>=2. A remaining rational point yields a forbidden collision. Degree 1 is immediate. Conversely a collision in Fq persists in every extension, so exceptionality implies permutation of Fq. This conservative bridge needs only standard normalization/genus and Hasse–Weil facts, not the source’s sharper union estimate.
