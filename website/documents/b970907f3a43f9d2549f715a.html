Statement

Let n\ge2 be an integer, let e_1,\ldots,e_n be the standard orthonormal basis of \mathbb R^n, and let a_1,\ldots,a_n be nonzero real numbers. Let S be the convex hull of 0,a_1e_1,\ldots,a_ne_n. Let F_0 be its facet opposite 0, and for i=1,\ldots,n let F_i be its facet opposite a_ie_i; a facet is the convex hull of all vertices except the indicated vertex. Writing \operatorname{vol}_{n-1} for ordinary intrinsic facet volume, \operatorname{vol}_{n-1}(F_0)^2=\sum_{i=1}^n\operatorname{vol}_{n-1}(F_i)^2.

Proof 1: Specialize the coordinate-projection identity — outline

Use the Conant–Beyer theorem: let U be a measurable subset of a k-dimensional affine subspace of \mathbb R^n. For any subset I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\} with exactly k elements, let U_I be the orthogonal projection of U onto the linear span of the coordinate vectors indexed by I. Then

\operatorname{vol}_k(U)^2=\sum_I\operatorname{vol}_k(U_I)^2.

The generalisation of de Gua's theorem to n-simplices with right-angle corners corresponds to the special case where k=n-1 and U is an (n-1)-simplex in \mathbb R^n with vertices on the coordinate axes. Here take U=F_0; its coordinate projections are the facets F_1,\ldots,F_n.
