Proof 1: Gaussian integral on the positive half-line — outline

Apply the source’s squaring identity to the nonnegative Gaussian and take the positive square root after the calculation.

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\left[\int _{0}^{\infty }\exp(-x^{2})\,\mathrm {d} x\right]^{2}&=\int _{0}^{1}\int _{0}^{\infty }2x\exp(-x^{2})\exp(-x^{2}y^{2})\,\mathrm {d} x\,\mathrm {d} y\\&=\int _{0}^{1}\int _{0}^{\infty }2x\exp \left[-x^{2}(y^{2}+1)\right]\,\mathrm {d} x\,\mathrm {d} y\\&=\int _{0}^{1}\left\{-{\frac {1}{y^{2}+1}}\exp \left[-x^{2}(y^{2}+1)\right]\right\}_{x=0}^{x=\infty }\,\mathrm {d} y\\&=\int _{0}^{1}{\frac {1}{y^{2}+1}}\,\mathrm {d} y\\&=\arctan(1)={\frac {\pi }{4}}\end{aligned}}}{\displaystyle \int _{0}^{\infty }\exp(-x^{2})\,\mathrm {d} x={\frac {\sqrt {\pi }}{2}}}
