Statement

Every finite undirected 4-regular graph has its edges partitioned into two 2-factors, where a 2-factor is a spanning subgraph with degree two at every vertex.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Degree four and degree-two spanning subgraphs are finite edge-count conditions, with the latter defined locally. Partitioning edges into two such sets needs elementary finite graph reasoning.

Proof 1: Alternating edges on an Eulerian circuit (outline)

It is sufficient to consider connected graphs, applying the construction to each component. A 4-regular graph can be factorized into two 2-factors by taking alternate edges in an Eulerian circuit.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Use the supplied Euler circuit and alternate its edges. The circuit length is even since the total degree is four times the vertex count, and each entrance-exit pair has one edge of each color. Only finite graph counting is required.
