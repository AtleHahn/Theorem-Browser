Statement

Let ABC be a nondegenerate triangle in the Euclidean plane. Choose points A', B', and C' on the lines BC, CA, and AB, respectively, allowing extensions of the sides. Suppose each of AB'C', A'BC', and A'B'C is a nondegenerate triangle, so that its circumcircle, the unique circle through its three vertices, is defined.

The circumcircles of these three triangles have exactly one common point, called the Miquel point.

High school only

Proof 1: Complex cross ratios and reflection, with a tangency limit (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (miquel_pivot, reflect_line_dist1/2 and miquel_concurrent, lines265–533).

Write p=A', q=B', r=C'. The linked concurrency theorem treats the nontangent case. For tangency, fix A,B,C,p,r and vary q slightly along CA. At most one admissible q makes the first circle tangent to the fixed second circle at r: its center must lie both on the line from r to the second center and on the perpendicular bisector of Ar, which have at most one intersection. That circle meets CA at A and at most one other point. Choose nontangent perturbations tending to q. Circumcenters vary continuously, and the first two centers remain distinct, since equal circles would contain the three distinct collinear points A,r,B. The source’s reflection formula therefore has a limit lying on all three limiting circles.

For uniqueness, if two distinct points belonged to all three circles, their pairwise radical axes would be the same line. It would contain p,q,r. The first circle then forces the two common points to be precisely q,r, while the second forces them to be p,r, contradicting the distinctness of p,q,r. This argument includes side extensions.

The linked argument uses planar complex arithmetic, the real cross-ratio criterion for a circle, reflection and linear equations for circumcenters. The tangency extension takes limits only of explicit rational formulas with nonzero denominators, using elementary algebraic error bounds. Circle-equation subtraction gives uniqueness. No complex analysis, differentiation or general topological theorem is needed.
