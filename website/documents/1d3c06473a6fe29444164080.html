Statement

Let three finite circles, pairwise externally tangent and tangent to the same line in a generalized Descartes configuration, have curvatures k_1,k_2,k_4>0, with k_1\ge k_2. Then k_4=k_1+k_2\pm2\sqrt{k_1k_2},\qquad \sqrt{k_4}=\sqrt{k_1}\pm\sqrt{k_2}. The sign is the one corresponding to the given circle.

Proof 1: Zero-curvature substitution and square roots

For four mutually tangent generalized circles with distinct tangencies, signed curvatures satisfy (k_1+k_2+k_3+k_4)^2=2(k_1^2+k_2^2+k_3^2+k_4^2); a line has curvature zero, an enclosing circle has curvature -1/r, and the other circles have curvature 1/r.

If one of the three circles is replaced by a straight line tangent to the remaining circles, then its curvature is zero and drops out of the Descartes identity. For instance, if {\displaystyle k_{3}=0}, then the Descartes identity can be factorized as

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}&{\bigl (}{\sqrt {k_{1}}}+{\sqrt {k_{2}}}+{\sqrt {k_{4}}}{\bigr )}{\bigl (}{{\sqrt {k_{2}}}+{\sqrt {k_{4}}}-{\sqrt {k_{1}}}}{\bigr )}\\[3mu]&\quad {}\cdot {\bigl (}{\sqrt {k_{1}}}+{\sqrt {k_{4}}}-{\sqrt {k_{2}}}{\bigr )}{\bigl (}{\sqrt {k_{1}}}+{\sqrt {k_{2}}}-{\sqrt {k_{4}}}{\bigr )}=0,\end{aligned}}}

and the quadratic formula for the fourth curvature simplifies to

{\displaystyle k_{4}=k_{1}+k_{2}\pm 2{\sqrt {k_{1}k_{2}}}.}

Taking the square root of both sides leads to another alternative formulation of this case (with {\displaystyle k_{1}\geq k_{2}}),

{\displaystyle {\sqrt {k_{4}}}={\sqrt {k_{1}}}\pm {\sqrt {k_{2}}}.}
