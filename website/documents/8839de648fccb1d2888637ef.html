Statement

Let (M,g) be a smooth Riemannian manifold, A a smooth vector field and x^1,\ldots,x^n a smooth local coordinate chart. Write A=A^i\partial/\partial x^i and let (g_{ij}) be the positive-definite metric matrix. Summation is understood over repeated indices. The function \operatorname{div}A=(\det g)^{-1/2}\partial_{x^i}(A^i\sqrt{\det g}) is independent of the choice of smooth local coordinates.

Proof 1: Metric transformation and cofactor differentiation

Let {\displaystyle (x^{1},x^{2},\ldots ,x^{n})\mapsto ({\bar {x}}^{1},\ldots ,{\bar {x}}^{n})} be a coordinate transformation with non-singular Jacobian. Then the classical transformation laws imply that {\displaystyle A={\bar {A}}^{k}{\frac {\partial }{\partial {\bar {x}}^{k}}}} where {\displaystyle {\bar {A}}^{k}={\frac {\partial {\bar {x}}^{k}}{\partial x^{j}}}A^{j}}. Similarly, if {\displaystyle g=g_{mk}\,dx^{m}\otimes dx^{k}={\bar {g}}_{ij}\,d{\bar {x}}^{i}\otimes d{\bar {x}}^{j}}, then {\displaystyle {\bar {g}}_{ij}=\,{\frac {\partial x^{m}}{\partial {\bar {x}}^{i}}}{\frac {\partial x^{k}}{\partial {\bar {x}}^{j}}}g_{mk}}. Writing this transformation law in terms of matrices yields {\displaystyle {\bar {g}}=\left({\frac {\partial x}{\partial {\bar {x}}}}\right)^{\text{T}}g\left({\frac {\partial x}{\partial {\bar {x}}}}\right)}, which implies {\displaystyle \det {\bar {g}}=\left(\det \left({\frac {\partial x}{\partial {\bar {x}}}}\right)\right)^{2}\det g}.

The sign of the nonzero Jacobian determinant \det(\partial x/\partial\bar x) is locally constant. Although the square root of the metric determinant transforms with its absolute value, this sign cancels between the outer factor and the factor inside the derivative in the following calculation.

Now one computes

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\operatorname {div} A&={\frac {1}{\sqrt {\det g}}}{\frac {\partial }{\partial x^{i}}}\left(A^{i}{\sqrt {\det g}}\right)\\&=\det \left({\frac {\partial x}{\partial {\bar {x}}}}\right){\frac {1}{\sqrt {\det {\bar {g}}}}}{\frac {\partial {\bar {x}}^{k}}{\partial x^{i}}}{\frac {\partial }{\partial {\bar {x}}^{k}}}\left({\frac {\partial x^{i}}{\partial {\bar {x}}^{\ell }}}{\bar {A}}^{\ell }\det \!\left({\frac {\partial x}{\partial {\bar {x}}}}\right)^{\!\!-1}\!{\sqrt {\det {\bar {g}}}}\right).\end{aligned}}}

In order to show that this equals {\displaystyle {\frac {1}{\sqrt {\det {\bar {g}}}}}{\frac {\partial }{\partial {\bar {x}}^{k}}}\left({\bar {A}}^{k}{\sqrt {\det {\bar {g}}}}\right)}, it suffices to show that

{\displaystyle {\frac {\partial {\bar {x}}^{k}}{\partial x^{i}}}{\frac {\partial }{\partial {\bar {x}}^{k}}}\left({\frac {\partial x^{i}}{\partial {\bar {x}}^{\ell }}}\det \!\left({\frac {\partial x}{\partial {\bar {x}}}}\right)^{\!\!\!-1}\right)=0\qquad {\text{for all }}\ell ,}

which is equivalent to

{\displaystyle {\frac {\partial }{\partial {\bar {x}}^{\ell }}}\det \left({\frac {\partial x}{\partial {\bar {x}}}}\right)=\det \left({\frac {\partial x}{\partial {\bar {x}}}}\right){\frac {\partial {\bar {x}}^{k}}{\partial x^{i}}}{\frac {\partial ^{2}x^{i}}{\partial {\bar {x}}^{k}\partial {\bar {x}}^{\ell }}}.}

Carrying out the differentiation on the left-hand side, we get:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}{\frac {\partial }{\partial {\bar {x}}^{\ell }}}\det \left({\frac {\partial x}{\partial {\bar {x}}}}\right)&=(-1)^{i+j}{\frac {\partial ^{2}x^{i}}{\partial {\bar {x}}^{\ell }\partial {\bar {x}}^{j}}}\det M(i|j)\\&={\frac {\partial ^{2}x^{i}}{\partial {\bar {x}}^{\ell }\partial {\bar {x}}^{j}}}\det \left({\frac {\partial x}{\partial {\bar {x}}}}\right){\frac {(-1)^{i+j}}{\det \left({\frac {\partial x}{\partial {\bar {x}}}}\right)}}\det M(i|j)=(\ast ),\end{aligned}}}

where {\displaystyle M(i|j)} denotes the matrix obtained from {\displaystyle \left({\frac {\partial x}{\partial {\bar {x}}}}\right)} by deleting the {\displaystyle i}th row and {\displaystyle j}th column. But Cramer's Rule says that

{\displaystyle {\frac {(-1)^{i+j}}{\det \left({\frac {\partial x}{\partial {\bar {x}}}}\right)}}\det M(i|j)}

is the {\displaystyle (j,i)}th entry of the matrix {\displaystyle \left({\frac {\partial {\bar {x}}}{\partial x}}\right)}. Thus

{\displaystyle (\ast )=\det \left({\frac {\partial x}{\partial {\bar {x}}}}\right){\frac {\partial ^{2}x^{i}}{\partial {\bar {x}}^{\ell }\partial {\bar {x}}^{j}}}{\frac {\partial {\bar {x}}^{j}}{\partial x^{i}}},}

completing the proof.
