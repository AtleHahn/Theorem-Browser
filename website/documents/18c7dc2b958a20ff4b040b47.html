Statement

For f\in L^1(\mathbb R)\cap L^2(\mathbb R) let \widehat f(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb R}f(x)e^{-2\pi ix\xi}\,dx. Assuming the Plancherel norm identity on this subspace, this Fourier map has a unique linear isometric extension \mathcal P:L^2(\mathbb R)\to L^2(\mathbb R).

Proof 1: Unique L2 extension of the real Fourier isometry — source argument

The Fourier map is linear and preserves the L^2 norm on L^1\cap L^2. This subspace is dense in L^2, and L^2 is complete. Apply the extension theorem for a linear isometry from a dense subspace into a complete normed space to obtain its unique norm-preserving extension.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Extend the Fourier isometry from the dense L1-intersect-L2 subspace using completeness of L2. General Lebesgue spaces and their Fourier norm identity are essential advanced prerequisites.
