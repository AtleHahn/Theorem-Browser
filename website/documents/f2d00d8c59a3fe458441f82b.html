Statement

Let f:I\to\mathbb R be concave on a real interval, meaning f(tx+(1-t)y)\geq tf(x)+(1-t)f(y) for x,y\in I and 0\leq t\leq1. For n\geq1 and x_1,\ldots,x_n\in I, f((x_1+\cdots+x_n)/n)\geq n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n f(x_i).

Level
High school only
Level justification

Concavity is explicitly the two-point inequality. The conclusion concerns a finite arithmetic mean, understandable through elementary real inequalities.

Proof 1: Apply concave Karamata to the constant mean list

Consider the real numbers x1, …, xn ∈ I and let

{\displaystyle a:={\frac {x_{1}+x_{2}+\cdots +x_{n}}{n}}}

denote their arithmetic mean. Arrange the x_i decreasingly. Then (x1, …, xn) majorizes (has dominating partial sums and the same total as) the n-tuple (a, a, …, a), since the arithmetic mean of the i largest numbers of (x1, …, xn) is at least as large as the arithmetic mean a of all the n numbers, for every i ∈ {1, …, n − 1}. By Karamata's inequality for the concave function f,

{\displaystyle f(x_{1})+f(x_{2})+\cdots +f(x_{n})\leq f(a)+f(a)+\cdots +f(a)=nf(a).}

Dividing by n gives Jensen's inequality.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Sort the finite list and compare the averages of its largest entries with the whole average, then apply the explicit concave majorization inequality. Elementary finite sums and the supplied concavity definition suffice.
