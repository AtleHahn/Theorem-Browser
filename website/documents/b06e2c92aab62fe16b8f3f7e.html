Statement

Let D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\} and let f:D\to\mathbb C be holomorphic and injective, with f(0)=0 and f'(0)=1. Set q(z)=f(z^2)/z^2 for z\ne0 and q(0)=1, and let h be the holomorphic square root of q on D normalized by h(0)=1. Then \varphi(z)=zh(z) is holomorphic and injective on D, is odd, and satisfies \varphi(0)=0 and \varphi'(0)=1.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex differentiation is a direct calculus extension. Biholomorphism means a bijective holomorphic map with holomorphic inverse, and the square root is explicitly a holomorphic function satisfying h squared equals q. The remaining image, derivative and injectivity conditions are familiar analysis.

Proof 1: Normalized analytic square-root recipe — outline

For a normalized injective holomorphic disk function f, apply the construction

\varphi(z)=z\left(\frac{f(z^2)}{z^2}\right)^{1/2}.

The quotient at zero is interpreted by removable extension, and the square root is its holomorphic branch with value 1 there. This construction yields an odd normalized injective holomorphic function.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Remove the quotient singularity, choose the normalized holomorphic square root and compare squared values to obtain oddness and injectivity. Undergraduate complex analysis suffices; the outline leaves routine branch and injectivity checks implicit.
