Statement

Let two distinct lines meet at S. Two distinct parallel lines not passing through S meet the first in A,B and the second in C,D, with A,C on one parallel and B,D on the other. Then \frac{|SA|}{|SB|}=\frac{|SC|}{|SD|}=\frac{|AC|}{|BD|}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The conclusion and configurations use elementary Euclidean lengths and parallels.

Proof 1: Central reflection to a ray configuration

If S is not between the parallels, the ray intercept theorem applies directly. If S is between them, reflect A and C through S. This produces the ray configuration: the reflected connecting line is parallel to the original one, and the lengths |SA|, |SC| and |AC| are unchanged. Apply the ray intercept theorem to obtain the displayed ratios. If the reflected parallel coincides with the other parallel, the corresponding endpoints coincide, so all three displayed ratios are directly equal to 1.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Reflect through the intersection when needed and apply the ray ratios, treating coincident reflected parallels separately. High-school Euclidean geometry suffices.
