Statement

Let (S,\Sigma,\mu) be a measure space, 1\le p<\infty, and f:S\to[-\infty,\infty] measurable. Write \|f\|_{L^p}=(\int_S|f|^p\,d\mu)^{1/p}, allowing +\infty. For \varepsilon>0,

\mu\{|f|\ge\varepsilon\}\le\varepsilon^{-p}\|f\|_{L^p}^p.
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The conclusion uses arbitrary level-set measures and an Lp norm defined through Lebesgue integration.

Proof 1: Insert the power transform into the measure inequality

Use the nondecreasing-transform measure inequality: for nonnegative nondecreasing \varphi with \varphi(\varepsilon)>0,

\mu\{|f|\ge\varepsilon\}\le\frac{\int_S\varphi(|f|)\,d\mu}{\varphi(\varepsilon)}.

Set \varphi(t)=t^p, the power transform in the stated measure version. It is nonnegative and nondecreasing on [0,\infty), and \varphi(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon^p>0. Substitution gives \mu\{|f|\ge\varepsilon\}\le\varepsilon^{-p}\int_S|f|^p\,d\mu=\varepsilon^{-p}\|f\|_{L^p}^p.
