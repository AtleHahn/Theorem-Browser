Statement

Let a<b be real, and let K:[a,b]^2\to\mathbb R be continuous, symmetric, and positive definite in the non-strict sense:

\sum_{i,j=1}^mK(x_i,x_j)c_ic_j\ge0

for every finite list x_1,\ldots,x_m\in[a,b] and every c_1,\ldots,c_m\in\mathbb R. Define the integral operator on real L^2([a,b]) by

(T_Kf)(t)=\int_a^bK(t,s)f(s)\,ds.

There is an orthonormal basis (e_j) of L^2([a,b]) consisting of eigenfunctions T_Ke_j=\lambda_je_j, with \lambda_j\ge0. Each eigenfunction whose eigenvalue is nonzero has a continuous representative. Using those representatives,

K(s,t)=\sum_j\lambda_je_j(s)e_j(t),

with absolute and uniform convergence on [a,b]^2. Terms with \lambda_j=0 are zero and can be omitted. Here L^2 consists of square-integrable functions modulo equality almost everywhere, with inner product \langle f,g\rangle=\int_a^bf(t)g(t)\,dt; orthonormal basis means unit mutually orthogonal vectors whose linear span is dense.
