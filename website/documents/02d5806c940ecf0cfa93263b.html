Statement

Let 0<\delta<1, and let \varphi be holomorphic on a neighborhood of \{z:\operatorname{Re}z\ge-\delta\}. Put \lambda(z)=\varphi(z)/\Gamma(1+z) and assume |\lambda(v+iw)|\le Ce^{Pv+A|w|} there, with C>0, P\in\mathbb R and A<\pi. Here \Gamma is the gamma function. The series F(x)=\sum_{n\ge0}\varphi(n)(-x)^n/n!, initially for 0<x<e^{-P}, continues to positive x by F(x)=(2\pi i)^{-1}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}\pi\lambda(-z)x^{-z}/\sin(\pi z)\,dz for 0<c<\delta. For 0<\operatorname{Re}s<\delta, its Mellin transform is \int_0^\infty x^{s-1}F(x)\,dx=\Gamma(s)\varphi(-s).

Proof 1: Substitute the factorial normalization and apply reflection

Use the source substitution \lambda(z)=\varphi(z)/\Gamma(1+z). Since \Gamma(1+n)=n!, the alternating \lambda series is precisely F. Hardy’s stated growth version gives the transform \pi\lambda(-s)/\sin(\pi s). Substitution and the gamma reflection identity give \pi\varphi(-s)/(\sin(\pi s)\Gamma(1-s))=\Gamma(s)\varphi(-s).

Normalize by gamma, apply Hardy master theorem under its half-plane growth conditions and use gamma reflection.
