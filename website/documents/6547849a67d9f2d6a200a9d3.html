Medium

Suitable continuity and Lipschitz assumptions make an initial state determine a unique local solution of a differential equation. This supplies a mathematical basis for local determinism in such dynamical models, while uniqueness alone does not guarantee global existence or practical predictability.

Statement

Let n\ge1 and let D\subset\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^n be a closed rectangle with (t_0,y_0) in its interior. Suppose f:D\to\mathbb R^n is continuous in t for each fixed y and there is L\ge0, independent of t, such that

\|f(t,y)-f(t,z)\|\le L\|y-z\|

whenever (t,y),(t,z)\in D. Then there is \varepsilon>0 such that the initial value problem

y'(t)=f(t,y(t)),\qquad y(t_0)=y_0

has a unique continuously differentiable solution on [t_0-\varepsilon,t_0+\varepsilon] whose graph lies in D. Derivatives at endpoints are interpreted one-sidedly.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Multivariable calculus and a locally stated Lipschitz estimate suffice to understand the full finite-dimensional local assertion.

Proof 1: Weighted contraction and time reversal (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorems3.12,3.13 and3.16, printed pp.29–35; Proposition3.8 and Theorem3.9).

The hypotheses imply joint continuity, since \|f(t,y)-f(s,z)\|\le L\|y-z\|+\|f(t,z)-f(s,z)\|. Choose a compact cylinder [t_0-a,t_0+a]\times\overline B(y_0,b) inside the rectangle and put M=\max\|f\| on it. In Theorem3.13 choose a time radius smaller than \min\{a,b/(M+1)\}; the endpoint is t_0 plus that radius, correcting the displayed omission of t_0. Apply Theorem3.16's time reversal for the left half. If L=0, use any positive larger Lipschitz bound in the weighted norm. Uniqueness among all solutions with graph in the original rectangle follows on a sufficiently small symmetric interval from \|y-z\|_\infty\le L\varepsilon\|y-z\|_\infty, with L\varepsilon<1.

The source sketches and attempts the contraction proof, but initially sets the time radius to 1/(2L) without ensuring it fits the given rectangle and uses incorrectly signed integral bounds for times before the initial time. Its later factorial estimate uses absolute integrals correctly but assumes the Picard self-map setup. A complete proof of the unconditional two-sided local theorem would require correcting that setup; no repaired proof is included.
