Statement

Let n\ge1 and let D\subset\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^n be a closed rectangle with (t_0,y_0) in its interior. Suppose f:D\to\mathbb R^n is continuous in t for each fixed y and there is L\ge0, independent of t, such that

\|f(t,y)-f(t,z)\|\le L\|y-z\|

whenever (t,y),(t,z)\in D. Then there is \varepsilon>0 such that the initial value problem

y'(t)=f(t,y(t)),\qquad y(t_0)=y_0

has a unique continuously differentiable solution on [t_0-\varepsilon,t_0+\varepsilon] whose graph lies in D. Derivatives at endpoints are interpreted one-sidedly.

Proof availability

The source sketches and attempts the contraction proof, but initially sets the time radius to 1/(2L) without ensuring it fits the given rectangle and uses incorrectly signed integral bounds for times before the initial time. Its later factorial estimate uses absolute integrals correctly but assumes the Picard self-map setup. A complete proof of the unconditional two-sided local theorem would require correcting that setup; no repaired proof is included.
