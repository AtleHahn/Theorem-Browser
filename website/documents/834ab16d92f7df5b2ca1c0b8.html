Statement

Let a_{i,k}≥0 for positive integers i,k, with a_{i,k}≤a_{i,k+1}. Then sup_k∑{i=1}^∞a{i,k}=∑{i=1}^∞sup_k a{i,k}, allowing +∞.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Nonnegative double sequences; Extended suprema; Finite sums and monotone limits. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Compare finite truncations before taking suprema

Since {\displaystyle a_{i,k}\leq \sup _{k}a_{i,k}} we have {\displaystyle \sum _{i}a_{i,k}\leq \sum _{i}\sup _{k}a_{i,k}} so {\displaystyle \sup _{k}\sum _{i}a_{i,k}\leq \sum _{i}\sup _{k}a_{i,k}}.

Conversely, we can interchange sup and sum for finite sums by reverting to the limit definition, so {\displaystyle \sum _{i=1}^{N}\sup _{k}a_{i,k}=\sup _{k}\sum _{i=1}^{N}a_{i,k}\leq \sup _{k}\sum _{i=1}^{\infty }a_{i,k}} hence {\displaystyle \sum _{i=1}^{\infty }\sup _{k}a_{i,k}\leq \sup _{k}\sum _{i=1}^{\infty }a_{i,k}}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Compare each finite truncation before taking suprema and then let the truncation grow. Undergraduate real analysis of nonnegative series suffices; no measure-theoretic monotone convergence is used.
