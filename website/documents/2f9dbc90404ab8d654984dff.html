Statement

Let f:X\to[0,\infty] be measurable on a measure space and let measurable A\subseteq B\subseteq X. Then \int_A f\,d\mu\le\int_B f\,d\mu.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level

Proof 1: Write the restricted integral with an indicator

Let \mathbf1_A be the indicator function of the set A, with f\mathbf1_A defined to be zero outside A. Write \operatorname{SF}(h) for the nonnegative finite-valued measurable simple functions below h. It can be deduced from the definition of Lebesgue integral that

\int_B f\mathbf1_A\,d\mu=\int_A f\,d\mu

if we notice that, for every s\in\operatorname{SF}(f\mathbf1_A), s=0 outside of A. Combined with monotonicity of the nonnegative integral, the inequality f\mathbf1_A\leq f implies

\int_A f\,d\mu=\int_B f\mathbf1_A\,d\mu\leq\int_B f\,d\mu.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Identify restriction by an indicator through measurable simple minorants and apply nonnegative integral monotonicity. General measure spaces and the Lebesgue integral construction require graduate measure theory.
