Statement

Let n\geq1 be an integer and \theta a real angle. Mark the distinct points among rotations through \theta,2\theta,\ldots,n\theta on a circle. The arcs between consecutive marked points in circular order have at most three distinct lengths. With a single marked point the sole gap is the full circumference.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The result concerns finitely many rotations and elementary circular geometry.

Proof 1: Liang’s rigid-gap argument (outline)

The following simple proof is due to Frank Liang. First suppose the marked points are distinct and there are at least two. Let {\displaystyle \theta } be the rotation angle generating a set of points as some number of consecutive multiples of {\displaystyle \theta } on a circle. Define a gap to be an arc {\displaystyle A} of the circle that extends between two adjacent points of the given set, and define a gap to be rigid if the larger of its two endpoint indices is maximal among gaps of the same length. From this definition, it follows that every gap has the same length as a rigid gap. If {\displaystyle A} is a rigid gap, then its rotated arc {\displaystyle A+\theta } cannot have both endpoints present and still be a gap, because it has the same length and would be one step later. The only ways for this to happen are for one of the endpoints of {\displaystyle A} to be the last point in the sequence of multiples of {\displaystyle \theta } (so that the corresponding endpoint of {\displaystyle A+\theta } is missing) or for one of the given points to land within {\displaystyle A+\theta }, preventing it from being a gap. A point can only land within {\displaystyle A+\theta } if it is the first point in the sequence of multiples of {\displaystyle \theta }, because otherwise its predecessor in the sequence would land within {\displaystyle A}, contradicting the assumption that {\displaystyle A} is a gap. So there can be at most three rigid gaps, the two on either side of the last point and the one in which the predecessor of the first point (if it were part of the sequence) would land. Because there are at most three rigid gaps, there are at most three lengths of gaps.

Repeated points mean that the finite orbit of the rotation has already closed. A finite full rotation orbit is equally spaced, so there is one gap length. The one-point case also has one gap length.
