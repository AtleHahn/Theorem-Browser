Statement

For a positive integer n, let F_n be the increasing sequence of reduced fractions a/b\in[0,1] with integers b\ge1, \gcd(a,b)=1 and b\le n, including 0/1 and 1/1. Then |F_n|=n(n+3)/2-\sum_{d=2}^n|F_{\lfloor n/d\rfloor}|.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The conclusion involves only a finite list of fractions and integer floors.

Proof 1: A floor-quotient recurrence for Farey length — outline

We use the Farey length identity

|F_n|=\frac12\left(3+\sum_{d=1}^n\mu(d)\left\lfloor\frac nd\right\rfloor^2\right),

and, by the Möbius inversion formula, obtain

|F_n|=\frac12(n+3)n-\sum_{d=2}^n|F_{\lfloor n/d\rfloor}|.

Here \mu is the number-theoretic Möbius function: \mu(1)=1, \mu(d)=0 if a prime square divides d, and otherwise \mu(d)=(-1)^r for r distinct prime factors. The inversion used is the floor-sum form: if g(n)=\sum_{d=1}^n\mu(d)h(\lfloor n/d\rfloor), then h(n)=\sum_{d=1}^ng(\lfloor n/d\rfloor).
