Statement

Let T be a consistent complete first-order theory in a countable language with an infinite model, and suppose T is categorical in at least one infinite cardinal. Exactly one of these holds: it is categorical only in \aleph_0 among infinite cardinals; only in all uncountable cardinals; or in every infinite cardinal.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement concerns the infinite-cardinal spectrum of a first-order theory.

Proof 1: The countable case and Morley’s uncountable dichotomy — outline

Morley's categoricity theorem confirms that these are the only possibilities: if T is a consistent and complete theory over a countable language, with at least one infinite model, then T is \kappa-categorical at some uncountable \kappa if and only if it is \kappa-categorical at all uncountable \kappa.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Separate countable categoricity from the all-or-none uncountable categoricity consequence of Morley. The case split is short, but complete theories and infinite cardinal categoricity are advanced model-theoretic prerequisites.
