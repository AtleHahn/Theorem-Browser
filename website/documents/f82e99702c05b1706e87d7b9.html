Statement

For every pair of positive integers a,d with \gcd(a,d)=1, there are infinitely many prime numbers of the form

a+nd\qquad(n=0,1,2,\ldots).

Equivalently, every residue class a modulo a positive integer d that is coprime to d contains infinitely many primes.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Only primes, coprimality and arithmetic progressions are needed for the statement; analytic L-functions used in proofs are not prerequisites.

Proof 1: Character sums and nonvanishing of Dirichlet L-series (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorem 1.1; §§2–5, especially Theorem 5.2).

In the character construction choose a primitive root of unity. Near s=1, use a local holomorphic logarithm of each nonprincipal L(s,\chi), which is nonzero there; it differs from the Euler-product branch by a constant multiple of 2\pi i. Orthogonality uses \overline{\chi(a)}=\chi(a)^{-1}. The resulting divergent prime sum is unchanged by removing the finitely many primes below a, so it gives infinitely many terms a+nd with n\ge0.
