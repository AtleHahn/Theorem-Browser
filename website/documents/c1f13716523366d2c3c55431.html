Statement

For 0\le x\le1, e^x\le\frac{1+x}{1-x^2/2}\le4.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement is a scalar inequality between an exponential and an explicit rational function; no derivative or limit occurs.

Proof 1: Move the exponential remainder bound to the left

Since e^0=1 and (e^x)'=e^x>0, the exponential is increasing. We use the property {\textstyle e^{\xi }<e^{x}} for {\textstyle 0<\xi <x} to estimate

{\displaystyle e^{x}=1+x+{\frac {e^{\xi }}{2}}x^{2}<1+x+{\frac {e^{x}}{2}}x^{2},\qquad 0<x\leq 1}

using the first-degree Taylor formula with second-derivative remainder. Then we solve for ex to deduce that

{\displaystyle e^{x}\leq {\frac {1+x}{1-{\frac {x^{2}}{2}}}}=2{\frac {1+x}{2-x^{2}}}\leq 4,\qquad 0\leq x\leq 1}

simply by maximizing the numerator and minimizing the denominator. The endpoint x=0 is immediate.

Level justification

Use the first-degree Taylor formula with a second-derivative remainder, bound that derivative by exp(x) and rearrange a positive denominator. Ordinary calculus and inequalities suffice.
