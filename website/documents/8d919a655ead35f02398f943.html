Statement

For real three-vectors a,b, the area of their spanned parallelogram is \|a\times b\|, using the Euclidean cross product.

Proof 1: Compare the squared norm with base-times-height area

In three dimensions, Lagrange's identity asserts that if a and b are vectors in \mathbb R^3 with lengths |a| and |b|, then Lagrange's identity can be written in terms of the cross product and dot product:

{\displaystyle |\mathbf {a} |^{2}|\mathbf {b} |^{2}-(\mathbf {a} \cdot \mathbf {b} )^{2}=|\mathbf {a} \times \mathbf {b} |^{2}}

Using the definition of angle based upon the dot product (for nonzero \mathbf a,\mathbf b), the left-hand side is

{\displaystyle \left|\mathbf {a} \right|^{2}\left|\mathbf {b} \right|^{2}\left(1-\cos ^{2}\theta \right)=\left|\mathbf {a} \right|^{2}\left|\mathbf {b} \right|^{2}\sin ^{2}\theta }

where θ is the angle formed by the vectors a and b. The area of a parallelogram with sides |a| and |b| and angle θ is known in elementary geometry to be

{\displaystyle \left|\mathbf {a} \right|\left|\mathbf {b} \right|\left|\sin \theta \right|,}

so the left-hand side of Lagrange's identity is the squared area of the parallelogram. Taking nonnegative square roots gives the claimed area. If either vector is zero, both the area and the cross product are zero.
