Statement

Let n\ge1. A full-rank lattice in \mathbb R^n is the integer span of a real basis; its covolume is the absolute determinant of that basis. Write \operatorname{vol} for n-dimensional volume. Let g be a norm on \mathbb R^n, K=\{x:g(x)\le1\}, and \Gamma a full-rank lattice of covolume d. Define \lambda_j as the smallest positive dilation factor for which \lambda_jK contains j linearly independent lattice vectors. Then \lambda_1\cdots\lambda_n\operatorname{vol}(K)\ge 2^n d/n!.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Norms on finite-dimensional real spaces; Lattices and covolume; Linear independence; Successive minima; Volume. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Compare the inscribed cross-polytope volume with the lattice determinant

A basis of linearly independent lattice vectors b_1,b_2,\ldots,b_n can be chosen with g(b_j)=\lambda_j (warning: it may not be a basis of the lattice, but only a basis of the ambient space).

The lower bound is proved by considering the convex polytope with 2n vertices at \pm b_j/\lambda_j, which is contained in K and has volume

\frac{2^n}{n!\lambda_1\lambda_2\cdots\lambda_n}

times an integer multiple of the volume of a primitive cell of the lattice (as seen by scaling the polytope by \lambda_j along each basis vector to obtain 2^n n-simplices with lattice-point vertices), the integer being the index of the \mathbb Z-span of the family (b_i)_i as a subgroup of the lattice.

Choose vectors attaining the minima, inscribe their scaled cross-polytope and compare its determinant volume with the lattice covolume. Undergraduate linear algebra, finite-dimensional compactness and elementary polytope volume suffice.
