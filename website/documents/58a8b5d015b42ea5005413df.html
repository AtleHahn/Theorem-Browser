Likely alternative prerequisite level: High school only; lowest recorded proof level: Undergraduate level. For each nonzero residue modulo p, Bezout's integer identity supplies its unique inverse. Pair residues with different inverses; each pair contributes one. A self-inverse satisfies (a-1)(a+1)=0 modulo p, so only 1,-1 remain for odd p, giving product -1. For p=2, 1=-1 modulo 2 directly. School modular arithmetic replaces Sylow theory and permutation groups.

Proof 1: Counting p-cycles with Sylow theory

Let p be a prime. One may easily prove this theorem by Sylow's third theorem. Indeed, observe that the number np of Sylow's p-subgroups in the symmetric group Sp is 1/(p-1) times the number of p-cycles in Sp, ie. (p − 2)!. On the other hand, np ≡ 1 (mod p). Hence, (p − 2)! ≡ 1 (mod p). So, (p − 1)! ≡ −1 (mod p).
