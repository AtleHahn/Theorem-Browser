Statement

Let n\ge1, let v\in\mathbb R or \mathbb C satisfy |v|\ge1, and put v_i=v for 1\le i\le n. Write k=\lfloor n/2\rfloor. Exactly \binom nk subsets I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\} have \sum_{i\in I}v_i in the closed disk (interval in the real case) of radius 1/2 centered at kv.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The claim concerns elementary counting, integer distances in the plane or comparison of nonnegative numbers with their squares. The definitions are explicit and no advanced structural concept is needed to understand it. This rating concerns understanding the statement, independently of the difficulty of its proof.

Proof 1: Equal-vector witness and subset counting — outline

A subset of cardinality j has sum jv. Its distance from kv is |j-k||v|, which is zero for j=k and at least one otherwise. Thus the disk selects exactly the subsets of cardinality k, of which there are \binom nk.
