Statement

Let F be an infinite field and E=F(\beta,\gamma) a finite separable extension. Then E=F(\beta+c\gamma) for all but finitely many c\in F.

Proof 1: Bound exceptional coefficients by conjugate pairs

For {\displaystyle c\in F}, suppose {\displaystyle \alpha =\beta +c\gamma } fails to be a primitive element, {\displaystyle F(\alpha )\subsetneq F(\beta ,\gamma )}. Then {\displaystyle \gamma \notin F(\alpha )}, since otherwise {\displaystyle \beta =\alpha -c\gamma \in F(\alpha )=F(\beta ,\gamma )}. Consider the minimal polynomials of {\displaystyle \beta ,\gamma } over {\displaystyle F(\alpha )}, respectively {\displaystyle f(X),g(X)\in F(\alpha )[X]}, and take a splitting field {\displaystyle L} containing all roots {\displaystyle \beta ,\beta ',\ldots } of {\displaystyle f(X)} and {\displaystyle \gamma ,\gamma ',\ldots } of {\displaystyle g(X)}. Since the extension is separable and {\displaystyle \gamma \notin F(\alpha )}, there is another root {\displaystyle \gamma '\neq \gamma }, and, by extension of embeddings to a splitting field, a field automorphism {\displaystyle \sigma :L\to L} which fixes {\displaystyle F(\alpha )} and takes {\displaystyle \sigma (\gamma )=\gamma '}. We then have {\displaystyle \sigma (\alpha )=\alpha }, and:

{\displaystyle \beta +c\gamma =\sigma (\beta +c\gamma )=\sigma (\beta )+c\,\sigma (\gamma )}, and therefore {\displaystyle c={\frac {\sigma (\beta )-\beta }{\gamma -\sigma (\gamma )}}}.

The images \sigma(\beta) and \sigma(\gamma) are roots of the fixed minimal polynomials of \beta and \gamma over F. Thus there are only finitely many possibilities for {\displaystyle \sigma (\beta )=\beta '} and {\displaystyle \sigma (\gamma )=\gamma '}, so only finitely many {\displaystyle c\in F} fail to give a primitive element {\displaystyle \alpha =\beta +c\gamma }. All other values give {\displaystyle F(\alpha )=F(\beta ,\gamma )}.
