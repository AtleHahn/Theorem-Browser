Statement

Let G be a finite group and p a prime. For a subgroup B of a group H, write

N_H(B)=\{h\in H:hBh^{-1}=B\},\qquad C_H(B)=\{h\in H:hb=bh\text{ for every }b\in B\}.

A p-subgroup has order a power of p. A p-local subgroup of G is a subgroup N_G(U) with U a nontrivial p-subgroup.

For any finite group H, let O_p(H) be its largest normal p-subgroup and let O_{p'}(H) be its largest normal subgroup of order relatively prime to p. Say that H is p-constrained if, on putting \overline H=H/O_{p'}(H), one has

C_{\overline H}(O_p(\overline H))\subseteq O_p(\overline H).

Assume that every p-local subgroup of G is p-constrained. Let P be a Sylow p-subgroup of G (its order is the largest power of p dividing |G|), and let A be an abelian normal subgroup of P such that C_P(A)=A and the minimum number of generators of A is at least three.

For every prime q\ne p, consider the q-subgroups Q\leq G normalized by A, meaning aQa^{-1}=Q for all a\in A, and maximal under inclusion among such q-subgroups. Then C_G(A) acts transitively on these maximal subgroups by conjugation: for any two of them Q_1,Q_2, there is g\in C_G(A) with

gQ_1g^{-1}=Q_2.
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Starting from undergraduate finite-group theory, all extra conditions are defined by familiar subgroup and quotient operations: largest normal subgroups of prescribed prime divisibility, their centralizers, and normalizers. These related definitions can be understood together within about 30 minutes, without learning the classification of finite groups or the theorem’s proof. For example the elementary abelian group (Z/pZ)^3 satisfies the local hypotheses with A=P. The conclusion is explicitly a conjugacy assertion.
