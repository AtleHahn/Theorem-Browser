Every positive integer N\equiv3\pmod4 has a prime divisor p\equiv3\pmod4.

Proof 1: Compare residues in a prime factorization

By cases, N is either composite or prime. If N is composite, then N has a prime factorization N=a_1a_2\cdots a_r, where each a_i is prime. Because N\equiv3\pmod4, N is odd and must be the product of only odd primes. Any odd prime p must satisfy p\equiv1\pmod4 or p\equiv3\pmod4. It cannot be that a_i\equiv1\pmod4 for every a_i, because if this were the case, then N\equiv1\pmod4. So there exists a prime a'\equiv3\pmod4 such that a'\mid N.

Otherwise, if N is prime, then N\equiv3\pmod4 by hypothesis, and we take p=N.

Factor the odd integer and observe that a product of factors all one modulo four stays one modulo four. Elementary prime factorization and remainders suffice.
