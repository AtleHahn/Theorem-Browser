Statement

For a pointed space Y, write \operatorname{SP}(Y) for its infinite symmetric product: finite unordered tuples of points of Y, with basepoint entries ignored, with the usual direct-limit topology of finite symmetric products. Let X be a pointed space with continuous commutative associative multiplication and strict identity e. For each integer n\geq1, let M_n be a pointed Moore space and let g_n:M_n\to X be a pointed continuous map. For the wedge map g:\bigvee_{n\geq1}M_n\to X, there is a continuous map F:\operatorname{SP}(\bigvee_{n\geq1}M_n)\to X given by F([z_1,\ldots,z_r])=g(z_1)\cdots g(z_r), with the empty tuple mapped to e.

Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Pointed Moore spaces and wedge sums; Continuous commutative associative multiplication; Infinite symmetric products. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Proof 1: Multiply the images of the coordinates — outline

The basepoint-preserving maps g_n:M_n\to X give a map

g:\bigvee_{n\geq1}M_n\to X.

Then the commutative and associative multiplication with strict identity in X yields a map

F:\operatorname{SP}\left(\bigvee_{n\geq1}M_n\right)\to X

given by summing up the images of the coordinates, or multiplying them in the multiplicative notation of the statement.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Extend the wedge map by iterated multiplication and descend through symmetric quotients and their direct limit. Moore spaces, wedges, symmetric products and limit topology form substantial algebraic-topological prerequisites.
