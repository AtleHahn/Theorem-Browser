Statement

Let f:X\to Y be a map between topological spaces. It is strongly closed if it sends every closed subset of X to a closed subset of Y, and relatively closed if these images are closed in the subspace f(X). Then f is strongly closed if and only if it is relatively closed and f(X) is closed in Y.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Open/closed maps and subspace topology are basic point-set topology. Both variants are explicitly defined by images, so the equivalence requires no further concepts.

Proof 1: Strong and relative closedness

Because X is always a closed subset of X, the image f(X)=\operatorname{Im}f of a strongly closed map f:X\to Y must be a closed subset of its codomain Y. In fact, a relatively closed map is a strongly closed map if and only if its image is a closed subset of its codomain.

The source leaves the subspace-topology verification of the converse implicit.

Level justification
