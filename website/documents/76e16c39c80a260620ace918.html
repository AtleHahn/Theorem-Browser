Statement

Let X be a smooth vector field on a smooth finite-dimensional manifold M. For each p\in M, there exists a smooth integral curve u:(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\to M with u(0)=p and u'(t)=X_{u(t)}, unique locally for this initial condition.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement is on general smooth manifolds and the proof imports local ODE existence and uniqueness.

Proof 1: Apply local ODE existence in coordinates

One begins by noting that an arbitrary smooth vector field {\displaystyle X} on a manifold {\displaystyle M} defines a family of curves, its integral curves {\displaystyle u:I\to M} (for intervals {\displaystyle I}). These are the solutions of {\displaystyle {\dot {u}}(t)=X_{u(t)}}, which is a system of first-order ordinary differential equations, whose solvability is guaranteed by the Picard–Lindelöf theorem. Here Picard–Lindelöf is applied in local coordinates, where smooth coefficients are locally Lipschitz, with the initial condition u(0)=p; it gives local existence and uniqueness.

Level justification

Transfer local ODE existence through charts for a vector field on an arbitrary smooth manifold. Smooth manifolds, tangent vectors and coordinate-compatible vector fields form a cumulative prerequisite burden not confidently small, so retain beyond undergraduate.
