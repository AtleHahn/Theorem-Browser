Statement

Put \omega=(-1+i\sqrt3)/2 and R=\mathbb Z[\omega]. For a prime \pi not dividing 3 and \pi\nmid\alpha, let \chi_\pi(\alpha)\in\{1,\omega,\omega^2\} be the unique value congruent to \alpha^{(N(\pi)-1)/3} modulo \pi, where N(a+b\omega)=a^2-ab+b^2. For a nonzero nonunit \lambda\in R coprime to 3\alpha, choose a prime-factor product \lambda=\prod_{j=1}^r\pi_j^{e_j} with positive integer exponents, and define

\chi_\lambda(\alpha)=\prod_{j=1}^r\chi_{\pi_j}(\alpha)^{e_j}.

This value is independent of the factorization. It is multiplicative in the numerator and denominator: whenever all indicated symbols are defined, \chi_\lambda(\alpha\beta)=\chi_\lambda(\alpha)\chi_\lambda(\beta) and \chi_{\lambda\mu}(\alpha)=\chi_\lambda(\alpha)\chi_\mu(\alpha).

Proof 1: Multiply the prime-character values — outline

We use unique factorization in \mathbb Z[\omega], the invariance of the prime cubic character under replacing a prime by an associate, and its numerator multiplicativity

\left(\frac{\alpha\beta}{\pi}\right)_3 =\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)_3\left(\frac{\beta}{\pi}\right)_3.

Here (\alpha/\pi)_3=\chi_\pi(\alpha).

The cubic character can be extended multiplicatively to composite numbers (coprime to 3) in the denominator in the same way the Legendre symbol is generalized into the Jacobi symbol:

\left(\frac{\alpha}{\lambda}\right)_3 =\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi_1}\right)_3^{e_1} \left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi_2}\right)_3^{e_2}\cdots,

where

\lambda=\pi_1^{e_1}\pi_2^{e_2}\pi_3^{e_3}\cdots.

Unique factorization and associate invariance make this product independent of the chosen factorization; numerator multiplicativity and combining factor lists give the two multiplicativity statements. These product checks are implicit in the source.
