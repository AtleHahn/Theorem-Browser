Statement

A positive integer is perfect if the sum of its positive proper divisors equals itself. A Mersenne prime is a prime of the form 2^p-1, p\ge2 an integer. There are infinitely many Mersenne primes if and only if there are infinitely many even perfect numbers.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The two infinitude assertions concern arbitrarily large positive integers satisfying locally defined arithmetic properties, not an abstract infinite-set construction.

Proof 1: Use the one-to-one arithmetic correspondence — outline

The Euclid–Euler theorem gives the correspondence 2^p-1\longleftrightarrow2^{p-1}(2^p-1) between Mersenne primes and even perfect numbers. This is a one-to-one correspondence: each Mersenne prime generates one even perfect number and vice versa. Therefore the two infinitude assertions are equivalent.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Use the explicit bijection of integer families to transfer infinitude. Elementary number theory and one-to-one correspondence suffice.
