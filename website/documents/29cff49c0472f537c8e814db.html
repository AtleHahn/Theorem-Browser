Statement

Write G=\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb C) for invertible complex two-by-two matrices, S=\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb C) for those of determinant 1, and N=\mathbb C^\times I for nonzero scalar matrices. Then \operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbb C):=G/N\cong S/\{I,-I\}=: \operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbb C).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Matrix groups, quotient groups and abelian-group direct sums are introductory algebra. The scalar/central subgroups and concrete summands are explicit; no projective geometry or general module classification is needed.

Proof 1: PGL₂(C) is isomorphic to PSL₂(C) — outline

The source chooses G,S,N as in the statement and gives S\cap N=\{I,-I\} and SN=G. The second isomorphism theorem gives (SN)/N\cong S/(S\cap N). Substituting the two specified subgroup identities gives precisely G/N\cong S/\{I,-I\}.
