Statement

Let n\ge1 be an integer, let \mu be Lebesgue measure on \mathbb R^n, and let A,B\subseteq\mathbb R^n be nonempty bounded open sets. For their Minkowski sum A+B=\{a+b:a\in A,b\in B\}, one has \mu(A+B)^{1/n}\ge\mu(A)^{1/n}+\mu(B)^{1/n}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Point-set topology supplies bounded open subsets of Euclidean space; the sum is explicitly defined. Their Lebesgue volume can be interpreted by finite box unions inside them, extending multivariable volume to arbitrary open sets. This small addition to topology and integration, together with the displayed nth-root inequality, fits about 30 minutes without general measurable-function theory.

Proof 1: Approximate open sets from inside by finite box unions outline

Use the finite-box Brunn–Minkowski inequality: for nonempty finite disjoint unions C,D of bounded axis-aligned boxes, \mu(C+D)^{1/n}\ge\mu(C)^{1/n}+\mu(D)^{1/n}.

In this setting, both bodies can be approximated arbitrarily well by unions of disjoint axis aligned rectangles contained in their interior; this follows from general facts about the Lebesgue measure of open sets. That is, we have a sequence of bodies {\textstyle A_{k}\subseteq A}, which are disjoint unions of finitely many axis aligned rectangles, where {\textstyle \mu (A\setminus A_{k})\leq 1/k}, and likewise {\textstyle B_{k}\subseteq B}. Then we have that {\textstyle A+B\supseteq A_{k}+B_{k}}, so {\textstyle \mu (A+B)^{1/n}\geq \mu (A_{k}+B_{k})^{1/n}\geq \mu (A_{k})^{1/n}+\mu (B_{k})^{1/n}}. The right hand side converges to {\textstyle \mu (A)^{1/n}+\mu (B)^{1/n}} as {\textstyle k\to \infty }, establishing this special case.

Level justification

The proof imports approximation of bounded open sets by finite box unions and passes an explicit volume inequality to the limit. The needed additional volume fact is concrete inner box approximation; together with set inclusion and real limits it is a manageable extension of undergraduate multivariable integration, without arbitrary measurable-set regularity.
