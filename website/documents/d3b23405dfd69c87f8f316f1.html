Statement

Let ABC be a nondegenerate triangle in the Euclidean plane and let V lie on none of its three side lines. Let A'B'C' be any nondegenerate triangle satisfying

A'B'\parallel CV,\qquad A'C'\parallel BV,\qquad B'C'\parallel AV.

Then the line through C' parallel to AB, the line through A' parallel to BC, and the line through B' parallel to AC are concurrent: there is one point lying on all three.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The entire construction and conclusion use elementary Euclidean incidence and parallelism.
