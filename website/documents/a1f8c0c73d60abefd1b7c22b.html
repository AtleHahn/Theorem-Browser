Statement

For an integer n\ge1, let E_{kl}\in M_n(\mathbb C) be the matrix units. Then E=\sum_{k,l}E_{kl}\otimes E_{kl} is positive semidefinite, and E^2=nE.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves complex matrix units; Kronecker products; positive semidefinite matrices. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Factor the matrix-unit tensor sum after squaring — outline

We observe that if

{\displaystyle E=\sum _{ij}E_{ij}\otimes E_{ij},}

then E=E* and E2=nE, so E=n−1EE* which is positive.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Multiply matrix units using E_ij E_kl=delta_jk E_il to get E²=nE, and observe conjugate-transpose symmetry. Then E=(1/n)EE star is positive. Ordinary finite-dimensional linear algebra plus the concrete Kronecker product suffices.
