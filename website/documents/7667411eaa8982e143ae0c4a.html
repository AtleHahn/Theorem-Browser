Statement

Let S be a semigroup and U\subseteq S a subsemigroup. A semigroup is a set with an associative binary operation; a subsemigroup is a nonempty subset closed under that operation. Define the dominion

\operatorname{Dom}_S(U)=\{d\in S: f(d)=g(d)\text{ whenever }f,g:S\to T\text{ are homomorphisms agreeing on }U\},

where T ranges over all semigroups and a homomorphism preserves multiplication.

Then d\in\operatorname{Dom}_S(U) if and only if either d\in U, or there are an integer m\geq1, elements u_1,\ldots,u_{m+1},v_1,\ldots,v_m\in U and x_1,\ldots,x_m,y_1,\ldots,y_m\in S satisfying all the following identities:

\begin{aligned} d&=x_1u_1,&u_1&=v_1y_1,\\ x_{i-1}v_{i-1}&=x_i u_i,&u_i y_{i-1}&=v_i y_i\quad(2\leq i\leq m),\\ x_mv_m&=u_{m+1},&u_{m+1}y_m&=d. \end{aligned}

Such a system is a zigzag of length m in S over U with value d.

The same characterization holds in the category of monoids: S and the target T have identity elements, U contains the identity of S, and the homomorphisms preserve identity as well as multiplication.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory algebra supplies associative operations and homomorphisms. Removing inverses to obtain semigroups and defining the all-homomorphisms agreement condition are a short extension; the conclusion is an explicit finite system of equations, without categorical machinery.
