Statement

For distinct vertices x,y in a finite undirected graph, the minimum number of edges whose deletion destroys every xy-path equals the maximum number of pairwise edge-disjoint xy-paths.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement concerns finite vertices, paths and deletion sets, with disjointness and the required counting conditions specified locally.

Proof 1: Digons and cancellation of opposite flows — outline

The directed edge version of Menger's theorem says that the minimum directed arc cut equals the maximum number of arc-disjoint directed paths.

The directed versions of the theorem immediately imply undirected versions: it suffices to replace each edge of an undirected graph with a pair of directed edges (a digon).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Replace undirected edges by opposite arcs, then cancel opposite uses and decompose the remaining integral flow. Undergraduate graph algorithms suffice; the implicit flow-decomposition step requires more than the bare directed-path theorem.
