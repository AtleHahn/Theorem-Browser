Statement

For real x, with i^2=-1, \cos x=(e^{ix}+e^{-ix})/2 and \sin x=(e^{ix}-e^{-ix})/(2i).

Level
High school only
Level justification

Euler’s formula is supplied in the companion, making both imaginary exponentials concrete cosine/sine expressions. The asserted two identities involve complex-number arithmetic and elementary trigonometric functions only. Accepting that formula does not require learning complex analysis.

Proof 1: Adding and subtracting Euler identities

Euler's formula states that

{\displaystyle e^{ix}=\cos x+i\,\sin x.}

Substituting {\displaystyle -x} for {\displaystyle x} gives the equation

{\displaystyle e^{-ix}=\cos x-i\,\sin x}

because cosine is an even function and sine is odd. These two equations can be solved for the sine and cosine to give

{\displaystyle \cos x={\frac {e^{ix}+e^{-ix}}{2}}\quad {\text{and}}\quad \sin x={\frac {e^{ix}-e^{-ix}}{2i}}.}
Level
High school only
Level justification

Write the given Euler formula at x and -x and add or subtract. Sine parity and complex arithmetic suffice.
