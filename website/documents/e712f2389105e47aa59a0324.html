Statement

Let \theta:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R be differentiable and let f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R be differentiable with f'(0)=0. Suppose I_1=\int_{\mathbb R^3}|\nabla\theta|^2\,dx and I_2=\int_{\mathbb R^3}f(\theta)\,dx are finite. For \lambda>0 set \theta_\lambda(x)=\theta(\lambda x) and E(\psi)=\int_{\mathbb R^3}(|\nabla\psi|^2+f(\psi))\,dx. Then E(\theta_\lambda)=I_1/\lambda+I_2/\lambda^3.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Multivariable calculus supplies gradients, integration and rescaling coordinates. The energy and dilated functions are explicitly defined, so the statement is an integral scaling identity requiring no variational or field-theoretic background.

Proof 1: Energy along spatial dilations in three dimensions — outline

Write E_\lambda=E(\theta_\lambda).

Define {\displaystyle \theta _{\lambda }(x)=\theta (\lambda x)\,} where {\displaystyle \lambda } is a positive constant, and write {\displaystyle I_{1}=\int (\nabla \theta )^{2}d^{3}x}, {\displaystyle I_{2}=\int f(\theta )d^{3}x}. Then

{\displaystyle E_{\lambda }=\int \left[(\nabla \theta _{\lambda })^{2}+f(\theta _{\lambda })\right]\,d^{3}x=I_{1}/\lambda +I_{2}/\lambda ^{3}.}
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The coordinate chain rule contributes lambda squared to the gradient term and dilation contributes lambda to the minus third power to volume. The assumed finite integrals then scale directly. Only a linear change of variables is applied, without further measure-theoretic convergence arguments.
