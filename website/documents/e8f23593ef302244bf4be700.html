Statement

Let W be the local complex analytic inverse of w\mapsto we^w with W(0)=0. In a neighborhood of zero, W(z)=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{(-n)^{n-1}}{n!}z^n=z-z^2+\frac32z^3-\frac83z^4+O(z^5).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Calculus and power series supply composition, coefficients, differentiation and local inversion. Formal series retain the same coefficient operations without analytic convergence; Lambert W is specified by its inverse equation. These direct definitions suffice for the displayed coefficient identities.

Proof 1: Exponential derivatives in Lagrange inversion

The Lambert W function is the function {\displaystyle W(z)} that is implicitly defined by the equation

{\displaystyle W(z)e^{W(z)}=z.}

We may use the Lagrange inversion theorem to compute the Taylor series of {\displaystyle W(z)} at {\displaystyle z=0.} We take {\displaystyle f(w)=we^{w}} and {\displaystyle a=0.} Recognizing that

{\displaystyle {\frac {d^{n}}{dx^{n}}}e^{\alpha x}=\alpha ^{n}e^{\alpha x},}

this gives

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}W(z)&=\sum _{n=1}^{\infty }\left[\lim _{w\to 0}{\frac {d^{n-1}}{dw^{n-1}}}e^{-nw}\right]{\frac {z^{n}}{n!}}\\{}&=\sum _{n=1}^{\infty }(-n)^{n-1}{\frac {z^{n}}{n!}}\\{}&=z-z^{2}+{\frac {3}{2}}z^{3}-{\frac {8}{3}}z^{4}+O(z^{5}).\end{aligned}}}

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Insert we^w in the local inverse formula and evaluate exponential derivatives at zero. Undergraduate complex power series and differentiation suffice.
