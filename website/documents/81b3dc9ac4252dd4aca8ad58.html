Statement

For every integer r\ge0 and real x\ge-2, (1+x)^r\ge1+rx. Use the convention 0^0=1.

Proof 1: Induction in steps of two

Bernoulli's inequality can be proved in the present case, in which {\displaystyle r} is a non-negative integer and {\displaystyle x\geq -2}, using mathematical induction in the following form:

  • we prove the inequality for {\displaystyle r\in \{0,1\}},
  • from validity for some r we deduce validity for {\displaystyle r+2}.

For {\displaystyle r=0},

{\displaystyle (1+x)^{0}\geq 1+0x}

is equivalent to {\displaystyle 1\geq 1} which is true.

Similarly, for {\displaystyle r=1} we have

{\displaystyle (1+x)^{r}=1+x\geq 1+rx.}

Now suppose the statement is true for {\displaystyle r=k}:

{\displaystyle (1+x)^{k}\geq 1+kx.}

Then it follows that

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}(1+x)^{k+2}&=(1+x)^{k}(1+x)^{2}\\&\geq (1+kx)\left(1+2x+x^{2}\right)\qquad \qquad \qquad {\text{ by hypothesis and }}(1+x)^{2}\geq 0\\&=1+2x+x^{2}+kx+2kx^{2}+kx^{3}\\&=1+(k+2)x+kx^{2}(x+2)+x^{2}\\&\geq 1+(k+2)x\end{aligned}}}

since {\displaystyle x^{2}\geq 0} as well as {\displaystyle x+2\geq 0}. By the modified induction we conclude the statement is true for every non-negative integer {\displaystyle r}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The two starting cases are equalities. Multiplying the induction hypothesis by the nonnegative square (1+x)^2 produces the remainder kx^2(x+2)+x^2, which is nonnegative on x>=-2. Two-step induction covers both parities. These are elementary polynomial and sign calculations.

Proof 2: Finite geometric sum and signs

For r=0 the assertion is equality. For r\ge1, Bernoulli's inequality

{\displaystyle (1+x)^{r}\geq 1+rx}

is equivalent to

{\displaystyle (1+x)^{r}-1-rx\geq 0,}

and by the formula for geometric series (using y = 1 + x) we get

{\displaystyle (1+x)^{r}-1=y^{r}-1=\left(\sum _{k=0}^{r-1}y^{k}\right)\cdot (y-1)=\left(\sum _{k=0}^{r-1}(1+x)^{k}\right)\cdot x}

which leads to

{\displaystyle (1+x)^{r}-1-rx=\left(\left(\sum _{k=0}^{r-1}(1+x)^{k}\right)-r\right)\cdot x=\left(\sum _{k=0}^{r-1}\left((1+x)^{k}-1\right)\right)\cdot x\geq 0.}

Now if {\displaystyle x\geq 0} then, by monotony of the powers, each summand {\displaystyle (1+x)^{k}-1=(1+x)^{k}-1^{k}\geq 0}, and therefore their sum is nonnegative, and hence so is its product with x in the last display.

If {\displaystyle 0\geq x\geq -2} then -1\le1+x\le1, so {\displaystyle 1\geq (1+x)^{k}} and thus all addends {\displaystyle (1+x)^{k}-1} are non-positive and hence so is their sum. Since the product of two non-positive numbers is non-negative, the last displayed inequality follows again.

Level justification
