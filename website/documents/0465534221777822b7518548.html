Statement

Write S^j=\{x\in\mathbb R^{j+1}:\|x\|_2=1\} and call a map odd if g(-x)=-g(x). For every integer n\ge1 and every continuous f:S^n\to\mathbb R^n, there exists x\in S^n with f(x)=f(-x).

Proof 1: Tucker labels and compactness (outline)

The Borsuk–Ulam theorem can be proved from Tucker's lemma. We use the following form: a triangulated n-ball whose vertices have labels in \{\pm1,\ldots,\pm n\}, with antipodal labels at antipodal boundary vertices, contains an edge with opposite labels. We also use the existence of arbitrarily fine antipodally symmetric triangulations of S^n compatible with its equator, so that a hemisphere is a triangulated n-ball.

Put g(x)=f(x)-f(-x). Then g:S^n\to\mathbb R^n is a continuous odd function, and a zero of g gives the required equality. Because g is continuous on a compact domain, it is uniformly continuous. Therefore, for every \epsilon>0, there is a \delta>0 such that, for every two points of S^n which are within \delta of each other, their images under g are within \epsilon of each other.

Choose such a triangulation of S^n with edges of length less than \delta. If g is zero at a vertex, we are done. Otherwise, label each vertex v of the triangulation with a label l(v)\in\{\pm1,\pm2,\ldots,\pm n\} in the following way:

  • The absolute value of the label is the index of the coordinate with the highest absolute value of g: |l(v)|=\arg\max_k(|g(v)_k|). In case of a tie, choose the least index.
  • The sign of the label is the sign of g at the above coordinate, so that: l(v)=\operatorname{sgn}(g(v)_{|l(v)|})|l(v)|.

Because g is odd, the labeling is also odd: l(-v)=-l(v). Hence, applying Tucker's lemma to a hemisphere of S^n yields two adjacent vertices u,v with opposite labels. Assume w.l.o.g., after renumbering the coordinates, that the labels are l(u)=1,l(v)=-1. By the definition of l, this means that in both g(u) and g(v), coordinate #1 is the largest coordinate in absolute value: in g(u) this coordinate is positive while in g(v) it is negative. By the construction of the triangulation, the distance between g(u) and g(v) is at most \epsilon, so in particular

|g(u)_1-g(v)_1|=|g(u)_1|+|g(v)_1|\leq\epsilon

(since g(u)_1 and g(v)_1 have opposite signs) and so |g(u)_1|\leq\epsilon. But since the largest coordinate of g(u) in absolute value is coordinate #1, this means that |g(u)_k|\leq\epsilon for each 1\leq k\leq n. So \|g(u)\|_2\leq\sqrt n\,\epsilon.

The above is true for every \epsilon>0; since S^n is compact there must hence be a point u in which \|g(u)\|_2=0.
