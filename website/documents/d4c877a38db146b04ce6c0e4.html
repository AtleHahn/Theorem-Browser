Statement

Let 1\le n\le m be integers and let f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^m be Lipschitz: |f(x)-f(x')|\le C|x-x'| for all x,x' and some finite C. Let A\subseteq\mathbb R^n be Lebesgue measurable and put N(f,A,y)=\#\{x\in A:f(x)=y\}, allowing the value +\infty. Write \mathcal L^n for Lebesgue measure and \mathcal H^n for normalized n-dimensional Hausdorff measure (agreeing with Lebesgue measure on \mathbb R^n). At points of differentiability let J_f(x)=\sqrt{\det(Df(x)^T Df(x))}; set J_f=0 on the Lebesgue-null set where f is not differentiable. The multiplicity function is \mathcal H^n-measurable, and

\int_A J_f(x)\,d\mathcal L^n(x)=\int_{\mathbb R^m}N(f,A,y)\,d\mathcal H^n(y).
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Hausdorff measure, measurability with respect to it and almost-everywhere Jacobians require substantial measure-theoretic preparation beyond introductory analysis; elementary determinant notation does not remove that burden.
