Let ABC be a nondegenerate Euclidean triangle, and extend BC past C to D. Then \angle ACD=\angle ABC+\angle CAB.

Proof 1: Subtract from the triangle-angle sum

Write a=\angle CAB, b=\angle BCA, c=\angle ABC and d=\angle ACD. If it is known that the sum of the measures of the angles in a triangle is 180^\circ, then the exterior angle theorem is proved as follows:

b+d=180^\circ,b+d=b+a+c,\therefore d=a+c.

High school only
Level justification

Subtract the common interior angle from the straight-angle and triangle-angle equations. Elementary plane geometry and linear algebraic rearrangement suffice.
