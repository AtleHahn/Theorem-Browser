Statement

Let X=U\cup V with U,V open and path connected and U\cap V nonempty and path connected. Fix w\in U\cap V and presentations

\pi_1(U,w)=\langle u_1,\ldots,u_k\mid\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_l\rangle,\quad \pi_1(V,w)=\langle v_1,\ldots,v_m\mid\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_n\rangle,\pi_1(U\cap V,w)=\langle w_1,\ldots,w_p\mid\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_q\rangle.

Let I,J be the inclusion-induced homomorphisms into the first two groups. Then

\pi_1(X,w)\cong\langle u_1,\ldots,u_k,v_1,\ldots,v_m\mid \alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_l,\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_n,I(w_i)J(w_i)^{-1}\ (1\le i\le p)\rangle.

Proof 1: Amalgam presentation recipe — outline

By the Seifert–Van Kampen theorem, under the stated hypotheses \pi_1(X,w) is the free product with amalgamation of \pi_1(U,w) and \pi_1(V,w), with respect to the (not necessarily injective) homomorphisms I:\pi_1(U\cap V,w)\to\pi_1(U,w) and J:\pi_1(U\cap V,w)\to\pi_1(V,w).

Given the group presentations in the statement, the amalgamation can be presented as

\pi_1(X,w)\cong \left\langle u_1,\ldots,u_k,v_1,\ldots,v_m \;\middle|\; \alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_l,\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_n, I(w_1)J(w_1)^{-1},\ldots,I(w_p)J(w_p)^{-1}\right\rangle.

In category theory, \pi_1(X,w) is the pushout, in the category of groups, of the diagram

\pi_1(U,w)\gets\pi_1(U\cap V,w)\to\pi_1(V,w).

The source states this presentation recipe without verifying its universal property.
