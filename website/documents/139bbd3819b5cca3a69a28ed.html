Statement

Let p be prime and n\ge1 an integer. Choose an \mathbb F_p-basis a_1,\ldots,a_n of E=\mathbb F_{p^n}. Then the norm form N(X)=N_{E/\mathbb F_p}(\sum_iX_i a_i) is a homogeneous polynomial of degree n whose only zero in \mathbb F_p^n is the origin.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement uses an extension of finite fields and its homogeneous norm form.

Proof 1: Take the norm of a basis expansion — outline

Using just one polynomial, we can take it to be the degree n polynomial given by the norm of X_1a_1+\cdots+X_na_n, where the elements a_1,\ldots,a_n form a basis of the finite field of order p^n over \mathbb F_p. The norm vanishes only at zero, and the basis expansion is zero precisely when all its coordinates are zero.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement uses a finite-field extension and its field norm as a homogeneous polynomial in basis coordinates. Retain beyond undergraduate provisionally: understanding the field norm construction and its polynomial interpretation is not confidently a small extension.
