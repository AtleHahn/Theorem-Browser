Statement

For a real algebraic irrational \alpha and \varepsilon>0, only finitely many reduced rational numbers x/y with integers x and y>0 satisfy |\alpha-x/y|<y^{-2-\varepsilon}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Algebraic irrational numbers are roots of integer polynomials, and the assertion bounds the number of rational fractions satisfying an explicit power inequality. Understanding this claim requires elementary arithmetic and algebra. This rating concerns understanding the statement, independently of the difficulty of its proof.

Proof 1: Specializing simultaneous approximation to one coordinate

The stated simultaneous approximation theorem says that for algebraic a_1,\ldots,a_n with 1,a_1,\ldots,a_n linearly independent over \mathbb Q, only finitely many rational tuples satisfy |a_i-x_i/y|<y^{-(1+1/n+\varepsilon)} for all i. Set n=1 and a_1=\alpha. Irrationality makes 1,\alpha linearly independent, and the exponent becomes 2+\varepsilon.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Set the dimension to one in the explicitly stated simultaneous approximation theorem and use rational linear independence of 1 and an irrational. Undergraduate linear algebra and real inequalities suffice; no subspace-theorem machinery is applied beyond its supplied consequence.
