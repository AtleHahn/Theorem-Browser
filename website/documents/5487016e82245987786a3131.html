Statement

Let P\in\mathbb R[x] have degree n\ge1 and n distinct real roots x_1<\cdots<x_n. Every complex zero of P' is real and lies in [x_1,x_n].

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Real and complex polynomial roots; Polynomial derivatives; Real intervals. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Apply Rolle’s theorem in the consecutive root intervals (outline)

For n\ge2, apply Rolle’s theorem between each consecutive pair x_i,x_{i+1}. This gives n-1 distinct real zeros of P' inside the root interval. Since P' has degree n-1, these exhaust its zeros. For n=1, the derivative is a nonzero constant and has no zeros.
