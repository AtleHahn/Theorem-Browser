Statement

Let \mathbf{Top} be the category of topological spaces and continuous maps and \mathbf{Set} the category of sets and functions. The forgetful functor U:\mathbf{Top}\to\mathbf{Set} is not monadic.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Monadicity of a forgetful functor requires substantial category-theoretic foundations. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Failure to reflect isomorphisms (outline)

A necessary condition in Beck's monadicity theorem is reflection of isomorphisms: if U(f) is an isomorphism, then f must be an isomorphism. Continuous bijections of topological spaces need not be homeomorphisms. Thus the forgetful functor does not reflect isomorphisms and is not monadic.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The continuous-bijection counterexample is elementary topology, but understanding the claimed failure of monadicity requires categories, monads and their algebra categories. That cumulative prerequisite burden is not confidently small.
