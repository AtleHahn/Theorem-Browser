Statement

The maximum chromatic number of a finite map on the torus is seven. Regions are connected and adjacency means sharing a boundary segment.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The torus is the familiar product of two circles or square with opposite edges identified. Finite map/graph coloring and crossing-free drawing are elementary, so the color bound requires no fundamental groups or surface classification.

Proof 1: Substitute the surface Euler characteristic

For closed surfaces with positive genus, the maximum number p of colors needed depends on the Euler characteristic \chi of the surface. Except for the Klein bottle, the Heawood map-color theorem gives

p=\left\lfloor\frac{7+\sqrt{49-24\chi}}2\right\rfloor,

where the outermost brackets denote the floor function.

The torus has Euler characteristic \chi=0 (and genus g=1) and thus p=7, so no more than seven colors are required to color any map on a torus. This upper bound of 7 is sharp: certain toroidal polyhedra, such as the Szilassi polyhedron, require seven colors. The map-color theorem and the stated sharpness example are used here without proof.
