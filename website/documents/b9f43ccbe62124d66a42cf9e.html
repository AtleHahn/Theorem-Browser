Statement

Let (X,\mathcal A,\mu) and (Y,\mathcal B,\nu) be sigma-finite measure spaces, with product measure \mu\times\nu. For measurable f:X\times Y\to\mathbb R, the product integral and both iterated integrals of |f| are equal, possibly infinite. If one of these three quantities is finite, the product and both iterated integrals of f are equal and finite (inner integrals interpreted almost everywhere).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Product measures, measurable functions and absolute Lebesgue integrability are beyond calculus.

Measure theory

none

Proof 1: Fubini–Tonelli theorem

Apply Tonelli’s theorem to |f|, giving equality of the three absolute-value integrals. If one is finite, the product integral of |f| is finite, so Fubini’s theorem applies to f.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

First apply Tonelli to the absolute value and then Fubini under the resulting integrability condition. General product-measure integration and almost-everywhere inner integrals require advanced analysis.
