Statement

Let X_1,X_2,\ldots be independent identically distributed real random variables with \mathbb EX_1=0, \mathbb EX_1^2=\sigma^2>0, and \rho=\mathbb E|X_1|^3<\infty. Let F_n be the distribution function of (X_1+\cdots+X_n)/(\sigma\sqrt n) and \Phi the standard normal distribution function. The two independent-summand error factors \psi_0=(\sum_{i=1}^n\sigma_i^2)^{-3/2}\sum_{i=1}^n\rho_i and \psi_1=(\sum_{i=1}^n\sigma_i^2)^{-1/2}\max_i(\rho_i/\sigma_i^2), where \sigma_i^2=\mathbb EX_i^2 and \rho_i=\mathbb E|X_i|^3, both equal \rho/(\sigma^3\sqrt n). Thus the general bounds \sup_x|F_n(x)-\Phi(x)|\le C_j\psi_j reduce to the identically distributed bound with constant C_j, for j=0,1.

Proof 1: Substitute the common moments — outline

In the case where the summands X1, ..., Xn have identical distributions

{\displaystyle \psi _{0}=\psi _{1}={\frac {\rho}{\sigma^{3}{\sqrt {n}}}},}

and thus the bounds stated by the identically distributed inequality and the two general bounds \sup_x|F_n(x)-\Phi(x)|\le C_j\psi_j, j=0,1, coincide apart from the constant.
