Statement

In the equality-free first-order language of membership, define a=b by \forall z(z\in a\leftrightarrow z\in b), and assume extensionality in the form (a=b)\Rightarrow\forall z(a\in z\leftrightarrow b\in z). Then for every formula \phi(x), with other parameters fixed and substitution into free occurrences understood, a=b implies \phi(a)\Rightarrow\phi(b).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Undergraduate set notation and quantified reasoning suffice after a brief introduction to formulas as recursively built expressions and free versus bound substitution. Equality and the required extensionality clause are supplied, and no model coding, completeness theorem or general set-theoretic development is needed.

Proof 1: Induction over membership atoms and formula constructors — outline

The definition of formulas is recursive, so a proof by induction is used. It suffices to use membership atoms and the constructors \neg, \land and \exists, with capture-free substitution.

Base formulas

Let {\displaystyle X,Y,Z}, be metavariables for any variables or sets, such that {\displaystyle X=Y}

Case 1: {\displaystyle \phi (X):(Z\in X)}

Assume {\displaystyle Z\in X}, then, by the definition of equality, {\displaystyle Z\in Y}, thus {\displaystyle (X=Y)\implies {\bigl [}Z\in X\Rightarrow Z\in Y{\bigr ]}}

Case 2: {\displaystyle \phi (X):(X\in Z)}

Assume {\displaystyle X\in Z}, then by the axiom of extensionality, {\displaystyle Y\in Z}, thus {\displaystyle (X=Y)\implies {\bigl [}X\in Z\Rightarrow Y\in Z{\bigr ]}}

These membership replacements apply successively when the variable occurs in both positions of an atom.

Recursive formulas

Let {\displaystyle \psi ,\varphi } be meta variables for any formulas each satisfying, with \chi denoting that formula, {\displaystyle (a=b)\implies {\bigl [}\chi (a)\Rightarrow \chi (b){\bigr ]}}. Let {\displaystyle X,Y}, be metavariables for any variables or sets, such that {\displaystyle X=Y}, and let {\displaystyle z} be a metavariable for any variable.

Case 1: {\displaystyle \neg (\psi )}

Since {\displaystyle X=Y}, then {\displaystyle Y=X} by symmetry of equality, therefore {\displaystyle {\bigl [}\psi (Y)\Rightarrow \psi (X){\bigr ]}}, by the induction hypothesis, therefore {\displaystyle {\bigl [}\neg \psi (X)\Rightarrow \neg \psi (Y){\bigr ]}} by contraposition, thus {\displaystyle (X=Y)\implies {\bigl [}\neg \psi (X)\Rightarrow \neg \psi (Y){\bigr ]}}

Case 2: {\displaystyle \psi \land \varphi }

Since {\displaystyle X=Y}, then {\displaystyle {\bigl [}\psi (X)\Rightarrow \psi (Y){\bigr ]}} and {\displaystyle {\bigl [}\varphi (X)\Rightarrow \varphi (Y){\bigr ]}}, which implies {\displaystyle {\bigl [}{\bigl (}\psi (X)\land \varphi (X){\bigr )}\Rightarrow {\bigl (}\psi (Y)\land \varphi (Y){\bigr )}{\bigr ]}}, thus {\displaystyle (X=Y)\implies {\bigl [}{\bigl (}\psi (X)\land \varphi (X){\bigr )}\Rightarrow {\bigl (}\psi (Y)\land \varphi (Y){\bigr )}{\bigr ]}}

Case 3: {\displaystyle \exists z(\psi )}

Since {\displaystyle X=Y}, {\displaystyle \psi (X,z)\Rightarrow \psi (Y,z)} assume by way of contradiction that the result is false, that is {\displaystyle \exists z(\psi (X,z))} is true but {\displaystyle \exists z(\psi (Y,z))} is false. By existential instantiation, let {\displaystyle z_{0}} denote the value such that {\displaystyle \psi (X,z_{0})} is true. Then {\displaystyle \psi (Y,z_{0})} is false by assumption, and therefore {\displaystyle \psi (X,z_{0})\Rightarrow \psi (Y,z_{0})} is false, which contradicts our induction hypothesis, and the result follows.
