Statement

For a multi-index \alpha=(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n)\in\mathbb N_0^n, write |\alpha|=\sum_i\alpha_i, \alpha!=\prod_i\alpha_i!, v^\alpha=\prod_i v_i^{\alpha_i}, and D^\alpha f=\partial_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots\partial_n^{\alpha_n}f. Let m\ge0 be an integer and let f be a real-valued C^m function on a neighborhood of the segment from a to x in \mathbb R^n, and put g(t)=f(a+t(x-a)). For integers 0\le j\le m and t\in[0,1], g^{(j)}(t)=\sum_{|\alpha|=j}\frac{j!}{\alpha!}D^\alpha f(a+t(x-a))(x-a)^\alpha.

Proof 1: Iterate the chain rule along the segment — outline

Parametrize the line segment between a and x by u(t)=a+t(x-a) and put g(t)=f(u(t)).

Applying the chain rule for several variables gives

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}g^{(j)}(t)&={\frac {d^{j}}{dt^{j}}}f({\boldsymbol {u}}(t))\\&={\frac {d^{j}}{dt^{j}}}f({\boldsymbol {a}}+t({\boldsymbol {x}}-{\boldsymbol {a}}))\\&=\sum _{|\alpha |=j}\left({\begin{matrix}j\\\alpha \end{matrix}}\right)(D^{\alpha }f)({\boldsymbol {a}}+t({\boldsymbol {x}}-{\boldsymbol {a}}))({\boldsymbol {x}}-{\boldsymbol {a}})^{\alpha }\end{aligned}}}

where {\displaystyle {\tbinom {j}{\alpha }}} is the multinomial coefficient. Here \binom j\alpha=j!/\alpha!; continuous mixed derivatives commute, and this coefficient counts their possible orders.

Level justification

Iterate the chain rule along a constant-direction line, commute the continuous mixed derivatives and count their orders by multinomial coefficients. Undergraduate multivariable calculus suffices.
