Statement

For conformable U,V over a unital ring, if I+UV and I+VU are invertible, then (I+UV)^{-1}=I-U(I+VU)^{-1}V.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory ring/group theory and linear algebra supply polynomial coefficients, ideals, determinants, bases and matrix products. Elementary transvections are explicitly given elementary row-operation matrices, and division ring means every nonzero element has an inverse. These direct definitions suffice without specialist theory.

Proof 1: Combine inverse expansion and push-through

To derive (I+UV)^{-1}=I-U(I+VU)^{-1}V, combine two identities. For a square matrix P with I+P invertible, the first follows from

{\displaystyle I=(I+P)^{-1}(I+P)=(I+P)^{-1}+(I+P)^{-1}P,}

thus,

{\displaystyle (I+P)^{-1}=I-(I+P)^{-1}P,}

and similarly

{\displaystyle (I+P)^{-1}=I-P(I+P)^{-1}.}

The second identity is the so-called push-through identity

{\displaystyle (I+UV)^{-1}U=U(I+VU)^{-1}}

that we obtain from

{\displaystyle U(I+VU)=(I+UV)U}

after multiplying by {\displaystyle (I+VU)^{-1}} on the right and by {\displaystyle (I+UV)^{-1}} on the left.

Putting all together,

{\displaystyle \left(I+UV\right)^{-1}=I-UV\left(I+UV\right)^{-1}=I-U\left(I+VU\right)^{-1}V.}

where the first and second equality come from the first and second identity, respectively.

Level justification

Combine the inverse expansion with the explicitly derived push-through identity. Undergraduate matrix algebra suffices.
