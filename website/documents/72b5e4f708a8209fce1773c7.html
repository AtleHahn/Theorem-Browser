Statement

Let \mathfrak g be a finite-dimensional nonabelian simple complex Lie algebra, and let \kappa(a,b)=\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{ad}(a)\operatorname{ad}(b)) be its Killing form, where \operatorname{ad}(a)(v)=[a,v]. Define the affine Lie algebra

\widehat{\mathfrak g}=(\mathfrak g\otimes_{\mathbb C}\mathbb C[t,t^{-1}])\oplus\mathbb Cc

by declaring c central and, for a,b\in\mathfrak g and integers j,k,

[a\otimes t^j,b\otimes t^k]=[a,b]\otimes t^{j+k}+j\,\delta_{j+k,0}\,\kappa(a,b)c,

extended bilinearly. Here \mathbb C[t,t^{-1}] is the algebra of finite Laurent polynomials and \delta_{r,0} equals 1 if r=0 and 0 otherwise.

The radical of \widehat{\mathfrak g} is its center \mathbb Cc, but there is no Lie subalgebra \mathfrak s with

\widehat{\mathfrak g}=\mathbb Cc\oplus\mathfrak s

as a vector-space direct sum. Thus this central radical has no Lie-subalgebra complement.

A Lie algebra has a bilinear alternating bracket satisfying the Jacobi identity. Simple means nonabelian with no ideals other than zero and the whole algebra. An ideal I satisfies [\mathfrak h,I]\subseteq I; it is solvable if its repeated derived subspaces I^{(0)}=I, I^{(r+1)}=[I^{(r)},I^{(r)}] eventually vanish. The radical here is the largest solvable ideal. The center consists of elements commuting with every element.
