Statement

Define F:[0,1]\rightrightarrows[0,1] by F(x)=\{3/4\} for x<1/2, F(1/2)=[0,1], and F(x)=\{1/4\} for x>1/2. A map is lower hemicontinuous at x_0 if every open set V meeting F(x_0) also meets F(x) throughout some neighbourhood of x_0. This F is not lower hemicontinuous at 1/2.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Set-valued functions; Lower hemicontinuity; Relative open sets. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Test an open interval around the left value — outline

The function

F(x)= \begin{cases} \{3/4\},&0\leq x<0.5,\\ [0,1],&x=0.5,\\ \{1/4\},&0.5<x\leq1 \end{cases}

is a set-valued function from the real interval [0,1] to itself. It has nonempty convex closed values. However, it is not lower hemicontinuous at 0.5.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Choose an open interval around three quarters that misses one quarter and compare values just to the right of one half. Undergraduate neighborhood continuity reasoning suffices; the set-valued condition is supplied locally.
