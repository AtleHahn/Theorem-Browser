Littlewood bounds imply prime-counting oscillation

Prime-counting errors eventually reverse inequalities that may appear stable over long finite ranges. This demonstrates why extensive numerical evidence cannot by itself establish a universal mathematical law.

Statement

Let \pi(x) be the number of primes at most x, and let \operatorname{li}(x) be the unoffset logarithmic integral \operatorname{li}(x)=\operatorname{PV}\int_0^x dt/\log t for x>1. The difference \pi(x)-\operatorname{li}(x) takes both positive and negative values for arbitrarily large real x.

Proof 1: Compare the two explicit Littlewood bounds

Littlewood’s external bounds give arbitrarily large x with \pi(x)>\operatorname{li}(x)+\frac13\frac{\sqrt{x}}{\log x}\log\log\log x, and arbitrarily large x with \pi(x)<\operatorname{li}(x)-\frac13\frac{\sqrt{x}}{\log x}\log\log\log x. The error term is positive for sufficiently large x, so the difference has each sign arbitrarily far out.

Level justification

Observe positivity of the explicit error term for large x and read off both signs from the supplied bounds. Undergraduate calculus suffices to understand the logarithmic integral, with principal value as a single symmetric-limit definition; no analytic number theory proof is used.
