Statement

A doubly stochastic n\times n real matrix has nonnegative entries and every row and column sums to 1. A permutation matrix has entries 0,1 with exactly one 1 in each row and column. For every integer n\ge1 and doubly stochastic X, there are finitely many permutation matrices P_1,\ldots,P_k and real numbers \theta_i\ge0 with \sum_i\theta_i=1 such that X=\sum_i\theta_iP_i.

Level

Level justification

Proof 1: Hall matching and successive support reduction — outline

Let X be a doubly stochastic matrix. Then we will show that there exists a permutation matrix P such that x_{ij} ≠ 0 whenever p_{ij} ≠ 0. Thus if we let \lambda be the smallest x_{ij} corresponding to a non-zero p_{ij}, the difference X – \lambda P will be a nonnegative scalar multiple of a doubly stochastic matrix and will have at least one more zero cell than X. Accordingly we may successively reduce the number of non-zero cells in X by applying the same argument to each normalized nonzero remainder and removing scalar multiples of permutation matrices until we arrive at the zero matrix, at which point we will have constructed a convex combination of permutation matrices equal to the original X.

For instance if {\displaystyle X={\frac {1}{12}}{\begin{pmatrix}7&0&5\\2&6&4\\3&6&3\end{pmatrix}}} then {\displaystyle P={\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\1&0&0\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix}}}, {\displaystyle \lambda ={\frac {2}{12}}}, and {\displaystyle X-\lambda P={\frac {1}{12}}{\begin{pmatrix}7&0&3\\0&6&4\\3&4&3\end{pmatrix}}}.

To find the required permutation matrix, construct a bipartite graph in which the rows of X are listed in one part and the columns in the other, and in which row i is connected to column j iff x_{ij} ≠ 0. Let {\displaystyle A} be any set of rows, and define {\displaystyle A'} as the set of columns joined to rows in A in the graph. We want to express the sizes {\displaystyle |A|} and {\displaystyle |A'|} of the two sets in terms of the x_{ij}.

For every i in A, the sum over j in A' of x_{ij} is 1, since all columns j for which x_{ij} ≠ 0 are included in A', and X is doubly stochastic; hence {\displaystyle |A|} is the sum over all i ∈ A, j ∈ A' of x_{ij}.

Meanwhile {\displaystyle |A'|} is the sum over all i (whether or not in A) and all j in A' of x_{ij} ; and this is ≥ the corresponding sum in which the i are limited to rows in A. Hence {\displaystyle |A'|\geq |A|}.

It follows that the conditions of Hall's marriage theorem are satisfied, and that we can therefore find a set of edges in the graph which join each row in X to exactly one (distinct) column. These edges define a permutation matrix whose non-zero cells correspond to non-zero cells in X.

Level justification

The proof checks Hall’s condition by row and column sums, finds a supported permutation, subtracts the smallest supported entry and normalizes the remainder. Each iteration removes a positive entry, so the finite process terminates, and row sums give total coefficient one. Matrices, finite convex combinations and elementary graph matching are a modest extension of undergraduate linear algebra.
