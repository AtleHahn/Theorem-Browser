Statement

Let M be a closed, oriented, connected smooth manifold of dimension n\ge2. Suppose it admits a transversely oriented codimension-one foliation of class C^1, with a nonempty set of singularities, all centers. Then there are exactly two singularities and M is homeomorphic to S^n. Closed means compact without boundary. Off its singularities, a codimension-one foliation is locally a partition into hypersurfaces given by one coordinate being constant; these connected pieces fit together into leaves. Transverse orientation is a consistent orientation of the one-dimensional direction normal to the leaves. A center is an isolated singularity near which the leaves are level sets of a function with a nondegenerate local maximum or minimum: in local coordinates its level sets are concentric (n-1)-spheres around the singular point. The sphere S^n is \{x\in\mathbb R^{n+1}:|x|=1\}.

Proof 1: Stability and two center neighborhoods (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorems A and C; Section 3.2, pp. 203–207, especially Proposition 6 and Lemma 4.).

For n>=3, an isolated center is a point component of the Bott–Morse singular set; Theorems A and C yield two point bases and two n-disks glued along their boundary, hence S^n. For n=2, orientation and transverse orientation orient the tangent line field off the centers. Choose a continuous tangent vector field that vanishes only at the finitely many centers, with index +1 at each (use the circular local model). Poincare–Hopf gives number of centers=chi(M)=2-2g>0. Thus g=0 and there are exactly two centers. The source's p.191 explicitly states the C^r extension for r>=1.
