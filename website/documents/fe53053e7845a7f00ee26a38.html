Statement

For a<b, let S\subset[a,b] be a non-Lebesgue-measurable set and set f=\chi_S-1/2. Then |f| is Lebesgue integrable on [a,b], but f is not measurable and hence is not Lebesgue integrable.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The construction uses nonmeasurable sets and Lebesgue integration.

Proof 1: A shifted nonmeasurable indicator

Let {\displaystyle S\subset [a,b]} be a nonmeasurable subset and consider {\displaystyle f=\chi _{S}-1/2,} where {\displaystyle \chi _{S}} is the characteristic function of {\displaystyle S.} Then {\displaystyle f} is not Lebesgue measurable and thus not integrable, but {\displaystyle |f|\equiv 1/2} is a constant function and clearly integrable.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Contrast the nonmeasurable shifted indicator with its constant integrable modulus. Although the arithmetic is elementary, the claim distinguishes Lebesgue measurability from Lebesgue integrability on arbitrary nonmeasurable sets; keep beyond undergraduate rather than assume this full framework is a small extension.
