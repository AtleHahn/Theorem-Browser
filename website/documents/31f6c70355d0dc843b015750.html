Statement

There is a continuous complex-linear extension operator E:C^\infty([0,\infty);\mathbb C)\to C^\infty(\mathbb R;\mathbb C), where derivatives on the half-line extend continuously to zero and both spaces carry uniform convergence of every derivative on compact sets. It agrees with f on [0,\infty) and maps functions supported in [0,R] into functions supported in [-R,R].

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement uses topology on infinite-dimensional spaces of smooth functions.

Proof 1: Moment coefficients and reflected dilations — outline

Coefficients

There exist sequences of complex coefficients a_m and positive real numbers b_m=2^m such that, for every integer j\ge0,

\sum_{m=1}^\infty a_m b_m^j=(-1)^j,\qquad \sum_{m=1}^\infty|a_m|b_m^j<\infty.

A solution to this system of equations can be obtained by taking b_m=2^m and seeking an entire function

g(z)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}a_mz^m

such that g(2^j)=(-1)^j for j\geq0, with g(0)=0. That such a function can be constructed follows from the Weierstrass theorem and Mittag-Leffler theorem. Its power series converges absolutely at every 2^j, giving the stated moment equations and absolute convergence.

Extension formula

For x\ge0 set E(f)(x)=f(x). For x<0, use

{\displaystyle \displaystyle {E(f)(x)=\sum _{m=1}^{\infty }a_{m}f(-b_{m}x)\varphi (-b_{m}x)\,\,\,(x<0),}}

where φ is a smooth function of compact support on R equal to 1 near 0 and the sequences (am), (bm) satisfy:

  • {\displaystyle b_{m}>0} tends to {\displaystyle \infty };
  • {\displaystyle \sum a_{m}b_{m}^{j}=(-1)^{j}} for {\displaystyle j\geq 0} with the sum absolutely convergent.

Take b_m=2^m and the coefficients just obtained. This is the source’s Seeley extension operator. The source states smoothness, continuity and the support property without supplying their verification.
