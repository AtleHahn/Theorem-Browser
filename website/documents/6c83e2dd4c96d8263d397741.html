Statement

Let G=(V,E) be a finite undirected simple graph, where edges join distinct vertices. A matching is a set of edges with no shared endpoints. Write \nu(G) for its largest possible size, and \operatorname{odd}(G-U) for the number of connected components of odd order after deleting a vertex subset U. The graph has a perfect matching (one covering every vertex) if and only if \operatorname{odd}(G-U)\le|U| for every U\subseteq V.

Proof 1: Set the unmatched-vertex maximum to zero

Use the Tutte–Berge formula:

\nu(G)=\frac12\min_{U\subseteq V}\bigl(|U|-\operatorname{odd}(G-U)+|V|\bigr).

The graphs with perfect matchings are the ones for which deleting any subset U of vertices creates at most |U| odd components. A subset U that creates at least |U| odd components can always be found in the empty set. In this case, by the Tutte–Berge formula, the size of the matching is |V|/2; that is, the maximum matching is a perfect matching.

Conversely, if there is a perfect matching, the formula forces \operatorname{odd}(G-U)\le|U| for every U. Thus, Tutte's theorem can be derived as a corollary of the Tutte–Berge formula.
