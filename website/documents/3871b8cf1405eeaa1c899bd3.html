Statement

Let f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R be 2\pi-periodic and satisfy a Hölder condition: there are C\geq0 and \alpha>0 such that |f(x)-f(y)|\leq C|x-y|^\alpha for all real x,y. Then its Fourier series converges uniformly to f.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Real analysis supplies uniform convergence and the explicit Holder condition. Fourier coefficients are ordinary integrals of this continuous periodic function against sines and cosines, a short calculus-based definition without Lp theory.

Proof 1: Apply the Dini–Lipschitz test to the Hölder condition — outline

For the periodic function, write \omega_f(\delta)=\max_t\max_{|\varepsilon|\leq\delta}|f(t)-f(t+\varepsilon)|. The Dini–Lipschitz test states that \omega_f(\delta)=o(1/\log(1/\delta)) as \delta\downarrow0 implies uniform convergence of the Fourier series to f. Any function obeying a Hölder condition satisfies this test, giving the conclusion.
