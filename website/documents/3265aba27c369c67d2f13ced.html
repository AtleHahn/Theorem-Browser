Simpler proof assessment

Likely alternative prerequisite level: High school only; lowest recorded proof level: Undergraduate level. The Prufer bijection repeatedly removes the smallest labeled leaf, recording its neighbor, to give a word of length n-2 over n labels. The inverse repeatedly joins the smallest label absent from the remaining word to its first label, then joins the last two labels. This handles n=2 by the empty word and proves the exact count using finite algorithms and induction, replacing eigenvalues and the matrix-tree theorem.

Statement

For n\ge2, the number of trees on the labeled vertex set \{1,\ldots,n\} is n^{n-2}. A tree is a connected graph with no cycles; a spanning tree contains every vertex of the given graph.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Proof 1: Cayley's formula

Here the Laplacian is L=D-A, where D is the diagonal vertex-degree matrix and A is the adjacency matrix. The labeled trees in the statement are precisely the spanning trees of the complete graph K_n.

Cayley's formula follows from Kirchhoff's theorem as a special case, since every vector with 1 in one place, −1 in another place, and 0 elsewhere is an eigenvector of the Laplacian matrix of the complete graph, with the corresponding eigenvalue being n. These vectors together span a space of dimension n − 1, so there are no other non-zero eigenvalues.

The spectral form of Kirchhoff's theorem says that the number of spanning trees is \frac1n times the product of the n-1 nonzero Laplacian eigenvalues. Here this gives

\frac1n n^{n-1}=n^{n-2}.
