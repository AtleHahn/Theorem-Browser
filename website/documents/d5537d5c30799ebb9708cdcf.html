Statement

Let k\ge2 and t\ge k be integers and put m=\lfloor(t-1)/(k-1)\rfloor. Partition an n-vertex set, where n\ge0 is an integer, into m parts. Form a k-uniform hypergraph by taking exactly those k-element sets not contained in a single part. It contains no complete k-uniform hypergraph on t vertices, and if the part sizes are s_1,\ldots,s_m, it has \binom nk-\sum_j\binom{s_j}k hyperedges, with \binom sk=0 for integers 0\le s<k.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Hyperedges are explicitly k-element subsets not contained in a partition part. The assertion combines this finite-set rule with a binomial count and a forbidden complete subset. Floor, binomial coefficients and finite partitions are elementary; the hypergraph name introduces no additional theory.

Proof 1: A missing k-subset in one part — outline

This is the source complement-of-disjoint-cliques construction. Among any t vertices, some part has at least k of them, because otherwise there would be at most m(k-1)\le t-1 vertices. Those k vertices do not form a hyperedge, preventing a complete t-vertex hypergraph. Its omitted hyperedges are exactly the k-sets lying in the separate parts; subtracting their counts gives the displayed total.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The pigeonhole count forces k of any t vertices into one part, giving a missing hyperedge. Subtract the disjoint counts of omitted k-subsets. Only finite combinatorics is used.
