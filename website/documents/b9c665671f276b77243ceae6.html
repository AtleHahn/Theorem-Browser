Statement

Let H,K be nontrivial finitely generated groups and G=H*K their free product. Then G has more than one end. For a locally finite connected graph, the number of ends is the supremum of the numbers of infinite components left after deleting finitely many edges; the number of ends of a finitely generated group is that of any finite-generator Cayley graph.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding free products, Cayley graphs and ends of infinite locally finite graphs requires abstract algebra and geometric group theory.

Proof 1: Cut defined by the first free-product syllable — outline

A cut in a Cayley graph is a partition (A,A^*) whose crossing edges form a finite set; it is essential when both vertex sets are infinite. Use the cuts-and-ends criterion: a Cayley graph has more than one end if and only if it has an essential cut.

Let X and Y be finite generating sets for H and K accordingly, so that S=X\cup Y is a finite generating set for G, and let \Gamma=\Gamma(G,S) be the Cayley graph of G with respect to S, with edges joining g to gs for s\in S. Let A consist of the trivial element and all the elements of G whose normal form expressions for G=H*K start with a nontrivial element of H. Thus A^* consists of all elements of G whose normal form expressions for G=H*K start with a nontrivial element of K. It is not hard to see that (A,A^*) is an essential cut in \Gamma, so that e(G)>1.
