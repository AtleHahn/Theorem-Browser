Statement

Let p be prime, f\in\mathbb Z[X], and k\ge1. Suppose every root of f\bmod p^k has derivative nonzero modulo p. For every \ell\ge k, reduction from roots modulo p^\ell to roots modulo p^k is a bijection. In particular their numbers agree. Here the formal derivative is (\sum_j c_jX^j)^\prime=\sum_{j\ge1}jc_jX^{j-1}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Roots and reduction concern integer remainders at successive prime powers. The formal derivative is explicitly a finite polynomial coefficient rule; uniqueness of lifts is a finite one-to-one correspondence, without p-adic completions or calculus.

Proof 1: Stability of the number of simple modular roots — proof

Using simple-root Hensel lifting (each root modulo p^k with derivative nonzero modulo p has exactly one lift modulo p^{k+1}), one can "lift" a root r of the polynomial f modulo pk to a new root s modulo pk+1 such that r ≡ s mod pk (by taking m = 1; taking larger m follows by induction). In fact, a root modulo pk+1 is also a root modulo pk, so the roots modulo pk+1 are precisely the liftings of roots modulo pk. The new root s is congruent to r modulo p, so the new root also satisfies {\displaystyle f'(s)\equiv f'(r)\not \equiv 0{\bmod {p}}.} So the lifting can be repeated, and starting from a solution rk of {\displaystyle f(x)\equiv 0{\bmod {p}}^{k}} we can derive a sequence of solutions rk+1, rk+2, ... of the same congruence for successively higher powers of p, provided that {\displaystyle f'(r_{k})\not \equiv 0{\bmod {p}}} for the initial root rk. This also shows that f has the same number of roots mod pk as mod pk+1, mod pk+2, or any other higher power of p, provided that the roots of f mod pk are all simple.

Level justification

Iterate the supplied unique finite lifting rule and note that reduction preserves the nonzero formal derivative. Congruences, induction and finite one-to-one correspondence suffice; no p-adic limit is involved.
