Statement

Let S be a nonempty bounded subset of a metric space (M,d_M), put d=\sup_{p,q\in S}d_M(p,q), and r=\inf_{c\in M}\sup_{p\in S}d_M(c,p). Then d/2\leq r\leq d.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Metric spaces, compactness, Cauchy sequences, infima and suprema belong to introductory analysis/topology. Total boundedness and enclosing radius are explicitly defined using metric balls and distances.

Proof 1: Use the triangle inequality and center a ball in the set

The first inequality is implied by the triangle inequality for the center of an enclosing ball and any two points of S: if the ball has radius \rho, then

d_M(p,q)\leq d_M(p,c)+d_M(c,q)\leq2\rho.

Taking the supremum over p,q\in S and the infimum over enclosing radii gives d/2\leq r. The second inequality follows since a closed ball of radius d centered at any point of S will contain all of S.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the triangle inequality before taking suprema and infima, and use a point of S as a center for the upper bound. Undergraduate metric spaces and real bounds suffice.
