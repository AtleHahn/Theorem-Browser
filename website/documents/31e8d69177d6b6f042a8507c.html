Statement

For discrete random variables X_1,\ldots,X_d with Shannon entropies, H(X_1,\ldots,X_d)\le\sum_{j=1}^d H(X_j).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

For discrete variables, joint and marginal laws are probability tables or countable lists, and Shannon entropy is the sum of -p log p with zero terms omitted. Marginalization and this one nonnegative-series definition form a small extension of undergraduate sums, functions and real analysis; no entropy of general measure spaces or stochastic convergence is asserted.

Proof 1: Choose singleton coordinate sets

Shearer’s inequality states that if subsets S_1,\ldots,S_n of \{1,\ldots,d\} cover each index at least r\ge1 times, then H(X_1,\ldots,X_d)\le r^{-1}\sum_i H((X_j)_{j\in S_i}). Choose n=d and S_i=\{i\}. Each index is covered once, so r=1, and the inequality becomes exactly the stated subadditivity bound.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Discrete Shannon entropy is a sum of probability-logarithm terms. Apply the fully stated coordinate-cover inequality to singleton subsets, each with cover multiplicity one. Elementary probability and real series suffice; no measure-theoretic entropy theory is needed.
