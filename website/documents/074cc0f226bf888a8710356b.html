Statement

For an open \Omega\subseteq\mathbb C, the vector space H^\infty(\Omega) of bounded holomorphic functions \Omega\to\mathbb C, with norm \|f\|_\infty=\sup_{z\in\Omega}|f(z)|, is complete. For \Omega empty, the unique function has norm zero.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Real analysis supplies derivatives, maxima, uniform convergence and completeness; complex arithmetic supplies the plane. Holomorphic means complex differentiable, and entire means so throughout the plane. With that short extension, the displayed bound or convergence assertion uses familiar analytic concepts without contour integrals or specialist function-space theory.

Proof 1: Take a bounded uniform limit and preserve holomorphy

Use completeness of bounded complex-valued functions under the supremum norm. A Cauchy sequence f_1,f_2,\ldots in H^\infty(\Omega) therefore has a bounded uniform limit f, which is continuous.

On any open disc contained in \Omega, by Cauchy's theorem, we know that

\oint_C f_n(z)\,dz=0

for every n, along any closed piecewise continuously differentiable curve C in the disc. Then the uniform convergence implies that

\oint_C f(z)\,dz =\oint_C\lim_{n\to\infty}f_n(z)\,dz =\lim_{n\to\infty}\oint_C f_n(z)\,dz =0

for every such closed curve C, and therefore by Morera's theorem f must be holomorphic.

This shows that, for any open set \Omega\subseteq\mathbb C, the set H^\infty(\Omega) of all bounded, analytic functions u:\Omega\to\mathbb C is a Banach space with respect to the supremum norm.
