Statement

Consider n\ge3 distinct lines in the Euclidean plane, with no parallel pair and no three concurrent. A triangular face means a bounded triangular region containing no other arrangement line in its interior. In a bounded face with at least four sides, at most two sides have adjacent interior angles whose sum is less than 180 degrees. If there are two, they are adjacent.

Proof 1: Use the exterior-angle sum

An edge e of a face f is called a positive side of f if the sum of the two angles made by e with its adjacent sides in f is less than 180^\circ.

Every bounded face that is not a triangle has at most two positive sides, and if there are two then they are adjacent. Indeed, two nonadjacent positive sides would force the corresponding exterior angles to have total greater than 360^\circ.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Each bounded arrangement face is convex. A positive side has the sum of the two adjacent exterior angles greater than 180 degrees. Two nonadjacent such sides would use four distinct vertices and force total exterior angle greater than 360 degrees. Any three sides of a polygon with at least four sides contain two nonadjacent sides. These are elementary polygon geometry and finite counting.
