Proof 1: Euler formula and irrational powers of pi

Euler’s formula states that if n is a positive integer then

{\displaystyle {\frac {1}{1^{2n}}}+{\frac {1}{2^{2n}}}+{\frac {1}{3^{2n}}}+{\frac {1}{4^{2n}}}+\cdots ={\frac {p}{q}}\pi ^{2n}}

for some rational number {\displaystyle p/q}. Specifically, writing the infinite series on the left as {\displaystyle \zeta (2n)}, he showed

{\displaystyle \zeta (2n)=(-1)^{n+1}{\frac {B_{2n}(2\pi )^{2n}}{2(2n)!}}}

where the {\displaystyle B_{n}} are the rational Bernoulli numbers. Once it was proved that {\displaystyle \pi ^{n}} is always irrational, this showed that {\displaystyle \zeta (2n)} is irrational for all positive integers n.
