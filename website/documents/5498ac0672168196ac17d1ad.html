Statement

Let a,b,x,y\in\mathbb R with a>0 and a^2=1+b^2+x^2+y^2. Put A=\begin{pmatrix}a+b&x+iy\\x-iy&a-b\end{pmatrix},\qquad g_t=\begin{pmatrix}\cosh t&i\sinh t\\-i\sinh t&\cosh t\end{pmatrix}. Conjugation A\mapsto g_tAg_t^* leaves b,x unchanged and sends (a,y) to (a\cosh2t+y\sinh2t,\ y\cosh2t+a\sinh2t). Here t\in\mathbb R and g_t^* denotes conjugate transpose.

Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Complex matrices and conjugate transpose; Hyperbolic-function identities. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. Proof 1: Matrix boost fixes two hyperboloid coordinates — source argument

Multiplication gives g_tAg_t^*=\begin{pmatrix}a\cosh2t+y\sinh2t+b&x+i(y\cosh2t+a\sinh2t)\\x-i(y\cosh2t+a\sinh2t)&a\cosh2t+y\sinh2t-b\end{pmatrix}. Comparing entries with the defining coordinates of A gives the asserted transformation and the two fixed coordinates.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Multiply the two-by-two complex matrices and compare entries using double-angle identities. Undergraduate matrix algebra suffices; no Lorentz representation theory is used.
