Statement

If a function assigns N distinct objects to at most m<N hash values, two distinct objects have the same hash value.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Understanding the claim requires finite counts, elementary arithmetic and assignment of objects to finitely many values or classes.

Mathematical Field
Combinatorics

Review notes

Proof 1: Pigeonhole counting

Use the pigeonhole principle: more than m objects assigned to m classes force two objects into one class.

Take the objects as the items and the hash values as the classes. Since there are more objects than possible values, the assignment has a collision.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Apply the pigeonhole principle to the explicitly finite objects and possible output values. Hash terminology introduces no additional mathematics.
