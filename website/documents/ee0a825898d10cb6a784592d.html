Statement

Let \varphi:A\to\mathcal L(H) be a -representation of a C-algebra on a Hilbert space, and let P be an orthogonal projection commuting with every \varphi(a). Then \varphi is the orthogonal direct sum of its restrictions to PH and (I-P)H.

Proof 1: Restrict to the two projection ranges — outline

If there exists a projection P in \mathcal L(H) where P\varphi(a)=\varphi(a)P for every a in A, then a subrepresentation \sigma of \varphi can be defined in a natural way: \sigma(a) is \varphi(a) restricted to the range of P. So \varphi then can be expressed as a direct sum of two subrepresentations \varphi=\varphi'\oplus\sigma, where \varphi' is the restriction to (I-P)H.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The invariant range calculation is simple, but the full claim concerns star-representations of C-star algebras, Hilbert-space reducing subspaces and representation direct sums. Those operator-algebra objects exceed a short baseline extension.
