Statement

For a nondegenerate convex minor-arc triangle ABC on the unit sphere, let A'B'C' be its polar triangle, with side lengths supplementary to the respective angles of ABC. If p' is its perimeter and \varepsilon is the area of ABC, then \varepsilon=2\pi-p'. Here |PQ| denotes minor great-circle angular length.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Spherical triangles use great-circle arcs and ordinary surface area on the unit sphere. The polar triangle is characterized by supplementary side/angle lengths; these concrete geometric definitions suffice without Riemannian-manifold theory.

Proof 1: Substitute the supplementary polar sides

Because each side of the dual triangle is the supplement of an internal angle of the original triangle, the spherical excess \varepsilon of \triangle ABC is a function of the perimeter p' of the dual triangle \triangle A'B'C':

\begin{aligned} \varepsilon&=\angle A+\angle B+\angle C-\pi\\ &=(\pi-|B'C'|)+(\pi-|C'A'|)+(\pi-|A'B'|)-\pi\\ &=2\pi-p', \end{aligned}

where the notation |PQ| means the angular length of the great-circle arc PQ. The first equality is Girard's theorem for the area on the unit sphere.
