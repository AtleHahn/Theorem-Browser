Statement

Let a,b be nonzero integers, neither dividing the other, and let d=\gcd(a,b). Exactly two integer pairs (x,y) satisfy ax+by=d, |x|<|b/d|, and |y|<|a/d|. If a,b>0, one pair has x>0>y and the other has y>0>x.

Proof 1: Signed Euclidean remainders — outline

Use the parametrization of all solutions: from one solution (x_0,y_0), every solution is (x_0-kb/d,y_0+ka/d) for an integer k.

This relies on a property of Euclidean division: given two non-zero integers c and d, if d does not divide c, there is exactly one pair (q, r) such that c = dq + r and 0 < r < |d|, and another one such that c = dq + r and −|d| < r < 0.

The two pairs of minimal Bézout coefficients are obtained from the given one (x_0,y_0) by choosing for k in the displayed parametrization either of the two integers bracketing x_0/(b/d).

Level justification

The supplied solution parametrization and the two signed remainders give exactly two possible x coordinates in the required interval. To check the corresponding y bound, first adjust signs so the reduced positive inputs A=a/d and B=b/d exceed one. For 0<x<B, y=(1-Ax)/B lies strictly between -A and zero; for -B<x<0 it lies between zero and A. The nondivisibility assumptions rule out a zero remainder. These are elementary integer inequalities and divisibility.
