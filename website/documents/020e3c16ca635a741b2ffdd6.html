Statement

For a field k and d\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}, the Krull dimension of S=k[y_1,\ldots,y_d] is at least d.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory polynomial and ring theory supplies degrees, divisibility, ideals and finite-field arithmetic. The radical consists of elements with a power in the ideal, and Krull dimension counts lengths of prime-ideal chains. These limited ideal-based definitions suffice without schemes, localization or field-extension arithmetic.

Proof 1: Coordinate prime-ideal chain

The chain of prime ideals 0\subsetneq(y_1)\subsetneq(y_1,y_2)\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq(y_1,\ldots,y_d) has length d. Hence the supremum of lengths of chains of prime ideals is at least d.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Exhibit the coordinate prime-ideal chain and apply the explicitly described chain-length definition. Undergraduate polynomial rings and prime ideals suffice with Krull dimension as one simple numerical definition; no dimension theory is proved.
