Statement

On \mathbb R^d with Lebesgue measure and integer d\ge1, if f\in L^1, g\in L^p and 1\le p\le\infty, then \|f*g\|_p\le\|f\|_1\|g\|_p, where (f*g)(x)=\int f(y)g(x-y)\,dy in its almost-everywhere sense.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The claim is a measure-theoretic convolution norm bound.

Proof 1: Interpolate the integrable-kernel endpoint bounds

Let f be a fixed integrable function and let T be the operator of convolution with f, i.e., for each function g we have Tg=f*g.

It follows from Fubini's theorem that T is bounded from L^1 to L^1 and it is trivial that it is bounded from L^\infty to L^\infty (both bounds are by \|f\|_1). Therefore the Riesz–Thorin theorem gives

\|f*g\|_p\leq\|f\|_1\|g\|_p.

Here we use Riesz–Thorin interpolation: a complex-linear operator with endpoint bounds L^{p_j}\to L^{q_j} of norms M_j has intermediate bound M_0^{1-\theta}M_1^\theta at reciprocal exponents interpolated with weights 1-\theta,\theta.
