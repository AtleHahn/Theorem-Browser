Statement

For every real number \alpha and every real number N\geq1, there exist integers p and q such that

1\leq q\leq N\qquad\text{and}\qquad |q\alpha-p|\leq\frac{1}{\lfloor N\rfloor+1}<\frac1N.

Here \lfloor N\rfloor is the greatest integer not exceeding N.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All quantifiers, the denominator constraint, absolute value and explicitly defined integer rounding are understandable through high-school arithmetic and inequalities. Number-theoretic proof methods are not statement prerequisites.

Review notes

  • Dirichlet’s foundational quantitative approximation theorem guarantees good rational approximations with a controlled denominator.

Proof 1: Small gaps between fractional parts (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (The theorem and its proof in the main entry).

Apply the linked theorem with n=\lfloor N\rfloor, so q\le n\le N and 1/(n+1)<1/N. For n=1, take q=1 and a nearest integer to \alpha. If two fractional parts coincide, their nonzero index difference gives error zero.

The source’s pigeonhole argument proves the weaker bound |qα−p|<1/N for integer N, not the displayed bound 1/(⌊N⌋+1). Its Minkowski argument supplies a nonzero lattice point in a body with error bound 1/N and does not establish the full stated denominator and error conclusion. No proof of the complete formulation above is supplied; the missing argument has not been reconstructed.

Level

Level justification

The argument uses fractional parts, finite ordering and the fact that nonnegative gaps summing to 1 cannot all exceed their average. That fact follows by adding inequalities. Nearest-integer rounding and the boundary cases require only high-school arithmetic.
