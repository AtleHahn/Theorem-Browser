Statement

Let finite project and machine sets be given, with nonnegative revenues r_i and costs c_j, and prescribed machines required by each project. A feasible selection consists of projects J and purchased machines Q containing every machine required by J, with profit \sum_{i\in J}r_i-\sum_{j\in Q}c_j. Form a directed network with arcs s\to p_i of capacity r_i, q_j\to t of capacity c_j, and p_i\to q_j of capacity +\infty for each requirement. A cut has capacity the sum of capacities of arcs leaving its source side. Then the maximum feasible profit is \sum_i r_i minus the minimum cut capacity.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Projects, requirements, cuts and capacities are all explicitly finite sets and sums. Infinite capacity is a rule excluding infeasible choices.

Proof 1: Match finite cuts with feasible selections

To solve the problem, let P be the set of projects not selected and Q be the set of machines purchased. Writing r(p_i)=r_i and c(q_j)=c_j, the profit is

g=\sum_i r(p_i)-\sum_{p_i\in P}r(p_i)-\sum_{q_j\in Q}c(q_j).

Since the first term does not depend on the choice of P and Q, this maximization problem can be formulated as a minimization problem instead, that is, minimize

g'=\sum_{p_i\in P}r(p_i)+\sum_{q_j\in Q}c(q_j).

The above minimization problem can then be formulated as a minimum-cut problem by constructing a network, where the source is connected to the projects with capacity r(p_i), and the machines are connected to the sink with capacity c(q_j). An edge (p_i,q_j) with infinite capacity is added if project p_i requires machine q_j.

In a finite cut, P consists of the projects on the sink side and Q of the machines on the source side. No selected project can have a required machine on the sink side, since that would give an infinite-capacity crossing. Thus the cut represents a feasible selection and its capacity is g'. Conversely a feasible selection gives such a finite cut by placing selected projects and purchased machines on the source side. Hence the maximum profit is \sum_i r_i minus the minimum cut capacity.

Level justification

Match feasible selections with finite cuts and express the lost revenue plus purchased costs as cut capacity. All network terms are explicitly defined, and school finite sums and set membership suffice; no max-flow theorem is required.
