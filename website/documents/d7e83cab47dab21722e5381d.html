Constant factors in computational running time can be changed by enriching a machine's finite alphabet and state set. This helps explain why asymptotic complexity abstracts away from such factors, while also showing that exact computational cost depends on the chosen representation model.

Statement

Let k>1 be an integer and let M be a k-tape Turing machine solving a problem in time at most f(n) on inputs of length n. For every real constant c>0, there is a k-tape Turing machine N solving the same problem in time at most

\frac{f(n)}{c}+2n+3.

The machine N may have a larger finite tape alphabet and a larger finite state set. The assertion holds for deterministic machines and for nondeterministic machines, preserving the corresponding computation model. For a nondeterministic time bound, every computation branch is bounded and acceptance has its usual existential-branch meaning.

Proof 1: Packed tape simulation with exact-time slack (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Montanaro, Computational Complexity, Theorem 4.1, printed p.13 (PDF page index12); with the exact-time adaptation below.).

Here is an exact-time adaptation of the packed simulation. A macroblock of at most m original steps touches only three adjacent packed cells on each tape. Allow 12 transitions to read them, write the updated contents, restore the correct packed-head positions and detect halting, simultaneously on all tapes. Input packing and return cost at most n+2+\lceil n/m\rceil steps. Thus, for original branch time T, the total is at most

n+2+\lceil n/m\rceil+12\lceil T/m\rceil.

Choose an integer m\ge\max(2,24c). This is at most T/(2c)+3n/2+15, which is at most T/c+2n+3 when n\ge24. During the same initial scan, finite control records inputs of length less than 24; their answers can be built into a finite table and returned in at most n+2 steps. No extra transitions are needed on longer inputs.

For nondeterministic decision machines, a macrotransition chooses a valid path segment of length at most m and records any halting outcome. Accepting paths correspond, and the same bound holds on every branch. The table handles the finitely many short inputs with the same accepted language. Finally T\le f(n).
