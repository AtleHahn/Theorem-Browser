Statement

Let p be an odd prime and let \chi:(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^\times\to\mathbb C^\times be a nontrivial group homomorphism, extended periodically to integers with \chi(a)=0 when p\mid a. Let \zeta be any primitive pth root of unity and put G(\chi)=\sum_{a=0}^{p-1}\chi(a)\zeta^a. Then G(\chi)/|G(\chi)| is a root of unity if and only if \chi(a) is 1 on nonzero quadratic residues modulo p and -1 on nonresidues. A root of unity is a complex number whose positive integer power equals 1; primitive pth means its order is exactly p.
