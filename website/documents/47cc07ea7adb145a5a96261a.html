Statement

Let f:X\to Y be a projective morphism of algebraic varieties. Let U\subseteq X be the locus of points isolated in their fibers. Then U\to Y factors as an open immersion U\to Z followed by a finite morphism Z\to Y.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves algebraic varieties and projective morphisms; fibers and isolated points; finite morphisms and open immersions. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Apply quasi-finite factorization — outline

Use the source-stated Grothendieck factorization theorem: if Y is quasi-compact and separated and g:U\to Y is separated, quasi-finite and finitely presented, then g factors as an open immersion followed by a finite morphism.

If f:X→Y is a projective morphism of varieties, then the set of points that are isolated in their fiber is quasifinite over Y. Then structure theorem for quasi-finite morphisms applies and yields the desired result.
