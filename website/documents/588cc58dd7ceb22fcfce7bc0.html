Statement

For each positive integer k, set O_k=(0,1/k). These are nonempty bounded open intervals, strictly decreasing, and \bigcap_{k\ge1}O_k=\varnothing.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement says that no positive real number is less than 1/n for every positive integer n. The explicit intervals and their common-membership condition are understandable through elementary inequalities.

Proof 1: Exclude positive candidates by an Archimedean integer

Any point in the intersection must be positive. For any number x>0 there exists some positive integer n (namely any n>1/x), such that 1/n<x. This is given by the Archimedean property of the real numbers. Therefore, no matter how small x>0, one can always find intervals O_n in the sequence such that x\notin O_n, implying that the intersection has to be empty.
