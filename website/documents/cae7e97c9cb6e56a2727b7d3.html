Statement

Let n,k\geq1 be integers, let v\in\mathbb R^n have Euclidean norm 1, and let R be a k\times n matrix with independent entries, each either symmetric \pm1, or taking \sqrt3,0,-\sqrt3 with probabilities 1/6,2/3,1/6. All entries use the same distribution. Put f(v)=Rv/\sqrt{k}. For every 0<\varepsilon<1, \Pr(\|f(v)\|^2\leq1-\varepsilon)\leq\exp[-\frac{k}{2}(\varepsilon^2/2-\varepsilon^3/3)].

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra supplies matrix multiplication and Euclidean norms. The finitely supported independent variables have explicitly given probabilities; expectation and the probability of a norm inequality can be learned from finite weighted sums. For the moment comparison, the standard normal distribution adds a familiar density integral. This is a limited extension using calculus and finite sums.

Proof 1: Negative exponential moments and a third-order estimate (outline)

Fix the unit vector v in the statement. Define Q_i:=\sum_jR_{ij}v_j. We have \|f(v)\|_2^2=\frac1k\sum_iQ_i^2. The Q_i are independent and identically distributed. Use the moment bounds \mathbb E Q_i^2=1 and \mathbb E Q_i^{2m}\leq(2m-1)!! for m\geq1. Write K_Y(t)=\ln\mathbb E[e^{tY}] for the cumulant generating function, and interpret -\ln0=+\infty in the probability bounds.

Begin with some {\textstyle t>0}, and expand the exponential to the second order:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}K_{Q_{i}^{2}}(-t)&=\ln E[e^{-Q_{i}^{2}t}]\\&\leq \ln E[1-Q_{i}^{2}t+Q_{i}^{4}t^{2}/2]\\&\leq \ln(1-t+3t^{2}/2)\\\end{aligned}}}{\displaystyle K_{{\frac {1}{k}}\sum _{i}Q_{i}^{2}}(-t)\leq k\ln(1-t/k+3t^{2}/(2k^{2}))}

So by the standard Chernoff bound method, for any {\textstyle t>0} and any {\textstyle \epsilon \in (0,1)},

{\displaystyle -\ln Pr\left({\frac {1}{k}}\sum _{i}Q_{i}^{2}\leq 1-\epsilon \right)\geq -k[(1-\epsilon )(t/k)+\ln(1-t/k+3t^{2}/(2k^{2}))]}

Plug in {\textstyle t={\frac {k\epsilon }{2(1+\epsilon )}}}, and simplify, we find the right side is

{\displaystyle \geq k\left({\frac {(\epsilon -1)\epsilon }{2(\epsilon +1)}}-\ln \left({\frac {7\epsilon ^{2}+12\epsilon +8}{8(\epsilon +1)^{2}}}\right)\right)}

and expand to third Taylor power,

{\displaystyle \geq k(\epsilon ^{2}/4-7\epsilon ^{3}/48)>{\frac {k}{2}}(\epsilon ^{2}/2-\epsilon ^{3}/3)}

Level justification

All random variables here have finite support. Independence factors their exponential moments, the stated second/fourth moments give the quadratic upper estimate, and the lower-tail Chernoff bound is an application of Markov to an exponential. Cumulant generating function means only the logarithm of that finite expectation. The final scalar Taylor bound can be checked by differentiation with a nonnegative rational derivative. Calculus, algebra and elementary finite probability suffice; no advanced concentration theory is required.
