Statement

Let H be a real or complex Hilbert space with inner product linear in its first variable, and (\varphi_n)_{n\ge0} an orthonormal sequence. A scalar sequence (c_n) has the form c_n=\langle f,\varphi_n\rangle for some f\in H if and only if \sum_{n\ge0}|c_n|^2<\infty.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Basic topology and real analysis supply compactness, completeness, continuity and sequence convergence; linear algebra supplies inner products and orthogonality. Hilbert completeness and the explicitly specified bounds or maps are direct extensions, with no spectral or weak-topology theory needed.

Proof 1: Construct the partial-sum limit and apply Bessel — outline

For square-summable coefficients, construct f by the norm limit f=\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{k=0}^N c_k\varphi_k. Conversely, Bessel’s inequality shows that the orthonormal coefficients of every f\in H are square summable.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the orthogonal-sum norm identity to make the partial sums Cauchy, take their limit and recover coefficients by continuity; Bessel proves necessity. Undergraduate series, complete metric spaces and inner products suffice, with no duality or measure theory.
