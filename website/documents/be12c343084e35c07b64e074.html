Statement

For a state (a positive linear functional of norm one) \Phi on a unital C-star algebra A, there exist a Hilbert space K, a unital star representation \pi:A\to B(K) and a cyclic unit vector \xi with \Phi(a)=\langle\pi(a)\xi,\xi\rangle. Cyclic means K=\overline{\operatorname{span}\pi(A)\xi}.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves States of C-star algebras; Hilbert-space representations; Cyclic vectors. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Specialize the dilation to a one-dimensional input

Let H in Stinespring's theorem be one-dimensional, i.e. the complex numbers. So \Phi now is a positive linear functional on A. Positive scalar functionals are completely positive, so Stinespring's theorem applies; choose a minimal Stinespring representation. Since \Phi is a state, that is, \Phi has norm 1, the isometry V:H\to K is determined by

V1=\xi

for some \xi\in K of unit norm. So

\begin{aligned} \Phi(a)&=\langle V^*\pi(a)V1,1\rangle_H\\ &=\langle\pi(a)V1,V1\rangle_K\\ &=\langle\pi(a)\xi,\xi\rangle_K, \end{aligned}

and we have recovered the GNS representation of states. Minimality means that K=\overline{\operatorname{span}\pi(A)VH}; since H=\mathbb C, this says that \xi is cyclic.
