Statement

Let a<b, ν≥0 integer, f\in C^{\nu+1}[a,b] and a bounded integrable kernel k satisfy Lf=\frac1{\nu!}\int_a^b k(\theta)f^{(\nu+1)}(\theta)\,d\theta. Then |Lf|\leq\|k\|_1\|f^{(\nu+1)}\|_\infty/\nu!, |Lf|\leq\|k\|_\infty\|f^{(\nu+1)}\|_1/\nu!, and |Lf|\leq\|k\|_2\|f^{(\nu+1)}\|_2/\nu!. Here \|g\|_1=\int_a^b|g|, \|g\|_2=(\int_a^b|g|^2)^{1/2} and \|g\|_\infty=\sup_{[a,b]}|g|.

Proof 1: Supremum bounds and integral Cauchy–Schwarz — outline

Take the absolute value of the supplied kernel representation. Bounding the second factor by its supremum gives the first inequality, bounding the kernel by its supremum gives the second, and the integral Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives the third. These are the three direct bounds the source draws from the representation.
