Statement

Let V be a linear isometry of a complex Hilbert space H, and put M_i=V^i(H)\ominus V^{i+1}(H) for i\ge0. Each M_i is wandering: V^n(M_i)\perp V^m(M_i) whenever distinct n,m\ge0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A Hilbert/Banach setting adds completeness to normed linear algebra. Operator powers, orthogonal projection, continuous scalar functionals and unit-ball norm bounds are direct extensions. The statements use only these explicit identities/convergence conditions, without weak topology or spectrum.

Proof 1: Orthogonal successive range differences — outline

Successive range differences

Successive applications of V give the descending closed ranges H_i=V^i(H), for i\ge0.

{\displaystyle M_{i}=H_{i}\ominus H_{i+1}=V^{i}(H\ominus V(H))\quad {\text{for}}\quad i\geq 0\;,}

then

{\displaystyle H=\left(\bigoplus _{i\geq 0}M_{i}\right)\oplus \left(\bigcap _{i\geq 0}H_{i}\right)=K_{1}\oplus K_{2}.}

The operator V maps each M_i isomorphically onto M_{i+1}.

Wandering property

A subspace M is called a wandering subspace of V if Vn(M) ⊥ Vm(M) for all n ≠ m. In particular, each Mi defined as M_i=V^i(H)\ominus V^{i+1}(H) is a wandering subspace of V.

Level

Level justification

Use nested ranges, preserved inner products and V^n M_i=M_(i+n) to show orthogonality. The needed part is elementary closed-subspace inner-product algebra; the displayed full Wold decomposition is not needed for the wandering conclusion.
