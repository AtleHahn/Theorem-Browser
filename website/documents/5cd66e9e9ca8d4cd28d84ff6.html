Statement

Let N\ge1 be an integer. Partition \{1,\ldots,2N\} into A_1<\cdots<A_N and B_1>\cdots>B_N. Then \sum_{i=1}^N|A_i-B_i|=N^2.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement uses ordered partitions of a finite set of integers, absolute values and finite sums.

Proof 1: Count the pairwise maxima

For any a,b, we have |a-b|=\max\{a,b\}-\min\{a,b\}. For this reason, it suffices to establish that the sets \{\max\{A_i,B_i\}:1\leq i\leq N\} and \{N+1,N+2,\ldots,2N\} coincide. Since the numbers A_i,B_i are all distinct, it suffices to show that for any 1\leq k\leq N, \max\{A_k,B_k\}>N. Assume the contrary that this is false for some k, and consider the N+1 positive integers

A_1,A_2,\ldots,A_k,B_k,B_{k+1},\ldots,B_N.

Clearly, these numbers are all distinct (due to the construction), but they are all at most N, which is a contradiction.

The remaining minima are therefore 1,\ldots,N, and the sum of absolute differences is

\sum_{j=N+1}^{2N}j-\sum_{j=1}^{N}j =\sum_{j=1}^{N}((N+j)-j)=N^2.

Level justification

If a paired maximum were at most N, the sorted lists would give N+1 distinct integers at most N. Thus the maxima and minima are the two halves, whose sum difference is N squared. School counting and finite sums suffice.
