Statement

Suppose N>1 and real bilinear functions z_i(x,y) on \mathbb R^N\times\mathbb R^N satisfy

(x_1^2+\cdots+x_N^2)(y_1^2+\cdots+y_N^2)=\sum_{i=1}^N z_i(x,y)^2.

Then there exist real orthogonal skew-adjoint N\times N matrices V_1,\ldots,V_{N-1} with V_i^2=-I and V_iV_j=-V_jV_i for i\ne j. In particular N is even.

Proof 1: Normalize the coefficient matrices

Assume that there is a composition law for two forms

(x_1^2+\cdots+x_N^2)(y_1^2+\cdots+y_N^2)=z_1^2+\cdots+z_N^2,

where z_i is bilinear in x and y. Thus

z_i=\sum_{j=1}^N a_{ij}(x)y_j,

where the matrix T(x)=(a_{ij}(x)) is linear in x. The relations above are equivalent to

T(x)T(x)^t=(x_1^2+\cdots+x_N^2)I.

Writing

T(x)=T_1x_1+\cdots+T_Nx_N,

the relations become

T_iT_j^t+T_jT_i^t=2\delta_{ij}I.

Now set V_i=(T_N)^tT_i. Thus V_N=I and V_1,\ldots,V_{N-1} are skew-adjoint, orthogonal, satisfying

V_i^2=-I,\qquad V_iV_j=-V_jV_i\quad(i\ne j,\ i,j<N).

Since V_i is an orthogonal matrix with square -I on a real vector space, N is even.

Level justification

Express the bilinear forms as a linear matrix pencil, compare quadratic coefficients and normalize by one orthogonal coefficient matrix. The parity conclusion follows from a real matrix squaring to -I. Undergraduate matrix and determinant algebra suffice.
