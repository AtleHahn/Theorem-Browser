Statement

Let c_{00} be the real vector space of sequences with finitely many nonzero terms, equipped with \|x\|_\infty=\sup_j|x_j|. This normed space is not complete.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra and real analysis supply kernels, quotients, projections, sup norms and completeness. The index or operator bound is explicitly defined, and sequence-space examples use specified coordinate formulas. No dual or weak-topology theory is required.

Proof 1: Truncated harmonic sequence — outline

To see that c_{00} is not complete, consider the sequence of sequences x^{(n)}\in c_{00} given by

x^{(n)}=\left(1,\frac12,\dots,\frac1n,0,0,\dots\right).

It converges in the supremum norm, as n\to\infty, to the sequence x^{(\infty)} given by

x^{(\infty)}=\left(1,\frac12,\dots,\frac1n,\dots\right),

which has all its terms nonzero, and so does not lie in c_{00}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The truncations have supremum tail error tending to zero, and their coordinate limit has infinite support. Norm-induced Cauchy convergence and completeness fit undergraduate metric-space analysis.
