Statement

Assume the Boolean prime ideal theorem: every nontrivial Boolean algebra has a prime ideal. The category \mathrm{Bool} of Boolean algebras and Boolean homomorphisms is dual to \mathrm{Stone}, the category of Stone spaces and continuous maps. Stone spaces are compact totally disconnected Hausdorff spaces.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Boolean algebras; Stone spaces; categorical restriction and duality; Boolean prime ideal theorem. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Restrict the coherent-space duality

Use the duality between coherent spaces and bounded distributive lattices under the Boolean prime ideal theorem. When restricting further to coherent spaces that are Hausdorff, one obtains the category Stone of so-called Stone spaces. On the side of \mathrm{DLat}_{01}, the restriction yields the subcategory Bool of Boolean algebras. Thus one obtains Stone's representation theorem for Boolean algebras.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Restrict coherent-space/distributive-lattice duality to the Hausdorff and Boolean subcategories. Categorical equivalence, coherent spaces and prime-ideal representation require advanced topology and category theory.
