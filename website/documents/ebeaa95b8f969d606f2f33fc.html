Statement

For any positive integers a,b, an (a+b)\times ab rectangle can be tiled by a\times b rectangles. It is a multiple of the brick dimensions in some orientation if and only if a divides b or b divides a; here a multiple means that the two target side lengths are integer multiples of the corresponding brick sides.

High school only
Level justification

The statement combines planar rectangle tilings with elementary positive-integer divisibility.

Proof 1: Join two differently oriented strips — outline

A rectangle of dimensions a and ab is packed with a copies of an a\times b brick placed side by side. A rectangle of dimensions ab and b is packed with copies of the same brick in the other orientation. Rotate one strip so that their length-ab sides are parallel, then place the strips side by side. The result is an (a+b)\times ab rectangle tiled by the original bricks.

The larger rectangle is a multiple of the brick if and only if the brick is harmonic, meaning that its shorter side divides its longer side.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Assemble two strips of widths a and b with common length ab, then check divisibility of a+b in either orientation. School rectangular geometry and integer divisibility suffice.
