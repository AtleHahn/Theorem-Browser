Statement

Let H be any infinite-dimensional complex Hilbert space, with no separability assumption. Let GL(H) be the set of invertible bounded complex-linear operators on H, equipped with the operator-norm topology. For every finite CW complex Y and every continuous map f:Y\to GL(H), there exist A_0\in GL(H) and a continuous map

F:Y\times[0,1]\to GL(H),\qquad F(y,0)=f(y),\quad F(y,1)=A_0.

Thus every such f is homotopic to a constant map.

A Hilbert space is an inner-product space complete in its induced norm. The operator norm is \|A\|=\sup_{\|x\|\le1}\|Ax\|, and invertibility here requires a bounded two-sided inverse. A finite CW complex is a space built by attaching finitely many closed balls as cells, identifying each boundary sphere by a continuous attaching map to the cells already attached.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Although the norm and homotopy formula are explicit, finite CW attachment, infinite-dimensional operator topology and the full mapping assertion require several additional structures beyond basic point-set topology. Conservative beyond-undergraduate classification.

Proof 1: Orthogonal blocks and infinite cancellation (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (§3.5, Theorem 3.22, Remark 3.23, proof pp.77–81; block cancellation in Theorem 3.21.).

Use Bleecker–Booss-Bavnbek Theorem 3.22, Remark 3.23 and its proof. Compactness of the finite CW complex allows approximation inside GL(H) by a map whose operators span a finite-dimensional space V. For the nonseparable case, the following cardinality adjustment is supplied here. For Hilbert dimension κ, replace the countable block selection by κ stages: before each stage fewer than κ finite-dimensional constraints have been imposed, so choose a unit vector orthogonal to all previous blocks and their inverse images under a basis of V, together with a reserved orthogonal vector. This gives κ mutually orthogonal finite-dimensional blocks and a fixed identity subspace of dimension κ. Use the same finite-dimensional rotation formula on every block; compactness and a uniform inverse bound ensure operator-norm continuity. The final infinite block cancellation fits the complementary space into countably many copies inside that κ-dimensional identity subspace. These are the source’s operations with the cardinality adjustment stated explicitly.
