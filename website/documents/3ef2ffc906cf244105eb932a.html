Statement

Let U\subset\mathbb R^3 be a bounded domain with piecewise smooth boundary and outward unit normal n. Functions are defined on a neighborhood of \overline U. Write \Delta=\nabla\cdot\nabla and \partial_n f=\nabla f\cdot n. If \psi\in C^2 is harmonic on U, then \int_{\partial U}\partial_n\psi\,dS=0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Multivariable derivatives; Divergence and gradient; Euclidean volume and surface integrals. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Integrate the zero Laplacian

Apply the divergence theorem to \nabla\psi: \int_{\partial U}\partial_n\psi\,dS=\int_U\Delta\psi\,dV=0.
