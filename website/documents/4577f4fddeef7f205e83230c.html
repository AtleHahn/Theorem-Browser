Statement

Let \mathbf a_1,\mathbf a_2,\mathbf a_3 be a basis of \mathbb R^3, put a_j=|\mathbf a_j|, and suppose f:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb C satisfies f(\mathbf r+\sum_j n_j\mathbf a_j)=f(\mathbf r) for integers n_j. Then g(x_1,x_2,x_3)=f(\sum_j x_j\mathbf a_j/a_j) has period a_j in coordinate j. The invertible coordinate transformation lets f be recovered from g.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Real three-dimensional bases; Complex-valued functions; Coordinate periods. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Use normalized lattice vectors as coordinates — outline

In order to develop {\displaystyle f(\mathbf {r} )} in a Fourier series, it is convenient to introduce an auxiliary function

{\displaystyle g(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\triangleq f(\mathbf {r} )=f{\left(x_{1}{\frac {\mathbf {a} _{1}}{a_{1}}}+x_{2}{\frac {\mathbf {a} _{2}}{a_{2}}}+x_{3}{\frac {\mathbf {a} _{3}}{a_{3}}}\right)}.}

Both {\displaystyle f(\mathbf {r} )} and {\displaystyle g(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})} contain essentially the same information. However, instead of the position vector {\displaystyle \mathbf {r} }, the arguments of {\displaystyle g} are coordinates {\displaystyle x_{1,2,3}} along the unit vectors {\displaystyle \mathbf {a} _{i}/{a_{i}}} of the Bravais lattice, such that {\displaystyle g} is an ordinary periodic function in these variables,

{\displaystyle g(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=g(x_{1}+a_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=g(x_{1},x_{2}+a_{2},x_{3})=g(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}+a_{3})\quad \forall \;x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}.}
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Increasing x_j by a_j adds the basis vector a_j to the physical argument, so periodicity follows from the hypothesis. The normalized basis is still a basis and hence the coordinate map is invertible. This is ordinary undergraduate linear algebra.
