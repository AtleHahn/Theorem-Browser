Statement

Let (S,\Sigma,\mu) have finite measure. Suppose measurable complex functions f_n converge almost everywhere to a measurable f and satisfy |f_n|\le M almost everywhere for a finite constant M\ge0. Then f is integrable and \int_S f_n\,d\mu\to\int_S f\,d\mu.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The finite-measure convergence statement uses measure-theoretic integration.

Mathematical Field
Measure theory

Review notes

Proof 1: Use the constant integrable dominator

First suppose the assumptions hold everywhere. Since the sequence is uniformly bounded, there is a real number M such that |f_n(x)|\leq M for all x\in S and for all n. Define g(x)=M for all x\in S. Then the sequence is dominated by g. Furthermore, g is integrable since it is a constant function on a set of finite measure. Therefore, the result follows from the dominated convergence theorem.

If the assumptions hold only \mu-almost everywhere, then there exists a \mu-null set N\in\Sigma such that the functions f_n\mathbf1_{S\setminus N} satisfy the assumptions everywhere on S, with limit f\mathbf1_{S\setminus N}. These replacements do not change the integrals.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof applies dominated convergence on a general finite measure space and handles almost-everywhere exceptions through measurable null sets. Understanding measurable complex functions and Lebesgue integration is substantial measure theory beyond the ordinary undergraduate baseline.
