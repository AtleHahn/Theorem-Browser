Statement

A monotone real sequence (a_n) has a finite real limit if and only if it is bounded. An increasing sequence then converges to sup_n a_n, and a decreasing one to inf_n a_n.

Level

Level justification

A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Use completeness for the monotone direction and bound a convergent tail

Convergence implies boundedness. Suppose {\displaystyle (a_{n})\to L\in \mathbb {R} }. By the {\displaystyle \varepsilon }-definition of limit, there exists {\displaystyle N} such that {\displaystyle |a_{n}-L|<1} for all {\displaystyle n\geq N}, hence {\displaystyle |a_{n}|\leq |L|+1} for {\displaystyle n\geq N}. Let {\displaystyle M=\max\{\,|a_{1}|,\dots ,|a_{N-1}|,\,|L|+1\,\}}. Then {\displaystyle |a_{n}|\leq M} for all {\displaystyle n}, so {\displaystyle (a_{n})} is bounded.

Boundedness implies convergence for a monotone sequence. Suppose {\displaystyle (a_{n})} is bounded and monotone.

  • If {\displaystyle (a_{n})} is nondecreasing and bounded above, set {\displaystyle c=\sup _{n}a_{n}}. For any {\displaystyle \varepsilon >0}, there exists {\displaystyle N} with {\displaystyle c-\varepsilon <a_{N}\leq c}; otherwise {\displaystyle c-\varepsilon } would be a smaller upper bound than {\displaystyle c}. For {\displaystyle n\geq N}, monotonicity gives {\displaystyle a_{N}\leq a_{n}\leq c}, hence {\displaystyle 0\leq c-a_{n}\leq c-a_{N}<\varepsilon }. Thus {\displaystyle a_{n}\to c=\sup _{n}a_{n}}.
  • If {\displaystyle (a_{n})} is nonincreasing and bounded below, either repeat the argument with {\displaystyle c=\inf _{n}a_{n}}, or apply the previous case to {\displaystyle (-a_{n})} to obtain {\displaystyle a_{n}\to \inf _{n}a_{n}}.

This proves the equivalence.
