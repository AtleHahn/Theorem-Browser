Statement

There exist positive integers n,m, a nonempty subset U\subseteq\mathbb R^n, and a map f:U\to\mathbb R^m such that

\|f(u)-f(v)\|_2\leq\|u-v\|_\infty\qquad(u,v\in U),

but there is no map F:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^m satisfying both F|_U=f and

\|F(x)-F(y)\|_2\leq\|x-y\|_\infty\qquad(x,y\in\mathbb R^n).

Here \|x\|_\infty=\max_j|x_j| is the maximum norm and \|z\|_2=(\sum_jz_j^2)^{1/2} is the Euclidean norm.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Finite-dimensional norms and Lipschitz continuity are directly accessible from linear algebra and introductory analysis; no Banach-space machinery is needed to understand this existential assertion.
