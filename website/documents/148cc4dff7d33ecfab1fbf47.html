Statement

Let \alpha,\beta,\mu\in\mathbb C with \mu\notin\mathbb Z and \operatorname{Re}(\alpha+\beta)>-1, and let 0<\gamma\leq1 be real. Let z\in\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}, and choose a logarithm \ell of z, so e^\ell=z. Use that same logarithm for all the Bessel functions below, defining the first-kind Bessel function of order \nu by

J_\nu(z)=\sum_{r=0}^\infty\frac{(-1)^r e^{(2r+\nu)(\ell-\log2)}}{r!\,\Gamma(r+\nu+1)}.

Here \Gamma(w) is the analytic continuation of \int_0^\infty t^{w-1}e^{-t}\,dt from \operatorname{Re}w>0; reciprocal gamma factors are interpreted by the entire function 1/\Gamma, including its zeros at nonpositive integers.

Then

\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^nJ_{\alpha-\gamma n}(z)J_{\beta+\gamma n}(z)}{n+\mu}=\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi\mu)}J_{\alpha+\gamma\mu}(z)J_{\beta-\gamma\mu}(z).

The bilateral sum is interpreted as \lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{n=-N}^N of the displayed summand. The restriction z\ne0 is the common domain for the noninteger-order powers; no value at the branch point is prescribed.
