Statement

Let X be a real random variable with mean \mu and finite variance \sigma^2>0. Then \Pr(\mu-\sqrt2\sigma<X<\mu+\sqrt2\sigma)\ge1/2.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The mean and variance of one real random quantity can be understood as its first and second centered probability-weighted integrals. These closely related definitions add a small preparation to undergraduate integration within about 30 minutes. The interval and probability-one-half conclusion then require only real inequalities, without probability convergence or independence.

Proof 1: Substitute square root of two and take the complement

Chebyshev's variance inequality gives \Pr(|X-\mu|\geq k\sigma)\leq1/k^2 for k>0.

As an example, using {\displaystyle k={\sqrt {2}}} shows that the probability values lie outside the interval {\displaystyle (\mu -{\sqrt {2}}\sigma ,\mu +{\sqrt {2}}\sigma )} does not exceed {\displaystyle 1/2}. Equivalently, it implies that the probability of values lying within the interval (i.e. its "coverage") is at least {\displaystyle 1/2}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the supplied variance inequality at k=sqrt(2), then take the complementary event. The calculation is elementary, but arbitrary random-variable means and variances require ordinary undergraduate probability.
