Statement

Let G be a nontrivial group satisfying the descending chain condition on normal subgroups: every descending sequence of normal subgroups is eventually constant. Then G is an internal direct product of finitely many nontrivial indecomposable subgroups, where indecomposable means not a direct product of two nontrivial subgroups.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory group theory supplies subgroups, quotients, actions, products and homomorphisms. Perfect means generated by commutators, torsion means finite element orders, and the chain conditions are explicit sequences of subgroups. The relevant one of these closely related definitions is a small extension, without representation theory.

Proof 1: Descending sequence of undecomposed direct factors — outline

The proof of existence is relatively straightforward: let S be the set of all normal subgroups that cannot be written as a finite direct product of indecomposable subgroups. Moreover, any indecomposable subgroup is (trivially) the one-term direct product of itself, hence is not in S. If existence fails, then S contains G; so we may iteratively construct a strictly descending series of direct factors in S. (A direct factor without such a decomposition splits into two nontrivial direct factors, at least one of which is still in S.) These are direct factors, and hence normal subgroups, of G; this contradicts the DCC.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

If finite decomposition fails, repeatedly choose a failing direct factor and contradict the explicitly defined descending-chain condition. Undergraduate normal subgroups and direct products suffice; the only added concepts are the stated chain condition and indecomposability.
