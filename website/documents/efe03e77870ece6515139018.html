Statement

Let f:X\to S and g:S^{\prime}\to S be continuous maps, X^{\prime}=X\times_S S^{\prime}, and let g^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to X and f^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to S^{\prime} be the projections. Work with sheaves of abelian groups; h^* means inverse image and R^r h_* is the rth right derived direct image. Assume X is Hausdorff, S is locally compact Hausdorff, and f is universally closed: for every continuous T\to S, the projection X\times_S T\to T is closed. Then for every sheaf \mathcal F of abelian groups on X, g^*R^rf_*\mathcal F\to R^rf^{\prime}_*g^{\prime*}\mathcal F is an isomorphism for each r\ge0. Equivalently, the total derived base-change map is a quasi-isomorphism.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Proof 1: Comparison of fiber-cohomology stalks

For s\in S, write X_s=f^{-1}(s). The proper-fiber stalk formula gives

(R^rf_*\mathcal F)_s =\varinjlim_{U\supseteq X_s}H^r(U,\mathcal F|_U) =H^r(X_s,\mathcal F|_{X_s}),

where U ranges over open neighborhoods of the fiber. The cohomological continuity assertion in the last equality is invoked without proof.

Thus, for s=g(t), writing X'_t=f'^{-1}(t), we have

(g^*R^rf_*\mathcal F)_t =H^r(X_s,\mathcal F|_{X_s}) =H^r(X'_t,(g'^*\mathcal F)|_{X'_t}) =(R^rf'_*(g'^*\mathcal F))_t.

Thus the base-change morphism is an isomorphism on stalks, and hence an isomorphism.

To encode all individual higher derived functors of f_* into one entity, this may equivalently be rephrased by saying that the base-change map

g^*Rf_*\mathcal F\longrightarrow Rf'_*g'^*\mathcal F

is a quasi-isomorphism.
