Statement

Let G be a compact Hausdorff group and \pi:G\to GL(V) a continuous representation on a finite-dimensional complex vector space. Then \pi(G) is a closed compact Lie subgroup of GL(V).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The compact topological groups, finite-dimensional representations, lie groups in this result go beyond finite-dimensional calculus and linear algebra.

Proof 1: A finite-dimensional image of a compact group is a Lie group — source argument

We consider the image of G in GL(V). It is compact and closed, since G is compact, and a subgroup of the Lie group GL(V). It follows by the closed subgroup theorem of Élie Cartan that the image of G is a Lie group also.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Compactness makes the matrix image closed, then Cartan supplies its Lie-group structure. The first step is basic topology, but the actual conclusion requires embedded Lie subgroups and manifold structure.
