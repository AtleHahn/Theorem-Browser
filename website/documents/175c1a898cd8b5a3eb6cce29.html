Statement

A 6\times6\times6 cube has no tiling by axis-aligned 1\times2\times4 bricks placed on the integer lattice; permutations of their three side directions are allowed. A tiling covers the cube by such bricks with disjoint interiors.

Level
High school only

Proof 1: Two-color cell imbalance — outline

Partition the cube into 27 smaller cubes of size 2\cdot2\cdot2 colored alternately black and white. This coloring has more unit cells of one color than of the other, but with this coloring any placement of the 1\cdot2\cdot4 grid brick must have equal numbers of cells of each color. Therefore, any tiling by bricks would also have equal numbers of cells of each color, an impossibility.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Compare the checkerboard cell imbalance with the equal color counts in each grid brick. School finite counting and rectangular geometry suffice.
