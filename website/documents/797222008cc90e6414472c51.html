Statement

The function f:\mathbb Q\to\mathbb Q defined by f(x)=x^2 has f(0)=0 and f(2)=4, but no rational x satisfies f(x)=2. Thus even this polynomial lacks the intermediate value property on a rational interval.

Level
High school only
Level justification

This concrete counterexample is an arithmetic assertion about rational squares.

Proof 1: The missing rational square root of two

The endpoint values are 0^2=0 and 2^2=4. A rational solution of x^2=2 would make \sqrt2 rational (its absolute value), contradicting the irrationality of \sqrt2. Hence the intermediate value 2 is not attained over \mathbb Q.
