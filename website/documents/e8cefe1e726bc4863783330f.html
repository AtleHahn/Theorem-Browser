Statement

Let G be a connected real Lie group with semisimple Lie algebra \mathfrak g (no nonzero solvable ideals), and let B(X,Y)=\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{ad}X\operatorname{ad}Y) be its Killing form, where \operatorname{ad}X(Y)=[X,Y]. A Lie algebra is solvable if its iterated commutator algebras eventually vanish. Choose a Cartan involution \theta, an involutive Lie algebra automorphism for which -B(X,\theta Y) is positive definite. Let \mathfrak k and \mathfrak p be its +1 and -1 eigenspaces, and let \mathfrak a\subseteq\mathfrak p be a maximal abelian subspace. For nonzero real linear forms \lambda on \mathfrak a, put \mathfrak g_\lambda=\{X:[H,X]=\lambda(H)X\text{ for all }H\in\mathfrak a\} and let \Sigma consist of those with nonzero root space. Choose positive roots \Sigma^+ by a vector H_0\in\mathfrak a with all \lambda(H_0)\ne0, taking \lambda(H_0)>0. Set \mathfrak n=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Sigma^+}\mathfrak g_\lambda, and let K,A,N be the connected Lie subgroups generated by \mathfrak k,\mathfrak a,\mathfrak n, respectively. Then \mathfrak g=\mathfrak k\oplus\mathfrak a\oplus\mathfrak n, and multiplication K\times A\times N\to G, (k,a,n)\mapsto kan, is a real analytic diffeomorphism. In particular the three factors of each element are unique.
