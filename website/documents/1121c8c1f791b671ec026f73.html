Statement

Let E=\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]2)\subset\mathbb R. Then [E:\mathbb Q]=3, \operatorname{Aut}(E/\mathbb Q)=\{1\}, and E/\mathbb Q is not Galois.

Proof 1: Use the unique real root to fix the generator

Let {\displaystyle E=\mathbb {Q} ({\sqrt[{3}]{2}})} and {\displaystyle F=\mathbb {Q} }. Then {\displaystyle E/F} is a finite extension, but not a splitting field over {\displaystyle F} (since the minimal polynomial of {\displaystyle {\sqrt[{3}]{2}}} has two nonreal complex roots that do not lie in {\displaystyle E}). The polynomial X^3-2 is irreducible by Eisenstein’s criterion at 2, so [E:F]=3. Since E\subset\mathbb R, the image of its generator must be its unique real cube root. Any {\displaystyle f\in G=\mathrm {Aut} (E/F)} is completely determined by {\displaystyle f({\sqrt[{3}]{2}})} and that

{\displaystyle 2=f({\sqrt[{3}]{2}})^{3}\implies f=1}

Thus, {\displaystyle G=\{1\}}, is the trivial group. In particular, {\displaystyle |G|=1<3=[E:F]}. This shows that {\displaystyle E/F} is not Galois.
