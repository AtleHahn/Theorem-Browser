Statement

For three mutually tangent spheres consisting of an outer circumscribing sphere and two inner nucleus spheres, with distinct pairwise tangency points, let six cyclic chain balls each touch those three spheres and its two neighbors. If their diameters are r_1,\ldots,r_6, the opposite sums satisfy 1/r_1+1/r_4=1/r_2+1/r_5=1/r_3+1/r_6.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The assertion compares reciprocal lengths of diameters in an explicitly defined finite sphere-tangency configuration.

Proof 1: Cancellation in the diameter formulas

Let D be the outer sphere diameter, d_1,d_2 the two nucleus diameters, and r_1,\ldots,r_6 the cyclic chain diameters in the enclosing-sphere configuration. Set a_j=D/d_j and c_j=D/r_j. Use the following externally supplied Irisawa diameter formulas:

K=\sqrt{3\left[a_1a_2+a_2c_1+c_1a_1-\left(\frac{a_1+a_2+c_1+1}{2}\right)^2\right]},\begin{aligned}c_2&=(a_1+a_2+c_1-1)/2-K,\\c_3&=(3a_1+3a_2-c_1-3)/2-K,\\c_4&=2a_1+2a_2-c_1-2,\\c_5&=(3a_1+3a_2-c_1-3)/2+K,\\c_6&=(a_1+a_2+c_1-1)/2+K.\end{aligned}

Adding opposite formulas gives c_1+c_4=c_2+c_5=c_3+c_6=2a_1+2a_2-2. Divide by D to obtain the claimed reciprocal-diameter sums.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Add the explicitly supplied opposite diameter formulas and divide by D. Only elementary algebra and the stated sphere configuration are needed.
