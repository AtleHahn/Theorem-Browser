Statement

Let Y_1,Y_2 be jointly Gaussian real random variables, each of mean zero and variance one, with correlation \rho=\mathbb E[Y_1Y_2]\in[-1,1]. Let Q:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R be measurable and suppose \mathbb E|Y_1Q(Y_2)| and \mathbb E|Y_2Q(Y_2)| are finite. Then

\mathbb E[Y_1Q(Y_2)]=\rho K_Q,\qquad K_Q=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb R}vQ(v)e^{-v^2/2}\,dv.

The coefficient is independent of \rho.

Proof 1: Integrate the Gaussian density in the undistorted variable — outline

First suppose |\rho|<1. Write y_1,y_2 for the coordinates in the density of (Y_1,Y_2), and put r_2=Q(Y_2). The notation \phi_{y_1r_2} below means the cross moment \mathbb E[Y_1Q(Y_2)].

It is a property of the two-dimensional normal distribution that the joint density of {\displaystyle y_{1}} and {\displaystyle y_{2}} depends only on their covariance and is given explicitly by the expression

{\displaystyle p(y_{1},y_{2})={\frac {1}{2\pi {\sqrt {1-\rho ^{2}}}}}e^{-{\frac {y_{1}^{2}+y_{2}^{2}-2\rho y_{1}y_{2}}{2(1-\rho ^{2})}}}}

where the standard Gaussian variables have correlation {\displaystyle \phi _{y_{1}y_{2}}=\rho }.

By absolute integrability, their cross moment is

{\displaystyle \phi _{y_{1}r_{2}}={\frac {1}{2\pi {\sqrt {1-\rho ^{2}}}}}\int _{-\infty }^{\infty }\int _{-\infty }^{\infty }y_{1}Q(y_{2})e^{-{\frac {y_{1}^{2}+y_{2}^{2}-2\rho y_{1}y_{2}}{2(1-\rho ^{2})}}}\,dy_{1}dy_{2}}.

Since

{\displaystyle \int _{-\infty }^{\infty }y_{1}e^{-{\frac {1}{2(1-\rho ^{2})}}y_{1}^{2}+{\frac {\rho y_{2}}{1-\rho ^{2}}}y_{1}}\,dy_{1}=\rho {\sqrt {2\pi (1-\rho ^{2})}}y_{2}e^{\frac {\rho ^{2}y_{2}^{2}}{2(1-\rho ^{2})}}},

the cross moment {\displaystyle \phi _{y_{1}r_{2}}} may be simplified as

{\displaystyle \phi _{y_{1}r_{2}}={\frac {\rho }{\sqrt {2\pi }}}\int _{-\infty }^{\infty }y_{2}Q(y_{2})e^{-{\frac {y_{2}^{2}}{2}}}\,dy_{2}}.

The integral above is seen to depend only on the distortion characteristic {\displaystyle Q()} and is independent of {\displaystyle \rho }.

Therefore, with

K_Q=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}y_2Q(y_2)e^{-y_2^2/2}\,dy_2,

the correlation can be rewritten in the form

\phi_{y_1r_2}=K_Q\rho.

This product identity also covers \rho=0. At \rho=\pm1, Y_1=\rho Y_2 almost surely, so the same equality follows directly from the one-dimensional Gaussian density.
