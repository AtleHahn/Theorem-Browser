Statement

Let d\geq1 and let X=(X^1,\ldots,X^d) be an \mathbb R^d-valued càdlàg semimartingale on a filtered probability space, indexed by t\geq0. Let f:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R be twice continuously differentiable, and write f_i=\partial f/\partial x_i and f_{ij}=\partial^2f/(\partial x_i\partial x_j). Then f(X) is a semimartingale, and almost surely, for all t\geq0,

\begin{aligned} f(X_t)=f(X_0) &+\sum_{i=1}^d\int_0^t f_i(X_{s-})\,dX_s^i +\frac12\sum_{i,j=1}^d\int_0^t f_{ij}(X_{s-})\,d[X^i,X^j]_s\\ &+\sum_{0<s\leq t}\left( f(X_s)-f(X_{s-}) -\sum_{i=1}^d f_i(X_{s-})\Delta X_s^i -\frac12\sum_{i,j=1}^d f_{ij}(X_{s-})\Delta X_s^i\Delta X_s^j \right). \end{aligned}

Here càdlàg means right-continuous with left limits, X_{s-} is the left limit, and \Delta X_s=X_s-X_{s-}. Each component of a semimartingale is the sum of a càdlàg local martingale and an adapted process of locally bounded variation. The integrals against X^i are semimartingale stochastic integrals. The bracket [X^i,X^j], normalized to be zero at time zero, is quadratic covariation: the limit in probability of sums of products of corresponding increments along partitions whose mesh tends to zero. Integrals against these brackets are Stieltjes integrals, and the last sum runs over jump times in (0,t].

Level justification

Understanding the full identity requires filtered probability, local martingales, semimartingale integration and quadratic covariation together with jump corrections. These are several interdependent concepts beyond the standard undergraduate background; the cumulative preparation cannot reasonably fit the 30-minute allowance. Local notation definitions clarify the formula but do not remove that burden.

Proof 1: Stochastic Taylor sums with a summable jump remainder (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorem1, equation6 and Lemma4).

Take U=\mathbb R^d and expand the repeated-index summation in equation(6). The stochastic sum limits are proved in Lemmas2–3 of the preceding note, and squared-jump summability controls the jump correction. In Lemma4 use the supremum of |R_{s,t}|/\|X_t-X_s\|^2 for the remainder bound. For its cutoff argument, isolate the finitely many jumps of size at least \varepsilon/4 before applying a continuous cutoff that vanishes below \varepsilon/2; the remaining partition increments are eventually smaller than \varepsilon/2. This avoids an equality-at-the-threshold issue. Equality for each fixed rational time, followed by right continuity of both sides, gives one probability-one event on which the formula holds for every t\ge0.
