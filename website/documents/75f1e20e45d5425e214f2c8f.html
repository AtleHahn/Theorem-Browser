Statement

Let d\geq1 and let X=(X^1,\ldots,X^d) be an \mathbb R^d-valued càdlàg semimartingale on a filtered probability space, indexed by t\geq0. Let f:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R be twice continuously differentiable, and write f_i=\partial f/\partial x_i and f_{ij}=\partial^2f/(\partial x_i\partial x_j). Then f(X) is a semimartingale, and almost surely, for all t\geq0,

\begin{aligned} f(X_t)=f(X_0) &+\sum_{i=1}^d\int_0^t f_i(X_{s-})\,dX_s^i +\frac12\sum_{i,j=1}^d\int_0^t f_{ij}(X_{s-})\,d[X^i,X^j]_s\\ &+\sum_{0<s\leq t}\left( f(X_s)-f(X_{s-}) -\sum_{i=1}^d f_i(X_{s-})\Delta X_s^i -\frac12\sum_{i,j=1}^d f_{ij}(X_{s-})\Delta X_s^i\Delta X_s^j \right). \end{aligned}

Here càdlàg means right-continuous with left limits, X_{s-} is the left limit, and \Delta X_s=X_s-X_{s-}. Each component of a semimartingale is the sum of a càdlàg local martingale and an adapted process of locally bounded variation. The integrals against X^i are semimartingale stochastic integrals. The bracket [X^i,X^j], normalized to be zero at time zero, is quadratic covariation: the limit in probability of sums of products of corresponding increments along partitions whose mesh tends to zero. Integrals against these brackets are Stieltjes integrals, and the last sum runs over jump times in (0,t].
