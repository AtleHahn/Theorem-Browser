Statement

Let d\ge1 and let \Lambda=\{\sum_{i=1}^d z_i v_i:z_i\in\mathbb Z\} for a basis v_1,\ldots,v_d of \mathbb R^d. Let L>0 be the volume of a fundamental parallelotope. If S\subset\mathbb R^d is bounded and Lebesgue measurable of volume A, some translate of S contains at least \lceil A/L\rceil distinct points of \Lambda. Equivalently, S contains that many distinct points all of whose pairwise differences lie in \Lambda.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement compares the Lebesgue volume of an arbitrary bounded measurable set with a lattice fundamental volume.

Proof 1: Cut into fundamental parallelotopes and count overlaps

Just as the integer lattice divides the plane into squares, an arbitrary lattice divides its space into fundamental regions (called parallelotopes) with the property that any one of these regions can be translated onto any other of them by adding the coordinates of a unique lattice point.

Choose the fundamental parallelotopes half-open so that they partition space, and put n=\lceil A/L\rceil. If A=0, the assertion is immediate.

Cut up {\displaystyle S} by parallelotopes, translate the pieces by translation vectors in {\displaystyle \Lambda } onto a single parallelotope without changing the total volume (counted with multiplicity). If every point had multiplicity at most n-1, the total volume would be at most (n-1)L<A. Therefore there must be a point {\displaystyle p} of multiplicity at least {\displaystyle \lceil A/L\rceil }. Use a translation that takes {\displaystyle p} to the origin.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The finite cut-and-translate argument is intuitive, but it is stated for arbitrary bounded Lebesgue-measurable sets and uses volume counted with multiplicity on lattice parallelotopes. A rigorous interpretation requires measurable-set volume beyond ordinary Riemann integration, together with the lattice geometry. The cumulative extension is borderline, so the conservative beyond-undergraduate classification is retained.

Statement used:

Application in proof:

Move the finitely many pieces into one cell and obtain a contradiction if multiplicity is everywhere below the ceiling.
