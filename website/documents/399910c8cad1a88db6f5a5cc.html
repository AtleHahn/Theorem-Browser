Statement

Let B>0, T=1/(2B) and -\pi/2<\theta<\pi/2. The functions x_\theta(t)=\cos(2\pi Bt+\theta)/\cos\theta all have samples x_\theta(nT)=(-1)^n for integers n, although distinct values of \theta give distinct functions.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The explicit example uses only trigonometric identities and substitutions.

Proof 1: A sine component vanishes at every sample

Consider the family of sinusoids generated by different values of \theta in this continuous-time signal:

\begin{aligned} x_\theta(t)&=\frac{\cos(2\pi Bt+\theta)}{\cos\theta}, \qquad-\pi/2<\theta<\pi/2\\ &=\cos(2\pi Bt)-\sin(2\pi Bt)\tan\theta. \end{aligned}

With T=1/(2B), the samples are given by:

x_\theta(nT)=\cos(\pi n)-\underbrace{\sin(\pi n)}_0\tan\theta=(-1)^n

regardless of the value of \theta. The coefficient \tan\theta is strictly increasing on the stated interval, so distinct values of \theta give distinct functions.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Expand the angle sum; the sine term vanishes at every integer sample. Tangent is injective on the stated interval, so the unsampled functions differ.
