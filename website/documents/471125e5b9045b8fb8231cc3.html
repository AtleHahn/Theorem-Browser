Statement

Let f:\{1,2,\ldots\}\to[0,\infty) be non-increasing. Then, with sums allowed to equal +\infty,

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f(n)\le\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^k f(2^k)\le2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f(n).

In particular, the original series converges if and only if its condensed series converges.

Proof 1: Dyadic runs and the offset comparison

To see the first inequality, the terms of the original series are rebracketed into runs whose lengths are powers of two, and then each run is bounded above by replacing each term by the largest term in that run. That term is always the first one, since by assumption the terms are non-increasing.

{\displaystyle {\begin{array}{rcccccccl}\displaystyle \sum \limits _{n=1}^{\infty }f(n)&=&f(1)&+&f(2)+f(3)&+&f(4)+f(5)+f(6)+f(7)&+&\cdots \\&=&f(1)&+&{\Big (}f(2)+f(3){\Big )}&+&{\Big (}f(4)+f(5)+f(6)+f(7){\Big )}&+&\cdots \\&\leq &f(1)&+&{\Big (}f(2)+f(2){\Big )}&+&{\Big (}f(4)+f(4)+f(4)+f(4){\Big )}&+&\cdots \\&=&f(1)&+&2f(2)&+&4f(4)&+&\cdots =\sum \limits _{n=0}^{\infty }2^{n}f(2^{n})\end{array}}}

To see the second inequality, these two series are again rebracketed into runs of power of two length, but "offset" as shown below, so that the run of {\textstyle 2\sum _{n=1}^{\infty }f(n)} which begins with {\textstyle f(2^{n})} lines up with the end of the run of {\textstyle \sum _{n=0}^{\infty }2^{n}f(2^{n})} which ends with {\textstyle f(2^{n})}, so that the former stays always "ahead" of the latter.

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\sum _{n=0}^{\infty }2^{n}f(2^{n})&=f(1)+{\Big (}f(2)+f(2){\Big )}+{\Big (}f(4)+f(4)+f(4)+f(4){\Big )}+\cdots \\&={\Big (}f(1)+f(2){\Big )}+{\Big (}f(2)+f(4)+f(4)+f(4){\Big )}+\cdots \\&\leq {\Big (}f(1)+f(1){\Big )}+{\Big (}f(2)+f(2){\Big )}+{\Big (}f(3)+f(3){\Big )}+\cdots =2\sum _{n=1}^{\infty }f(n)\end{aligned}}}

Each comparison is valid for the grouped nonnegative partial sums and hence for their limits, allowing +\infty. The two-sided bound shows that one series is finite if and only if the other is finite.

Level justification

The proof groups a nonnegative series in dyadic blocks, bounds each block using monotonicity, and passes to increasing partial-sum limits. This is standard undergraduate series analysis, including the infinite-sum case.
