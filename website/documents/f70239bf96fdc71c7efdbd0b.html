Statement

An equitable k-coloring of a finite simple undirected graph assigns one of k colors to every vertex, gives adjacent vertices different colors, and has any two color-class sizes differing by at most one; empty classes are allowed. For n\geq1, let K_{2n+1,2n+1} consist of two disjoint sets of 2n+1 vertices, with every cross-set pair adjacent and no within-set edges. It has an equitable two-coloring but has no equitable (2n+1)-coloring.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Only finite pair counting and the explicit adjacency rule are required.

Proof 1: Rule out pairing an odd bipartition side

This graph has an equitable 2-coloring, given by its bipartition. However, it does not have an equitable (2n + 1)-coloring: any equitable partition of the vertices into that many color classes would have to have exactly two vertices per class, but the two sides of the bipartition cannot each be partitioned into pairs because they have an odd number of vertices.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The two bipartition classes give an equitable two-coloring. With 2n+1 colors all classes would be pairs contained in an odd-sized side, which is impossible.
