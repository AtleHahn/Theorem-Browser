Statement

Let A,B,C\in\mathbb R^2 be noncollinear. Put B^{\prime}=B-A, C^{\prime}=C-A. Define D^{\prime} and U^{\prime} by

{{\begin{aligned}U'_{x}&={\frac {1}{D'}}\left[C'_{y}({B'_{x}}^{2}+{B'_{y}}^{2})-B'_{y}({C'_{x}}^{2}+{C'_{y}}^{2})\right],\\[5pt]U'_{y}&={\frac {1}{D'}}\left[B'_{x}({C'_{x}}^{2}+{C'_{y}}^{2})-C'_{x}({B'_{x}}^{2}+{B'_{y}}^{2})\right]\end{aligned}}}{D'=2(B'_{x}C'_{y}-B'_{y}C'_{x}).\,}

Then the circumcenter is U=A+U^{\prime} and the circumradius is |U^{\prime}|. Planar vector sums and scalar multiples mean the corresponding operations on each of the two Cartesian coordinates.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All planar vectors are explicitly pairs of real coordinates; the displayed formula uses elementary arithmetic, squares, lengths and coordinate translation or weighted averages.

Proof 1: Translate a vertex to the origin

The Cartesian coordinates of the circumcenter {\displaystyle U=\left(U_{x},U_{y}\right)} are

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}U_{x}&={\frac {1}{D}}\left[\left(A_{x}^{2}+A_{y}^{2}\right)(B_{y}-C_{y})+\left(B_{x}^{2}+B_{y}^{2}\right)(C_{y}-A_{y})+\left(C_{x}^{2}+C_{y}^{2}\right)(A_{y}-B_{y})\right]\\[5pt]U_{y}&={\frac {1}{D}}\left[\left(A_{x}^{2}+A_{y}^{2}\right)(C_{x}-B_{x})+\left(B_{x}^{2}+B_{y}^{2}\right)(A_{x}-C_{x})+\left(C_{x}^{2}+C_{y}^{2}\right)(B_{x}-A_{x})\right]\end{aligned}}}

with

{\displaystyle D=2\left[A_{x}(B_{y}-C_{y})+B_{x}(C_{y}-A_{y})+C_{x}(A_{y}-B_{y})\right].\,}

Without loss of generality this can be expressed in a simplified form after translation of the vertex A to the origin of the Cartesian coordinate systems, i.e., when {\displaystyle A'=A-A=(A'_{x},A'_{y})=(0,0).} In this case, the coordinates of the vertices {\displaystyle B'=B-A} and {\displaystyle C'=C-A} represent the vectors from vertex A' to these vertices. Observe that this trivial translation is possible for all triangles, and the coordinates of the circumcenter {\displaystyle U'=(U'_{x},U'_{y})} of the triangle △A'B'C' follow as

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}U'_{x}&={\frac {1}{D'}}\left[C'_{y}({B'_{x}}^{2}+{B'_{y}}^{2})-B'_{y}({C'_{x}}^{2}+{C'_{y}}^{2})\right],\\[5pt]U'_{y}&={\frac {1}{D'}}\left[B'_{x}({C'_{x}}^{2}+{C'_{y}}^{2})-C'_{x}({B'_{x}}^{2}+{B'_{y}}^{2})\right]\end{aligned}}}

with

{\displaystyle D'=2(B'_{x}C'_{y}-B'_{y}C'_{x}).\,}

Due to the translation of vertex A to the origin, the circumradius r can be computed as

{\displaystyle r=\|U'\|={\sqrt {{U'_{x}}^{2}+{U'_{y}}^{2}}}}

and the actual circumcenter of △ABC follows as

{\displaystyle U=U'+A}

Level justification

Translate A to zero in the supplied coordinate formulas, cancel zero terms, and translate the result back. The radius is the ordinary distance to the origin. These are elementary Cartesian calculations.
