Statement

Let n\ge1, 1\le p\le\infty, and let A\in\mathbb C^{n\times n} be diagonalizable, A=V\Lambda V^{-1}, with V invertible and \Lambda diagonal. Write \Lambda(A) for the eigenvalue set of A, \|\cdot\|_p for the vector p-norm and its induced matrix norm, and \kappa_p(V)=\|V\|_p\|V^{-1}\|_p. For any \lambda^a\in\mathbb C and nonzero \boldsymbol v^a\in\mathbb C^n, put \boldsymbol r=A\boldsymbol v^a-\lambda^a\boldsymbol v^a. There exists \lambda\in\Lambda(A) with |\lambda-\lambda^a|\le\kappa_p(V)\|\boldsymbol r\|_p/\|\boldsymbol v^a\|_p.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves complex matrices; eigenvalues; diagonalization; induced matrix norms; eigenpair residual vectors. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Bauer–Fike residual bound proof

We can suppose λa ∉ Λ(A), otherwise take λ = λa and the result is trivially true since κp(V) ≥ 1. So (A − λaI)−1 exists, so we can write:

{\displaystyle {\boldsymbol {v}}^{a}=\left(A-\lambda ^{a}I\right)^{-1}{\boldsymbol {r}}=V\left(\Lambda-\lambda ^{a}I\right)^{-1}V^{-1}{\boldsymbol {r}}}

since A is diagonalizable; taking the p-norm of both sides, we obtain:

{\displaystyle \left\|{\boldsymbol {v}}^{a}\right\|_{p}=\left\|V\left(\Lambda-\lambda ^{a}I\right)^{-1}V^{-1}{\boldsymbol {r}}\right\|_{p}\leq \|V\|_{p}\left\|\left(\Lambda-\lambda ^{a}I\right)^{-1}\right\|_{p}\left\|V^{-1}\right\|_{p}\|{\boldsymbol {r}}\|_{p}=\kappa _{p}(V)\left\|\left(\Lambda-\lambda ^{a}I\right)^{-1}\right\|_{p}\|{\boldsymbol {r}}\|_{p}.}

However

{\displaystyle \left(\Lambda-\lambda ^{a}I\right)^{-1}}

is a diagonal matrix and its p-norm is easily computed:

{\displaystyle \left\|\left(\Lambda-\lambda ^{a}I\right)^{-1}\right\|_{p}=\max _{\|{\boldsymbol {x}}\|_{p}\neq 0}{\frac {\left\|\left(\Lambda-\lambda ^{a}I\right)^{-1}{\boldsymbol {x}}\right\|_{p}}{\|{\boldsymbol {x}}\|_{p}}}=\max _{\lambda \in \Lambda (A)}{\frac {1}{\left|\lambda -\lambda ^{a}\right|}}={\frac {1}{\min _{\lambda \in \Lambda (A)}\left|\lambda -\lambda ^{a}\right|}}}

whence:

{\displaystyle \min _{\lambda \in \Lambda (A)}\left|\lambda -\lambda ^{a}\right|\leq \kappa _{p}(V){\frac {\|{\boldsymbol {r}}\|_{p}}{\left\|{\boldsymbol {v}}^{a}\right\|_{p}}}.}
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The argument uses a diagonalized resolvent, the defining induced-norm inequality and the fact that a diagonal matrix has induced p-norm equal to its largest diagonal modulus. These are finite-dimensional undergraduate linear algebra facts. The spectral case is handled separately before inversion, and the final division is valid because the approximate eigenvector is nonzero.
