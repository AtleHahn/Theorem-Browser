Statement

Let (L,\mathcal N) be a classically interpreted formal language with negation and Gödel numbering g. Suppose it has the semantic diagonal property: for each one-variable formula B(v) there is a sentence D with \mathcal N\models D\leftrightarrow B(g(D)). Call the language strongly semantically self-representational if predicates and function symbols in L define all semantic concepts of L, including truth of its sentences. Then it is not strongly semantically self-representational.

Proof 1: Apply undefinability to the truth component — outline

Strong semantic self-representation would in particular provide an internal predicate defining truth of every sentence. Tarski’s general undefinability theorem excludes such a predicate under the stated diagonal property, so this stronger self-representation is impossible.
