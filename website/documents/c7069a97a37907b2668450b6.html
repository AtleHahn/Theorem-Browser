Statement

For a Euclidean p-simplex \sigma, p\ge1, every simplex of its barycentric subdivision has diameter at most p/(p+1)\operatorname{diam}\sigma. A simplex is the convex hull of affinely independent points; subdivision vertices are the averages of nonempty nested sets of original vertices. The constant is optimal over all p-simplices.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Euclidean simplices; convex combinations; barycenters; diameters; limits. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Nested-face averages and a degenerating simplex — outline

By construction, each simplex in the barycentric subdivision has the vertices of the form {\displaystyle b(F_{0}),\cdots } for a chain of faces

{\displaystyle \sigma =F_{p}\supset \cdots \supset F_{0}}

where {\displaystyle F<G} means {\displaystyle F} is a proper face of {\displaystyle G}, and, thinking {\displaystyle F} consisting of vertices, the barycenter {\displaystyle b(F)} of {\displaystyle F} is

{\displaystyle b(F)={\frac {1}{\#F}}\sum _{v\in F}v.}

Now, given faces {\displaystyle F<G}, with {\displaystyle g:=\#G,f:=\#F}, we have:

{\displaystyle b(F)-b(G)={\frac {g-f}{g}}\left({\frac {1}{f}}\sum _{v\in F}v-{\frac {1}{g-f}}\sum _{v\in G-F}v\right).}

Then the expression after {\displaystyle {\frac {g-f}{g}}} is in

{\displaystyle \langle v\mid v\in G\rangle +\langle -v\mid v\in G\rangle \subset \langle v-w\mid v,w\in G\rangle }

where the bracket means convex hull. Thus, since {\displaystyle (g-f)/g\leq (g-1)/g\leq p/(p+1)},

{\displaystyle |b(F)-b(G)|\leq {\frac {p}{p+1}}\max _{v,w\in G}|v-w|.}

This implies the diameter of {\displaystyle \langle b(F_{i})\mid i\rangle } is bounded by the claimed bound.

Finally, to see the estimate cannot be improved, first note: for the vertices {\displaystyle v_{i}} of {\displaystyle \sigma },

{\displaystyle b(\sigma )-v_{0}={\frac {1}{p+1}}\sum _{i\geq 1}(v_{i}-v_{0}).}

Thus, for example, if {\displaystyle \sigma } is the 2-simplex with vertices {\displaystyle v_{0}=(0,0),v_{1}=(1,0),v_{2}=(1,\epsilon )}, then the ratio {\displaystyle |b(\sigma )-v_{0}|/\operatorname {diam} (\sigma )} approaches {\displaystyle {\frac {2}{3}}} as {\displaystyle \epsilon } goes to zero. A similar argument works in higher dimension. {\displaystyle \square }

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof is vector averaging, convex combinations, the norm triangle inequality and a limiting family of Euclidean simplices. The supplied nested-face description makes barycentric subdivision a concrete finite construction; no simplicial homology or abstract topology is used. Undergraduate linear algebra and analysis suffice. For sharpness in dimension p, take v0=0, v1=e1 and v_i=e1+epsilon e_i for i=2,...,p; these remain affinely independent for epsilon>0, while their diameter tends to 1 and the full barycenter distance tends to p/(p+1).
