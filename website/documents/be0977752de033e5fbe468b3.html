Statement

Let X\in\mathbb R^{n\times K} be deterministic with full column rank, and let y=X\beta+\varepsilon, where \beta\in\mathbb R^K is unknown and deterministic, E\varepsilon=0 and \operatorname{Cov}(\varepsilon)=\sigma^2 I_n with 0\le\sigma^2<\infty. Put B=(X^TX)^{-1}X^T and \widehat\beta=By. Assume \sigma^2>0, C=B+D, DX=0, and \ell\in\mathbb R^K. Then \operatorname{Var}(\ell^TCy)-\operatorname{Var}(\ell^TBy)=\sigma^2\|D^T\ell\|^2. Equality holds exactly when D^T\ell=0, in which case the two contrast estimators are identical functions of y.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Random-vector means and covariance are finite integral moment definitions extending undergraduate matrix algebra. Contrasts are the displayed linear functionals; no general statistical optimality theory is needed.

Proof 1: Evaluate the covariance excess on a contrast

Write \widetilde\beta=Cy. Use the covariance identity

\operatorname{Cov}(\widetilde\beta) =\sigma^2(X^TX)^{-1}+\sigma^2DD^T =\operatorname{Cov}(\widehat\beta)+\sigma^2DD^T.

Then

\begin{aligned} \operatorname{Var}(\ell^T\widetilde\beta) &=\ell^T\operatorname{Cov}(\widetilde\beta)\ell\\ &=\sigma^2\ell^T(X^TX)^{-1}\ell+\sigma^2\ell^TDD^T\ell\\ &=\operatorname{Var}(\ell^T\widehat\beta) +\sigma^2(D^T\ell)^T(D^T\ell)\\ &\ge\operatorname{Var}(\ell^T\widehat\beta). \end{aligned}

Moreover, since \sigma^2>0, equality holds if and only if D^T\ell=0. We calculate

\begin{aligned} \ell^T\widetilde\beta &=\ell^T\left(((X^TX)^{-1}X^T+D)y\right)\\ &=\ell^T(X^TX)^{-1}X^Ty+\ell^TDy\\ &=\ell^T\widehat\beta+(D^T\ell)^Ty\\ &=\ell^T\widehat\beta \qquad(D^T\ell=0). \end{aligned}

Thus equality in the variance bound makes the two contrast estimators identical functions of y.

Level justification

Apply the covariance excess to the contrast vector and recognize the resulting squared Euclidean norm. Undergraduate probability, regression and linear algebra suffice.
