Statement

Let X be a second countable locally compact Hausdorff abelian topological group, written additively, with no nonzero x satisfying 2x=0. Let \xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n, n\ge1, be independent X-valued random variables with Borel probability laws \mu_j. Write \widehat X for the group of continuous homomorphisms \chi:X\to\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|=1\}, with the topology of uniform convergence on compact subsets, and \widehat\mu(\chi)=\int_X\chi(x)\,d\mu(x). Suppose every \widehat\mu_j is nowhere zero. Let \alpha_j,\beta_j be continuous group automorphisms with continuous inverses, and assume both \beta_i\alpha_i^{-1}+\beta_j\alpha_j^{-1} and \beta_i\alpha_i^{-1}-\beta_j\alpha_j^{-1} are such automorphisms for every i\ne j, where addition of maps is pointwise. Put L_1=\sum_j\alpha_j(\xi_j) and L_2=\sum_j\beta_j(\xi_j). If (L_1,L_2) and (L_1,-L_2) have the same distribution, then each \mu_j is Gaussian: there exist x_j\in X and a continuous nonnegative q_j:\widehat X\to\mathbb R such that \widehat\mu_j(\chi)=\chi(x_j)e^{-q_j(\chi)},\qquad q_j(\chi+\psi)+q_j(\chi-\psi)=2q_j(\chi)+2q_j(\psi). Addition in \widehat X means multiplication of characters. Second countable means that the topology has a countable base, and locally compact means every point has a compact neighborhood.
