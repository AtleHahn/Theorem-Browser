Statement

For integers n\ge1 and k\ge0, the coefficient of t_1^0\cdots t_n^0 in the finite Laurent expansion \prod_{1\le i\ne j\le n}(1-t_i/t_j)^k equals (nk)!/(k!)^n.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Multivariate Laurent coefficients or symmetric-polynomial multi-index expansions require undergraduate algebraic notation and several interacting definitions. A bright US college student should be able to understand the statement after about 30 minutes of focused background research; this rating concerns understanding the claim, not proving it.

Proof 1: Use the evaluated Dyson integral at an even exponent — outline

Let a_i denote the exponent attached to the factors (1-t_i/t_j), and set a_i=k and \beta=2k.

When all the values ai are equal to β/2, the constant term in Dyson's conjecture is the value of Dyson's integral

{\displaystyle {\frac {1}{(2\pi )^{n}}}\int _{0}^{2\pi }\cdots \int _{0}^{2\pi }\prod _{1\leq j<k\leq n}|e^{i\theta _{j}}-e^{i\theta _{k}}|^{\beta }\,d\theta _{1}\cdots d\theta _{n}.}

Dyson's integral is a special case of Selberg's integral after a change of variable and has value

{\displaystyle {\frac {\Gamma (1+\beta n/2)}{\Gamma (1+\beta /2)^{n}}}}

which gives another proof of Dyson's conjecture in this special case.

Here a_i=k and \beta=2k, so the value is (nk)!/(k!)^n.
