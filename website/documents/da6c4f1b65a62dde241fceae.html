Statement

Let A\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}, b\in\mathbb R^m and c\in\mathbb R^n, and suppose

P=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:Ax\le b\}

is nonempty and bounded, with the inequalities interpreted coordinatewise. The minimum and maximum of the linear objective c^Tx on P are attained at vertices of P. Every optimal point is a vertex or belongs to a face consisting entirely of optimal points. In particular, if two vertices attain the same minimum (respectively maximum), so does every point on their connecting line segment.

A vertex is a point that is not a nontrivial convex combination of two distinct points of P. A face is the intersection of P with a supporting hyperplane; P itself is also allowed as a face, including when the objective is constant on P.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Finite systems of linear inequalities and linear objectives use standard linear algebra. Vertices and supporting faces are a small, explicitly described extension of undergraduate convex geometry.
