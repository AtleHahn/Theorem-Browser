Statement

Let R be a commutative Noetherian ring and let homogeneous f_1,\ldots,f_k\in R[x_1,\ldots,x_n] have specified degrees. Suppose a fixed degree D\ge0 has the following property for every homomorphism \varphi:R\to K to a field: the specialized polynomials have a common nonzero zero over an algebraic closure of K exactly when their degree-D Macaulay matrix fails to have full row rank. Form the degree-D coefficient matrix M_D over R, with rows the degree-D monomials and columns the monomial multiples of the f_i of degree D. Let \mathfrak r\subseteq R be generated by all row-sized square minors of M_D (the zero ideal if there are fewer columns than rows). Then the specialized polynomials have a common nonzero zero exactly when \varphi(\mathfrak r)=0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Noetherian rings, ideals and algebraic field extensions add related definitions to undergraduate ring/polynomial algebra. The matrix and uniform common-zero test are explicit, so no elimination theory is required.

Proof 1: Specialize maximal minors of a fixed coefficient matrix

Specializing the coefficients of M_D gives the degree-D Macaulay matrix over K. A matrix over a field fails to have full row rank exactly when all its row-sized minors vanish. Determinants commute with coefficient specialization, so this is equivalent to every generator of \mathfrak r mapping to zero. The assumed uniform rank test now gives the desired common-zero criterion.
