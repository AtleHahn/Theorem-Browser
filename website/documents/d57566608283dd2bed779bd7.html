Statement

The snake-lemma exactness conclusion fails for groups when the cokernel of a homomorphism is the quotient by the normal closure of its image. Consider exact rows 1\to C_3\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}}C_3\to1 and 1\to1\to S_3\hookrightarrow A_5, with vertical maps 1\to1, C_3\hookrightarrow S_3 and C_3\hookrightarrow A_5 through an embedding S_3\hookrightarrow A_5. The corresponding proposed kernel-cokernel sequence is not exact.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Finite permutation groups, quotient groups and normal closure are undergraduate algebra. Exactness adds image-equals-kernel and the proposed kernel/cokernel sequence, a limited related definition.

Proof 1: Compare the middle and right categorical cokernels

Consider the alternating group {\displaystyle A_{5}}: this contains a subgroup isomorphic to the symmetric group {\displaystyle S_{3}}, which in turn can be written as a semidirect product of cyclic groups: {\displaystyle S_{3}\simeq C_{3}\rtimes C_{2}}. This gives rise to the following diagram with exact rows:

{\displaystyle {\begin{matrix}&1&\to &C_{3}&\to &C_{3}&\to 1\\&\downarrow &&\downarrow &&\downarrow \\1\to &1&\to &S_{3}&\to &A_{5}\end{matrix}}}

The horizontal maps between copies of C_3 are the identity, and the other nontrivial maps are inclusions. Since {\displaystyle A_{5}} is simple, the right vertical arrow has trivial cokernel. Meanwhile the quotient group {\displaystyle S_{3}/C_{3}} is isomorphic to {\displaystyle C_{2}}. The sequence in the statement of the snake lemma is therefore

{\displaystyle 1\longrightarrow 1\longrightarrow 1\longrightarrow 1\longrightarrow C_{2}\longrightarrow 1,}

which indeed fails to be exact at C_2: the incoming image is trivial, whereas the kernel of the outgoing map is all of C_2.
