Statement

Let S\subset\mathbb R^3 be a nonempty compact connected smooth regular surface without boundary. If its Gaussian curvature is constant on S, then S is a round sphere: there are c\in\mathbb R^3 and r>0 such that

S=\{x\in\mathbb R^3:\|x-c\|=r\}.

A smooth regular surface here is a smooth embedded two-dimensional manifold: near each point it has a smooth parametrization by an open subset of \mathbb R^2 with linearly independent first derivatives, giving the surface its subspace topology. The words without boundary exclude surface patches with edges. Gaussian curvature is K=\det(-dN), where N is either local smooth unit normal and -dN is viewed as an endomorphism of the tangent plane. Equivalently it is the product of the two principal curvatures, the eigenvalues of this map; changing the normal does not change their product. The norm in the conclusion is Euclidean.
