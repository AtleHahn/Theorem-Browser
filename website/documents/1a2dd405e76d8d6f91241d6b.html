Statement

Let G be a finite group of order g, let C be a conjugacy class of G with h elements, and let n\ge1 be an integer. Then \gcd(hn,g)\ \bigm|\ \#\{x\in G:x^n\in C\}. A conjugacy class consists of all y^{-1}cy for a fixed c\in G and y\in G. In particular, if n divides |G|, the number of solutions to x^n=1 in G is divisible by n.

Proof 1: Counting homomorphisms with cyclic indexing (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (PJM 302 (2019), DOI 10.2140/pjm.2019.302.437; Theorem 0 p.438, main theorem and Brauer lemma pp.443–444, Section 2 p.445, Section 5 p.449.).

Fix c\in C and let r count its nth roots. Theorem 0 gives \gcd(n,|C_G(c)|)\mid r. Conjugation gives the same count for every member of C, and |C_G(c)|=g/h. Thus h\gcd(n,g/h)=\gcd(hn,g) divides hr, the required count.

Level justification

The linked cyclic-indexing proof, including its integer-indexed dependency, uses subgroup actions, centralizers, Sylow theory and normal forms for free and semidirect products. The added preparation is the word/product notation and tail/core definitions, which fit the reading allowance on top of undergraduate abstract algebra. The counting and twisting arguments introduce no further specialist prerequisite.
