Statement

For propositional clauses, allow expansion by an input clause but, after the initial expansion, require it to contain the complement of a literal on the branch (weak connectedness) or of the leaf literal (strong connectedness). Neither restriction is proof-confluent: an unsatisfiable clause set can admit an open tableau with no allowed extension although it also has a closed tableau.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement supplies the complementary-literal restrictions and describes failure of proof confluence as a stuck open tree despite an available closed tree. In the companion, the one-branch trees for a, b and not-b make these finite truth-value and extension rules concrete. Understanding this propositional counterexample needs elementary finite reasoning, not a course in formal logic.

Proof 1: Compare a stuck initial choice with a closing choice

The connectedness conditions, when applied to the propositional (clausal) case, make the resulting calculus non-confluent. As an example, \{a,b,\neg b\} is unsatisfiable, but applying clause expansion to a generates the chain \mathrm{true}-a, which is not closed and to which no other expansion rule can be applied without violating either strong or weak connectedness.

Starting instead with b permits expansion by \neg b and closes the tableau.
