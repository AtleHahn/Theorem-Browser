Statement

Let k\ge1 be an integer and \alpha>0. Write \log^{\circ j} and \exp^{\circ j} for j-fold iterates of the natural logarithm and exponential, with zeroth iterate the identity. Put N=\lfloor\exp^{\circ k}(0)\rfloor+1. Then

\sum_{n\ge N}\frac{1}{n\,\log n\,\log\log n\cdots\log^{\circ(k-1)}n\,(\log^{\circ k}n)^\alpha}

converges if \alpha>1 and diverges if 0<\alpha\le1. The product between n and the final powered logarithm is empty when k=1.

Proof 1: Repeated logarithmic shifts under condensation — outline

Cauchy condensation says that for a non-increasing nonnegative sequence a_n, the series \sum_{n\ge1}a_n converges if and only if \sum_{j\ge0}2^j a_{2^j} converges. Apply condensation repeatedly. Each condensation moves the logarithmic factors one place to the left, removing the leading factor of the form 1/n. After k such steps, convergence is governed by the p-series \sum m^{-\alpha}, which converges exactly when \alpha>1.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply condensation repeatedly and compare the rescaled logarithmic tails with their unshifted equivalents until reaching a p-series. Undergraduate real analysis and series comparison suffice.
