Statement

Let f be entire and n\ge0 an integer. If |f(z)|\le M|z|^n for some M>0 and all sufficiently large |z|, then f is a polynomial of degree at most n.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex differentiability extends the calculus derivative. Power series, circle parametrization, bounds and convergence are familiar analysis; the hypotheses and formulas are explicit, with no abstract complex geometry or distribution theory.

Proof 1: Let higher Taylor coefficients vanish by Cauchy estimates

Take the Taylor series representation of f,

f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_kz^k.

Cauchy's coefficient formula on a circle of sufficiently large radius r gives, for all k\ge0,

|a_k|\le\frac1{2\pi}\frac{Mr^n}{r^{k+1}}(2\pi r)=Mr^{n-k}.

So, if k>n, then

|a_k|\le\lim_{r\to\infty}Mr^{n-k}=0.

Therefore, a_k=0, and f is a polynomial of degree at most n.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the Cauchy coefficient estimate on arbitrarily large circles to kill every coefficient above degree n. Undergraduate complex analysis suffices.
