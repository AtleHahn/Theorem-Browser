Statement

Let 1<p<\infty, f\in L^p(\mathbb R), and y>0. Define u(x,y)=\frac1\pi\int f(s)\frac{y}{(x-s)^2+y^2}\,ds,\qquad v(x,y)=\frac1\pi\int f(s)\frac{x-s}{(x-s)^2+y^2}\,ds. Then F(x+iy)=u(x,y)+iv(x,y) is holomorphic on the upper half-plane.

Proof 1: Poisson and conjugate-Poisson kernels give a holomorphic function — outline

The kernels satisfy \frac{i}{\pi z}=\frac{y}{\pi(x^2+y^2)}+i\frac{x}{\pi(x^2+y^2)},\qquad z=x+iy. Hence the combined integral is the Cauchy integral F(z)=\int f(s)i/[\pi(z-s)]\,ds, holomorphic on the upper half-plane by the Cauchy-integral theorem used here.
