Statement

Let g be a smooth nondegenerate symmetric metric on a smooth manifold M, and let \nabla be an affine connection. Assume Xg(Y,Z)=g(\nabla_XY,Z)+g(Y,\nabla_XZ) and \nabla_XY-\nabla_YX=[X,Y] for all smooth vector fields. Then 2g(\nabla_XY,Z)=Xg(Y,Z)+Yg(X,Z)-Zg(X,Y)+g([X,Y],Z)-g([X,Z],Y)-g([Y,Z],X).

In a coordinate chart (x^1,\ldots,x^m) write \partial_i=\partial/\partial x^i and (\nabla_XY)^i=X^j\partial_jY^i+X^jY^k\Gamma^i_{jk}. Repeated upper/lower indices are summed from 1 to m. With g_{ij}=g(\partial_i,\partial_j) and (g^{ij})=(g_{ij})^{-1}, the equivalent coordinate formula is \Gamma^k_{ij}=\tfrac12g^{kl}(\partial_ig_{jl}+\partial_jg_{il}-\partial_lg_{ij}).

Proof 1: Add two compatibility equations, subtract the third, and use torsion-freeness

Torsion-freeness refers to the condition that

{\displaystyle \nabla _{X}Y-\nabla _{Y}X=[X,Y],}

and metric-compatibility refers to the condition that

{\displaystyle X\left(g(Y,Z)\right)=g(\nabla _{X}Y,Z)+g(Y,\nabla _{X}Z),}

where X, Y, and Z are arbitrary vector fields. Metric compatibility and torsion-freeness give the computation

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}X\left(g(Y,Z)\right)&+Y\left(g(X,Z)\right)-Z\left(g(X,Y)\right)\\&={\Big (}g(\nabla _{X}Y,Z)+g(Y,\nabla _{X}Z){\Big )}+{\Big (}g(\nabla _{Y}X,Z)+g(X,\nabla _{Y}Z){\Big )}-{\Big (}g(\nabla _{Z}X,Y)+g(X,\nabla _{Z}Y){\Big )}\\&=g(\nabla _{X}Y+\nabla _{Y}X,Z)+g(\nabla _{X}Z-\nabla _{Z}X,Y)+g(\nabla _{Y}Z-\nabla _{Z}Y,X)\\&=g(2\nabla _{X}Y+[Y,X],Z)+g([X,Z],Y)+g([Y,Z],X).\end{aligned}}}

The equations displayed above can be rearranged to produce the Koszul formula or identity

{\displaystyle 2g(\nabla _{X}Y,Z)=X\left(g(Y,Z)\right)+Y\left(g(X,Z)\right)-Z\left(g(X,Y)\right)+g([X,Y],Z)-g([X,Z],Y)-g([Y,Z],X).}

For X=\partial_i, Y=\partial_j and Z=\partial_l, the Lie brackets vanish. This reduces to the first Christoffel identity

2\Gamma^p_{ij}g_{pl}=\partial_i g_{jl}+\partial_j g_{il}-\partial_l g_{ij}.

It can be contracted with the inverse of the metric, g^{kl}, to find the second Christoffel identity:

\Gamma^k_{ij}=\tfrac12 g^{kl}(\partial_i g_{jl}+\partial_j g_{il}-\partial_l g_{ij}).
