Statement

Let n\ge1 and let f\in\mathbb C[z_1,\ldots,z_n] be symmetric under every permutation of its variables and affine in each variable separately (its degree in any one variable is at most one). Let A be a circular region of the complex plane: an open or closed disk, the exterior of a disk with its boundary either included or excluded, or an open or closed half-plane. Assume that either A is convex or the total degree of f is n. Then for every \zeta_1,\ldots,\zeta_n\in A there is \zeta\in A such that f(\zeta_1,\ldots,\zeta_n)=f(\zeta,\ldots,\zeta).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Undergraduate algebra and plane topology suffice; separate-variable degree and the explicit circular-region definition are small extensions.

Proof 1: Stability-preserving polarization (external)

A link to the proof can be found here. Wagner, §4, Theorem 4.1 and §§4.1–4.3, pp. 11–14

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The argument combines polynomial factorization, fractional linear substitutions and averaging of finitely many coefficients. Stability preservation follows from polynomial identities and nonnegative quadratic expressions; the limiting step uses continuity of roots for polynomials of bounded degree. These ingredients and their proofs fit undergraduate algebra and analysis, with stability and polarization supplied as brief definitions.
