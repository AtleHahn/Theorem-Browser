Statement

Let b\ge2 be an integer and f(x)=\sum_{j=0}^m a_jx^j\in\mathbb Z[x] with 0\le a_j\le b-1. If f(b) is prime, then f is irreducible in \mathbb Z[x]: it is a nonzero nonunit and cannot be written as a product of two nonunits in that ring. The units of \mathbb Z[x] are 1 and -1.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Polynomial multiplication and integer divisibility suffice with the explicit factorization definition, including constant prime polynomials.

Review notes

  • For a nonconstant digit polynomial f(b)>=b, its content is at most b-1 and divides the prime value, so the content is one. In degree one a nontrivial integer constant factor is therefore impossible.
