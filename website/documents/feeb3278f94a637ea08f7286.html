Statement

For a nondegenerate tetrahedron with vertices A,B,C,D, the four segments joining each vertex to the centroid of the opposite triangular face meet at the vertex centroid S=(A+B+C+D)/4. Each segment is divided by S in the ratio 3:1, with the longer part adjacent to the vertex.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Tetrahedra, facet centroids and ratios on medians are elementary spatial geometry.

Proof 1: Separate one tetrahedron vertex from the opposite three — outline

The simplex version of Commandino's theorem is easy to prove via the following, more general result, which is analogous to the way levers in physics work.

Let m,k\geq1 be natural numbers, so that in a real vector space \mathcal V, m+k pairwise different points X_1,\dots,X_m,Y_1,\dots,Y_k are given. Let S_X be the centroid of the points X_i (i=1,\dots,m), let S_Y be the centroid of the points Y_j (j=1,\dots,k), and let S be the centroid of all of these m+k points. Then, one has

S=S_X+\frac{k}{m+k}(S_Y-S_X) =\frac{m}{m+k}S_X+\frac{k}{m+k}S_Y.

In particular, when S_X\ne S_Y, the centroid S lies on the line through S_X,S_Y and divides the segment in a ratio of k:m.

For the tetrahedron, take m=1 and k=3, separating each vertex from the other three vertices.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Apply the explicitly supplied weighted-average identity to one vertex and the other three. Coordinatewise averaging places the centroid three quarters along each median. Only elementary coordinates and segment ratios are needed.
