Statement

Let \theta>0 and let X have density p_\theta(x)=\theta e^{-x}/(1+e^{-x})^{\theta+1} on \mathbb R. Put T(x)=\log(1+e^{-x}). \mathbb ET(X)=1/\theta.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Explicit real density; Expectation of a log statistic. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Differentiate the skew-logistic log-partition function

The density can be rewritten as

{\displaystyle {\frac {e^{-x}}{1+e^{-x}}}\exp[-\theta \log \left(1+e^{-x}\right)+\log(\theta )]}

Notice this is an exponential family with natural parameter

{\displaystyle \eta =-\theta ,}

sufficient statistic

{\displaystyle T=\log \left(1+e^{-x}\right),}

and log-partition function

{\displaystyle A(\eta )=-\log(\theta )=-\log(-\eta )}

So using the exponential-family mean identity \operatorname E(T)=A'(\eta),

{\displaystyle \operatorname {E} \left[\log \left(1+e^{-X}\right)\right]=\operatorname {E} (T)={\frac {\partial A(\eta )}{\partial \eta }}={\frac {\partial }{\partial \eta }}[-\log(-\eta )]={\frac {1}{-\eta }}={\frac {1}{\theta }},}
