Statement

For a\geq0 and b>0, ab\leq e^a-b+b\ln b.

Proof 1: Apply the scalar conjugate inequality

Convex-conjugate inequality

By denoting the convex conjugate of a real function f by g, where

g(b)=\sup_{a\in\mathbb R}(ab-f(a)),

we obtain

ab\leq f(a)+g(b).

This follows immediately from the definition of the convex conjugate. For a convex function f this also follows from the Legendre transformation.

Exponential specialization

The Legendre transform of f(a)=e^a-1 is g(b)=1-b+b\ln b for b>0, hence ab\leq e^a-b+b\ln b for a\geq0 and b>0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The convex conjugate is explicitly a scalar supremum. Apply its defining bound and the supplied exponential conjugate, which is a single-variable maximization formula. This is a small extension of elementary calculus optimization.
