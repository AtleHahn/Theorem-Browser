Statement

Let r:\widehat{\mathbb C}\to\widehat{\mathbb C} be a nonconstant rational map on the Riemann sphere \widehat{\mathbb C}=\mathbb C\cup\{\infty\}. Suppose two disjoint circular regions C_1,C_2 contain, respectively, all zeros and all poles of r, including any zero or pole at infinity. Then every critical point of r belongs to C_1\cup C_2. A circular region is one of the two components of the sphere cut by a circle, with or without its boundary circle. In a plane coordinate these are disks, disk exteriors, or half-planes, with infinity included according to this sphere convention. A critical point is a point where the local degree of r is greater than one: in local complex coordinates at the point and its image the map has expansion c w^m+O(w^{m+1}) with c\ne0 and m\ge2. At ordinary finite points this means r'=0; zeros and poles of order greater than one, including those at infinity, are also critical.
