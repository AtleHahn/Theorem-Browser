Statement

Let s be the 2\pi-periodic continuation of s(x)=x/\pi for -\pi<x<\pi; its endpoint values do not affect the integrals. Define a_0=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}s(x)\,dx, a_n=\pi^{-1}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}s(x)\cos(nx)\,dx and b_n=\pi^{-1}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}s(x)\sin(nx)\,dx for integers n\ge1. Then a_0=a_n=0 and b_n=2(-1)^{n+1}/(\pi n).

Proof 1: Evaluate the sawtooth coefficient integrals

In this case, the Fourier coefficients are given by

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}a_{0}&=0.\\a_{n}&={\frac {1}{\pi }}\int _{-\pi }^{\pi }s(x)\cos(nx)\,dx=0,\quad n\geq 1.\\b_{n}&={\frac {1}{\pi }}\int _{-\pi }^{\pi }s(x)\sin(nx)\,dx\\&=-{\frac {2}{\pi n}}\cos(n\pi )+{\frac {2}{\pi ^{2}n^{2}}}\sin(n\pi )\\&={\frac {2\,(-1)^{n+1}}{\pi n}},\quad n\geq 1.\end{aligned}}}

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Evaluate the defined integrals using oddness and integration by parts. Undergraduate calculus suffices; no Fourier convergence theorem is used.
