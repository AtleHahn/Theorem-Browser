Statement

Let V be a real or complex normed vector space, U a linear subspace, and P:V→U a bounded linear projection, so Pu=u for u∈U. Write \|P\| for its operator norm. Then every v∈V satisfies \|v-Pv\|\le(1+\|P\|)\inf_{u\in U}\|v-u\|.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra supplies projections and norms; the operator norm is the supremum on the unit ball. The displayed approximation bound is a direct norm estimate, without weak topology or spectral theory.

Proof 1: Compare with an arbitrary subspace approximation

The proof is a one-line application of the triangle inequality: for any u in U, by writing v − Pv as (v − u) + (u − Pu) + P(u − v), it follows that

{\displaystyle \|v-Pv\|\leq \|v-u\|+\|u-Pu\|+\|P(u-v)\|\leq (1+\|P\|)\|u-v\|}

where the last inequality uses the fact that u = Pu together with the definition of the operator norm ||P||.

Taking the infimum over u\in U gives the conclusion.

Level justification

Insert any subspace approximant, use that the projection fixes it and bound the remaining operator term before taking an infimum. Undergraduate normed linear algebra and elementary analysis suffice; the operator norm is used only through its defining inequality, without duality or completeness.
