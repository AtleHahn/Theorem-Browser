Statement

Let b\ge2 and n\ge0 be integers satisfying n\equiv1\pmod6,\quad n>1. Then (b-1)b^n+1 is composite.

Proof 1: Use the stated polynomial divisor — outline

The exponent of Williams primes of the second kind cannot be congruent to 1 modulo 6 (except 1 itself), since the corresponding polynomial in b is reducible. If n\equiv 1\pmod6, then (b-1)b^n+1 is divisible by b^2-b+1.
