Statement

Let P>0 and s\in C^2(\mathbb R) be complex-valued and P-periodic. Put S[n]=P^{-1}\int_0^P s(x)e^{-2\pi inx/P}\,dx and s_N=\sum_{|n|\le N}S[n]e^{2\pi inx/P}. Its Fourier coefficients are absolutely summable and s_N\to s uniformly.

Proof 1: Compare decaying coefficients with the reciprocal-square series — outline

This result can be proven easily for s\in C^2, since in that case n^2S[n] tends to zero as |n|\to\infty, by the Fourier coefficient-decay theorem. The Fourier series is therefore absolutely summable. The sum of this series is a continuous function, equal to s, since the Fourier series converges in L^1 to s (by mean-square convergence on the finite interval). In the absolutely summable case, the inequality

\sup_x|s(x)-s_N(x)|\le\sum_{|n|>N}|S[n]|

proves uniform convergence.

Level justification
