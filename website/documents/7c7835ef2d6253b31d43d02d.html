Statement

Let n\geq1 and let \Omega_1,\ldots,\Omega_n be finite probability spaces. Equip \Omega=\prod_{i=1}^n\Omega_i with the product probability measure \mathbb P, so the probability of a configuration x=(x_1,\ldots,x_n) is the product of its coordinate probabilities.

For any two events A,B\subseteq\Omega, define A\square B to be the set of configurations x for which there are disjoint sets I,J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\} such that every configuration agreeing with x on I belongs to A, and every configuration agreeing with x on J belongs to B.

Then

\mathbb P(A\square B)\leq\mathbb P(A)\mathbb P(B).

The two events need not be monotone. The sets I and J are called disjoint certificates for their occurrence.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Only finite sets, products of probabilities and explicit finite quantifiers are needed. Certificates are defined directly through agreement of coordinate lists; no measure theory or percolation background is required.

Proof 1: Reimer’s linear-independence counting argument (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorem2.1; Sections3–5 through Lemma5.2, pp.3–14).

Disjoint certificates I,J may be replaced by S=I and S^c, since fixing more coordinates preserves a certificate. In the dyadic reduction, fixing every bit in a coordinate block fixes its image coordinate. Thus a pair of disjoint certificates lifts to disjoint bit blocks, even when some outcomes have probability zero; only this inclusion is needed. In the subcube reduction, attach each certificate to its original configuration before taking bitwise complements, rather than evaluating the certificate map at a complemented point outside its domain.
