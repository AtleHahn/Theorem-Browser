Statement

Let f:I\to\mathbb R be any function on an interval. At an interior point x, define D^+f(x) and D_+f(x) as the limsup and liminf, respectively, of (f(x+h)-f(x))/h as h\downarrow0, and define D^-f(x) and D_-f(x) as the limsup and liminf of (f(x)-f(x-h))/h. Values may be infinite. Except on a set of Lebesgue measure zero, exactly one of the following holds at each interior point:

  1. f has a finite derivative.
  2. D^+f=D_-f\in\mathbb R, D^-f=+\infty, and D_+f=-\infty.
  3. D^-f=D_+f\in\mathbb R, D^+f=+\infty, and D_-f=-\infty.
  4. D^-f=D^+f=+\infty and D_-f=D_+f=-\infty.

No continuity or measurability of f is assumed. A set has measure zero if, for every \varepsilon>0, it can be covered by countably many intervals with total length less than \varepsilon.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Real-analysis limits and the elementary interval-cover definition of null sets suffice; no general integration theory is needed for the complete four-case statement.

One-sided slope bounds, interval coverings and variation estimates establish the opposite-derivative identities. The supporting density and bounded-variation arguments use outer measure defined by interval covers and variation defined by finite sums. These add a short set of concepts to undergraduate real analysis; the proof does not require integration or measure-decomposition theory.
