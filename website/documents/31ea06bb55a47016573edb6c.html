Statement

Let m\ge5 circles C_{\{1\}},\ldots,C_{\{m\}} in the Euclidean plane pass through a common point P_\varnothing. In general position, the alternating construction below continues through every subset of \{1,\ldots,m\}: even-sized subsets S determine points P_S, and odd-sized subsets determine circles C_S, with the following incidence rule. For every S and every i\in S, the object indexed by S is incident with the object indexed by S\setminus\{i\}. The construction is recursive by subset size. For S=\{i,j\}, take the second intersection P_S of the two original circles, other than P_\varnothing. For any larger odd S, take the circle through the already constructed points P_{S\setminus\{i\}} for the three smallest elements i of S. For any larger even S, take the second intersection of two circles C_{S\setminus\{i\}} and C_{S\setminus\{j\}}, where i,j are the two smallest elements of S, other than their already shared point P_{S\setminus\{i,j\}}. General position means that these determinations are nondegenerate throughout: every chosen triple consists of distinct noncollinear points, and each chosen pair of circles has two distinct transverse intersections (the circles cross rather than touch). The theorem asserts that the other required incidences hold and the objects do not depend on which defining triple or pair is chosen. In particular, for five original circles the five points obtained from their four-circle subsets lie on one circle; for six original circles the six circles obtained from their five-circle subsets pass through one point. The same alternating conclusion holds for every finite number of original circles.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Despite the indexing, every object and hypothesis uses only elementary plane circles, noncollinearity, intersection and finite recursive selection; no university-level geometry is needed to understand the five- and six-circle cases or their stated continuation.
