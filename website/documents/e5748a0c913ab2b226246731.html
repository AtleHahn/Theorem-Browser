Statement

For n≥1 and positive x₁,…,xₙ, (x₁+⋯+xₙ)/n≥(x₁⋯xₙ)^{1/n}.

High school only

Proof 1: Compare unit and uniform exponent vectors

For a real vector a=(a_1,\ldots,a_n), define the a-mean of the positive numbers x_1,\ldots,x_n by

[a]=\frac1{n!}\sum_\sigma x_{\sigma(1)}^{a_1}\cdots x_{\sigma(n)}^{a_n},

where the sum extends over all permutations of \{1,\ldots,n\}. Muirhead's inequality states that [a]\leq[b] if the decreasing exponent vector b majorizes a: their total sums are equal and \sum_{i=1}^j a_i\leq\sum_{i=1}^j b_i for 1\leq j<n.

Let

{\displaystyle a_{G}=\left({\frac {1}{n}},\ldots ,{\frac {1}{n}}\right)}

and

{\displaystyle a_{A}=(1,0,0,\ldots ,0).}

For n=1 the inequality is equality. For n>1 we have

\sum_{i=1}^j a_{Ai}=1>\sum_{i=1}^j a_{Gi}=\frac{j}{n}\quad(1\leq j<n), \qquad \sum_{i=1}^n a_{Ai}=\sum_{i=1}^n a_{Gi}=1.

Then

[aA] ≥ [aG],

which is

{\displaystyle {\frac {1}{n!}}(x_{1}^{1}\cdot x_{2}^{0}\cdots x_{n}^{0}+\cdots +x_{1}^{0}\cdots x_{n}^{1})(n-1)!\geq {\frac {1}{n!}}(x_{1}\cdot \cdots \cdot x_{n})^{1/n}n!}

yielding the inequality.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof needs permutation averages of monomials and majorization, both defined explicitly as finite sums. From undergraduate algebra and finite combinatorics, the additional preparation is just the partial-sum comparison of exponent vectors. The uniform vector has partial sums j/n and the unit vector has partial sums one; counting (n-1)! copies of each variable evaluates the other average. No proof of Muirhead or broader majorization theory is required.
