Statement

Let P be a finite set of primes. A positive integer is P-smooth if all its prime factors belong to P, with 1 allowed. For every set S of P-smooth positive integers, there is a finite subset F\subseteq S such that each s\in S is divisible by some f\in F. The empty set is covered by F=\varnothing.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement defines the allowed integers by their prime factors and asks for a finite list of divisors; it requires only elementary number-theoretic concepts, although its proof imports a deeper ordering theorem.

Proof 1: Transfer the finite-orthant form of Dickson’s lemma

The divisibility relation among the P-smooth numbers gives these numbers the structure of a partially ordered set isomorphic to (\mathbb N_0^{|P|},\leq), where \leq is coordinatewise order.

By Dickson's lemma, every subset of \mathbb N_0^{|P|} may be covered by a finite set of positive orthants, whose apexes all belong to the subset. Thus, for any set S of P-smooth numbers, there is a finite subset of S such that every element of S is divisible by one of the numbers in this subset.
