Statement

Every finitely generated commutative algebra A over a commutative Noetherian ring R is finitely presented: A\simeq R[X_0,\ldots,X_{n-1}]/(p_0,\ldots,p_{N-1}) for finite n,N.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory ring theory supplies polynomial rings and quotient ideals. Noetherian means every ideal is finitely generated; a finitely generated algebra is a polynomial-ring quotient, and the finite presentation is explicitly stated. These closely related definitions are within the calibrated Hilbert-basis allowance.

Commutative algebra

Proof 1: Finite generation of the relation ideal

If A is a finitely generated R-algebra, then we know that

A\simeq R[X_0,\dots,X_{n-1}]/\mathfrak a,

where \mathfrak a is an ideal. Hilbert's basis theorem implies that \mathfrak a must be finitely generated, say \mathfrak a=(p_0,\dots,p_{N-1}), i.e. A is finitely presented.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Write a finitely generated algebra as a polynomial-ring quotient and use the supplied Hilbert basis theorem on its kernel ideal. With undergraduate rings and ideals, Noetherian means every ideal finitely generated and finite presentation is the displayed finite relation quotient; this is a small related algebraic extension.
