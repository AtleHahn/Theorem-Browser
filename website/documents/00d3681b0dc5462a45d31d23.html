Statement

Let G=(V,E) be a finite simple undirected graph. Its incidence partial order has as elements the disjoint union V\sqcup E, with the only strict comparisons

v<e\quad\text{when the vertex }v\text{ is an endpoint of the edge }e.

The order dimension of a finite partial order is the least number of total orders on the same elements whose intersection is that partial order: a comparison holds in the partial order exactly when it holds in every one of the total orders.

Then G is planar if and only if its incidence partial order has order dimension at most three. Planar means that G can be drawn in the Euclidean plane with edges meeting only at shared endpoints.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Finite comparisons, lists and planar drawings suffice after the explicit definitions; no topology or order-theory machinery is needed.
