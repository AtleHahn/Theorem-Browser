Statement

For real- or complex-valued random variables X,Y on a probability space with finite pth moments, 1\le p<\infty, (\mathbb E|X+Y|^p)^{1/p}\le(\mathbb E|X|^p)^{1/p}+(\mathbb E|Y|^p)^{1/p}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The pth moments are scalar nonnegative integral averages. A probability-space interpretation of integration is a limited addition to undergraduate analysis; no abstract function-space theory is needed to read this inequality.

Proof 1: Specialize to the probability measure

Use the L^p Minkowski inequality \|f+g\|_p\leq\|f\|_p+\|g\|_p, where \|f\|_p=(\int|f|^p\,d\mu)^{1/p}.

In probabilistic terms, given the probability space (\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P), and letting \mathbb E denote the expectation operator, for real- or complex-valued random variables X and Y with finite pth moments, Minkowski's inequality reads

\left(\mathbb E[|X+Y|^p]\right)^{1/p} \leq\left(\mathbb E[|X|^p]\right)^{1/p} +\left(\mathbb E[|Y|^p]\right)^{1/p}.
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Read the supplied integral norm inequality as an expectation inequality on a probability space. Undergraduate probability with moments suffices for this application; no construction of general Lp spaces or proof of Minkowski is needed.
