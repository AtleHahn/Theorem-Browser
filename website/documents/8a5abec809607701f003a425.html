Statement

There is an absolute constant C>0 with the following property. Let \mu be a Borel probability measure on the circle \mathbb T=\mathbb R/\mathbb Z, and let m be normalized Lebesgue measure on this circle, so that its total length is 1. Define

\widehat\mu(k)=\int_{\mathbb T} e^{2\pi i k\theta}\,d\mu(\theta)\qquad(k\in\mathbb Z),

where i^2=-1. Then, for every positive integer n,

\sup_{A\text{ an arc of }\mathbb T}|\mu(A)-m(A)| \leq C\left(\frac1n+\sum_{k=1}^n\frac{|\widehat\mu(k)|}{k}\right).

The constant C is independent of \mu and n.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex exponentials and suprema are undergraduate topics, but arbitrary Borel measures and their integrals plus circle measure/Fourier coefficients impose a cumulative extension not confidently within thirty minutes from the stated baseline. Conservatively beyond undergraduate; no Fourier proof machinery is required.
