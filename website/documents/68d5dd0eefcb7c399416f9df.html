Statement

A drawing of a finite graph on a surface places vertices at distinct points and represents edges by arcs whose interiors avoid the vertices; crossings are finitely many transverse intersections of edge interiors, with no triple crossings. A graph embeds in the surface if it has a drawing without crossings. Two edges are independent if they have no common endpoint. For a surface S, a finite graph embeds in S if and only if it has a drawing on S in which every pair of distinct edges crosses an even number of times. Here a surface is a connected two-dimensional topological manifold.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The local-plane definition of a surface and graph arcs are a short extension of undergraduate topology; no classification of surfaces or homology is needed.
