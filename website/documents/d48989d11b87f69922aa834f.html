Statement

For n\ge0, let D_n count words obtained by rearranging n copies each of three symbols in three successive blocks of length n, with no symbol in a position originally occupied by that same symbol. Copies of a symbol are indistinguishable. Then D_n=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk^3=[t_1^nt_2^nt_3^n]\frac1{1-t_1t_2-t_1t_3-t_2t_3-2t_1t_2t_3}, where the last expression is a formal-series coefficient.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Finite word rearrangements, binomial coefficients and ordinary formal-series coefficients are undergraduate combinatorics. The coefficient operator is the usual expansion rule, with every count specified explicitly.

Proof 1: Count the allowed symbols and specialize the determinant — outline

Use MacMahon’s master theorem: for a complex m\times m matrix A=(a_{ij}), define G(k)=[x_1^{k_1}\cdots x_m^{k_m}]\prod_{i=1}^m(\sum_j a_{ij}x_j)^{k_i}. Here brackets mean monomial coefficient. For T=\operatorname{diag}(t_1,\ldots,t_m), the formal identity is \sum_{k\in\mathbb N_0^m}G(k)t_1^{k_1}\cdots t_m^{k_m}=\det(I-TA)^{-1}.

Let {\displaystyle A={\begin{pmatrix}0&1&1\\1&0&1\\1&1&0\end{pmatrix}}}, then {\displaystyle G(n,n,n)=\left[x_{1}^{n}x_{2}^{n}x_{3}^{n}\right]\left(x_{2}+x_{3}\right)^{n}\left(x_{1}+x_{3}\right)^{n}\left(x_{1}+x_{2}\right)^{n}} is the number of derangements of the word {\displaystyle x_{1}^{n}x_{2}^{n}x_{3}^{n}}, i.e. ways to permute the {\displaystyle 3n} symbols of {\displaystyle x_{1}^{n}x_{2}^{n}x_{3}^{n}}, such that each {\displaystyle x_{1}} lands in the location previously occupied by some {\displaystyle x_{2}} or {\displaystyle x_{3}}, etc. By MacMahon's master theorem,

{\displaystyle G(n,n,n)=\sum _{k=0}^{n}{\binom {n}{k}}^{3}=[t_{1}^{n}t_{2}^{n}t_{3}^{n}]{\frac {1}{1-t_{1}t_{2}-t_{1}t_{3}-t_{2}t_{3}-2t_{1}t_{2}t_{3}}}}

Equal symbols are indistinguishable. The binomial coefficient balance and determinant expansion in the displayed formula are implicit in the source.

Interpret coefficients as finite word counts, balance the three exponents and apply the fully stated determinant generating-function identity. Linear algebra and polynomial coefficients suffice with the small formal-series extension; no analytic convergence or proof of MacMahon is needed.
