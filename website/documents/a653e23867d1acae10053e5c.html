Statement

Let X be a Hausdorff space and let Y be a Hausdorff space having a countable pi-base: a countable collection of nonempty open sets such that every nonempty open subset of Y contains a member of the collection. Give X\times Y its product topology. For A\subseteq X\times Y, put A_x=\{y\in Y:(x,y)\in A\}.

If A has the Baire property, then

A\text{ is meager in }X\times Y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\{x\in X:A_x\text{ is meager in }Y\}\text{ is comeager in }X.

Likewise,

A\text{ is comeager in }X\times Y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\{x\in X:A_x\text{ is comeager in }Y\}\text{ is comeager in }X.

In each equivalence the forward implication holds even without the Baire-property assumption on A.

A set is nowhere dense if its closure has empty interior, meager if it is a countable union of nowhere dense sets, and comeager if its complement is meager. It has the Baire property if its symmetric difference with some open set is meager. Hausdorff means that distinct points have disjoint open neighborhoods. No Baire-space assumption on X or Y is imposed.

Level justification

The cumulative interaction of pi-bases, category, Baire measurability and almost-all section quantifiers is not confidently within a short extension of basic point-set topology.
