Statement

Let X be a Hausdorff space and let Y be a Hausdorff space having a countable pi-base: a countable collection of nonempty open sets such that every nonempty open subset of Y contains a member of the collection. Give X\times Y its product topology. For A\subseteq X\times Y, put A_x=\{y\in Y:(x,y)\in A\}.

If A has the Baire property, then

A\text{ is meager in }X\times Y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\{x\in X:A_x\text{ is meager in }Y\}\text{ is comeager in }X.

Likewise,

A\text{ is comeager in }X\times Y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\{x\in X:A_x\text{ is comeager in }Y\}\text{ is comeager in }X.

In each equivalence the forward implication holds even without the Baire-property assumption on A.

A set is nowhere dense if its closure has empty interior, meager if it is a countable union of nowhere dense sets, and comeager if its complement is meager. It has the Baire property if its symmetric difference with some open set is meager. Hausdorff means that distinct points have disjoint open neighborhoods. No Baire-space assumption on X or Y is imposed.

Proof 1: Pi-base sections and category decomposition (external)

The following editorial argument completes the stated scope. A supporting source is Baire Category (Theorem 14 and proof p.4.).

The following argument extends the source to the stated pi-base hypotheses: first prove the usual forward claim for a closed nowhere-dense F. For each member B_i of a countable pi-base of Y, the set E_i={x:B_i subset F_x} is closed and nowhere dense: a nonempty open subset would give an open rectangle in F. Outside their union F_x is nowhere dense. Extend by countable unions to meager sets. For the converse write A symmetric-difference O subset N, with O open and N meager. Outside a meager set E of X, O_x is meager. Let U_i be the union of all open W with W times B_i subset O. If B_i is nonmeager, U_i subset E and thus U_i is meager; if B_i is meager, U_i times B_i is automatically meager. The open union R=union_i U_i times B_i is meager and dense in O, by the pi-base property. Since O is open, O minus R is nowhere dense in X times Y (its closure can add only the boundary of O). Thus O, and hence A, is meager. Apply this equivalence to the complement for the comeager assertion. Empty spaces cause no difficulty. This argument does not infer that a nonempty open set must be nonmeager.
