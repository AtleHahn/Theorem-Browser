Statement

Let L_1,L_2 be first-order languages with L=L_1\cap L_2, let M be an L_1-structure and N an L_2-structure. If their L-reducts are elementarily equivalent, there exists an L_1-structure M' and elementary embeddings M\to M' in L_1 and N\upharpoonright L\to M'\upharpoonright L in L.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The assertion concerns elementary embeddings and equivalence of reducts of arbitrary first-order structures, beyond calculus and linear algebra.

Proof 1: Take a model of two elementary diagrams — outline

For a first-order language {\displaystyle L} and a set {\displaystyle S}, let {\displaystyle L_{S}} denote the language obtained from {\displaystyle L} by adding 0-ary operations bijectively corresponding to the set {\displaystyle S}. For a first-order language {\displaystyle L}, an {\displaystyle L}-structure {\displaystyle M} and a subset {\displaystyle S\subseteq M}, let {\displaystyle M_{S}} denote the {\displaystyle L_{S}}-structure obtained from {\displaystyle M} by interpreting each 0-ary operation {\displaystyle s\in S} as {\displaystyle s} itself. For a first-order language {\displaystyle L}, its expansion {\displaystyle L'} and an {\displaystyle L'}-structure {\displaystyle M}, let {\displaystyle M\upharpoonright L} denote the reduct of {\displaystyle M} in {\displaystyle L}. Here \operatorname{Th}(A) denotes all sentences true in A. Names for different structures are fresh and disjoint unless they name an already identified common elementary substructure; in that case the same names are used. The notation (L_1)_{M,N} means that names for both structures have been added to L_1.

Using the compactness theorem, it is not hard to show that, for every {\displaystyle L_{1}}-structure {\displaystyle M} and an {\displaystyle L_{2}}-structure {\displaystyle N}, if {\displaystyle M\upharpoonright L} is elementarily equivalent to {\displaystyle N\upharpoonright L}, then the theory

{\displaystyle \operatorname {Th} (M_{M})\cup \operatorname {Th} ((N\upharpoonright L)_{N})}

is satisfiable.

For an {\displaystyle L_{1}}-structure {\displaystyle M} and an {\displaystyle L_{2}}-structure {\displaystyle N}, if {\displaystyle M\upharpoonright L} is elementarily equivalent to {\displaystyle N\upharpoonright L}, then for some {\displaystyle L_{1}}-structure {\displaystyle M'}, there exist an {\displaystyle L_{1}}-elementary embedding {\displaystyle M\hookrightarrow M'} and an {\displaystyle L}-elementary embedding {\displaystyle N\upharpoonright L\hookrightarrow M'\upharpoonright L}. Indeed, take an {\displaystyle (L_{1})_{M,N}}-model

{\displaystyle M''\models \operatorname {Th} (M_{M})\cup \operatorname {Th} ((N\upharpoonright L)_{N})}

and put {\displaystyle M'=M''\upharpoonright L_{1}}.
