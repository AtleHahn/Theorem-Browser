Statement

Let \Sigma=\operatorname{Th}(\mathbb R;+,-,\cdot,0,1,<), the set of all first-order ordered-field sentences true in the real numbers. There is an ordered-field model K\models\Sigma with an element \varepsilon such that 0<\varepsilon<1/n for every positive integer n.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement concerns nonstandard models of the full first-order theory of a field.

Proof 1: Adjoin finitely satisfiable smallness constraints

Consider the theory obtained by adding a new constant symbol \varepsilon to the language and adjoining to \Sigma the axiom \varepsilon>0 and the axioms \varepsilon<1/n for all positive integers n. Clearly, the standard real numbers \mathbb R are a model for every finite subset of these axioms, because the real numbers satisfy everything in \Sigma and, by suitable choice of \varepsilon, can be made to satisfy any finite subset of the axioms about \varepsilon. By the compactness theorem, there is a model K that satisfies \Sigma and also contains an infinitesimal element \varepsilon.
