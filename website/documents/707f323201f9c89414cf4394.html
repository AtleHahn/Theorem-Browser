Statement

If y_n>0 and y_{n+1}/y_n\to\ell\in[0,+\infty], then y_n^{1/n}\to\ell.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves positive real sequences; successive ratios; nth roots; extended limits. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Telescope a product of successive ratios

Suppose we are given a sequence {\displaystyle (y_{n})_{n\geq 1}} and we are asked to compute

{\displaystyle \lim _{n\to \infty }{\sqrt[{n}]{y_{n}}},}

defining {\displaystyle y_{0}=1} and {\displaystyle x_{n}=y_{n}/y_{n-1}} we obtain

{\displaystyle \lim _{n\to \infty }{\sqrt[{n}]{x_{1}\dots x_{n}}}=\lim _{n\to \infty }{\sqrt[{n}]{\frac {y_{1}\dots y_{n}}{y_{0}\cdot y_{1}\dots y_{n-1}}}}=\lim _{n\to \infty }{\sqrt[{n}]{y_{n}}},}

if we apply the geometric-mean theorem (the geometric means of a positive sequence converge to its finite or infinite limit)

{\displaystyle \lim _{n\to \infty }{\sqrt[{n}]{y_{n}}}=\lim _{n\to \infty }x_{n}=\lim _{n\to \infty }{\frac {y_{n}}{y_{n-1}}}.}

Level justification

Telescope successive ratios and apply the stated geometric-mean limit theorem, including zero and infinite limits. Undergraduate real-sequence analysis suffices.
