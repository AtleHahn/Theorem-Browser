Statement

A relation R on a set X is reflexive if xRx for every x\in X, symmetric if xRy implies yRx, and transitive if xRy and yRz imply xRz. An equivalence relation has all three properties. Let R be any binary relation on X. Define a\sim b if some integer n\geq0 and x_0,\ldots,x_n\in X satisfy x_0=a, x_n=b, and x_{i-1}Rx_i or x_iRx_{i-1} for 1\leq i\leq n. Then \sim is the smallest equivalence relation containing R, and equals the intersection of all equivalence relations on X containing R.

Proof 1: The equivalence relation generated by finite chains — outline

The intersection of any collection of equivalence relations over X (binary relations viewed as a subset of {\displaystyle X\times X}) is also an equivalence relation. This yields a convenient way of generating an equivalence relation: given any binary relation R on X, the equivalence relation generated by R is the intersection of all equivalence relations containing R (also known as the smallest equivalence relation containing R). Concretely, R generates the equivalence relation

{\displaystyle a\sim b} if there exists an integer {\displaystyle n\geq0} and elements {\displaystyle x_{0},\ldots ,x_{n}\in X} such that {\displaystyle a=x_{0}}, {\displaystyle b=x_{n}}, and {\displaystyle x_{i-1}\mathrel {R} x_{i}} or {\displaystyle x_{i}\mathrel {R} x_{i-1}}, for {\displaystyle i=1,\ldots ,n.}
