Statement

Let f be injective and holomorphic on D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}, with f(0)=0. Write q(z)=zf'(z)/f(z), extended by q(0)=1, and let L be its analytic logarithm normalized by L(0)=0. Then |q(z)|\le(1+|z|)/(1-|z|) for z\in D.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex differentiation and complex exponentials extend calculus. A normalized analytic logarithm is a function L with exp(L)=q and the supplied value at zero; its imaginary part defines the continuous argument. These direct function identities suffice for the explicit bound without Riemann surfaces.

Proof 1: Modulus bound for the logarithmic derivative of a univalent function

The source Grunsky estimate gives |L(z)|\le\log((1+|z|)/(1-|z|)). Taking real parts gives \log|q(z)|=\Re L(z)\le\log((1+|z|)/(1-|z|)). Exponentiation gives the result.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Take the real part of the supplied logarithm bound and exponentiate. Retain beyond undergraduate provisionally for the underlying univalent-function and global analytic-logarithm framework, despite elementary final scalar inequalities.
