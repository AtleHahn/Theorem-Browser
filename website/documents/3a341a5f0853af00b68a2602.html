Statement

Let \theta range over an open subset of \mathbb R^d, and let an observation X have density f(x;\theta). Let T(X)\in\mathbb R^d be an estimator with mean \psi(\theta) and finite covariance matrix \Sigma(\theta). Assume the score vector s_\theta(x)=\nabla_\theta\log f(x;\theta) exists where the density is positive, its second moments are finite, and differentiation under the integral is valid for the normalization integral and each estimator mean:

\partial_{\theta_j}\int g(x)f(x;\theta)\,dx=\int g(x)\partial_{\theta_j}f(x;\theta)\,dx\qquad(g=1,T_1,\ldots,T_d).

Let I(\theta)=E_\theta[s_\theta(X)s_\theta(X)^T] be the Fisher information matrix, assumed positive definite, and let \Phi(\theta)=D\psi(\theta), with entries \Phi_{ij}=\partial\psi_i/\partial\theta_j. Then

\Sigma(\theta)\succeq\Phi(\theta)I(\theta)^{-1}\Phi(\theta)^T.

If \Sigma(\theta) is also invertible, the complementary form is

I(\theta)\succeq\Phi(\theta)^T\Sigma(\theta)^{-1}\Phi(\theta).

Here A\succeq B means v^T(A-B)v\geq0 for every real vector v. In particular, for an unbiased estimator, \psi(\theta)=\theta and \Sigma(\theta)\succeq I(\theta)^{-1}.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The matrix algebra is undergraduate, but understanding parametric statistical models, scores, estimator means and differentiation regularity cumulatively goes beyond the assumed baseline; the complete statistical formulation cannot confidently be learned in the short allowance.
