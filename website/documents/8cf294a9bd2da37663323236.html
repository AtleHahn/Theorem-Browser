Statement

Let (a_i)_{i\ge0} be real or complex numbers. The series \sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_i converges if and only if for every \varepsilon>0 there is an integer N\ge0 such that |a_{n+1}+\cdots+a_{n+p}|<\varepsilon for all integers n>N and p\ge1.

Proof 1: The Cauchy criterion applied to partial sums

The test works because the space {\displaystyle \mathbb {R} } of real numbers and the space {\displaystyle \mathbb {C} } of complex numbers (with the metric given by the absolute value) are both complete. From here, the series is convergent if and only if the partial sums

{\displaystyle s_{n}:=\sum _{i=0}^{n}a_{i}}

are a Cauchy sequence.

For any real or complex sequence {\displaystyle a_{k}}, the Cauchy criterion for sequences implies that the infinite series

{\displaystyle \sum _{k=0}^{\infty }a_{k}}

converges if and only if for every {\displaystyle \varepsilon >0} there is a number N, such that m ≥ n ≥ N imply

{\displaystyle |s_{m}-s_{n}|=\left|\sum _{k=n+1}^{m}a_{k}\right|<\varepsilon .}
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Series convergence is convergence of its partial sums, and their differences are precisely finite tails. Apply completeness of R or C through the sequence Cauchy criterion. This is standard undergraduate real analysis.
