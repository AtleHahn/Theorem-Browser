Statement

Let (X,d) be complete and f:X\to\mathbb R\cup\{+\infty\} lower semicontinuous, bounded below and not identically infinite. If \varepsilon>0 and f(x_0)\le\inf_X f+\varepsilon, there exists v with f(v)\le f(x_0), d(v,x_0)\le\sqrt\varepsilon, and f(x)+\sqrt\varepsilon\,d(v,x)>f(v) for every x\ne v.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Real analysis supplies complete metric spaces, infima and inequalities. Lower semicontinuity means closed sublevel sets; allowing positive infinity is an order convention. These small extensions suffice to understand the stated minimization and perturbation conditions.

Proof 1: Set the corollary parameter to square-root epsilon

Use the Ekeland corollary: for every \lambda>0 there exists v with f(v)\le f(x_0), d(x_0,v)\le\lambda, and f(x)+(\varepsilon/\lambda)d(v,x)>f(v) for x\ne v. Set \lambda=\sqrt\varepsilon.

Level

Level justification

Apply the explicitly supplied metric distance-slope conclusion with lambda equal to sqrt epsilon. Complete metric spaces, infima and the lower-semicontinuity definition are a modest extension of introductory analysis; the proof does not invoke differentiable variational machinery.
