Statement

Let H be a real Hilbert space and \varphi:H\to\mathbb R\cup\{+\infty\} be proper, convex and lower semicontinuous. Proper means finite at some point; lower semicontinuity means each sublevel set is closed. Define its subdifferential by

\partial\varphi(y)=\{u\in H:\varphi(z)\ge\varphi(y)+\langle u,z-y\rangle\text{ for all }z\in H\}

when \varphi(y)<\infty, and the empty set otherwise. For \alpha>0 let J_\alpha x be the unique y satisfying x-y\in\alpha\partial\varphi(y), and put A_\alpha x=(x-J_\alpha x)/\alpha. The envelope

\varphi_\alpha(x)=\inf_{y\in H}\left(\frac{\|y-x\|^2}{2\alpha}+\varphi(y)\right)

is finite, convex and Fréchet differentiable on H, with

\varphi_\alpha(x)=\frac\alpha2\|A_\alpha x\|^2+\varphi(J_\alpha x),\qquad D\varphi_\alpha(x)[h]=\langle A_\alpha x,h\rangle.

For every x\in H, \varphi_\alpha(x) increases to \varphi(x) as \alpha\downarrow0, allowing the limit +\infty. A Hilbert space is a complete inner-product space; Fréchet differentiation means the displayed linear approximation has error o(\|h\|).
