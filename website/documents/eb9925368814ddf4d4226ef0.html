Failure of Beth definability restricted to finite models

Uniqueness of interpretation across finite models alone does not ensure an explicit definition valid across all models. This qualifies the connection between semantic determination and expressibility: changing the class of structures under consideration can change what definability conclusions are justified.

Statement

There exist first-order languages L\subseteq L^{\prime}, an L^{\prime}-theory T, and an L^{\prime}-formula \varphi(\bar x) such that any two finite models A,B of T with the same L-reduct give the same truth value to \varphi(\bar a) for every tuple \bar a in their common underlying set, yet no L-formula \psi(\bar x) is equivalent to \varphi(\bar x) modulo T. Here a reduct forgets the symbols outside L, and equivalence modulo T means agreement for every tuple in every model of T.
