Statement

For d\ge1 and locally integrable f:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb C, define Mf(x)=\sup_{r>0}|B(x,r)|^{-1}\int_{B(x,r)}|f(y)|dy, where B(x,r) is the ball centered at x of radius r and |\cdot| denotes Lebesgue measure. Then Mf is measurable.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Lebesgue measurability and Lp spaces require measure theory beyond the calculus baseline.

Proof 1: Supremum of continuous averages

Since {\displaystyle f} is locally integrable, the averages are jointly continuous in x and r, so the maximal function Mf, being the supremum over r > 0, is measurable.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Use continuity of ball averages of a locally Lebesgue-integrable function and measurability of their supremum. The local-integrability and general measurable-function framework requires measure theory.
