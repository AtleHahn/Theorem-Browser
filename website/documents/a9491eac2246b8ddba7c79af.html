Statement

Fix z_0\in\mathbb C, r>0 and M<\infty. A sequence of holomorphic functions on B(z_0,r) bounded in modulus by M has a subsequence converging uniformly on B(z_0,r/2).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Real analysis supplies derivatives, maxima, uniform convergence and completeness; complex arithmetic supplies the plane. Holomorphic means complex differentiable, and entire means so throughout the plane. With that short extension, the displayed bound or convergence assertion uses familiar analytic concepts without contour integrals or specialist function-space theory.

Proof 1: Cauchy estimates give equicontinuity

Dependency

On a compact Hausdorff space X, a pointwise bounded equicontinuous family in C(X,\mathbb R) has compact closure for the uniform norm. Equicontinuity means that for each x and \varepsilon>0 there is a neighborhood U of x with |f(y)-f(x)|<\varepsilon for every f in the family and y\in U. The criterion applies to complex functions by their real and imaginary parts.

Cauchy derivative estimate

When f is holomorphic in an open disk D1 = B(z0, r), with modulus bounded by M, then (for example by Cauchy's formula) its derivative f′ has modulus bounded by ⁠2M/r⁠ in the smaller disk D2 = B(z0, ⁠r/2⁠). If a family of holomorphic functions on D1 is bounded by M on D1, it follows that the family F of restrictions to D2 is equicontinuous on D2. Therefore, a sequence converging uniformly on D2 can be extracted. This is a first step in the direction of Montel's theorem.

Level

Level justification

The proof derives derivative control from the Cauchy formula and uses equicontinuity to extract a uniform subsequence. Cauchy estimates require complex analysis beyond the baseline, although the compactness criterion itself is accessible from undergraduate topology.
