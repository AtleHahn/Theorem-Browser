Statement

Let a nondegenerate tetrahedron in Euclidean three-dimensional space have an inscribed sphere tangent to each face. If a face has vertices A,B,C and tangency point T, associate to it the unordered triple, with multiplicities,

\{\angle ATB,\angle BTC,\angle CTA\}.

The four faces have the same unordered triple of angles. The angles are the ordinary angles between the three segments from the interior point T to the vertices of its face.

Review notes

  • A geometric relation equating the contact-angle triples of all four faces of an arbitrary tetrahedron.

Proof 1: Equal tangent lengths and opposite edge angles (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Opening Lemma and proof; Theorem of Bang paragraph.).

Assign to each tetrahedron edge AB the angle at the tangency point in either adjacent face. The two triangles with base AB are congruent: their other sides are the equal tangent lengths from A and from B. Thus this angle is well defined. In every face its three assigned angles sum to 360^\circ. The lemma applied to the three pairs of opposite edges makes the angles on opposite edges equal, so every face has the same triple, including multiplicities.
