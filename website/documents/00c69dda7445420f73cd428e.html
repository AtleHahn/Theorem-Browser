Statement

Let f:N_{>0}→N_{≥0} be nondecreasing and unbounded. Define f*(n)=#{x≥1:f(x)<n}. Then f* is nondecreasing and unbounded, and (f*)*(k)=f(k) for every k≥1, where the star operation again counts positive arguments with value less than k.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Only explicitly defined integer functions, monotonicity, elementary quantifiers and finite initial-segment counts are used; no general infinite-set theorem is required.

Proof 1: Unwind the counting inverse twice — outline

The counting definition is finite because f is unbounded and nondecreasing. Increasing n cannot decrease the count. For each k, taking n>f(k) gives f*(n)≥k, so f* is unbounded. By monotonicity,

f^*(n)<k\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f(k)\geq n.

There are exactly f(k) positive integers n with this property. Thus (f*)*(k)=f(k), which is the source stated consequence of the counting definition.
