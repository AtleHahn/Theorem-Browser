Statement

Let M be a finite-length module over a ring R, and let f:M\to M be an R-module endomorphism. There exists an integer k\ge1 such that M=\ker(f^k)\oplus\operatorname{im}(f^k).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Finite module length, kernels and internal direct sums are abstract module-theoretic objects rather than ordinary finite-dimensional vector spaces.

Proof 1: Stabilize kernels and images

Take an endomorphism f of M and consider the following two chains of submodules:

  • The first is the descending chain \operatorname{im}(f)\supseteq\operatorname{im}(f^2)\supseteq\operatorname{im}(f^3)\supseteq\cdots.
  • The second is the ascending chain \ker(f)\subseteq\ker(f^2)\subseteq\ker(f^3)\subseteq\cdots.

Because M has finite length, both of these chains must eventually stabilize, so there is some n\geq1 with \operatorname{im}(f^n)=\operatorname{im}(f^{n'}) for all n'\geq n, and some m\geq1 with \ker(f^m)=\ker(f^{m'}) for all m'\geq m.

Let now k=\max\{n,m\}, and note that by construction \operatorname{im}(f^{2k})=\operatorname{im}(f^k) and \ker(f^{2k})=\ker(f^k).

We claim that \ker(f^k)\cap\operatorname{im}(f^k)=0. Indeed, every x\in\ker(f^k)\cap\operatorname{im}(f^k) satisfies x=f^k(y) for some y\in M but also f^k(x)=0, so that

0=f^k(x)=f^k(f^k(y))=f^{2k}(y),

therefore y\in\ker(f^{2k})=\ker(f^k) and thus x=f^k(y)=0.

Moreover, \ker(f^k)+\operatorname{im}(f^k)=M: for every x\in M, there exists some y\in M such that f^k(x)=f^{2k}(y) (since f^k(x)\in\operatorname{im}(f^k)=\operatorname{im}(f^{2k})), and thus

f^k(x-f^k(y))=f^k(x)-f^{2k}(y)=0,

so that x-f^k(y)\in\ker(f^k) and thus

x\in\ker(f^k)+f^k(y)\subseteq\ker(f^k)+\operatorname{im}(f^k).

Consequently, M is the direct sum of \operatorname{im}(f^k) and \ker(f^k).
