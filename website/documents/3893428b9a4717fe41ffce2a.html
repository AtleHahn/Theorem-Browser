Statement

Let Q be a nonempty quasigroup: each equation ax=b or ya=b has a unique solution. Suppose (zx)(yz)=(z(xy))z for every x,y,z∈Q. Then Q has a two-sided identity and is a loop.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory algebra supplies binary operations, identity, inverses and generated substructures. A loop removes associativity while retaining unique division; the Moufang identities are explicit equations. Isotopy/translation, where used, is defined by specified bijections. These direct variations of group axioms suffice for understanding the full claim.

Proof 1: Construct a left identity and use cancellation twice

Let a be any element of Q, and let e be the unique element such that ae=a.

Then for any x in Q,

(xa)x=(x(ae))x=(xa)(ex).

Cancelling xa on the left gives x=ex so that e is a left identity element.

Now for any y in Q,

ye=(ey)(ee)=(e(ye))e=(ye)e.

Cancelling e on the right gives y=ye, so e is also a right identity element.

Therefore, e is a two-sided identity element.

Level justification

Choose the unique right-identity solution for one element and use the displayed identity and cancellation twice to make it a global two-sided identity. Undergraduate algebra and the locally defined quasigroup operation suffice.
