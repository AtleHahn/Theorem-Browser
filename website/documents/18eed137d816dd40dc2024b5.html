Statement

For every nonempty bounded open set V\subset\mathbb R^2, \operatorname{diam}V=\operatorname{diam}\partial V, where \operatorname{diam}A=\sup\{|x-y|:x,y\in A\}.

Proof 1: A bounded open planar set and its boundary have equal diameter — outline

The set \overline V\times\overline V is compact, so two points u,v\in\overline V maximize their distance. These maximizing points must lie on \partial V. The diameters of V and of its boundary therefore both equal |u-v|.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Compactness gives a maximizing pair in the closure. Neither point can be interior, since moving it slightly away from the other increases the distance. Density of V in its closure shows the closure and V have the same diameter. This is undergraduate Euclidean topology and continuity.
