Statement

Let five distinct points in \mathbb R^2 have no three collinear. If a nonzero polynomial Q(x,y)=Ax^2+Bxy+Cy^2+Dx+Ey+F vanishes at all five, then at least one of A,B,C is nonzero, and Q is not a product of two real affine-linear polynomials of degree one.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The hypotheses and conclusion concern five real points, collinearity and an explicitly displayed quadratic polynomial factoring into affine-linear factors, all elementary algebra and plane geometry.

Proof 1: Exclude a linear equation and a product of two lines

If A=B=C=0, the nonzero equation Dx+Ey+F=0 would put all five points on a line; a nonzero constant has no zeros. Thus the polynomial is quadratic. If it split into two lines, their union would contain the five points. By the pigeonhole principle at least one of the lines would contain three of the five, contradicting the hypothesis.

Level
High school only
Level justification

A nonzero linear polynomial cannot vanish on the five points, and two lines covering five points would put three on one line. This uses elementary coordinate geometry and pigeonhole counting.
