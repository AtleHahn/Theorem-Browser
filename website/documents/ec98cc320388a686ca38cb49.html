Statement

For a positive prime p\equiv1\pmod3, the congruence x^3\equiv2\pmod p is solvable if and only if p=a^2+27b^2 for some integers a,b.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement is an existence equivalence for integer cubes and squares.

Proof 1: Rescale the variable divisible by three — outline

A theorem of Fermat gives a representation p=a^2+3c^2. Use the stated Euler criterion: in the representation p=a^2+3c^2, two is a cubic residue exactly when 3\mid c. Rewriting c=3b gives precisely p=a^2+27b^2.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Use the two supplied integer representation criteria and replace a coefficient divisible by three with three times an integer. The actual argument needs only elementary integer algebra.
