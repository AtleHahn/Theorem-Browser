Statement

Let p\geq2 and k_1,\ldots,k_p\geq1 be integers with n=\sum_i k_i. With \binom n{k_1,\ldots,k_p}=n!/(k_1!\cdots k_p!), one has \sum_{j=1}^p\binom{n-1}{k_1,\ldots,k_j-1,\ldots,k_p}=\binom n{k_1,\ldots,k_p}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Finite counting and factorial algebra are elementary.

Proof 1: Common denominator and sum of indices

The algebraic derivation is as follows. Let p\geq2, let k_1,\ldots,k_p be positive integers, and let n=k_1+\cdots+k_p. Then

\begin{aligned} &\binom{n-1}{k_1-1,k_2,\ldots,k_p} +\binom{n-1}{k_1,k_2-1,\ldots,k_p} +\cdots+\binom{n-1}{k_1,k_2,\ldots,k_p-1}\\ &=\frac{(n-1)!}{(k_1-1)!k_2!\cdots k_p!} +\frac{(n-1)!}{k_1!(k_2-1)!\cdots k_p!} +\cdots+\frac{(n-1)!}{k_1!k_2!\cdots(k_p-1)!}\\ &=\frac{k_1(n-1)!}{k_1!k_2!\cdots k_p!} +\frac{k_2(n-1)!}{k_1!k_2!\cdots k_p!} +\cdots+\frac{k_p(n-1)!}{k_1!k_2!\cdots k_p!}\\ &=\frac{(k_1+k_2+\cdots+k_p)(n-1)!}{k_1!k_2!\cdots k_p!}\\ &=\frac{n(n-1)!}{k_1!k_2!\cdots k_p!} =\frac{n!}{k_1!k_2!\cdots k_p!} =\binom n{k_1,k_2,\ldots,k_p}. \end{aligned}
Level
High school only
Level justification

Put the factorial fractions over a common denominator and use the sum of the indices. Elementary algebra suffices.
