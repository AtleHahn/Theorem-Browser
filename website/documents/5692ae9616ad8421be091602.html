Statement

Let X be a set and K:X\times X\to\mathbb R be symmetric and positive semidefinite, meaning every finite sum \sum_{i,j}a_ia_jK(x_i,x_j) is nonnegative. There is a unique Hilbert space H of functions on X with reproducing kernel K.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The conclusion asserts existence and uniqueness of an entire Hilbert function space for a prescribed positive kernel.

Proof 1: Complete the kernel span and use orthogonal uniqueness — outline

For all x in X, define K_x=K(x,\cdot). Let H_0 be the linear span of \{K_x:x\in X\}. Define an inner product on H_0 by

\left\langle\sum_{j=1}^n b_jK_{y_j},\sum_{i=1}^m a_iK_{x_i}\right\rangle_{H_0} =\sum_{i=1}^m\sum_{j=1}^n a_ib_jK(y_j,x_i),

which implies K(x,y)=\langle K_x,K_y\rangle_{H_0}. The symmetry of this inner product follows from the symmetry of K. Its representation independence and nondegeneracy on the span of the kernel functions follow from positive semidefiniteness, using the kernel-form Cauchy–Schwarz inequality.

Let H be the completion of H_0 with respect to this inner product. Then H consists of functions of the form

f(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_iK_{x_i}(x) \quad\text{where}\quad \lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{p\geq0} \left\|\sum_{i=n}^{n+p}a_iK_{x_i}\right\|_{H_0}=0.

Here evaluations extend continuously to the completion. Now we can check the reproducing property:

\langle f,K_x\rangle_H =\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_i\langle K_{x_i},K_x\rangle_{H_0} =\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_iK(x_i,x)=f(x).

To prove uniqueness, let G be another Hilbert space of functions for which K is a reproducing kernel. For every x and y in X, the reproducing property implies that

\langle K_x,K_y\rangle_H=K(x,y)=\langle K_x,K_y\rangle_G.

By linearity, \langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_H=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_G on the span of \{K_x:x\in X\}. Then H\subset G, identifying H with its isometric image, because G is complete and contains H_0 and hence contains its completion.

Now we need to prove that every element of G is in H. Let f be an element of G. Since H is a closed subspace of G, we can write f=f_H+f_{H^\perp} where f_H\in H and f_{H^\perp}\in H^\perp. Now if x\in X then, since K is a reproducing kernel of G and H:

\begin{aligned} f(x)&=\langle K_x,f\rangle_G\\ &=\langle K_x,f_H\rangle_G+\langle K_x,f_{H^\perp}\rangle_G\\ &=\langle K_x,f_H\rangle_G =\langle K_x,f_H\rangle_H=f_H(x), \end{aligned}

where we have used the fact that K_x belongs to H so that its inner product with f_{H^\perp} in G is zero. This shows that f=f_H in G and concludes the proof.
