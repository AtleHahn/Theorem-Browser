Statement

Let X be a smooth connected projective curve over an algebraically closed field k, of genus g\ge2, and let C\subseteq\mathbb P^{g-1}_k be its canonical image. At least one of the following holds:

  • C is the common zero set of the homogeneous quadratic polynomials vanishing on it.
  • C, as an abstract smooth curve, is trigonal: it admits a finite morphism of degree three to \mathbb P^1_k.
  • C, as an abstract smooth curve, is isomorphic to a smooth plane quintic, a curve in \mathbb P^2_k defined by a homogeneous polynomial of degree five.

The canonical map is the map given by a basis of the g-dimensional space of regular differential one-forms on X, viewed as sections of its canonical line bundle. Its image is C. For nonhyperelliptic X this is an embedding and identifies X with C; hyperelliptic means admitting a degree-two morphism to \mathbb P^1_k, in which case the image is the rational normal curve, projectively equivalent to the image of [s:t]\mapsto[s^{g-1}:s^{g-2}t:\cdots:t^{g-1}]. The first alternative is set-theoretic: it says that a point of \mathbb P^{g-1}_k lies in C exactly when all those quadratics vanish there; no claim that a chosen list is a complete intersection is made. The alternatives are not asserted to be mutually exclusive.

Proof 1: Canonical quadrics: an expert proof outline (external)

A link to the proof outline can be found here (p.157 explicitly states arbitrary characteristic; §3 ideal generators; §4 identity (4.2), dichotomy (4.7), minimal-degree lemma (4.8), classification (4.9), and exceptional cases (4.10)–(4.12), pp.170–174.).

This is an expert proof outline with the following low-genus supplements supplied here. For nonhyperelliptic genus at least five use Saint-Donat §§3–4: quadratic relations f and additional cubics G generate the ideal; Petri’s identity reduces the cubics when its coefficients do not all vanish. Otherwise the quadrics contain a surface of minimal degree. Classification of these surfaces and adjunction give a trigonal ruling or a plane quintic. This argument is explicitly over arbitrary characteristic. Ideal generation implies the weaker set-theoretic conclusion required here. For the remaining cases: the hyperelliptic canonical image is the rational normal curve of degree d=g-1, whose ideal is generated by the 2-by-2 minors of the matrix with rows (x0,...,x[d-1]) and (x1,...,xd); for d=1 its image is all P1, cut out by the zero ideal. In genus three a nonhyperelliptic curve is a smooth plane quartic; lines through any point of it, after subtracting that point, give a base-point-free degree-three pencil. In genus four the canonical model is a quadric-cubic complete intersection in P3; a ruling on the quadric gives a degree-three pencil, also for the quadric cone using its resolved ruling. These standard low-genus canonical descriptions are named dependencies and valid over the stated field.
