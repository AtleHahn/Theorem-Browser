Statement

Let a group G act by isometries on a nonempty proper geodesic metric space X. Suppose the action is properly discontinuous and cocompact. Then G is finitely generated, and for every finite generating set T and every x_0\in X, the orbit map (G,d_T)\to X, g\mapsto gx_0, is a quasi-isometry. Here d_T is the word metric, with T symmetrized if necessary.

A proper metric space has compact closed balls. Proper discontinuity means that \{g\in G:gK\cap K\ne\varnothing\} is finite for every compact K\subseteq X. Cocompactness means that X/G is compact; under these hypotheses the translates of a closed ball cover X. This is the geodesic case explicitly treated in the source's proof.

Proof 1: Geodesic subdivision and orbit bounds — outline

Setup

Given an element g of G, its word norm |g| with respect to the generating set S is defined to be the shortest length of a word {\displaystyle w} over S whose evaluation {\displaystyle {\bar {w}}} is equal to g. Given two elements g,h in G, the distance d(g,h) in the word metric with respect to S is defined to be {\displaystyle |g^{-1}h|}. Equivalently, d(g,h) is the shortest length of a word w over S such that {\displaystyle g{\bar {w}}=h}.

The word metric on G can also be defined without assuming that the generating set S is closed under inverse. To do this, first symmetrize S, replacing it by a larger generating set consisting of each {\displaystyle s} in S as well as its inverse {\displaystyle s^{-1}}. Then define the word metric with respect to S to be the word metric with respect to the symmetrization of S.

Orbit estimates

Write B(x,r) for the closed ball. Fix any {\displaystyle x_{0}\in X}. Since the action is cocompact and {\displaystyle X} is proper, we may find {\displaystyle R>0} such that the {\displaystyle G}-translates of {\displaystyle B(x_{0},R)} cover the whole space {\displaystyle X}. That is,

{\displaystyle X=\bigcup _{g\in G}{B(gx_{0},R)}}\tag{1}

Consider the subset {\displaystyle S=\{g\mid gx_{0}\in B(x_{0},2R+1)\}} which is finite, because the action is proper.

If gx_0=x_0, then g\in S and \|g\|_S\le1, giving (3) directly. Otherwise, divide the geodesic between {\displaystyle x_{0}} and {\displaystyle gx_{0}} into {\displaystyle n} pieces, where each piece is of length {\displaystyle 1}, except possibly the last piece, which may be less than {\displaystyle 1}. Denote the endpoints of these pieces by

{\displaystyle p_{0}(=x_{0}),p_{1},\dots ,p_{n}(=gx_{0})}.

For each {\displaystyle p_{i}}, we may take {\displaystyle g_{i}\in G} such that {\displaystyle p_{i}\in B(g_{i}x_{0},R)}. Setting {\displaystyle g_{0}=e} and {\displaystyle g_{n}=g}. By a triangle inequality, and since the action is by isometries, we have

{\displaystyle d(x_{0},g_{i-1}^{-1}g_{i}x_{0})=d(g_{i-1}x_{0},g_{i}x_{0})\leq 2R+1}\tag{2}

Thus, by definition of {\displaystyle S}, {\displaystyle g_{i-1}^{-1}g_{i}=:s_{i}\in S}. Therefore, {\displaystyle g=s_{1}s_{2}\cdots s_{n}} and hence {\displaystyle \|g\|_{S}\leq n}. Also, {\displaystyle g} is arbitrary, so {\displaystyle S} generates {\displaystyle G}.

Additionally, because each {\displaystyle p_{i}} lies along a geodesic from {\displaystyle x_{0}} to {\displaystyle gx_{0}} with segment lengths of at most {\displaystyle 1}, the following inequality holds

{\displaystyle d(x_{0},gx_{0})\geq n-1\geq \|g\|_{S}-1}\tag{3}

By repeatedly applying the generator displacement bound d(x_0,sx_0)\le2R+1 along a shortest word for g, and by the triangle inequality, it's easy to see that

{\displaystyle d(x_{0},gx_{0})\leq (2R+1)\cdot \|g\|_{S}}\tag{4}

Now, (3) and (4) imply that for any {\displaystyle u,v\in G},

{\displaystyle d_{S}(u,v)-1\leq d(ux_{0},vx_{0})\leq (2R+1)\cdot d_{S}(u,v)}\tag{5}

where we take {\displaystyle u^{-1}v} as the above {\displaystyle g}.

By (1), every point of X lies within distance R of the orbit, so the orbit map is coarsely surjective.

Change of finite generating set

The required finite-word-metric comparison is supplied by the archived Word metric article, “Bilipschitz invariants of a group”:

In general, the word metric on a group G is not unique, because different symmetric generating sets give different word metrics. However, finitely generated word metrics are unique up to bilipschitz equivalence: if {\displaystyle S}, {\displaystyle T} are two symmetric, finite generating sets for G with corresponding word metrics {\displaystyle d_{S}}, {\displaystyle d_{T}}, then there is a constant {\displaystyle K\geq 1} such that for any {\displaystyle g,h\in G},

{\displaystyle {\frac {1}{K}}\,d_{T}(g,h)\leq d_{S}(g,h)\leq K\,d_{T}(g,h)}.

This constant K can be taken to be the maximum of 1 and the {\displaystyle d_{S}} word norms of elements of {\displaystyle T} and the {\displaystyle d_{T}} word norms of elements of {\displaystyle S}. This proof is also easy: any word over S can be converted by substitution into a word over T, expanding the length of the word by a factor of at most K, and similarly for converting words over T into words over S.

The orbit estimates (5), coarse surjectivity, and this comparison give the assertion for every finite generating set.
