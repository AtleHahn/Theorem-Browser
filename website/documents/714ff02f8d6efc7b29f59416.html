Statement

Let n\ge1, let g be n times continuously differentiable on an open real interval I, and let f be n times continuously differentiable on an open interval containing g(I). Then D^n(f\circ g)(x)=\sum_{k=1}^nf^{(k)}(g(x))B_{n,k}(g'(x),\ldots,g^{(n-k+1)}(x)). For order zero the identity is simply f(g(x)), using B_{0,0}=1 without derivative arguments. For 1\le k\le n, define B_{n,k}(z_1,\ldots,z_{n-k+1})=\sum \frac{n!}{m_1!\cdots m_n!}\prod_{j=1}^{n-k+1}(z_j/j!)^{m_j}, summed over nonnegative integers with \sum m_j=k and \sum j m_j=n. Put B_{0,0}=1 and B_{n,0}=0 for n>0; B_n=\sum_{k=0}^nB_{n,k}.

Proof 1: Group terms by the order of the outer derivative

Use the scalar Faà di Bruno formula in multiplicities:

\frac{d^n}{dx^n}f(g(x)) =\sum_{\sum jm_j=n}\frac{n!}{m_1!\,m_2!\cdots m_n!} f^{(m_1+\cdots+m_n)}(g(x)) \prod_{j=1}^n\left(\frac{g^{(j)}(x)}{j!}\right)^{m_j}.

Combining the terms with the same value of m_1+m_2+\cdots+m_n=k and noticing that m_j has to be zero for j>n-k+1 leads to a somewhat simpler formula expressed in terms of partial (or incomplete) exponential Bell polynomials:

\frac{d^n}{dx^n}f(g(x)) =\sum_{k=1}^n f^{(k)}(g(x))B_{n,k}\bigl(g'(x),g''(x),\dots,g^{(n-k+1)}(x)\bigr).

For n>0 the polynomials B_{n,0} are zero and thus summation in the formula can start with k=1. For n=0, the identity is simply f(g(x)), with B_{0,0}=1 and no derivative arguments.
