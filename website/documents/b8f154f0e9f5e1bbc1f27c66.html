Statement

Let C\subset\mathbb R^2 be a Jordan curve, the image of an injective continuous map of the circle, and let U be the bounded component of its complement. For every P\in U and A\in C there is an injective continuous map \gamma:[0,1]\to\mathbb R^2 with \gamma(0)=P, \gamma(1)=A, and \gamma([0,1))\subset U.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Basic topology supplies continuous injections, connected components, path connectedness and homeomorphisms. The sphere or Jordan curve is explicitly a familiar Euclidean subset or continuous circle image; the full assertion needs no homology or manifold machinery used by its proof.

Proof 1: Transport disk accessibility by Schönflies — outline

Use the source-stated Jordan–Schönflies extension theorem: a parametrization of C by the unit circle extends to a homeomorphism of the plane, identifying the unit disk interior with U and its boundary with C. In the disk, an interior point can be joined to any boundary point by a Jordan arc whose remaining points lie inside the disk. Under this homeomorphism that accessibility property gives a Jordan arc from P to A, lying otherwise in U.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Transport a straight disk segment across the supplied plane homeomorphism. Undergraduate point-set topology and continuous injective arcs suffice; no proof of Schonflies is needed.
