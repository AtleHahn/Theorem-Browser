Statement

Let S\subseteq\mathbb R^d be nonempty, compact and convex, and let \varphi assign each x\in S a nonempty convex subset \varphi(x)\subseteq S. Suppose its graph is closed: whenever x_j\to x, y_j\to y in S and y_j\in\varphi(x_j), one has y\in\varphi(x). Then some x\in S satisfies x\in\varphi(x).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves compact convex subsets of Euclidean space; set-valued functions; closed graphs via sequences. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Approximate selections and Brouwer fixed points

For a subset A of the Euclidean product space, write [A]_\varepsilon for its \varepsilon-neighborhood.

By the approximate selection theorem, there exists a sequence of continuous {\displaystyle f_{n}:S\to S} such that {\displaystyle \operatorname {graph} (f_{n})\subset [\operatorname {graph} (\varphi )]_{1/n}}. By Brouwer fixed-point theorem, there exists a sequence {\displaystyle x_{n}} such that {\displaystyle f_{n}(x_{n})=x_{n}}, so {\displaystyle (x_{n},x_{n})\in [\operatorname {graph} (\varphi )]_{1/n}}.

Since {\displaystyle S} is compact, we can take a convergent subsequence {\displaystyle x_{n_j}\to x}. Then {\displaystyle (x,x)\in \operatorname {graph} (\varphi )} since it is a closed set.
