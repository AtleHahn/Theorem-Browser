Statement

For n\ge2 and R>0, let B_R=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:|x|\le R\} and let \omega_n be the volume of the unit ball. Here A+B=\{a+b:a\in A,b\in B\} denotes the Minkowski sum. The outer Minkowski content of its boundary is \lim_{\delta\downarrow0}\frac{\operatorname{vol}(B_R+B_\delta)-\operatorname{vol}(B_R)}{\delta}=n\omega_nR^{n-1}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Euclidean balls; Volume; Minkowski sums; One-sided limits. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. Proof 1: Differentiate the radius-scaling formula for ball volume

Taking the compact set to be B_R gives the following formula for the surface area of the sphere of radius R, S_R:=\partial B_R:

\begin{aligned} \lambda(S_R) &=\lim_{\delta\downarrow0} \frac{\operatorname{vol}(B_R+B_\delta)-\operatorname{vol}(B_R)}{\delta}\\ &=\lim_{\delta\downarrow0}\frac{[(R+\delta)^n-R^n]\omega_n}{\delta}\\ &=nR^{n-1}\omega_n. \end{aligned}

Here B_R+B_\delta=B_{R+\delta} and \omega_n is the volume of the unit ball.

Level justification

Add Euclidean balls, apply radius volume scaling and take the derivative of R to the nth power. Undergraduate Euclidean n-space and calculus suffice; Minkowski content is explicitly defined by this limit.
