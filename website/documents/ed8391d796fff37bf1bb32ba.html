Statement

Let K be a field of characteristic different from 2 and 3. Let a,b,c,d\in K, with a\ne0, and set f(x)=ax^3+bx^2+cx+d. Put \Delta_0=b^2-3ac and \Delta_1=2b^3-9abc+27a^2d. If both are zero, the root -b/(3a) has multiplicity three. Otherwise choose a nonzero C satisfying C^3=(\Delta_1\pm\sqrt{\Delta_1^2-4\Delta_0^3})/2 in an algebraic closure, and a primitive cube root of unity \xi. The roots with multiplicity are x_k=-(b+\xi^kC+\Delta_0/(\xi^kC))/(3a), k=0,1,2.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory algebra supplies polynomial factorization, multiplicity and field characteristic. An algebraic closure only supplies all polynomial roots here, and a primitive cube root is a nonidentity root of X cubed minus one. The displayed finite formulas need no Galois or extension-degree theory.

Proof 1: Translate Cardano’s formula — outline

Put p=(3ac-b^2)/(3a^2) and q=(2b^3-9abc+27a^2d)/(27a^3). The substitution x=t-b/(3a) gives f(t-b/(3a))=a(t^3+pt+q).

Use the depressed-cubic Cardano formula: if W\ne0 and W^3=-q/2\pm\sqrt{q^2/4+p^3/27}, its roots are \xi^kW-p/(3\xi^kW). Applying the translation gives the following formulas in \Delta_0 and \Delta_1.

The formula is rather complicated, so it is worth splitting it into smaller formulas.

Let

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\Delta _{0}&=b^{2}-3ac,\\\Delta _{1}&=2b^{3}-9abc+27a^{2}d.\end{aligned}}}

Then let

{\displaystyle C={\sqrt[{3}]{\frac {\Delta _{1}\pm {\sqrt {\Delta _{1}^{2}-4\Delta _{0}^{3}}}}{2}}},}

where the symbols {\displaystyle {\sqrt {{~}^{~}}}} and {\displaystyle {\sqrt[{3}]{{~}^{~}}}} are interpreted as any square root and any cube root, respectively (in the algebraic closure, every nonzero element has two square roots and three cube roots in the stated characteristics). The sign "±" before the square root is either "+" or "–"; the choice is almost arbitrary, and changing it amounts to choosing a different square root. However, if a choice yields C = 0 (this occurs if {\displaystyle \Delta _{0}=0}), then the other sign must be selected instead. The case \Delta_0=\Delta_1=0 is treated separately below. With these conventions, one of the roots is

{\displaystyle x=-{\frac {1}{3a}}\left(b+C+{\frac {\Delta _{0}}{C}}\right).}

The other two roots can be obtained by changing the choice of the cube root in the definition of C, or, equivalently by multiplying C by a primitive cube root of unity, that is (-1\pm\sqrt{-3})/2. In other words, the three roots are

{\displaystyle x_{k}=-{\frac {1}{3a}}\left(b+\xi ^{k}C+{\frac {\Delta _{0}}{\xi ^{k}C}}\right),\qquad k\in \{0,1,2\}{\text{,}}}

where \xi=(-1+\sqrt{-3})/2.

In the remaining case, if {\displaystyle \Delta _{0}=\Delta _{1}=0,} the three roots equal {\displaystyle {\frac {-b}{3a}},} which means that the cubic polynomial can be factored as {\displaystyle \textstyle a(x+{\frac {b}{3a}})^{3}.} A straightforward computation allows verifying that the existence of this factorization is equivalent with {\displaystyle \Delta _{0}=\Delta _{1}=0.}
