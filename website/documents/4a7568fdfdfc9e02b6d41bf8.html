Statement

For real x>1, \sum_{n\ge1}(2nx^{2n})^{-1}=-\tfrac12\log(1-x^{-2}), and this tends to zero as x\to\infty.

Proof 1: Logarithm power-series substitution

The trivial zeros −2, −4, −6, −8, ... can be handled separately:

{\displaystyle \sum _{n=1}^{\infty }{\frac {1}{2n\,x^{2n}}}=-{\frac {1}{2}}\log \left(1-{\frac {1}{x^{2}}}\right),}

which tends to zero as x\to\infty.

The source states this logarithmic sum without expanding the power-series substitution.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Substitute u=x^(-2) in the logarithm series and use continuity at zero. Ordinary power-series analysis suffices.
