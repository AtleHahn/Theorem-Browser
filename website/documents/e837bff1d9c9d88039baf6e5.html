Statement

Write BPI for the assertion that whenever an ideal I and a filter F of a Boolean algebra are disjoint, some prime ideal contains I and is disjoint from F. Assume BPI. If \mathcal F is a nonempty family of nonempty subsets of a set X, closed under finite intersections and supersets, then \mathcal F is contained in a maximal such family, called an ultrafilter.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

From abstract algebra and sets, a Boolean algebra is a complemented distributive lattice modeled by union, intersection and complement of subsets. Ideals and filters are the corresponding downward/join and upward/meet closed families; prime and maximal impose explicit closure and containment conditions. BPI is stated as an assumption and the set-filter conclusion is defined. These closely related order-algebra definitions fit about 30 minutes using the powerset example; no independence or choice-principle theory is needed.

Proof 1: Apply the dual principle to a powerset — outline

For an ideal of a Boolean algebra, being prime is equivalent to being maximal. Its dual establishes the equivalence of prime filters and ultrafilters.

One can exploit the fact that the dual orders of Boolean algebras are exactly the Boolean algebras themselves. Hence, taking the equivalent dual of BPI gives the extension statement where every occurrence of ideal is replaced by filter, and conversely.

For the special case where the Boolean algebra under consideration is a powerset with the subset ordering, the maximal filter theorem is called the ultrafilter lemma. The subsets of any set form a Boolean algebra partially ordered by inclusion, so this applies to the filter \mathcal F on X.

Level justification

The proof treats BPI as an assumed extension principle and applies order reversal and the powerset model. Ideals and filters here are downward- and upward-closed families closed under the corresponding finite operation; prime versus maximal is supplied as a theorem. These are related set-order definitions, a small extension of undergraduate algebra. No independence or model theory of choice principles is used.
