Statement

Let D\subseteq\mathbb R^2 be open. Let g and h be smooth symmetric covariant 2-tensor fields on D, with g positive definite. Suppose they satisfy the Euclidean Gauss–Codazzi equations. Then every point of D has a connected open neighborhood U and a smooth embedding F:U\to\mathbb R^3 with a smooth unit normal field N such that its first and second fundamental forms are g|_U and h|_U:

\langle dF(u),dF(v)\rangle=g(u,v),\qquad \langle \overline\nabla_u(dF(v)),N\rangle=h(u,v).

Here \overline\nabla denotes ordinary differentiation in Euclidean space, and u,v are tangent vector fields on U. Any two realizations on the same connected neighborhood, with their chosen normal fields, differ by a rigid motion of \mathbb R^3.

For precision, let \nabla be the Levi-Civita connection of g, define the shape operator A by g(Au,v)=h(u,v), and use the curvature convention

R(u,v)w=\nabla_u\nabla_vw-\nabla_v\nabla_uw-\nabla_{[u,v]}w.

The Gauss–Codazzi equations mean, for all smooth tangent fields u,v,w,

R(u,v)w=h(v,w)Au-h(u,w)Av,\qquad (\nabla_u h)(v,w)=(\nabla_v h)(u,w).

A rigid motion is a map x\mapsto Qx+b with Q orthogonal and b\in\mathbb R^3.

Proof availability

The source points to Frobenius's theorem by describing an associated first-order system for the coordinate derivatives of the embedding and its normal. It does not write that system, verify its integrability conditions, or give the existence and uniqueness argument. This is a proof sketch, not a retained full proof. The later global continuation argument assumes the local theorem and does not prove it.
