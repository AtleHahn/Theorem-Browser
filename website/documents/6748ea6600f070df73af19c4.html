Statement

With normalized angular measure d\theta/(2\pi) on S^1, the functions e^{i\theta}\mapsto e^{in\theta}, n\in\mathbb Z, form an orthonormal basis of L^2(S^1).

Level justification

Proof 1: Complex exponentials form a Hilbert basis on the circle — source argument

For the group of complex numbers of magnitude 1, G=S^1, the irreducible representations are one-dimensional and given by

\pi_n(e^{i\theta})=e^{in\theta}.

There is then a single matrix coefficient for each representation, the function

u_n(e^{i\theta})=e^{in\theta}.

The matrix-coefficient orthonormal-basis part of the Peter–Weyl theorem then asserts in this case that these functions form an orthonormal basis for L^2(S^1). Its normalization by the square root of the representation dimension is 1 here.
