Statement

Let m\geq1 be an integer, let A=(a_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq m} be a complex matrix, and let x_1,\ldots,x_m and t_1,\ldots,t_m be commuting formal variables. For every tuple \mathbf k=(k_1,\ldots,k_m) of nonnegative integers, define

G(\mathbf k)=[x_1^{k_1}\cdots x_m^{k_m}]\prod_{i=1}^m\left(\sum_{j=1}^m a_{ij}x_j\right)^{k_i},

where [x_1^{k_1}\cdots x_m^{k_m}] means the coefficient of that monomial. Put T=\operatorname{diag}(t_1,\ldots,t_m). Then

\sum_{k_1,\ldots,k_m\geq0}G(k_1,\ldots,k_m)t_1^{k_1}\cdots t_m^{k_m} =\frac{1}{\det(I_m-TA)}.

The equality is an identity of formal power series in t_1,\ldots,t_m; the determinant has constant term 1, so its reciprocal is well defined as such a series. Here I_m is the m\times m identity matrix.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra and polynomial algebra supply the main objects; treating series coefficientwise and inverting a series with constant term one is a small extension of undergraduate power-series manipulation. No combinatorial species or noncommutative algebra needed.
