Statement

Let \Omega=\prod_{j=1}^n\Omega_j be a product of finite probability spaces. A set I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\} certifies an event A at x\in\Omega if every configuration agreeing with x on I belongs to A. The binary event A\square B consists of configurations with disjoint certificates for A and B. For an integer k\ge2, let D(A_1,\ldots,A_k) consist of configurations having pairwise disjoint certificates for all A_i. Then \Pr(D(A_1,\ldots,A_k))\le\prod_{i=1}^k\Pr(A_i).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Disjoint occurrence via coordinate certificates in product probability spaces requires several interacting undergraduate probability and set-theoretic definitions. A bright US college student should be able to understand the statement after about 30 minutes of focused background research; this rating concerns understanding the claim, not proving it.

Proof 1: Certificate inclusion and repeated binary inequality

Use the binary BKR inequality

\Pr(A\square B)\leq\Pr(A)\Pr(B)

for every pair of events A,B in the given product space.

The k-ary BKR operation D(A_1,\ldots,A_k) is the set of configurations x where there are pairwise disjoint subsets of indices I_i\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\} such that I_i witnesses the membership of x in A_i. This operation satisfies:

D(A_1,\ldots,A_k)\subseteq \left(\cdots\left((A_1\square A_2)\square A_3\right)\square\cdots\right)\square A_k,

whence

\begin{aligned} \Pr(D(A_1,\ldots,A_k)) &\leq\Pr\left(\left(\cdots\left((A_1\square A_2)\square A_3\right)\square\cdots\right)\square A_k\right)\\ &\leq\Pr(A_1)\Pr(A_2)\cdots\Pr(A_k) \end{aligned}

by repeated use of the original BKR inequality.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The certificate definition is explicit in a finite product space. The union of the first k-1 disjoint certificates certifies their iterated binary event, remaining disjoint from the last certificate. Repeated use of the stated two-event bound then proves the result. Finite probability and set reasoning suffice; the new certificate operation is a small, concrete extension, not general measure theory.
