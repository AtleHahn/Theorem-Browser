Statement

Let n,m\ge1 be integers and x_{ia} (1\le i\le n, 1\le a\le m) commuting complex polynomial variables. On \mathbb C[x_{ia}] put E_{ij}=\sum_{a=1}^m x_{ia}\partial/\partial x_{ja}. Write [P,Q]=PQ-QP for the operator commutator and \delta_{ij} for the Kronecker delta. Then [E_{ij},E_{kl}]=\delta_{jk}E_{il}-\delta_{il}E_{kj}, and the linear map e_{ij}\mapsto E_{ij} is a representation of \mathfrak{gl}_n(\mathbb C).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Polynomial partial derivatives and linear operators are undergraduate calculus and linear algebra. The bracket is explicitly the commutator, and representation here means preserving that displayed bracket identity on matrix units. No Lie-group or representation classification is required.

Proof 1: Compare the polarization and matrix-unit commutators — outline

Let n,m\ge1 be integers and x_{ia} (1\le i\le n, 1\le a\le m) commuting complex polynomial variables. On \mathbb C[x_{ia}] put E_{ij}=\sum_{a=1}^m x_{ia}\partial/\partial x_{ja}. Write [P,Q]=PQ-QP for the operator commutator and \delta_{ij} for the Kronecker delta.

One can easily see that such operators satisfy the commutation relations:

{\displaystyle [E_{ij},E_{kl}]=\delta _{jk}E_{il}-\delta _{il}E_{kj}.~~~~~~~~~}

Here {\displaystyle [a,b]} denotes the commutator {\displaystyle ab-ba}. These are the same commutation relations which are satisfied by the matrices {\displaystyle e_{ij}} which have zeros everywhere except the position {\displaystyle (i,j)}, where 1 stands. ({\displaystyle e_{ij}} are sometimes called matrix units). Hence we conclude that the correspondence {\displaystyle \pi :e_{ij}\mapsto E_{ij}} defines a representation of the Lie algebra {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {gl}}_{n}} in the vector space of polynomials of x_{ia}.
