For a finite extension L/K of finite fields, H^2(\operatorname{Gal}(L/K),L^\times)=0, equivalently the relative Brauer group is trivial.

Proof 1: Norm computation and cyclic cohomology

Let |K|=q, and let L/K be a finite extension of degree n, so that |L|=q^n. Then \operatorname{Gal}(L/K) is a cyclic group of order n, and the standard method of computing cohomology of finite cyclic groups shows that

H^2(\operatorname{Gal}(L/K),L^\times)\cong K^\times/N_{L/K}(L^\times),

where the norm map N_{L/K}:L^\times\to K^\times is given by

N_{L/K}(\alpha)=\prod_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)}\sigma(\alpha) =\alpha\cdot\alpha^q\cdot\alpha^{q^2}\cdots\alpha^{q^{n-1}} =\alpha^{\frac{q^n-1}{q-1}}.

Taking \alpha to be a generator of the cyclic group L^\times, we find that N_{L/K}(\alpha) has order q-1, and therefore it must be a generator of K^\times. This implies that N_{L/K} is surjective, and therefore H^2(\operatorname{Gal}(L/K),L^\times) is trivial.
