Statement

Let P be a finite poset and J(P) its lower-set lattice, with meet intersection, join union, bottom \varnothing and top P. Let \mathbf2=\{0<1\} be the two-element bounded lattice. Every bounded lattice homomorphism h:J(P)\to\mathbf2 has the form

h(S)=j_x(S)=\begin{cases}1,&x\in S,\\0,&x\notin S,\end{cases}

for a unique x\in P. A bounded lattice homomorphism preserves binary meets, binary joins, bottom and top.

Level justification

Undergraduate sets and orders supply inclusion, inverse images, intersections and unions. Lower sets and lattice homomorphisms are explicitly defined using these operations, making the finite representation and preservation claims a small extension.

Proof 1: Minimum one-fiber and its principal generator — outline

If x is any element of P, one may define a bounded lattice homomorphism j_x that maps all lower sets containing x to 1 and all other lower sets to 0. And, for any bounded lattice homomorphism from J(P) to \mathbf2, the elements of J(P) that are mapped to 1 must have a unique minimal element I (the meet of all elements mapped to 1), which must be join-irreducible (it cannot be the join of any set of elements mapped to 0).

Here we use the finite lower-set fact implicit in the source: a nonempty join-irreducible lower set is a principal lower set \mathord\downarrow x=\{y:y\le x\} for a unique x\in P. Thus every bounded lattice homomorphism has the form j_x for some x.

Level justification

Intersect the finite one-fiber, identify its principal lower-set generator, and recover membership evaluation. Undergraduate discrete mathematics and elementary order theory suffice; the lattice homomorphism and irreducibility conditions are concrete finite set operations.
