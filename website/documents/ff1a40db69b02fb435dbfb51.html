Statement

Let \Omega\subset\mathbb C be a bounded Jordan domain and let A^2(\Omega) be the holomorphic functions f with \int_\Omega|f|^2\,dA<\infty, where dA denotes planar area measure. Equip A^2(\Omega) with \langle f,g\rangle=\int_\Omega f\overline g\,dA. Gram–Schmidt applied to 1,z,z^2,\ldots yields an orthonormal basis of A^2(\Omega) consisting of polynomials.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement asks for a complete infinite-dimensional holomorphic orthonormal basis, beyond finite-dimensional Gram–Schmidt.

Proof 1: An orthonormal polynomial basis for a bounded Jordan domain — outline

By the Farrell–Markushevich theorem, complex polynomials form a dense subspace of the Bergman space of a domain bounded by a simple closed Jordan curve. The Gram–Schmidt process can be used to construct an orthonormal basis in the Bergman space.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The Gram–Schmidt step is familiar linear algebra, but the complete argument concerns holomorphic functions with an area-L2 norm, polynomial density in a Bergman Hilbert space and an infinite orthonormal basis. Understanding the analytic function space and its approximation theorem requires cumulative complex and functional analysis beyond the short undergraduate allowance.

Gram–Schmidt on a linearly independent sequence with dense span gives a Hilbert-space orthonormal basis
Kind:

Theorem

Treatment:

Cited without proof

Statement used:

Applying Gram–Schmidt to a linearly independent sequence with dense span in a Hilbert space gives an orthonormal sequence whose closed span is the whole space.

Application in proof:

Turn the monomials into a complete orthonormal sequence of polynomials.

Application level:

Undergraduate level

Level justification:

Finite-step Gram–Schmidt is undergraduate linear algebra; completeness here adds only norm-closed span rather than algebraic spanning by finite sums.

Review notes:

A nonzero polynomial cannot vanish on the open domain, so the monomials are linearly independent as functions. Each Gram–Schmidt step is a finite linear combination and remains a polynomial.
