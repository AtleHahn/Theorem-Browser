Statement

Let R be a commutative unital ring, let V be an R-module generated by e_1,\ldots,e_n with n\ge1, and let \varphi\in\operatorname{End}_R(V). Suppose A=(a_{ij})\in M_n(R) satisfies \varphi(e_j)=\sum_i a_{ij}e_i. For p(t)=\det(tI_n-A), one has p(\varphi)=0 in \operatorname{End}_R(V).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Rings and vector-space linear algebra supply scalar operations, finite generators, determinants and polynomial evaluation on operators. A module replaces field scalars by ring scalars without assuming a basis; an endomorphism preserves these operations. This closely related extension and the distinction between generators and a basis fit about 30 minutes. No module classification is needed to understand the full formulation.

Proof 1: Cayley–Hamilton for a finitely generated module

Local adjugate lemma

We use the notion of the adjugate matrix adj(M) of an {\displaystyle n\times n} matrix M, the transpose of its cofactor matrix. This is a matrix whose coefficients are given by polynomial expressions in the coefficients of M (in fact, by certain {\displaystyle (n-1)\times (n-1)} determinants), in such a way that the following fundamental relations hold,

{\displaystyle \operatorname {adj} (M)\cdot M=\det(M)I_{n}=M\cdot \operatorname {adj} (M)~.}

These relations are a direct consequence of the basic properties of determinants: evaluation of the (i, j) entry of the matrix product on the left gives the expansion by column i of the determinant of the matrix obtained from M by replacing column i by a copy of column j, which is det(M) if i = j and zero otherwise; the matrix product on the right is similar, but for expansions by rows.

Being a consequence of just algebraic expression manipulation, these relations are valid for matrices with entries in any commutative ring (commutativity must be assumed for determinants to be defined in the first place). This is important to note here, because these relations will be applied below for matrices with non-numeric entries such as polynomials.

Apply to the generator column

Interpret each entry a_{ij} as the endomorphism of scalar multiplication by a_{ij}. The entries of \varphi I_n-A all lie in the commutative subring R[\varphi]\subseteq\operatorname{End}_R(V). Thus its determinant is defined and equals p(\varphi).

The given equations for φ on the generators e1, ..., en are

{\displaystyle \varphi (e_{i})=\sum _{j=1}^{n}A_{j,i}e_{j}\quad {\text{for }}i=1,\ldots ,n.}

One can interpret these as n components of one equation in Vn, whose members can be written using the matrix-vector product M(n, End(V)) × Vn → Vn that is defined as usual, but with individual entries ψ ∈ End(V) and v in V being "multiplied" by forming {\displaystyle \psi (v)}; this gives:

{\displaystyle \varphi I_{n}\cdot E=A^{\operatorname {tr} }\cdot E,}

where {\displaystyle E\in V^{n}} is the element whose component i is ei (in other words it is the generators e1, ..., en of V written as a column of vectors). Writing this equation as

{\displaystyle (\varphi I_{n}-A^{\operatorname {tr} })\cdot E=0\in V^{n}}

one recognizes the transpose of the matrix {\displaystyle \varphi I_{n}-A} considered above, and its determinant (computed over R[φ]) is also p(φ). To derive from this equation that p(φ) = 0 ∈ End(V), one left-multiplies by the adjugate matrix of {\displaystyle \varphi I_{n}-A^{\operatorname {tr} }}, which is defined in the matrix ring M(n, R[φ]), giving

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}0&=\operatorname {adj} (\varphi I_{n}-A^{\operatorname {tr} })\cdot \left((\varphi I_{n}-A^{\operatorname {tr} })\cdot E\right)\\[1ex]&=\left(\operatorname {adj} (\varphi I_{n}-A^{\operatorname {tr} })\cdot (\varphi I_{n}-A^{\operatorname {tr} })\right)\cdot E\\[1ex]&=\left(\det(\varphi I_{n}-A^{\operatorname {tr} })I_{n}\right)\cdot E\\[1ex]&=(p(\varphi )I_{n})\cdot E;\end{aligned}}}

the associativity of matrix-matrix and matrix-vector multiplication used in the first step is a purely formal property of those operations, independent of the nature of the entries. Now component i of this equation says that p(φ)(ei) = 0 ∈ V; thus p(φ) vanishes on all ei, and since these elements generate V it follows that p(φ) = 0 ∈ End(V), completing the proof.
