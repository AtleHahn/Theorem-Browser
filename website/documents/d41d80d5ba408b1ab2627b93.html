Statement

For an integer d\ge1 and 1\le p,r,s\le\infty satisfying 1/r+1/p=1+1/s, functions f\in L^r(\mathbb R^d) and g\in L^p(\mathbb R^d) satisfy \|f*g\|_s\le\|f\|_r\|g\|_p, where f*g(x)=\int f(y)g(x-y)\,dy almost everywhere.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The inequality compares norms of a convolution for general Lebesgue functions.

Proof 1: Interpolate twice, fixing each convolution factor in turn — outline

Use Riesz–Thorin interpolation: a complex-linear operator with endpoint bounds L^{p_j}\to L^{q_j} of norms M_j has intermediate bound M_0^{1-\theta}M_1^\theta at reciprocal exponents interpolated with weights 1-\theta,\theta.

Let  f  be a fixed integrable function and let T be the operator of convolution with  f , i.e., for each function g we have Tg =  f  ∗ g.

It follows from Fubini's theorem that T is bounded from L1 to L1 and it is trivial that it is bounded from L∞ to L∞ (both bounds are by || f ||1). Therefore the Riesz–Thorin theorem gives

{\displaystyle \|f*g\|_{p}\leq \|f\|_{1}\|g\|_{p}.}

We take this inequality and switch the role of the operator and the operand, or in other words, we think of S as the operator of convolution with g, and get that S is bounded from L1 to Lp. Further, since g is in Lp we get, in view of Hölder's inequality, that S is bounded from Lq to L∞, where again ⁠1/p⁠ + ⁠1/q⁠ = 1. So interpolating we get

{\displaystyle \|f*g\|_{s}\leq \|f\|_{r}\|g\|_{p}}

where the connection between p, r and s is

{\displaystyle {\frac {1}{r}}+{\frac {1}{p}}=1+{\frac {1}{s}}.}

At degenerate endpoint equalities, use the corresponding direct endpoint estimates.
