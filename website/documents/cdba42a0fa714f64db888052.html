Statement

Let G be a finite group whose Sylow 2-subgroups are generalized quaternion groups. Assume that G has no nontrivial normal subgroup of odd order. Then its center

Z(G)=\{z\in G:zg=gz\text{ for every }g\in G\}

has order 2.

Here a generalized quaternion 2-group has, for some integer m\geq2, the presentation

\langle x,y\mid x^{2^m}=y^4=1,\ x^{2^{m-1}}=y^2,\ y^{-1}xy=x^{-1}\rangle.

A Sylow 2-subgroup has order equal to the largest power of 2 dividing |G|.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory group theory supplies normality, center and Sylow subgroups. The single explicit two-generator quaternion presentation is a small extension of familiar finite-group examples; no character or block theory is needed for the statement.

Proof 1: Quaternion involution and the Z-star theorem (external)

A supporting source can be found here (Section8.1, Theorem8.1.1, p.139; given below.). The deduction below supplies the argument for the stored statement.

The following deduction from the cited Z-star theorem is supplied here: a generalized quaternion Sylow2 subgroup P has exactly one involution z and center <z> of order2. If z commuted with a distinct G-conjugate zprime, they would generate a four-group, which would lie in some Sylow2 subgroup, impossible for a generalized quaternion group. Glauberman Z-star therefore places z in the inverse image of Z(G/O_2prime(G)). The hypothesis makes O_2prime(G)=1, hence z is central. The odd-order part of Z(G) is a normal odd-order subgroup, so is trivial. Every central2-element lies in P and then in Z(P)=<z>. Consequently Z(G)=<z> has order2.
