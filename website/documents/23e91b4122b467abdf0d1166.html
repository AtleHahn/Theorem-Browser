Statement

Let m\geq2, let \lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_m be distinct real numbers, and let u_1,\ldots,u_m\in\mathbb C. Define D(x)=\min_{k\in\mathbb Z}|x-k|, the distance to the nearest integer. Suppose the set

E=\{D(\lambda_r-\lambda_s):1\leq r,s\leq m,\ D(\lambda_r-\lambda_s)>0\}

is nonempty, and put \tau=\min E. Then

\left|\sum_{\substack{1\leq r,s\leq m\\r\ne s}}\frac{u_r\overline{u_s}}{\lambda_r-\lambda_s}\right|\leq\frac{\pi}{\tau}\sum_{r=1}^m|u_r|^2.

The bar denotes complex conjugation and |u| complex modulus. The sum is over ordered pairs. The spacing used here is distance modulo integers, as defined above, even though the denominator is the actual real difference.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Explicit finite sums and elementary real/complex arithmetic suffice; the unusual spacing is defined by rounding to integers and requires no quotient-space theory.
