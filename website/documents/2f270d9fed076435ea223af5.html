Statement

For nonnegative real x,y, (x+y)/2\ge\sqrt{xy}, with equality exactly when x=y.

Proof 1: Nonnegativity of a square

This follows from the fact that the square of a real number is always non-negative (greater than or equal to zero) and from the identity (a ± b)2 = a2 ± 2ab + b2:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}0&\leq (x-y)^{2}\\&=x^{2}-2xy+y^{2}\\&=x^{2}+2xy+y^{2}-4xy\\&=(x+y)^{2}-4xy.\end{aligned}}}

Hence (x + y)2 ≥ 4xy, with equality when (x − y)2 = 0, i.e. x = y. The AM–GM inequality then follows from taking the nonnegative square root of both sides and then dividing both sides by 2.

Proof 2: Specialize the symmetric averages — outline

Use the stated Maclaurin theorem: for nonnegative a_1,\ldots,a_n, put S_k=\binom nk^{-1}\sum_{i_1<\cdots<i_k}a_{i_1}\cdots a_{i_k}. Then S_1\ge S_2^{1/2}\ge\cdots\ge S_n^{1/n}, with equality throughout exactly when all a_i are equal.

For n=2, Maclaurin's inequality gives the arithmetic mean-geometric mean inequality for two non-negative numbers.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The imported theorem is explicitly a finite inequality between symmetric averages. At n=2 those averages are simply (x+y)/2 and xy, so its first comparison and equality condition give the claim directly. Only finite sums, products, combinations and square roots are needed to apply it; its proof is not part of this argument.
