Statement

Let q=p^a, where p is prime and a\geq1, and let \mathbb F_q be the field with q elements. There is a bijection

\{\mathbb F_q\text{-isogeny classes of simple abelian varieties over }\mathbb F_q\} \longleftrightarrow \{\text{conjugacy classes over }\mathbb Q\text{ of }q\text{-Weil numbers}\}.

It sends a simple abelian variety to the conjugacy class of an eigenvalue of its q-power Frobenius endomorphism on a rational Tate module.

A q-Weil number is an algebraic integer \alpha such that |\sigma(\alpha)|=\sqrt q for every field embedding \sigma:\mathbb Q(\alpha)\hookrightarrow\mathbb C. Algebraic integer means a root of a monic polynomial with integer coefficients. Two such numbers are conjugate over \mathbb Q if they have the same minimal polynomial over \mathbb Q; individual conjugate roots are not distinguished in the target.

An abelian variety is a smooth projective geometrically connected algebraic group. Simple means positive-dimensional with no nonzero proper abelian subvariety defined over \mathbb F_q. Isogeny here means a surjective homomorphism of algebraic groups with finite kernel, defined over \mathbb F_q; simplicity and isogeny are relative to this field, not its algebraic closure.

To specify the map, fix a prime \ell\ne p. Set

T_\ell A=\varprojlim_n A[\ell^n](\overline{\mathbb F}_q),\qquad V_\ell A=T_\ell A\otimes_{\mathbb Z_\ell}\mathbb Q_\ell.

Here A[\ell^n] denotes points killed by multiplication by \ell^n, and the inverse limit consists of sequences (x_n) with \ell x_{n+1}=x_n. The rings \mathbb Z_\ell=\varprojlim_n\mathbb Z/\ell^n\mathbb Z and their fraction fields \mathbb Q_\ell are the \ell-adic integers and numbers. Frobenius raises coordinates to their qth powers and acts on V_\ell A. Its characteristic polynomial has integer coefficients, so its roots may be regarded as algebraic numbers in \mathbb C. For a simple A, these roots belong to the single conjugacy class used above; the class is independent of \ell.
