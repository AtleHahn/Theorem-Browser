Statement

Let N,x_1,y_1,k_1,x_2,y_2,k_2 be integers with x_i^2-Ny_i^2=k_i for i=1,2. Both pairs (X,Y)=(x_1x_2+Ny_1y_2,x_1y_2+x_2y_1) and (X,Y)=(x_1x_2-Ny_1y_2,x_1y_2-x_2y_1) satisfy X^2-NY^2=k_1k_2.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The hypotheses and composition formula use elementary integer algebra.

Proof 1: Compose the integer equations using the expanded identity outline

The identity

{\displaystyle (x_{1}^{2}-Ny_{1}^{2})(x_{2}^{2}-Ny_{2}^{2})=(x_{1}x_{2}+Ny_{1}y_{2})^{2}-N(x_{1}y_{2}+x_{2}y_{1})^{2}}

is verified by expansion. Using this, we can "compose" triples (x_1,y_1,k_1) and (x_2,y_2,k_2) that are solutions of x^2-Ny^2=k, to generate the new triples

(x_1x_2+Ny_1y_2,\ x_1y_2+x_2y_1,\ k_1k_2)

and

(x_1x_2-Ny_1y_2,\ x_1y_2-x_2y_1,\ k_1k_2).

For the second triple, replace y_1 by -y_1 in the identity; its square and the input equation are unchanged.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Expand the squares and cancel the mixed terms; then insert the two given quadratic equations. Changing the sign of y1 gives the second pair. Only integer polynomial algebra is needed.
