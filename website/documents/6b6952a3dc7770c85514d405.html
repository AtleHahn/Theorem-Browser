A sphere and a plane are not locally isometric

Curvature makes even a small spherical patch intrinsically different from a planar region: distances cannot all be preserved by flattening it. This gives operational meaning to curved geometry and separates intrinsic spatial measurements from a mere choice of visual representation.

Statement

No nonempty open patch of a sphere of radius R>0 is locally isometric to a plane.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Local isometry between open surface patches is a differential-geometric object not defined in this statement.

Mathematical Field

Proof 1: Compare the two Gaussian curvatures

A sphere of radius R has constant Gaussian curvature 1/R^2>0, whereas a plane has curvature 0. The external Theorema Egregium says a local isometry must preserve Gaussian curvature. Hence such an isometry cannot exist.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Compare Gaussian curvatures using their invariance under local isometry. Gaussian curvature, regular-surface metrics and local isometry together are not confidently within a small extension of the baseline; the numerical contradiction alone does not establish accessibility.
