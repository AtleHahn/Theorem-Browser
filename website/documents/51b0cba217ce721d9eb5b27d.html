Statement

Let (f_n) be a uniformly bounded sequence of real-valued differentiable functions on [a,b] whose derivatives are uniformly bounded in absolute value. Then a subsequence converges uniformly on [a,b].

Level justification

These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Mean value theorem gives uniform equicontinuity

Dependency

For real-valued continuous functions on a compact interval, uniform boundedness and uniform equicontinuity imply that every sequence has a uniformly convergent subsequence.

Derivative bound

The hypotheses of the stated Arzelà–Ascoli theorem are satisfied by a uniformly bounded sequence {fn} of differentiable functions with uniformly bounded derivatives. Indeed, uniform boundedness of the derivatives implies by the mean value theorem that for all x and y,

{\displaystyle \left|f_{n}(x)-f_{n}(y)\right|\leq K|x-y|,}

where K>0 is a uniform bound for the absolute values of the derivatives. So, given ε > 0, let \delta=\varepsilon/(2K) to verify the definition of equicontinuity of the sequence. Thus the stated Arzelà–Ascoli theorem applies.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The mean value theorem gives a common Lipschitz bound, hence the same delta works for every function in the sequence. This verifies the explicitly stated equicontinuity hypothesis of Arzela–Ascoli on a compact interval. Uniform convergence, boundedness and the shared continuity modulus are standard undergraduate analysis; no general function-space compactness theorem needs to be learned or proved.
