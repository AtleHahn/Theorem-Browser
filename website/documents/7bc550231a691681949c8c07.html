Statement

Let X be a real random variable on a probability space with finite second moment, mean \mu=\mathbb E X, and variance \sigma^2=\mathbb E[(X-\mu)^2]. Let \lambda>0. Then \Pr(|X-\mu|\ge\lambda)\le\frac{2\sigma^2}{\sigma^2+\lambda^2}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The mean and variance of one real random quantity are explicitly defined as expectations, interpreted as probability-weighted integrals from undergraduate analysis. The event is an absolute-value inequality. These related additions fit about 30 minutes and do not require independence, probability convergence or construction of the underlying measure.

Proof 1: Add the two disjoint tails

Use Cantelli’s upper-tail inequality: for any real random variable Y of finite variance and \lambda>0, \Pr(Y-\mathbb EY\ge\lambda)\le\frac{\operatorname{Var}(Y)}{\operatorname{Var}(Y)+\lambda^2}. Applied to X and -X, this bounds both \Pr(X-\mu\ge\lambda) and \Pr(X-\mu\le-\lambda) by \sigma^2/(\sigma^2+\lambda^2). Since \lambda>0, these events are disjoint and their union is \{|X-\mu|\ge\lambda\}. Thus \Pr(|X-\mu|\ge\lambda)=\Pr(X-\mu\ge\lambda)+\Pr(X-\mu\le-\lambda)\le\frac{2\sigma^2}{\sigma^2+\lambda^2}.
