Statement

The condition that a projective-plane order q\equiv1 or 2\pmod4 be a sum of two integer squares is not sufficient for existence: order 10 satisfies the condition but has no finite projective plane.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A finite projective plane is a finite point-line incidence structure with the usual unique-line and unique-intersection axioms; its order counts points on a line minus one. This concrete combinatorial definition is a small addition to elementary sets and arithmetic, sufficient for the nonexistence claim at order ten.

Proof 1: Use the external order-ten nonexistence theorem outline

The Bruck–Ryser condition allows the existence of a plane of order 10. Since a projective plane of order 10 has been shown not to exist using a combination of coding theory and large-scale computer search, the condition is evidently not sufficient for the existence of a projective plane.

Level justification

Check 10=1^2+3^2 and use the imported nonexistence result. A finite projective plane is a finite point-line incidence structure with unique joins and intersections and the usual nondegeneracy condition; its order counts points per line minus one. These combinatorial definitions are a small extension of undergraduate discrete mathematics. The coding-theoretic search proving nonexistence is not part of this argument.
