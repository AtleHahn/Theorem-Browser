Statement

Let K be algebraically closed, and let f_1,\ldots,f_n\in K[x_0,\ldots,x_n] be homogeneous of positive degrees d_1,\ldots,d_n, with finitely many common points in \mathbb P^n(K). Define their U-resultant by

R(U_0,\ldots,U_n)=\operatorname{Res}(f_1,\ldots,f_n,U_0x_0+\cdots+U_nx_n).

For a common point [\alpha_0:\cdots:\alpha_n], define its multiplicity to be the exponent of the factor \alpha_0U_0+\cdots+\alpha_nU_n in R. The sum of these multiplicities is d_1\cdots d_n.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves algebraically closed fields; projective hypersurfaces; multivariate resultant; linear-factor multiplicities. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Proof 1: Bézout count from the U-resultant

Macaulay's resultant is a polynomial function of the coefficients of n homogeneous polynomials in n indeterminates that is zero if and only if the polynomials have a nontrivial (that is some component is nonzero) common zero in an algebraically closed field containing the coefficients.

The U-resultant is a particular instance of Macaulay's resultant, introduced also by Macaulay. Given n homogeneous polynomials {\displaystyle f_{1},\ldots ,f_{n}} in n + 1 indeterminates {\displaystyle x_{0},\ldots ,x_{n},} the U-resultant is the resultant of {\displaystyle f_{1},\ldots ,f_{n},} and {\displaystyle U_{0}x_{0}+\cdots +U_{n}x_{n},} where the coefficients {\displaystyle U_{0},\ldots ,U_{n}} are auxiliary indeterminates. For the finite intersection in the statement, the U-resultant is a nonzero homogeneous polynomial in {\displaystyle U_{0},\ldots ,U_{n},} whose degree is the product of the degrees of the {\displaystyle f_{i}.}

The U-resultant can be factorized into linear (in the {\displaystyle U_{i}}) polynomials over an algebraically closed field containing the coefficients of the {\displaystyle f_{i}.} These linear factors correspond to the common zeros of the {\displaystyle f_{i}} in the following way: to each common zero {\displaystyle (\alpha _{0},\ldots ,\alpha _{n})} corresponds a linear factor {\displaystyle (\alpha _{0}U_{0}+\cdots +\alpha _{n}U_{n}),} and conversely.

This proves Bézout's theorem, if the multiplicity of a common zero is defined as the multiplicity of the corresponding linear factor of the U-resultant.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof applies a multivariate elimination theorem about the U-resultant of a finite projective intersection, including nonvanishing, homogeneous degree and a linear-factor correspondence. Understanding the general projective hypersurface and resultant construction is substantial algebraic geometry beyond the short undergraduate extension. Defining multiplicity by factor exponents simplifies the last step but does not make the underlying elimination theorem elementary.

Statement used:

For homogeneous f1,...,fn in n+1 variables with finite projective common-zero set, the resultant with the auxiliary linear form sum U_i x_i is nonzero homogeneous in U of degree product d_i. Its linear factors correspond exactly to the projective common points, with factors sum alpha_i U_i.

Application in proof:

Sum the exponents of these linear factors to obtain the total degree product d_i.

Application level:

Beyond undergraduate level

Level justification:

The application requires multivariate resultant elimination and its projective factorization theorem.

Review notes:

This is a special factorization property of the U-resultant, not the false assertion that arbitrary multivariable polynomials over an algebraically closed field factor into linear forms.
