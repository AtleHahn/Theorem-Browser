Statement

Let (X,Y) and (W,Z) be pairs of real random variables, each pair defined on one probability space, with possibly different spaces for the two pairs. Their Borel probability laws on \mathbb R^2 are equal if and only if \mathbb P(X\le a,Y\le b)=\widetilde{\mathbb P}(W\le a,Z\le b) for every a,b\in\mathbb R.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement concerns joint Borel probability measures rather than only real two-variable functions.

Proof 1: Use intersections of the coordinate half-line events — outline

For each a,b, the joint cumulative probability is \mathbb P[X^{-1}(( -\infty,a])\cap Y^{-1}(( -\infty,b])]. The family of intersections of these coordinate half-line inverse images is a pi-system generated by the random pair. The pi-lambda theorem therefore shows that the joint cumulative distribution function determines the joint law. Conversely equality of joint laws gives equality of these probabilities.
