The relation between the sides of a right triangle changes with curvature, replacing the Euclidean sum of squares by a hyperbolic identity. This supplies a precise alternative to treating familiar geometry as an unconditional truth about space; empirical application requires a choice of geometric model.

Statement

In the hyperbolic plane of constant curvature -1/R^2, R>0, a right triangle with legs a,b and opposite side c satisfies \cosh(c/R)=\cosh(a/R)\cosh(b/R).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Hyperbolic triangles and their cosine law require geometry outside the baseline.

Proof 1: Set the included angle to a right angle in the hyperbolic cosine law

The hyperbolic cosine law gives \cosh(c/R)=\cosh(a/R)\cosh(b/R)-\sinh(a/R)\sinh(b/R)\cos\gamma. For a right angle \gamma=\pi/2 the last term vanishes.

Level justification
