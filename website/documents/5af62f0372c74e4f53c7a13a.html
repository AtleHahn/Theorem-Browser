Statement

Put \phi=(1+\sqrt5)/2. Say real numbers x,y are equivalent if y=(ax+b)/(cx+d) for integers a,b,c,d with ad-bc=\pm1 and cx+d\ne0. If x is equivalent to \phi and C>\sqrt5, only finitely many distinct rational numbers p/q, with p\in\mathbb Z, q\in\mathbb Z_{>0}, satisfy |x-p/q|<1/(Cq^2). Thus a universal improvement of the Hurwitz constant beyond \sqrt5 must exclude the entire equivalence class of \phi.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The equivalence relation is given explicitly by integer coefficients and a real rational expression; the conclusion compares rational errors with a square-root constant and quantifies over finite or infinite numerical lists, without an undefined abstract object.

Proof 1: Transfer the golden-ratio obstruction

For every C>\sqrt5, Hurwitz’s golden-ratio sharpness statement gives only finitely many approximants with error less than 1/(Cq^2). Equivalent real numbers are approximable to the same degree: their Markov approximation threshold is the same. Hence the same obstruction applies to every number equivalent to \phi, so those numbers must be excluded before improving the universal constant.

Level justification

Transfer the supplied rational-approximation threshold through the explicitly defined integer fractional-linear equivalence. Undergraduate real sequences and inequalities suffice; the threshold adds only the best constant for infinitely many displayed rational approximations.
