Statement

Let n\ge1 be an integer and A\in\mathbb R^{n\times n} satisfy A^T=A. There are a real orthogonal matrix Q, meaning Q^TQ=I, and a real diagonal matrix \Lambda such that A=Q\Lambda Q^T.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Finite real symmetric matrices; Orthogonal matrices; Diagonalization. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Assemble the imported real orthonormal eigenbasis

A real symmetric matrix has real eigenvalues and its eigenvectors can be chosen real and orthonormal. Take these vectors as the columns of Q and their eigenvalues as the diagonal entries of \Lambda. The eigenvector equations give AQ=Q\Lambda; since Q^{-1}=Q^T, the decomposition is A=Q\Lambda Q^T.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Assemble the supplied orthonormal eigenbasis into Q and multiply the eigenvector equations columnwise. Standard undergraduate matrix spectral theory suffices.
