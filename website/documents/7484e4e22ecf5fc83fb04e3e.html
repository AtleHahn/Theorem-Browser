Statement

On the real Hilbert space X=L^2(0,1), define Au=u' with domain D(A)=\{u\in H^1(0,1):u(1)=0\}, where endpoint values are Sobolev traces. Then A generates a strongly continuous contraction semigroup: bounded operators T(t), t\ge0, with T(0)=I, T(t+s)=T(t)T(s), \|T(t)\|\le1, and T(t)x\to x as t\downarrow0, whose generator is A.

Level justification

These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Proof 1: Dissipativity and the integral resolvent formula

Use the Lumer–Phillips theorem: a densely defined dissipative operator on a Hilbert space generates a strongly continuous contraction semigroup if A-\lambda I is onto for some \lambda>0.

Consider X = L2([0, 1]; R) with its usual inner product, and let Au = u′ with domain D(A) equal to those functions u in the Sobolev space H1([0, 1]; R) with u(1) = 0. D(A) is dense. Moreover, for every u in D(A),

{\displaystyle \langle u,Au\rangle =\int _{0}^{1}u(x)u'(x)\,\mathrm {d} x=-{\frac {1}{2}}u(0)^{2}\leq 0,}

so that A is dissipative. The ordinary differential equation u' − λu = f, u(1) = 0 has a unique solution u in H1([0, 1]; R) for any f in L2([0, 1]; R), namely

{\displaystyle u(x)={\rm {e}}^{\lambda x}\int _{1}^{x}{\rm {e}}^{-\lambda t}f(t)\,dt}

so that the surjectivity condition is satisfied. Hence, by the Lumer–Phillips theorem A generates a contraction semigroup.
