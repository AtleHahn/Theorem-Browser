Statement

For a field K, write \mathcal P(K)=\bigcup_{n\ge1}K((T^{1/n})), where K((T^{1/n})) consists of formal series \sum_{k\ge k_0}c_kT^{k/n} with k_0\in\mathbb Z and c_k\in K. Then \mathcal P(K) is a field under formal series addition and multiplication.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The formal Laurent series are defined explicitly by bounded-below exponent sequences. Their coefficientwise arithmetic extends undergraduate polynomials/fields, with common denominators handling the rational exponents.

Proof 1: Common Laurent-series field

Two series with denominators n,m belong to the common Laurent-series field K((T^{1/N})), where N is a common multiple of n,m. Their sum and product can be computed there. The inverse of any nonzero series belongs to the same Laurent-series field as that series. Thus the union is closed under the field operations and inherits their laws.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Put each pair of series in a common denominator stage, invoke the stated Laurent-series field theorem and inherit the field laws. Undergraduate fields and formal coefficientwise polynomial arithmetic suffice; formal Laurent series and common-denominator inclusions are the small extension used, with no valuation topology or direct-limit machinery.
