Statement

Let \mu be a probability measure on \mathbb R and let f,g:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R be increasing measurable functions such that f,g,fg are integrable. Then \int fg\,d\mu\ge(\int f\,d\mu)(\int g\,d\mu).

Proof 1: Integrate the product of two same-sign differences — outline

For every x,y, the product [f(x)-f(y)][g(x)-g(y)] is nonnegative. Therefore \int\!\int[f(x)-f(y)][g(x)-g(y)]\,d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y)\ge0. Expanding this double integral gives 2\bigl(\int fg\,d\mu-(\int f\,d\mu)(\int g\,d\mu)\bigr), proving the inequality.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Expand a nonnegative double integral and use Fubini. The assertion involves arbitrary probability measures and Lebesgue integrability, requiring measure and product integration theory.
