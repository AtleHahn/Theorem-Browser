Statement

Let G be a nontrivial linearly ordered abelian group, with order invariant under addition. Write |g| for the positive one of g,-g when g\ne0. Suppose that for every pair of nonzero elements g,h there are positive integers N,M with N|g|>|h| and M|h|>|g|. Then G admits an injective order-preserving homomorphism into the additive ordered group of real numbers.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Ordered groups combine familiar abelian groups with an order preserved by addition. The Archimedean comparison is fully specified, and the conclusion is an injective order-preserving homomorphism to the reals. These use elementary algebra and real order with one direct extension.

Proof 1: Identify the singleton-index Hahn group with the real line

Hahn's embedding theorem states that every linearly ordered abelian group G can be embedded as an ordered subgroup of the additive group \mathbb R^\Omega endowed with a lexicographical order, where \mathbb R is the additive group of real numbers (with its standard order), \Omega is the set of Archimedean equivalence classes of G, and \mathbb R^\Omega is the set of all functions from \Omega to \mathbb R which vanish outside a well-ordered set.

Two nonzero elements g and h of G are Archimedean equivalent if there exist natural numbers N and M such that N|g|>|h| and M|h|>|g|. The group G is Archimedean if all nonzero elements are Archimedean-equivalent. In this case, since G is nontrivial, \Omega is a singleton, so \mathbb R^\Omega is just the group of real numbers.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

From introductory group theory and orders, add an addition-invariant total order and the displayed equivalence relation comparing positive integer multiples. The hypothesis makes the set of nonzero equivalence classes a singleton. In the supplied Hahn theorem, functions on that singleton are just real numbers, every support is automatically well ordered, and the lexicographic order is ordinary order. This small specialization needs no theory of general Hahn groups or a proof of Hahn’s theorem.
