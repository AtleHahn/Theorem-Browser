Statement

For every finite nonempty alphabet A and integer r\ge1, there is n\ge1 such that every r-coloring of the words A^n has a monochromatic combinatorial line. Such a line is obtained by fixing letters in some positions, choosing a nonempty set of remaining positions, and putting the same freely varying letter a\in A in every remaining position.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All finite combinatorial objects are explicitly defined; the conclusion needs no abstract Ramsey terminology.

Proof 1: Set the subcube dimensions to zero and one

In the Graham–Rothschild theorem take m=1, k=0 and the trivial action on the alphabet. The zero-dimensional cubes are individual words, and a one-dimensional cube is exactly a line with fixed coordinates and one common variable letter. The theorem therefore gives a line all of whose words have the same color.
