Statement

The function f(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-2}\sin((2^{n^3}+1)x/2) on [0,\pi], extended evenly to [-\pi,\pi] and then 2\pi-periodically, is continuous on \mathbb R.

Proof 1: Use the explicit summable sine-series recipe — outline

Take the even and 2\pi-periodic function f defined for all x in [0,\pi] by

f(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1{n^2} \sin\left[\left(2^{n^3}+1\right)\frac x2\right].

The source states that this function is continuous; it does not spell out the uniform-convergence or endpoint checks.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The terms are uniformly bounded by 1/n squared, so the series is a uniform limit of continuous functions. Even extension matches at zero and gives identical values at plus and minus pi, making periodic extension continuous. This is standard real analysis.
