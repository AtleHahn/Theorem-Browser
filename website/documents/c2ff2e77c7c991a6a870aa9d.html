Statement

For v\in H^2(\mathbb R^2) and every R>0, there is an absolute constant C such that

\|\widehat v\|_{L^1(\mathbb R^2)}\le C\|v\|_{H^1}\sqrt{\log(1+R)}+C\frac{\|v\|_{H^2}}{1+R}.

The Fourier transform is taken in any fixed standard normalization. Here H^k denotes the real- or complex-valued Sobolev space with square-integrable weak derivatives through order k, with its usual norm.

Proof 1: Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz at a frequency radius

By the Fourier characterization of the Sobolev norms, there is an absolute constant C>0 such that

\|(1+|\xi|)\widehat v\|_{L^2(\mathbb R^2)}\le C\|v\|_{H^1}, \qquad \|(1+|\xi|^2)\widehat v\|_{L^2(\mathbb R^2)}\le C\|v\|_{H^2}.

For any R>0, one writes:

\begin{aligned} \|\widehat v\|_{L^1(\mathbb R^2)} &=\int_{|\xi|<R}|\widehat v(\xi)|\,d\xi +\int_{|\xi|>R}|\widehat v(\xi)|\,d\xi\\ &=\int_{|\xi|<R}(1+|\xi|)|\widehat v(\xi)|\frac1{1+|\xi|}\,d\xi +\int_{|\xi|>R}(1+|\xi|^2)|\widehat v(\xi)|\frac1{1+|\xi|^2}\,d\xi\\ &\le C\|v\|_{H^1}\left(\int_{|\xi|<R}\frac1{(1+|\xi|)^2}\,d\xi\right)^{1/2} +C\|v\|_{H^2}\left(\int_{|\xi|>R}\frac1{(1+|\xi|^2)^2}\,d\xi\right)^{1/2}, \end{aligned}

owing to the preceding inequalities and to the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality. This yields

\|\widehat v\|_{L^1(\mathbb R^2)} \le C\|v\|_{H^1}(\log(1+R))^{1/2} +C\frac{\|v\|_{H^2}}{1+R}.
