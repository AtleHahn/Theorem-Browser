Statement

Every finite simple planar graph is the intersection graph of straight line segments in the Euclidean plane. Explicitly, if G=(V,E) is a finite graph with no loops or multiple edges that can be drawn in the plane without edges crossing except at common endpoints, then there is a line segment s_v\subset\mathbb R^2 for each vertex v\in V such that, for all distinct u,v\in V,

\{u,v\}\in E\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad s_u\cap s_v\ne\varnothing.

Level justification

The construction uses finite graph induction, planar separation, affine segment placement and continuous perturbations that preserve finitely many incidence conditions. Its auxiliary premodels record finite constraints, and an acyclic order controls how points move. Undergraduate analysis and linear algebra suffice; the additional graph and geometric definitions are local and introduce no further prerequisite theory.
