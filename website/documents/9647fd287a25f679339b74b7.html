Statement

For nonnegative random variables X_1,\ldots,X_n on a common probability space and s>0, define N[n,s]=\max\{|I|:I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\},\ \sum_{i\in I}X_i\le s\}. No independence is assumed. Suppose each X_i is uniformly distributed on [0,1]. Then \mathbb E N[n,s]\le\min\{n,\sqrt{2sn}\}.

Level justification

Proof 1: Solve the uniform-marginal BRS equation

BRS inequality (dependency)

For nonnegative random variables X_1,\ldots,X_n on a common probability space and s>0, define N[n,s]=\max\{|I|:I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\},\ \sum_{i\in I}X_i\le s\}. No independence is assumed.

For identically distributed nonnegative random variables with absolutely continuous distribution function F, one has \mathbb E N[n,s]\le nF(t) whenever t>0 solves n\int_0^t x\,dF(x)=s.

Uniform distribution calculation

For 0<s\le n/2, the uniform distribution has F(t)=t for 0\le t\le1. The BRS equation becomes

{\displaystyle n\int _{0}^{t}xdx=nt^{2}/2=s.}

The solution is {\displaystyle t={\sqrt {2s/n}}}, and thus from the stated BRS inequality

{\displaystyle E(N[n,s])\leq n\,F(t)=n{\sqrt {2s/n}}={\sqrt {2sn}}}.

Since one always has {\displaystyle N[n,s]\leq n}, this bound becomes trivial for {\displaystyle s\geq nE(X_1)=n/2}.

Thus the bound is \min\{n,\sqrt{2sn}\} for all s>0.

Use the fully stated BRS bound with the uniform density, integrate x on [0,t], solve the quadratic and combine with the trivial count bound. Undergraduate elementary continuous probability and calculus suffice; no general joint measure construction or proof of BRS is needed.
