Classical electrostatic forces alone cannot stably hold an unconstrained collection of point charges at rest. This is an impossibility result for a physical model: explaining stable matter requires relaxing its assumptions, and the conclusion does not rule out stability from motion, constraints or other interactions.

Statement

A collection of point charges in three-dimensional space cannot be maintained in a stable stationary equilibrium solely by their mutual electrostatic forces. The charges are free to move in all three spatial directions; no mechanical constraints, motion-dependent stabilization or other forces are included.

Here the electrostatic interaction is the classical inverse-square interaction: like charges repel and opposite charges attract along the line joining them, with force magnitude proportional to the product of their charge magnitudes divided by the square of their separation. Stationary equilibrium means that the net force on every charge is zero. Stability means that sufficiently small displacements from the equilibrium are restored rather than producing an unstable direction.

Level justification

Proof 1: Spherical mean value and a single-charge displacement (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Earnshaw’s Theorem from the Reciprocation Theorem).

Freeze every charge except one. Its variable contribution to the total energy is q\phi(x), where \phi is the potential of the other charges. In a ball excluding them, the spherical mean of this energy equals its central value. If that value were a local minimum, continuity would force equality on every small sphere and hence constancy throughout the ball. Such a displacement has no restoring force. This argument covers a vanishing Hessian as well as either sign of the movable charge.
