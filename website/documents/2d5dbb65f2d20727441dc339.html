Proof 1: Frey curve and incompatible modularity conclusions

Suppose that for a prime p\ge5, the Fermat equation

a^p+b^p=c^p

has a solution with nonzero integers, hence a counterexample to Fermat's Last Theorem. Then the elliptic curve

y^2=x(x-a^p)(x+b^p)

cannot be modular, by the Frey–Ribet implication. Its three roots are distinct, since a,b,c are nonzero and a^p+b^p=c^p\ne0.

The modularity theorem states that every elliptic curve over \mathbb Q is modular. Thus, the theorem for this family of elliptic curves, called Hellegouarch–Frey curves, implies that no such solution exists. The link between the Fermat counterexample and nonmodularity is used here as an external theorem.
