Statement

Let S be a closed surface with a two-sided simple closed curve and an embedded closed annular neighborhood A\cong S^1\times[0,1], where S^1=\{s\in\mathbb C:|s|=1\}. On A define f(s,t)=(s e^{2\pi it},t) and outside A let f be the identity. This gives a self-homeomorphism of S fixing both boundary circles of A.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Basic topology supplies homeomorphisms and subspaces, and complex arithmetic supplies rotations. A surface and an annular neighborhood can be understood through a locally planar space containing a copy of the given circle strip. The map is specified explicitly on that strip and is the identity elsewhere, without bundle or differential-geometric prerequisites.

Proof 1: Twist the circle coordinate and extend by the identity — outline

Give A coordinates (s, t) where s is a complex number of the form {\displaystyle e^{i\theta }} with {\displaystyle \theta \in [0,2\pi ],} and t ∈ [0, 1].

Let f be the map from S to itself which is the identity outside of A and inside A we have

{\displaystyle f(s,t)=\left(se^{i2\pi t},t\right).}

Then f is a Dehn twist about the core curve of A.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

On the annulus the inverse is explicitly (s,t) maps to (s exp(-2 pi i t),t). Both maps equal the identity at t=0 and t=1, so they paste with the identity outside the annulus. From basic topology and complex arithmetic, a surface and its embedded annular neighborhood are concrete local-coordinate settings, a small extension. No classification of surfaces, mapping class groups or isotopy theory is needed.

Treatment:

Statement used:

For a finite closed cover, continuous maps on its pieces that agree on overlaps define a continuous map on the whole space.

Application in proof:

Use the annulus A and S minus the interior of A; apply the same argument to the explicit inverse.
