Statement

Let \mathfrak g be a finite-dimensional Lie algebra over an algebraically closed field k of characteristic zero, and let \mathfrak r be its solvable radical. Every nonzero finite-dimensional simple \mathfrak g-module V is isomorphic to W\otimes k_\chi, where W is a simple \mathfrak g/\mathfrak r-module regarded as a \mathfrak g-module, and k_\chi is a one-dimensional module defined by a linear functional \chi:\mathfrak g\to k vanishing on [\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g].

Proof 1: Simple modules split off a character of the radical — source deduction

Write \pi:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak{gl}(V) for the representation, and for a linear functional \lambda on the radical write

V_\lambda=\{v\in V:\pi(X)v=\lambda(X)v\text{ for all }X\in\operatorname{rad}(\mathfrak g)\}.

By Lie's theorem, we can find a linear functional {\displaystyle \lambda } of {\displaystyle \operatorname {rad} ({\mathfrak {g}})} so that there is a nonzero weight space {\displaystyle V_{\lambda }} of {\displaystyle \operatorname {rad} ({\mathfrak {g}})}. By the ideal-weight-space invariance lemma (in a finite-dimensional characteristic-zero representation, a simultaneous eigenspace for an ideal is invariant under the ambient Lie algebra), {\displaystyle V_{\lambda }} is also a {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {g}}}-module; so {\displaystyle V=V_{\lambda }}. In particular, for each {\displaystyle X\in \operatorname {rad} ({\mathfrak {g}})}, {\displaystyle \operatorname {tr} (\pi (X))=\dim(V)\lambda (X)}. Extend \lambda by \lambda(X)=\operatorname{tr}(\pi(X))/\dim V to a linear functional on {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {g}}} that vanishes, since traces of commutators are zero, on {\displaystyle [{\mathfrak {g}},{\mathfrak {g}}]}; {\displaystyle \lambda } is then a one-dimensional representation of {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {g}}}. Now, {\displaystyle (\pi ,V)\simeq (\pi ,V)\otimes (-\lambda )\otimes \lambda }. Since {\displaystyle \pi } coincides with {\displaystyle \lambda } on {\displaystyle \operatorname {rad} ({\mathfrak {g}})}, we have that {\displaystyle V\otimes (-\lambda )} is trivial on {\displaystyle \operatorname {rad} ({\mathfrak {g}})} and thus is the pullback of a (simple) representation of {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {g}}/\operatorname {rad} ({\mathfrak {g}})}. {\displaystyle \square }

Here \lambda and -\lambda in tensor products denote the one-dimensional modules with those characters; the extended character \lambda is the \chi of the statement.
