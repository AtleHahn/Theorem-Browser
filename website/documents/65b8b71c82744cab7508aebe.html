Statement

Let q\ge1 be an integer, X a complex analytic space, and \mathcal F a coherent analytic sheaf on X. If X is q-pseudoconvex, then

\dim_{\mathbb C}H^i(X,\mathcal F)<\infty\qquad\text{for every integer }i\ge q.

Here q-pseudoconvex means that X admits a smooth real exhaustion function \varphi which is q-convex outside a compact subset. Exhaustion means that \{x:\varphi(x)<c\} is relatively compact in X for every real c.

Here a complex analytic space may have nilpotents in its structure sheaf; it is not required to be reduced or smooth. A coherent analytic sheaf is a locally finitely presented module over that structure sheaf. H^i(X,\mathcal F) denotes sheaf cohomology, the ith right derived functor of global sections. Use the following q-convexity convention. A real C^\infty function on an analytic space is q-convex at a point if, in a local embedding into an open subset of \mathbb C^N, it is the restriction of a smooth function whose Levi matrix (\partial^2\varphi/\partial z_j\partial\bar z_k) has at least N-q+1 positive eigenvalues at that point. Smooth functions are understood on the underlying reduced space through such local embeddings; the sheaf \mathcal F and its cohomology still use the full analytic-space structure.
