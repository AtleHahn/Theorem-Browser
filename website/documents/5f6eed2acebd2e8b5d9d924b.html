Statement

Work over a field of characteristic zero. For X\subseteq\mathbb A^4 defined by y^2-x^3=x^4+xz^2-w^3=0, blow up the origin. In the x-chart its strict transform X_1 has ideal (x-y^2,y^2+z^2-w^3). The regular ambient center C_1=\{x=y=0\} meets X_1 in the singular cusp z^2=w^3.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Affine schemes and ideals; Point blowups; Strict transforms; Regular centers and singular intersections. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Compute the center–transform intersection — outline

The transformed ideal in the x-chart is (x-y^2,y^2(y^2+z^2-w^3)) and the strict-transform ideal is (x-y^2,y^2+z^2-w^3). Intersecting the latter with C_1 sets x=y=0 and leaves z^2-w^3=0. This cusp is not regular at the origin, although C_1 is a regular coordinate plane. Thus regularity of an ambient center does not guarantee regularity of its intersection with the strict transform.

Compute the strict-transform ideal in an affine blowup chart and intersect with a regular ambient center. Schemes, blowups, saturation and local singularity criteria require algebraic geometry beyond polynomial substitution alone.
