Statement

Let M=\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\B^*&C\end{bmatrix} be a positive-semidefinite Hermitian matrix over \mathbb C, where A is p\times p, C is q\times q, and B is p\times q for positive integers p,q. Here B^* is the conjugate transpose. Then \det M\le\det A\det C. If M is positive definite, equality holds exactly when B=0.

Level justification

A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Normalize diagonal blocks and apply eigenvalue AM–GM

Assume that A and C are positive definite. We have that A^{-1} and C^{-1} are positive definite. Let

D:=\begin{bmatrix}A&0\\0&C\end{bmatrix}.

We note that

\begin{aligned} D^{-1/2}MD^{-1/2} &=\begin{bmatrix}A^{-1/2}&0\\0&C^{-1/2}\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}A&B\\B^*&C\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}A^{-1/2}&0\\0&C^{-1/2}\end{bmatrix}\\ &=\begin{bmatrix}I_p&A^{-1/2}BC^{-1/2}\\ C^{-1/2}B^*A^{-1/2}&I_q\end{bmatrix}. \end{aligned}

Applying the AM–GM inequality to the nonnegative eigenvalues of D^{-1/2}MD^{-1/2}, we see

\det(D^{-1/2}MD^{-1/2}) \leq\left(\frac{1}{p+q}\operatorname{tr}(D^{-1/2}MD^{-1/2})\right)^{p+q} =1^{p+q}=1.

By multiplicativity of determinant, we have

\begin{aligned} \det(D^{-1/2})\det(M)\det(D^{-1/2})&\leq1\\ \Longrightarrow\quad\det(M)&\leq\det(D)=\det(A)\det(C). \end{aligned}

In this case, equality holds if and only if M=D, that is, all entries of B are 0: equality in AM–GM means that every eigenvalue of the normalized Hermitian matrix is 1, so that matrix is the identity.

For \varepsilon>0, as A+\varepsilon I_p and C+\varepsilon I_q are positive definite, we have

\det(M+\varepsilon I_{p+q})\leq \det(A+\varepsilon I_p)\det(C+\varepsilon I_q).

Taking the limit as \varepsilon\to0 proves the inequality. From the inequality we note that if M is invertible, then both A and C are invertible and we get the desired equality condition.
