Statement

Let p(n) count the partitions of the nonnegative integer n into positive integer summands, with their order ignored. For every integer k\ge0, p(5k+4)\equiv0\pmod{5}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

An integer partition is a sum of positive integers with order ignored; the congruences are elementary counting statements.

Mathematical Field
Combinatorics
Required topics
Integer partitionsDivisibility
Review notes
none

Proof 1: Extract coefficients of the q-product identity — outline

Write (q^d)_\infty=\prod_{j\ge1}(1-q^{dj}). Use the source identity \sum_{k\ge0}p(5k+4)q^k=5\frac{(q^5)_\infty^5}{(q)_\infty^6}. The product quotients have integer coefficients as formal power series, and every term on the right has a prefactor divisible by 5. Comparing coefficients gives the congruence.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the supplied q-product identity, note coefficientwise integrality of the product quotient and compare coefficients. Undergraduate generating functions suffice; formal infinite products are handled one coefficient at a time, with only finitely many factors contributing. No modular-form theory is needed.
