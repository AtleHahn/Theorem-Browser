Statement

Let R be a ring and I an additive subgroup. If (r+I)(s+I)=rs+I is independent of the representatives for all r,s\in R, then I is a two-sided ideal: R I\subseteq I and I R\subseteq I.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory algebra supplies cosets, ideals, normal subgroups and quotient maps. All named composites are explicitly given by formulas, so understanding the claim requires no abstract adjunction theory.

Proof 1: Substitute zero representatives to obtain absorption

For r,s\in R and \alpha,\beta\in I, representative independence gives (r+\alpha)(s+\beta)+I=rs+I. Setting r=s=0 shows \alpha\beta\in I. Setting \alpha=s=0 gives r\beta\in I, and setting r=\beta=0 gives \alpha s\in I. These are the required left and right absorption properties.
