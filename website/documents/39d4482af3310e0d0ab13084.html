Statement

Let P be a bounded three-dimensional convex Euclidean polyhedron. Write \Omega_v for its interior solid angle at vertex v in steradians, \theta_e for its interior dihedral angle at edge e in radians, and f for its number of faces. Then \sum_v\Omega_v-2\sum_e\theta_e+2\pi f-4\pi=0.

Proof 1: Collect vertex, lune, hemisphere and full-sphere terms

For a three-dimensional polyhedron the Gram–Euler theorem reads:

\sum_v\Omega_v-2\sum_e\theta_e+\sum_F2\pi-4\pi=0,

where \Omega_v is the solid angle at a vertex, \theta_e the dihedral angle at an edge (the solid angle of the corresponding spherical lune is twice as big), the third sum counts the faces (each with an interior hemisphere angle of 2\pi) and the last term is the interior solid angle (full sphere or 4\pi). With f faces, the third sum is 2\pi f.
