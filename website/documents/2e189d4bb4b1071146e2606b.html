Statement

A proper vertex coloring assigns distinct colors to the two ends of every edge. Graphs here are finite and simple, with no loops or repeated edges. Let G be planar and let v be adjacent to every other vertex. Then G-v is three-colorable.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The finite graph, noncrossing drawing and color constraints are explicit elementary combinatorial objects.

Proof 1: Exclude the universal vertex’s color

By the four-color theorem, G has a proper four-coloring. Every other vertex is adjacent to v, so none can use the color assigned to v. The remaining graph therefore uses at most three colors.

Level justification

Apply the four-color theorem and exclude the universal vertex color from every other vertex. School finite graph coloring suffices.
