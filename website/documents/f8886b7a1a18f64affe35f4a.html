Statement

Let f:M\to N be a continuous map of closed oriented n-manifolds, and let D_M:H^k(M;\mathbb Z)\to H_{n-k}(M;\mathbb Z) and D_N be their duality isomorphisms. If D_N=f_*D_Mf^*, then f^*:H^k(N;\mathbb Z)\to H^k(M;\mathbb Z) is injective. Thus the formula cannot hold for a map with noninjective pullback.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Induced homology/cohomology maps; Duality isomorphisms. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Use injectivity of the composite

The composite f_*D_Mf^* equals the isomorphism D_N. Since an isomorphism is injective, its first-applied factor f^* must be injective. This is the obstruction in the source’s warning about arbitrary continuous maps.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the elementary injective-composite observation, but the full maps are induced homology/cohomology maps and manifold duality isomorphisms. Understanding those objects requires graduate algebraic topology.
