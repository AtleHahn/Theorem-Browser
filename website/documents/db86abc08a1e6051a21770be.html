Statement

If H is a Lebesgue-measurable proper additive subgroup of \mathbb R, then m(H)=0.

Proof 1: Steinhaus and the subgroup neighborhood criterion — outline

By Steinhaus, a positive-measure subgroup would have H-H=H containing a neighborhood of zero. An additive subgroup of the reals containing such a neighborhood is all of \mathbb R. Therefore a proper measurable subgroup must have measure zero.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply Steinhaus to an arbitrary Lebesgue-measurable subgroup and use the interval-neighborhood subgroup criterion. Understanding the full measurable-set hypothesis beyond length of elementary sets is not developed locally; retain beyond undergraduate provisionally rather than presume that background.
