Statement

Let S be a closed connected oriented surface of genus g\ge2. Let Q(X_j,Y_j) be a sequence of marked quasi-Fuchsian representations of \pi_1(S), where X_j,Y_j are the two marked conformal boundary structures in Teichmüller space (identifying the opposite orientation on the second boundary in the usual way). Suppose

X_j\longrightarrow[\lambda],\qquad Y_j\longrightarrow[\mu]

in the Thurston compactification, with nonzero measured laminations \lambda,\mu. Assume that for every nonzero measured lamination \nu on S,

i(\nu,\lambda)+i(\nu,\mu)>0.

Then the quasi-Fuchsian representations have an algebraically convergent subsequence up to conjugation: there are indices j_k, representatives \rho_{j_k} of Q(X_{j_k},Y_{j_k}), and a homomorphism \rho:\pi_1(S)\to\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb C) such that \rho_{j_k}(\gamma)\to\rho(\gamma) for every \gamma\in\pi_1(S). Here Teichmüller space consists of marked complex structures on S, identified by conformal diffeomorphisms respecting the marking up to isotopy. A quasi-Fuchsian representation is discrete and faithful into \operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb C), the orientation-preserving isometry group of hyperbolic three-space, with Jordan-curve limit set. The quotients of its two complementary domains on the sphere at infinity give X_j,Y_j. A measured geodesic lamination is a closed union of disjoint complete simple geodesics for a reference hyperbolic metric on S, with a locally finite transverse measure invariant under transport along leaves. Its projective class identifies positive rescalings of that measure. The intersection number i is the nonnegative continuous extension of weighted geometric intersection of closed curves to measured laminations. Convergence X_j\to[\lambda] means that there are positive scalars c_j\to0 such that c_j\ell_{X_j}(a)\to i(\lambda,a) for every essential simple closed curve a, where \ell_X(a) is its geodesic length in the hyperbolic metric of X; the same definition applies to Y_j and \mu. The filling condition is unchanged by positive rescaling of either lamination.
