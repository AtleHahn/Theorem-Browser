Statement

A finite term is a finite rooted ordered tree: variables and constants are leaves, and a function symbol with its specified arity labels each internal node. Equality means identical trees. A substitution simultaneously replaces each occurrence of a variable by its assigned finite term, leaving all other symbols fixed. Let a,b be distinct constants, x,y distinct variables and * a binary symbol. The equation x*a=y*b has no syntactic unifier. Modulo commutativity of *, it has the unifier \{x\mapsto b,y\mapsto a\}.

Proof 1: Constant clash versus a commutative swap

If a and b are distinct constants, the equation x*a=y*b has no solution with respect to purely syntactic unification, where nothing is known about the operator *.

However, if * is known to be commutative, then the substitution \sigma=\{x\mapsto b,y\mapsto a\} solves the equation, since

\begin{aligned} (x*a)\sigma &=b*a &&\text{by substitution application}\\ &=a*b &&\text{by commutativity of }*\\ &=(y*b)\sigma &&\text{by (converse) substitution application}. \end{aligned}

High school only
Level justification

Syntactic equality would require the distinct right constants to agree. Under commutativity, the displayed substitution gives ba and ab, which agree. This is elementary symbolic manipulation.
