Statement

Let n\ge1 be an integer and let permutations act on \{1,\ldots,n\}. If x_1\le\cdots\le x_n are real and \sigma is a permutation, then \sum_i x_ix_{n-i+1}\le\sum_i x_ix_{\sigma(i)}\le\sum_i x_i^2.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The assertion concerns finite products of an ordered real sequence under a permutation.

none

Proof 1: Substitute the same sequence in both slots

As a simple example, consider real numbers {\displaystyle x_{1}\leq \cdots \leq x_{n}}: By applying the rearrangement inequality with {\displaystyle y_{i}:=x_{i}} for all {\displaystyle i=1,\ldots ,n,} it follows that

{\displaystyle x_{1}x_{n}+\cdots +x_{n}x_{1}\leq x_{1}x_{\sigma (1)}+\cdots +x_{n}x_{\sigma (n)}\leq x_{1}^{2}+\cdots +x_{n}^{2}}

for every permutation {\displaystyle \sigma } of {\displaystyle 1,\ldots ,n.}

Level
High school only
Level justification

Substitute the same finite ordered sequence into both slots of the stated rearrangement inequality. School finite sums and inequalities suffice.
