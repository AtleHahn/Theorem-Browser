Statement

Let M,N be real continuous local martingales on a filtered probability space, and let H,K be real measurable processes. Write \langle M\rangle,\langle N\rangle for their quadratic variations and \langle M,N\rangle for their quadratic covariation, all starting at zero. For every t\ge0, almost surely,

\int_0^t |H_s||K_s|\,|d\langle M,N\rangle_s|\le\left(\int_0^t H_s^2\,d\langle M\rangle_s\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_0^t K_s^2\,d\langle N\rangle_s\right)^{1/2}.

The integrals are pathwise Lebesgue–Stieltjes integrals and |d\langle M,N\rangle| is the total variation measure of the signed covariation measure. The nonnegative integrals may be extended-valued, with 0\cdot\infty=0 in this inequality. Quadratic variation is the limit in probability of sums of squared increments along partitions with mesh tending to zero; covariation uses products of increments. A local martingale becomes a martingale when stopped at each of a sequence of stopping times tending almost surely to infinity.
