Statement

Let a be normal in a unital C*-algebra A and let \sigma(a)=\bigsqcup_{k=1}^n\sigma_k be a finite partition into closed subsets, with n\geq1. Put p_k=\chi_{\sigma_k}(a). Then each p_k is a projection commuting with a, p_jp_k=\delta_{jk}p_k, \sum_kp_k=1, and a=\sum_kap_k.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Proof 1: Characteristic-function products and sums

Since all {\displaystyle \sigma _{k}} are closed and their complements are finite unions of the other closed pieces, the characteristic functions {\displaystyle \chi _{\sigma _{k}}} are continuous on {\displaystyle \sigma (a)}. Now let {\displaystyle p_{k}:=\chi _{\sigma _{k}}(a)} be defined using the continuous functional calculus. As the {\displaystyle \sigma _{k}} are pairwise disjoint, {\displaystyle \chi _{\sigma _{j}}\chi _{\sigma _{k}}=\delta _{jk}\chi _{\sigma _{k}}} and {\textstyle \sum _{k=1}^{n}\chi _{\sigma _{k}}=\chi _{\cup _{k=1}^{n}\sigma _{k}}=\chi _{\sigma (a)}={\textbf {1}}} holds and thus the {\displaystyle p_{k}} satisfy the claimed properties, as can be seen from the properties of the continuous functional calculus. For the decomposition a=\sum_k a_k, let {\displaystyle a_{k}=ap_{k}=\operatorname {Id} (a)\cdot \chi _{\sigma _{k}}(a)=(\operatorname {Id} \cdot \chi _{\sigma _{k}})(a)}. Here \operatorname{Id}(z)=z is the coordinate function. The functional calculus is a unital star-homomorphism; the real idempotent functions give projections, and their commutation with \operatorname{Id} gives commutation with a.
