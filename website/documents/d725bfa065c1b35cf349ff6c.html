Statement

Let \varphi\in\mathbb R with \cos(\varphi/2)\ne0, and set t=\tan(\varphi/2). Then

\sin\varphi=\frac{2t}{1+t^2},\quad\cos\varphi=\frac{1-t^2}{1+t^2},\quad\tan\varphi=\frac{2t}{1-t^2},\quad\cot\varphi=\frac{1-t^2}{2t},\quad\sec\varphi=\frac{1+t^2}{1-t^2},\quad\csc\varphi=\frac{1+t^2}{2t},

where quotient identities apply when their denominators are nonzero.

High school only

All functions are elementary real trigonometry and the parameter is explicitly defined.

Proof 1: Divide the double-angle identities

Using double-angle formulae and the Pythagorean identity 1+\tan^2(\varphi/2)=1/\cos^2(\varphi/2) gives

\begin{aligned} \sin\varphi &=2\sin(\varphi/2)\cos(\varphi/2)\\ &=\frac{2\sin(\varphi/2)\cos(\varphi/2)/\cos^2(\varphi/2)} {1+\tan^2(\varphi/2)} =\frac{2\tan(\varphi/2)}{1+\tan^2(\varphi/2)} =\frac{2t}{1+t^2}, \end{aligned}\begin{aligned} \cos\varphi &=\cos^2(\varphi/2)-\sin^2(\varphi/2)\\ &=\frac{(\cos^2(\varphi/2)-\sin^2(\varphi/2))/\cos^2(\varphi/2)} {1+\tan^2(\varphi/2)} =\frac{1-\tan^2(\varphi/2)}{1+\tan^2(\varphi/2)} =\frac{1-t^2}{1+t^2}. \end{aligned}

Taking the quotient of the formulae for sine and cosine yields

\tan\varphi=\frac{2\tan(\varphi/2)}{1-\tan^2(\varphi/2)} =\frac{2t}{1-t^2}.

The remaining formulas are the reciprocals, wherever defined.

Proof 2: Intersect the projecting line and circle — outline

Geometrically, the construction goes like this: for a point (\cos\varphi,\sin\varphi)\ne(-1,0) on the unit circle, draw the line passing through it and the point (-1,0). This line crosses the y-axis at some point y=t. One can show using simple geometry that t=\tan(\varphi/2). The equation for the drawn line is y=(1+x)t. The equation for the intersection of the line and circle is then a quadratic equation involving t. The two solutions are (-1,0) and (\cos\varphi,\sin\varphi). This allows us to write the latter as rational functions of t:

\sin\varphi=\frac{2t}{1+t^2},\qquad \cos\varphi=\frac{1-t^2}{1+t^2}.

The parameter t represents the stereographic projection of the point (\cos\varphi,\sin\varphi) onto the y-axis with the center of projection at (-1,0). Thus, the tangent half-angle formulae give conversions between the stereographic coordinate t on the unit circle and the standard angular coordinate \varphi. Quotients and reciprocals give the other formulas in the statement.
