Statement

Let X be a real Hilbert space and let \mu,\nu be Radon Gaussian Borel probability measures on X. Let their means be m_\mu,m_\nu\in X and their covariance operators be C_\mu,C_\nu. Then \mu and \nu have the same Borel null sets if and only if all three conditions hold:

  1. H:=\operatorname{ran}C_\mu^{1/2}=\operatorname{ran}C_\nu^{1/2}.
  2. m_\mu-m_\nu\in H.
  3. On X_0=\overline H, with closure in the original norm of X, the operator TT^*-I_{X_0} is Hilbert–Schmidt, where T=C_\mu^{-1/2}C_\nu^{1/2}:X_0\to X_0.

In condition 3, the inverse C_\mu^{-1/2} means the inverse of the injective restriction C_\mu^{1/2}:X_0\to H, and is applied only to vectors in its range H. When condition 1 holds, the composition T is an everywhere-defined bounded operator on X_0; T^* is its adjoint for the original Hilbert inner product. An operator S is Hilbert–Schmidt if \sum_e\|Se\|^2<\infty for an orthonormal basis of X_0. If X_0=\{0\} this condition is vacuous. A Radon Gaussian probability measure is inner regular by compact sets and has a possibly degenerate real normal image under every continuous real linear functional. For \eta=\mu or \nu, its mean and covariance are characterized, for all u,v\in X, by

\langle m_\eta,u\rangle=\int_X\langle x,u\rangle\,d\eta(x),\qquad \langle C_\eta u,v\rangle=\int_X\langle x-m_\eta,u\rangle\langle x-m_\eta,v\rangle\,d\eta(x).

The square roots are the positive operator square roots. The common range H is the Cameron–Martin space as a set; its own Hilbert norm need not be the ambient norm used for the closure and adjoint above. Covariances of finite rank and zero rank are allowed.
