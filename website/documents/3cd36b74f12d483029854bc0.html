Statement

Let X be a scheme, F a coherent sheaf of O_X-modules, and p a point. Write m_p for the maximal ideal of O_{X,p}. Any basis of the fiber F(p)=F_p/m_pF_p over its residue field lifts to a minimal generating set of the stalk F_p.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves schemes; coherent sheaves; stalks; residue-field fibers; local module generators. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Apply the local module statement at the stalk

Let {\displaystyle F} be a coherent sheaf of {\displaystyle {\mathcal {O}}_{X}}-modules over an arbitrary scheme {\displaystyle X}. The stalk of {\displaystyle F} at a point {\displaystyle p\in X}, denoted by {\displaystyle F_{p}}, is a finitely generated module over the local ring {\displaystyle ({\mathcal {O}}_{X,p},{\mathfrak {m}}_{p})} and the fiber of {\displaystyle F} at {\displaystyle p} is the vector space {\displaystyle F(p)=F_{p}/{\mathfrak {m}}_{p}F_{p}}. Nakayama's lemma implies that a basis of the fiber {\displaystyle F(p)} lifts to a minimal set of generators of {\displaystyle F_{p}}.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Nakayama is applied to a coherent sheaf stalk and its residue-field fiber. Schemes, sheaves, stalks, and local module structure are substantial prerequisites even though the algebraic conclusion is directly quoted.
