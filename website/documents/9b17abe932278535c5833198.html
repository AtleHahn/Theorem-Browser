Statement

Let K be a compact subset of a Hausdorff locally convex topological vector space X, and let (C_i)_{i\in I} be closed convex subsets of X. If the family (K\cap C_i)_{i\in I} has the finite intersection property (every finite intersection is nonempty), then K\cap\bigcap_{i\in I}C_i\ne\varnothing.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Point-set topology supplies compactness, Hausdorffness, closed subsets and neighborhood bases. A topological vector space adds continuity of vector addition and scalar multiplication; local convexity means a base of convex neighborhoods of zero. These related definitions can be understood from topology and linear algebra within about 30 minutes. The finite-intersection condition is supplied, so no duality or seminorm machinery is needed.

Proof 1: Apply compactness to relatively closed intersections

Compactness implies convex compactness because a topological space is compact if and only if every family of closed subsets having the finite intersection property has nonempty intersection. Apply this characterization to the closed subsets K\cap C_i of K. It only involves those closed subsets that are also intersections with convex sets, rather than all closed subsets.
