Statement

Let X be a real random variable, a\in\mathbb R, and M(t)=\mathbb E e^{tX}. For every t>0 such that M(2t)<\infty and M(t)\ge e^{ta},

\Pr(X>a)\ge\left(1-\frac{e^{ta}}{M(t)}\right)^2\frac{M(t)^2}{M(2t)}.

The supremum of these expressions is also a lower bound. For admissible t<0 the same expression instead bounds \Pr(X<a) from below.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

M(t) is explicitly the expectation of the positive scalar transform exp(tX). A real random variable, event probability and expectation as an average/integral are the additional probability notions; no independence or distributional convergence occurs. These linked scalar moment definitions fit brief preparation from undergraduate integration, exponentials and suprema.

Proof 1: Substitute the exponential into Paley–Zygmund — outline

Apply the external Paley–Zygmund inequality to Y=e^{tX} with \theta=e^{ta}/M(t):

\Pr(Y>\theta\mathbb EY)\ge(1-\theta)^2\frac{(\mathbb EY)^2}{\mathbb EY^2},\qquad0\le\theta\le1.

Here \mathbb EY=M(t) and \mathbb EY^2=M(2t). For positive t the event is X>a, giving the stated expression and its supremum. For negative t it is X<a.
