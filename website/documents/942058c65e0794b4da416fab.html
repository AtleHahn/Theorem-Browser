Statement

Let c,d,e\in\mathbb C, d\ne0. If p satisfies p^6+2cp^4+(c^2-4e)p^2-d^2=0, then p\ne0. Put q=(p^2+c-d/p)/2 and s=(p^2+c+d/p)/2. Then x^4+cx^2+dx+e=(x^2+px+q)(x^2-px+s).

Proof 1: Match coefficients of two quadratic factors

The assumed equation has nonzero constant term -d^2, so p\ne0. To factor the quartic, compare coefficients:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}x^{4}+c\,x^{2}+d\,x+e&=(x^{2}+p\,x+q)\,(x^{2}-p\,x+s)\\&=x^{4}+(s+q-p^{2})\,x^{2}+p\,(s-q)\,x+q\,s\quad \Rightarrow \end{aligned}}}{\displaystyle p^{2}+c=s+q\quad \quad {\frac {d}{p}}=s-q\quad \quad e=q\,s\quad \Rightarrow }{\displaystyle (p^{2}+c)^{2}-\left({\frac {d}{p}}\right)^{2}=4\,e}

This cubic equation in {\displaystyle p^{2}} is known as Descartes' Resolvent. Add and subtract the first two coefficient equations to get q and s:

q=\frac{p^2+c-d/p}{2},\qquad s=\frac{p^2+c+d/p}{2}.

The assumed equation, divided by p^2, is exactly (p^2+c)^2-(d/p)^2=4e, so these coefficients also satisfy qs=e.
