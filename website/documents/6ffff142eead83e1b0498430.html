Statement

Label each of n vertices by a word of length m over \{0,1,*\}. Suppose the labels of each pair of distinct vertices have exactly one coordinate containing opposite symbols 0,1. For coordinate k, let L_k and R_k be the vertices with symbols 0 and 1, respectively. The edge sets \{\{u,v\}:u\in L_k,v\in R_k\} partition the edges of the complete graph on these vertices; empty parts may be discarded.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The finite labels and coordinate construction are fully explained and require only elementary finite combinatorics.

Proof 1: Use the opposite-symbol coordinates

For each position of the label strings, one can define a complete bipartite graph in which one side of the bipartition consists of the vertices labeled with 0 in that position and the other side consists of vertices labeled 1, omitting vertices labeled *. For the complete graph, every two vertices have exactly one position with opposite labels 0,1, so every edge must belong to exactly one of these complete bipartite graphs. Thus, a labeling of this type corresponds to a partition of the edges of the complete graph into complete bipartite graphs, with the length of the labels giving the number of graphs in the partition before empty parts are discarded.
