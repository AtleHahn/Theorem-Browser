Statement

For real r\ge1 and x>-1/r, \log(1+x)\ge r^{-1}\log(1+rx), and hence (1+x)^r\ge1+rx.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement compares real logarithms on positive arguments and the equivalent real-power expressions.

Proof 1: Two-point concavity of the logarithm

Using convexity, re-cast the desired inequality to {\displaystyle \log(1+x)\geq {\frac {1}{r}}\log(1+rx)} for real {\displaystyle r\geq 1} and real {\displaystyle x>-1/r}. This inequality can be proved using the fact that the {\displaystyle \log } function is concave, and then using Jensen's inequality in the form {\displaystyle \log(p\,a+(1-p)b)\geq p\log(a)+(1-p)\log(b)} to give: {\displaystyle \log(1+x)=\log({\frac {1}{r}}(1+rx)+{\frac {r-1}{r}})\geq {\frac {1}{r}}\log(1+rx)+{\frac {r-1}{r}}\log 1={\frac {1}{r}}\log(1+rx)} which is the desired inequality.

Exponentiation gives (1+x)^r\ge1+rx.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof uses the concavity inequality for logarithm at a two-point convex combination, with weights 1/r and 1-1/r, then exponentiates. Understanding concavity and its defining weighted inequality is a small undergraduate calculus extension; the negativity of the logarithm’s second derivative gives the relevant shape. All arguments of logarithms are positive by x>-1/r and r>=1.
