Statement

Let K=\mathbb C. An algebraic subset of K^m is the common zero set of polynomial equations with coefficients in K. The algebraic set Y=\{(x,y)\in K^2:xy-1=0\} projects onto K\setminus\{0\} under (x,y)\mapsto x, and that projection is not algebraic.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Algebraic set is explicitly defined as a common polynomial zero set, and projection is explicitly the first-coordinate map. The example involves only complex arithmetic and the equation xy=1. Understanding the infinite common-zero-set condition is elementary polynomial reasoning, without varieties or geometric structure theory.

Proof 1: Project the equation xy equals one

Consider the hyperbola defined by the equation

xy-1=0.

Over the complex numbers, the projection is the complement of 0, defined as \{x\in\mathbb C\mid x\ne0\}, which is not an algebraic set. Indeed, xy=1 has a solution y precisely when x\ne0, and the algebraic sets in \mathbb C are \mathbb C itself, the empty set and the finite sets: a nonzero polynomial has only finitely many roots. This example shows that, over the complex numbers, the projection of an algebraic set may be non-algebraic.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The equation xy=1 is solvable precisely for nonzero x. A polynomial vanishing at all nonzero complex numbers must be zero, so those points cannot be exactly a polynomial zero set. Only elementary polynomial roots and the supplied definition of algebraic set are needed.
