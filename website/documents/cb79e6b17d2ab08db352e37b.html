Statement

Let X\subset\mathbf P^3_k be a smooth cubic surface over an algebraically closed field, identified with the blowup of \mathbf P^2_k at six distinct points in general position: no three are collinear and all six do not lie on one conic. Under this identification, its 27 lines are the six exceptional curves, the strict transforms of the 15 lines through pairs of the six points, and the strict transforms of the six conics through each choice of five points.

These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Proof 1: Construct exceptional curves and transforms of lines and conics — outline

Given an identification between a cubic surface X and the blow-up of {\displaystyle \mathbf {P} ^{2}} at 6 points in general position, the 27 lines on X can be viewed as: the 6 exceptional curves created by blowing up, the birational transforms of the 15 lines through pairs of the 6 points in {\displaystyle \mathbf {P} ^{2}}, and the birational transforms of the 6 conics containing all but one of the 6 points. The cubic embedding is anticanonical: the restriction of the hyperplane line bundle \mathcal O(1) on \mathbf P^3 to X is \omega_X^{-1}, by the adjunction formula. The exact count of 27 lines on a smooth cubic surface is used here; the source does not expand the degree and distinctness checks for the listed curves.
