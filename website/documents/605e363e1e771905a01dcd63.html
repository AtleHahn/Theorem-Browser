Statement

For real c<d, the function h(x)=\begin{cases}\exp(-1/((x-c)(d-x))),&c<x<d,\\0,&x\notin(c,d).\end{cases} is infinitely differentiable on \mathbb R, positive on (c,d), and has support [c,d].

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Continuous real functions; Smooth compactly supported test functions; Differentiation; Real exponential function. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Extend the interval exponential by zero — outline

Use the displayed exponential construction, extended by zero off (c,d). The source explicitly identifies it as a C^\infty bump; positivity and support follow from the formula, while the routine vanishing of all one-sided derivatives at the endpoints is left implicit.
