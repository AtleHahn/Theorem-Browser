Statement

For standard Brownian motion W with W(0)=0, every a>0 and t\ge0 satisfy \mathbb P(\sup_{0\le s\le t}W(s)\ge a)=2\mathbb P(W(t)\ge a).

Proof 1: Pair the paths reflected after their first hit — outline

Assuming W(0)=0, due to the continuity of Wiener processes, each path on [0,t] which finishes above the threshold a at time t must have reached a at some earlier time t_a\leq t for the first time. It can cross level a multiple times; we take the earliest.

For every such path, you can define another path W' on [0,t] that is reflected or vertically flipped on the sub-interval (t_a,t) symmetrically around level a from the original path. By the strong Markov property and symmetry of the subsequent Brownian increments, the reflected process has the same Brownian law. These reflected paths reach W'(t_a)=a, but finish below a. Thus, of all the paths that reach a on [0,t], half will finish below a, and half will finish above. Hence, the probability of finishing above a is half that of reaching a.

For t>0, the event W(t)=a has probability zero, so the same equality holds with \geq a. At t=0, both probabilities vanish.

Level justification

The path-reflection argument uses first hitting times and the strong Markov property to preserve Brownian law. Those stochastic concepts exceed a small undergraduate extension even though the final pairing and factor of two are elementary.
