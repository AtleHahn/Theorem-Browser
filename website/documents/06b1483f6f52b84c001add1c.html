Statement

Let a<b be real and let K:[a,b]^2\to\mathbb R be continuous and symmetric. Suppose it is positive definite in the non-strict sense: \sum_{i,j}K(x_i,x_j)c_ic_j\ge0 for all finite point lists and real coefficients. On real L^2[a,b], put (T_Kf)(t)=\int_a^bK(t,s)f(s)\,ds. Then L^2[a,b] has an orthonormal basis of eigenfunctions of T_K, every eigenvalue is nonnegative, and the eigenfunctions with nonzero eigenvalues have continuous representatives.

Proof 1: Ascoli compactness, the spectral theorem and positive Riemann sums

The operator T_K is symmetric, by symmetry of K.

To show compactness, show that the image of the unit ball of L^2[a,b] under T_K is uniformly bounded and equicontinuous and apply Ascoli's theorem, to show that the image of the unit ball is relatively compact in C([a,b]) with the uniform norm and a fortiori in L^2[a,b].

Now apply the spectral theorem for compact self-adjoint operators on Hilbert spaces to T_K to show the existence of the orthonormal basis \{e_i\}_i of L^2[a,b]:

\lambda_i e_i(t)=[T_K e_i](t)=\int_a^b K(t,s)e_i(s)\,ds.

If \lambda_i\ne0, the eigenfunction e_i is seen to have a continuous representative on [a,b].

Finally, to show non-negativity of the eigenvalues one can write \lambda\langle f,f\rangle=\langle f,T_Kf\rangle and express the right hand side as an integral well-approximated by its Riemann sums, which are non-negative by positive-definiteness of K, implying \lambda\langle f,f\rangle\geq0, implying \lambda\geq0. For a nonzero eigenvalue the eigenfunction has a continuous representative by the integral equation; a zero eigenvalue already satisfies the inequality.
