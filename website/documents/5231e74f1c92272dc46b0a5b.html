Statement

Let A_\bullet be a simplicial abelian group with face maps d_i:A_n\to A_{n-1}. Put N(A)_0=A_0 and N(A)_n=\bigcap_{i=0}^{n-1}\ker(d_i) for n\geq1, and define \partial_n=(-1)^nd_n on N(A)_n for n\geq1, with \partial_0=0. These groups and maps form a nonnegatively graded chain complex. A simplicial homomorphism f:A_\bullet\to B_\bullet acts by restrictions f_n|_{N(A)_n}, making N a functor to nonnegative chain complexes of abelian groups.

Proof 1: Face identities and naturality — outline

The differentials are well defined because of the simplicial identity

d_i\circ d_n=d_{n-1}\circ d_i:A_n\to A_{n-2}\qquad(0\leq i\leq n-2),

showing that the image of d_n:N(A)_n\to A_{n-1} is in the kernel of each d_i for 0\leq i\leq n-2. This is because the definition of N(A)_n gives d_i(N(A)_n)=0.

Now, composing these differentials gives

N(A)_n\xrightarrow{(-1)^nd_n}N(A)_{n-1} \xrightarrow{(-1)^{n-1}d_{n-1}}N(A)_{n-2}

and the composition map (-1)^n(-1)^{n-1}d_{n-1}\circ d_n. For n\geq2, this composition is the zero map because of the simplicial identity

d_{n-1}\circ d_n=d_{n-1}\circ d_{n-1}

and the inclusion N(A)_n\subseteq\ker d_{n-1}. Together with \partial_0=0, this shows that the normalized chain complex is a nonnegatively graded chain complex.

A simplicial abelian group is a functor \Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{Ab}, where \Delta is the category of finite nonempty ordered sets and order-preserving maps, and morphisms A_\bullet\to B_\bullet are given by natural transformations. Their component maps commute with the face maps, so their restrictions to the normalized groups give chain maps, and the normalized chain complex construction is functorial.
