Statement

Let P be the decision problems solvable in deterministic polynomial time, NP those whose yes-instances have polynomially checkable polynomial-size certificates, and NPI the problems in NP that belong neither to P nor to the NP-complete problems (under polynomial-time many-one reductions). Then \mathrm P=\mathrm{NP} if and only if \mathrm{NPI} is empty.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

P and NP are defined operationally by algorithms and certificates. Many-one reducibility and completeness add a small related definition of translating decision instances, sufficient to interpret the class equality without a complexity course.

Proof 1: Ladner theorem and the definition of NP-intermediate

Use Ladner's theorem: if \mathrm P\ne\mathrm{NP}, then there exists a problem in NP that is neither in P nor NP-complete. Thus emptiness of NPI implies \mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}. Conversely, any NPI problem belongs to NP but not to P, so its existence implies \mathrm P\ne\mathrm{NP}. Equivalently, if \mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}, NPI is empty.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Take the contrapositive of Ladner theorem and use the stated class definition for the converse. Undergraduate computational complexity suffices; no diagonalization proof is needed.
