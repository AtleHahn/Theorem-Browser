Statement

Let S be a closed connected surface. Let c(S) be the least integer such that every finite simple graph embedded in S can have its vertices colored with c(S) colors so that adjacent vertices receive different colors. An embedding draws edges as arcs meeting only at common endpoints. A closed surface is a compact two-dimensional manifold without boundary.

If S is not the Klein bottle, then

c(S)=\left\lfloor\frac{7+\sqrt{49-24\chi(S)}}2\right\rfloor,

where \chi(S) is its Euler characteristic, computed as vertices minus edges plus faces in a triangulation, and \lfloor t\rfloor is the greatest integer at most t. For the Klein bottle, c(S)=6. Thus each displayed number is an upper bound for all embedded graphs and is attained by some embedded graph.

For an orientable surface of genus g>0 (a sphere with g handles), the formula reads

c(S)=\left\lfloor\frac{7+\sqrt{1+48g}}2\right\rfloor.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Basic undergraduate topology plus a short introduction to triangulated closed surfaces, Euler characteristic and handles suffices for this explicit finite coloring assertion. No classification proof or embedding constructions are needed.
