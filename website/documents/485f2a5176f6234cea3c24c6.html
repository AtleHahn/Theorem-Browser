Statement

Consider a homogeneous linear second-order ordinary differential equation

{\displaystyle y''+p(x)y'+q(x)\,y=0}

on an interval I of the real line with real- or complex-valued continuous functions p and q. Abel's identity states that the Wronskian {\displaystyle W=W(y_{1},y_{2})} of two real- or complex-valued solutions {\displaystyle y_{1}} and {\displaystyle y_{2}} of this differential equation, that is the function defined by the determinant

{\displaystyle W(y_{1},y_{2})(x)={\begin{vmatrix}y_{1}(x)&y_{2}(x)\\y'_{1}(x)&y'_{2}(x)\end{vmatrix}}=y_{1}(x)\,y'_{2}(x)-y'_{1}(x)\,y_{2}(x),\quad x\in I,}

satisfies the relation

{\displaystyle W(y_{1},y_{2})(x)=W(y_{1},y_{2})(x_{0})\cdot \exp \left(-\int _{x_{0}}^{x}p(t)\,dt\right),\quad x\in I,}

for each point {\displaystyle x_{0}\in I}.

Level justification

Understanding the statement involves differentiation and integration; determinants; real and complex scalar functions. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Differentiating the Wronskian

Differentiating the Wronskian using the product rule gives (writing {\displaystyle W} for {\displaystyle W(y_{1},y_{2})} and omitting the argument {\displaystyle x} for brevity)

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}W'&=y_{1}'y_{2}'+y_{1}y_{2}''-y_{1}''y_{2}-y_{1}'y_{2}'\\&=y_{1}y_{2}''-y_{1}''y_{2}.\end{aligned}}}

Solving for {\displaystyle y''} in the original differential equation yields

{\displaystyle y''=-(py'+qy).}

Substituting this result into the derivative of the Wronskian function to replace the second derivatives of {\displaystyle y_{1}} and {\displaystyle y_{2}} gives

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}W'&=-y_{1}(py_{2}'+qy_{2})+(py_{1}'+qy_{1})y_{2}\\&=-p(y_{1}y_{2}'-y_{1}'y_{2})\\&=-pW.\end{aligned}}}

Simplifying the equation to

{\displaystyle W'+pW=0}

yields a homogeneous first order linear differential equation that can be solved by using the integrating factor {\displaystyle \exp \left(\int p(x)dx\right)} and initial condition {\displaystyle W(x_{0})}

{\displaystyle W(x)=W(x_{0})\cdot \exp \left(-\int _{x_{0}}^{x}{p(t)dt}\right)}

Level justification

The calculation differentiates y1*y2′-y1′*y2, substitutes the two differential equations and simplifies to W′=-pW. The integrating factor yields the stated formula, including W(x0)=0, using only calculus and elementary determinants. Complex-valued functions are treated componentwise and introduce no complex-analysis theorem.
