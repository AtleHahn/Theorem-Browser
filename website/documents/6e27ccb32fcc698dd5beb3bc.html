Statement

Let U\subset\mathbb R^d be a bounded domain with piecewise smooth boundary and outward unit normal n. Functions are defined on a neighborhood of \overline U. Write \Delta=\nabla\cdot\nabla and \partial_n f=\nabla f\cdot n. For \psi\in C^1 and \varphi\in C^2, \int_U(\psi\Delta\varphi+\nabla\psi\cdot\nabla\varphi)\,dV=\int_{\partial U}\psi\partial_n\varphi\,dS.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Multivariable derivatives; Divergence and gradient; Euclidean volume and surface integrals. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Apply the divergence theorem to psi times grad phi

This identity is derived from the divergence theorem applied to the vector field {\displaystyle \mathbf {F} =\psi \nabla \varphi } while using an extension of the product rule that {\displaystyle \nabla \cdot \left(\psi \mathbf {X} \right)=\nabla \psi \cdot \mathbf {X} +\psi \nabla \cdot \mathbf {X} } , where {\displaystyle \psi } and {\displaystyle \varphi } are scalar functions and {\displaystyle \mathbf {X} } is a vector field.

Let {\displaystyle \psi } and {\displaystyle \varphi } be scalar functions defined on some region U ⊂ Rd, and suppose that {\displaystyle \psi } is once continuously differentiable and {\displaystyle \varphi } is twice continuously differentiable. Using the product rule above with letting X = ∇φ, integration of {\displaystyle \nabla \cdot \mathbf {F} =\nabla \cdot \left(\psi \nabla \varphi \right)} over U results in the Green's first identity:

{\displaystyle {\begin{array}{lcl}\int _{U}\left(\psi \,\Delta \varphi +\nabla \psi \cdot \nabla \varphi \right)\,dV&=&\oint _{\partial U}\psi \left(\nabla \varphi \cdot \mathbf {n} \right)\,dS\\&=&\oint _{\partial U}\psi \,\nabla \varphi \cdot d\mathbf {S} \end{array}}}

where {\displaystyle \Delta } (or {\displaystyle \nabla ^{2}}) in {\displaystyle \Delta f=\nabla ^{2}f=\nabla \cdot \nabla f} is the Laplace operator, ∂U is the boundary of region U, n is the outward pointing unit normal to the surface element dS, and dS = ndS is the oriented surface element.

The Integration by parts source also writes this same product-divergence calculation componentwise. In its notation, \Omega=U, u=\varphi, v=\psi, n=d, and \Gamma=\partial U:

Consider the continuously differentiable vector fields {\displaystyle \mathbf {U} =u_{1}\mathbf {e} _{1}+\cdots +u_{n}\mathbf {e} _{n}} and {\displaystyle v\mathbf {e} _{1},\ldots ,v\mathbf {e} _{n}}, where {\displaystyle \mathbf {e} _{i}} is the i-th standard basis vector for {\displaystyle i=1,\ldots ,n}. Now apply the product-divergence integration identity to each {\displaystyle u_{i}} times the vector field {\displaystyle v\mathbf {e} _{i}}:

{\displaystyle \int _{\Omega }u_{i}{\frac {\partial v}{\partial x_{i}}}\,d\Omega \ =\ \int _{\Gamma }u_{i}v\,\mathbf {e} _{i}\cdot {\hat {\mathbf {n} }}\,d\Gamma -\int _{\Omega }{\frac {\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{i}}}v\,d\Omega .}

Summing over i gives a new integration by parts formula:

{\displaystyle \int _{\Omega }\mathbf {U} \cdot \nabla v\,d\Omega \ =\ \int _{\Gamma }v\mathbf {U} \cdot {\hat {\mathbf {n} }}\,d\Gamma -\int _{\Omega }v\,\nabla \cdot \mathbf {U} \,d\Omega .}

The case {\displaystyle \mathbf {U} =\nabla u}, where {\displaystyle u\in C^{2}({\bar {\Omega }})}, is known as the first of Green's identities:

{\displaystyle \int _{\Omega }\nabla u\cdot \nabla v\,d\Omega \ =\ \int _{\Gamma }v\,\nabla u\cdot {\hat {\mathbf {n} }}\,d\Gamma -\int _{\Omega }v\,\nabla ^{2}u\,d\Omega .}
