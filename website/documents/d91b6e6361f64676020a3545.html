Statement

An arithmetical theory is effectively axiomatized when its theorems can be enumerated by an algorithm. Write T\vdash A for provability, \ulcorner A\urcorner for a sentence’s natural-number code, \bar n for the numeral for n, and Q for Robinson arithmetic. Let T be a consistent effectively axiomatized extension of Q. Let \operatorname{Prf}_T(n,m) be the standard arithmetized proof relation and \operatorname{Prov}_T(m)=\exists n\operatorname{Prf}_T(n,m). If T\vdash G\leftrightarrow\neg\operatorname{Prov}_T(\ulcorner G\urcorner), then T\nvdash G.

Proof 1: Consistency makes a Gödel fixed point unprovable — source argument

If T\vdash G, the actual proof has a natural-number code, yielding T\vdash\operatorname{Prov}_T(\ulcorner G\urcorner). But the fixed-point equivalence and G yield the negation of that same sentence. This contradicts consistency.
