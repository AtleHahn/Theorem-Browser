Statement

Let X\subset\mathbb{CP}^N be a complex projective algebraic variety of complex dimension n\ge1, and let Y be a hyperplane section such that U=X\setminus Y is smooth. Restriction induces isomorphisms H^k(X;\mathbb Z)\to H^k(Y;\mathbb Z) for 0\le k<n-1 and an injection for k=n-1.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Complex projective varieties; Hyperplane sections; Integral singular cohomology. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Proof 1: Cell attachment and the relative exact sequence (outline)

Thom and Bott interpret Y as the vanishing locus in X of a section of a line bundle. An application of Morse theory to this section implies that X can be constructed, up to homotopy relative to Y, from Y by adjoining cells of dimension n or more. We use this cell-attachment conclusion as an external theorem.

The relative cellular cochain groups are zero below degree n, so H^j(X,Y;\mathbb Z)=0 for j<n. The long exact sequence contains

H^k(X,Y;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow H^k(X;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow H^k(Y;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow H^{k+1}(X,Y;\mathbb Z).

Both relative terms vanish for k<n-1, giving an isomorphism; the first vanishes for k=n-1, giving injectivity.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the cell-attachment theorem to kill low relative cohomology and read restriction maps from exactness. Projective varieties, cellular cohomology and relative exact sequences require graduate algebraic topology and geometry.
