Statement

Let L(\phi^A,\partial_\nu\phi^A,x) be a smooth first-order Lagrangian density on a spacetime domain in \mathbb R^4, and let smooth scalar component fields \phi^A satisfy the Euler–Lagrange equations. Write p_A^\mu=\partial L/\partial(\partial_\mu\phi^A); repeated indices are summed. Assume the Euler–Lagrange equations \partial_\mu p_A^\mu=\partial L/\partial\phi^A. Consider an infinitesimal symmetry \delta x^\mu=\varepsilon X^\mu, \delta\phi^A=\varepsilon\Psi^A, leaving the action on every region invariant when both fields and region are transformed. Then j^\sigma=p_A^\sigma(X^\mu\partial_\mu\phi^A-\Psi^A)-LX^\sigma satisfies \partial_\sigma j^\sigma=0.

Proof 1: Fixed-coordinate variation and moving boundary

Under an infinitesimal transformation, write the variation in the coordinates as

x^\mu\longrightarrow\xi^\mu=x^\mu+\delta x^\mu,

whereas the transformation of the scalar field variables is expressed as

\phi^A\longrightarrow\alpha^A(\xi)=\phi^A(x)+\delta\phi^A(x).

The field variations \delta\phi^A result from two factors: intrinsic changes in the fields themselves and changes in coordinates, since the transformed field \alpha^A depends on the transformed coordinates \xi^\mu. To isolate the intrinsic changes, the field variation at a single point x is defined by

\alpha^A(x)=\phi^A(x)+\bar\delta\phi^A(x).

If the coordinates are changed, the region of spacetime over which the Lagrangian is being integrated also changes; denote the original region and its transformed version by \Omega and \Omega'. All variations below are taken to first order.

The assumption that the transformation does not change the action may be written

\int_{\Omega'}L(\alpha^A,\alpha^A_{,\nu},\xi)\,d^4\xi -\int_\Omega L(\phi^A,\phi^A_{,\nu},x)\,d^4x=0,

where a comma subscript indicates a partial derivative, \phi^A_{,\sigma}=\partial_\sigma\phi^A.

Since \xi is a dummy variable of integration, and since the change in the boundary is infinitesimal, the two integrals may be combined using the four-dimensional divergence theorem:

\int_\Omega\left\{ [L(\alpha^A,\alpha^A_{,\nu},x)-L(\phi^A,\phi^A_{,\nu},x)] +\partial_\sigma[L(\phi^A,\phi^A_{,\nu},x)\delta x^\sigma] \right\}\,d^4x=0.

The difference in Lagrangians can be written to first order as

L(\alpha^A,\alpha^A_{,\nu},x)-L(\phi^A,\phi^A_{,\nu},x) =\frac{\partial L}{\partial\phi^A}\bar\delta\phi^A +\frac{\partial L}{\partial\phi^A_{,\sigma}}\bar\delta\phi^A_{,\sigma}.

However, because the variations are defined at the same point, the variation and the derivative can be done in reverse order; they commute:

\bar\delta\phi^A_{,\sigma} =\bar\delta\frac{\partial\phi^A}{\partial x^\sigma} =\frac{\partial}{\partial x^\sigma}(\bar\delta\phi^A).

Using the Euler–Lagrange field equations

\partial_\sigma\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial\phi^A_{,\sigma}}\right) =\frac{\partial L}{\partial\phi^A},

the difference in Lagrangians can be written as

\begin{aligned} &L(\alpha^A,\alpha^A_{,\nu},x)-L(\phi^A,\phi^A_{,\nu},x)\\ &\quad=\partial_\sigma\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial\phi^A_{,\sigma}}\right) \bar\delta\phi^A +\frac{\partial L}{\partial\phi^A_{,\sigma}}\bar\delta\phi^A_{,\sigma}\\ &\quad=\partial_\sigma\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial\phi^A_{,\sigma}} \bar\delta\phi^A\right). \end{aligned}

Thus the change in the action can be written as

\int_\Omega\partial_\sigma\left\{ \frac{\partial L}{\partial\phi^A_{,\sigma}}\bar\delta\phi^A +L\delta x^\sigma\right\}\,d^4x=0.

Since this holds for any region \Omega, the integrand must be zero:

\partial_\sigma\left\{ \frac{\partial L}{\partial\phi^A_{,\sigma}}\bar\delta\phi^A +L\delta x^\sigma\right\}=0.

For the symmetry in the statement,

\delta x^\mu=\varepsilon X^\mu,\qquad \delta\phi^A=\varepsilon\Psi^A =\bar\delta\phi^A+\varepsilon X^\mu\partial_\mu\phi^A.

Here X^\mu\partial_\mu\phi^A is the Lie derivative of the scalar field in the X direction. These equations imply that the field variation taken at one point equals

\bar\delta\phi^A =\varepsilon\Psi^A-\varepsilon X^\mu\partial_\mu\phi^A.

Differentiating the divergence equation with respect to \varepsilon at \varepsilon=0 and changing the sign yields the conservation law

\partial_\sigma j^\sigma=0,

where

j^\sigma =\left[\frac{\partial L}{\partial\phi^A_{,\sigma}} X^\mu\partial_\mu\phi^A-LX^\sigma\right] -\frac{\partial L}{\partial\phi^A_{,\sigma}}\Psi^A.
