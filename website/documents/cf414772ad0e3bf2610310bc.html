Statement

Let \xi\in\mathbb R be neither rational nor a quadratic irrational, and let C>160/9. There are infinitely many distinct real numbers \alpha of algebraic degree at most two such that |\xi-\alpha|<C H(\alpha)^{-3}\max(1,\xi^2). For a quadratic irrational \alpha, write its primitive integer minimal polynomial with positive leading coefficient as ax^2+bx+c, and put H(\alpha)=\max(|a|,|b|,|c|). For a rational \alpha=u/v in lowest terms with v>0, put H(\alpha)=\max(|u|,v). Primitive means the coefficients have greatest common divisor one.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The minimal polynomial in degree two and coefficient height are a small extension of familiar undergraduate algebra; understanding proof methods in Diophantine approximation is not required.

Proof 1: Minimal points and determinant estimates (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Acta Arithmetica 13 (1967), pp.169–176; §§2–6 proof, especially Lemma 4 and final comparison.).

The linked §§2–6 use minimal points and determinant estimates. For the displayed constants, the following algebraic clarification is supplied here: assumed failure bounds P(x)=2xix+y by C2|x|^3*|L(x)| with C2<9/160; use successive minimal points for L(x)=xi^2x+xiy+z, their integer recurrences and infinitely many independent triples. In §5 the two unwanted determinant contributions are each <(32/9)C2, while the remaining contribution is <2 P_{n+1}X_n L_{n-1}. Thus (22) must read P_{n+1}X_n L_{n-1}>1/2-(32/9)C2, with the factor 1/2 coming from the remaining doubled determinant term. In §6 combine this with L_{m-1}<(4/3)X_m^-2: the lower bound is (3/8-4C2)X_m L_m. The upper bound is (8/3)C2 X_n L_n. The needed comparison is (8/3)C2<3/8-4C2, equivalent to C2<9/160. This forces increase along independent-triple indices, contradicting X_nL_n tending to zero. Negation and reciprocation handle the other real values of xi. An intermediate constant below C absorbs the reciprocal error. The height is that of the primitive minimal polynomial; infinitely many distinct approximants follow from the error tending to zero and the hypothesis on xi.
