Statement

There are probability vectors P_\varepsilon,Q with \delta(P_\varepsilon,Q)\to0 as \varepsilon\downarrow0, while D_{\rm KL}(P_\varepsilon\Vert Q)=+\infty for every \varepsilon>0 in the family. Here \delta(P,Q)=\frac12\sum_x|P(x)-Q(x)| and D_{\rm KL}=\sum_xP(x)\ln(P(x)/Q(x)) with the extended-divergence convention.

Proof 1: Mass at a zero-probability point

For 0<\varepsilon<1, on \{0,1\} take Q(0)=0,Q(1)=1 and P_\varepsilon(0)=\varepsilon,P_\varepsilon(1)=1-\varepsilon. The variation distance is \varepsilon, whereas positive P_\varepsilon mass at the point where Q vanishes gives infinite divergence.
