Statement

For complex a_0\ne0,a_2,a_4, put z_\pm=(-a_2\pm\sqrt{a_2^2-4a_0a_4})/(2a_0). The four roots, counted with multiplicity, of a_0x^4+a_2x^2+a_4=0 are \sqrt{z_+},-\sqrt{z_+},\sqrt{z_-},-\sqrt{z_-}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement applies the quadratic formula to x squared and then takes complex square roots. This is elementary polynomial algebra; counting repeated roots adds no substantial college prerequisite. This rating concerns understanding the statement, independently of the difficulty of its proof.

Proof 1: Substitute the square of the variable

The quartic equation takes the form

{\displaystyle a_{0}x^{4}+a_{2}x^{2}+a_{4}=0\,\!}

and thus is a biquadratic equation, which is easy to solve: let {\displaystyle z=x^{2}}, so our equation becomes

{\displaystyle a_{0}z^{2}+a_{2}z+a_{4}=0\,\!}

which is a simple quadratic equation, whose solutions are easily found using the quadratic formula:

{\displaystyle z={\frac {-a_{2}\pm {\sqrt {a_{2}^{2}-4a_{0}a_{4}}}}{2a_{0}}}\,\!}

When we've solved it (i.e. found these two z values), we can extract x from them

{\displaystyle x_{1}=+{\sqrt {z_{+}}}\,\!}{\displaystyle x_{2}=-{\sqrt {z_{+}}}\,\!}{\displaystyle x_{3}=+{\sqrt {z_{-}}}\,\!}{\displaystyle x_{4}=-{\sqrt {z_{-}}}\,\!}

If either of the z solutions were negative or complex numbers, then some of the x solutions are complex numbers.
