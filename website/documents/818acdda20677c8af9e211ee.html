Statement

For a smooth closed oriented four-manifold M and a characteristic embedded sphere \Sigma (its mod-two homology class is dual to w_2(M)), \sigma(M)\equiv\Sigma\cdot\Sigma\pmod{16}.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Characteristic embedded spheres, Stiefel-Whitney classes, intersection forms and signature require substantial four-manifold topology. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Set the surface Arf invariant to zero for a sphere

The Freedman–Kirby theorem gives \sigma(M)\equiv\Sigma\cdot\Sigma+8\operatorname{Arf}(M,\Sigma)\pmod{16} for a characteristic surface. For a sphere H_1(\Sigma;\mathbb Z/2)=0, so its indicated quadratic form has Arf invariant zero. The congruence reduces to the assertion.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the characteristic-surface congruence and vanish the Arf term using the sphere first homology. Characteristic classes, four-manifold intersection forms and Arf invariants require advanced topology.
