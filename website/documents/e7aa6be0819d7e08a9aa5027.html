Statement

Let G=(X,Y,E) be a finite bipartite graph with disjoint vertex classes X,Y. A matching is a set of edges sharing no endpoints, and it saturates X when every x∈X is on a matching edge. Put N(W)=\{y\in Y:xy\in E\text{ for some }x\in W\}. A fractional matching assigns weights w_e≥0 to edges, with incident weight at most 1 at each vertex; it saturates X when incident weight is exactly 1 at every x∈X. The following are equivalent: an X-saturating matching exists; an X-saturating fractional matching exists; |N(W)|≥|W| for every W⊆X.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Matching, saturation, neighbors and fractional weights are locally defined on a finite bipartite graph. The assertion compares finite sums of nonnegative weights with vertex counts, requiring elementary inequalities.

Proof 1: Integral and fractional Hall matchings are equivalent

An ordinary matching gives a fractional one by assigning weights 1 to its edges and 0 elsewhere. Conversely, for a fractional matching saturating X, the sum of incident weights over W⊆X is |W|. These edges meet only vertices of N(W), whose total capacity is at most |N(W)|, so |W|≤|N(W)|. Hall’s theorem gives an ordinary matching. This closes the cycle of implications.
