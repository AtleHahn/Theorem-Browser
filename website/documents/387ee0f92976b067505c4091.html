Statement

For every fixed treewidth bound k, proper three-colorability of finite graphs of treewidth at most k can be decided in time linear in the input graph size. Here a tree decomposition consists of a tree with vertex bags, covering all vertices and edges, such that the bags containing any given vertex form a connected subtree; width at most k means each bag has at most k+1 vertices.

Proof 1: Applying Courcelle’s theorem to the coloring formula (outline)

The property of a graph being colorable with three colors (represented by three sets of vertices R,G,B) may be defined by the monadic second-order formula

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\exists R\ \exists G\ \exists B\ {\Bigl (}&\forall v\ (v\in R\vee v\in G\vee v\in B)\wedge {}\\&\forall u\ \forall v\ {\bigl (}(u\in R\wedge v\in R)\rightarrow \lnot \operatorname {adj} (u,v){\bigr )}\wedge {}\\&\forall u\ \forall v\ {\bigl (}(u\in G\wedge v\in G)\rightarrow \lnot \operatorname {adj} (u,v){\bigr )}\wedge {}\\&\forall u\ \forall v\ {\bigl (}(u\in B\wedge v\in B)\rightarrow \lnot \operatorname {adj} (u,v){\bigr )}{\Bigr )}\end{aligned}}}

Here uppercase variables denote sets of vertices and lowercase variables denote individual vertices.

The first part of this formula ensures that the three color classes cover all the vertices of the graph, and the rest ensures that they each form an independent set. (It would also be possible to add clauses to the formula to ensure that the three color classes are disjoint, but this makes no difference to the result.)

Conversely, the color classes of a proper three-coloring satisfy the formula. Courcelle's theorem states that every fixed graph property definable in monadic second-order graph logic can be decided in linear time on graphs of any fixed bounded treewidth. Thus, by Courcelle's theorem, three-colorability of graphs of bounded treewidth may be tested in linear time.
