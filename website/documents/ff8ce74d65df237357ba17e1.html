Statement

A correspondence C:\Theta\rightrightarrows X assigns subsets of X to points of \Theta. It is upper hemicontinuous if for every \theta and open G\supseteq C(\theta), some neighborhood U of \theta satisfies C(\theta')\subseteq G for all \theta'\in U. It is lower hemicontinuous if for every open G meeting C(\theta), some such U has C(\theta')\cap G\ne\varnothing throughout U. Under the hypotheses that X,\Theta are topological spaces, f:X\times\Theta\to\mathbb R is continuous and C:\Theta\rightrightarrows X is upper and lower hemicontinuous with nonempty compact values, define m(\theta)=\min_{x\in C(\theta)}f(x,\theta) and N(\theta)=\{x\in C(\theta):f(x,\theta)=m(\theta)\}. Then m is continuous and N is upper hemicontinuous with nonempty compact values.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Basic topology and real analysis supply neighborhoods, compactness and extrema. The two kinds of hemicontinuity are explicitly defined set-valued analogues of continuity; the maximizer or minimizer set is then given directly. A short interval-valued example clarifies the additional conditions without further theory.
