Statement

Let K be a field of characteristic different from 3. Let a,b,c,d\in K, with a\ne0, and set f(x)=ax^3+bx^2+cx+d. Put p=(3ac-b^2)/(3a^2) and q=(2b^3-9abc+27a^2d)/(27a^3). The substitution x=t-b/(3a) gives f(t-b/(3a))=a(t^3+pt+q).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory algebra supplies polynomial factorization, multiplicity and field characteristic. An algebraic closure only supplies all polynomial roots here, and a primitive cube root is a nonidentity root of X cubed minus one. The displayed finite formulas need no Galois or extension-degree theory.

Proof 1: Translate the variable — outline

Let K be a field of characteristic different from 3. Let

{\displaystyle ax^{3}+bx^{2}+cx+d=0}

be a cubic equation. The change of variable

{\displaystyle x=t-{\frac {b}{3a}}}

gives a cubic (in t) that has no term in t2.

After dividing by a one gets the depressed cubic equation

{\displaystyle t^{3}+pt+q=0,}

with

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}t={}&x+{\frac {b}{3a}}\\p={}&{\frac {3ac-b^{2}}{3a^{2}}}\\q={}&{\frac {2b^{3}-9abc+27a^{2}d}{27a^{3}}}.\end{aligned}}}

The roots {\displaystyle x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}} of the original equation are related to the roots {\displaystyle t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}} of the depressed equation by the relations

{\displaystyle x_{i}=t_{i}-{\frac {b}{3a}},}

for {\displaystyle i=1,2,3}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Expand the translated cubic and collect coefficients. The calculation is elementary, but understanding an arbitrary field and its characteristic is introductory abstract algebra; characteristic not three ensures the denominators exist.
