Simpler proof assessment

Likely alternative prerequisite level: Undergraduate level; lowest recorded proof level: Beyond undergraduate level. Let S be the supremum of all finite rectangular sums, allowing infinity. Every partial row sum is bounded by the appropriate rectangular sums; taking successive suprema shows each iterated series equals S. Equivalently, any finite set of indices is contained in a rectangle and nonnegativity makes enlargement monotone. Both orders therefore agree even when divergent. Only nonnegative series and real suprema are needed, not product measure theory.

If a_{mn}\ge0 for all positive integers m,n, then \sum_m\sum_n a_{mn}=\sum_n\sum_m a_{mn} as values in [0,\infty].

Proof 1: Nonnegative double series can be summed in either order — outline

A special case of Tonelli's theorem is in the interchange of the summations,

\sum_m\sum_n a_{mn}=\sum_n\sum_m a_{mn},

where a_{mn} are non-negative for all m and n. Here integration is with respect to counting measure. The crux of the theorem is that the interchange of order of summation holds even if the series diverges. With all elements non-negative, there are no subsequences diverging to -\infty.

This is the source's specialization of Tonelli's theorem, whose proof is an external prerequisite.
