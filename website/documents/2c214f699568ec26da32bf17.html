Statement

Let K be a number field, let n\geq1 be an integer, and let S be a finite set of finite primes of K. For a finite prime \mathfrak p (a nonzero prime ideal in the ring of integers), write K_{\mathfrak p} for the completion. Define

K(n,S)=\{x\in K:x\in K_{\mathfrak p}^{n}\text{ for every }\mathfrak p\notin S\},

where F^n=\{y^n:y\in F\} for a field F.

For s\geq2, put \eta_s=\zeta_{2^s}+\zeta_{2^s}^{-1}, with compatible primitive 2^s-th roots of unity in an algebraic closure, and write i=\zeta_4. A field of characteristic zero is called s-special if it contains \eta_s but contains none of i, \eta_{s+1} and i\eta_{s+1}. Over \mathbb C, \eta_s=2\cos(2\pi/2^s). If K is s-special, let

S_0=\{\mathfrak p:K_{\mathfrak p}\text{ is }s\text{-special}\};

these are primes above 2.

Then K(n,S)=K^n unless there is an s\geq2 such that K is s-special, 2^{s+1}\mid n, and S_0\subseteq S. In this exceptional case the kernel of the natural map

K^\times/K^{\times n}\longrightarrow\prod_{\mathfrak p\notin S}K_{\mathfrak p}^\times/K_{\mathfrak p}^{\times n}

has order two and is generated by the class of \eta_{s+1}^{n}. Here F^\times is the multiplicative group of nonzero elements of F.
