Statement

Let G be a connected complex semisimple group, T\subset B a maximal torus in a Borel subgroup, and \lambda an integral weight of T dominant for the chosen positive roots. Let L_\lambda on G/B be the homogeneous holomorphic line bundle associated to the character -\lambda of B (extended trivially over its unipotent radical). If V_\lambda denotes the irreducible representation of highest weight \lambda, then

H^0(G/B,L_\lambda)\cong V_\lambda^*,\qquad H^i(G/B,L_\lambda)=0\quad(i>0).

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Proof 1: Specialize to the identity Weyl element

Use the Borel–Weil–Bott theorem with the stated line-bundle convention: if there is a Weyl element w for which w*\lambda=w(\lambda+\rho)-\rho is dominant, that element is unique and the only nonzero cohomology is in degree \ell(w), where it is V_{w*\lambda}^*. Here \rho is half the sum of positive roots and \ell is Weyl-group length. For dominant \lambda, choose w=e. Then e*\lambda=\lambda and \ell(e)=0, so the asserted degree-zero representation and higher-degree vanishing follow.

Level justification

The identity Weyl element makes the substitution immediate, but the full claim involves flag varieties, homogeneous line bundles, sheaf cohomology and highest-weight representations. This is a substantial combination of graduate geometry and representation theory.
