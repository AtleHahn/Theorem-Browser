Statement

Let A_1,\ldots,A_m be events in a probability space. Let N count those that occur, and put S_k=\sum_{J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\},\ |J|=k}\Pr(\cap_{j\in J}A_j), with S_0=1. Let V be a real or complex vector space and let c_j\in V. For a sequence c=(c_0,c_1,\ldots), define (Ec)_j=c_{j+1} and \Delta=E-I; thus (\Delta c)_j=c_{j+1}-c_j. Then \mathbb E[c_N]=\sum_{k=0}^mS_k(\Delta^kc)_0, where \mathbb E[c_N]=\sum_{n=0}^m\Pr(N=n)c_n is a finite sum.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Only finitely many events and their intersections occur, so expectation is an explicit finite probability-weighted sum. Polynomial multiplication and shifts/differences of sequences are given by formulas. Elementary finite probability and linear algebra suffice without measure-theoretic integration.

Proof 1: Take expectations of the finite pointwise identity

Let A_1,\ldots,A_m\subseteq\Omega, m\ge0, and put N(\omega)=\sum_{j=1}^m1_{A_j}(\omega) and N_k(\omega)=\sum_{J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\},\ |J|=k}1_{\cap_{j\in J}A_j}(\omega). An empty intersection is \Omega, so N_0=1. The combinatorial formula gives c_{N(\omega)}=\sum_{k=0}^mN_k(\omega)\sum_{l=0}^k(-1)^{k-l}\binom kl c_l. Since \Delta^k=(E-I)^k=\sum_{l=0}^k(-1)^{k-l}\binom kl E^l, the inner sum is (\Delta^kc)_0. Take expectations in this pointwise finite identity. Each \mathbb E[N_k]=S_k by the definition of N_k, giving the result.

Level justification

Expand finite differences by the binomial theorem and take finite expectations. Undergraduate linear algebra and probability suffice; the vector expectation is explicitly a finite sum.
