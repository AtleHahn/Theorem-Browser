Statement

Over Q, for variables x_1,…,x_n, the first n power sums p_k=Σ_i x_i^k are algebraically independent and generate the algebra of symmetric polynomials.

Level

Level justification

Polynomial evaluation, vector scaling and linear algebra are undergraduate concepts. Algebraic independence means no nonzero polynomial relation, and symmetric means invariant under variable permutations. These direct polynomial definitions suffice without algebraic geometry.

Proof 1: The first n power sums generate the symmetric polynomial algebra — outline

Write e_0=1 and let e_k be the sum of all distinct products of k distinct variables from x_1,\ldots,x_n. For a given n, the elementary symmetric polynomials e_k(x_1,\ldots,x_n) for k=1,\ldots,n form an algebraic basis for the algebra of symmetric polynomials over \mathbb Q: every polynomial expression in the x_i that is invariant under all permutations of those variables is given by a polynomial expression in those elementary symmetric polynomials, and this expression is unique up to equivalence of polynomial expressions. This is the fundamental theorem of symmetric polynomials, used here without proof.

Newton's identities,

ke_k=\sum_{i=1}^k(-1)^{i-1}e_{k-i}p_i,

also permit expressing the elementary symmetric polynomials in terms of the power sum symmetric polynomials, showing that any symmetric polynomial can also be expressed in the power sums. In fact the first n power sums also form an algebraic basis for the algebra of symmetric polynomials.

The source states this last consequence without a separate verification of algebraic independence.

Undergraduate polynomial rings suffice; algebraic independence here means absence of polynomial relations, a small extension. The source leaves its final independence verification implicit.
