Statement

The set A=\{1/n:n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\}\subset\mathbb C\setminus\{0\} is analytic in the punctured plane, but its closure A\cup\{0\} is not analytic in \mathbb C.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Real analysis and plane topology supply closure, isolated points and accumulation. In this one-complex-dimensional statement, the extra definitions are complex differentiability and a set being locally the common zero set of holomorphic functions. Linear functions give concrete local examples at each isolated point. These definitions are a small extension within about 30 minutes; analytic spaces in several variables and their structure theory are not part of the formulation.

Proof 1: Accumulate isolated points at the puncture — outline

The set of points (1/n,0) in the complex plane is analytic in the complex plane minus the origin, but its closure in the complex plane is not. Here (1/n,0) denotes the complex number 1/n, for n=1,2,\ldots.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

From real analysis and plane topology, add complex differentiability and the definition of an analytic subset as a set locally cut out by holomorphic functions. Near an isolated point 1/n the equation z-1/n=0 suffices, and away from A the equation 1=0 gives the empty set. At zero, every local defining function for the closure would have accumulating zeros, so the stated identity theorem would make it identically zero, forcing a whole neighborhood into the closure. This concrete one-dimensional use is a small extension; no several-variable analytic-space theory is needed.

Statement used:

A relatively closed discrete subset of an open subset of C is locally the zero set of a holomorphic function: use z-a near one of its isolated points and the constant 1 at a point outside the set.

Application in proof:

Apply to {1/n} in C minus {0}.

Application level:

Undergraduate level

Level justification:

This reduces to isolated points, neighborhoods and linear complex functions.

Review notes:

Relative closedness is required: mere isolatedness of each member would not suffice if the set had a missing accumulation point inside the domain. Here its sole finite accumulation point is outside the punctured plane.

Identity theorem for holomorphic functions
Kind:

Theorem

Treatment:

Implicitly assumed

Statement used:

A holomorphic function on a connected open subset of C with zeros accumulating at a point in that open set is identically zero.

Application in proof:

On a small disk about zero, every putative local defining function vanishing on A would vanish identically, contradicting the proposed proper zero set.

Application level:

Undergraduate level

Level justification:

To apply the stated theorem in this plane example requires only complex differentiability and the familiar notion of an accumulation point, not its complex-analytic proof.
