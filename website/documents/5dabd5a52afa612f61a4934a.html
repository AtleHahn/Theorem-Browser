Statement

For n≥1 and variables x_1,\ldots,x_n, let p_k=\sum_jx_j^k for k≥1, let e_0=1, and let e_k=\sum_{j_1<\cdots<j_k}x_{j_1}\cdots x_{j_k}, with e_k=0 for k>n. Let h_0=1 and h_k=\sum_{a_1+\cdots+a_n=k,\ a_j\ge0}x_1^{a_1}\cdots x_n^{a_n}. Over Q, for k≥0, h_k=\sum_{\sum_{i=1}^k i m_i=k}\prod_{i=1}^k\frac{p_i^{m_i}}{i^{m_i}m_i!}, where all m_i are nonnegative integers and the k=0 sum is 1.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All polynomial families and integer-index sums are explicitly defined and finite. Rational coefficients, factorials and the weighted multiplicity equation need elementary algebra, despite dense multi-index notation.

Proof 1: Induct on weighted multiplicities in the homogeneous recurrence outline

Use the recurrence mh_m=\sum_{i=1}^m h_{m-i}p_i, with h_0=1. In the following display, f(m;m_1,\ldots,m_l)=\prod_{i=1}^l(i^{m_i}m_i!)^{-1} for \sum i m_i=m; a term with a negative multiplicity is zero. The coefficient calculation treats the p_i as formal indeterminates before substituting the power sums.

It can be proved by considering the following inductive step:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}mf(m;m_{1},\ldots ,m_{l})&=f(m-1;m_{1}-1,\ldots ,m_{l})+\cdots +f(m-l;m_{1},\ldots ,m_{l}-1)\\m_{1}\prod _{i=1}^{l}{\frac {1}{i^{m_{i}}m_{i}!}}+\cdots +lm_{l}\prod _{i=1}^{l}{\frac {1}{i^{m_{i}}m_{i}!}}&=m\prod _{i=1}^{l}{\frac {1}{i^{m_{i}}m_{i}!}}\end{aligned}}}

Verify the proposed finite coefficient formula in the supplied recurrence by canceling factorials; the sum of i m_i is m. High-school polynomial arithmetic and induction suffice.
