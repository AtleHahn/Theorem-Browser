Statement

Let P>0 and let s be a complex P-periodic function integrable over one period. Put S[n]=P^{-1}\int_0^P s(x)e^{-2\pi inx/P}\,dx. With Fourier convention \mathcal Fh(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb R}h(x)e^{-2\pi i\xi x}\,dx interpreted for tempered distributions, \mathcal Fs=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}S[n]\delta(\xi-n/P).

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Tempered distributions, their Fourier transform and convergence of an infinite Dirac-mass expansion require a substantial distribution-theory framework. Proof 1: Invert the coefficient-weighted Dirac comb — outline

Use the Fourier series coefficients to modulate a Dirac comb:

S(\xi):=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}S[n]\delta\left(\xi-\frac nP\right),

where \xi represents a continuous frequency variable. The “teeth” of the comb are spaced at multiples of 1/P, the fundamental frequency. The function s(x) can be recovered from this representation by an inverse Fourier transform:

\begin{aligned} \mathcal F^{-1}\{S\}(x) &=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \left(\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}S[n]\delta\left(\xi-\frac nP\right)\right) e^{2\pi i\xi x}\,d\xi\\ &=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}S[n] \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\delta\left(\xi-\frac nP\right)e^{2\pi i\xi x}\,d\xi\\ &=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}S[n]e^{2\pi inx/P} =s(x). \end{aligned}

These sums, delta evaluations and the equality to the periodic function are understood in the sense of tempered distributions, as specified in the source's distribution convention. The Fourier integral of a periodic function need not converge as an ordinary integral.
