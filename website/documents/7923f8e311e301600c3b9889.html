Statement

Let G be a group. A composition series is a finite chain 1=H_0\triangleleft H_1\triangleleft\cdots\triangleleft H_n=G of strict inclusions in which every factor H_{j+1}/H_j is simple, equivalently every H_j is maximal proper normal in H_{j+1}. Any two composition series of G have the same length and the same factors up to permutation and group isomorphism.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory group theory supplies subgroups, quotients, actions, products and homomorphisms. Perfect means generated by commutators, torsion means finite element orders, and the chain conditions are explicit sequences of subgroups. The relevant one of these closely related definitions is a small extension, without representation theory.

Proof 1: Applying refinement to nonrefinable series (outline)

Use the Schreier refinement theorem: any two finite subnormal series of a group admit equivalent refinements, meaning refinements with the same factors up to permutation and isomorphism. A composition series admits no insertion of additional subgroups into its subnormal chain. Consequently applying the refinement theorem to two composition series leaves their nontrivial factors unchanged, and gives the required equivalence.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the supplied refinement theorem to the explicitly defined composition series, which have no proper intermediate normal insertions. Undergraduate normal subgroups, simple quotient groups and finite chains suffice; no proof of Schreier refinement is required.
