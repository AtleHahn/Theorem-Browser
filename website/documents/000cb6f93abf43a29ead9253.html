Statement

Let C_0 be any constant for which, for all finite independent zero-mean real random variables X_1,\ldots,X_n with \sigma_i^2=\mathbb EX_i^2>0 and \rho_i=\mathbb E|X_i|^3<\infty, the distribution function F_n of (\sum_iX_i)/(\sum_i\sigma_i^2)^{1/2} satisfies \sup_x|F_n(x)-\Phi(x)|\le C_0(\sum_i\sigma_i^2)^{-3/2}\sum_i\rho_i, where \Phi is the standard normal distribution function. Then C_0\ge(\sqrt{10}+3)/(6\sqrt{2\pi}).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The universal quantifier ranges over arbitrary independent real random variables with moment conditions and controls the distribution of their normalized sum relative to a normal law. Understanding expectation, independence, moments and cumulative distributions together is additional probability preparation beyond the stated baseline. The elementary restriction to iid variables does not establish that this full formulation fits 30 minutes; retain Beyond undergraduate conservatively. The cumulative prerequisite preparation cannot confidently fit approximately 30 minutes beyond the stipulated undergraduate baseline. Under the conservative threshold rule, the classification is therefore assessed as Beyond undergraduate level. Uncertainty about the exact preparation time is retained in this justification but no longer leaves the educational assessment provisional.

Proof 1: Restrict to identically distributed summands — outline

For identically distributed summands, the general error factor equals \rho_1/(\sigma_1^3\sqrt n). Esseen’s lower bound for any universal constant in this identically distributed inequality is C\ge(\sqrt{10}+3)/(6\sqrt{2\pi}).

Regarding C0, obviously, the lower bound established by Esseen (1956) remains valid:

{\displaystyle C_{0}\geq {\frac {{\sqrt {10}}+3}{6{\sqrt {2\pi }}}}=0.4097\ldots .}
