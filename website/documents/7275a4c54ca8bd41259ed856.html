Statement

Call a subset of \mathbb R^n semialgebraic if it is defined by a finite Boolean combination of real polynomial equalities and inequalities. Then it is a finite union of sets each specified by finitely many simultaneous conditions p(x)=0 and q(x)>0.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Semialgebraic is defined by finite Boolean combinations of polynomial signs. The conclusion is a finite logical normal form.

Proof 1: Put polynomial sign conditions in disjunctive normal form — outline

Put the defining Boolean formula in disjunctive normal form. Each disjunct is a finite conjunction of polynomial sign conditions. Standard algebraic sign manipulations replace weak inequalities, disequalities and negated comparisons by finite disjunctions of equalities and strict positivity conditions; distributing those finite disjunctions gives the required finite union.
