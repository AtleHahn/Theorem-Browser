Statement

Let X be a set. For R\subseteq X\times X, define R^c=(X\times X)\setminus R and R^{-1}=\{(y,x):(x,y)\in R\}. Relations with R\ne R^{-1} partition into four-element classes \{R,R^c,R^{-1},(R^{-1})^c\}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Injectivity/surjectivity on infinite sets or structural properties of arbitrary relations require introductory undergraduate set theory and discrete mathematics. A bright US college student should be able to understand the statement after about 30 minutes of focused background research; this rating concerns understanding the claim, not proving it.

Proof 1: Fourfold classes of nonsymmetric relations — outline

The nonsymmetric relations can be grouped into quadruples (relation, complement, inverse, inverse complement), namely

\{R,R^c,R^{-1},(R^{-1})^c\}.

The source supplies this grouping without the routine distinctness and partition checks.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply complement and inverse to arbitrary binary relations and check their four-element orbits; diagonal pairs exclude equality of a relation with the complement of its inverse. Undergraduate elementary set relations suffice, including the arbitrary-set partition argument left implicit in the outline.

Routine omissions

The two involutions commute. Distinctness follows from nonsymmetry and the diagonal, which prevents a relation from being the complement of its converse; these routine set checks are implicit in the supplied grouping recipe.
