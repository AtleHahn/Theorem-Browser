Statement

Propositions are finite Boolean expressions, AB denotes conjunction and \neg A negation. Write v(A\mid X) for a numerical plausibility assigned to A under information X; assume logically equivalent propositions and conditioning information give the same values whenever the expressions used are defined. Assume these values lie in [0,1] and satisfy v(AB\mid X)=v(A\mid X)v(B\mid AX) for the displayed conditioning expressions. Assume also a function f:[0,1]\to[0,1] represents negation: v(\neg A\mid X)=f(v(A\mid X)). Let B=\neg(AD), and put x=v(A\mid X)>0 and y=v(B\mid X)>0. Then x f\!\left(\frac{f(y)}x\right)=y f\!\left(\frac{f(x)}y\right). The assertion concerns these realized values, for which the displayed ratios are in the domain of f.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Finite Boolean expressions and the numerical product and negation rules are defined in the statement. The conclusion is a function identity at the specified positive realized values; understanding it needs substitution, division and elementary Boolean reasoning, not a general solution theory for functional equations.

Proof 1: A functional equation for realized complementary plausibilities

Because “a double negative is an affirmative”, consistency with logic gives

f(f(v(B\mid AX)))=v(B\mid AX).

The product and negation rules and consistency with logic imply that

v(AB\mid X)=v(A\mid X)f(v(\neg B\mid AX)) =v(A\mid X)f\left(\frac{v(A\neg B\mid X)}{v(A\mid X)}\right).

Since AB is logically equivalent to BA, we also get

v(A\mid X)f\left(\frac{v(A\neg B\mid X)}{v(A\mid X)}\right) =v(B\mid X)f\left(\frac{v(B\neg A\mid X)}{v(B\mid X)}\right).

If, in particular, B=\neg(AD), then also A\neg B=\neg B and B\neg A=\neg A and we get

v(A\neg B\mid X)=v(\neg B\mid X)=f(v(B\mid X))

and

v(B\neg A\mid X)=v(\neg A\mid X)=f(v(A\mid X)).

Abbreviating v(A\mid X)=x and v(B\mid X)=y we get the functional equation

x\,f\left(\frac{f(y)}x\right)=y\,f\left(\frac{f(x)}y\right).

Use double negation, commute conjunction and substitute B=not(AD); divide the assumed product identities by the positive realized values. This is Boolean algebra and elementary function substitution.
