Statement

Let V be an infinite-dimensional normed linear space, and let U\subset V be a bounded open set containing 0. Let F:\partial U\to V be a compact map, meaning a continuous map that sends bounded subsets to sets with compact closure. Suppose \|F(x)\|\ge\alpha for every x\in\partial U, for some constant \alpha>0. Then there exist x_0\in\partial U and a real number \lambda>0 such that x_0=\lambda F(x_0). The boundary and compactness are taken in the norm topology.

Proof 1: Finite-dimensional approximation and Brouwer degree (external)

The following editorial argument completes the stated scope. A supporting source is Birkhoff–Kellogg theorems on invariant directions for multimaps (Agarwal–O’Regan Theorem 2.13 and Remarks 2.14–2.15, pp.441–442; full arbitrary-domain finite-dimensional-degree bridge given below.).

The following editorial argument treats arbitrary bounded open domains using finite-dimensional degree. Uniformly approximate the compact boundary map F by continuous maps F_j with finite-dimensional range E_j, with sup_boundary ||F_j-F|| tending to zero and ||F_j||>=alpha/2. Construct these approximations by a finite partition of unity on a finite cover of the relatively compact image of F. Choose v_j outside E_j, possible because V is infinite-dimensional, and put W_j=E_j+R v_j (underlying real spaces if needed), D_j=U intersect W_j. This is a bounded finite-dimensional open neighborhood of zero, and its relative boundary lies in boundary U. Extend F_j restricted to boundary D_j continuously to a map H_j:closure D_j -> E_j by the finite-dimensional Tietze extension theorem. Set K_j(x)=H_j(x)+max(0,alpha/2-||H_j(x)||)v_j. Then K_j never vanishes, and equals F_j on boundary D_j. Since closure D_j is compact, min||K_j||>0. The Brouwer degree of x-lambda K_j(x) is 1 at lambda=0. For sufficiently large lambda it is 0: homotope to -lambda K_j without boundary zeros, using lambda min||K_j||>sup||x||, and -lambda K_j has no zero anywhere. Homotopy invariance therefore forces x_j=lambda_j F_j(x_j) for a boundary point x_j and lambda_j>0. Choose r,R with ball_r contained in U and U contained in ball_R. Boundary norms imply r/(sup||F||+1)<=lambda_j<=2R/alpha for large j. Compactness of F and uniform approximation give a subsequence with F_j(x_j) convergent in V and lambda_j converging to a positive lambda. Thus x_j converges to x in boundary U, continuity gives x=lambda F(x), and the conclusion follows. Completeness of V is not used.
