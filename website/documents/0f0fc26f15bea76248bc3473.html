Statement

If g,h are real differentiable functions near a and g(a)>0, then (g^h)'(a)=h(a)g(a)^{h(a)-1}g'(a)+g(a)^{h(a)}\log(g(a))h'(a).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Real derivatives; Positive real powers; Logarithms. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Differentiate the exponential of exponent times logarithm

On a neighborhood of a where g>0, use the following rule for the differentiable functions g,h.

The most general power rule is the functional power rule: for any functions {\textstyle g} and {\textstyle h},

{\displaystyle (g^{h})'=\left(e^{h\ln g}\right)'=g^{h}\left(g'{h \over g}+h'\ln g\right),\quad }

wherever both sides are well defined.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Positivity persists locally by continuity. Write g^h=exp(h log g) and apply the product and chain rules. Standard calculus suffices.

Proof 2: Use the two partial derivatives of the real power

For u>0, use the two-input chain rule, evaluated at (g(a),h(a)).

The case of exponentiation

{\displaystyle f(u,v)=u^{v}}

is slightly more complicated, as

{\displaystyle D_{1}f=vu^{v-1},}

and, as {\displaystyle u^{v}=e^{v\ln u},}

{\displaystyle D_{2}f=u^{v}\ln u.}

It follows that

{\displaystyle {\frac {d}{dx}}\left(g(x)^{h(x)}\right)=h(x)g(x)^{h(x)-1}{\frac {d}{dx}}g(x)+g(x)^{h(x)}\ln g(x)\,{\frac {d}{dx}}h(x).}

Level justification

Apply the two-variable chain rule to u^v on u>0 with the displayed partial derivatives. This is standard multivariable calculus.
