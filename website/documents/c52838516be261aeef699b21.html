Statement

Fix an integer d\ge1 and let \lambda_d denote Lebesgue measure. If E\subseteq\mathbb R^d is Lebesgue measurable and lies in the union of a family \mathcal F of positive-radius balls with uniformly bounded radii, some countable disjoint subfamily \mathcal G\subseteq\mathcal F satisfies \lambda_d(E)\leq5^d\sum_{C\in\mathcal G}\lambda_d(C).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement bounds the measure of a measurable set using a countable selection of balls.

Proof 1: Disjoint selection and volume scaling

Index the family as \{B_j:j\in J\}. Write 5B for the ball with the same center and five times the radius.

By the infinite Vitali covering lemma for bounded-radius ball families, we may choose a subcollection {\displaystyle \left\{B_{j}:j\in J'\right\}} which is disjoint and such that {\textstyle \bigcup _{j\in J'}5B_{j}\supset \bigcup _{j\in J}B_{j}\supset E}. Therefore,

{\displaystyle \lambda _{d}(E)\leq \lambda _{d}^{*}{\biggl (}\bigcup _{j\in J}B_{j}{\biggr )}\leq \lambda _{d}{\biggl (}\bigcup _{j\in J'}5B_{j}{\biggr )}\leq \sum _{j\in J'}\lambda _{d}(5B_{j}).}

Now, since increasing the radius of a d-dimensional ball by a factor of five increases its volume by a factor of 5d, we know that

{\displaystyle \sum _{j\in J'}\lambda _{d}(5B_{j})=5^{d}\sum _{j\in J'}\lambda _{d}(B_{j})}

and thus

{\displaystyle \lambda _{d}(E)\leq 5^{d}\sum _{j\in J'}\lambda _{d}(B_{j}).}

Here \lambda_d^* denotes outer measure; the arbitrary original union need not be measurable.
