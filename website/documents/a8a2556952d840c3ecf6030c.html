Statement

Fix R>0 and a positive integer N. There exist constants c,C>0 such that, for all integers n\ge N, c n^R\le\prod_{k=N}^{n}(1+R/k)\le C n^R.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement compares an explicitly given finite product with a real power for all positive integers, using positive constants and elementary inequalities. No limit or convergence concept occurs in the assertion. This rating concerns understanding the statement, independently of the difficulty of its proof.

Proof 1: Sum logarithms and exponentiate

The estimate \log(1+R/k)=R/k+O(k^{-2}) gives \log\prod_{k=N}^{n}(1+R/k)=R\sum_{k=N}^{n}1/k+O(1)=R\log n+O(1). Exponentiation gives the stated bounds.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Sum the logarithmic Taylor estimates, use the harmonic asymptotic and exponentiate the bounded error. Undergraduate real analysis suffices.
