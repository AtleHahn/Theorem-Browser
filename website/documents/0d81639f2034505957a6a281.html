Statement

For n\ge2, put A_1=m_1^2-\sum_{j=2}^n m_j^2, A_j=2m_1m_j for j\ge2, and C=\sum_{j=1}^n m_j^2. Positive integer parameters with m_1^2>\sum_{j=2}^n m_j^2 satisfy \sum A_j^2=C^2. Dividing by the common gcd gives every primitive positive solution exactly once when the parameters themselves are setwise coprime.

Proof 1: Source calculation — outline

The expression

\left(m_1^2-m_2^2-\cdots-m_n^2\right)^2 +\sum_{k=2}^n(2m_1m_k)^2 =\left(m_1^2+\cdots+m_n^2\right)^2

is a Pythagorean n-tuple for any tuple of positive integers (m_1,\ldots,m_n) with m_1^2>m_2^2+\cdots+m_n^2. The Pythagorean n-tuple can be made primitive by dividing out by the largest common divisor of its values.

Furthermore, any primitive Pythagorean n-tuple a_1^2+\cdots+a_n^2=c^2 can be found by this approach. Use

(m_1,\ldots,m_n)=(c+a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_n)

to get a Pythagorean n-tuple by the formula in the statement and divide out by the largest common integer divisor, which is 2m_1=2(c+a_1). Dividing out by the largest common divisor of these (m_1,\ldots,m_n) values gives the same primitive Pythagorean n-tuple; and there is a one-to-one correspondence between tuples of setwise coprime positive integers (m_1,\ldots,m_n) satisfying m_1^2>m_2^2+\cdots+m_n^2 and primitive Pythagorean n-tuples.

The source leaves the algebraic verification and recovery of the parameter ratios implicit.
