Statement

Let X_1,X_2,\ldots be positive real random variables and put P_n=\prod_{i=1}^n X_i. Suppose \sum_{i=1}^n\log X_i\Rightarrow Z, where Z\sim\mathcal N(m,v) for m\in\mathbb R and v>0. Then P_n\Rightarrow e^Z, the lognormal law defined as the exponential of that normal law.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

There is one scalar distributional convergence notion, readable through distribution-function limits, and normal/lognormal laws, the latter explicitly defined by exponentiation. These are related probability definitions extending undergraduate analysis.

Proof 1: Take logarithms and exponentiate the distributional limit

The logarithm of a product is the sum of the logarithms: \log P_n=\sum_{i=1}^n\log X_i. Thus the hypothesis gives a normal limit for \log P_n. Exponentiating, using continuity of the exponential under convergence in distribution, gives the lognormal limit for P_n.

Level justification

Write the product as the exponential of the sum of logarithms and apply the stated continuous mapping theorem. Undergraduate probability convergence and a continuous scalar transformation suffice.
