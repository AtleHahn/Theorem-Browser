Statement

Fix an integer n\geq1, write I_n for the identity matrix and

J=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_n\\-I_n&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad \mathrm{Sp}(2n,\mathbb R)=\{g\in\mathrm{GL}_{2n}(\mathbb R):g^TJg=J\}.

Every g\in\mathrm{Sp}(2n,\mathbb R) has a unique factorization g=kav with factors in the following sets (using u for the upper-triangular block):

K=\mathrm{Sp}(2n,\mathbb R)\cap\mathrm{SO}(2n) =\left\{\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\-B&A\end{pmatrix}:A+iB\in\mathrm U(n),\ A,B\in M_n(\mathbb R)\right\},A_0=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}D&0\\0&D^{-1}\end{pmatrix}:D\text{ is diagonal with positive real entries}\right\},N=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}u&M\\0&u^{-T}\end{pmatrix}:u\text{ is upper triangular with diagonal }1,\quad uM^T=Mu^T\right\}.

All blocks in these displays are real n-by-n matrices. More precisely, k\in K, a\in A_0 and v\in N. Multiplication K\times A_0\times N\to\mathrm{Sp}(2n,\mathbb R) is a real analytic diffeomorphism.

Here g^T is transpose, u^{-T}=(u^{-1})^T, M_n(\mathbb R) is the space of real n-by-n matrices, and \mathrm{GL} denotes invertible matrices. The groups \mathrm{SO}(2n)=\{Q:Q^TQ=I_{2n},\det Q=1\} and \mathrm U(n)=\{U:\overline U^TU=I_n\} are the special orthogonal and unitary groups. Real analytic diffeomorphism means the map and its inverse are smooth bijections locally given by convergent real power series in manifold coordinates.
