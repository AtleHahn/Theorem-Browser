Statement

Let B be a ball contained in an open set \Omega\subset\mathbb R^n, integer m\ge1, and \psi\in C_c^\infty(B) with \psi\ge0 and \int_B\psi=1. Multiindices have nonnegative integer components, \alpha!=\prod_i\alpha_i!, and x^\alpha=\prod_i x_i^{\alpha_i}. For x,y\in\mathbb R^n, define coefficients a_{\gamma,\beta} by (x-y)^\alpha=\sum_{\gamma+\beta=\alpha}a_{\gamma,\beta}x^\gamma y^\beta. The formula Q^m u(x)=\sum_{|\alpha|<m}\sum_{\gamma+\beta=\alpha}(-1)^{|\alpha|}a_{\gamma,\beta}x^\gamma\int_B u(y)D^\alpha[y^\beta\psi(y)]\,dy/\alpha! defines a polynomial of degree at most m-1 for every u\in L^1(\Omega), agrees with the averaged Taylor definition on W^{m-1,p}(\Omega) for 1\le p\le\infty, and depends only on u on B.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The extension and agreement use Lebesgue integrability and weak derivatives.

Proof 1: The integrated coefficients require only L1 regularity outline

For u\in W^{m-1,p}(\Omega), the averaged Taylor polynomial is

Q^m u(x)=\int_B T_y^m u(x)\psi(y)\,dy,\qquad T_y^m u(x)=\sum_{|\alpha|<m}\frac{D^\alpha u(y)}{\alpha!}(x-y)^\alpha.

Working out the integral, and using the weak derivatives of {\displaystyle u} and integration by parts, we obtain the following alternative forms

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}Q^{m}u(x)&=\sum \limits _{\vert \alpha \vert <m}\sum \limits _{\gamma +\beta =\alpha }{\frac {1}{\alpha !}}a_{\gamma ,\beta }x^{\gamma }\int _{B}D^{\alpha }u(y)y^{\beta }\psi (y)\operatorname {d} y\\&=\sum _{|\alpha |<m}\sum _{\gamma +\beta =\alpha }{\frac {(-1)^{\vert \alpha \vert }}{\alpha !}}a_{\gamma ,\beta }x^{\gamma }\int _{B}u(y)D^{\alpha }\left[y^{\beta }\psi (y)\right]\operatorname {d} y.\end{aligned}}}

The latter form allows relaxing of the smoothness requirements on {\displaystyle u} from being a member of {\displaystyle W^{m-1,p}} to that of merely being a member of {\displaystyle L^{1}(\Omega )}. While the averaged Taylor polynomial is not a pointwise construction like the classical Taylor polynomial it is nonetheless local to the ball {\displaystyle B} and it may be repeated as needed.

Level justification

The coefficient estimate itself uses bounded smooth functions against an integrable function, but agreement with the original construction requires Sobolev spaces, weak derivatives and integration against compactly supported test functions. The full argument’s measure-theoretic and weak-derivative framework exceeds a short extension of undergraduate analysis. The polynomial degree follows separately from |gamma|<=|alpha|<m.
