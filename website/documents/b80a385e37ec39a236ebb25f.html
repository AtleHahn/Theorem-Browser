Statement

For n≥1 and a_i,b_i∈C, (\sum_i|a_i|^2)(\sum_i|b_i|^2)-|\sum_i a_ib_i|^2=\sum_{i<j}|a_i\overline{b_j}-a_j\overline{b_i}|^2.

Proof 1: Compare quartic product terms and factor conjugate pairs

Let {\displaystyle a_{i},b_{i}\in \mathbb {C} } be complex numbers and the overbar represents complex conjugate.

Compare homogeneous degrees in the variables and their conjugates. The product identity

{\displaystyle \prod _{i=1}^{n}\left(1-a_{i}{\bar {a}}_{i}-b_{i}{\bar {b}}_{i}+a_{i}{\bar {a}}_{i}b_{i}{\bar {b}}_{i}\right)=\prod _{i=1}^{n}\left(1-a_{i}{\bar {a}}_{i}\right)\prod _{i=1}^{n}\left(1-b_{i}{\bar {b}}_{i}\right)}

reduces to the complex Lagrange's identity when fourth order terms, in a series expansion, are considered.

In order to prove it, expand the product on the LHS of the product identity in terms of series up to fourth order. To this end, recall that products of the form {\displaystyle \left(1+x_{i}\right)} can be expanded in terms of sums as

{\displaystyle \prod _{i=1}^{n}\left(1+x_{i}\right)=1+\sum _{i=1}^{n}x_{i}+\sum _{i<j}^{n}x_{i}x_{j}+{\mathcal {O}}^{3+}(x),}

where {\displaystyle {\mathcal {O}}^{3+}(x)} means terms with order three or higher in {\displaystyle x}.

{\displaystyle \prod _{i=1}^{n}\left(1-a_{i}{\bar {a}}_{i}-b_{i}{\bar {b}}_{i}+a_{i}{\bar {a}}_{i}b_{i}{\bar {b}}_{i}\right)=1-\sum _{i=1}^{n}\left(a_{i}{\bar {a}}_{i}+b_{i}{\bar {b}}_{i}\right)+\sum _{i=1}^{n}a_{i}{\bar {a}}_{i}b_{i}{\bar {b}}_{i}+\sum _{i<j}^{n}\left(a_{i}{\bar {a}}_{i}a_{j}{\bar {a}}_{j}+b_{i}{\bar {b}}_{i}b_{j}{\bar {b}}_{j}\right)+\sum _{i<j}^{n}\left(a_{i}{\bar {a}}_{i}b_{j}{\bar {b}}_{j}+a_{j}{\bar {a}}_{j}b_{i}{\bar {b}}_{i}\right)+{\mathcal {O}}^{5+}.}

The two factors on the RHS are also written in terms of series

{\displaystyle \prod _{i=1}^{n}\left(1-a_{i}{\bar {a}}_{i}\right)\prod _{i=1}^{n}\left(1-b_{i}{\bar {b}}_{i}\right)=\left(1-\sum _{i=1}^{n}a_{i}{\bar {a}}_{i}+\sum _{i<j}^{n}a_{i}{\bar {a}}_{i}a_{j}{\bar {a}}_{j}+{\mathcal {O}}^{5+}\right)\left(1-\sum _{i=1}^{n}b_{i}{\bar {b}}_{i}+\sum _{i<j}^{n}b_{i}{\bar {b}}_{i}b_{j}{\bar {b}}_{j}+{\mathcal {O}}^{5+}\right).}

The product of this expression up to fourth order is

{\displaystyle \prod _{i=1}^{n}\left(1-a_{i}{\bar {a}}_{i}\right)\prod _{i=1}^{n}\left(1-b_{i}{\bar {b}}_{i}\right)=1-\sum _{i=1}^{n}\left(a_{i}{\bar {a}}_{i}+b_{i}{\bar {b}}_{i}\right)+\left(\sum _{i=1}^{n}a_{i}{\bar {a}}_{i}\right)\left(\sum _{i=1}^{n}b_{i}{\bar {b}}_{i}\right)+\sum _{i<j}^{n}\left(a_{i}{\bar {a}}_{i}a_{j}{\bar {a}}_{j}+b_{i}{\bar {b}}_{i}b_{j}{\bar {b}}_{j}\right)+{\mathcal {O}}^{5+}.}

Substitution of these two results in the product identity give

{\displaystyle \sum _{i=1}^{n}a_{i}{\bar {a}}_{i}b_{i}{\bar {b}}_{i}+\sum _{i<j}^{n}\left(a_{i}{\bar {a}}_{i}b_{j}{\bar {b}}_{j}+a_{j}{\bar {a}}_{j}b_{i}{\bar {b}}_{i}\right)=\left(\sum _{i=1}^{n}a_{i}{\bar {a}}_{i}\right)\left(\sum _{i=1}^{n}b_{i}{\bar {b}}_{i}\right).}

The product of two conjugates series can be expressed as series involving the product of conjugate terms. The conjugate series product is

{\displaystyle \left(\sum _{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\right)\left(\sum _{i=1}^{n}{\bar {x}}_{i}\right)=\sum _{i=1}^{n}x_{i}{\bar {x}}_{i}+\sum _{i<j}^{n}\left(x_{i}{\bar {x}}_{j}+{\bar {x}}_{i}x_{j}\right),}

thus

{\displaystyle \left(\sum _{i=1}^{n}a_{i}b_{i}\right)\left(\sum _{i=1}^{n}{\overline {a_{i}b_{i}}}\right)-\sum _{i<j}^{n}\left(a_{i}b_{i}{\bar {a}}_{j}{\bar {b}}_{j}+{\bar {a}}_{i}{\bar {b}}_{i}a_{j}b_{j}\right)+\sum _{i<j}^{n}\left(a_{i}{\bar {a}}_{i}b_{j}{\bar {b}}_{j}+a_{j}{\bar {a}}_{j}b_{i}{\bar {b}}_{i}\right)=\left(\sum _{i=1}^{n}a_{i}{\bar {a}}_{i}\right)\left(\sum _{i=1}^{n}b_{i}{\bar {b}}_{i}\right).}

The terms of the last two series on the LHS are grouped as

{\displaystyle a_{i}{\bar {a}}_{i}b_{j}{\bar {b}}_{j}+a_{j}{\bar {a}}_{j}b_{i}{\bar {b}}_{i}-a_{i}b_{i}{\bar {a}}_{j}{\bar {b}}_{j}-{\bar {a}}_{i}{\bar {b}}_{i}a_{j}b_{j}=\left(a_{i}{\bar {b}}_{j}-a_{j}{\bar {b}}_{i}\right)\left({\bar {a}}_{i}b_{j}-{\bar {a}}_{j}b_{i}\right),}

in order to obtain the complex Lagrange's identity:

{\displaystyle \left(\sum _{i=1}^{n}a_{i}b_{i}\right)\left(\sum _{i=1}^{n}{\overline {a_{i}b_{i}}}\right)+\sum _{i<j}^{n}\left(a_{i}{\bar {b}}_{j}-a_{j}{\bar {b}}_{i}\right)\left({\overline {a_{i}{\bar {b}}_{j}-a_{j}{\bar {b}}_{i}}}\right)=\left(\sum _{i=1}^{n}a_{i}{\bar {a}}_{i}\right)\left(\sum _{i=1}^{n}b_{i}{\bar {b}}_{i}\right).}

In terms of the moduli,

{\displaystyle \left|\sum _{i=1}^{n}a_{i}b_{i}\right|^{2}+\sum _{i<j}^{n}\left|a_{i}{\bar {b}}_{j}-a_{j}{\bar {b}}_{i}\right|^{2}=\left(\sum _{i=1}^{n}\left|a_{i}\right|^{2}\right)\left(\sum _{i=1}^{n}\left|b_{i}\right|^{2}\right).}
Level
High school only
Level justification

Expand finite products, group terms by polynomial degree and factor conjugate pairs. All sums are finite and elementary complex arithmetic suffices; the order notation only abbreviates unused higher polynomial degrees.

Proof 2: Specialize Binet–Cauchy with conjugate entries

The Binet–Cauchy identity in two rows is (\sum a_ic_i)(\sum b_id_i)-(\sum a_id_i)(\sum b_ic_i)=\sum_{i<j}(a_ib_j-a_jb_i)(c_id_j-c_jd_i). First write the second input row as b_i'=\overline{b_i}, and take c_i=\overline{a_i}, d_i=b_i. Then the two norm-square factors are \sum|a_i|^2 and \sum|b_i|^2, the subtracted product is |\sum a_ib_i|^2, and the right-hand factors are the conjugate pair a_i\overline{b_j}-a_j\overline{b_i} and \overline{a_i}b_j-\overline{a_j}b_i. This is the stated identity.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Insert conjugate entries into the fully displayed polynomial identity and identify squared moduli. Elementary complex algebra suffices.
