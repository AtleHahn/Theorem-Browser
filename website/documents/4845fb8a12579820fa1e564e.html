Statement

For |\rho|<1, define

p_\rho(u,v)=\frac1{2\pi\sqrt{1-\rho^2}}\exp\!\left(-\frac{u^2+v^2-2\rho uv}{2(1-\rho^2)}\right).

For every integer n\ge1,

\partial_\rho^n p_\rho(u,v)=\partial_u^n\partial_v^n p_\rho(u,v).
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves explicit real exponential function; multivariable partial derivatives. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Compare Gaussian Fourier multipliers — outline

Put y_1=u, y_2=v, p=p_\rho and \Phi=\Phi_\rho. The joint characteristic function of the random variables {\displaystyle y_{1}} and {\displaystyle y_{2}} is by definition the integral

{\displaystyle \Phi (\omega _{1},\omega _{2})=\int _{-\infty }^{\infty }\int _{-\infty }^{\infty }p(y_{1},y_{2})e^{i(\omega _{1}y_{1}+\omega _{2}y_{2})}\,dy_{1}dy_{2}=\exp \left\{-{\frac {\omega _{1}^{2}+\omega _{2}^{2}+2\rho \omega _{1}\omega _{2}}{2}}\right\}}.

From the two-dimensional inversion formula of Fourier transform, it follows that

{\displaystyle p(y_{1},y_{2})={\frac {1}{4\pi ^{2}}}\int _{-\infty }^{\infty }\int _{-\infty }^{\infty }\Phi (\omega _{1},\omega _{2})e^{-i(\omega _{1}y_{1}+\omega _{2}y_{2})}\,d\omega _{1}d\omega _{2}={\frac {1}{4\pi ^{2}}}\int _{-\infty }^{\infty }\int _{-\infty }^{\infty }\exp \left\{-{\frac {\omega _{1}^{2}+\omega _{2}^{2}+2\rho \omega _{1}\omega _{2}}{2}}\right\}e^{-i(\omega _{1}y_{1}+\omega _{2}y_{2})}\,d\omega _{1}d\omega _{2}}.

Differentiation in \rho gives \partial_\rho^n\Phi_\rho=(-1)^n\omega_1^n\omega_2^n\Phi_\rho. The same multiplier is obtained by applying \partial_u^n\partial_v^n to the exponential in the inverse Fourier integral, since (-i)^{2n}=(-1)^n. Thus the two derivatives of p_\rho agree.
