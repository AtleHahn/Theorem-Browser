Statement

Let X_1,\ldots,X_n be independent centered real variables with finite variance and S_i=\sum_{j=1}^iX_j. For \lambda>0, \Pr(\max_{1\le i\le n}|S_i|\ge\lambda)\le\mathbb E[S_n^2]/\lambda^2.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The conclusion uses probabilities and expectations of functions of independent random variables; no martingale theorem is needed merely to understand it.

Proof 1: Use the partial-sum martingale and the second-moment bound

Use the filtration \mathcal F_j=\sigma(X_1,\ldots,X_j). If {\displaystyle X_{1},X_{2},\dots } is a sequence of real-valued independent random variables, each with mean zero, then for 1\leq j<n it is clear that:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\operatorname {E} \left[X_{1}+\cdots +X_{j}+X_{j+1}\mid X_{1},\ldots ,X_{j}\right]&=X_{1}+\cdots +X_{j}+\operatorname {E} \left[X_{j+1}\mid X_{1},\ldots ,X_{j}\right]\\&=X_{1}+\cdots +X_{j},\end{aligned}}}

Consequently, the partial sums {\displaystyle S_{j}=X_{1}+\dots +X_{j}} form a martingale. Note that Jensen's inequality implies that {\displaystyle |S_{j}|} is a nonnegative submartingale if {\displaystyle S_{j}} is a martingale. Hence, taking {\displaystyle p=2} in Doob's moment tail inequality gives:

{\displaystyle P\left[\max _{1\leq i\leq n}\left|S_{i}\right|\geq \lambda \right]\leq {\frac {\operatorname {E} \left[S_{n}^{2}\right]}{\lambda ^{2}}},}

which is precisely the statement of Kolmogorov's inequality.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Construct a martingale with its natural filtration, use conditional Jensen and invoke the Doob maximal bound. Conditional expectation with respect to sigma algebras and martingale inequalities form a substantial prerequisite framework.
