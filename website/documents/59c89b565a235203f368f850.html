Statement

Let T:E\to F be an open bounded linear map between Banach spaces. There exists C>0 such that for every y\in F there is x\in E with Tx=y and \|x\|\le C\|y\|.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Banach means complete normed vector space, a direct combination of linear algebra and metric completeness. Bounded linear maps and open maps have familiar norm/topological definitions; the claim adds no dual-space or spectral constructions.

Proof 1: Scale a neighborhood of zero

For some r>0, we have B_F(0,2)\subset T(B_E(0,r)), where B means an open ball. For nonzero y\in F, we then have

\frac{y}{\|y\|}=T\left(\frac{x}{\|y\|}\right)

for some \frac{x}{\|y\|} in B_E(0,r). That is, Tx=y with \|x\|<r\|y\|. For y=0, take x=0. Thus C=r suffices.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use openness at zero to put a target ball inside an image ball, then scale each nonzero target and handle zero separately. Undergraduate normed-space topology and linear algebra suffice; completeness is an unused hypothesis, not an additional theorem application.
