Statement

Let E be a real Riesz space with the principal projection property. For every e\in E with e\geq0 and every f in the principal ideal

I_e=\{x\in E: |x|\leq ce\text{ for some real }c\geq0\},

there are sequences (s_n) and (t_n) of e-simple elements such that s_n\leq s_{n+1}, t_n\geq t_{n+1}, and both sequences converge e-uniformly to f.

A real Riesz space is an ordered real vector space whose order is preserved by translation and multiplication by nonnegative scalars and in which every pair has a supremum x\vee y and infimum x\wedge y. Write |x|=x\vee(-x). Elements x,y are disjoint if |x|\wedge|y|=0. A component of e is an element 0\leq p\leq e with p\wedge(e-p)=0. An e-simple element is a finite real linear combination of pairwise disjoint components of e. A sequence (u_n) converges e-uniformly to f if for every real \varepsilon>0 there is N such that |u_n-f|\leq\varepsilon e for all n\geq N.

An order ideal is a vector subspace J such that y\in J and |x|\leq|y| imply x\in J. A band is an order ideal closed under suprema of its nonempty subsets of nonnegative elements whenever these suprema exist in E. The principal band generated by x is the smallest band containing x. The principal projection property means that for every principal band B, each element of E has a unique decomposition as a sum of an element of B and an element of B^\perp, where B^\perp=\{y\in E: |y|\wedge|b|=0\text{ for every }b\in B\}.
