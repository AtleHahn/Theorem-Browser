Statement

Let n\ge1 and m\ge1 be integers. Let V_1,\ldots,V_n be nonatomic probability measures on a circle. If a set A is a union of at most m arcs with V_i(A)=w for all i, then its complement, with endpoints assigned arbitrarily, is a union of at most m arcs with common value 1-w.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The conclusion concerns complements and common values of arbitrary nonatomic measures, requiring measure theory.

Proof 1: Complement the arcs and their common value — outline

Take C_{1-w}:=C\setminus C_w, where C is the circle and C_w=A. If C_w is a union of at most m intervals in a circle, then C_{1-w} is also a union of at most m intervals. The value measures are normalized such that all partners value the entire cake as 1, so V_i(C_{1-w})=1-w. Nonatomicity makes endpoint choices irrelevant.

Level justification

Complement the finitely many arcs and subtract their measures from one. The proof uses nonatomic probability measures on a circle, including null endpoint choices, rather than only continuous density integrals. Retain beyond undergraduate provisionally because the measure-theoretic prerequisites are not confidently a small extension.
