Statement

Every finite field F is quasi-algebraically closed: every homogeneous polynomial f\in F[X_1,\ldots,X_n] of positive degree d<n has a zero in F^n other than the origin.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory algebra supplies finite fields, polynomial evaluation, irreducibility and vector subspaces. The Mobius function, asymptotic notation or uniform finite counting probability is a limited numerical definition where used. These statements require no field-extension norm or Galois theory.

Proof 1: Apply Chevalley to one homogeneous polynomial — outline

Use Chevalley’s theorem: polynomials of total degree sum less than their number of variables, with no constant terms, have a common nonzero zero over a finite field. Apply it to the single homogeneous polynomial of degree d<n. Its positive degree implies its constant term is zero, so it has the required nonzero zero.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the supplied common-zero theorem to one positive-degree homogeneous polynomial. Quasi-algebraic closure is explicitly defined here; finite fields and polynomial degree are introductory abstract algebra.
