Statement

Let G be a finite group, p a prime, F a field of characteristic p, Q\le G a p-subgroup, and QC_G(Q)\le H\le N_G(Q), where C_G(Q) and N_G(Q) denote the centralizer and normalizer. Let \operatorname{Br}:Z(FG)\to Z(FH) be the Brauer map obtained by projecting a group-algebra element onto its terms supported on C_G(Q). If e_B is the identity of a block B of FG, then \operatorname{Br}(e_B) is an idempotent, possibly zero. If nonzero, it is a sum of mutually orthogonal primitive idempotents of Z(FH), each the identity of a block of FH.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding blocks of modular group algebras, their central primitive idempotents and the Brauer map requires several layers of representation theory beyond a short extension of undergraduate group theory. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Ring homomorphism and primitive decomposition — outline

The Brauer homomorphism is a ring homomorphism. Since it is a ring homomorphism, for any block B of FG, the Brauer homomorphism sends the identity element of B to an idempotent element (possibly to 0). If the idempotent is non-zero, it may be decomposed as a sum of (mutually orthogonal) primitive idempotents of Z(FH). Each of these primitive idempotents is the multiplicative identity of some block of FH.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The elementary identity Br(e)^2=Br(e) occurs inside modular group algebras and block theory. Brauer projection, centers and primitive central idempotents require substantial additional algebra beyond introductory rings.
