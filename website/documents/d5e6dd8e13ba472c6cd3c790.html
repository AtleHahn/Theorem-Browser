Statement

Let f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb C and put h(x)=f(-\log x) for x>0. Suppose \varphi(s)=\int_0^\infty x^{s-1}h(x)\,dx converges absolutely in a strip a<\operatorname{Re}s<b. Suppose also that the Mellin inversion identity h(x)=(2\pi i)^{-1}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}x^{-s}\varphi(s)\,ds is valid for x>0 and some c\in(a,b), with the indicated improper-integral interpretation. Then the bilateral Laplace transform \mathcal B f(s)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-st}f(t)\,dt equals \varphi(s) in that strip, and f(t)=(2\pi i)^{-1}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}e^{st}\varphi(s)\,ds for t\in\mathbb R.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Calculus supplies substitution and improper integrals. The complex exponential and parametrized vertical-line integral extend these directly, and both transform and inversion formulas are supplied as hypotheses. No independent transform or distribution theory is assumed for the implication.

Proof 1: Transfer Mellin inversion by the logarithmic substitution

Since the two-sided Laplace transform can be defined as

\{\mathcal Bf\}(s)=\{\mathcal M[f(-\ln x)]\}(s)=\{\mathcal Mh\}(s),

the assumed Mellin inversion identity can be immediately applied to it also, with x=e^{-t}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Substitute x=exp(-t) in the absolutely convergent improper integral and in the assumed inversion formula. Undergraduate complex integration suffices; no Mellin inversion proof or distribution theory is needed.
