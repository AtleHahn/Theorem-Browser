Statement

Let \Omega\subset\mathbb R^n be a nonempty bounded open set, u(x)=a\cdot x+b with a\in\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\} and b\in\mathbb R, and let m\ge2 be an integer. For any 1\le p,q,r\le\infty and 0<\theta\le1, no finite constant C can make

\|\nabla u\|_{L^p(\Omega)}\le C\|D^m u\|_{L^r(\Omega)}^\theta\|u\|_{L^q(\Omega)}^{1-\theta}

true. Here D^m denotes all derivatives of order m.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Multivariable calculus supplies affine functions and their ordinary partial derivatives. The additional Lp notation here means the pth root of the integral of the pth power of the absolute value, with the essential maximum for p=infinity. For these bounded continuous functions on a bounded open set, those concrete norm definitions and volume suffice within about 30 minutes. No weak derivatives or Sobolev spaces occur in the formulation.

Proof 1: Use a nonconstant affine function

Since \Omega is bounded and has finite Lebesgue measure, any affine function belongs to L^p(\Omega) for every p (including p=+\infty). Of course, it holds much more: affine functions belong to C^\infty(\Omega) and all their derivatives of order greater than or equal to two are identically equal to zero in \Omega.

The homogeneous Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality fails to be true for any nonconstant affine function on \Omega, since a contradiction is immediately achieved when j=1 and m\geq2: its gradient is the nonzero constant a, so the left side is positive, while the right side is zero. It therefore cannot hold in general for integrable functions defined on bounded domains.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The argument uses only an affine function and its derivatives: the gradient is a nonzero constant and all derivatives of order at least two vanish. The needed Lp facts reduce to the explicit norm of a constant and boundedness on a bounded domain. Learning these norm formulas is a small extension of undergraduate multivariable integration; no weak derivatives or Sobolev theory are used.
