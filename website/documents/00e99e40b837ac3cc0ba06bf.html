Statement

Let \alpha\in\mathbb R and let p,q be positive integers. If |\alpha-p/q|<1/(2q^2), then the rational value p/q is a convergent of the simple continued fraction of \alpha. Here [a_0;a_1,\ldots] denotes a simple continued fraction with integer a_0 and positive integer later coefficients; its convergents are its successive finite truncations.

Level justification

Proof 1: Append a complete quotient exceeding one — outline

Use the reduced representation of the rational value, again denoted p/q: reducing its denominator only enlarges the allowed error bound. If \alpha=p/q, this is the terminal convergent of a finite expansion.

Suppose α, p, q are such that {\displaystyle \left|\alpha -{\frac {p}{q}}\right|<{\frac {1}{2q^{2}}}}, and assume that α > p/q. Then we may write {\displaystyle \alpha -{\frac {p}{q}}={\frac {\theta }{q^{2}}}}, where 0 < θ < 1/2. We write p/q as a finite continued fraction [a0; a1, ..., an], where due to the fact that each rational number has two distinct representations as finite continued fractions differing in length by one (namely, one where an = 1 and one where an ≠ 1), we may choose n to be even. (In the case where α < p/q, put \theta=(p/q-\alpha)q^2 and choose n to be odd; the calculation below then has the corresponding minus signs.) Let p0/q0, ..., pn/qn = p/q be the convergents of this continued fraction expansion. When n=0, use the standard predecessor conventions p_{-1}=1, q_{-1}=0. Set {\displaystyle \omega :={\frac {1}{\theta }}-{\frac {q_{n-1}}{q_{n}}}}, so that {\displaystyle \theta ={\frac {q_{n}}{q_{n-1}+\omega q_{n}}}} and thus,

{\displaystyle \alpha ={\frac {p}{q}}+{\frac {\theta }{q^{2}}}={\frac {p_{n}}{q_{n}}}+{\frac {1}{q_{n}(q_{n-1}+\omega q_{n})}}={\frac {(p_{n}q_{n-1}+1)+\omega p_{n}q_{n}}{q_{n}(q_{n-1}+\omega q_{n})}}={\frac {p_{n-1}q_{n}+\omega p_{n}q_{n}}{q_{n}(q_{n-1}+\omega q_{n})}}={\frac {p_{n-1}+\omega p_{n}}{q_{n-1}+\omega q_{n}}},}

where we have used the fact that pn−1 qn - pn qn−1 = (-1)n and that n is even. Now, this equation implies that α = [a0; a1, ..., an, ω]. Since q_{n-1}/q_n\le1, the fact that 0 < θ < 1/2 implies that ω > 1, we conclude that the continued fraction expansion of α must be [a0; a1, ..., an, b0, b1, ...], where [b0; b1, ...] is the continued fraction expansion of ω, and therefore that pn/qn = p/q is a convergent of the continued fraction of α.
