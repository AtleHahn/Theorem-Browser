Statement

Let a Euclidean circle have center C and radius r>0, and let a line be specified by distinct points A,B, with C not on AB. Using a fixed-aperture compass, one can construct exactly the intersection points of the line and circle, or determine that there are none.

Proof 1: Reflect the circle across the line

Write C(r) for the circle with center C and radius r. Use the compass reflection construction: the reflection of C across AB is the other intersection of the circles centered at A and B through C. A fixed-aperture compass transfers the given radius.

  1. Construct the point D, which is the reflection of point C across line AB. Under the assumption of this case, C\ne D.
  2. Construct a circle D(r).
  3. The intersections of circle C(r) and the new circle D(r) are points P and Q. If the two circles are externally tangential then P=Q. If the two circles do not intersect then neither does the circle with the line.
  4. Points P and Q are the intersection points of circle C(r) and the line AB. If P=Q then the line is tangential to the circle C(r).

Indeed, common points of the two equal circles are equidistant from C,D and hence lie on their perpendicular bisector AB; conversely every point of AB on the first circle is on the second.

Proof 2: Invert the line in the given circle — outline

Use the compass constructions of point inversion and of the center of a circle through three noncollinear points. Write C(r) for the given circle and E(C) for the circle centered at E through C.

  1. Invert points A and B in circle C(r) to points A' and B' respectively. Under the assumption of this case, points A',B',C are not collinear.
  2. Find the center E of the circle passing through points C,A',B'.
  3. Construct circle E(C), which represents the inversion of the line AB in circle C(r).
  4. P and Q are the intersection points of circles C(r) and E(C). If the two circles are internally tangential then P=Q, and the line is also tangential.

Inversion fixes every point of its inversion circle, so these are exactly the required intersections, with none when the circles are disjoint.
