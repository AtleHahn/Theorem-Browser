Statement

Let V:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R be a continuous T-periodic potential, T>0, and fix E\in\mathbb C. Let u,v solve -y''+Vy=Ey with u(0)=1,u'(0)=0,v(0)=0,v'(0)=1. There are constants A,B,C,D\in\mathbb C such that u(x+T)=Au(x)+Bv(x) and v(x+T)=Cu(x)+Dv(x) for every x\in\mathbb R.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Second-order linear ODEs; Solution bases; Complex matrices. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Expand the translated solutions in the same basis

Because the potential is periodic, superposition implies that there exist constants A,B,C,D with

u(x+T)=Au(x)+Bv(x),\qquad v(x+T)=Cu(x)+Dv(x).

In matrix form,

\begin{pmatrix}u(x+T)\\v(x+T)\end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\C&D\end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix}u(x)\\v(x)\end{pmatrix},

expressing the monodromy of the fundamental solution. The matrix here is called the monodromy matrix.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Periodicity makes each shifted solution another solution, so expand in the normalized two-dimensional solution basis. Undergraduate linear ODE theory and linear algebra suffice.
