Statement

Let P,Q be probability measures on a measurable space (\mathcal X,\mathcal F). Write \mu=P+Q, p=dP/d\mu, q=dQ/d\mu. Put D=D_{\rm KL}(P\parallel Q)=\int\log(dP/dQ)\,dP if P\ll Q, and D=+\infty otherwise; use e^{-\infty}=0. Put a=\int_{\mathcal X}\sqrt{pq}\,d\mu. Then a^2\ge e^{-D}.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement uses Radon–Nikodym derivatives and relative entropy for general probability measures.

Proof 1: Jensen inequality for the logarithm — outline

If D=+\infty, the asserted lower bound is zero and is immediate. Assume D<+\infty. We write (\cdot)^2=\exp(2\log(\cdot)) and apply Jensen's inequality:

\begin{aligned} a^2&=\exp\left(2\log\int\sqrt{pq}\,d\mu\right)\\ &=\exp\left(2\log\int p\sqrt{q/p}\,d\mu\right)\\ &=\exp\left(2\log\mathbb E_P[(\sqrt{p/q})^{-1}]\right)\\ &\ge\exp\left(\mathbb E_P[-\log(p/q)]\right)=e^{-D}. \end{aligned}

Ratios in the expectations are evaluated P-almost everywhere.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The numerical Jensen step is short, but this proof works with arbitrary probability measures, Radon–Nikodym derivatives, absolute continuity, almost-everywhere ratios and extended relative entropy. Understanding and checking those objects and integrability qualifications requires measure-theoretic probability, substantially more than a small addition to introductory real analysis.
