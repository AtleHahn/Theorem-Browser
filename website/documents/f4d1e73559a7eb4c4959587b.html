Statement

There is a linear isometric embedding i:C([0,1],\mathbb R)\to C([0,1],\mathbb R) whose image contains no nonzero function differentiable at any point of [0,1].

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Real analysis supplies continuous functions, uniform bounds and norm convergence; linear algebra supplies linearity and finite dimension. The continuous-function norm is the supremum norm, and an isometry preserves it. These direct normed-space definitions suffice without dual or spectral theory.

Proof 1: Apply the strengthened theorem to the continuous-function space

Use Rodríguez-Piazza’s theorem, as stated in the source: every real separable Banach space X has a linear isometric embedding i:X\to C([0,1],\mathbb R) for which every nonzero image function is nowhere differentiable. This conclusion applies to the space C([0,1],\mathbb R) itself, yielding the stated self-embedding.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the supplied isometric-embedding theorem to the concrete continuous-function space. Undergraduate real analysis and normed-space definitions suffice: completeness is uniform-limit completeness, separability is countable approximation, and linear isometry means preservation of the supremum norm. No construction of the embedding or duality is required.
