Statement

Let Q be a positive definite quadratic form with rational coefficients in n\ge1 variables: Q(x)>0 for every nonzero x\in\mathbb R^n. Then its rational zero set is \{0\} and contains no rational vector subspace of positive dimension. Thus Birch’s conclusion about positive-dimensional common zero subspaces cannot be extended to all even-degree forms.

Proof 1: Use positive definiteness — outline

The restriction to odd degree is necessary: even-degree forms, such as positive definite quadratic forms, may take the value zero only at the origin. Such a zero set cannot contain a vector subspace of positive dimension.

Level justification

Apply the explicitly defined positive definiteness to rule out every nonzero real and hence rational zero, then exclude a positive-dimensional subspace. Undergraduate quadratic forms and vector spaces suffice.
