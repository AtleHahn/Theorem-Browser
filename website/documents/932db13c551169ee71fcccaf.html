Likely alternative prerequisite level: Undergraduate level; lowest recorded proof level: Beyond undergraduate level. Milnor's checked analytic proof establishes the nonexistence of a nonzero tangent field on every even-dimensional sphere using multivariable calculus and a volume-polynomial argument. It covers the smooth fields and all odd ambient n>=3 in this statement. The new ingredients are ordinary derivatives, determinants and change of variables rather than local index or homology. The candidate is undergraduate, not high school.

Statement

For odd integers n\geq3, the sphere S^{n-1} admits no smooth nowhere-zero tangent vector field. Here S^{n-1}=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:|x|=1\}, and tangency means perpendicularity to x.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Euclidean unit spheres; smooth vector-valued functions; perpendicular tangent vectors; nowhere-zero functions. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Euler characteristic contradicts an empty zero set outline

Use Poincaré–Hopf: on a compact manifold without boundary, a smooth tangent vector field with isolated zeros has total zero index equal to the manifold's Euler characteristic. A nowhere-zero field has empty zero set and total index zero. But n-1 is even, so \chi(S^{n-1})=2. These are incompatible, proving that a zero must exist.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The actual contradiction uses the manifold Euler characteristic and total local zero index from Poincare-Hopf. Understanding these invariants requires differential/algebraic topology even with an empty zero set.
