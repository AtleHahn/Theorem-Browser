Statement

Let X be a Banach space, r>0, 0<c<1, and h:B(0,r)→X with h(0)=0 and Lipschitz constant at most c. For f=I+h, B(0,(1-c)r)\subset f(B(0,r))\subset B(0,(1+c)r).

Level justification

Linear algebra and analysis supply bilinearity, norms, derivatives, sequences and closure. Banach completeness or boundedness is a direct metric condition, and the maps are explicitly given. No weak topology, duality or spectral theory is needed to understand these assertions.

Proof 1: Ball inclusions for an identity plus a contraction — outline

We show f(B(0,r))\supset B(0,(1-c)r). The idea is to note that this is equivalent to, given a point y in B(0,(1-c)r), finding a fixed point of the map

F:\overline B(0,r')\to\overline B(0,r'),\qquad x\mapsto y-h(x),

where 0<r'<r is such that \|y\|\leq(1-c)r' and the bar means a closed ball. To find a fixed point, we use the contraction mapping theorem, and checking that F is a well-defined strict-contraction mapping is straightforward. In particular,

\|F(x)\|\leq\|y\|+c\|x\|\leq r'.

Finally, we have f(B(0,r))\subset B(0,(1+c)r) since

\|f(x)\|=\|x+h(x)-h(0)\|\leq(1+c)\|x\|<(1+c)r

for x\in B(0,r).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

For the inner inclusion choose a smaller closed ball invariant under x to y-h(x), then apply the contraction theorem. The outer inclusion is a triangle estimate. Complete normed spaces and Lipschitz maps are a small extension of metric analysis and linear algebra.
