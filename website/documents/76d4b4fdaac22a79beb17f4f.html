Statement

Let f:[1,\infty)\to[0,\infty) be non-increasing, and assume g(u)=2^u f(2^u) is monotone on [0,\infty). Then \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f(n) converges if and only if \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^k f(2^k) converges.

Proof 1: An exponential substitution between two integral tests

The integral test for convergence gives us, in the case of nonnegative non-increasing f, that \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f(n) converges if and only if

\int_1^\infty f(x)\,dx

converges. The substitution x=2^u yields the integral

(\log2)\int_0^\infty2^uf(2^u)\,du.

If we also have that the function u\mapsto2^uf(2^u) is monotone, the integral test applies again and gives the equivalence with convergence of the condensed series

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}2^nf(2^n).

For a nonnegative nondecreasing transformed function, both its integral and its series diverge unless the function is identically zero, when both converge.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the integral test, substitute x=2^u, then apply the test to the monotone transformed function. The nondecreasing nonzero case gives simultaneous divergence. These are standard improper-integral and series techniques.
