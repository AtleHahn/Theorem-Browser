Statement

Fix a real \lambda>0. For each positive integer n\ge\lambda, let S_n have binomial distribution with n trials and success probability \lambda/n. Then

\sum_{k=0}^\infty\left|\Pr(S_n=k)-e^{-\lambda}\frac{\lambda^k}{k!}\right|<\frac{2\lambda^2}{n},

so this sum tends to zero as n\to\infty.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The binomial distribution comes from finitely many independent Bernoulli trials, with explicit binomial probabilities. The Poisson probabilities are displayed as a factorial series. Elementary probability and convergence suffice for the quantitative comparison.

Proof 1: Equal-probability specialization of Le Cam’s bound

Fix \lambda>0. For each integer n\ge\lambda, take n independent Bernoulli variables with common success probability p_i=\lambda/n; their sum has the binomial distribution of S_n. Le Cam's inequality for independent Bernoulli variables with positive total mean states

\sum_{k\ge0}\left|\Pr(S_n=k)-e^{-\sum_i p_i}\frac{(\sum_i p_i)^k}{k!}\right|<2\sum_{i=1}^n p_i^2.

Here \sum_i p_i=\lambda and 2\sum_i p_i^2=2n(\lambda/n)^2=2\lambda^2/n. The latter tends to zero, giving the claimed convergence.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Represent the binomial as a sum of equal-probability Bernoulli variables and substitute their mean and square sum in the explicit Le Cam bound. Undergraduate discrete probability and elementary limits suffice.
