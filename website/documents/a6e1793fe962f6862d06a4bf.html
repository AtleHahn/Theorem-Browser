Statement

Let X be a real random variable on a probability space with Lebesgue density f_X, and let A be an event. Write q(x)=P(A\mid X=x) for a measurable version of the conditional probability. Then P(A)=\int_{\mathbb R}q(x)f_X(x)\,dx. In particular for a real random variable Y and Borel set B, one may take A=\{Y\in B\}.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The formula involves conditional probabilities on zero-probability level sets, interpreted through measurable versions, and measure-theoretic integration.

Proof 1: Express the distribution measure using its density

The law of total probability states

P(A)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}P(A\mid X=x)\,dF_X(x),

where F_X is the distribution function of X. If X admits a density function f_X, then the result is

P(A)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}P(A\mid X=x)f_X(x)\,dx.

Moreover, for the specific case where A=\{Y\in B\}, where B is a Borel set, this yields

P(Y\in B)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}P(Y\in B\mid X=x)f_X(x)\,dx.

The conditional probabilities are the measurable versions specified in the statement; the continuous law of total probability is an external prerequisite.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Use total probability for measurable conditional-probability versions and replace the distribution measure by its Lebesgue density. Conditional probabilities on continuous fibers and measure integration require advanced probability.
