Proof 1: The only homology trace is one

The Lefschetz fixed-point theorem says, in particular, that if the Lefschetz number

L(f)=\sum_n(-1)^n\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(f_*:H_n(B;\mathbb Q)\to H_n(B;\mathbb Q)\bigr)

of a continuous self-map f of a finite simplicial complex B is nonzero, then f must have a fixed point.

If B is a ball (or more generally is nonempty and contractible) then the Lefschetz number is one because the only non-zero simplicial homology group is H_0(B;\mathbb Q)\cong\mathbb Q and f acts as the identity on this group, so f has a fixed point.
