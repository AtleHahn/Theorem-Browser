Statement

Let \Omega\subset\mathbb R^n be a bounded region with smooth boundary and outward unit normal \nu. For a smooth real function f near \overline\Omega, \int_\Omega\nabla f\,dV=\int_{\partial\Omega}f\nu\,dS.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Gradient; Vector-valued integrals; Outward normals. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Test against arbitrary constant vectors

Write \vec n=\nu for the outward unit normal; volume and surface measures are understood in the following integrals. Let {\displaystyle {\vec {c}}} be a constant vector. Then

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}0&=\int _{\Omega }{\vec {\nabla }}\cdot {\vec {c}}f-\int _{\partial \Omega }{\vec {n}}\cdot {\vec {c}}f&{\text{by the divergence theorem}}\\&=\int _{\Omega }{\vec {c}}\cdot {\vec {\nabla }}f-\int _{\partial \Omega }{\vec {c}}\cdot {\vec {n}}f\\&={\vec {c}}\cdot \int _{\Omega }{\vec {\nabla }}f-{\vec {c}}\cdot \int _{\partial \Omega }{\vec {n}}f\\&={\vec {c}}\cdot \left(\int _{\Omega }{\vec {\nabla }}f-\int _{\partial \Omega }{\vec {n}}f\right)\end{aligned}}}

Since this holds for any {\displaystyle {\vec {c}}} (in particular, for every basis vector), the result follows.
