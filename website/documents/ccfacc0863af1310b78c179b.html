Statement

Let D=\{|z|<1\} and D^*=\{|z|>1\}\cup\{\infty\}, and let f:S^1\to S^1 be a smooth orientation-preserving diffeomorphism. Let W be the complex vector space of pairs (a,b) holomorphic on D and D^* respectively, with smooth boundary values satisfying a(\zeta)=b(f(\zeta)) on S^1 and b(\infty)=0. If W is finite-dimensional, then W=\{(0,0)\}.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Holomorphic boundary-value spaces exceed calculus and basic linear algebra.

Proof 1: Positive powers contradict finite dimensionality — outline

A nonzero element of W would consist of a pair of holomorphic functions on D and D^* which have smooth boundary values on the circle related by f. Since the holomorphic function on D^* vanishes at \infty, the positive powers of this pair also provide solutions, which are linearly independent, contradicting the fact that W is finite-dimensional.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The power argument depends on holomorphic functions on the disk and exterior including infinity, smooth welded boundary values, the maximum principle and open mapping. Those linked complex-analysis concepts exceed a small extension even though linear independence is elementary algebra.
