Statement

Let M be a smooth Riemannian surface and let p be an interior point. For sufficiently small r>0, let C(r) be the Riemannian length of the geodesic circle \{q:d_M(p,q)=r\} and A(r) the Riemannian area of the geodesic disk \{q:d_M(p,q)<r\}. Here d_M is distance measured by infima of lengths of curves on the surface. If K(p) is the Gaussian curvature at p, then

K(p)=\lim_{r\downarrow0}\frac{3(2\pi r-C(r))}{\pi r^3}=\lim_{r\downarrow0}\frac{12(\pi r^2-A(r))}{\pi r^4}.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Geodesic distance and curvature on an arbitrary Riemannian surface require differential geometry beyond the baseline; familiar limit expressions do not remove that burden.
