Statement

Let K be a complete non-archimedean valued field and let K^{\mathrm{sep}} be a separable closure with the extended absolute value. Let \alpha,\beta\in K^{\mathrm{sep}}, and let \alpha=\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\ldots,\alpha_n be the distinct conjugates of \alpha over K. If |\alpha-\beta|<|\alpha-\alpha_i| for i=2,\ldots,n, then K(\alpha)\subseteq K(\beta).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Valued fields and separable algebraic extensions are beyond undergraduate calculus and linear algebra.

Proof 1: Take a degree-one comparison polynomial — outline

Use the source’s generalized Krasner theorem: for a monic f^*=\prod_{k=1}^n(X-\alpha_k^*) over a Henselian field, a nonempty partition I\sqcup J=\{1,\ldots,n\}, and g=\prod_{i\in I}(X-\alpha_i) over the algebraic closure, if v(\alpha_i-\alpha_i^*)>v(\alpha_i^*-\alpha_j^*) for every i\in I,j\in J, then the coefficients of \prod_{i\in I}(X-\alpha_i^*) lie in the field generated by the coefficients of g. Apply its degree-one-g case: take f^* to be the minimal polynomial of \alpha, take I=\{1\} with \alpha_1^*=\alpha, and put g=X-\beta. Using the additive valuation v=-\log|\cdot| (with v(0)=+\infty), the hypothesis is exactly the displayed closeness condition, and the conclusion says \alpha\in K(\beta). Thus K(\alpha)\subseteq K(\beta). If \alpha\in K, the inclusion is immediate.
