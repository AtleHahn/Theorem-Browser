Statement

Let ABC be a nondegenerate Euclidean triangle, with opposite side lengths a,b,c, respective interior angles \alpha,\beta,\gamma, semiperimeter s=(a+b+c)/2, and inradius r>0. Then r=\sqrt{\frac{(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}s}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The result concerns lengths, square roots and elementary geometry; the proof uses the displayed cotangent addition formula and algebra.

Proof 1: Incircle and half-angle calculation

The points of tangency of the incircle with the sides of the triangle break the perimeter into six segments, in three pairs. In each pair the segments are of equal length. For example, the two segments adjacent to vertex A are equal. If we pick one segment from each pair, their sum will be the semiperimeter s. The segments from B and C along side BC add up to a, so either segment adjacent to A must be of length s-a. The other segments similarly have lengths s-b or s-c.

The radius to a tangency point is perpendicular to the side, and the segment from the incenter to a vertex bisects the vertex angle. Using the definition of the cotangent function in the resulting right triangle, we have

\cot\frac{\alpha}{2}=\frac{s-a}{r},

and similarly for the other two angles.

For the inradius formula, we start from the general addition formula:

\cot(u+v+w)= \frac{\cot u+\cot v+\cot w-\cot u\cot v\cot w} {1-\cot u\cot v-\cot v\cot w-\cot w\cot u}.

Applying it to

\cot\left(\frac12\alpha+\frac12\beta+\frac12\gamma\right) =\cot\frac{\pi}{2}=0,

we obtain

\cot\frac{\alpha}{2}\cot\frac{\beta}{2}\cot\frac{\gamma}{2} =\cot\frac{\alpha}{2}+\cot\frac{\beta}{2}+\cot\frac{\gamma}{2}.

(This is also the triple cotangent identity.)

Substituting the values obtained in the first part, we get

\begin{aligned} \frac{s-a}{r}\frac{s-b}{r}\frac{s-c}{r} &=\frac{s-a}{r}+\frac{s-b}{r}+\frac{s-c}{r}\\ &=\frac{3s-2s}{r}\\ &=\frac{s}{r}. \end{aligned}

Multiplying through by r^3/s gives the value of r^2, and taking the positive square root proves the assertion.
