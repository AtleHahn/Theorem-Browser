Statement

In an abelian category, let

0\longrightarrow A\xrightarrow{q}B\xrightarrow{r}C\longrightarrow0

be a short exact sequence. The following are equivalent:

  1. There is a morphism t:B\to A with tq=\operatorname{id}_A.
  2. There is a morphism u:C\to B with ru=\operatorname{id}_C.
  3. There is an isomorphism h:B\to A\oplus C such that hq is the canonical inclusion of A and rh^{-1} is the canonical projection onto C.

Here A\oplus C denotes the biproduct; short exactness means that q is the kernel of r and r is the cokernel of q.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The cumulative categorical prerequisites are substantial; an elementary abelian-group example does not suffice to understand the statement for arbitrary abelian categories.

Proof 1: Kernel factorization and biproduct inverses (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Proposition2.26 and proof, pp.13–15).

In the notation of the statement, a section u gives qt=1_B-ur by the kernel property. Then tq=1_A, tu=0, and the inverse maps are h=(t,r) and h^{-1}=(q,u). Starting instead with a retraction t, the cokernel property gives ur=1_B-qt and the same identities. Thus the isomorphism respects the specified injection and projection, not merely the objects.
