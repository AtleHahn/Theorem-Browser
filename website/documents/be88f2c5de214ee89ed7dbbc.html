Statement

Let X be a choice set, f:X\times[0,1]\to\mathbb R, V(t)=\sup_{x\in X}f(x,t) and X^*(t)=\{x:f(x,t)=V(t)\}. Suppose each f(x,\cdot) is differentiable and absolutely continuous, and there is Lebesgue-integrable b:[0,1]\to[0,\infty) with |f_t(x,t)|\le b(t) for every x and almost every t. Suppose X^*(t)\ne\varnothing almost everywhere. Then V is finite and absolutely continuous, and every choice of x^*(s)\in X^*(s) at almost every s satisfies V(t)=V(0)+\int_0^t f_t(x^*(s),s)\,ds. Values of the integrand on its null exceptional set do not affect the integral.

Proof 1: Control value oscillation and integrate the envelope derivative — outline

Using the definition of V, observe that for any {\displaystyle t^{\prime },t^{\prime \prime }\in \lbrack 0,1]} with {\displaystyle t^{\prime }<t^{\prime \prime }},

{\displaystyle |V(t^{\prime \prime })-V(t^{\prime })|\leq \sup _{x\in X}|f(x,t^{\prime \prime })-f(x,t^{\prime })|=\sup _{x\in X}\left\vert \int _{t^{\prime }}^{t^{\prime \prime }}f_{t}(x,t)dt\right\vert \leq \int _{t^{\prime }}^{t^{\prime \prime }}b(t)dt.}

This implies that {\displaystyle V} is absolutely continuous. Therefore, {\displaystyle V} is differentiable almost everywhere, and using the differentiability-point envelope formula V^{\prime}(s)=f_t(x^*(s),s) yields the stated integral formula. Q.E.D.
