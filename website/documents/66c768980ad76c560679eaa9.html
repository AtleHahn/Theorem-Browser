Statement

If a regular pentagon has side length a>0 and diagonal length b, then b/a=(1+\sqrt5)/2.

Proof 1: Apply Ptolemy and solve the positive quadratic

Apply Ptolemy's theorem to four successive vertices of the regular pentagon.

Consider the relation between the length a of the side and the (common) length b of the 5 chords in a regular pentagon. By completing the square, the relation yields the golden ratio:

{\displaystyle {\begin{array}{rl}b\cdot b\,\;\;\qquad \quad \qquad =&\!\!\!\!a\!\cdot \!a+a\!\cdot \!b\\b^{2}\;\;-ab\quad \qquad =&\!\!a^{2}\\{\frac {b^{2}}{a^{2}}}\;\;-{\frac {ab}{a^{2}}}\;\;\;\qquad =&\!\!\!{\frac {a^{2}}{a^{2}}}\\\left({\frac {b}{a}}\right)^{2}-{\frac {b}{a}}+\left({\frac {1}{2}}\right)^{2}=&\!\!1+\left({\frac {1}{2}}\right)^{2}\\\left({\frac {b}{a}}-{\frac {1}{2}}\right)^{2}=&\!\!\quad {\frac {5}{4}}\\{\frac {b}{a}}-{\frac {1}{2}}\;\;\;=&\!\!\!\!\pm {\frac {\sqrt {5}}{2}}\\{\frac {b}{a}}>0\,\Rightarrow \,\varphi ={\frac {b}{a}}=&\!\!\!\!{\frac {1+{\sqrt {5}}}{2}}\end{array}}}

Apply Ptolemy to four vertices and solve the resulting positive quadratic ratio. School Euclidean geometry and algebra suffice.
