Statement

For every positive integer n, z\mapsto z^n is holomorphic on \mathbb C and has derivative nz^{n-1}.

Level

Level justification

Complex differentiability, holomorphic logarithm branches or conformal bijections are closely related extensions; the contour integral can be understood by parametrization and the branch is explicitly fixed where needed. No Riemann surfaces, residues or differential forms are required by these statements.

Proof 1: Binomial expansion in a complex increment

Let f(z)=z^n. If the exponent n is a positive integer, there is no need for a branch cut: one may define positive integral complex powers through complex multiplication, and show that f'(z)=nz^{n-1} for all complex z, from the definition of the derivative and the binomial theorem.

The binomial calculation, with a complex increment h, is

\begin{aligned} f'(z)&=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{(z+h)^n-z^n}{h}\\ &=\lim_{h\to0}\frac1h\left[\sum_{j=0}^n\binom njz^{n-j}h^j-z^n\right]\\ &=\lim_{h\to0}\sum_{j=1}^n\binom njz^{n-j}h^{j-1}\\ &=nz^{n-1}. \end{aligned}

Since \binom n1=n, and every term with j\ge2 contains a positive power of h, those terms tend to zero. The calculation includes z=0; for n=1 the derivative is 1.
