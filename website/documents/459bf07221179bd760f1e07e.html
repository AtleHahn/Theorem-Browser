Statement

Let n,N\ge1 and let \alpha^1,\ldots,\alpha^n,\beta be Hermitian N\times N complex matrices with

\alpha^i\alpha^j+\alpha^j\alpha^i=2\delta_{ij}I_N,\qquad\beta^2=I_N,\qquad\alpha^i\beta+\beta\alpha^i=0.

Set D_0=-\mathrm i\sum_{j=1}^n\alpha^j\partial_j. Let g:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R be continuous with g(0)=0 and G(s)=\int_0^s g(t)\,dt. Suppose \omega\in\mathbb R and \phi:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb C^N is locally essentially bounded, belongs to H^1(\mathbb R^n;\mathbb C^N), has G(\phi^*\beta\phi)\in L^1, and satisfies

\omega\phi=D_0\phi+g(\phi^*\beta\phi)\beta\phi

in distributions. Then

\omega\int_{\mathbb R^n}\phi^*\phi\,dx=\frac{n-1}{n}\int_{\mathbb R^n}\phi^*D_0\phi\,dx+\int_{\mathbb R^n}G(\phi^*\beta\phi)\,dx.

Here * means conjugate transpose, \delta_{ij} is 1 for i=j and 0 otherwise, and H^1 consists of square-integrable functions whose first weak derivatives are square-integrable. The equation in distributions means equality after testing against smooth compactly supported vector functions and integrating derivative terms by parts. Integrals are Lebesgue integrals.
