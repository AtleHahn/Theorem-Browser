Statement

Let n,N\ge1 and let \alpha^1,\ldots,\alpha^n,\beta be Hermitian N\times N complex matrices with

\alpha^i\alpha^j+\alpha^j\alpha^i=2\delta_{ij}I_N,\qquad\beta^2=I_N,\qquad\alpha^i\beta+\beta\alpha^i=0.

Set D_0=-\mathrm i\sum_{j=1}^n\alpha^j\partial_j. Let g:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R be continuous with g(0)=0 and G(s)=\int_0^s g(t)\,dt. Suppose \omega\in\mathbb R and \phi:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb C^N is locally essentially bounded, belongs to H^1(\mathbb R^n;\mathbb C^N), has G(\phi^*\beta\phi)\in L^1, and satisfies

\omega\phi=D_0\phi+g(\phi^*\beta\phi)\beta\phi

in distributions. Then

\omega\int_{\mathbb R^n}\phi^*\phi\,dx=\frac{n-1}{n}\int_{\mathbb R^n}\phi^*D_0\phi\,dx+\int_{\mathbb R^n}G(\phi^*\beta\phi)\,dx.

Here * means conjugate transpose, \delta_{ij} is 1 for i=j and 0 otherwise, and H^1 consists of square-integrable functions whose first weak derivatives are square-integrable. The equation in distributions means equality after testing against smooth compactly supported vector functions and integrating derivative terms by parts. Integrals are Lebesgue integrals.

Proof 1: A compactly supported Dirac multiplier (external)

The following editorial argument completes the stated scope. A supporting source is On the meaning of the Vakhitov–Kolokolov stability criterion for the nonlinear Dirac equation (Comech, Section 3, Lemma 3.2, printed p.7, with the supplied cutoff justification.).

The following cutoff argument justifies Comech’s Lemma 3.2 under the stated weak regularity. Write D=D0, s=phibeta phi, and V=x chi_R with a smooth radial cutoff equal to one on radius R and zero outside 2R. Local boundedness and continuous g imply G(s) is locally W1,1 with derivative g(s) ds. The distributional equation is an L2_loc equality, so multiply by V dot grad(phi), take twice the real part and integrate. The quadratic Dirac identity, proved first for smooth phi and passed by local H1 approximation, is 2 Re integral (D phi) (V dot grad phi) = Re integral phi*[-i alpha^k (partial_k V_j) partial_j - (div V)D]phi. It uses only first derivatives after integration by parts. The mass and nonlinear terms become minus integral (div V)|phi|^2 and minus integral (div V)G(s). For V=x chi_R the main Dirac term is (1-n) integral chi_R phiDphi; every extra Dirac term is bounded by C integral_{R<|x|<2R}|phi||grad phi| and tends to zero by H1. Mass and G cutoff errors tend to zero by their assumed L1 integrability. Let R tend to infinity to obtain n omega integral |phi|^2=(n-1) integral phiDphi+n integral G(s). All terms are integrable; D is selfadjoint on H1 so its full integral is real. No derivative of g or H2 regularity is used.
