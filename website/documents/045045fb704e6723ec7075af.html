Statement

Let (\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P) be a probability space and (\mathcal F_k)_{k=0}^N a filtration, meaning an increasing sequence of sub-sigma-algebras of \mathcal F, with integer N\ge1. Let X_k be real integrable \mathcal F_k-measurable random variables, and let c_1,\ldots,c_N be finite nonnegative deterministic constants such that |X_k-X_{k-1}|\le c_k almost surely for every 1\le k\le N. Put S=\sum_{k=1}^N c_k^2.

For every real \epsilon>0, when S>0 the following bounds hold:

  • If (X_k,\mathcal F_k) is a supermartingale, then\mathbb P(X_N-X_0\ge\epsilon)\le\exp\left(-\frac{\epsilon^2}{2S}\right).
  • If it is a submartingale, then\mathbb P(X_N-X_0\le-\epsilon)\le\exp\left(-\frac{\epsilon^2}{2S}\right).
  • If it is a martingale, then\mathbb P(|X_N-X_0|\ge\epsilon)\le2\exp\left(-\frac{\epsilon^2}{2S}\right).

Here supermartingale means \mathbb E[X_k\mid\mathcal F_{k-1}]\le X_{k-1} almost surely, submartingale means the reverse inequality, and martingale means equality. If S=0, then X_N=X_0 almost surely and all three displayed tail probabilities are zero.

Proof 1: Symmetric-interval specialization

Upper-tail Azuma inequality (dependency)

Let (X_t,\mathcal F_t)_{t=0}^n be a real-valued supermartingale, n\ge1. Suppose real \mathcal F_{t-1}-measurable random variables A_t,B_t and deterministic constants 0<c_t<\infty satisfy

A_t\le X_t-X_{t-1}\le B_t,\qquad B_t-A_t\le c_t\quad\text{almost surely},\quad 1\le t\le n.

Then, for every \epsilon>0,

\mathbb P(X_n-X_0\ge\epsilon)\le \exp\left(-\frac{2\epsilon^2}{\sum_{t=1}^nc_t^2}\right).

Argument

Note that by setting A_k=-c_k,B_k=c_k, we could obtain the symmetric Azuma inequality. Here the interval-width constants in the general inequality are 2c_k.

For a submartingale, -X is a supermartingale with the same absolute increment bounds. Applying the upper-tail inequality to -X gives the stated lower-tail bound. A martingale is both a supermartingale and a submartingale; the union bound for its two tail events gives the factor 2 in the two-sided inequality.

The positive-width substitution also covers zero individual bounds by applying it with c_k+\delta for every \delta>0 and taking \delta\downarrow0 when S>0. If S=0, every increment is zero almost surely. These observations specify the degenerate-bound convention without dividing by zero.
