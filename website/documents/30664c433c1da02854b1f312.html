Statement

Let ABC be a nondegenerate triangle whose altitude from C meets the interior of AB at D. Set p=AD>0, q=DB>0 and h=CD>0. If h^2=pq, then \angle ACB=90^\circ.

Proof 1: SAS similarity and complementary angles

We have a triangle ABC in which h^2=pq holds and need to show that the angle at C is a right angle. Now because of h^2=pq we also have \frac hp=\frac qh. Together with \angle ADC=\angle CDB=90^\circ, the triangles ADC, CDB have an angle of equal size and have corresponding pairs of legs with the same ratio. This means the triangles are similar by SAS, which yields \angle DBC=\angle ACD and

\begin{aligned} \angle ACB&=\angle ACD+\angle DCB\\ &=\angle ACD+(90^\circ-\angle DBC)\\ &=\angle ACD+(90^\circ-\angle ACD)\\ &=90^\circ. \end{aligned}
