Statement

Let F be a bounded plane region of positive area A in the xz-plane on the side x>0 of the z-axis, with piecewise smooth boundary and a one-to-one continuously differentiable parametrization having nonzero Jacobian in the interior. Let R=A^{-1}\int_Fx\,dA be its centroid distance from that axis. Rotating F about the z-axis produces a solid of volume V=2\pi RA.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Definite integrals; Centroids; Surfaces and solids of revolution. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. Proof 1: Pappus volume theorem by integration

Let A be the area of F, W the solid of revolution of F, and V the volume of W. Suppose F starts in the xz-plane and rotates around the z-axis. The distance of the centroid of F from the z-axis is its x-coordinate

R=\frac{\int_F x\,dA}{A},

and the theorem states that

V=A\cdot2\pi R=2\pi\int_F x\,dA.

To show this, let {\displaystyle F} be in the xz-plane, parametrized by {\displaystyle \mathbf {\Phi } (u,v)=(x(u,v),0,z(u,v))} for {\displaystyle (u,v)\in F^{*}}, a parameter region. Since {\displaystyle {\boldsymbol {\Phi }}} is essentially a mapping from {\displaystyle \mathbb {R} ^{2}} to {\displaystyle \mathbb {R} ^{2}}, the area of {\displaystyle F} is given by the change of variables formula:

{\displaystyle A=\int _{F}dA=\iint _{F^{*}}\left|{\frac {\partial (x,z)}{\partial (u,v)}}\right|\,du\,dv=\iint _{F^{*}}\left|{\frac {\partial x}{\partial u}}{\frac {\partial z}{\partial v}}-{\frac {\partial x}{\partial v}}{\frac {\partial z}{\partial u}}\right|\,du\,dv,}

where {\displaystyle \left|{\tfrac {\partial (x,z)}{\partial (u,v)}}\right|} is the absolute value of the determinant of the Jacobian matrix of the change of variables.

The solid {\displaystyle W} has the toroidal parametrization {\displaystyle {\boldsymbol {\Phi }}(u,v,\theta )=(x(u,v)\cos \theta ,x(u,v)\sin \theta ,z(u,v))} for {\displaystyle (u,v,\theta )} in the parameter region {\displaystyle W^{*}=F^{*}\times [0,2\pi ]}; and its volume is

{\displaystyle V=\int _{W}dV=\iiint _{W^{*}}\left|{\frac {\partial (x,y,z)}{\partial (u,v,\theta )}}\right|\,du\,dv\,d\theta .}

Expanding,

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\left|{\frac {\partial (x,y,z)}{\partial (u,v,\theta )}}\right|&=\left|\det {\begin{bmatrix}{\frac {\partial x}{\partial u}}\cos \theta &{\frac {\partial x}{\partial v}}\cos \theta &-x\sin \theta \\[6pt]{\frac {\partial x}{\partial u}}\sin \theta &{\frac {\partial x}{\partial v}}\sin \theta &x\cos \theta \\[6pt]{\frac {\partial z}{\partial u}}&{\frac {\partial z}{\partial v}}&0\end{bmatrix}}\right|\\[5pt]&=\left|-{\frac {\partial z}{\partial v}}{\frac {\partial x}{\partial u}}\,x+{\frac {\partial z}{\partial u}}{\frac {\partial x}{\partial v}}\,x\right|=\ \left|-x\,{\frac {\partial (x,z)}{\partial (u,v)}}\right|=x\left|{\frac {\partial (x,z)}{\partial (u,v)}}\right|.\end{aligned}}}

The last equality holds because F lies on the side x>0 of the axis of rotation. Now,

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}V&=\iiint _{W^{*}}\left|{\frac {\partial (x,y,z)}{\partial (u,v,\theta )}}\right|\,du\,dv\,d\theta \\[1ex]&=\int _{0}^{2\pi }\!\!\!\!\iint _{F^{*}}x(u,v)\left|{\frac {\partial (x,z)}{\partial (u,v)}}\right|du\,dv\,d\theta \\[6pt]&=2\pi \iint _{F^{*}}x(u,v)\left|{\frac {\partial (x,z)}{\partial (u,v)}}\right|\,du\,dv\\[1ex]&=2\pi \int _{F}x\,dA\end{aligned}}}

by change of variables.

Since R=A^{-1}\int_F x\,dA, the final integral equals 2\pi RA.
