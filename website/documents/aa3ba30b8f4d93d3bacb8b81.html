A derivation using a temporary assumption can be transformed into a proof of an implication. This formalizes hypothetical reasoning and assumption discharge, connecting a relation between proofs and premises with a connective inside the logical language.

Statement

Use the axiom schemes

\mathrm K:\ P\to(Q\to P),\qquad \mathrm S:\ (P\to(Q\to R))\to((P\to Q)\to(P\to R)),

with arbitrary formulas substituted for the letters. Modus ponens infers V from U and U\to V. A proof from premises is a finite list in which each line is a premise, an axiom instance, or follows by modus ponens from earlier lines. Additional fixed axiom schemes may be present, but modus ponens is the only inference rule. Write \Gamma\vdash C when such a proof ends in C. For any set of premises \Gamma and formulas H,C, if \Gamma\cup\{H\}\vdash C, then \Gamma\vdash H\to C; in particular H\vdash C implies \vdash H\to C.

Proof 1: Transform every proof line by induction

Local self-implication lemma

From the axiom schemes \mathrm K and \mathrm S one can quickly deduce the theorem scheme P\to P (reflexivity of implication), which is used below:

  1. (P\to((Q\to P)\to P))\to((P\to(Q\to P))\to(P\to P)) from \mathrm S with P,(Q\to P),P.
  2. P\to((Q\to P)\to P) from \mathrm K with P,(Q\to P).
  3. (P\to(Q\to P))\to(P\to P) from modus ponens applied to step 2 and step 1.
  4. P\to(Q\to P) from \mathrm K with P,Q.
  5. P\to P from modus ponens applied to step 4 and step 3.

Transform the proof

Suppose that we have that \Gamma and H together prove C, and we wish to show that \Gamma proves H\to C. For each step T in the deduction that is a premise in \Gamma (a reiteration step) or an axiom, we can apply modus ponens to the axiom \mathrm K, T\to(H\to T), to get H\to T. If the step is H itself (a hypothesis step), we apply the theorem scheme to get H\to H.

If the step is the result of applying modus ponens to A and A\to T, we first make sure that these have been converted to H\to A and H\to(A\to T) and then we take the axiom \mathrm S,

(H\to(A\to T))\to((H\to A)\to(H\to T)),

and apply modus ponens to get (H\to A)\to(H\to T) and then again to get H\to T. At the end of the proof we will have H\to C as required, except that now it only depends on \Gamma, not on H.
