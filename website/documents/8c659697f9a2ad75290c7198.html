Statement

Let X=(X_1,\ldots,X_n) and Y=(Y_1,\ldots,Y_n) be centered real Gaussian random vectors such that

\mathbb EX_i^2=\mathbb EY_i^2\quad(1\le i\le n),\qquad\mathbb EX_iX_j\le\mathbb EY_iY_j\quad(i\ne j).

Then for all u_1,\ldots,u_n\in\mathbb R,

\mathbb P(X_i\le u_i\text{ for every }i)\le\mathbb P(Y_i\le u_i\text{ for every }i).

A Gaussian vector means every real linear combination of its coordinates is normally distributed, allowing zero variance; centered means each coordinate has expectation zero. No joint Gaussian assumption on the combined pair (X,Y) is required.
