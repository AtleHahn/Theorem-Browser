Statement

Let f be entire and suppose |f(z)|\le M|z| for all z\in\mathbb C and some M>0. Then f(z)=az for a complex constant a.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Real analysis supplies bounds, uniform convergence and derivatives. Holomorphic means complex differentiable, and entire means so on all of C. These direct extensions suffice for the full bound, image or convergence claim; no Riemann surfaces or complex-geometric structures occur.

Proof 1: Specialize the source polynomial-growth coefficient argument

Take the Taylor series representation of {\displaystyle f},

{\displaystyle f(z)=\sum _{k=0}^{\infty }a_{k}z^{k}.}

The Cauchy coefficient estimate on a circle of radius r shows that for all {\displaystyle k\geq 0},

{\displaystyle |a_{k}|\leq Mr^{1-k}.}

So, if {\displaystyle k>1}, then

{\displaystyle |a_{k}|\leq \lim _{r\to \infty }Mr^{1-k}=0.}

Therefore, {\displaystyle a_{k}=0}. Thus f is affine and then, by referring back to the original inequality at z=0, we have that the constant term is zero.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply Cauchy estimates on arbitrarily large circles to eliminate coefficients above degree one, then use the bound at zero. Undergraduate complex analysis suffices.
