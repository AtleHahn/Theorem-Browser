Statement

Let f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb C be 2\pi-periodic, integrable over a period, and of bounded variation in a neighborhood of x. Assume f is continuous at x. Write c_k=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(t)e^{-ikt}\,dt and S_nf(x)=\sum_{k=-n}^{n}c_ke^{ikx}. Then S_nf(x)\to f(x).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Local bounded variation and the integrable-function setting remain prerequisites not reduced to ordinary calculus by the source.

Proof 1: Specialize the one-sided-limit mean at a continuity point

Jordan’s test gives \lim_{n\to\infty}S_nf(x)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to0}(f(x+\varepsilon)+f(x-\varepsilon))/2. Continuity at x makes the right-hand side f(x).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply local Dirichlet–Jordan convergence to a generally integrable periodic function and identify the one-sided values by continuity. General integrability together with local bounded variation and Fourier convergence is not confidently a small extension here; retain beyond undergraduate conservatively.
