Statement

Let a>0, and let f(z)=\phi(z)+i\psi(z) be the single-valued complex potential of a planar potential flow, with all its singularities outside the closed disk |z|\leq a. Thus f is holomorphic on a neighborhood of that disk. The flow in |z|>a after insertion of the circular cylinder |z|=a has complex potential

w(z)=f(z)+\overline{f\!\left(\frac{a^2}{\overline z}\right)}.

The added term is holomorphic in the exterior and introduces no exterior singularities, so w has the same singularities there as f. Moreover \operatorname{Im}w=0 on |z|=a, so the circle is a streamline (a level set of the stream function). Complex conjugation is denoted by a bar; \phi and \psi are the velocity potential and stream function.

Proof 1: Reflection of the analytic interior and the boundary calculation

The reflected term is holomorphic for |z|>a: writing the interior Taylor expansion f(\zeta)=\sum_{n\geq0}c_n\zeta^n gives

\overline{f(a^2/\overline z)}=\sum_{n\geq0}\overline{c_n}\,a^{2n}z^{-n}.

Consequently adding it does not change the exterior singularities of f. On |z|=a one has a^2/\overline z=z, so the source's displayed calculation gives

w(z)=f(z)+\overline{f(z)}=2\operatorname{Re}f(z).

Thus the stream function \operatorname{Im}w vanishes on the circle, which is the required streamline boundary condition.
