A shortest cycle is inside a part or meets the separator

Statement

Let a finite directed graph have vertex partition A\sqcup S\sqcup B with no arcs between A and B. Give the arcs real weights and consider simple directed cycles. If exact minimum-weight cycles in the induced subgraphs on A,B, and exact minimum-weight cycles through each s\in S, are available, the least of these candidates is a global minimum-weight cycle, or there is no cycle if all candidate sets are empty.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The separator, weighted simple cycles and available minima are finite graph and real-number notions.

Proof 1: A shortest cycle is inside a part or meets the separator — source argument

Any cycle avoiding S is connected and cannot pass between A and B, so it is entirely in one of the induced parts. Every other cycle contains some s\in S and is considered by that vertex’s query. Consequently every global minimizer is represented among the recursive or separator candidates, and choosing the least proves the claim.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Every cycle either stays in one exclusive part or meets the separator. Comparing the supplied exact candidates requires only finite graph paths and minima.
