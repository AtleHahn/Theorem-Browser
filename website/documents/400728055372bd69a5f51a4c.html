Statement

Let k\ge1 be an integer, \psi(z)=\Gamma'(z)/\Gamma(z) the digamma function, and \psi^{(r)} its rth derivative. As meromorphic identities in z, \sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\psi(z+j/k)=k[\psi(kz)-\log k], and, for integers m>1, \sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\psi^{(m-1)}(z+j/k)=k^m\psi^{(m-1)}(kz). Pointwise equalities are read where the displayed values are finite.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The digamma/polygamma functions are defined as logarithmic derivatives of Gamma. Meromorphic means holomorphic apart from poles; understanding this single special function and the supplied identity is a limited extension of complex differentiation and calculus.

Proof 1: Logarithmic differentiation of Gauss multiplication

Use Gauss's multiplication formula \prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\Gamma(z+j/k)=(2\pi)^{(k-1)/2}k^{1/2-kz}\Gamma(kz). Logarithmic differentiation gives \sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\psi(z+j/k)=-k\log k+k\psi(kz). Differentiating this identity m-1 further times removes the constant and gives the higher-polygamma formula, with the chain rule providing the factor k^m. These are logarithmic-derivative identities of meromorphic functions, interpreted away from poles.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Take logarithmic derivatives of the fully displayed Gamma multiplication identity, then repeatedly differentiate using the chain rule. Undergraduate complex analysis and meromorphic logarithmic derivatives suffice with Gamma as one supplied special function; its construction and multiplication theorem proof are not needed.
