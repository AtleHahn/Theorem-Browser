Statement

For every fixed real n, \lim_{x\to+\infty}x^ne^{-x}=0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Limits of real functions; Elementary real functions. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Differentiate until the power is nonpositive

For n>0, this is an example involving \infty/\infty:

\lim_{x\to\infty}x^n e^{-x} =\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{x^n}{e^x} \ \stackrel{\mathrm H}{=}\ \lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{nx^{n-1}}{e^x} =n\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{x^{n-1}}{e^x},

where \stackrel{\mathrm H}{=} denotes an application of L'Hôpital's rule. Repeatedly apply L'Hôpital's rule until the exponent is zero (if n is an integer) or negative (if n is not an integer) to conclude that the limit is zero. At this final stage the numerator is bounded and the denominator tends to infinity. An initially nonpositive n is already this case.

Level justification

Apply LHopital repeatedly while the power is positive; after finitely many differentiations the numerator is bounded and the exponential grows without bound. This is standard calculus for real powers.
