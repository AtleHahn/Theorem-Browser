Statement

Let a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4,a_6\in\mathbb Z and suppose

E:y^2+a_1xy+a_3y=x^3+a_2x^2+a_4x+a_6

is a nonsingular cubic. If an affine rational point P=(x,y) has finite order in the elliptic-curve group E(\mathbb Q), then either x,y\in\mathbb Z, or P has order 2 and x=m/4, y=n/8 for integers m,n. The group has the point at infinity as identity. Its chord-and-tangent rule defines the sum as the inverse of the third intersection with the joining line (the tangent for equal points); inversion is (x,y)\mapsto(x,-y-a_1x-a_3). Intersections are counted with multiplicity. Finite order means some positive multiple of P equals the identity.
