Statement

Let C be a convex body in \mathbb R^n, where n\geq1: a compact convex set with nonempty interior. Its minimal width is

w(C)=\inf_{\|u\|=1}\left(\max_{x\in C}\langle u,x\rangle-\min_{x\in C}\langle u,x\rangle\right).

A plank is a closed region

P=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:a\leq\langle u,x\rangle\leq b\},\qquad \|u\|=1,\quad a<b,

and its width is w(P)=b-a. If finitely many planks P_1,\ldots,P_m cover C, then

\sum_{i=1}^m w(P_i)\geq w(C).

Here \langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle and \|\cdot\| denote the Euclidean inner product and norm. The planks need not be parallel to one another.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Undergraduate Euclidean linear algebra and elementary compactness/infima suffice; arbitrary dimension makes the complete formulation beyond high-school geometry.

Proof 1: Signed sums and shrinking convex bodies (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorem 2.2; §2.2.1, Lemmas 2.3–2.5 and their proofs.).

If the total width were strictly smaller than w(C), enlarge all planks slightly while preserving that inequality, so a point outside their interiors is outside the original closed planks. In Lemma 2.3 the longest chord length \ell in any fixed direction satisfies \ell\ge w(C): the difference body C-C contains the ball of radius w(C), since all its support values are at least w(C). This supplies the width comparison without requiring perpendicular supporting planes at the chord endpoints.
