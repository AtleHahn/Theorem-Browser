Statement

Let Z be a finite connected graph equipped at each vertex with a finite generating set for an assigned group. Define c(Z) as the total size of these generating sets plus the free rank of \pi_1(Z). Consider successive moves of the two source types: (i) identify two edges with common initial vertex and distinct terminal vertices, merging the terminal generating sets; (ii) identify two edges with both endpoints in common, and add at most one generator to one vertex set. Assume the moves have the stated graph-rank effects: unchanged in (i), decreased by one in (ii). Then c never increases, and any such succession of moves terminates in finitely many steps.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Vertex groups need only undergraduate groups and finite generating sets. For a finite graph, its fundamental group is represented by edge loops reduced by backtracking and is free on edges outside a spanning tree. This concrete graph-loop interpretation adds a short topology/algebra extension; the rank changes of both moves are assumed.

Proof 1: Complexity count for the two stated graph folds

In the first type of source fold, joining two generating sets increases neither their total size nor the unchanged underlying graph rank. In the second type the graph rank drops by one while at most one generator is added. Thus neither move increases c. Both identify two edges into one, decreasing the finite edge count, so infinitely many moves are impossible.
