Statement

Let n\ge1 be an integer. For nonempty compact sets K,L\subseteq\mathbb R^n and 0\leq t\leq1, define (1-t)K+tL=\{(1-t)k+t\ell:k\in K,\ell\in L\}. Then \operatorname{vol}_n((1-t)K+tL)^{1/n}\geq(1-t)\operatorname{vol}_n(K)^{1/n}+t\operatorname{vol}_n(L)^{1/n}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement defines the weighted vector sum of two compact sets. From undergraduate topology and calculus, compact-set Lebesgue volume can be learned via box approximation as an extension of ordinary Euclidean volume. That addition and the nth-root concavity formula fit about 30 minutes; random sets are proof machinery and are not needed to understand the statement.

Proof 1: Two-atom random-set specialization outline

Apply the external random-set inequality: if X is a random nonempty compact set with integrable maximum norm and

\mathbb E[X]=\{\mathbb E[V]:V\text{ is an integrable selection of }X\},

then \operatorname{vol}_n(\mathbb E[X])^{1/n}\geq\mathbb E[\operatorname{vol}_n(X)^{1/n}].

For 0<t<1, take the two-point probability space with atom probabilities 1-t,t, and put X=K,L on those atoms respectively. A selection is exactly a choice k\in K,\ell\in L, and compactness makes it integrable. Consequently \mathbb E[X]=(1-t)K+tL, whereas the right side of the random-set inequality is (1-t)\operatorname{vol}_n(K)^{1/n}+t\operatorname{vol}_n(L)^{1/n}. Substitution proves the assertion. At t=0 or t=1 it is equality.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The random-set theorem is used only on a two-atom space. A selection is explicitly a pair of points, its expectation is their weighted average, and the expected volume root is a two-term weighted sum. This concrete application needs only undergraduate vector geometry, finite probability and volume; no general measurable-selection theory is used.
