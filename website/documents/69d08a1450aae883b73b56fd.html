Statement

Let (\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P) be a probability space and let A_i\in\mathcal F for i\ge1. Then \mathbb P(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}A_i)\le\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbb P(A_i). The finite version is obtained by taking all later events empty.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory analysis supplies sets, sequences and nonnegative series. The additional probability-space definition consists of a family of events closed under countable set operations and a nonnegative countably additive function of total mass one. These axioms and elementary examples suffice to understand the displayed countable-union inequality within about 30 minutes, without integration or measure construction theory.

Proof 1: Induction followed by continuity from below

For finite collections of events, Boole's inequality can be proved by induction. First, for any event {\displaystyle A_{1}}, one trivially has

{\displaystyle \mathbb {P} (A_{1})\leq \mathbb {P} (A_{1}).}

Next, assume that the inequality holds for any collection of {\displaystyle n} events, and consider n+1 events A_1,\ldots,A_{n+1}. By the induction hypothesis, we have

{\displaystyle {\mathbb {P} }\left(\bigcup _{i=1}^{n}A_{i}\right)\leq \sum _{i=1}^{n}{\mathbb {P} }(A_{i}).}

By applying the equality {\displaystyle \mathbb {P} (A\cup B)=\mathbb {P} (A)+\mathbb {P} (B)-\mathbb {P} (A\cap B),} to {\displaystyle A=A_{1}\cup \cdots \cup A_{n}} and {\displaystyle B=A_{n+1},} we get

{\displaystyle \mathbb {P} \left(\bigcup _{i=1}^{n+1}A_{i}\right)=\mathbb {P} \left(\bigcup _{i=1}^{n}A_{i}\right)+\mathbb {P} (A_{n+1})-\mathbb {P} \left(\bigcup _{i=1}^{n}A_{i}\cap A_{n+1}\right).}

Since, by the first axiom of probability,

{\displaystyle {\mathbb {P} }\left(\bigcup _{i=1}^{n}A_{i}\cap A_{n+1}\right)\geq 0,}

we have

{\displaystyle \mathbb {P} \left(\bigcup _{i=1}^{n+1}A_{i}\right)\leq \mathbb {P} \left(\bigcup _{i=1}^{n}A_{i}\right)+\mathbb {P} (A_{n+1}),}

and therefore

{\displaystyle \mathbb {P} \left(\bigcup _{i=1}^{n+1}A_{i}\right)\leq \sum _{i=1}^{n}\mathbb {P} (A_{i})+\mathbb {P} (A_{n+1})=\sum _{i=1}^{n+1}\mathbb {P} (A_{i}).}

The countable version of Boole's inequality can then be deduced from its finite version by taking n to infinity and using the continuity from below of probability measures.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Finite induction uses the two-event addition rule, then continuity from below passes to the countable union. A probability measure here can be understood through its event algebra and countable-additivity axiom; the extra continuity property is stated explicitly. This is a small extension of undergraduate probability and real-series limits, without integration or measure construction.

Proof 2: Disjointify by the first occurring event

Let events {\displaystyle A_{1},A_{2},A_{3},\dots } in our probability space be given. The countable additivity of the measure {\displaystyle \mathbb {P} } states that if {\displaystyle B_{1},B_{2},B_{3},\dots } are pairwise disjoint events, then

{\displaystyle \mathbb {P} \left(\bigcup _{i}B_{i}\right)=\sum _{i}\mathbb {P} (B_{i}).}

Set

{\displaystyle B_{i}:=A_{i}-\bigcup _{j=1}^{i-1}A_{j}.}

Then {\displaystyle B_{1},B_{2},B_{3},\dots } are pairwise disjoint. We claim that:

{\displaystyle \bigcup _{i=1}^{\infty }A_{i}=\bigcup _{i=1}^{\infty }B_{i}.}

One inclusion is clear. Indeed, since {\displaystyle B_{i}\subset A_{i}} for all i, thus {\displaystyle \bigcup _{i=1}^{\infty }B_{i}\subset \bigcup _{i=1}^{\infty }A_{i}}.

For the other inclusion, let {\displaystyle x\in \bigcup _{i=1}^{\infty }A_{i}} be given. Write {\displaystyle k} for the minimum positive integer such that {\displaystyle x\in A_{k}}. Then {\displaystyle x\in A_{k}-\bigcup _{j=1}^{k-1}A_{j}=B_{k}}. Thus {\displaystyle x\in \bigcup _{i=1}^{\infty }B_{i}}. Therefore {\displaystyle \bigcup _{i=1}^{\infty }A_{i}\subset \bigcup _{i=1}^{\infty }B_{i}}.

Therefore

{\displaystyle \mathbb {P} \left(\bigcup _{i}A_{i}\right)=\mathbb {P} \left(\bigcup _{i}B_{i}\right)=\sum _{i}\mathbb {P} (B_{i})\leq \sum _{i}\mathbb {P} (A_{i}),}

where the last inequality holds because {\displaystyle B_{i}\subset A_{i}} implies that {\displaystyle \mathbb {P} (B_{i})\leq \mathbb {P} (A_{i}),} for all i.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Removing earlier events creates disjoint measurable events with the same union. Countable additivity and monotonicity give the inequality termwise. The needed probability-space structure is just the supplied countable set operations and probability axioms, accessible from undergraduate sets and real series; no measure integration theory is needed.
