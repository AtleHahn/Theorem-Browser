Statement

Let R be a UFD with fraction field F. Every nonzero f\in F[x] is an F^\times multiple of a primitive f_0\in R[x], unique up to a unit of R. Moreover primitive representatives satisfy \operatorname{pp}(fg)\sim\operatorname{pp}(f)\operatorname{pp}(g) for nonzero f,g\in F[x], where \sim denotes association by a unit of R.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory ring theory supplies primes, polynomial gcds, units and unique factorization. A fraction field generalizes ordinary fractions, and primitive means coefficient gcd one. These closely related polynomial definitions suffice.

Proof 1: Primitive representatives of polynomials over a fraction field — outline

Let {\displaystyle R} be a unique factorization domain with field of fractions {\displaystyle F}. If {\displaystyle f\in F[x]} is a nonzero polynomial over {\displaystyle F} then for some nonzero {\displaystyle d} in {\displaystyle R}, {\displaystyle df} has coefficients in {\displaystyle R}, and so – factoring out the gcd {\displaystyle q} of the coefficients – we can write {\displaystyle df=qf'} for some primitive polynomial {\displaystyle f'\in R[x]}. As one can check, this polynomial {\displaystyle f'} is unique up to the multiplication by a unit and is called the primitive part (or primitive representative) of {\displaystyle f} and is denoted by {\displaystyle \operatorname {pp} (f)}. The procedure is compatible with product: {\displaystyle \operatorname {pp} (fg)\sim\operatorname {pp} (f)\operatorname {pp} (g)}.

Here primitive means that the coefficients have gcd a unit, and \sim denotes equality up to multiplication by a unit of R. Compatibility uses multiplicativity of coefficient gcds; the source leaves the uniqueness and compatibility checks implicit.
