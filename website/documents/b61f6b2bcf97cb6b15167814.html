Statement

For a simple bipartite graph with parts U,V of sizes m,n, write z(m,n;s,t) for the largest edge count when no s vertices of U and t vertices of V form a complete bipartite subgraph K_{s,t}. Let G be a simple graph on n vertices with no K_{s,t} subgraph. Its bipartite double cover has two copies of its vertex set, with u in one part adjacent to v in the other exactly when uv is an edge of G. This cover has 2|E(G)| edges and has no K_{s,t}. Consequently z(n,n;s,t)\geq2\operatorname{ex}(n,K_{s,t}), where \operatorname{ex} is the largest edge count of an n-vertex graph avoiding K_{s,t}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The two-copy graph, forbidden complete bipartite subgraph and both largest-edge-count functions are defined concretely. The assertion compares finite edge counts under this copying operation. Understanding finite graphs, subsets and largest integer counts through these definitions requires elementary reasoning despite the extremal-graph notation.

Proof 1: Double every edge

We can transform a graph with {\displaystyle n} vertices and no copy of {\displaystyle K_{s,t}} into a bipartite graph with {\displaystyle n} vertices on each side of its bipartition, twice as many edges and still no copy of {\displaystyle K_{s,t}}, by taking its bipartite double cover.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Each original edge gives two edges between the copied parts. A complete bipartite subgraph in the cover projects injectively within each part; the two projected parts cannot overlap, since overlap would require a loop in the original simple graph. It would therefore give the forbidden original subgraph. This is finite graph reasoning and counting.
