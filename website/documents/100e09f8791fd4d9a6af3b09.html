Statement

A finite simple undirected graph G=(V,E) has vertices V and edges consisting of unordered pairs of distinct vertices. A proper vertex coloring assigns different colors to the ends of every edge. Let x_1,\ldots,x_n list the nonempty graph vertices in nonincreasing degree order, where d(x) is the number of neighbors of x. Greedy coloring in that order uses at most \max_{1\le i\le n}\min\{d(x_i)+1,i\} colors.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The finite objects and coloring constraints are defined locally; only elementary counting and algebra are needed.

Proof 1: Degree-ordered greedy coloring bound — outline

The greedy algorithm considers the vertices in a specific order {\displaystyle x_{1}}, ..., {\displaystyle x_{n}} and assigns to {\displaystyle x_{i}} the smallest available color not used by {\displaystyle x_{i}}'s neighbours among {\displaystyle x_{1}}, ..., {\displaystyle x_{i-1}}, adding a fresh color if needed.

If the vertices are ordered according to their degrees, the resulting greedy coloring uses at most {\displaystyle {\text{max}}_{i}{\text{ min}}\{d(x_{i})+1,i\}} colors, at most one more than the graph's maximum degree.

Level
High school only
Level justification

At step i there are at most min(d(x_i),i-1) already colored neighbors, so one of the first min(d(x_i)+1,i) colors is available. Take the largest bound over the finite steps.

Routine omissions

The routine check is omitted: at step i there are at most d(x_i) forbidden colors and at most i−1 earlier vertices. The least available color is therefore at most min(d(x_i)+1,i).
