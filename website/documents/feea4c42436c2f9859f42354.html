Statement

Let n,k\geq1 be integers, let v\in\mathbb R^n have Euclidean norm 1, and let R be a k\times n matrix with independent entries, each either symmetric \pm1, or taking \sqrt3,0,-\sqrt3 with probabilities 1/6,2/3,1/6. All entries use the same distribution. Put f(v)=Rv/\sqrt{k}. For every \varepsilon>0, \Pr(\|f(v)\|^2\geq1+\varepsilon)\leq\exp[-\frac{k}{2}(\varepsilon-\ln(1+\varepsilon))]\leq\exp[-\frac{k}{2}(\varepsilon^2/2-\varepsilon^3/3)].

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra supplies matrix multiplication and Euclidean norms. The finitely supported independent variables have explicitly given probabilities; expectation and the probability of a norm inequality can be learned from finite weighted sums. For the moment comparison, the standard normal distribution adds a familiar density integral. This is a limited extension using calculus and finite sums.

Proof 1: Positive exponential moments and Chernoff optimization

Fix the unit vector v in the statement. Define Q_i:=\sum_jR_{ij}v_j. We have \|f(v)\|_2^2=\frac1k\sum_iQ_i^2. The Q_i are independent and identically distributed. Use the moment bounds \mathbb E Q_i^2=1 and \mathbb E Q_i^{2m}\leq(2m-1)!! for m\geq1. Write K_Y(t)=\ln\mathbb E[e^{tY}] for the cumulant generating function, and interpret -\ln0=+\infty in the probability bounds.

For any {\textstyle t\in (0,1/2)}, we can compute the cumulant generating function

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}K_{Q_{i}^{2}}(t)&=\ln E[e^{Q_{i}^{2}t}]\\&=\ln \sum _{m=0}^{\infty}{\frac {t^{m}}{m!}}E[Q_{i}^{2m}]\\&\leq \ln \left(1+\sum _{m=1}^{\infty}{\frac {t^{m}}{m!}}(2m-1)!!\right)\\&=-{\frac {1}{2}}\ln(1-2t)\end{aligned}}}

Similarly, for any {\textstyle t\in (0,k/2)},

{\displaystyle K_{{\frac {1}{k}}\sum _{i}Q_{i}^{2}}(t)=\sum _{i}K_{Q_{i}^{2}}(t/k)\leq -{\frac {k}{2}}\ln(1-2t/k)}

So by the standard Chernoff bound method, for any {\textstyle t\in (0,k/2)} and any {\textstyle \epsilon >0},

{\displaystyle -\ln Pr\left({\frac {1}{k}}\sum _{i}Q_{i}^{2}\geq 1+\epsilon \right)\geq (1+\epsilon )t+{\frac {k}{2}}\ln(1-2t/k)}

The right side is maximized at {\textstyle t={\frac {k\epsilon }{2(1+\epsilon )}}}, at which point we have

{\displaystyle -\ln Pr\left({\frac {1}{k}}\sum _{i}Q_{i}^{2}\geq 1+\epsilon \right)\geq {\frac {k}{2}}(\epsilon -\ln(1+\epsilon ))\geq {\frac {k}{2}}(\epsilon ^{2}/2-\epsilon ^{3}/3)}
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The variables have finite support, so expanding an exponential and interchanging its convergent power series with expectation is an interchange with a finite sum. The moment comparison bounds that series by a Gaussian-square exponential moment; independence adds the logarithms. The Chernoff step is exponential Markov, and the optimizing t and final logarithm inequality are one-variable calculus. A brief introduction to moment-generating notation suffices beyond calculus, linear algebra and finite probability.
