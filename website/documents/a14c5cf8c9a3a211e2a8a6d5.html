Statement

Contexts \Gamma,\Delta,\Pi,\Lambda,\Sigma,\Theta are finite multisets of formulas (finite unordered lists, repetitions allowed). A sequent \Gamma\vdash\Delta is a pair of such multisets. A derivation is a finite rooted tree of sequents, whose child labels are premises of the rule at their parent. Concatenation of contexts denotes multiset union. The Cut rule takes \Gamma\vdash\Delta,A and A,\Pi\vdash\Lambda to \Gamma,\Pi\vdash\Delta,\Lambda. The left-conjunction rule replaces B,C in an antecedent by B\land C, leaving the other formulas unchanged. Given derivations of \Gamma\vdash\Delta,A and A,B,C,\Pi\vdash\Lambda, a cut performed after the left-conjunction step on the second derivation can instead be performed before that step, with the same endsequent \Gamma,B\land C,\Pi\vdash\Delta,\Lambda.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All multiset, tree and inference-rule definitions are supplied. The assertion compares two orders of removing the cut formula and replacing B,C by their conjunction; these are elementary finite symbolic operations.

Proof 1: Move the cut before the conjunction step

{\displaystyle {\frac {\Gamma \vdash \Delta ,A\qquad {\frac {A,B,C,\Pi \vdash \Lambda }{A,B\land C,\Pi \vdash \Lambda }}\land _{L}}{\Gamma ,B\land C,\Pi \vdash \Delta ,\Lambda }}\operatorname {Cut} \quad \implies \quad {\frac {{\frac {\Gamma \vdash \Delta ,A\qquad A,B,C,\Pi \vdash \Lambda }{\Gamma ,B,C,\Pi \vdash \Delta ,\Lambda }}\operatorname {Cut} }{\Gamma ,B\land C,\Pi \vdash \Delta ,\Lambda }}\land _{L}.}

The cut simply moves one step past {\displaystyle \land _{L}} without interaction. It would bubble upwards until it meets "resistance" in the form of principal cuts.
