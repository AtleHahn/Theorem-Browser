Statement

If z\in\mathbb Z[i] has norm |z|^2 equal to a positive rational prime, then z is a Gaussian prime.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Gaussian integers are concrete complex numbers with integer coordinates. Ring ideals, quotient cardinality, prime elements and congruences are introductory algebra; the norm and primary/quartic conditions are explicit finite arithmetic definitions. No field-extension norm or ideal-class theory is required.

Proof 1: Source argument

If z=pq with two Gaussian nonunits, both integer norms |p|^2,|q|^2 exceed one, and norm multiplicativity gives |z|^2=|p|^2|q|^2, a composite integer. The contrapositive proves irreducibility, which equals primality in the Gaussian unique factorization domain.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The norm factorization proves irreducibility, then the supplied Gaussian UFD property converts irreducibility to primality. That distinction and unique-factorization domains require introductory abstract algebra.
