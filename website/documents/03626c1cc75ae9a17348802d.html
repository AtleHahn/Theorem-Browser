Statement

Give \mathbb Q its usual metric d(x,y)=|x-y|. For each positive integer k, set C_k=\mathbb Q\cap[\sqrt2,\sqrt2+1/k]. These sets are nonempty, closed and bounded in \mathbb Q, and strictly decreasing, but \bigcap_{k\ge1}C_k=\varnothing.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Real analysis supplies the rational subspace metric, countability and interval covers. Measure zero here means covers by intervals of arbitrarily small total length, a limited null-set definition without general integration.

Proof 1: Intervals converging to an omitted irrational point — outline

In the rational-number space, take the intervals with endpoints \sqrt2 and \sqrt2+1/k. Their intersections with \mathbb Q are the same whether the real endpoints are included or excluded. They give the stated closed, bounded nested sets with empty intersection.

Level justification

Use rational density, irrational endpoints and relative closedness in the rational metric subspace. Undergraduate real analysis and elementary metric topology suffice; the shrinking intervals isolate the omitted irrational limit.
