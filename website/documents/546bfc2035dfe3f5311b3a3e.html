Statement

Let A=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb Z}A_n be a commutative unital Noetherian graded ring. Then A^+=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}A_n is a finitely generated A_0-algebra.

Proof 1: Induct above a finite homogeneous degree cutoff — outline

Let I be the ideal of A generated by all homogeneous elements of A of positive degree. Since A is Noetherian, I is actually generated by finitely many f_i, homogeneous of positive degree. If I=0, then A^+=A_0, so assume I\ne0.

If f is homogeneous of positive degree, then we can write

f=\sum_i g_if_i

with g_i homogeneous. If f has sufficiently large degree, then each g_i has degree positive and strictly less than that of f.

Also, each degree piece A_n is a finitely generated A_0-module. Indeed, let N_i be an increasing chain of A_0-submodules of A_n. Then the chain of ideals N_iA stabilizes in finitely many steps; so does the chain

N_i=N_iA\cap A_n.

The ascending-chain criterion for modules therefore applies.

Choose module generators for the finitely many positive degree pieces up to the largest degree of an f_i. Thus, by induction on degree using the expression above, we see that A^+ is a finitely generated A_0-algebra.
