Statement

Let v=(v_1,\ldots,v_n)\in\mathbb R^n have \sum_jv_j^2=1. Let R_1,\ldots,R_n be independent variables, all with either the symmetric \pm1 distribution, or with values \sqrt3,0,-\sqrt3 of probabilities 1/6,2/3,1/6. Put Q=\sum_jR_jv_j. If Z is standard normal, then for every integer m\geq1, \mathbb E Q^{2m-1}=0=\mathbb E Z^{2m-1} and \mathbb E Q^{2m}\leq\mathbb E Z^{2m}=(2m-1)!!, where (2m-1)!!=1\cdot3\cdots(2m-1).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra supplies matrix multiplication and Euclidean norms. The finitely supported independent variables have explicitly given probabilities; expectation and the probability of a norm inequality can be learned from finite weighted sums. For the moment comparison, the standard normal distribution adds a familiar density integral. This is a limited extension using calculus and finite sums.

Proof 1: Termwise comparison after expanding moments

{\textstyle E[Q^{2m-1}]=0} is easy: just apply the fact that {\textstyle E[R_{j_{1}}\dots R_{j_{l}}]=0} when {\textstyle l} is odd, since we can decompose it into a product of expectations, and one of those is the expectation of an odd power of a Rademacher variable (or of the symmetric three-valued variable), which is zero.

First consider the symmetric \pm1 distribution. Now, the trick is that we can rewrite {\textstyle Z} as {\textstyle Z=\sum _{i}Z_{i}v_{i}}, where the variables {\textstyle Z_{1},\dots ,Z_{n}} are independent standard gaussians. Then we need to compare:

{\displaystyle E[Q^{2m}]=\sum _{j_{1},j_{2},\dots ,j_{2m-1},j_{2m}}E[R_{j_{1}}R_{j_{2}}\dots R_{j_{2m-1}}R_{j_{2m}}]v_{j_{1}}v_{j_{2}}\dots v_{j_{2m-1}}v_{j_{2m}}}

and

{\displaystyle E[Z^{2m}]=\sum _{j_{1},j_{2},\dots ,j_{2m-1},j_{2m}}E[Z_{j_{1}}Z_{j_{2}}\dots Z_{j_{2m-1}}Z_{j_{2m}}]v_{j_{1}}v_{j_{2}}\dots v_{j_{2m-1}}v_{j_{2m}}}

In the top sum, a term

{\displaystyle E[R_{j_{1}}R_{j_{2}}\dots R_{j_{2m-1}}R_{j_{2m}}]v_{j_{1}}v_{j_{2}}\dots v_{j_{2m-1}}v_{j_{2m}}}

decomposes into a product of expectations, times {\textstyle v_{j_{1}}v_{j_{2}}\dots v_{j_{2m-1}}v_{j_{2m}}}. The product of expectations is zero, unless the indices {\textstyle j_{1},j_{2},\dots ,j_{2m}} are paired off. In that case, the term {\textstyle v_{j_{1}}v_{j_{2}}\dots v_{j_{2m-1}}v_{j_{2m}}} is the square of something, and so

{\displaystyle v_{j_{1}}v_{j_{2}}\dots v_{j_{2m-1}}v_{j_{2m}}\geq 0}

while {\textstyle R_{j_{1}}R_{j_{2}}\dots R_{j_{2m-1}}R_{j_{2m}}} is also the square of {\textstyle \pm 1}, and so

{\displaystyle E[R_{j_{1}}R_{j_{2}}\dots R_{j_{2m-1}}R_{j_{2m}}]=1}

In the bottom sum, we run a similar argument with each such term

{\displaystyle E[Z_{j_{1}}Z_{j_{2}}\dots Z_{j_{2m-1}}Z_{j_{2m}}]v_{j_{1}}v_{j_{2}}\dots v_{j_{2m-1}}v_{j_{2m}}}

but in this case, since we have {\textstyle E[Z^{2m}]=(2m-1)!!\geq 1}, we find that in each case,

{\displaystyle E[R_{j_{1}}R_{j_{2}}\dots R_{j_{2m-1}}R_{j_{2m}}]v_{j_{1}}v_{j_{2}}\dots v_{j_{2m-1}}v_{j_{2m}}\leq E[Z_{j_{1}}Z_{j_{2}}\dots Z_{j_{2m-1}}Z_{j_{2m}}]v_{j_{1}}v_{j_{2}}\dots v_{j_{2m-1}}v_{j_{2m}}}

And so, summing all of them up, {\textstyle E[Q^{2m}]\leq E[Z^{2m}]}.

The same argument works for the other case. Specifically, if {\textstyle R_{j}} is distributed like that, then {\textstyle E[R_{j}^{2m}]=3^{m-1}\leq (2m-1)!!}, and the proof goes through exactly the same way.

Level justification

Expand the moments of the finite linear combinations and factor by independence. Odd multiplicities vanish by symmetry; surviving terms have nonnegative even powers of the coefficients. Compare the even moments of each discrete variable with the quoted normal moments. Basic finite probability, polynomial expansions and the density interpretation of a standard normal variable suffice; the two normal-distribution identities can be learned as established results without measure-theoretic probability.

Standard normal moments: odd moments vanish and even moments equal (2m-1)!!
Kind:

Theorem

Treatment:

Cited without proof

Used at:

Proof 1 — Termwise comparison after expanding moments

Statement used:

A standard normal Z has zero odd moments and E[Z^(2m)]=(2m-1)!! for m>=1.

Application in proof:

For even multiplicities compare discrete moments 1 or 3^(m-1) with normal moments; use symmetry when any multiplicity is odd.

Application level:

Undergraduate level

Level justification:

The formula is a one-dimensional Gaussian integral and the double factorial is explicit. From m=1,2 onward the inequality 3^(m-1)<=(2m-1)!! follows by multiplying factors at least three.

Review notes:

The linked Gaussian-integral statement yields the even moments at alpha=1/2 after normalizing the density; odd moments vanish by symmetry.
