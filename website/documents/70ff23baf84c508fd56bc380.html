Statement

Let (a_m)_{m\ge0} be any sequence of real numbers and let I\subset\mathbb R be an open interval containing 0. There is F\in C^{\infty}(I) with F^{(m)}(0)=a_m for every m\ge0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves real sequences; smooth real functions; higher derivatives. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Concentrate each monomial near zero — outline

Choose a smooth bump function \psi with compact support in a bounded interval about 0, equal identically to 1 near 0. For each m, let

F_m(t)=\frac{t^m}{m!}\cdot\psi\left(\frac{t}{\varepsilon_m}\right)\cdot a_m,

where \varepsilon_m is chosen sufficiently small that

\|F_m^{(j)}\|_\infty\leq 2^{-m}

for j<m. These estimates imply that each sum

\sum_{m\geq0}F_m^{(j)}

is uniformly convergent and hence that

F=\sum_{m\geq0}F_m

is a smooth function with

F^{(j)}=\sum_{m\geq0}F_m^{(j)}.

By construction

F^{(m)}(0)=a_m.

Restrict F to I.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Each cutoff monomial has derivative bounds of order epsilon_m^(m-j) for j<m, so epsilon_m can enforce all finitely many required bounds. The geometric tail controls every fixed derivative series. Uniform differentiation gives smoothness, and the cutoff is constant near zero so only the matching monomial contributes at each derivative order. This is undergraduate real analysis with a small bump-function extension.
