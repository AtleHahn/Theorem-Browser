Statement

Let q be a prime power and 2\leq t\leq q. In V=\mathbb F_q^2, for every polynomial p of degree at most t−1, let C_p=\{(x,p(x)):x\in\mathbb F_q\}. There are q^t distinct sets C_p, each of size q; no two have t common points. Their t-subsets partition exactly the t-point subsets having distinct first coordinates. The resulting incidence graph has parts of size q^t,q^2, has q^{t+1} edges and contains no K_{2,t} with its two vertices in the polynomial part and its t vertices in the point part.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory polynomial and ring theory supplies degrees, divisibility, ideals and finite-field arithmetic. The radical consists of elements with a power in the ideal, and Krull dimension counts lengths of prime-ideal chains. These limited ideal-based definitions suffice without schemes, localization or field-extension arithmetic.

Proof 1: Polynomial interpolation and incidence graph

Set n=q^2, m=q^t, k=q, and let H be the union of the t-element subsets of all the sets C_p.

Let {\displaystyle \mathbb {F} _{q}} be the finite field of size {\displaystyle q} and {\displaystyle V=\mathbb {F} _{q}\times \mathbb {F} _{q}}. For every polynomial {\displaystyle p(\cdot )} of degree at most {\displaystyle t-1} over {\displaystyle \mathbb {F} _{q}}, define {\displaystyle C_{p}=\{(x,p(x)):x\in \mathbb {F} _{q}\}\subset V}. Let {\displaystyle {\mathcal {C}}} be the collection of all {\displaystyle C_{p}}, so that {\displaystyle |{\mathcal {C}}|=q^{t}=n^{t/2}} and every {\displaystyle C_{p}} has size {\displaystyle q={\sqrt {n}}}. Clearly no two members of {\displaystyle {\mathcal {C}}} can share {\displaystyle t} members. The only {\displaystyle t}-sets in {\displaystyle V} that do not belong to {\displaystyle H} are those that have at least two points sharing the same first coordinate.

Incidence construction

Label the n points of V by [n] and the m sets C_p by A_1,\ldots,A_m.

Let H=\bigcup_{i=1}^m\binom{A_i}{t}, the family partitioned into the t-subsets of the indexed sets A_i.

Supposing {\displaystyle A_{1},\dots ,A_{m}\subset [n]} is a partition of {\displaystyle H} into {\displaystyle m} cliques of size {\displaystyle k}, we can let {\displaystyle G} be the {\displaystyle m\times n} bipartite graph with {\displaystyle V_{1}=\{1,\dots ,m\}} and {\displaystyle V_{2}=[n]}, such that {\displaystyle i\sim v} in {\displaystyle G} if and only if {\displaystyle v\in A_{i}}. Since the {\displaystyle A_{i}} form a clique partition, {\displaystyle G} cannot contain a copy of K_{2,t} with the two vertices in V_1 and the t vertices in V_2.
