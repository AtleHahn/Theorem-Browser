Statement

Let a>b>0 be relatively prime integers and let n\geq1 be an integer. There is a prime p that divides a^n-b^n and divides none of the integers a^k-b^k for 1\leq k<n, except in exactly the following cases:

  • n=1 and a-b=1;
  • n=2 and a+b is a power of 2;
  • n=6, a=2, and b=1.

In each exceptional case no such prime exists. A prime with the stated property is called a primitive prime divisor; relatively prime means having no common positive divisor other than 1.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Only elementary integer arithmetic and divisibility are needed; primitive prime divisor is explicitly defined by finitely many earlier powers.
