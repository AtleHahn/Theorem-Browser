Statement

Let a positive stock process satisfy dS_t=S_t(\sigma\,dB_t+\mu\,dt), where B is standard Brownian motion and \mu,\sigma,r are real constants. Let f(t,s) be continuously differentiable in time and twice continuously differentiable in positive s. Suppose a self-financing portfolio holding f_s(t,S_t) stock units and cash accruing at rate r has value V_t=f(t,S_t) and dynamics dV_t=r(V_t-f_s(t,S_t)S_t)\,dt+f_s(t,S_t)\,dS_t. Then, along the stock process, f_t(t,S_t)+\tfrac12\sigma^2S_t^2f_{ss}(t,S_t)+rS_tf_s(t,S_t)-rf(t,S_t)=0 for almost every time, almost surely.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The full assertion uses stochastic differential equations, Brownian differentials and self-financing stochastic dynamics. Understanding their precise meaning needs stochastic-integration foundations beyond a half-hour introduction. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Comparison of Itô and self-financing portfolio differentials

Suppose a stock price follows a geometric Brownian motion given by the stochastic differential equation dS=S(\sigma\,dB+\mu\,dt). Then, if the value of an option at time t is f(t,S_t), Itô's lemma gives

df(t,S_t)=\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}+\frac12(S_t\sigma)^2\frac{\partial^2f}{\partial s^2}\right)\,dt+\frac{\partial f}{\partial s}\,dS_t,

with derivatives evaluated at (t,S_t).

The term \frac{\partial f}{\partial s}\,dS represents the change in value in time dt of the trading strategy consisting of holding an amount \frac{\partial f}{\partial s} of the stock. If this trading strategy is followed, and any cash held is assumed to grow at the risk free rate r, then the total value V of this self-financing portfolio satisfies the SDE

dV_t=r\left(V_t-\frac{\partial f}{\partial s}S_t\right)\,dt+\frac{\partial f}{\partial s}\,dS_t.

This strategy replicates the option if V_t=f(t,S_t). Combining these equations gives the Black–Scholes equation along the stock process:

\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}+\frac{\sigma^2S_t^2}{2}\frac{\partial^2f}{\partial s^2} +rS_t\frac{\partial f}{\partial s}-rf=0,

for almost every time, almost surely.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The calculation requires Brownian motion, stochastic differential equations, Itô’s formula and equality of stochastic portfolio dynamics. These form a substantial stochastic-calculus background. Cancelling the common stochastic term is short, but does not make the underlying application accessible from ordinary undergraduate calculus alone.
