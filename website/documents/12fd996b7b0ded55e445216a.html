Statement

In the real projective plane, let six distinct tangent lines to a nonsingular conic form a hexagon P_1P_2P_3P_4P_5P_6 in that order: its sides are the lines P_1P_2,P_2P_3,\ldots,P_6P_1, all tangent to the conic. Then its three principal diagonals

P_1P_4,\qquad P_2P_5,\qquad P_3P_6

are concurrent at a projective point, called the Brianchon point. Concurrency may occur at infinity when viewed in an affine chart.

The real projective plane consists of the one-dimensional subspaces of \mathbb R^3; its lines correspond to two-dimensional subspaces. A nonsingular conic is the projective zero set of a nondegenerate homogeneous quadratic form, and tangency is to this conic. The hexagon need not be convex.

Level justification

From undergraduate linear algebra, projective points and lines are one- and two-dimensional subspaces of R^3. The homogeneous quadratic equation defines a conic; tangent lines and concurrency extend familiar plane geometry. These closely related notions form a modest reading extension, without the duality machinery needed for a proof.
