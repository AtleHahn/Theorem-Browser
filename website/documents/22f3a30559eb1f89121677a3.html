Statement

Let a transversal meet two distinct lines in a Euclidean or hyperbolic plane at distinct points. At each intersection, call the angles opening toward the other intersection interior; the other two are exterior. Alternate interior angles are interior angles on opposite sides of the transversal. Corresponding angles lie on the same side of the transversal, one interior and one exterior. Consecutive interior angles are interior angles on the same side. Parallel here means nonintersecting. Each of the following conditions implies the two lines are parallel: corresponding angles are equal; or consecutive interior angles sum to two right angles.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

All angle positions and parallelism as nonintersection are defined locally. Understanding the additional hyperbolic setting requires a brief introduction to its lines and angles, but the assertion uses only incidence and angle comparisons shared with Euclidean geometry. No model construction, curvature or differential geometry is needed; this fits a small extension of undergraduate geometric knowledge.

Proof 1: Reduce both criteria to equal alternate angles

Local alternate-angle criterion

If a transversal intersects two lines so that alternate interior angles are congruent, then the lines are parallel. Euclid proves this by contradiction: If the lines are not parallel then they must intersect and a triangle is formed. Then one of the alternate angles is an exterior angle equal to the other angle which is an opposite interior angle in the triangle. This contradicts the exterior-angle theorem (Euclid, Proposition 16), which states that an exterior angle of a triangle is always greater than the opposite interior angles.

This result extends in two ways. First, if a transversal intersects two lines so that corresponding angles are congruent, then the lines are parallel. Second, if a transversal intersects two lines so that interior angles on the same side of the transversal are supplementary, then the lines are parallel. These follow from the alternate-angle criterion by applying the fact that opposite angles of intersecting lines are equal and that adjacent angles on a line are supplementary.
