Statement

Let p be prime, let T,U be algebraically independent over \mathbb F_p, and set E=\mathbb F_p(T,U) and F=\mathbb F_p(T^p,U^p). This degree-p^2 extension has no primitive element. For every \alpha\in E\setminus F, F(\alpha) is a proper intermediate field.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Abstract field theory and separability exceed calculus/linear algebra.

Proof 1: Bound every simple subextension by Frobenius

In fact, for any {\displaystyle \alpha =g(T,U)} in {\displaystyle E\setminus F}, the Frobenius endomorphism shows that the element {\displaystyle \alpha ^{p}} lies in F , so α is a root of {\displaystyle f(X)=X^{p}-\alpha ^{p}\in F[X]}, and α cannot be a primitive element (of degree p2 over F), but instead F(α) is a non-trivial intermediate field.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Use Frobenius on a rational function field and compare each simple extension degree with the inseparable two-variable extension degree. Algebraic independence, field extensions and inseparability create a cumulative prerequisite burden beyond a confident small extension.
