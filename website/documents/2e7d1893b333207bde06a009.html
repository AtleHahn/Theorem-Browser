Statement

Let X be a compact triangulable topological space and let f:X\to X be continuous. For each n\ge0, let f_{*,n} be the induced linear map on singular homology H_n(X;\mathbb Q). Define

\Lambda_f=\sum_{n\ge0}(-1)^n\operatorname{tr}(f_{*,n}:H_n(X;\mathbb Q)\to H_n(X;\mathbb Q)).

The homology spaces are finite dimensional and vanish in sufficiently large degrees, so this sum is finite. If \Lambda_f\ne0, then f(x)=x for some x\in X.

If the fixed-point set \operatorname{Fix}(f)=\{x\in X:f(x)=x\} is finite, the stronger index formula is

\sum_{x\in\operatorname{Fix}(f)}\operatorname{ind}(f,x)=\Lambda_f.

Here \operatorname{ind}(f,x) is the local topological fixed-point index at the isolated fixed point x, counting its fixed-point multiplicity with sign. In Euclidean neighborhoods it is the degree of the map (y-f(y))/\|y-f(y)\| on a sufficiently small surrounding sphere; for a polyhedron it is the corresponding local index defined using a Euclidean neighborhood retraction. A triangulable space is one homeomorphic to the realization of a simplicial complex. Trace is the sum of diagonal entries of a matrix of a linear map, independent of the basis.
