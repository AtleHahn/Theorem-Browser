Robinson's joint consistency theorem

Two consistent first-order theories can be combined consistently when they share a complete theory in their common language. This makes precise a condition for integrating accounts with overlapping vocabulary, rather than assuming that separate consistency alone guarantees joint compatibility.

Statement

Let T_1,T_2 be consistent first-order theories in languages L_1,L_2 with common language L=L_1\cap L_2. If their common theory T_1\cap T_2 is complete in L (decides every L-sentence), then T_1\cup T_2 is consistent.

Proof 1: Alternate elementary-diagram extensions and take their limit — outline

For a first-order language {\displaystyle L} and a set {\displaystyle S}, let {\displaystyle L_{S}} denote the language obtained from {\displaystyle L} by adding 0-ary operations bijectively corresponding to the set {\displaystyle S}. For a first-order language {\displaystyle L}, an {\displaystyle L}-structure {\displaystyle M} and a subset {\displaystyle S\subseteq M}, let {\displaystyle M_{S}} denote the {\displaystyle L_{S}}-structure obtained from {\displaystyle M} by interpreting each 0-ary operation {\displaystyle s\in S} as {\displaystyle s} itself. For a first-order language {\displaystyle L}, its expansion {\displaystyle L'} and an {\displaystyle L'}-structure {\displaystyle M}, let {\displaystyle M\upharpoonright L} denote the reduct of {\displaystyle M} in {\displaystyle L}. Here \operatorname{Th}(A) denotes all sentences true in A. Names for different structures are fresh and disjoint unless they name an already identified common elementary substructure; in that case the same names are used. The notation (L_1)_{M,N} means that names for both structures have been added to L_1.

Let {\displaystyle L} be a first-order language and {\displaystyle L_{1}} and {\displaystyle L_{2}} its expansions such that {\displaystyle L_{1}\cap L_{2}=L}. Let {\displaystyle T_{1}} be a consistent theory in {\displaystyle L_{1}} and {\displaystyle T_{2}} a consistent theory in {\displaystyle L_{2}}. Suppose that {\displaystyle T_{1}\cap T_{2}} is a complete theory in {\displaystyle L}. We shall show that {\displaystyle T_{1}\cup T_{2}} is a consistent theory.

Using the compactness theorem, it is not hard to show that, for every {\displaystyle L_{1}}-structure {\displaystyle M} and an {\displaystyle L_{2}}-structure {\displaystyle N}, if {\displaystyle M\upharpoonright L} is elementarily equivalent to {\displaystyle N\upharpoonright L}, then the theory

{\displaystyle \operatorname {Th} (M_{M})\cup \operatorname {Th} ((N\upharpoonright L)_{N})}

is satisfiable.

For an {\displaystyle L_{1}}-structure {\displaystyle M} and an {\displaystyle L_{2}}-structure {\displaystyle N}, if {\displaystyle M\upharpoonright L} is elementarily equivalent to {\displaystyle N\upharpoonright L}, then for some {\displaystyle L_{1}}-structure {\displaystyle M'}, there exist an {\displaystyle L_{1}}-elementary embedding {\displaystyle M\hookrightarrow M'} and an {\displaystyle L}-elementary embedding {\displaystyle N\upharpoonright L\hookrightarrow M'\upharpoonright L}. Indeed, take an {\displaystyle (L_{1})_{M,N}}-model

{\displaystyle M''\models \operatorname {Th} (M_{M})\cup \operatorname {Th} ((N\upharpoonright L)_{N})}

and put {\displaystyle M'=M''\upharpoonright L_{1}}.

Now take a model {\displaystyle M_{0}\models T_{1}} and a model {\displaystyle N_{0}\models T_{2}}. Since {\displaystyle T_{1}\cap T_{2}} is an {\displaystyle L}-complete theory, we have {\displaystyle T_{1}\cap T_{2}=\operatorname {Th} (M_{0}\upharpoonright L)=\operatorname {Th} (N_{0}\upharpoonright L)}. Therefore, there exist an {\displaystyle L_{2}}-structure {\displaystyle N_{1}}, an {\displaystyle L_{2}}-elementary embedding {\displaystyle N_{0}\hookrightarrow N_{1}} and an {\displaystyle L}-elementary embedding {\displaystyle M_{0}\upharpoonright L\hookrightarrow N_{1}\upharpoonright L}. Identifying M_0 with its elementary image in N_1, and using the same names for these elements, since {\displaystyle (L_{1})_{M_{0}}\cap (L_{2})_{M_{0}}=L_{M_{0}}} and {\displaystyle (M_{0}\upharpoonright L)_{M_{0}}} is elementarily equivalent to {\displaystyle (N_{1}\upharpoonright L)_{M_{0}}}, there exist an {\displaystyle (L_{1})_{M_{0}}}-structure {\displaystyle (M_{1})_{M_{0}}}, {\displaystyle (L_{1})_{M_{0}}}-elementary embedding {\displaystyle (M_{0})_{M_{0}}\hookrightarrow (M_{1})_{M_{0}}} and an {\displaystyle L_{M_{0}}}-elementary embedding {\displaystyle (N_{1}\upharpoonright L)_{M_{0}}\hookrightarrow (M_{1}\upharpoonright L)_{M_{0}}}. In particular, there exist an {\displaystyle L_{1}}-structure {\displaystyle M_{1}}, an {\displaystyle L_{1}}-elementary embedding {\displaystyle M_{0}\hookrightarrow M_{1}} and an {\displaystyle L}-elementary embedding {\displaystyle N_{1}\upharpoonright L\hookrightarrow M_{1}\upharpoonright L}.

By induction, there exist {\displaystyle L_{1}}-structures {\displaystyle M_{0},M_{1},\dots }, {\displaystyle L_{2}}-structures {\displaystyle N_{0},N_{1},\dots } and elementary embeddings

{\displaystyle M_{i}\hookrightarrow M_{i+1}}{\displaystyle N_{i}\hookrightarrow N_{i+1}}{\displaystyle M_{i}\upharpoonright L\hookrightarrow N_{i+1}\upharpoonright L}{\displaystyle N_{i+1}\upharpoonright L\hookrightarrow M_{i+1}\upharpoonright L}

which make the following diagram commute.

{\displaystyle {\begin{matrix}M_{0}&\to &M_{1}&\to &M_{2}&\to &\cdots \\&\searrow &\uparrow &\searrow &\uparrow &\searrow &\cdots \\N_{0}&\to &N_{1}&\to &N_{2}&\to &\cdots \end{matrix}}}

(In the above diagram, the horizontal arrows are elementary embeddings in {\displaystyle L_{1}} or {\displaystyle L_{2}}, and the vertical and diagonal arrows are elementary embeddings in {\displaystyle L}.) Finally, by the elementary-chain theorem, the colimit of the above diagram is easily seen to be a model of {\displaystyle T_{1}\cup T_{2}}.
