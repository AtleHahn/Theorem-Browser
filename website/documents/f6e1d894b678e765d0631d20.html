Statement

For a real or complex series \sum_{n\ge1}a_n, put C=\limsup_{n\to\infty}|a_n|^{1/n}\in[0,\infty]. If C<1, the series converges absolutely. If C>1, it diverges. More generally it diverges if |a_n|^{1/n}>1 for infinitely many n.

Proof 1: Compare a tail with a geometric series — outline

The proof of the convergence of a series Σan is an application of the comparison test.

When C<1, choose k with C<k<1. If for all n ≥ N (N some fixed natural number) we have {\displaystyle {\sqrt[{n}]{|a_{n}|}}\leq k<1}, then {\displaystyle |a_{n}|\leq k^{n}<1}. Since the geometric series {\displaystyle \sum _{n=N}^{\infty }k^{n}} converges so does {\displaystyle \sum _{n=N}^{\infty }|a_{n}|} by the comparison test. Hence Σan converges absolutely.

If {\displaystyle {\sqrt[{n}]{|a_{n}|}}>1} for infinitely many n, then an fails to converge to 0, hence the series is divergent.

When C>1, the condition in the last paragraph holds.
