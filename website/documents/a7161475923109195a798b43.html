Statement

A Euclidean Hurwitz algebra is a nonzero finite-dimensional real unital algebra A (not assumed associative), with a positive-definite quadratic form q satisfying q(ab)=q(a)q(b). Write (a,b)=\frac12(q(a+b)-q(a)-q(b)), \|a\|^2=q(a), a^*=-a+2(a,1)1, L(a)b=ab, R(a)b=ba, and \operatorname{Re}a=(a,1)1. The involution is isometric and satisfies (ab)^*=b^*a^*, aa^*=a^*a=\|a\|^2 1, L(a^*)=L(a)^* and R(a^*)=R(a)^*. Here operator stars mean adjoints. Moreover \operatorname{Re}(ab)=\operatorname{Re}(ba), \operatorname{Re}((ab)c)=\operatorname{Re}(a(bc)), L(a^2)=L(a)^2 and R(a^2)=R(a)^2 (alternativity).

The algebra axioms and all involution/multiplication formulas are explicitly specified. Understanding these finite algebraic conditions is a small extension without classification theory.

Proof 1: Polarize the multiplicative norm

Polarizing the norm identity gives

2(a,b)(c,d)=(ac,bd)+(ad,bc).

Setting b=1 or d=1 gives L(a^*)=L(a)^* and R(c^*)=R(c)^*. Consequently

\operatorname{Re}(ab)=(ab,1)1=(a,b^*)1=(ba,1)1=\operatorname{Re}(ba).

Similarly,

\operatorname{Re}((ab)c)=((ab)c,1)1=(ab,c^*)1=(b,a^*c^*)1=(bc,a^*)1=(a(bc),1)1=\operatorname{Re}(a(bc)).

It follows that

((ab)^*,c)=(ab,c^*)=(b,a^*c^*)=(1,b^*(a^*c^*))=(1,(b^*a^*)c^*)=(b^*a^*,c),

so (ab)^*=b^*a^*. The polarized identity also gives

\|a\|^2(c,d)=(ac,ad)=(a^*(ac),d),

hence L(a^*)L(a)=\|a\|^2I. Applying this to 1 gives a^*a=\|a\|^2 1, and replacing a by a^* gives aa^*=\|a\|^2 1. Substituting a^*=-a+2(a,1)1 in L(a^*)L(a)=L(a^*a) gives L(a)^2=L(a^2). The right-multiplication identity follows analogously. The defining reflection a\mapsto a^* has period two and preserves the inner product and norm.
