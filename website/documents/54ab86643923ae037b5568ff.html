Statement

In the field \mathbb Q(i)=\{a+bi:a,b\in\mathbb Q\} with i^2=-1, its norm N:\mathbb Q(i)\to\mathbb Q is given by N(a+bi)=a^2+b^2 and satisfies N(zw)=N(z)N(w).

Proof 1: Apply the same product calculation to rational coordinates outline

For rational numbers a,b,c,d, the norm is given by

N(a+bi)=a^2+b^2.

Expanding the product, the multiplicativity calculation is

|a+bi|^2\cdot|c+di|^2=|(ac-bd)+i(ad+bc)|^2,

and by the definition of absolute value this is in turn equivalent to

(a^2+b^2)\cdot(c^2+d^2)=(ac-bd)^2+(ad+bc)^2.

This is the Brahmagupta–Fibonacci identity, verified by expansion.
