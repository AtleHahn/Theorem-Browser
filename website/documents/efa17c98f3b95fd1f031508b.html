Statement

On a connected compact Riemann surface, let D=\sum_P n_P[P] be a divisor, a finite integer sum of points, and put \deg D=\sum_P n_P. If E-D=(f) for a nonzero meromorphic function f, where (f) records zero orders positively and pole orders negatively, then \deg E=\deg D.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement concerns meromorphic divisors and linear equivalence on compact Riemann surfaces; no principal-degree theorem is needed merely to understand it.

Proof 1: Degree is invariant under linear equivalence

A principal divisor (f) has degree zero. Since two linearly equivalent divisors differ by such a divisor, their degrees are equal.
