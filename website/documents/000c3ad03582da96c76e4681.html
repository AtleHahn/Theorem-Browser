Statement

Assume the axiom of choice. Let I,J be nonempty sets and S_{ij} sets for i\in I,j\in J. Write J^I for all functions I\to J. Then \bigcup_i\bigcap_jS_{ij}=\bigcap_{f\in J^I}\bigcup_iS_{i,f(i)}\subseteq\bigcup_{g\in I^J}\bigcap_jS_{g(j),j}=\bigcap_j\bigcup_iS_{ij}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Undergraduate sets, functions, orders and infinite sequences suffice. Choice, well-ordering or maximal-chain principles are explicit set-selection/order assertions where invoked, not a demand to learn their independence proofs. The displayed quantifiers and constructions can be understood through familiar subsets and functions.

Proof 1: Specialize the choice-index identities and chain inclusion

Use the complete distributive laws \bigcap_{i\in I}\bigcup_{j\in J_i}T_{ij}=\bigcup_{f\in\prod_iJ_i}\bigcap_{i\in I}T_{i,f(i)},\qquad \bigcup_{i\in I}\bigcap_{j\in J_i}T_{ij}=\bigcap_{f\in\prod_iJ_i}\bigcup_{i\in I}T_{i,f(i)}.

In the particular case where all {\displaystyle J_{i}} are equal (that is, {\displaystyle J_{i}=J_{i_{2}}} for all {\displaystyle i,i_{2}\in I,} which is the case with the family {\displaystyle \left(S_{i,j}\right)_{(i,j)\in I\times J},} for example), then letting {\displaystyle J} denote this common set, the Cartesian product will be {\displaystyle {\textstyle \prod }J_{\bullet }~{\stackrel {\scriptscriptstyle {\text{def}}}{=}}~{\textstyle \prod \limits _{i\in I}}J_{i}={\textstyle \prod \limits _{i\in I}}J=J^{I},} which is the set of all functions of the form {\displaystyle f~:~I~\to ~J.} The complete distributive laws, respectively, become:

{\displaystyle \bigcap _{i\in I}\;\bigcup _{j\in J}S_{i,j}=\bigcup _{f\in J^{I}}\;\bigcap _{i\in I}S_{i,f(i)}}{\displaystyle \bigcup _{i\in I}\;\bigcap _{j\in J}S_{i,j}=\bigcap _{f\in J^{I}}\;\bigcup _{i\in I}S_{i,f(i)}}

which when combined with the general inclusion \bigcup_i\bigcap_j S_{i,j}\subseteq\bigcap_j\bigcup_i S_{i,j} implies:

{\displaystyle \bigcup _{i\in I}\;\bigcap _{j\in J}S_{i,j}~=~\bigcap _{f\in J^{I}}\;\bigcup _{i\in I}S_{i,f(i)}~~{\subseteq }~~\bigcup _{g\in I^{J}}\;\bigcap _{j\in J}S_{g(j),j}~=~\bigcap _{j\in J}\;\bigcup _{i\in I}S_{i,j}}

where

  • on the left hand side, the indices {\displaystyle f{\text{ and }}i} range over {\displaystyle f\in J^{I}{\text{ and }}i\in I} (so the subscripts of {\displaystyle S_{i,f(i)}} range over {\displaystyle i\in I{\text{ and }}f(i)\in f(I)\subseteq J})
  • on the right hand side, the indices {\displaystyle g{\text{ and }}j} range over {\displaystyle g\in I^{J}{\text{ and }}j\in J} (so the subscripts of {\displaystyle S_{g(j),j}} range over {\displaystyle j\in J{\text{ and }}g(j)\in g(J)\subseteq I}).
