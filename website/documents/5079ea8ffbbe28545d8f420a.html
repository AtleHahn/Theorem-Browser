Statement

Let k be a field, let n\geq0 be an integer, and set R=k[x_1,\ldots,x_n].

Every finitely generated R-module M admits a free resolution of length at most n: there are finitely generated free R-modules F_0,\ldots,F_n and an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow F_n\longrightarrow F_{n-1}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow F_0\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow0.

Exact means that the image of each map is the kernel of the next. A free module has a basis over R, so a finitely generated free module is isomorphic to some R^d; zero modules may pad a shorter resolution.

More generally, every R-module, whether finitely generated or not, has projective dimension at most n, and the global dimension of R is exactly n. Here a projective module is a direct summand of a free module, projective dimension is the least length of a resolution by projective modules, and global dimension is the supremum of these dimensions over all R-modules.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Modules, exact sequences, projective summands and homological dimensions build on one another; their cumulative burden exceeds a short extension of introductory ring theory.
