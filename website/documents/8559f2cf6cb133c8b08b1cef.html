Statement

Let a closed Euclidean disk have diameter d>0. If it is covered by m strips, each lying between parallel lines at distance one, then m\ge d.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement compares a finite count of unit-width strips with an elementary disk diameter.

Proof 1: Apply the plank-width bound to a disk

Use Bang’s plank theorem: if a convex body of minimal width w is covered by planks, the sum of their widths is at least w. The disk’s width is d in every direction, and the m unit-width planks have total width m. Therefore m\ge d.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Use the explicitly stated sum-of-widths theorem and the constant diameter width of the disk. Elementary planar geometry and addition suffice to apply the theorem.
