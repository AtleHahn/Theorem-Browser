Statement

Let d\ge1 be an integer and N a point process on \mathbb R^d, viewed as a random locally finite counting measure, not necessarily simple. Its intensity measure is \Lambda(B)=\mathbb E[N(B)] for Borel sets B. Sums over N count multiplicities. Suppose N is stationary with constant intensity density \lambda>0, so \Lambda(dx)=\lambda\,dx. For every measurable real f with \int|f(x)|\,dx<\infty, \mathbb E\!\left[\sum_{x\in N}f(x)\right]=\lambda\int_{\mathbb R^d}f(x)\,dx.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Proof 1: Take the constant intensity outside the integral

Use Campbell’s mean formula: if \int |f|\,d\Lambda<\infty, then \mathbb E\sum_{x\in N}f(x)=\int f\,d\Lambda. For a stationary point process N with constant density \lambda>0, Campbell's formula reduces to a volume integral:

\mathbb E\left[\sum_{x\in N}f(x)\right]=\lambda\int_{\mathbb R^d}f(x)\,dx.
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Factoring out a constant does not remove the need to understand the point process as a random counting measure and Campbell integration for arbitrary measurable integrable functions. The prerequisite burden remains measure-theoretic probability.
