Statement

Let (Y_n)_{n\ge1} be independent identically distributed real random variables with \mathbb E Y_n=0 and \operatorname{Var}(Y_n)=1, and put S_n=\sum_{j=1}^nY_j. For integers n\ge3 put T_n=S_n/\sqrt{2n\log\log n}, with natural logarithms. Then T_n\to0 in probability, but T_n does not converge almost surely to zero.

Proof 1: Central-limit scaling and the iterated-logarithm law — outline

The central limit theorem gives S_n/\sqrt n\Rightarrow Z for a standard normal Z. Since 1/\sqrt{2\log\log n}\to0, the standard vanishing deterministic scaling rule for convergence in distribution gives

\frac{S_n}{\sqrt{2n\log\log n}}\Rightarrow0,

which is convergence in probability because the limit is constant. On the other hand the stated law of the iterated logarithm gives

\limsup_n\frac{|S_n|}{\sqrt{2n\log\log n}}=1\quad\text{almost surely}.

Thus the same normalized sequence does not converge almost surely to zero.
