Statement

Let U(\mathfrak{gl}_n(\mathbb C)) be the unital algebra generated by E_{ij} with [E_{ij},E_{kl}]=\delta_{jk}E_{il}-\delta_{il}E_{kj}, for n\ge1. For an operator matrix A, let \det_{\rm col}(A)=\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)A_{\sigma(1),1}\cdots A_{\sigma(n),n}. For a central indeterminate t, define C_0,\ldots,C_{n-1} by \det_{\rm col}(tI+E+\operatorname{diag}(n-1,\ldots,0))=t^{[n]}+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}t^{[k]}C_k, where t^{[0]}=1 and t^{[k]}=t(t+1)\cdots(t+k-1). Each C_k lies in the center of this algebra.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory ring theory and linear algebra supply associative products, commutators, determinants and polynomial coefficients. Here the algebra is explicitly presented by generators and relations, interpretable as linear combinations of words modulo those relations. The column determinant fixes the order of every product and the monic rising powers form a polynomial basis in central t. These concrete extensions fit about 30 minutes; no Lie representations or enveloping-algebra structure theorem is required.

Proof 1: Pass from commutation with generators to the center — outline

Use the Capelli coefficient commutation theorem: each coefficient C_k defined in the statement commutes with every generator E_{ij}. The algebra U(\mathfrak{gl}_n) is generated by these E_{ij} subject to the displayed commutation relations alone. Thus the coefficient commutation theorem shows that the C_k belong to its center.
