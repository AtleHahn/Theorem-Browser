Statement

Let S\subseteq\mathbb R^3 be open and path connected, let F:S\to\mathbb R^3 be continuous with path-independent piecewise smooth line integrals, and fix a\in S. Define U(r)=-\int_{\gamma[a,r]}F\cdot du. Then U is well-defined and differentiable, F=-\nabla U, and the work along any piecewise smooth curve from p to q is U(p)-U(q).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Multivariable differentiation; Parametrized line integrals. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Apply the converse and change the potential sign

The source applies the converse gradient theorem to the path-independent force, with a minus sign in the defining integral. It yields F=-\nabla U. Applying the gradient theorem to this identity gives W=-\bigl(U(q)-U(p)\bigr).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the converse gradient theorem with a minus sign, then integrate the resulting gradient along the path. Undergraduate vector calculus suffices.
