Statement

Let (M,\omega) be a smooth symplectic manifold and let k\geq1 be odd. Suppose \alpha is a smooth differential k-form on M such that

\mathcal L_{X_H}\alpha=0\qquad\text{for every smooth function }H:M\to\mathbb R.

Then \alpha=0.

Here a differential k-form is a smooth field of alternating k-linear forms on tangent spaces. A symplectic form is a differential 2-form \omega with exterior derivative d\omega=0 and with nondegenerate pairing at each point: \omega_p(v,w)=0 for all w\in T_pM implies v=0. The Hamiltonian vector field X_H is determined by \omega(X_H,\cdot)=dH. The Lie derivative \mathcal L_{X_H}\alpha is the derivative at time zero of the pullback of \alpha by the local flow of X_H. Thus the hypothesis is invariance under every Hamiltonian local flow. Connectedness of M is not assumed.

Proof 1: A local Hamiltonian half-turn (external)

The following editorial argument completes the stated scope. A supporting source is Geometría de los Sistemas Dinámicos (Teorema11, pp.56–57, equation21.).

Here is a direct editorial proof: fix p and choose Darboux coordinates centered at p. A quadratic Hamiltonian proportional to sum_i(q_i^2+p_i^2)/2 generates simultaneous rotations; choose its time so that its linear flow is -Id. Multiply this Hamiltonian by a smooth bump function equal to 1 near p and supported in the chart. The resulting global smooth Hamiltonian fixes p, and the derivative of its time-t flow at p is the same rotation, hence -Id at the chosen time (the sign convention only reverses the direction of rotation). The hypothesis implies invariance under this flow. Therefore alpha_p=(-Id)^*alpha_p=(-1)^k alpha_p=-alpha_p, so alpha_p=0 over the reals. Since p was arbitrary, alpha=0. Degrees above dim M are already zero.
