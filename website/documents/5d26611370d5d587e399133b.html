Statement

For every positive prime p\equiv1\pmod3, there are integers L,M such that 4p=L^2+27M^2.

Level

Proof 1: Choose the quadratic expression whose second variable is divisible by three — outline

A theorem of Fermat states that every prime p ≡ 1 (mod 3) can be written as p = a2 + 3b2.

Letting m = a + b and n = a − b, we obtain p = m2 − mn + n2 (which equals (n − m)2 − (n − m)n + n2 = m2 + m(n − m) + (n − m)2, so m and n are not determined uniquely). Thus,

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}4p&=(2m-n)^{2}+3n^{2}\\&=(2n-m)^{2}+3m^{2}\\&=(m+n)^{2}+3(m-n)^{2}\end{aligned}}}

and it is a straightforward exercise to show that exactly one of m, n, or m − n is a multiple of 3, so

{\displaystyle p={\frac {1}{4}}(L^{2}+27M^{2}),}

where L,M are integers. The source leaves the modulo-three check as an exercise.
