Statement

Let p_1,p_2,p_3\in\mathbb R^2 be noncollinear and let u_1,u_2,u_3\in\mathbb R^2. For p\in\mathbb R^2, write a_j=p-p_j. The three equations a_j\cdot v=a_j\cdot u_j have a solution v\in\mathbb R^2 if and only if \det\begin{pmatrix}a_1^T&a_1\cdot u_1\\a_2^T&a_2\cdot u_2\\a_3^T&a_3\cdot u_3\end{pmatrix}=0. If a solution exists, it is unique.

Proof 1: Test consistency of the three velocity constraints (outline)

Rearrange the three edge constraints as the displayed linear system in the two components of v. The source gives the augmented determinant criterion. Noncollinearity of the anchors ensures the first two columns have rank two, so consistency is equivalent to the augmented matrix having rank two, that is, to its determinant vanishing. Rank two also gives uniqueness.
