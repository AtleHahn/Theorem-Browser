Statement

Let V:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R be a continuous T-periodic potential, T>0, and fix E\in\mathbb C. Let u,v solve -y''+Vy=Ey with u(0)=1,u'(0)=0,v(0)=0,v'(0)=1. Write u(x+T)=Au(x)+Bv(x) and v(x+T)=Cu(x)+Dv(x), and put M=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\C&D\end{pmatrix}. If a nonzero solution obeys y(x+T)=\xi y(x), then \det M=1,\qquad \xi^2-(A+D)\xi+1=0,\qquad \xi+\xi^{-1}=A+D.

Proof 1: Use a left eigenvector and the constant Wronskian

Writing a nonzero Floquet solution as y(x)=a\,u(x)+b\,v(x) shows that (a,b) must be a left eigenvector of M, so \xi satisfies

\xi^2-(A+D)\xi+\det M=0.

Because the Wronskian of the solutions u and v is constant, with initial value 1, \det M=1, so this equation simplifies to

\xi^2-(A+D)\xi+1=0

or equivalently

\xi+\xi^{-1}=A+D.
