Statement

There is an injective continuous map \gamma:[0,1]\to\mathbb R^2 whose image has positive two-dimensional Lebesgue measure. Its image is a Jordan arc.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Continuity and injectivity are basic topology. Positive planar area of a compact image can be understood through rectangle covers, a limited extension of area; no integration of arbitrary measurable functions or manifold structure is involved.

Proof 1: Cover a planar fat Cantor set by an arc

Take a two-dimensional Smith–Volterra–Cantor set: a compact totally disconnected subset of the plane with positive planar Lebesgue measure. By the Denjoy–Riesz theorem it is contained in a Jordan arc. The containing arc has positive measure as well.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the stated existence of a compact totally disconnected planar set of positive area and the theorem placing such a set in an injective continuous arc. Undergraduate point-set topology suffices, with planar Lebesgue measure used only as area and its monotonicity; neither the Cantor-set construction nor the arc theorem is proved.
