Statement

Let X have gamma density p(x)=\beta^\alpha x^{\alpha-1}e^{-\beta x}/\Gamma(\alpha) for x>0, where \alpha,\beta>0 and \Gamma(a)=\int_0^\infty t^{a-1}e^{-t}\,dt. With \psi=(\log\Gamma)\prime, \mathbb E\log X=\psi(\alpha)-\log\beta.

Level justification

Proof 1: Differentiate the gamma log-partition function

As an introductory example, consider the gamma distribution, whose distribution is defined by

{\displaystyle p(x)={\frac {\beta ^{\alpha }}{\Gamma (\alpha )}}x^{\alpha -1}e^{-\beta x}.}

The natural-parameter form shows that the natural parameter is given by

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\eta _{1}&=\alpha -1,\\\eta _{2}&=-\beta ,\end{aligned}}}

the reverse substitutions are

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\alpha &=\eta _{1}+1,\\\beta &=-\eta _{2},\end{aligned}}}

the sufficient statistics are (log x, x), and the log-partition function is

{\displaystyle A(\eta _{1},\eta _{2})=\log \Gamma (\eta _{1}+1)-(\eta _{1}+1)\log(-\eta _{2}).}

We can find the mean of the sufficient statistics as follows. First, for η1:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\operatorname {E} [\log x]&={\frac {\partial }{\partial \eta _{1}}}A(\eta _{1},\eta _{2})\\[0.5ex]&={\frac {\partial }{\partial \eta _{1}}}\left[\log \Gamma (\eta _{1}+1)-(\eta _{1}+1)\log(-\eta _{2})\right]\\[1ex]&=\psi (\eta _{1}+1)-\log(-\eta _{2})\\[1ex]&=\psi (\alpha )-\log \beta ,\end{aligned}}}

Where {\displaystyle \psi (x)} is the digamma function (derivative of log gamma), and we used the reverse substitutions in the last step.
