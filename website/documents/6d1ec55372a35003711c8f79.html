A maximal-extension principle for Boolean algebras is equivalent to the Boolean prime ideal principle. This reveals the choice content hidden in apparently structural existence claims; it should not be conflated with the full axiom of choice.

Statement

An ideal in a Boolean algebra is a nonempty lower set closed under finite joins; a filter is a nonempty upper set closed under finite meets. An ideal or filter is proper if it is not the whole algebra. A proper ideal J is prime if a\wedge b\in J implies a\in J or b\in J. A maximal ideal is a proper ideal maximal among proper ideals. Write BPI for the assertion that whenever an ideal I and a filter F of a Boolean algebra are disjoint, some prime ideal contains I and is disjoint from F. Then BPI is equivalent to: every ideal I disjoint from a filter F extends to an ideal maximal among all ideals disjoint from F.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement supplies the ideal/filter definitions and distinguishes maximal among all proper ideals from maximal among those avoiding F. An undergraduate familiar with algebraic structures and subsets can add the Boolean lattice operations and this restricted containment order within about 30 minutes. The complete assertion is an equivalence of the two stated extension properties, not an independence theorem.

Proof 1: Use the two ideal characterizations in opposite directions

For ideals of a Boolean algebra, primeness and maximality among proper ideals are equivalent. Thus BPI supplies an ideal containing I which is maximal among proper ideals and disjoint from F, hence maximal among ideals disjoint from F.

The converse is easily established by noting the following theorem: For any distributive lattice L, if an ideal is maximal among all ideals of L that are disjoint from a given filter F, then it is a prime ideal. Since any Boolean algebra is a distributive lattice, this shows the desired implication.
