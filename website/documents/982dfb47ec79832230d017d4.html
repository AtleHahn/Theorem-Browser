Statement

Let A be a set equipped with any specified collection of operations of finite arity, and let \alpha be a congruence on A. There is an inclusion-preserving bijection between congruences \beta on A containing \alpha and congruences on the quotient algebra A/\alpha. It sends \beta to the relation \beta/\alpha defined by

[a]_\alpha\mathrel{\beta/\alpha}[b]_\alpha\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a\mathrel\beta b.

Its inverse sends a congruence \gamma on A/\alpha to \{(a,b):[a]_\alpha\mathrel\gamma[b]_\alpha\}. Both maps preserve inclusion, hence give an isomorphism of the congruence lattices (the first restricted to the interval of congruences containing \alpha). A congruence is an equivalence relation compatible with every specified operation: if a_j\sim b_j for all inputs of an operation f, then f(a_1,\ldots,a_m)\sim f(b_1,\ldots,b_m). The quotient has equivalence classes as elements and operations induced by the same formulas on representatives. Congruences are ordered by inclusion as subsets of A\times A; meet is intersection and join is the smallest congruence containing their union.

Level justification

Undergraduate quotient groups/rings and equivalence relations suffice; allowing several finite-arity operations and imposing the same compatibility condition is one small extension, and the order maps are explicit.
