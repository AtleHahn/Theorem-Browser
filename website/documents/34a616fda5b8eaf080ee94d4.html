Statement

Let \mathcal U be an ultrafilter on a set I: I\in\mathcal U, \varnothing\notin\mathcal U, it is closed under finite intersections and supersets in I, and exactly one of A,I\setminus A lies in it for every A\subseteq I. Define m:\mathcal P(I)\to\{0,1\} by m(A)=1 when A\in\mathcal U, and m(A)=0 otherwise. Then m(\varnothing)=0, m(I)=1, and m(A\cup B)=m(A)+m(B) for disjoint subsets A,B. Moreover, for any family (M_i)_{i\in I} of nonempty sets and tuples a,b\in\prod_{i\in I}M_i, agreement on a set in \mathcal U is equivalent to m(\{i:a_i\ne b_i\})=0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Basic topology and sets supply compactness, families and intersections. A filter/ultrafilter is a family satisfying the listed closure/maximality conditions, with its extension principle assumed. Here these set-family definitions require no ultraproduct or model-theoretic semantics.

Proof 1: The ultrafilter membership recipe — outline

One may define a finitely additive measure m on the index set I by saying m(A)=1 if A\in\mathcal U and m(A)=0 otherwise. Then two members of the Cartesian product agree on a set in \mathcal U precisely if they are equal almost everywhere on the index set, meaning that their disagreement set has measure zero.
