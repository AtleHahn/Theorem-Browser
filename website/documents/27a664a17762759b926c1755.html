Statement

The series f(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{z}{k(z-k)} converges normally on compact subsets of \mathbb C\setminus\mathbb Z_{>0} and defines a meromorphic function on \mathbb C with a simple pole of residue 1 at every positive integer and no other poles.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Normal convergence means summable uniform bounds on compact sets. Meromorphicity and a simple pole’s residue add local complex differentiability and the coefficient of 1/(z-k), a limited linked extension of undergraduate series and analysis.

Proof 1: M-test for the explicit regularized series — outline

Suppose that we desire a meromorphic function with simple poles of residue 1 at all positive integers. We can let

f(z)=z\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k(z-k)}.

This series converges normally on any compact subset of \mathbb C\setminus\mathbb Z_{>0} (as can be shown using the M-test) to a meromorphic function with the desired properties.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Bound the series tail on each compact set by a constant times 1/k squared, then separate the singular summand at each integer. Undergraduate complex analysis and uniform convergence suffice.
