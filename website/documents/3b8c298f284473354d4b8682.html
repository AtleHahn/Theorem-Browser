Statement

If u,v\in\mathbb R, W=u+iv\in\mathbb C, |W|\le1 and W\ne-1, then \operatorname{Re}\frac{1-W}{1+W}=\frac{1-|W|^2}{(1+u)^2+v^2}\ge0.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The expression is an explicit identity and inequality in the real and imaginary parts of a complex number. Elementary complex arithmetic and squares suffice to understand it. This rating concerns understanding the statement, independently of the difficulty of its proof.

Mathematical Field
High School Mathematics
Required topics
Complex real parts and the unit-disk inequality
Review notes
none

Proof 1: Divide complex numbers and compare the numerator — outline

Let W=u+iv and R=(1-W)/(1+W), then the real part of R is given by,

\operatorname{Re}(R)=\frac{1-|W|^2}{(1+u)^2+v^2}.

Because |W|\leq1 and W\ne-1, then \operatorname{Re}(R)\geq0.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Multiply numerator and denominator by 1+u-iv. The real numerator becomes 1-u²-v²>=0 and the denominator is positive because W is not -1. This is elementary complex-number arithmetic.
