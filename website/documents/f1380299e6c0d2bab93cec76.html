Statement

Let (X,\mathcal A,\mu) have finite measure, and let (f_n)\subseteq L^1(\mu) be real- or complex-valued functions. If \lim_n\int_A f_n\,d\mu exists finitely for every measurable A, then (f_n) is uniformly integrable: \lim_{M\to\infty}\sup_n\int_{\{|f_n|>M\}}|f_n|\,d\mu=0.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement concerns convergence of Lebesgue integrals on every measurable set and uniform tail integrals.

Proof 1: Finite-measure criterion applied to the converse outline

The converse Vitali theorem gives an L^1 bound on (f_n) and uniformly absolutely continuous integrals: for every \varepsilon>0 some \delta>0 satisfies \mu(A)<\delta\Rightarrow\sup_n\int_A|f_n|\,d\mu<\varepsilon. Use the finite-measure criterion stated with the definitions: an L^1 family is uniformly integrable if and only if it is bounded in L^1 and has uniformly absolutely continuous integrals. Both requirements hold, so the criterion proves uniform integrability.
