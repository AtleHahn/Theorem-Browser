Statement

Let G,G' be groups with identities e,e', and let H\le G\times G'. Write p_1,p_2 for the coordinate projections and put A=p_1(H), B=p_2(H), N=\{a:(a,e')\in H\} and N'=\{b:(e,b)\in H\}. Then N\trianglelefteq A, N'\trianglelefteq B, and the image of H in A/N\times B/N' is the graph of an isomorphism A/N\to B/N'.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory group theory supplies subgroups, quotients, actions, products and homomorphisms. Perfect means generated by commutators, torsion means finite element orders, and the chain conditions are explicit sequences of subgroups. The relevant one of these closely related definitions is a small extension, without representation theory.

Proof 1: Restrict the coordinate projections to their images

The coordinate maps from H onto their images p_1(H) and p_2(H) are surjective. Apply Goursat’s lemma to H\le p_1(H)\times p_2(H); its two coordinate kernels are exactly N and N'.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Restrict both coordinate maps to their images and apply the supplied subdirect-product lemma. Undergraduate groups, kernels, normal subgroups and quotient isomorphisms suffice.
