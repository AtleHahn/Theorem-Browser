Statement

For irrational a and each positive integer M, there are integers n\ne0 and q with |n|\le M and 0<|na-q|<1/M.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement is a finite approximation bound involving irrational numbers, integer multiples and absolute-value inequalities.

Proof 1: Subtract two multiples in one fractional-part interval

Write \{x\}=x-\lfloor x\rfloor for the fractional part. The pigeonhole principle says that more objects than classes force two objects into one class. For the given positive integer M, by the pigeonhole principle there must be distinct {\displaystyle n_{1},n_{2}\in \{1,2,\ldots ,M+1\}} such that {\displaystyle n_{1}a} and {\displaystyle n_{2}a} are in the same integer subdivision of size {\displaystyle 1/M} (there are only {\displaystyle M} such subdivisions between consecutive integers). In particular, one can find {\displaystyle n_{1}},{\displaystyle n_{2}} such that

{\displaystyle n_{1}a\in \left(p+{\frac {k}{M}},\ p+{\frac {k+1}{M}}\right),\quad n_{2}a\in \left(q+{\frac {k}{M}},\ q+{\frac {k+1}{M}}\right),}

for some integers {\displaystyle p}, {\displaystyle q} and {\displaystyle k\in \{0,1,\cdots ,M-1\}}. One can then easily verify that

{\displaystyle (n_{2}-n_{1})a\in \left(q-p-{\frac {1}{M}},q-p+{\frac {1}{M}}\right).}

Put n=n_2-n_1 and take the integer q-p. Then 0<|na-(q-p)|<1/M and 0<|n|\le M; the nonzero error and avoidance of subdivision endpoints follow from irrationality.
