Statement

For nonnegative integers m,n and a prime p, write m_i,n_i for their base-p digits. Then p\mid\binom mn if and only if n_i>m_i for at least one i, with \binom mn=0 if n>m.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement concerns elementary integer arithmetic and finite binomial coefficients.

Proof 1: Detect zero factors in the digit product — outline

Use Lucas's theorem:

\binom mn\equiv\prod_i\binom{m_i}{n_i}\pmod p.

One consequence is that the binomial coefficient \binom mn is divisible by the prime p if and only if at least one of the digits of the base-p representation of n is greater than the corresponding digit of m.

The source states this consequence without writing out the factorial nondivisibility check for the digit factors.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Apply the explicit digit congruence and note that a digit binomial coefficient with both entries below p is nonzero modulo p exactly when its lower entry does not exceed its upper entry. Elementary factorial divisibility suffices.
