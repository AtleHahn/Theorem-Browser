Statement

Let h:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb R be measurable and integrable on each finite interval [0,x]. Suppose L=\lim_{x\to\infty}\int_0^xh(t)\,dt exists finitely. Then for every real s>0 the improper integral \int_0^\infty e^{-st}h(t)\,dt converges, and \lim_{s\downarrow0}\int_0^\infty e^{-st}h(t)\,dt=L.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The measurable integrand and its integrability on finite intervals require Lebesgue integration; the remaining claim concerns improper integrals and one-sided limits.

Proof 1: Integrate by parts and apply the Abelian theorem — outline

Put f(x)=\int_0^x h(t)\,\mathrm dt. Then f is locally absolutely continuous and bounded, with f(0)=0.

To see this, notice that {\displaystyle f'(t)=h(t)} almost everywhere and apply the Abelian final value theorem for bounded measurable functions with a finite limit to {\displaystyle f} after an integration by parts: For {\displaystyle s>0,}

{\displaystyle s\int _{0}^{\infty }e^{-st}f(t)\,\mathrm {d} t={\Big [}-e^{-st}f(t){\Big ]}_{t=0}^{\infty }+\int _{0}^{\infty }e^{-st}f'(t)\,\mathrm {d} t=\int _{0}^{\infty }e^{-st}h(t)\,\mathrm {d} t.}

By the Abelian final value theorem, the left-hand side converges to {\displaystyle \lim _{x\to \infty }f(x)} for {\displaystyle s\to 0.}

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The actual hypothesis allows arbitrary locally Lebesgue-integrable measurable h, not merely continuous h. The proof needs an absolutely continuous primitive, equality of its derivative with h almost everywhere, and integration by parts in that setting before invoking the final-value theorem. Those linked measure-theoretic notions exceed a small addition to a calculus/Riemann-integration baseline. The familiar-looking integration-by-parts formula alone is not the whole argument.

Fundamental theorem and integration by parts for locally absolutely continuous functions
Kind:

Theorem

Treatment:

Implicitly assumed

Statement used:

For locally Lebesgue-integrable h, f(x)=integral_0^x h is locally absolutely continuous with f′=h almost everywhere; integration by parts holds for an absolutely continuous factor and a smooth factor on each finite interval.

Application in proof:

Integrate exp(-st) against f′ on finite intervals and let the upper endpoint tend to infinity. Bounded f makes the exponential boundary term vanish and the transformed integral absolutely convergent.

Application level:
