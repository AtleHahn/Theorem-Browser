Statement

For real plane vectors u=(u_1,u_2) and v=(v_1,v_2), (u_1v_1+u_2v_2)^2\le(u_1^2+u_2^2)(v_1^2+v_2^2). Equality holds exactly when one vector is zero or the nonzero vectors point in the same or opposite directions.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The finite coordinate inequality and equality cases use elementary plane geometry.

Proof 1: Bound the squared cosine

If one vector is zero, both sides vanish. Otherwise, letting \theta be their angle,

(u\cdot v)^2=(\|u\|\|v\|\cos\theta)^2\le\|u\|^2\|v\|^2.

Equality occurs exactly when \cos^2\theta=1, that is, when the vectors have the same or opposite directions.

High school only
Level justification

Use the elementary plane dot-product cosine formula and cos squared at most one, checking the zero-vector case. School vectors and trigonometry suffice.
