Statement

Let (\Omega_1,\mu_1) and (\Omega_2,\mu_2) be sigma-finite measure spaces, with domain Lp spaces on the first and target Lq spaces on the second. Let 0<\theta<1, use 1/\infty=0, take M_0,M_1\ge0, take 1\le p_j,q_j\le\infty for j=0,1, and let T:L^{p_0}+L^{p_1}\to L^{q_0}+L^{q_1} be complex-linear with endpoint norms at most M_j. Put 1/p_\theta=(1-\theta)/p_0+\theta/p_1 and 1/q_\theta=(1-\theta)/q_0+\theta/q_1. For every simple function f\in L^{p_\theta}, \|Tf\|_{q_\theta}\le M_0^{1-\theta}M_1^\theta\|f\|_{p_\theta}.

The sigma-algebras of the two measure spaces are denoted by \Sigma_1 and \Sigma_2.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement bounds an operator on simple functions in general measure spaces, with two Lebesgue-space endpoint norms.

Proof 1: Interpolate a finite analytic pairing — outline

By symmetry, let us assume {\textstyle p_{0}<p_{1}} (the case {\textstyle p_{0}=p_{1}} trivially follows from log-convexity of the target L^q norms). Let {\textstyle f} be a simple function, that is

{\displaystyle f=\sum _{j=1}^{m}a_{j}\mathbf {1} _{A_{j}}}

for some finite {\textstyle m\in \mathbb {N} }, {\textstyle a_{j}=\left\vert a_{j}\right\vert \mathrm {e} ^{\mathrm {i} \alpha _{j}}\in \mathbb {C} } and {\textstyle A_{j}\in \Sigma _{1}}, {\textstyle j=1,2,\dots ,m}. Similarly, let {\textstyle g} denote a simple function {\textstyle \Omega _{2}\to \mathbb {C} }, namely

{\displaystyle g=\sum _{k=1}^{n}b_{k}\mathbf {1} _{B_{k}}}

for some finite {\textstyle n\in \mathbb {N} }, {\textstyle b_{k}=\left\vert b_{k}\right\vert \mathrm {e} ^{\mathrm {i} \beta _{k}}\in \mathbb {C} } and {\textstyle B_{k}\in \Sigma _{2}}, {\textstyle k=1,2,\dots ,n}.

The case f=0 is immediate, as is the case when the target measure is identically zero. Omit zero coefficients and take the level sets in each sum to be disjoint. By normalization, assume \|f\|_{p_\theta}=1 and \|g\|_{q_\theta'}=1, where 1/q+1/q'=1, including 1'=\infty and \infty'=1. Since p_0<p_1 and 0<\theta<1, p_\theta<\infty; thus every nonzero level set of f has finite measure and f belongs to both endpoint domain spaces. Similarly the nonzero level sets of g have finite measure when q_\theta'<\infty. The remaining case q_\theta'=\infty is exactly q_0=q_1=1 and is treated below.

Next, we define the two complex functions

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}u:\mathbb {C} &\to \mathbb {C} &v:\mathbb {C} &\to \mathbb {C} \\z&\mapsto u(z)={\frac {1-z}{p_{0}}}+{\frac {z}{p_{1}}}&z&\mapsto v(z)={\frac {1-z}{q_{0}}}+{\frac {z}{q_{1}}}.\end{aligned}}}

Note that, for {\textstyle z=\theta }, {\textstyle u(\theta )=p_{\theta }^{-1}} and {\textstyle v(\theta )=q_{\theta }^{-1}}. We then extend {\textstyle f} and {\textstyle g} to depend on a complex parameter {\textstyle z} as follows:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}f_{z}&=\sum _{j=1}^{m}\left\vert a_{j}\right\vert ^{\frac {u(z)}{u(\theta )}}\mathrm {e} ^{\mathrm {i} \alpha _{j}}\mathbf {1} _{A_{j}}\\g_{z}&=\sum _{k=1}^{n}\left\vert b_{k}\right\vert ^{\frac {1-v(z)}{1-v(\theta )}}\mathrm {e} ^{\mathrm {i} \beta _{k}}\mathbf {1} _{B_{k}}\end{aligned}}}

so that {\textstyle f_{\theta }=f} and {\textstyle g_{\theta }=g}. Here, we are implicitly excluding the case {\textstyle q_{0}=q_{1}=1}, which yields {\textstyle v\equiv 1}: In that case, one can simply take {\textstyle g_{z}=g}, independently of {\textstyle z}, and the following argument will only require minor adaptations.

Let us now introduce the function

{\displaystyle \Phi (z)=\int _{\Omega _{2}}(Tf_{z})g_{z}\,\mathrm {d} \mu _{2}=\sum _{j=1}^{m}\sum _{k=1}^{n}\left\vert a_{j}\right\vert ^{\frac {u(z)}{u(\theta )}}\left\vert b_{k}\right\vert ^{\frac {1-v(z)}{1-v(\theta )}}\gamma _{j,k}}

where {\textstyle \gamma _{j,k}=\mathrm {e} ^{\mathrm {i} (\alpha _{j}+\beta _{k})}\int _{\Omega _{2}}(T\mathbf {1} _{A_{j}})\mathbf {1} _{B_{k}}\,\mathrm {d} \mu _{2}} are constants independent of {\textstyle z}. We readily see that {\textstyle \Phi (z)} is an entire function, bounded on the strip {\textstyle 0\leq \operatorname {Re} z\leq 1}. Then, in order to prove the stated simple-function estimate, we only need to show that

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\left\vert \Phi (\mathrm {i} y)\right\vert &\leq \|T\|_{L^{p_{0}}\to L^{q_{0}}}&&{\text{and}}&\left\vert \Phi (1+\mathrm {i} y)\right\vert &\leq \|T\|_{L^{p_{1}}\to L^{q_{1}}}\end{aligned}}}

for all {\textstyle f_{z}} and {\textstyle g_{z}} as constructed above. Indeed, if the boundary estimates hold true, by Hadamard three-lines theorem,

{\displaystyle \left\vert \Phi (\theta +\mathrm {i} 0)\right\vert ={\biggl \vert }\int _{\Omega _{2}}(Tf)g\,\mathrm {d} \mu _{2}{\biggr \vert }\leq \|T\|_{L^{p_{0}}\to L^{q_{0}}}^{1-\theta }\|T\|_{L^{p_{1}}\to L^{q_{1}}}^{\theta }}

for all {\textstyle f} and {\textstyle g}. This means, by fixing {\textstyle f}, that

{\displaystyle \sup _{g}{\biggl \vert }\int _{\Omega _{2}}(Tf)g\,\mathrm {d} \mu _{2}{\biggr \vert }\leq \|T\|_{L^{p_{0}}\to L^{q_{0}}}^{1-\theta }\|T\|_{L^{p_{1}}\to L^{q_{1}}}^{\theta }}

where the supremum is taken with respect to all {\textstyle g} simple functions with {\textstyle \lVert g\rVert _{q_{\theta }'}=1}. The left-hand side can be rewritten by means of the following lemma. The endpoint bounds put Tf in both endpoint target spaces, and log-convexity of L^q norms gives Tf\in L^{q_\theta} before the lemma is applied.

Lemma—Let {\textstyle 1\leq p,p'\leq \infty } be conjugate exponents and let {\textstyle f} be a function in {\textstyle L^{p}(\mu _{1})}. Then

{\displaystyle \lVert f\rVert _{p}=\sup {\biggl |}\int _{\Omega _{1}}fg\,\mathrm {d} \mu _{1}{\biggr |}}

where the supremum is taken over all simple functions {\textstyle g} in {\textstyle L^{p'}(\mu _{1})} such that {\textstyle \lVert g\rVert _{p'}\leq 1}.

In our case, the lemma above implies

{\displaystyle \lVert Tf\rVert _{q_{\theta }}\leq \|T\|_{L^{p_{0}}\to L^{q_{0}}}^{1-\theta }\|T\|_{L^{p_{1}}\to L^{q_{1}}}^{\theta }}

for all simple function {\textstyle f} with {\textstyle \lVert f\rVert _{p_{\theta }}=1}. Equivalently, for a generic simple function,

{\displaystyle \lVert Tf\rVert _{q_{\theta }}\leq \|T\|_{L^{p_{0}}\to L^{q_{0}}}^{1-\theta }\|T\|_{L^{p_{1}}\to L^{q_{1}}}^{\theta }\lVert f\rVert _{p_{\theta }}.}

Proof of the boundary estimates.

Let us now prove that the boundary estimates hold. The sequence {\textstyle (A_{j})_{j=1}^{m}} consists of disjoint subsets in {\textstyle \Sigma _{1}} and, thus, each {\textstyle \xi \in \Omega _{1}} in the support of f belongs to one of them, say {\textstyle A_{\hat {\jmath }}}. Then, for {\textstyle z=\mathrm {i} y},

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\left\vert f_{\mathrm {i} y}(\xi )\right\vert &=\left\vert \left\vert a_{\hat {\jmath }}\right\vert ^{\frac {u(\mathrm {i} y)}{u(\theta )}}\right\vert \\&=\left\vert \exp {\biggl (}\log \left\vert a_{\hat {\jmath }}\right\vert {\frac {p_{\theta }}{p_{0}}}{\biggr )}\exp {\biggl (}-\mathrm {i} y\log \left\vert a_{\hat {\jmath }}\right\vert p_{\theta }{\biggl (}{\frac {1}{p_{0}}}-{\frac {1}{p_{1}}}{\biggr )}{\biggr )}\right\vert \\&=\left\vert a_{\hat {\jmath }}\right\vert ^{\frac {p_{\theta }}{p_{0}}}\\&=\left\vert f(\xi )\right\vert ^{\frac {p_{\theta }}{p_{0}}}\end{aligned}}}

which implies that {\textstyle \lVert f_{\mathrm {i} y}\rVert _{p_{0}}\leq \lVert f\rVert _{p_{\theta }}^{\frac {p_{\theta }}{p_{0}}}}. With a parallel argument, each {\textstyle \zeta \in \Omega _{2}} in the support of g belongs to one of the sets supporting {\textstyle g}, say {\textstyle B_{\hat {k}}}, and

{\displaystyle \left\vert g_{\mathrm {i} y}(\zeta )\right\vert =\left\vert b_{\hat {k}}\right\vert ^{\frac {1-1/q_{0}}{1-1/q_{\theta }}}=\left\vert g(\zeta )\right\vert ^{\frac {1-1/q_{0}}{1-1/q_{\theta }}}=\left\vert g(\zeta )\right\vert ^{\frac {q_{\theta }'}{q_{0}'}}\implies \lVert g_{\mathrm {i} y}\rVert _{q_{0}'}\leq \lVert g\rVert _{q_{\theta }'}^{\frac {q_{\theta }'}{q_{0}'}}.}

We can now bound {\textstyle \Phi (\mathrm {i} y)}: By applying Hölder’s inequality with conjugate exponents {\textstyle q_{0}} and {\textstyle q_{0}'}, we have

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\left\vert \Phi (\mathrm {i} y)\right\vert &\leq \lVert Tf_{\mathrm {i} y}\rVert _{q_{0}}\lVert g_{\mathrm {i} y}\rVert _{q_{0}'}\\&\leq \|T\|_{L^{p_{0}}\to L^{q_{0}}}\lVert f_{\mathrm {i} y}\rVert _{p_{0}}\lVert g_{\mathrm {i} y}\rVert _{q_{0}'}\\&=\|T\|_{L^{p_{0}}\to L^{q_{0}}}\lVert f\rVert _{p_{\theta }}^{\frac {p_{\theta }}{p_{0}}}\lVert g\rVert _{q_{\theta }'}^{\frac {q_{\theta }'}{q_{0}'}}\\&=\|T\|_{L^{p_{0}}\to L^{q_{0}}}.\end{aligned}}}

We can repeat the same process for {\textstyle z=1+\mathrm {i} y} to obtain {\textstyle \left\vert f_{1+\mathrm {i} y}(\xi )\right\vert =\left\vert f(\xi )\right\vert ^{p_{\theta }/p_{1}}}, {\textstyle \left\vert g_{1+\mathrm {i} y}(\zeta )\right\vert =\left\vert g(\zeta )\right\vert ^{q_{\theta }'/q_{1}'}} and, finally,

{\displaystyle \left\vert \Phi (1+\mathrm {i} y)\right\vert \leq \|T\|_{L^{p_{1}}\to L^{q_{1}}}\lVert f_{1+\mathrm {i} y}\rVert _{p_{1}}\lVert g_{1+\mathrm {i} y}\rVert _{q_{1}'}=\|T\|_{L^{p_{1}}\to L^{q_{1}}}.}

Outside the respective supports the functions are zero. Infinite endpoint exponents use the essential supremum: a nonzero coefficient raised to a purely imaginary exponent has modulus one. When q_0=q_1=1, use the constant family g_z=g throughout, so its L^\infty norm stays one. Finally, \|T\|_{L^{p_j}\to L^{q_j}}\leq M_j gives the constants in the statement.
