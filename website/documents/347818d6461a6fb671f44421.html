Statement

If G is a finite simple undirected graph on n\ge2 vertices with Laplacian eigenvalues 0,\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_{n-1}, then its spanning-tree count is t(G)=n^{-1}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\lambda_i. Here the Laplacian is L=D-A, where D is the diagonal vertex-degree matrix and A is the adjacency matrix.

Proof 1: The spectral matrix-tree formula from weighted forests

The weighted forest theorem says that the coefficient q_1 of x in x\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(x+\lambda_i) is the sum of products of component sizes over one-component spanning forests. Such forests are spanning trees and each has weight n. Hence nt(G)=q_1=\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\lambda_i, which gives the formula.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Read the coefficient of x as the product of nonzero-index eigenvalues and identify each one-component forest weight as n. Undergraduate matrices and finite graphs suffice for the supplied weighted forest formula.
