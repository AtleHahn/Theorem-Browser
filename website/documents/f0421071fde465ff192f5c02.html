Statement

If p_j is the jth prime, then for every positive integer n, \sum_{j=1}^n1/p_j is not an integer.

Proof 1: Preserve an odd numerator and even denominator

The first partial sum is ⁠1/2⁠, which has the form ⁠odd/even⁠. If the nth partial sum (for n ≥ 1) has the form ⁠odd/even⁠, write it as u/v, with u odd and v even. Then the (n + 1)st sum is

\frac uv+\frac1{p_{n+1}}=\frac{up_{n+1}+v}{vp_{n+1}}.

The (n + 1)st prime pn + 1 is odd; since this sum also has an ⁠odd/even⁠ form, this partial sum cannot be an integer (because 2 divides the denominator but not the numerator), and the induction continues.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Maintain an odd numerator and even denominator when adding reciprocals of odd primes. School parity and induction suffice.

Proof 2: Inspect divisibility of the product denominator

Rewrite the expression for the sum of the first n reciprocals of primes (or indeed the sum of the reciprocals of any nonempty finite set of primes) in terms of the least common denominator, which is the product of all these primes. Then each of these primes divides all but one of the numerator terms and hence does not divide the numerator itself; but each prime does divide the denominator. Thus the expression is irreducible and is non-integer.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Use the product of distinct primes as denominator and inspect the numerator modulo each prime. School prime divisibility and fractions suffice.
