Statement

Define L_0=\varnothing, L_{\alpha+1}=\operatorname{Def}(L_\alpha), and L_\lambda=\bigcup_{\alpha<\lambda}L_\alpha for limit ordinals \lambda, where \operatorname{Def}(S) consists of subsets of S first-order definable in (S,\in) using parameters from S. Let \alpha be an ordinal and X\subseteq L_\alpha a transitive set, meaning x\in X implies x\subseteq X. Suppose (X,\in) is an elementary substructure of (L_\alpha,\in): every first-order formula with parameters from X has the same truth value in the two structures. Then X=L_\beta for some ordinal \beta\le\alpha.

Proof 1: Condensation with a successor-stage argument (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Chapter 12, Theorem 12.1.1 and its staged proof, pp.53 onward; supplement with explicit successor argument given below.).

The linked condensation proof treats limit \alpha. Its collapse fixes every transitive subset, so it is the identity on X. For the successor case, the following direct argument completes the scope. If \alpha=\delta+1, then L_\delta is the unique element z of L_\alpha such that every element of L_\alpha is a subset of z: all elements are subsets of L_\delta, and the singletons of its elements belong to L_\alpha. Elementarity puts L_\delta in X, and transitivity puts all its elements in X. Each element of \operatorname{Def}(L_\delta) is uniquely specified by a formula relativized to L_\delta with parameters in L_\delta; elementarity therefore puts it in X. Thus X=L_\alpha. The case \alpha=0 is immediate.
