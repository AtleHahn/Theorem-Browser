Statement

Let (W_t)_{0\leq t\leq1} be a centered Gaussian process with covariance \mathbb E(W_sW_t)=\min(s,t). There exist independent standard normal variables \xi_k such that

W_t=\sqrt2\sum_{k=1}^\infty\xi_k\frac{\sin((k-\tfrac12)\pi t)}{(k-\tfrac12)\pi},

with mean-square convergence uniform in t\in[0,1].

Proof 1: Insert the Wiener eigenpairs and normalize the coefficients

Use the covariance eigenpairs

e_k(t)=\sqrt2\sin\left(\left(k-\tfrac12\right)\pi t\right), \qquad \lambda_k=\frac1{(k-\tfrac12)^2\pi^2}.

The Karhunen–Loève theorem gives W_t=\sum_k Z_ke_k(t), with convergence in mean square uniform in t, where the Gaussian coefficients Z_k are independent and have variance \lambda_k. Put \xi_k=Z_k/\sqrt{\lambda_k}.

This gives the following representation of the Wiener process: there is a sequence of independent Gaussian random variables with mean zero and variance 1 such that

W_t=\sqrt2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\xi_k \frac{\sin((k-\tfrac12)\pi t)}{(k-\tfrac12)\pi}.

The representation here is for t\in[0,1]; convergence is in the L^2 norm and uniform in t.
