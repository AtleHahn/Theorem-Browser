Statement

Let a=(\sqrt2)^{\sqrt2}, using positive real powers. Then a is transcendental, while a^{\sqrt2}=2. A number is transcendental if it is not a zero of any nonzero polynomial with rational coefficients.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Algebraic numbers are roots of nonzero rational polynomials and transcendental numbers are not; these definitions and the real-power constants require only elementary algebra.

Proof 1: Evaluate the nested positive real power

Imported theorem

Gelfond–Schneider: if a,b are algebraic numbers, a\notin\{0,1\} and b\notin\mathbb Q, then every value \exp(bL) with \exp(L)=a is transcendental. A number is algebraic if it is a root of a nonzero polynomial with rational coefficients.

Specialization

Gelfond–Schneider applied to the algebraic irrational base and exponent \sqrt2 makes a transcendental. For positive real powers, (u^v)^w=u^{vw}, and hence a^{\sqrt2}=(\sqrt2)^{\sqrt2\sqrt2}=(\sqrt2)^2=2.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the explicitly stated transcendence consequence for square root two and then ordinary positive-real power laws. Polynomial-root definitions of algebraic numbers and basic real powers are a small extension of undergraduate algebra; no algebraic-number theory proof is used.
