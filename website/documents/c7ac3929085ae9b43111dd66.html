Statement

Let f:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C be entire. Suppose there are constants C>0, \tau\in\mathbb R, and c<\pi such that

|f(z)|\leq C e^{\tau|z|}\quad(z\in\mathbb C),\qquad |f(iy)|\leq C e^{c|y|}\quad(y\in\mathbb R).

If f(n)=0 for every integer n\geq0, then f(z)=0 for every z\in\mathbb C. Here entire means complex differentiable at every point of the complex plane.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Undergraduate calculus and complex numbers suffice to learn the single definition of complex differentiability and entire function; the remaining hypotheses are explicit exponential inequalities. No specialized entire-function growth theory is required for understanding this fully specified statement.

Proof 1: Sine quotient and half-plane uniqueness (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (§1.8.3, Satz 1.28 and Lemma 1.8 with proofs, PDF pages 33–34; supporting Phragmen–Lindelof results §1.8.1–1.8.2.).

Apply Satz 1.28 to g(z)=f(-iz)/\sin(i\pi z) in the upper half-plane, removing its zeros at z=in. On half-circles |z|=n+1/2 the reciprocal sine is uniformly bounded: away from the imaginary axis its hyperbolic term is bounded below, and near that axis the half-integer radii stay away from zeros. Thus g has finite exponential type, while on the real axis it decays at rate \pi-c>0. Satz 1.28 gives g=0, and the identity theorem gives f=0.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The sine quotient requires holomorphic removal of zeros and the identity theorem. Its half-plane uniqueness input uses complex logarithm branches, maximum-modulus arguments on truncated sectors, and uniform exponential damping. Following this chain also requires the local analytic-function theory behind those principles, beyond real-variable calculus and introductory analysis. Taken together, this exceeds the additional prerequisite allowance; the rating is conservative for this boundary case.
