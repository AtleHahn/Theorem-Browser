Statement

Let H be a real or complex inner-product space, with inner product linear in the first argument, and let \|x\|^2=\langle x,x\rangle. Assume H is complete and (e_k)_{k\ge1} is an orthonormal sequence. Then for every x\in H, \sum_{k=1}^\infty|\langle x,e_k\rangle|^2\le\|x\|^2.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra supplies inner products and orthonormality, and analysis supplies completeness and nonnegative series. Arbitrary nonnegative sums are explicitly defined by finite subsums. This is a direct extension of orthogonal-coordinate inequalities without dual-space or operator theory.

Proof 1: Orthogonal projection and bounded coefficient sums

The inequality follows from the non-negativity of the norm of a vector. For any natural number {\displaystyle n}, let

{\displaystyle x_{n}=\sum _{k=1}^{n}\langle x,e_{k}\rangle e_{k}}

This vector {\displaystyle x_{n}} is the projection of {\displaystyle x} onto the subspace spanned by the first {\displaystyle n} basis vectors. The vector {\displaystyle x-x_{n}} is orthogonal to this subspace, and thus orthogonal to {\displaystyle x_{n}} itself. By the Pythagorean theorem for inner product spaces, we have {\displaystyle \|x\|^{2}=\|x_{n}\|^{2}+\|x-x_{n}\|^{2}}. The proof proceeds by computing {\displaystyle \|x-x_{n}\|^{2}}:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}0\leq \left\|x-\sum _{k=1}^{n}\langle x,e_{k}\rangle e_{k}\right\|^{2}&=\left\langle x-\sum _{k=1}^{n}\langle x,e_{k}\rangle e_{k},\;x-\sum _{j=1}^{n}\langle x,e_{j}\rangle e_{j}\right\rangle \\&=\|x\|^{2}-\sum _{k=1}^{n}{\overline {\langle x,e_{k}\rangle }}\langle x,e_{k}\rangle -\sum _{j=1}^{n}\langle x,e_{j}\rangle \langle e_{j},x\rangle +\sum _{k=1}^{n}\sum _{j=1}^{n}\langle x,e_{k}\rangle {\overline {\langle x,e_{j}\rangle }}\langle e_{k},e_{j}\rangle \\&=\|x\|^{2}-\sum _{k=1}^{n}|\langle x,e_{k}\rangle |^{2}-\sum _{j=1}^{n}|\langle x,e_{j}\rangle |^{2}+\sum _{k=1}^{n}|\langle x,e_{k}\rangle |^{2}\\&=\|x\|^{2}-\sum _{k=1}^{n}|\langle x,e_{k}\rangle |^{2}\end{aligned}}}

This holds for any {\displaystyle n\geq 1}. Since the partial sums are non-negative and bounded above by {\displaystyle \|x\|^{2}}, the series {\displaystyle \sum _{k=1}^{\infty }|\langle x,e_{k}\rangle |^{2}} converges and its sum is less than or equal to {\displaystyle \|x\|^{2}}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Expand each finite residual norm using orthonormality, then use bounded monotone convergence of the nonnegative partial sums. Undergraduate linear algebra and real analysis suffice. Completeness of H is stated but not needed for this inequality, because the proof only takes a real limit of coefficient sums, not a vector-series limit.
