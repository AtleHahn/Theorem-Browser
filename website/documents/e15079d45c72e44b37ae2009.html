Statement

Let D be a nonzero finite-dimensional associative unital division algebra over \mathbb R, with real scalars central. If D is commutative, then D\cong\mathbb R or D\cong\mathbb C as a real algebra.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

From introductory rings and real linear algebra, a real algebra combines a real vector space with bilinear multiplication, and division requires inverses for all nonzero elements. Associativity, commutativity, a unit and scalar-preserving isomorphisms are familiar algebraic conditions. These related definitions fit about 30 minutes; quaternions are proof prerequisites rather than needed to understand the two stated alternatives.

Proof 1: Eliminate the noncommutative quaternion case

According to Frobenius's theorem, every finite-dimensional associative division algebra over the real numbers is isomorphic to one of \mathbb R, \mathbb C, or \mathbb H. Of these three algebras, \mathbb R and \mathbb C are commutative, but \mathbb H is not. As a consequence, the only commutative division algebras under the stated hypotheses are \mathbb R and \mathbb C.
