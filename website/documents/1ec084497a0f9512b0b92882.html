Statement

Let L/K be finite cyclic Galois with group G=\langle\sigma\rangle of order n. A multiplicative 1-cocycle is a function \phi:G\to L^\times satisfying \phi(gh)=\phi(g)g(\phi(h)). It is determined by a=\phi(\sigma), for which N(a)=1. Its values are \phi(\sigma^i)=\prod_{j=0}^{i-1}\sigma^j(a). A coboundary \phi(g)=b/g(b) is determined by \phi(\sigma)=b/\sigma(b). Thus equality of cocycles and coboundaries gives the norm-one formulation of Hilbert 90.

Proof 1: Evaluate the cocycle on powers of a generator

Write b^\sigma=\sigma(b) and N(a)=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\sigma^j(a).

For cyclic {\displaystyle G=\{1,\sigma ,\ldots ,\sigma ^{n-1}\}}, a 1-cocycle is determined by {\displaystyle \phi (\sigma )=a\in L^{\times }}, with {\displaystyle \phi (\sigma ^{i})=a\,\sigma (a)\cdots \sigma ^{i-1}(a)} and:

{\displaystyle 1=\phi (1)=\phi (\sigma ^{n})=a\,\sigma (a)\cdots \sigma ^{n-1}(a)=N(a).}

On the other hand, a 1-coboundary is determined by {\displaystyle \phi (\sigma )=b/b^{\sigma }}. Equating cocycles and coboundaries gives the norm-one formulation of Hilbert’s Theorem 90: N(a)=1 implies a=b/\sigma(b) for some b\in L^\times.

Level justification

Although the cocycle recurrence and norm are displayed, the full setting includes a cyclic Galois extension and its automorphism action, together with cocycles and coboundaries. Retain beyond undergraduate provisionally because that combined prerequisite burden is not a confidently small extension of introductory field theory.
