Statement

Every finite alternative division ring is a field. Here an alternative division ring is a set D with an abelian-group addition and a multiplication distributing over addition on both sides, with identity 1\ne0, such that for each a\ne0 both maps x\mapsto ax and x\mapsto xa are bijections and, for all x,y\in D, x(xy)=(xx)y and (yx)x=y(xx). The conclusion says that multiplication is associative and commutative.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory abstract algebra provides rings, fields and groups. Dropping associativity and replacing it with two displayed identities is a small extension; division is explicitly given by bijections. No classification or finite-field proof theory is needed.

Proof 1: Left multiplications and a matrix-field criterion (external)

A link to the proof can be found here. Theorem 4.3, p.10; preceding Theorems 4.1–4.2 and §3 matrix theorem.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Left multiplication turns the finite alternative ring into a family of matrices. The inverse property follows from associator identities and induction on products, while the finite division-ring dependency follows from a class equation and cyclotomic polynomials. These arguments require undergraduate abstract algebra and linear algebra; the extra nonassociative notation is a small extension of ring identities.
