Statement

For integers j\ge1 and k\in\mathbb Z, let I_{j,k}=\int_0^1\theta^j e^{-2\pi i k\theta}\,d\theta, where i^2=-1. For the recurrence take I_{0,0}=1 and I_{0,k}=0 for k\ne0.

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}I_{j,k}&={\begin{cases}{\frac {1}{j+1}},&k=0;\\[4pt]-{\frac {1}{2\pi \imath \cdot k}}+{\frac {j}{2\pi \imath \cdot k}}I_{j-1,k},&k\neq 0\end{cases}}\\[6pt]&={\begin{cases}{\frac {1}{j+1}},&k=0;\\[4pt]-\sum \limits _{m=1}^{j}{\frac {j!}{(j+1-m)!}}\cdot {\frac {1}{(2\pi \imath \cdot k)^{m}}},&k\neq 0.\end{cases}}\end{aligned}}}

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves complex exponential; definite integrals; factorials. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. Proof 1: Integration by parts and recurrence

Integration by parts yields the recurrence relation that

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}I_{j,k}&={\begin{cases}{\frac {1}{j+1}},&k=0;\\[4pt]-{\frac {1}{2\pi \imath \cdot k}}+{\frac {j}{2\pi \imath \cdot k}}I_{j-1,k},&k\neq 0\end{cases}}\\[6pt]&={\begin{cases}{\frac {1}{j+1}},&k=0;\\[4pt]-\sum \limits _{m=1}^{j}{\frac {j!}{(j+1-m)!}}\cdot {\frac {1}{(2\pi \imath \cdot k)^{m}}},&k\neq 0.\end{cases}}\end{aligned}}}

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Integrate by parts once to get the recurrence and unroll it finitely; the zero frequency is the polynomial integral. This is ordinary calculus with complex exponentials.
