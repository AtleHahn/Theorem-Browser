Statement

An isometric embedding of a Riemannian manifold (M,g) in \mathbb R^n is a continuously differentiable topological embedding F such that

\langle dF_p(u),dF_p(v)\rangle_{\mathbb R^n}=g_p(u,v)\qquad(p\in M,\ u,v\in T_pM).

An isometric immersion satisfies the same identity but need not be a topological embedding.

Continuously differentiable approximation (Nash–Kuiper). Let (M,g) be an m-dimensional Riemannian manifold, let n\geq m+1, and let f:M\to\mathbb R^n be a smooth short embedding (respectively immersion). Short means that

\|df_p(v)\|^2\leq g_p(v,v)\qquad(v\in T_pM\setminus\{0\}).

There is a sequence of C^1 isometric embeddings (respectively immersions) F_j:M\to\mathbb R^n converging uniformly to f.

Smooth and analytic embedding. If (M,g) is an m-dimensional Riemannian manifold of class C^k, where 3\leq k\leq\infty, or is real analytic, it admits an isometric embedding in some \mathbb R^n of the same regularity. The dimension can be chosen to satisfy

n\leq\frac{m(3m+11)}2\quad\text{if }M\text{ is compact},

and

n\leq\frac{m(m+1)(3m+11)}2\quad\text{if }M\text{ is noncompact}.

Here a topological embedding is a map that is a homeomorphism onto its image, and T_pM is the tangent space at p.
