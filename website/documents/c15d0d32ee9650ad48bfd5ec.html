Statement

If a finite group H of prime-power order acts on a finite set \Omega, and \Omega^H=\{x:hx=x\text{ for all }h\in H\}, then |\Omega|\equiv|\Omega^H|\pmod p, where |H| is a power of the prime p.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory group theory supplies normal subgroups, cosets, kernels, actions and automorphisms. Complement, normalizer, centralizer or fixed-point sets are explicitly defined by these operations, so no representation or extension-field theory is needed.

Proof 1: Partition into orbits

Any element x\in\Omega not fixed by H will lie in an orbit of order |H|/|H_x| by orbit–stabilizer (where H_x denotes the stabilizer), which is a multiple of p by assumption. The result follows immediately by writing |\Omega| as the sum of |Hx| over all distinct orbits Hx and reducing modulo p.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Partition the finite set into orbits; orbit-stabilizer makes each nonsingleton orbit size a positive power of p, so it vanishes modulo p. Undergraduate introductory group theory suffices.
