Statement

Let K be an algebraically closed field, and let P,Q\in K[x,y,t] be homogeneous polynomials of positive degrees p,q with no common factor of positive degree. Their common points lie in \mathbb P^2(K). In coordinates chosen so that \deg_y P=p, \deg_y Q=q, and projection [x:y:t]\mapsto[x:t] separates their common points, define the multiplicity of an intersection point to be the multiplicity of its corresponding linear factor in R(x,t)=\operatorname{Res}_y(P,Q). The total of these multiplicities is pq.

Proof 1: Bézout count for plane curves using resultant multiplicities (outline)

Let P and Q be two homogeneous polynomials with no common factor of positive degree in the indeterminates x, y, t of respective degrees p and q. Their zeros are the homogeneous coordinates of two projective curves. Thus the homogeneous coordinates of their intersection points are the common zeros of P and Q.

By collecting together the powers of one indeterminate, say y, one gets univariate polynomials whose coefficients are homogeneous polynomials in x and t.

For technical reasons, one must apply a change of coordinates so the degrees in y of P and Q equal their total degrees (p and q), and each line passing through two intersection points does not pass through the point (0, 1, 0) (this means that projection to [x:t] separates the intersection points).

The resultant R(x ,t) of P and Q with respect to y is a nonzero homogeneous polynomial in x and t that has the following property: {\displaystyle R(\alpha ,\tau )=0} with {\displaystyle (\alpha ,\tau )\neq (0,0)} if and only if there exists {\displaystyle \beta } such that {\displaystyle (\alpha ,\beta ,\tau )} is a common zero of P and Q (see Resultant § Zeros). The above technical condition ensures that {\displaystyle \beta } is unique. The first above technical condition means that the degrees used in the definition of the resultant are p and q; this implies that the degree of R is pq (see Resultant § Homogeneity).

As R is a homogeneous polynomial in two indeterminates, factorization over the algebraically closed field implies that R is a product of pq linear polynomials. If one defines the multiplicity of a common zero of P and Q as the number of occurrences of the corresponding factor in the product, Bézout's theorem is thus proved.

Level justification

The conclusion is factor counting once the resultant properties are supplied, but the full proof combines projective curves over an arbitrary algebraically closed field, separating coordinate choices, elimination by a resultant and its homogeneous degree formula. This cumulative preparation is not confidently a short extension of basic polynomial algebra. Retain Beyond undergraduate provisionally; the explicit factor-based multiplicity avoids general intersection theory but does not remove the elimination and coordinate prerequisites.
