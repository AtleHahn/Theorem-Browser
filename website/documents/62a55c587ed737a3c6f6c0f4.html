Statement

Let X be a smooth projective complex surface, K a canonical divisor and D a divisor. Write \chi(D)=\sum_{i=0}^2(-1)^i\dim H^i(X,\mathcal O(D)), and use a dot for intersection numbers. Then \chi(D)=\chi(0)+\tfrac12 D\cdot(D-K).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Smooth projective complex surfaces; Divisors and intersection numbers; Sheaf cohomology. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Expand the line-bundle characteristic classes

Write h^i(L)=\dim H^i(X,L), and let c_j(X)=c_j(TX). Products of characteristic classes below are evaluated on X.

For surfaces, the Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem is essentially the Riemann–Roch theorem for surfaces combined with the Noether formula. To see this, recall that for each divisor D on a surface there is an invertible sheaf L = O(D) such that the vector space of meromorphic sections associated to D is H^0(X,L). For surfaces the Todd class is {\displaystyle 1+c_{1}(X)/2+(c_{1}(X)^{2}+c_{2}(X))/12}, and the Chern character of the sheaf L is just {\displaystyle 1+c_{1}(L)+c_{1}(L)^{2}/2}, so the Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem states that

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\chi (D)&=h^{0}(L)-h^{1}(L)+h^{2}(L)\\&={\frac {1}{2}}c_{1}(L)^{2}+{\frac {1}{2}}c_{1}(L)\,c_{1}(X)+{\frac {1}{12}}\left(c_{1}(X)^{2}+c_{2}(X)\right)\end{aligned}}}

Fortunately this can be written in a clearer form as follows. First putting D = 0 shows that

{\displaystyle \chi (0)={\frac {1}{12}}\left(c_{1}(X)^{2}+c_{2}(X)\right)}     (Noether's formula)

For invertible sheaves (line bundles) the second Chern class vanishes. Here c_1(L) corresponds to D and c_1(X) to -K. The products of second cohomology classes can be identified with intersection numbers in the Picard group, and we get a more classical version of Riemann Roch for surfaces:

{\displaystyle \chi (D)=\chi (0)+{\frac {1}{2}}(D.D-D.K)}
