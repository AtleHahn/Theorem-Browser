Statement

Let n\ge1 be an integer. In Euclidean n-space with orthonormal Cartesian coordinates, points p=(p_1,\ldots,p_n) and q=(q_1,\ldots,q_n) have segment length d(p,q)=\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^n(p_j-q_j)^2}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Finite-dimensional Euclidean space; Orthonormal Cartesian coordinates; Finite sums and square roots. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Repeat Pythagoras along the coordinate directions — outline

Repeatedly apply the Pythagorean theorem to obtain the sum of the squared coordinate differences, then take its nonnegative square root.
