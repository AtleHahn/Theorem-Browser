Statement

Let (X,\|\cdot\|) be a Banach space and (x_n) a sequence in X. If \sum_{n=1}^\infty\|x_n\|<\infty, then \sum_{n=1}^\infty x_n converges in X.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Real analysis supplies Cauchy completeness, metrics and absolute convergence, and algebra supplies the additive operations. A Banach space is a normed vector space complete for its norm metric; the normed-group axioms are explicitly supplied. This directly extends familiar convergence to another complete metric setting.

Proof 1: Norm tails control vector tails

Let the ambient space be the Banach space {\displaystyle (X,\|\,\cdot \,\|).} Let {\textstyle \sum x_{n}} be an absolutely convergent series in {\displaystyle X.} As {\textstyle \sum _{k=1}^{n}\|x_{k}\|} is a Cauchy sequence of real numbers, for any {\displaystyle \varepsilon >0} and large enough natural numbers {\displaystyle m>n} it holds:

{\displaystyle \left|\sum _{k=1}^{m}\|x_{k}\|-\sum _{k=1}^{n}\|x_{k}\|\right|=\sum _{k=n+1}^{m}\|x_{k}\|<\varepsilon .}

By the triangle inequality for the norm ǁ⋅ǁ, one immediately gets:

{\displaystyle \left\|\sum _{k=1}^{m}x_{k}-\sum _{k=1}^{n}x_{k}\right\|=\left\|\sum _{k=n+1}^{m}x_{k}\right\|\leq \sum _{k=n+1}^{m}\|x_{k}\|<\varepsilon ,}

which means that {\textstyle \sum _{k=1}^{n}x_{k}} is a Cauchy sequence in {\displaystyle X,} hence the series is convergent in {\displaystyle X.}

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof bounds a tail of vector/group partial sums by the corresponding tail of the convergent nonnegative real series, making the partial sums Cauchy. Completeness then supplies the limit. Understanding a normed vector space and completeness for its norm metric is a small extension of undergraduate linear algebra, group theory and metric-space analysis. No functional-analysis theorem beyond the defining completeness property is needed.
