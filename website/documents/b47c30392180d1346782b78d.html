Statement

Let P>0 and u,v\in L^1(\mathbb R) be complex-valued. Define their almost-everywhere periodizations u_P(x)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z}u(x-mP) and v_P(x)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z}v(x-mP). These define integrable functions on one period. Put r_P(x)=\int_0^P u_P(t)v_P(x-t)\,dt, defined almost everywhere. For an integrable P-periodic function w define \widehat w[k]=P^{-1}\int_0^P w(x)e^{-2\pi ikx/P}\,dx. Then r_P is integrable on one period and \widehat {r_P}[k]=P\widehat {u_P}[k]\widehat {v_P}[k] for every integer k.

Proof 1: Factoring the period double integral — outline

By Fubini's theorem, the integrable periodizations give an integrable convolution on one period, and the order of integration may be interchanged. Thus

\begin{aligned} \widehat{r_P}[k] &=\frac1P\int_0^P\left(\int_0^P u_P(\tau)v_P(x-\tau)\,d\tau\right)e^{-i2\pi kx/P}\,dx\\ &=\int_0^P u_P(\tau)\left(\frac1P\int_0^P v_P(x-\tau)e^{-i2\pi kx/P}\,dx\right)d\tau\\ &=\int_0^P u_P(\tau)e^{-i2\pi k\tau/P} \underbrace{\left(\frac1P\int_0^P v_P(x-\tau)e^{-i2\pi k(x-\tau)/P}\,dx\right)} _{\widehat{v_P}[k],\ \text{due to periodicity}}\,d\tau\\ &=\underbrace{\left(\int_0^P u_P(\tau)e^{-i2\pi k\tau/P}\,d\tau\right)} _{P\widehat{u_P}[k]}\widehat{v_P}[k]. \end{aligned}
