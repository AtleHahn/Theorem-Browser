Statement

Let n\ge1, 1\le p\le\infty, and let A\in\mathbb C^{n\times n} be diagonalizable, A=V\Lambda V^{-1}, with V invertible and \Lambda diagonal. Write \Lambda(A) for the eigenvalue set of A, \|\cdot\|_p for the vector p-norm and its induced matrix norm, and \kappa_p(V)=\|V\|_p\|V^{-1}\|_p. Suppose also that A is invertible. For each \delta A\in\mathbb C^{n\times n} and eigenvalue \mu of A+\delta A, there exists \lambda\in\Lambda(A) such that |\lambda-\mu|/|\lambda|\le\kappa_p(V)\|A^{-1}\delta A\|_p.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves complex matrices; eigenvalues; diagonalization; induced matrix norms. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Relative Bauer–Fike bound proof

We use the Bauer–Fike bound: if M=V D V^{-1} with D diagonal and \nu is an eigenvalue of M+E, then some \eta\in\Lambda(M) satisfies |\nu-\eta|\le\kappa_p(V)\|E\|_p.

Choose a nonzero eigenvector \boldsymbol v for \mu of A+\delta A.

Since μ is an eigenvalue of A + δA and det(A) ≠ 0, by multiplying by −A−1 from left we have:

{\displaystyle -A^{-1}(A+\delta A){\boldsymbol {v}}=-\mu A^{-1}{\boldsymbol {v}}.}

If we set:

{\displaystyle A^{a}=\mu A^{-1},\qquad (\delta A)^{a}=-A^{-1}\delta A}

then we have:

{\displaystyle \left(A^{a}+(\delta A)^{a}-I\right){\boldsymbol {v}}={\boldsymbol {0}}}

which means that 1 is an eigenvalue of Aa + (δA)a, with v as an eigenvector. Now, the eigenvalues of Aa are ⁠μ/*λi*⁠, while it has the same eigenvector matrix as A. Applying the Bauer–Fike theorem to Aa + (δA)a with eigenvalue 1, gives us:

{\displaystyle \min _{\lambda \in \Lambda (A)}\left|{\frac {\mu }{\lambda }}-1\right|=\min _{\lambda \in \Lambda (A)}{\frac {|\lambda -\mu |}{|\lambda |}}\leq \kappa _{p}(V)\left\|A^{-1}\delta A\right\|_{p}}

Level justification

The proof rewrites the eigenvector equation using A inverse and applies the explicitly supplied absolute perturbation bound to mu A inverse. Diagonalization, induced matrix norms and elementary complex algebra are undergraduate linear algebra; the condition number is explicitly a product of two norms. If mu=0, the transformed matrix is zero and is still diagonalized by the same V, so the application remains valid.
