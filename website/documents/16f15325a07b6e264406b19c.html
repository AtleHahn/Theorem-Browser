Statement

Let U\subseteq\mathbb C be simply connected and open, let a_1,\ldots,a_n be distinct points of U, and let f be holomorphic on U\setminus\{a_1,\ldots,a_n\}. For every closed rectifiable curve \gamma in U avoiding these points, \oint_\gamma f(z)\,dz=2\pi i\sum_{k=1}^n I(\gamma,a_k)\operatorname{Res}(f,a_k), where I is winding number and the residue is the coefficient of (z-a_k)^{-1} in the local Laurent expansion.

Proof 1: Reduce contours and apply Stokes — outline

The general plane curve γ must first be reduced, using the standard contour-reduction theorem, to a set of simple closed curves {\displaystyle \{\gamma _{i}\}} whose total is equivalent to {\displaystyle \gamma } for integration purposes; this reduces the problem to finding the integral of {\displaystyle f\,dz} along a Jordan curve {\displaystyle \gamma _{i}} with interior ⁠{\displaystyle V}⁠. The requirement that {\displaystyle f} be holomorphic on {\displaystyle U_{0}=U\smallsetminus \{a_{k}\}} is equivalent to the statement that the exterior derivative {\displaystyle d(f\,dz)=0} on ⁠{\displaystyle U_{0}}⁠. By Stokes’ theorem, if two planar regions {\displaystyle V} and {\displaystyle W} of {\displaystyle U} enclose the same subset {\displaystyle \{a_{j}\}} of ⁠{\displaystyle \{a_{k}\}}⁠, the regions {\displaystyle V\smallsetminus W} and {\displaystyle W\smallsetminus V} lie entirely in ⁠{\displaystyle U_{0}}⁠, hence

{\displaystyle \int _{V\smallsetminus W}d(f\,dz)-\int _{W\smallsetminus V}d(f\,dz)}

is well-defined and equal to zero. Consequently, the contour integral of f\,dz along \gamma_i=\partial V is equal to the sum of a set of integrals along paths \eta_j, each enclosing an arbitrarily small region around a single a_j. By the local Laurent coefficient integral formula, each positively oriented small-contour integral is 2\pi i\operatorname{Res}(f,a_j). Summing over \{\gamma_i\}, we recover the final expression of the contour integral in terms of the winding numbers \{I(\gamma,a_k)\}.
