Statement

A Pythagorean field is one in which every sum of two squares is a square. No finite field extension of \mathbb Q is Pythagorean.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Finite field extensions belong to undergraduate abstract algebra, and Pythagorean is explicitly the two-square closure property.

Proof 1: Apply Diller–Dress by contraposition

The Diller–Dress theorem says that if E/F is finite and E is Pythagorean, then F is Pythagorean. Every algebraic number field is finite over \mathbb Q, whereas \mathbb Q is not Pythagorean. Thus such a field cannot be Pythagorean.

Level justification

Apply the supplied finite-extension descent theorem contrapositively, using that 1+1 is not a rational square. Undergraduate fields and finite extension degree suffice; Pythagorean is a single explicitly defined square-closure property.
