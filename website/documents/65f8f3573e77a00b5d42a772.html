Statement

For every integer n\ge1, (n!)^{-1/n}\le e/(n+1), where e is Euler’s number.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The assertion compares finite products and roots with a specified real constant.

Proof 1: Apply the Stirling lower bound at n plus one — outline

The Stirling-type inequality {\displaystyle n!\geq {\sqrt {2\pi n}}\,n^{n}\mathrm {e} ^{-n}} applied to {\displaystyle n+1} implies

{\displaystyle (n!)^{-1/n}\leq {\frac {\mathrm {e} }{n+1}}} for all {\displaystyle n\geq 1.}

Level
High school only
Level justification

Apply the supplied explicit factorial inequality at n+1, divide by n+1 and use sqrt(2 pi(n+1))>=e for n>=1. This gives n!>=(n+1)^n/e^n, and taking positive nth roots proves the bound. Only algebra and comparisons of familiar constants are needed.
