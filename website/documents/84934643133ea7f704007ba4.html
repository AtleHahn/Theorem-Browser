Statement

Let n\ge2 be an integer, 1\le p<n, and p^*=np/(n-p). Then W^{1,p}(\mathbb R^n) embeds continuously in L^{p^*}(\mathbb R^n), and \|u\|_{p^*}\le C_{n,p}\|Du\|_p for every real u\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb R^n). Here W^{1,p} consists of the L^p functions with first weak derivatives in L^p.

Proof 1: Set the derivative orders to one and zero

Use the general Sobolev embedding theorem as an external prerequisite: for integers k>\ell\ge0, 1\le p<q<\infty, p<n, and 1/p-k/n=1/q-\ell/n, it gives W^{k,p}(\mathbb R^n)\subseteq W^{\ell,q}(\mathbb R^n) continuously and \|D^\ell u\|_q\le C\|D^k u\|_p.

In the special case of k = 1 and ℓ = 0, Sobolev embedding gives

{\displaystyle W^{1,p}(\mathbf {R} ^{n})\subseteq L^{p^{*}}(\mathbf {R} ^{n})}

where p∗ is the Sobolev conjugate of p, given by

{\displaystyle {\frac {1}{p^{*}}}={\frac {1}{p}}-{\frac {1}{n}}}

and for every {\displaystyle f\in W^{1,p}(\mathbf {R} ^{n})}, one has {\displaystyle f\in L^{p^{*}}(\mathbf {R} ^{n})} and

{\displaystyle {\biggl (}\int _{\mathbf {R} ^{n}}\vert f\vert ^{p^{*}}{\biggr )}^{\frac {1}{p^{*}}}\leq C{\biggl (}\int _{\mathbf {R} ^{n}}\vert \nabla f\vert ^{p}{\biggr )}^{\frac {1}{p}}.}

Proof 2: Set the interpolation weight to one

Use the Gagliardo–Nirenberg theorem as an external prerequisite: for integers 0\le j<m, 1\le q,r\le\infty, 1\le P<\infty, and j/m\le\theta\le1 with

\frac1P=\frac jn+\theta\left(\frac1r-\frac mn\right)+\frac{1-\theta}{q},

it gives \|D^ju\|_P\le C\|D^mu\|_r^\theta\|u\|_q^{1-\theta} for real u\in L^q(\mathbb R^n) with weak derivatives D^mu\in L^r. If j=0,q=\infty,rm<n, additionally require decay at infinity or membership in some finite L^s. If r>1 and m-j-n/r is a nonnegative integer, require \theta<1. The constant is independent of u, and 1/\infty=0.

The Sobolev embedding can be obtained from the Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality setting \theta=1 and the remaining parameters in such a way that

\frac1P-\frac jn=\frac1r-\frac mn

and the other hypotheses are satisfied. The result reads then

\|D^j u\|_{L^P(\mathbb R^n)}\le C\|D^m u\|_{L^r(\mathbb R^n)}.

In particular, setting j=0 and m=1 yields that P=r^*, namely the Sobolev conjugate exponent of r, and we have the embedding

W^{1,r}(\mathbb R^n)\hookrightarrow L^{r^*}(\mathbb R^n).

In this embedding we also assume that u\in L^r(\mathbb R^n). Taking q=r=p<n ensures that both exceptional cases are avoided. With P=p^* this is the stated estimate; the W^{1,p} norm controls the gradient norm and hence the embedding is continuous.
