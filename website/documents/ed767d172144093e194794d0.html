Statement

Let n\ge2r\ge2, choose an element x and an r-subset Y not containing x of an n-element set. Let \mathcal H consist of Y and all r-subsets containing x and at least one element of Y. Then \mathcal H is intersecting and has \binom{n-1}{r-1}-\binom{n-r-1}{r-1}+1 members. Binomial coefficients with too small a nonnegative upper index are zero.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The explicit finite-family recipe uses elementary subset conditions and counts.

Proof 1: Choose a center and an intersecting exceptional set — outline

Choose x and Y as in the statement, and add Y to the family of all r-subsets containing both x and at least one element of Y. This is the displayed construction with the stated count.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Any two ordinary members share x and each meets the exceptional Y. Count all r-sets through x and subtract those avoiding Y, then add Y. Elementary finite sets and binomial counting suffice.
