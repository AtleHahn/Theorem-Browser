Statement

Let Z\ge0 be a random variable with 0<\mathbb E[Z^2]<\infty, and put \mu=\mathbb E[Z] and \sigma^2=\operatorname{Var}(Z). For 0\le\theta\le1, \mathbb P(Z>\theta\mu)\ge(1-\theta)^2\mu^2/\mathbb E[Z^2].

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement needs scalar probability and first/second integral moments, a limited probability interpretation of undergraduate integration.

Proof 1: Split the expectation and apply Cauchy–Schwarz

{\displaystyle \operatorname {E} [Z]=\operatorname {E} [Z\,\mathbf {1} _{\{Z\leq \theta \operatorname {E} [Z]\}}]+\operatorname {E} [Z\,\mathbf {1} _{\{Z>\theta \operatorname {E} [Z]\}}].}

The first addend is at most {\displaystyle \theta \operatorname {E} [Z]}, while the second is at most {\displaystyle \operatorname {E} [Z^{2}]^{1/2}\operatorname {P} (Z>\theta \operatorname {E} [Z])^{1/2}} by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality. The desired inequality then follows. ∎

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Split the expectation by a threshold and bound its upper part with Cauchy-Schwarz and an indicator. Undergraduate probability and second moments suffice.
