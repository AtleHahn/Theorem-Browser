Statement

Let H be a real Hilbert space, I:H→R continuous, r,a>0, and v∈H with ||v||>r. Suppose I(u)≥a whenever ||u||=r. Let Γ be all continuous paths g:[0,1]→H with g(0)=0 and g(1)=v, and set c=inf_{g∈Γ} max_{0≤t≤1} I(g(t)). Then c≥a.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

An inner-product space with complete norm is a Hilbert space. Continuous paths, spheres, extrema and infima are familiar analysis/topology concepts; the barrier inequality needs no variational derivatives or critical-point theory.

Proof 1: Every connecting path crosses the high sphere

Consider I as describing elevation. Between the origin and v lies a range of mountains (at \|u\|=r) where the elevation is at least a>0. In order to travel along a path g from the origin to v, we must pass over the mountains. Thus the maximum elevation along each such path is at least a, and so is the infimum c of these maxima.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the real intermediate value theorem to the norm along each path, then take the infimum of the resulting maximum bounds. Undergraduate metric continuity and real analysis suffice; no variational or infinite-dimensional critical-point theory is used.
