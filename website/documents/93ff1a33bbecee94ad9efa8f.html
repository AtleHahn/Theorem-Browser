Statement

Let G be a group with identity e acting on a set X from the left, so ex=x and g(hx)=(gh)x. For x\in X let G_x=\{g:gx=x\}. The map from left cosets G/G_x to the orbit Gx given by gG_x\mapsto gx is a bijection.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory group theory supplies normal subgroups, cosets, kernels, actions and automorphisms. Complement, normalizer, centralizer or fixed-point sets are explicitly defined by these operations, so no representation or extension-field theory is needed.

Proof 1: Orbit–stabilizer bijection

For a fixed x in X, consider the map f:G\to X given by g\mapsto gx. By definition the image f(G) of this map is the orbit Gx. The condition for two elements to have the same image is

f(g)=f(h)\iff gx=hx\iff(g^{-1}h)x=x \iff g^{-1}h\in G_x\iff h\in gG_x.

In other words, f(g)=f(h) if and only if g and h lie in the same coset for the stabilizer subgroup G_x. Thus, the fiber f^{-1}(\{y\}) of f over any y in Gx is such a coset, and every such coset also occurs as a fiber. Therefore f induces a bijection

G/G_x\cong Gx

between the set G/G_x of cosets for the stabilizer subgroup and the orbit Gx, which sends gG_x\mapsto gx.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Identify equal orbit images exactly with membership in the same stabilizer coset, giving the induced bijection. Standard undergraduate group actions and cosets suffice.
