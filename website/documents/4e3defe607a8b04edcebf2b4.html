Statement

Consider formulas built from propositional variables using implication \to and negation. Let a derivation be a finite sequence whose lines are instances of the schemas (A1) P\to(Q\to P) and (A2) (P\to(Q\to R))\to((P\to Q)\to(P\to R)), or follow from earlier lines U and U\to V by modus ponens, yielding V. For every formula A, the formula A\to A has such a derivation.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The formulas and derivations are finite expressions governed by two displayed patterns and a stated inference rule.

Proof 1: Five-line derivation from the two implication schemas

Choose any formula B. The following five lines form a derivation:

  1. A\to((B\to A)\to A), an instance of A1.
  2. (A\to((B\to A)\to A))\to((A\to(B\to A))\to(A\to A)), an instance of A2.
  3. (A\to(B\to A))\to(A\to A), by modus ponens from lines 1 and 2.
  4. A\to(B\to A), an instance of A1.
  5. A\to A, by modus ponens from lines 4 and 3.
Level
High school only
Level justification

Verify the three explicit pattern instances and two modus-ponens steps. Finite symbolic substitution suffices; the calculus is fully specified.
