Statement

Let P,Q be probability measures on a measurable space (\mathcal X,\mathcal F). Write \mu=P+Q, p=dP/d\mu, q=dQ/d\mu, and v=d_{\rm TV}(P,Q)=\sup_{A\in\mathcal F}|P(A)-Q(A)|. Put D=D_{\rm KL}(P\parallel Q)=\int\log(dP/dQ)\,dP if P\ll Q, and D=+\infty otherwise; use e^{-\infty}=0. For every A\in\mathcal F, with A^c=\mathcal X\setminus A, P(A)+Q(A^c)\ge\tfrac12e^{-D}.

Proof 1: Apply the local total-variation bound and rearrange — outline

Put a=\int_{\mathcal X}\sqrt{pq}\,d\mu.

Local Bretagnolle–Huber bound

The proof is in two steps:

  1. Prove using Cauchy–Schwarz that the total variation is related to the Bhattacharyya coefficient:

    1-v^2\geq\left(\int\sqrt{pq}\,d\mu\right)^2.

  2. Prove by an application of Jensen's inequality that

    \left(\int\sqrt{pq}\,d\mu\right)^2\geq e^{-D}.

When D=+\infty, the bound follows from v\leq1; for the logarithmic calculation below suppose D<+\infty.

Total-variation identities

First notice that

v=1-\int\min(p,q)\,d\mu=\int\max(p,q)\,d\mu-1.

To see this, take an event A^* maximizing |P(A)-Q(A)| over A\in\mathcal F and, by replacing it with its complement if necessary, suppose that P(A^*)\geq Q(A^*), so that v=P(A^*)-Q(A^*). Then we can rewrite

v=\int_{A^*}\max(p,q)\,d\mu-\int_{A^*}\min(p,q)\,d\mu.

And then adding and removing \int_{(A^*)^c}\max(p,q)\,d\mu or \int_{(A^*)^c}\min(p,q)\,d\mu we obtain both identities.

Cauchy–Schwarz estimate

Then, by Cauchy–Schwarz,

\begin{aligned} 1-v^2&=(1-v)(1+v)\\ &=\left(\int\min(p,q)\,d\mu\right)\left(\int\max(p,q)\,d\mu\right)\\ &\ge\left(\int\sqrt{\min(p,q)\max(p,q)}\,d\mu\right)^2 =\left(\int\sqrt{pq}\,d\mu\right)^2, \end{aligned}

because pq=\min(p,q)\max(p,q).

Relative entropy estimate

We write (\cdot)^2=\exp(2\log(\cdot)) and apply Jensen's inequality:

\begin{aligned} a^2&=\exp\left(2\log\int\sqrt{pq}\,d\mu\right)\\ &=\exp\left(2\log\int p\sqrt{q/p}\,d\mu\right)\\ &=\exp\left(2\log\mathbb E_P[(\sqrt{p/q})^{-1}]\right)\\ &\ge\exp\left(\mathbb E_P[-\log(p/q)]\right)=e^{-D}. \end{aligned}

Ratios in the expectations are evaluated P-almost everywhere.

Combining the two estimates gives v\le\sqrt{1-e^{-D}}, and the weaker bound v\le1-\tfrac12e^{-D} follows.

Event bound

Indeed, by definition of the total variation, for any A\in\mathcal F,

\begin{aligned}Q(A)-P(A)&\le v\le1-\tfrac12e^{-D}\\&=Q(A)+Q(A^c)-\tfrac12e^{-D}.\end{aligned}

Rearranging gives P(A)+Q(A^c)\ge\tfrac12e^{-D}.
