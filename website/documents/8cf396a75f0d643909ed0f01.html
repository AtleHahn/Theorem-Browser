Statement

For positive integers n,r, every coloring of the n-element subsets of \mathbb N with r colors has an infinite set all of whose n-element subsets have one color.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Graphs, finite subset colorings and infinite sequences are elementary discrete mathematics. Matchings are disjoint edges and a ray is an infinite nonrepeating path; the conditions are explicitly given. No graph-minor or set-theoretic independence machinery is needed.

Proof 1: Specialize the tree theorem to the natural-number chain

Import Milliken’s tree theorem: in a finitely branching rooted tree with every branch of height \omega, every finite coloring of its strongly embedded subtrees of height n admits a strongly embedded infinite subtree all of whose height-n strong subtrees have one color. Here a strong subtree has the induced order, preserves one descendant for each immediate successor at every nonterminal node, and has levels on a common increasing selection of ambient levels. Take the source’s tree to be the chain \mathbb N with its usual order. Each level has one point and branching has one successor; its strong height-n subtrees are exactly increasing n-element subsets. A strong infinite subtree is an infinite subset, so Milliken’s conclusion is the required monochromatic family.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Specialize the fully stated strong-subtree theorem to the one-successor natural-number tree. The local definition reduces strong subtrees to increasing selections. Undergraduate orders and discrete mathematics suffice for this application without the advanced proof of Milliken.
