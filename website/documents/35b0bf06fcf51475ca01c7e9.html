Statement

Every bounded linear operator T on a nonseparable complex Hilbert space H has a nonzero proper closed invariant subspace.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra supplies invariant subspaces and linear operators; analysis/topology supplies completeness, closedness and countable dense sets. Hilbert means complete inner-product space, and nonseparable means no countable dense subset. These directly related definitions suffice for the existence claim without spectral theory.

Proof 1: Use the separable closed span of one orbit

For a nonzero vector x\in H, let [x] denote the linear subspace generated by the sequence \{T^n(x):n\geq0\}. The norm closure of the linear orbit [x] is separable (by construction) and hence a proper subspace and also invariant. It is nonzero since it contains x.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Take the closed linear span of one countable orbit, use rational complex combinations for separability and continuity for invariance, and compare with the assumed nonseparability. Undergraduate normed-space and metric topology suffice with separability as a small extension; no Hilbert duality or spectral theory is used.
