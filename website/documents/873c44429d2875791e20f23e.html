Statement

Let f:X\to Y be a function and L,R arbitrary sets. For any set A, use f(A)=\{f(x):x\in A\cap X\} , f^{-1}(B)=\{x\in X:f(x)\in B\}, and F_y=f^{-1}(\{y\})=\{x\in X:f(x)=y\}. Then f(X\setminus R)=Y\setminus\{y\in Y:F_y\subseteq R\}=f(X)\setminus\{y\in f(R):F_y\subseteq R\}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The fiber and image definitions reduce both expressions to existence of an input outside R. Empty fibers and complements require elementary set reasoning.

Proof 1: Substitute the whole domain in the fiber formula

Apply the image-difference fiber formula with L=X. Since every fiber lies in X, the fiber condition becomes F_y\subseteq R. This gives the formula with outer set Y, and its restriction to f(X). A y\in f(X) satisfying that fiber condition has a nonempty fiber contained in R, so y\in f(R). Thus the excluded values within f(X) may be indexed by f(R), giving the second equality.

Level justification

Set L to the whole domain in the fiber formula and distinguish empty fibers from nonempty contained fibers. Elementary sets and functions suffice.
