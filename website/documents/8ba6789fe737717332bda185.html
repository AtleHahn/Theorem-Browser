Statement

On a real or complex vector space, at most one inner product (with a fixed convention for complex linearity) induces a specified norm.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Norms, inner products and linear functionals are linear-algebra concepts; the operator norm is explicitly the unit-ball supremum. The claims use only these definitions and inequalities, without weak topologies or spectral analysis.

Proof 1: Recover all inner products by polarization

The inner product generating a norm is unique as a consequence of the polarization identity. In the real case, the polarization identity is given by any of the expressions

\begin{aligned} \langle x,y\rangle &=\tfrac14\bigl(\|x+y\|^2-\|x-y\|^2\bigr)\\ &=\tfrac12\bigl(\|x+y\|^2-\|x\|^2-\|y\|^2\bigr)\\ &=\tfrac12\bigl(\|x\|^2+\|y\|^2-\|x-y\|^2\bigr). \end{aligned}

In the complex case, with the inner product linear in the second variable, it is given by

\langle x,y\rangle =\tfrac14\bigl(\|x+y\|^2-\|x-y\|^2\bigr) +\tfrac i4\bigl(\|ix+y\|^2-\|ix-y\|^2\bigr).

If the norm is described by an inner product, then it must satisfy these formulas. Thus the uniqueness is guaranteed.
