Statement

For an integer k\geq1, set L_1(x)=\ln x and L_{j+1}(x)=\ln L_j(x). Let N_k be the least positive integer where L_k(N_k) is defined and at least 1. Empty products below equal 1. For every \epsilon>0, \sum_{n=N_k}^{\infty}[n(\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}L_j(n))L_k(n)^{1+\epsilon}]^{-1} converges.

Proof 1: Convergence after increasing the final logarithm power — proof

To see the convergence of the series, note that by the power rule and repeated application of the chain rule,

-\frac{d}{dx}\frac{1}{\epsilon L_k(x)^\epsilon} =\frac{1}{L_k(x)^{1+\epsilon}}\frac{d}{dx}L_k(x) =\cdots=\frac{1}{x\bigl(\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}L_j(x)\bigr)L_k(x)^{1+\epsilon}},

hence

\int_{N_k}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{x\bigl(\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}L_j(x)\bigr)L_k(x)^{1+\epsilon}} =\left.-\frac{1}{\epsilon L_k(x)^\epsilon}\right|_{N_k}^{\infty}<\infty.

The integrand is positive and decreasing, so the integral test gives convergence of the series.
