Statement

Let T be a consistent computably enumerable extension of Robinson arithmetic Q, and let S=\forall n\,R(n) be a \Pi_1 sentence undecidable in T, meaning T\nvdash S and T\nvdash\neg S. Assume the usual numeralwise correctness of this property: any failure at a standard natural number m yields T\vdash\neg S. Every model of T in which S is false contains a nonstandard natural number witnessing that failure; such a model exists.

Proof 1: Exclude a standard witness by numeralwise verification (outline)

Since S cannot be proven in T, the completeness theorem implies the existence of a model of T in which S is false. In fact, S is a \Pi_1 sentence, i.e. it states that some finitistic property is true of all natural numbers; so if it is false in a model, then one of the model's natural numbers is a counterexample. If this counterexample existed within the standard natural numbers, its existence would disprove S within T, by the assumed numeralwise correctness; but the assumed undecidability makes this impossible, so the counterexample must not be a standard number, and thus any model of T in which S is false must include non-standard numbers.
