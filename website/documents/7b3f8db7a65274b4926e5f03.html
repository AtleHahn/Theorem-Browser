Statement

Let R be an associative ring with identity and let \operatorname{Mod}\!\text{-}R be the category of all right R-modules. Let \mathcal C be a full subcategory closed under isomorphisms and arbitrary direct sums, such that in every short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow M_1\longrightarrow M_2\longrightarrow M_3\longrightarrow0,

M_2 belongs to \mathcal C if and only if both M_1 and M_3 do. Then the Serre quotient (\operatorname{Mod}\!\text{-}R)/\mathcal C is a Grothendieck category.

The Serre quotient has the same objects as the module category and morphism groups

\operatorname{Hom}(M,N)=\varinjlim_{M',N'}\operatorname{Hom}_R(M',N/N'),

where M'\subseteq M, N'\subseteq N, M/M'\in\mathcal C, and N'\in\mathcal C. Transition maps restrict to smaller M' and project modulo larger N'; morphisms represented in this way are identified when they agree after such restriction and projection. Composition is induced by composition after these refinements.

A Grothendieck category is an abelian category admitting all small coproducts, exact filtered colimits, and a generator. Abelian means additive, with kernels and cokernels, and with every monomorphism a kernel and every epimorphism a cokernel. Exact filtered colimits means that taking colimits of a filtered diagram of short exact sequences preserves exactness; an indexing category is filtered when every finite diagram in it has a cocone. A generator is an object G such that \operatorname{Hom}(G,-) is faithful, meaning it distinguishes distinct parallel morphisms.
