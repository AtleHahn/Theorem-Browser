Statement

Let V be a finite-dimensional real or complex inner product space with its induced norm. There is a basis e_1,\ldots,e_n such that each \|e_i\|=1 and each dual coordinate functional e^i has norm one, where e^i(e_j)=\delta_{ij} and \|e^i\|=\sup_{\|v\|\le1}|e^i(v)|.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves finite-dimensional real or complex inner product spaces; orthonormal bases; dual basis and dual norm. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Orthonormal coordinate functionals

Take any orthonormal basis \{e_i\} of V; the dual basis is \{(e_i\mid\cdot)\}. (Use the convention that the inner product is linear in its second argument.)

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Choose an orthonormal basis and identify its coordinate functionals with inner products; Cauchy–Schwarz gives norm at most one and evaluation at the basis vector gives equality. Finite-dimensional undergraduate linear algebra suffices.
