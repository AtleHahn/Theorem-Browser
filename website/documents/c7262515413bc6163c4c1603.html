Statement

Let G=(V,E) be a finite simple graph. For disjoint nonempty X,Y\subseteq V, put d(X,Y)=e(X,Y)/(|X||Y|), where e(X,Y) counts edges between X and Y. A pair (X,Y) is \varepsilon-regular if

|d(A,B)-d(X,Y)|\le\varepsilon

whenever A\subseteq X, B\subseteq Y, |A|\ge\varepsilon|X|, and |B|\ge\varepsilon|Y|.

For every \varepsilon>0 and positive integer m, there is an integer M\ge m such that every such graph with |V|\ge M has a partition into nonempty sets V_1,\ldots,V_k, where m\le k\le M, satisfying

\sum_{\substack{1\le i,j\le k,\ i\ne j\\(V_i,V_j)\text{ not }\varepsilon\text{-regular}}}|V_i||V_j|\le\varepsilon|V|^2.

The sum is over ordered pairs of distinct parts. No equal-size requirement is imposed in this formulation.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Graphs are finite vertices and edges; density, regularity and weighted exception count are explicitly defined using elementary finite counting.
