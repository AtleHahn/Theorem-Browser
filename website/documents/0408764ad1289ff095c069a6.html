Statement

For a convex Euclidean quadrilateral ABCD, choose E so that ABED is a parallelogram. If M,N are the midpoints of AC,BD, then |CE|=2|MN|.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Parallelogram bisection and the elementary intercept theorem give the segment-length equality.

Proof 1: Apply the triangle midpoint relation

{\displaystyle M} being the midpoint of {\displaystyle AC} yields {\displaystyle {\tfrac {|AC|}{|AM|}}=2}. Since {\displaystyle N} is the midpoint of {\displaystyle BD} it is also the midpoint of {\displaystyle AE}, as {\displaystyle AE} and {\displaystyle BD} are both diagonals of the parallelogram {\displaystyle ABED}. This yields {\displaystyle {\tfrac {|AE|}{|AN|}}=2} and hence {\displaystyle {\tfrac {|AC|}{|AM|}}={\tfrac {|AE|}{|AN|}}}. If A,C,E are collinear, halving positions along their line gives |MN|=|CE|/2, including C=E. Otherwise, it follows from the intercept theorem (and its converse) that {\displaystyle CE} and {\displaystyle NM} are parallel and {\displaystyle |CE|^{2}=(2|NM|)^{2}=4|NM|^{2}}, which gives |CE|=2|MN|.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The parallelogram diagonals make N the midpoint of AE, while M is the midpoint of AC. The triangle midpoint relation gives MN=CE/2. The companion separately handles collinearity and C=E by halving positions, so no nondegenerate-triangle assumption is silently applied there. All reasoning is elementary Euclidean geometry.
