Statement

Let G be finite, let \rho:G\to\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb C) be irreducible with character \chi, and suppose the conjugacy class of g has size q^d with q prime, d\ge0 an integer and q\nmid n. Then \chi(g)/n is an algebraic integer.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The normalized character and integrality claim require abstract representation and algebraic-number notions.

Proof 1: Use Bézout on the class size and character degree

Use the class-sum integrality result that q^d\chi(g)/n is an algebraic integer. Also, \chi(g) is an algebraic integer, being a sum of roots of unity.

Since q is relatively prime to n, by Bézout's identity there are two integers x and y such that:

xq^d+yn=1\quad\text{therefore}\quad \frac{\chi(g)}n=x\frac{q^d\chi(g)}n+y\chi(g).

Because a linear combination with integer coefficients of algebraic integers is again an algebraic integer, this proves the statement.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The Bezout combination is elementary, but its inputs require irreducible complex characters, conjugacy-class scalar integrality and algebraic integers together. That representation-theoretic prerequisite burden exceeds a small extension of introductory algebra.
