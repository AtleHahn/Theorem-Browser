Statement

Every finite group G of even order g>2 has a proper subgroup H with |H|>g^{1/3}. Here order means number of elements and proper means H\ne G.

Review notes

  • A nontrivial quantitative structural bound for every finite group of even order.

Proof 1: Counting involutions and central quotients (external)

The following editorial argument completes the stated scope. A supporting source is On Groups of Even Order (Section II, §§7–8: Lemma (2A), Corollary (2B), estimate (2C), and Theorem (2D) with proof, printed pp. 567–569.).

Theorem (2D), proved from (2A)–(2C), is the exact strict bound. Its counting argument treats noncentral involutions and then uses induction through a central involution, with order four as a base case. The remaining case has group order 2m with m odd. For this case an elementary supplement replaces the cited normal-complement result: the sign of the regular permutation representation is a homomorphism to {±1}; an involution acts as m disjoint transpositions and has sign -1. Its kernel is therefore a subgroup of index two. Its order m exceeds (2m)^(1/3) when m>1. This supplies the odd-index step using only finite-group actions.
