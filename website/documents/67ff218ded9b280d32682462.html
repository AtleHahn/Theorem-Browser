A rigidity-matrix row basis selects a spanning edge set

Statement

For a finite simple graph G=(V,E) with n vertices, a planar framework is a tuple p=(p_v)_{v\in V}\in(\mathbb R^2)^V. Define its rigidity matrix R(p) by (R(p)u)_{ij}=(p_i-p_j)\cdot(u_i-u_j) for each edge ij. If n\ge2 and \operatorname{rank}R(p)=2n-3, there is a spanning subgraph (V,E') with exactly 2n-3 edges whose rigidity rows are independent. Every subset of these rows remains independent.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Finite graphs; Planar bar-joint frameworks; Rigidity matrices; Matrix rank and nullity. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Select independent rows and their edges

By assumption, \operatorname{rank}R(p)=2|V|-3. In particular, R(p) has 2|V|-3 independent rows. Each row of R(p) corresponds to an edge of G, so the submatrix R(p)_{G'} with just the independent rows corresponds to a subgraph G'=(V,E') such that |E'|=2|V|-3. Furthermore, any subgraph H=(V',F) of G' corresponds to a submatrix R(p)_H of R(p)_{G'}. Since the rows of R(p)_{G'} are independent, so are the rows of R(p)_H.

Level justification

Select a row basis of the explicitly defined rigidity matrix and retain its corresponding edges. Undergraduate matrix rank and independence suffice; no rigidity theorem is used.
