Statement

Let X_1,\ldots,X_n be independent normal random variables with common mean \mu and variance q>0, where n\ge1 is an integer. With known \mu, define T_b=(n+2)^{-1}\sum_i(X_i-\mu)^2. Then \operatorname{Var}(T_b)=2nq^2/(n+2)^2 and E[(T_b-q)^2]=2q^2/(n+2)<2q^2/n, the unbiased Cramér–Rao lower bound.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement compares variance and expected squared error of a biased estimator in a normal sampling model; these statistical quantities are not elementary algebraic variables.

Proof 1: Rescale the variance and add squared bias

Write \sigma^2=q. In the first part below T=T_0=n^{-1}\sum_i(X_i-\mu)^2; in the final part T=T_b.

Suppose X is a normally distributed random variable with known mean {\displaystyle \mu } and unknown variance {\displaystyle \sigma ^{2}}. Consider the following statistic:

{\displaystyle T={\frac {\sum _{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}-\mu )^{2}}{n}}.}

Then T is unbiased for {\displaystyle \sigma ^{2}}, as {\displaystyle E(T)=\sigma ^{2}}. What is the variance of T?

{\displaystyle \operatorname {var} (T)=\operatorname {var} \left({\frac {\sum _{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}-\mu )^{2}}{n}}\right)={\frac {\sum _{i=1}^{n}\operatorname {var} (X_{i}-\mu )^{2}}{n^{2}}}={\frac {n\operatorname {var} (X-\mu )^{2}}{n^{2}}}={\frac {1}{n}}\left[\operatorname {E} \left\{(X-\mu )^{4}\right\}-\left(\operatorname {E} \{(X-\mu )^{2}\}\right)^{2}\right]}

(the second equality follows directly from the definition of variance and the fact that {\displaystyle X_{i}}'s are independent). The first term is the fourth moment about the mean and has value {\displaystyle 3(\sigma ^{2})^{2}}; the second is the square of the variance, or {\displaystyle (\sigma ^{2})^{2}}. Thus

{\displaystyle \operatorname {var} (T)={\frac {2(\sigma ^{2})^{2}}{n}}.}

Now, what is the Fisher information in the sample? Recall that the score {\displaystyle V} is defined as

{\displaystyle V={\frac {\partial }{\partial \sigma ^{2}}}\log \left[L(\sigma ^{2},X)\right]}

where {\displaystyle L} is the likelihood function. Thus in this case,

{\displaystyle \log \left[L(\sigma ^{2},X)\right]=\log \left[{\frac {1}{\sqrt {2\pi \sigma ^{2}}}}e^{-(X-\mu )^{2}/{2\sigma ^{2}}}\right]=-\log({\sqrt {2\pi \sigma ^{2}}})-{\frac {(X-\mu )^{2}}{2\sigma ^{2}}}}{\displaystyle V={\frac {\partial }{\partial \sigma ^{2}}}\log \left[L(\sigma ^{2},X)\right]={\frac {\partial }{\partial \sigma ^{2}}}\left[-\log({\sqrt {2\pi \sigma ^{2}}})-{\frac {(X-\mu )^{2}}{2\sigma ^{2}}}\right]=-{\frac {1}{2\sigma ^{2}}}+{\frac {(X-\mu )^{2}}{2(\sigma ^{2})^{2}}}}

where the second equality is from elementary calculus. Thus, the information in a single observation is just minus the expectation of the derivative of {\displaystyle V}, or

{\displaystyle I=-\operatorname {E} \left({\frac {\partial V}{\partial \sigma ^{2}}}\right)=-\operatorname {E} \left(-{\frac {(X-\mu )^{2}}{(\sigma ^{2})^{3}}}+{\frac {1}{2(\sigma ^{2})^{2}}}\right)={\frac {\sigma ^{2}}{(\sigma ^{2})^{3}}}-{\frac {1}{2(\sigma ^{2})^{2}}}={\frac {1}{2(\sigma ^{2})^{2}}}.}

Thus the information in a sample of {\displaystyle n} independent observations is just {\displaystyle n} times this, or {\displaystyle {\frac {n}{2(\sigma ^{2})^{2}}}.}

Writing I_n=nI for the information in the sample, the Cramér–Rao bound for unbiased estimators states that

{\displaystyle \operatorname {var} (T)\geq {\frac {1}{I_n}}.}

In this case, the inequality is saturated (equality is achieved), showing that the estimator is efficient.

However, we can achieve a lower mean squared error using a biased estimator. The estimator

{\displaystyle T={\frac {\sum _{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}-\mu )^{2}}{n+2}}.}

obviously has a smaller variance, which is in fact

{\displaystyle \operatorname {var} (T)={\frac {2n(\sigma ^{2})^{2}}{(n+2)^{2}}}.}

Its bias is

{\displaystyle \left({\frac {n}{n+2}}-1\right)\sigma ^{2}=-{\frac {2\sigma ^{2}}{n+2}}}

so its mean squared error is

{\displaystyle \operatorname {MSE} (T)=\left({\frac {2n}{(n+2)^{2}}}+{\frac {4}{(n+2)^{2}}}\right)(\sigma ^{2})^{2}={\frac {2(\sigma ^{2})^{2}}{n+2}}}

which is less than what unbiased estimators can achieve according to the Cramér–Rao bound.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The moment and rescaling calculations are undergraduate probability and calculus. However, the supplied proof also derives and interprets Fisher information through a likelihood score and compares all unbiased estimators through the Cramér–Rao bound. Likelihood, score, information, estimator bias and the regularity assumptions behind that universal comparison form a cumulative statistical prerequisite package. Without assuming a specialist statistics course, a short extension is not confidently justified, so retain Beyond undergraduate provisionally.

Cramér–Rao inequality for unbiased estimators, with additive Fisher information for independent observations
Kind:

Theorem

Treatment:

Cited without proof

Statement used:

In a regular one-parameter model, an unbiased estimator of the parameter has variance at least the reciprocal of its Fisher information; information adds for independent observations.

Application in proof:

Use the calculated one-observation information 1/(2q^2).

Application level:

Beyond undergraduate level

Level justification:

The application requires understanding the score-information identities and the regularity allowing differentiation of the model’s expectations.

Review notes:

The normal density has parameter-independent support and smooth integrable derivatives locally for q>0, so this model meets the standard regularity conditions. The bound is for unbiased estimators; the smaller biased MSE is not a contradiction.
