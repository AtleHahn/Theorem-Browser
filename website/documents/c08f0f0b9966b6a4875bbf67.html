Statement

Fix an integer k\ge2 and a finite k-uniform hypergraph F with at least one hyperedge. A k-uniform hypergraph on n vertices is a family of k-element subsets of an n-element set. Let \operatorname{ex}(n,F) be the largest size of such a family containing no copy of F. Then \pi(F)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{ex}(n,F)/\binom nk exists and lies in [0,1].

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves finite uniform hypergraphs; forbidden subhypergraphs; normalized extremal counts; limits of real sequences. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Apply bounded monotone convergence — outline

Use the source-stated monotonicity: \operatorname{ex}(n,F)/\binom nk is nonincreasing for integers n\ge k. The source concludes that its limit therefore converges. The normalized counts lie in [0,1], so bounded monotone convergence gives the asserted limit.
