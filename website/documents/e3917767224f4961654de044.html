Statement

Let T be a compact region homeomorphic to a closed disk in a Riemannian surface, whose piecewise smooth boundary consists of three geodesic arcs meeting at interior angles \theta_1,\theta_2,\theta_3. Then \theta_1+\theta_2+\theta_3=\pi+\int_TK\,dA.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement uses Riemannian surfaces, disk-bounding geodesic triangles and Gaussian-curvature integrals, beyond the stipulated calculus and linear-algebra baseline.

Proof 1: Angle sum of a geodesic triangle — proof

For the disk region T in the statement, the Gauss–Bonnet formula is

{\displaystyle \int _{T}K=2\pi -\sum \alpha -\int _{\partial T}\kappa _{g},}

where T is a geodesic triangle. We can then apply Gauss–Bonnet to the surface T formed by the inside of that triangle and the piecewise boundary of the triangle.

The geodesic curvature of the bordering geodesics is 0, and the Euler characteristic of T is 1.

Hence the sum of the turning angles of the geodesic triangle is equal to 2π minus the total curvature within the triangle. Since the turning angle at a corner is equal to π minus the interior angle, we can rephrase this as follows:

The sum of interior angles of a geodesic triangle is equal to π plus the total curvature enclosed by the triangle: {\displaystyle \sum (\pi -\alpha )=\pi +\int _{T}K.}

Here \alpha_i=\pi-\theta_i are the exterior turns, and the curvature integral uses the Riemannian area measure.

Level justification

Apply Gauss-Bonnet to the disk, remove the zero geodesic-curvature terms and convert exterior turns to interior angles. Riemannian surfaces, geodesics and integrated Gaussian curvature remain substantial differential geometry.
