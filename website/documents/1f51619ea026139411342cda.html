Statement

Let 0<\delta<1 and let \lambda be holomorphic on a neighborhood of \{z:\operatorname{Re}z\ge-\delta\}, with |\lambda(v+iw)|\le Ce^{Pv+A|w|} there, where C>0, P is real and A<\pi. The series F(x)=\sum_{n\ge0}(-x)^n\lambda(n), initially defined for 0<x<e^{-P}, continues to positive x by the Mellin–Barnes integral in the proof. For 0<\operatorname{Re}s<\delta, \int_0^\infty x^{s-1}F(x)\,dx=\pi\lambda(-s)/\sin(\pi s).

Proof 1: Hardy’s residue and Mellin-inversion argument — outline

Let \lambda(z) be an analytic single-valued function defined on a neighborhood of the half plane

H(\delta)=\{z\in\mathbb C:\operatorname{Re}z\ge-\delta\}

for some 0<\delta<1. Suppose that, for some A<\pi, \lambda satisfies the growth condition

|\lambda(v+iw)|\le Ce^{Pv+A|w|}

for all z=v+iw\in H(\delta). Let 0<x<e^{-P}. This implies the series F(x)=\sum_{n=0}^\infty(-x)^n\lambda(n) converges. Observing that \pi/\sin(\pi z) has poles at the nonpositive integers z=-n with residue (-1)^n, application of the residue theorem yields

F(x)=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi z)}\lambda(-z)x^{-z}\,dz

for any 0<c<\delta. This integral converges absolutely and uniformly for c\in(a,b) for any 0<a<b<\delta, and defines the continuation of F to positive x. Applying the Mellin inversion theorem yields

\int_0^\infty x^{s-1}F(x)\,dx=\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi s)}\lambda(-s),\qquad 0<\operatorname{Re}s<\delta.

The source leaves the contour estimates and the details of Mellin inversion implicit.
