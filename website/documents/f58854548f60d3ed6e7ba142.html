Beth's theorem shows that, in first-order logic, a property uniquely determined by the vocabulary of a theory is explicitly definable in that vocabulary. It connects semantic dependence with linguistic expressibility and informs debates about definition and reduction; the conclusion depends on the logical framework.

Statement

Let L\subseteq L^{\prime} be first-order languages, let T be a set of L^{\prime}-sentences, and let \varphi(x_1,\ldots,x_n) be an L^{\prime}-formula. A structure interprets the symbols of its language on a nonempty underlying set; its reduct to L forgets interpretations of symbols outside L. The following conditions are equivalent.

(1) Whenever A and B are models of T with exactly the same L-reduct (in particular the same underlying set), A\models\varphi(\bar a) if and only if B\models\varphi(\bar a) for every tuple \bar a in that set.

(2) There is an L-formula \psi(x_1,\ldots,x_n) such that every model of T satisfies \forall\bar x\,(\varphi(\bar x)\leftrightarrow\psi(\bar x)).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The simultaneous syntax/model/reduct/definability machinery is beyond the ordinary undergraduate baseline and cannot confidently be learned as a small extension.
