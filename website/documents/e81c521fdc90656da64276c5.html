For positive x,y, x²+y²≥2xy.

Proof 1: Compare (2,0) with (1,1)

Write \sum_{\mathrm{sym}}x_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots x_n^{\alpha_n} for the sum over all n! permutations of the variables. We use Muirhead's inequality: for decreasing exponent vectors b,a with equal total sum and \sum_{i\le j}b_i\ge\sum_{i\le j}a_i for j<n, the symmetric sum for b is at least the symmetric sum for a. These inequalities say that b majorizes a.

We seek to prove that x^2+y^2\ge2xy by using bunching (Muirhead's inequality). We transform it in the symmetric-sum notation:

\sum_{\mathrm{sym}}x^2y^0\ge\sum_{\mathrm{sym}}x^1y^1.

The sequence (2,0) majorizes the sequence (1,1), thus the inequality holds by bunching.

Compare the two explicitly defined exponent vectors by their partial sums and identify the two symmetric sums. School algebra and finite inequalities suffice to apply Muirhead.
