Statement

Fix a>0. Let H consist of the continuous representatives of L^2(\mathbb R) functions whose L^2 Fourier transforms F are supported in [-a,a], with \langle f,g\rangle=\int_{\mathbb R}f\overline g. Then H is an RKHS, |f(x)|\le\sqrt{a/\pi}\|f\|_2, and K_x(y)=\sin(a(y-x))/(\pi(y-x)), taking value a/\pi when y=x.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The domain is a Lebesgue L² Fourier-support subspace with pointwise continuous representatives and a reproducing kernel.

Proof 1: Use Fourier inversion, Plancherel and the shifted sinc

Since H is a closed subspace of L^2(\mathbb R), it is a Hilbert space.

From the Fourier inversion theorem, we have

{\displaystyle f(x)={\frac {1}{2\pi }}\int _{-a}^{a}F(\omega )e^{ix\omega }\,d\omega .}

It then follows by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and Plancherel's theorem that, for all {\displaystyle x},

{\displaystyle |f(x)|\leq {\frac {1}{2\pi }}{\sqrt {2a\int _{-a}^{a}|F(\omega )|^{2}\,d\omega }}={\frac {\sqrt {2a}}{2\pi }}{\sqrt {\int _{-\infty }^{\infty }|F(\omega )|^{2}\,d\omega }}={\sqrt {\frac {a}{\pi }}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}.}

This inequality shows that the evaluation functional is bounded, proving that {\displaystyle H} is indeed a RKHS.

Here \operatorname{sinc}(t)=\sin(\pi t)/(\pi t), with value 1 at t=0. The kernel function {\displaystyle K_{x}} in this case is given by

{\displaystyle K_{x}(y)={\frac {a}{\pi }}\operatorname {sinc} \left({\frac {a}{\pi }}(y-x)\right)={\frac {\sin(a(y-x))}{\pi (y-x)}}.}

The L^2 Fourier transform of {\displaystyle K_{x}(y)} defined above is given almost everywhere by

{\displaystyle \widehat K_x(\omega)={\begin{cases}e^{-i\omega x}&{\text{if }}\omega \in [-a,a],\\0&{\textrm {otherwise}},\end{cases}}}

which is a consequence of the time-shifting property of the Fourier transform. Consequently, using Plancherel's theorem, we have

{\displaystyle \langle f,K_{x}\rangle _{L^{2}}=\int _{-\infty }^{\infty }f(y)\cdot {\overline {K_{x}(y)}}\,dy={\frac {1}{2\pi }}\int _{-a}^{a}F(\omega )\cdot e^{i\omega x}\,d\omega =f(x).}

Thus we obtain the reproducing property of the kernel.

Level justification

The proof combines Hilbert subspaces of L2, Fourier inversion and Plancherel, point evaluation on continuous representatives and a reproducing kernel. These are cumulative harmonic-analysis and functional-analysis prerequisites beyond the short undergraduate allowance. Checking the displayed constants by Cauchy–Schwarz is only one part of understanding the full argument.
