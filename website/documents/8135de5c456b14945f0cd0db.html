Statement

Let X and Y be Banach spaces over the same field \mathbb R or \mathbb C, and let T:D(T)\subseteq X\to Y be a linear operator with dense domain and closed graph in X\times Y. Write X' and Y' for the continuous linear duals. Its transpose T':D(T')\subseteq Y'\to X' is defined by

D(T')=\{y'\in Y': x\mapsto y'(Tx)\text{ is continuous on }D(T)\text{ in the norm inherited from }X\},

with T'y' the unique continuous extension of that functional to X.

Write R(S) and N(S) for the range and kernel of an operator S. The following conditions are equivalent:

  1. R(T) is norm-closed in Y.
  2. R(T') is norm-closed in X'.
  3. R(T)=\{y\in Y:y'(y)=0\text{ for every }y'\in N(T')\}.
  4. R(T')=\{x'\in X':x'(x)=0\text{ for every }x\in N(T)\}.

A Banach space is a complete normed vector space; its continuous dual carries the operator norm.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The full unbounded-operator formulation combines Banach duals, restricted dense domains, continuous-extension transposes and annihilator duality. These interdependent functional-analysis objects exceed a confident 30-minute extension of undergraduate normed-space and linear-algebra background. The conservative classification is beyond undergraduate.

Proof 1: Closed graph and annihilator sum duality (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorem 2.19, p.46; Theorem 2.16 and Corollary 2.18).

For complex spaces, apply the real-space argument to the underlying real Banach spaces. The isometric real-linear correspondence f\mapsto\operatorname{Re}f between complex and real continuous duals has inverse \ell\mapsto[x\mapsto\ell(x)-i\ell(ix)]. It intertwines the transposes and preserves annihilators of complex subspaces, so all four real equivalences give the stated complex ones.
