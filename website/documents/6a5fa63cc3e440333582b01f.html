Statement

A graph here is a finite simple undirected graph. A cycle is a closed path with at least three distinct vertices, and a feedback vertex set meets every cycle. Let f satisfy the Erdős–Pósa alternative: for every integer k\ge1, every graph either has k vertex-disjoint cycles or a feedback vertex set of size at most f(k). If a graph has two vertex-disjoint cycles but no three, its minimum feedback-set size lies between 2 and f(3).

Level
High school only
Level justification

Cycle packing and feedback sets are explicitly finite graph notions, and the alternative is assumed.

Proof 1: Combine packing count with the k=3 theorem

The two disjoint cycles require at least two removed vertices. Since no three disjoint cycles exist, the assumed alternative for f with k=3 supplies a feedback set of size at most f(3).

Level justification

Each of the two disjoint cycles requires its own removed vertex, and the explicit alternative at k=3 gives the upper bound. Elementary finite graph counting suffices.
