Statement

For an integer n\ge1, let S^n=\{x\in\mathbb R^{n+1}:\|x\|=1\} and b_j=\dim_{\mathbb Q}H_j(S^n;\mathbb Q). The Euler characteristic \chi(S^n)=\sum_{j\ge0}(-1)^jb_j equals 1+(-1)^n: it is 0 for odd n and 2 for even n.

Proof 1: Insert the two nonzero homology ranks

For n\ge1, the n-dimensional sphere has singular homology groups equal to

{\displaystyle H_{k}(\mathrm {S} ^{n})={\begin{cases}\mathbb {Z} ,&{\text{if }}k=0{\text{ or }}k=n,\\\{0\}&{\text{otherwise,}}\end{cases}}}

hence has Betti number 1 in dimensions 0 and n, and all other Betti numbers are 0. Its Euler characteristic is then χ = 1 + (−1) n  ; that is, either 0 if n is odd, or 2 if n is even.

Level justification

Insert the ranks of singular homology into its alternating sum. Understanding homology and Betti numbers of spaces remains an algebraic-topology prerequisite; the final arithmetic does not remove that burden.
