Statement

For a nonnegative Lebesgue-measurable function h:\mathbb R\to[0,\infty), put L_h(t)=\{x:h(x)\ge t\}. Then \int_{\mathbb R}h(x)\,dx=\int_0^\infty\mu(L_h(t))\,dt, with extended nonnegative integrals allowed.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The identity is a measure-theoretic integral formula.

Proof 1: Apply nonnegative Fubini to the superlevel sets outline

Let L_h(t)=\{x:h(x)\ge t\}. For nonnegative h, Fubini’s theorem gives \int_{\mathbb R}h(x)\,dx=\int_{t\ge0}\mu(L_h(t))\,dt.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply nonnegative Fubini to the indicator of 0<=t<=h(x). General measurable superlevel sets and extended Lebesgue product integrals require measure theory.
