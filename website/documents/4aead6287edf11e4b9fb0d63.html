Statement

In a standard double bubble in \mathbb R^3, suppose the two enclosed volumes and the radii r_1,r_2>0 of the two outer spherical patches are equal. Then the separating surface is a flat disk. Here a standard double bubble has three spherical or planar patches meeting along a circle at angles of 120^\circ.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The locally described sphere/disk configuration and its conclusion require only elementary solid geometry.

Proof 1: Substitute equal outer radii — outline

Use the standard-double-bubble reciprocal-radius relation 1/r_1=1/r_2+1/r_3, with r_3 the separating radius. When r_1=r_2, solving for the middle radius leads to the zero-denominator case. The separating surface is then a flat disk, interpreted as a patch of an infinite-radius sphere.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The standard bubble is explicitly defined by spherical or planar patches. Insert equal radii into the supplied reciprocal-radius relation; its stated zero-curvature convention gives the flat disk. No variational or surface-curvature theory must be developed.
