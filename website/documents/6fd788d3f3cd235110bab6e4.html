Statement

Let ABC be a nondegenerate triangle and P an interior point. Write d_a,d_b,d_c for the perpendicular distances of P from the lines BC,CA,AB, respectively. Then PA+PB+PC\ge2(d_a+d_b+d_c).

Proof 1: Compare bisector segments with distances to side lines

Barrow inequality (dependency)

Let ABC be a nondegenerate triangle and P a point strictly inside it. Let U,V,W be the intersections of the internal bisectors of \angle BPC,\angle CPA,\angle APB with BC,CA,AB, respectively. Then PA+PB+PC\ge2(PU+PV+PW).

Comparison with perpendicular distances

The source’s strengthening comparison is

PA+PB+PC\ge2(PU+PV+PW)\ge2(d_a+d_b+d_c).

Here U,V,W lie on the respective sides, so their distances from P are at least the corresponding distances from P to the side lines.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Apply the supplied triangle inequality for internal angle-bisector segments and compare each segment with the perpendicular distance to its side line. All prerequisites are elementary Euclidean geometry.
