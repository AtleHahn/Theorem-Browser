Statement

Let (X,\Sigma) be a measurable space and let \mu be a countably additive signed measure with \mu(\varnothing)=0, allowed to take at most one of +\infty,-\infty. A measurable set is positive (negative) if every measurable subset has nonnegative (nonpositive) measure. Given a Hahn partition X=P\sqcup N, define for E\in\Sigma \mu^+(E)=\mu(E\cap P),\qquad\mu^-(E)=-\mu(E\cap N). Then \mu^+,\mu^- are nonnegative measures, at least one is finite, \mu=\mu^+-\mu^-, and \mu^+(N)=\mu^-(P)=0.

Proof 1: Construct positive and negative measures from a Hahn partition (outline)

The source prescribes the two restrictions. Positivity and negativity of the pieces make the restricted values nonnegative. Intersecting any disjoint measurable union with P or N and applying countable additivity proves that each restriction is a measure. Splitting E=(E\cap P)\sqcup(E\cap N) gives \mu(E)=\mu^+(E)-\mu^-(E). The two total masses cannot both be infinite, since that would make \mu(P)=+\infty and \mu(N)=-\infty, forbidden for a signed measure. Finally P\cap N=\varnothing gives the stated vanishing.
