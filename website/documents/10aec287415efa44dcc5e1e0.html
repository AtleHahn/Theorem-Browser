Statement

Every smooth Riemannian surface homeomorphic to the two-dimensional sphere S^2 has at least three geometrically distinct simple closed geodesics.

A Riemannian metric assigns smoothly varying inner products to the tangent planes, hence lengths to curves. A geodesic is a curve parametrized at constant speed that locally minimizes length on sufficiently short subarcs. A simple closed geodesic is a nonconstant periodic geodesic whose image is an embedded circle, with no self-intersections. Geometrically distinct means having different images, so changing the starting point, orientation or parametrization does not count as a new geodesic.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Surface topology and the explicit local-length definition of geodesics are accessible through undergraduate multivariable calculus and a short geometric introduction; no curvature or min-max machinery is needed.
