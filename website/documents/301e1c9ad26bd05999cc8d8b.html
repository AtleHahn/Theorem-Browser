Statement

Let \mu>0 and let f:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R be continuously differentiable, with an attained global minimum f^*=f(x^*). A continuously differentiable function f is \mu-strongly convex if f(v)\geq f(u)+\langle\nabla f(u),v-u\rangle+\frac\mu2\|v-u\|^2 for all u,v. If f is \mu-strongly convex, then \frac12\|\nabla f(x)\|^2\geq\mu(f(x)-f^*) for every x.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves multivariable real calculus; Euclidean gradients and norms; convexity inequalities. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Source argument

By definition, {\textstyle f(y)\geq f(x)+\nabla f(x)^{T}(y-x)+{\frac {\mu }{2}}\lVert y-x\rVert ^{2}}. Now, minimize the left side, we have

{\displaystyle f(x^{*})\geq f(x)+\nabla f(x)^{T}(x^{*}-x)+{\frac {\mu }{2}}\lVert x^{*}-x\rVert ^{2}}

then minimize the right side, we have

{\displaystyle f(x)+\nabla f(x)^{T}(x^{*}-x)+{\frac {\mu }{2}}\lVert x^{*}-x\rVert ^{2}\geq f(x)-{\frac {1}{2\mu }}\|\nabla f(x)\|^{2}}

Combining the two, we have the {\textstyle \mu }-PL inequality.
