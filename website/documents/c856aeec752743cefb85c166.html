Statement

Let a,b>0 and r>-1 be real. With \Gamma(s)=\int_0^\infty t^{s-1}e^{-t}\,dt for s>0, \int_0^\infty x^r e^{-ax^b}\,dx=\frac{\Gamma((r+1)/b)}{b a^{(r+1)/b}}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Real improper integrals; Gamma function defined by an integral; Real powers. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Power-exponential integral in terms of Gamma — proof

The substitution t=ax^b in the Gamma integral gives \Gamma(z)=a^z b\int_0^\infty x^{bz-1}e^{-ax^b}\,dx. Set z=(r+1)/b.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Substitute t=a x^b in the explicitly defined Gamma integral and set z=(r+1)/b. Ordinary real improper integration suffices.
