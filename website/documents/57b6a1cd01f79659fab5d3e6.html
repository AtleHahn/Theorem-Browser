Statement

Let k be an integer and f\in C^1([k,k+1];\mathbb C). With P_1(x)=x-\lfloor x\rfloor-1/2, \int_k^{k+1}f(x)\,dx=(f(k)+f(k+1))/2-\int_k^{k+1}f^{\prime}(x)P_1(x)\,dx.

Proof 1: Integrate against the local affine Bernoulli polynomial

Let k be an integer, and consider the integral

\int_k^{k+1}f(x)\,dx=\int_k^{k+1}u\,dv,

where

\begin{aligned} u&=f(x),\\ du&=f'(x)\,dx,\\ dv&=dx,\\ v&=B_1(x-k)=x-k-\tfrac12. \end{aligned}

Here B_1(t)=t-\tfrac12, so v=P_1(x) for k\leq x<k+1; the affine function B_1(x-k) supplies the endpoint values in integration by parts. Integrating by parts, we get

\begin{aligned} \int_k^{k+1}f(x)\,dx &=[uv]_k^{k+1}-\int_k^{k+1}v\,du\\ &=[f(x)B_1(x-k)]_k^{k+1}-\int_k^{k+1}f'(x)P_1(x)\,dx\\ &=B_1(1)f(k+1)-B_1(0)f(k)-\int_k^{k+1}f'(x)P_1(x)\,dx. \end{aligned}

Using B_1(0)=-\tfrac12 and B_1(1)=\tfrac12 gives the identity.
