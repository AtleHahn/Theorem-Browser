Statement

Let \Omega\subseteq\mathbb C be open and let f:\Omega\to\mathbb C be continuous. Suppose there is a countable set E\subseteq\Omega such that the real partial derivatives \partial f/\partial x and \partial f/\partial y exist at every z=x+iy\in\Omega\setminus E.

Then f is holomorphic on \Omega if and only if

\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(z)+i\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}(z)=0\qquad(z\in\Omega\setminus E).

Here holomorphic means complex differentiable at every point of the open set, and no continuity of the partial derivatives is assumed.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Multivariable calculus and real-analysis countability suffice; complex differentiability is a short extension of ordinary difference quotients. The statement gives the Cauchy-Riemann equation explicitly and requires no advanced function theory.

Proof 1: Baire category and rectangle integrals (external)

The following editorial argument completes the stated scope. A supporting source is Complex Analysis in One Variable, Section 1.6: The Looman–Menchoff theorem (excerpt) (Printed pp.44–50, Lemmas 4–5 and Theorem 1; countable-exception Baire and interpolation modifications given below.).

The following extension of Narasimhan §1.6, Lemmas 4–5 and Theorem 1, allows a countable exceptional set. Let S be the relatively closed set where f is not locally holomorphic. S has no isolated points, since continuity makes an isolated analytic singularity removable. Cover S by the closed sets of points having uniformly bounded coordinate difference quotients at scale 1/k, together with the countably many singleton exceptional points. Those singletons are nowhere dense in S. Baire therefore gives a nonempty relatively open piece of S on which the coordinate bounds are uniform, just as in the source proof. Lemma 4 still holds when the derivative fails on a countable set: its piecewise-linear interpolation is Lipschitz, its derivative agrees with the original derivative at nonisolated points of the closed set wherever both derivatives exist, and the omitted points have measure zero. The proof of Lemma 5 then works on each horizontal and vertical section (each has at most countably many exceptions), with derivative integrands defined arbitrarily on those null points. The Cauchy–Riemann integrand is zero almost everywhere. The source rectangle subdivision and outer-measure estimate force every rectangle integral in that neighborhood to vanish; Morera contradicts its meeting S. This proves S empty. The converse is immediate. The countable exceptional set and the relatively closed singular set play different roles in this argument.
