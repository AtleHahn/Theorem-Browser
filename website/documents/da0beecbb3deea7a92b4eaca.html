Statement

Let A be a reduced commutative Noetherian ring with identity and dimension one, and let K be its total ring of fractions. Suppose B is a reduced ring with A\subseteq B\subseteq L, where L is a finite K-algebra. Write KB for the K-subalgebra generated by B. There is an intermediate ring A\subseteq C\subseteq B finite as an A-module, Noetherian and of dimension one, whose total ring of fractions is KB.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The full claim combines reduced Noetherian rings, Krull dimension, total rings of fractions and finiteness of extensions. Understanding their interaction requires more than a brief introduction to commutative algebra. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Clearing denominators in a finite spanning set — outline

First observe that A\subseteq B\subseteq KB and KB is finite over K, so we may assume without loss of generality that L=KB. Then

L=Kx_1+\cdots+Kx_n

for some x_1,\ldots,x_n\in B. Since each x_i is integral over K, there exists a non-zero-divisor a_i\in A such that a_ix_i is integral over A, by clearing denominators. Let

C=A[a_1x_1,\ldots,a_nx_n].

Then C is finite as an A-module, hence is a one-dimensional Noetherian ring, and

C\subseteq B\subseteq Q(C),\qquad Q(C)=KB,

where Q(C) denotes the total ring of fractions of C. Thus we can substitute C for A and reduce to the case in which B lies in the total ring of fractions of the base ring.
