Proof 1: Apply the complement theorem to the subgroup of order three

An example where the Schur–Zassenhaus theorem applies is the symmetric group on 3 symbols, S_3, which has a normal subgroup of order 3 (isomorphic with C_3) which in turn has index 2 in S_3 (in agreement with the theorem of Lagrange), so S_3/C_3\cong C_2. Since 2 and 3 are relatively prime, the Schur–Zassenhaus theorem applies and S_3\cong C_3\rtimes C_2. Note that the automorphism group of C_3 is C_2 and the automorphism of C_3 used in the semidirect product that gives rise to S_3 is the nontrivial automorphism that permutes the two nonidentity elements of C_3.

Level justification

Apply the stated normal coprime-order complement theorem to A3 and identify the nontrivial action of C2. Introductory finite group theory plus the semidirect-product description as a normal factor and complement is a small related extension; no cohomological proof is invoked.
