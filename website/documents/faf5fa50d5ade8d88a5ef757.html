Statement

Let R be an associative ring with identity, not necessarily commutative, let I be a two-sided ideal, and let M\subseteq R be a nonempty subset closed under multiplication. If I\cap M=\varnothing, then there exists a proper two-sided prime ideal P such that

I\subseteq P,\qquad P\cap M=\varnothing.

An ideal is an additive subgroup closed under multiplication on both sides by elements of R. Prime here means that for any two-sided ideals J,K, the inclusion JK\subseteq P implies J\subseteq P or K\subseteq P, where JK consists of finite sums of products jk with j\in J, k\in K. In a commutative ring this is equivalent to ab\in P implying a\in P or b\in P. Proper means P\ne R. The hypotheses imply 0\notin M. The usual convention 1\in M is allowed but is not required for the statement.
