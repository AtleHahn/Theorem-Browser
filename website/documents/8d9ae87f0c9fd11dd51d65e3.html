Statement

Let 1\le m<N, and write \mathbb R^N=\mathbb R^m\times\mathbb R^{N-m}. For f\in L^1(\mathbb R^N;\mathbb C) define p(u)=\int_{\mathbb R^{N-m}}f(u,v)\,dv almost everywhere. For the Fourier convention \widehat f(\xi,\eta)=\int f(u,v)e^{-2\pi i(u\cdot\xi+v\cdot\eta)}\,du\,dv, one has p\in L^1(\mathbb R^m) and \widehat p(\xi)=\widehat f(\xi,0).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Ordinary integrable-function Fourier transforms and almost-everywhere projections use measure-theoretic integration beyond the calculus/linear-algebra rubric.

Proof 1: Set the complementary frequencies to zero (outline)

Use the same integration calculation as in two dimensions, with u,v replacing the scalar coordinates. Fubini gives the existence and integrability of p. Setting \eta=0 removes v from the exponential, and

\widehat f(\xi,0)=\int_{\mathbb R^m}\left(\int_{\mathbb R^{N-m}}f(u,v)\,dv\right)e^{-2\pi i u\cdot\xi}\,du=\widehat p(\xi).

This is the source's explicitly prescribed higher-dimensional generalization.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Use Fubini for arbitrary L1 functions to form an almost-everywhere projection, then set complementary Fourier frequencies to zero. General Lebesgue integrability, almost-everywhere definitions and repeated integration require measure-theoretic analysis.
