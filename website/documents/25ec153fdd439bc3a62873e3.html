Statement

Let f have a power-series expansion at zero, and write [x^i]f(x) for its coefficient of x^i. For every integer k\geq1, [x^k]f(x/(1+x))=\sum_{i=1}^k\binom{k-1}{i-1}(-1)^{k-i}[x^i]f(x).

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Expand each composed monomial — outline

Given an ordinary generating function f, since

\left(\frac{x}{1-x}\right)^i=\sum_{k=i}^{\infty}\binom{k-1}{i-1}x^k\qquad(i\geq1),

a variation of the binomial transform is

[x^k]f\left(\frac{x}{1+x}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^k\binom{k-1}{i-1}(-1)^{k-i}[x^i]f(x)\qquad(k\geq1).
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Expand each composed monomial x^i(1+x)^(-i) and collect the coefficient of x^k. Only i<=k contribute, so the extraction is finite. Taylor/binomial series and coefficient manipulation are undergraduate calculus and algebra; formal generating-function notation is a small extension of that background.
