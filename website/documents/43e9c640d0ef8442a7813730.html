Statement

Let X be an abelian variety of dimension n\ge1 over a field, let L be an ample line bundle, and let R=\bigoplus_{j\ge0}H^0(X,L^j). Then \operatorname{Spec}R is Cohen–Macaulay if and only if n=1.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Abelian varieties, ample line bundles, section rings and Cohen–Macaulay local algebra form a substantial chain of graduate prerequisites that cannot reasonably be learned in half an hour. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Empty index range and H¹ obstruction

For the abelian variety X and ample line bundle L in the statement, the section ring

R=\bigoplus_{j\ge0}H^0(X,L^j)

is Cohen–Macaulay if and only if the cohomology group H^i(X,L^j) is zero for all 1\le i\le n-1 and all integers j. It follows that the affine cone \operatorname{Spec}R over an abelian variety X is Cohen–Macaulay when X has dimension 1, but not when X has dimension at least 2 (because H^1(X,\mathcal O_X) is not zero).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The empty-index-range and nonzero-cohomology deductions are short, but the invoked criterion relates a section ring, an affine scheme, the Cohen–Macaulay property and sheaf cohomology of line-bundle powers. Understanding those objects and checking the j=0,i=1 instance requires substantial algebraic geometry and commutative algebra. Treating the criterion as established does not make its application undergraduate.
