Statement

Let G be a group generated by a finite symmetric set S (so S=S^{-1}). Let b_S(n) be the number of elements of G expressible as a product of at most n elements of S, with the empty product included.

There is a polynomial p such that b_S(n)\leq p(n) for every integer n\geq0 if and only if G has a nilpotent subgroup of finite index.

Finite index means that there are only finitely many cosets of the subgroup. A group N is nilpotent if its lower central series N_1=N, N_{j+1}=[N,N_j] reaches \{1\} after finitely many steps; [N,N_j] is the subgroup generated by all commutators xyx^{-1}y^{-1} with x\in N, y\in N_j.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory group theory supplies generators, subgroups and cosets. Counting bounded-length words and the explicitly defined iterated commutator subgroups are a small focused extension; understanding the equivalence needs no geometric group theory machinery.
