Statement

Let a<c<b, let f:[a,b]\to\mathbb R be Riemann integrable, and suppose F is continuous on [a,b] with F'=f on (a,b). Then \int_a^bf(x)\,dx=\int_a^cf(x)\,dx+\int_c^bf(x)\,dx.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves One-variable differentiation; Continuity; Riemann integration. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Integral additivity from an antiderivative — proof

By the Newton–Leibniz formula,

\begin{aligned} \int_a^b f(x)\,dx&=F(b)-F(a),\\ \int_a^c f(x)\,dx&=F(c)-F(a),\quad\text{and}\\ \int_c^b f(x)\,dx&=F(b)-F(c). \end{aligned}

The result follows from

F(b)-F(a)=F(c)-F(a)+F(b)-F(c).
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply Newton–Leibniz on each of the three intervals and cancel the shared endpoint value. Standard undergraduate single-variable calculus suffices.
