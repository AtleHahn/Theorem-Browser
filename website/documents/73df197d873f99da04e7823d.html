Statement

Let X,Y be real random variables on a probability space with \mathbb E[Y^2]<\infty. Write m(X)=\mathbb E[Y\mid X], \operatorname{Var}(Y\mid X)=\mathbb E[Y^2\mid X]-m(X)^2, and \operatorname{Var}(Z)=\mathbb E[Z^2]-(\mathbb E Z)^2. Then \operatorname{Var}(Y)=\mathbb E[\operatorname{Var}(Y\mid X)]+\operatorname{Var}(m(X)).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement concerns measure-theoretic conditional moments, beyond the calculus/linear algebra baseline.

Proof 1: Apply total expectation to the first two moments

Using {\displaystyle \operatorname {Var} (Y)=\operatorname {E} [Y^{2}]-\operatorname {E} [Y]^{2}} and the law of total expectation:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\operatorname {E} [Y^{2}]&=\operatorname {E} \left[\operatorname {E} (Y^{2}\mid X)\right]\\&=\operatorname {E} \left[\operatorname {Var} (Y\mid X)+(\operatorname {E} [Y\mid X])^{2}\right].\end{aligned}}}

Subtract {\displaystyle \operatorname {E} [Y]^{2}=\left(\operatorname {E} [\operatorname {E} (Y\mid X)]\right)^{2}} and regroup to arrive at {\displaystyle \operatorname {Var} (Y)=\operatorname {E} \left[\operatorname {Var} (Y\mid X)\right]+\operatorname {Var} \left(\operatorname {E} [Y\mid X]\right).}

Level justification

Apply total expectation to the first two moments and regroup the variance terms. The actual arbitrary-random-variable conditional expectation is measure-theoretic, beyond elementary finite conditional probabilities.
