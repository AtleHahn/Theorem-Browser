Statement

Let 0≤a_{i,k}≤a_{i,k+1}≤K_i for positive integers i,k, with ∑_iK_i<∞. Then ∑ia{i,k} increases to the finite value ∑i sup_k a{i,k}.

Proof 1: Bound column sums and invoke nonnegative-sum convergence

The infinite matrix consists of non-negative real numbers a_{i,k}\ge0, with weakly increasing rows, each bounded by a_{i,k}\le K_i, where the bounds are summable: \sum_iK_i<\infty. The non-decreasing column sums

\sum_i a_{i,k}\le\sum_iK_i

are bounded, hence convergent. By the monotone convergence theorem for non-negative sums, the limit of the column sums is equal to the sum of the “limit column” \sup_k a_{i,k}, which elementwise is the supremum over the row.

Bound the increasing column sums and interchange the increasing limit with a nonnegative series using the stated theorem. Undergraduate real sequence and series analysis suffices; this is a countable sum, not general measure integration.
