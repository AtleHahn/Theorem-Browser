Statement

Let n>0 be a nonsquare integer, and let positive integers x_1,y_1 satisfy x_1^2-ny_1^2=1. Define x_k+y_k\sqrt n=(x_1+y_1\sqrt n)^k for integers k\geq1. Then x_k=T_k(x_1) and y_k=y_1U_{k-1}(x_1), where T_k,U_k are Chebyshev polynomials of the first and second kind.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The Chebyshev polynomials enter through the supplied polynomial power identity and integer-coefficient property. The remaining statement uses integer powers and quadratic surds, with no analytic polynomial theory needed.

Proof 1: Substitution into the Chebyshev power identity

Use the source’s Chebyshev power identity T_k(t)+U_{k-1}(t)\sqrt{t^2-1}=(t+\sqrt{t^2-1})^k. Substitute t=x_1. Since x_1^2-1=ny_1^2 and y_1>0, \sqrt{x_1^2-1}=y_1\sqrt n. Using the integer coefficients of the Chebyshev polynomials, comparing rational and square-root coefficients with the defined Pell power gives x_k=T_k(x_1) and y_k=y_1U_{k-1}(x_1). The Chebyshev power identity itself is an imported prerequisite, not proved by this substitution.
