Statement

Let I,J be nondegenerate real intervals and f:I\to J a continuous bijection. Let F be an antiderivative of f (continuous on I and with F^{\prime}=f in its interior). Then H(y)=yf^{-1}(y)-F(f^{-1}(y)) is continuous on J and satisfies H^{\prime}(y)=f^{-1}(y) in its interior. All antiderivatives of f^{-1} are H+C for real constants C.

Proof 1: Integral of an inverse function — outline

It follows from the intermediate value theorem that f is strictly monotone. Consequently, f maps intervals to intervals, so is an open map and thus a homeomorphism. Since f and the inverse function f^{-1}:J\to I are continuous, they have antiderivatives by the fundamental theorem of calculus.

It is possible to check that for every y in the interior of J, the derivative of the function y\mapsto yf^{-1}(y)-F(f^{-1}(y)) is equal to f^{-1}(y). In other words:

\forall x\in\operatorname{int} I\quad \lim_{h\to0}\frac{(x+h)f(x+h)-xf(x)-\left(F(x+h)-F(x)\right)} {f(x+h)-f(x)}=x.

To this end, it suffices to apply the mean value theorem to F between x and x+h, taking into account that f is monotonic.

The displayed composition is continuous on J, and antiderivatives on an interval differ by a constant. The source leaves the mean-value estimate and the passage through the continuous inverse implicit.
