Statement

Let n\ge0 and let P\in\mathbb C[X,Y_0,\ldots,Y_n] satisfy P(z,\Gamma(z),\Gamma^{\prime}(z),\ldots,\Gamma^{(n)}(z))=0 for every z\in D=\mathbb C\setminus\{0,-1,-2,\ldots\}. Define Q=P(X+1,XY_0,XY_1+Y_0,\ldots,XY_n+nY_{n-1}), with only XY_0 when n=0. Then Q(z,\Gamma(z),\ldots,\Gamma^{(n)}(z))=0 on D.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Calculus supplies improper integrals, derivatives and asymptotic notation. Gamma is defined by its integral and recurrence, extended away from its listed poles; these formulas and complex differentiability are limited additions sufficient for the stated identity or bound, without special-function theory.

Proof 1: Differentiate the gamma functional equation and substitute

Use the gamma functional equation \Gamma(z+1)=z\Gamma(z). Observe that for all {\displaystyle z\in \mathbb {C} \smallsetminus \mathbb {Z} _{\leq 0}} we have:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}&P\left(z+1;\Gamma (z+1),\Gamma '(z+1),\Gamma ''(z+1),\ldots ,\Gamma ^{(n)}(z+1)\right)\\[1ex]&=P\left(z+1;z\Gamma (z),[z\Gamma (z)]',[z\Gamma (z)]'',\ldots ,[z\Gamma (z)]^{(n)}\right)\\[1ex]&=P\left(z+1;z\Gamma (z),z\Gamma '(z)+\Gamma (z),z\Gamma ''(z)+2\Gamma '(z),\ldots ,z{\Gamma ^{(n)}}(z)+n{\Gamma ^{(n-1)}}(z)\right).\end{aligned}}}

If we define a second polynomial {\displaystyle Q\in \mathbb {C} [X;Y_{0},Y_{1},\ldots ,Y_{n}]} by the transformation

{\displaystyle Q~{\stackrel {\text{df}}{=}}~P(X+1;XY_{0},XY_{1}+Y_{0},XY_{2}+2Y_{1},\ldots ,XY_{n}+nY_{n-1}),}

then we obtain the following algebraic differential equation for {\displaystyle \Gamma }:

{\displaystyle \forall z\in \mathbb {C} \setminus \mathbb {Z} _{\leq 0}:\qquad Q\left(z;\Gamma (z),\Gamma '(z),\ldots ,{\Gamma ^{(n)}}(z)\right)\equiv 0.}

In these displays, truncate each list after derivative order n; for n=0, the substitution for Y_0 is just XY_0. The vanishing follows from the hypothesis on P, since z\in D implies z+1\in D.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Differentiate the gamma functional equation on its complex domain and substitute in the differential polynomial. The formal product rule is elementary, but the full gamma function away from its poles presupposes its complex continuation; retain beyond undergraduate provisionally.
