Statement

Let R>0 and let f be holomorphic on an open neighborhood of the closed complex disk |z|\le R. For 0<r<R, put A=\max_{|z|\le R}\operatorname{Re}f(z). Then

\max_{|z|\le r}|f(z)|\le\frac{2r}{R-r}A+\frac{R+r}{R-r}|f(0)|.
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Real analysis supplies derivatives, maxima, uniform convergence and completeness; complex arithmetic supplies the plane. Holomorphic means complex differentiable, and entire means so throughout the plane. With that short extension, the displayed bound or convergence assertion uses familiar analytic concepts without contour integrals or specialist function-space theory.

Proof 1: Half-plane transformation and Schwarz lemma

Define A by

{\displaystyle A=\sup _{|z|\leq R}\operatorname {Re} f(z).}

If f is constant c, the inequality follows from {\displaystyle (R+r)|c|+2r\operatorname {Re} c\geq (R-r)|c|}, so we may assume f is nonconstant. First let f(0) = 0. Since Re f is harmonic, Re f(0) is equal to the average of its values around any circle centered at 0. That is,

{\displaystyle \operatorname {Re} f(0)=\int _{0}^{1}\operatorname {Re} f(R{\rm {e}}^{2\pi {\rm {i}}s}){\,{\rm {d}}}s.}

Since f is regular and nonconstant, we have that Re f is also nonconstant. Since Re f(0) = 0, we must have Re {\displaystyle f(z)>0} for some z on the circle {\displaystyle |z|=R}, so we may take {\displaystyle A>0}. By the harmonic maximum principle, on |z|<R the function f maps into the half-plane P to the left of the x=A line. Roughly, our goal is to map this half-plane to a disk, apply Schwarz's lemma there, and make out the stated inequality.

{\displaystyle w\mapsto w/A-1} sends P to the standard left half-plane. {\displaystyle w\mapsto R(w+1)/(w-1)} sends the left half-plane to the disk of radius R centered at the origin. The composite, which maps 0 to 0, is the desired map:

{\displaystyle w\mapsto {\frac {Rw}{w-2A}}.}

From Schwarz's lemma applied to the composite of this map and f, we have

{\displaystyle {\frac {|Rf(z)|}{|f(z)-2A|}}\leq |z|.}

Take |z| ≤ r. The above becomes

{\displaystyle R|f(z)|\leq r|f(z)-2A|\leq r|f(z)|+2Ar}

so

{\displaystyle |f(z)|\leq {\frac {2Ar}{R-r}}},

as claimed. In the general case, we may apply the above to f(z)-f(0):

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}|f(z)|-|f(0)|&\leq |f(z)-f(0)|\leq {\frac {2r}{R-r}}\sup _{|w|\leq R}\operatorname {Re} (f(w)-f(0))\\&\leq {\frac {2r}{R-r}}\left(\sup _{|w|\leq R}\operatorname {Re} f(w)+|f(0)|\right),\end{aligned}}}

which, when rearranged, gives the claim.

Proof 2: Cauchy coefficients and a geometric series

Local coefficient estimate

Let M be an upper bound for \operatorname{Re}f on |z|=R. For every integer j\geq1, we first prove |f^{(j)}(0)|\leq 2j!(M-\operatorname{Re}f(0))/R^j.

Put h=f-f(0), u_h=\operatorname{Re}h and B=M-\operatorname{Re}f(0). Then h(0)=0, u_h\leq B on the boundary, and h^{(j)}=f^{(j)} for j\geq1.

Do three contour integrals around the circle |z|=R using Cauchy integral formula:

{\displaystyle h^{(j)}(0)/j!={\frac {1}{2\pi i}}\oint {\frac {h(z)}{z^{j+1}}}dz=\int _{0}^{1}{\frac {h(Re^{2\pi it})}{R^{j}e^{2\pi ijt}}}dt}{\displaystyle \int _{0}^{1}h(Re^{2\pi it})e^{2\pi ijt}dt={\frac {1}{2\pi iR^{j}}}\oint h(z)z^{j-1}dz=0}{\displaystyle \int _{0}^{1}h(Re^{2\pi it})dt={\frac {1}{2\pi i}}\oint h(z)z^{-1}dz=h(0)=0}

Pick angle {\displaystyle \theta }, so that {\displaystyle e^{-i\theta }h^{(j)}(0)=|h^{(j)}(0)|}. Then by linearly combining the three integrals, we get

{\displaystyle \int _{0}^{1}h(Re^{2\pi it})(1+\cos(2\pi jt+\theta ))\,dt={\frac {1}{2}}R^{j}|h^{(j)}(0)|/j!}

The imaginary part vanishes, and the real part gives

{\displaystyle \int _{0}^{1}u_h(Re^{2\pi it})(1+\cos(2\pi jt+\theta ))\,dt={\frac {1}{2}}R^{j}|h^{(j)}(0)|/j!}

The integral is bounded above by {\displaystyle B\int _{0}^{1}(1+\cos(2\pi jt+\theta ))\,dt=B}, so we have the result.

Taylor-series bound

Take M=A.

It suffices to prove the case of {\displaystyle f(0)=0}.

By the local coefficient estimate, using the Taylor expansion,

{\displaystyle |f(z)|\leq \sum _{n=0}^{\infty }{\frac {1}{n!}}|f^{(n)}(0)|\cdot |z|^{n}\leq |f(0)|+\sum _{n=1}^{\infty }2M(r/R)^{n}={\frac {2r}{R-r}}M}

For general f, apply this to h=f-f(0) with bound A-\operatorname{Re}f(0):

|f(z)|\leq |f(0)|+\frac{2r}{R-r}(A-\operatorname{Re}f(0)) \leq\frac{2r}{R-r}A+\frac{R+r}{R-r}|f(0)|.
