Statement

Let G be a finite group and let C=\{gxg^{-1}:g\in G\} be a conjugacy class. If \langle a,b\rangle is nilpotent for every a,b\in C, then C is contained in a nilpotent subgroup of G. Here \langle a,b\rangle denotes the subgroup generated by a,b. A group H is nilpotent if its upper central series reaches H after finitely many steps: Z_0(H)=\{1\} and Z_{i+1}(H) is the inverse image of the center of H/Z_i(H).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory group theory supplies conjugacy, quotients and centers. The only additional construction is iterating the center in a quotient; the recursive definition and abelian example make this a small extension within the reading allowance.
