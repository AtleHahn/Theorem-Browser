An infinite model of a countable first-order theory can be replaced by models of every infinite cardinality. The axioms consequently cannot uniquely determine the size of their intended infinite universe.

Statement

If a first-order theory T in a countable signature has an infinite model, then for every infinite cardinal \kappa it has a model of cardinality \kappa.

Level

Proof 1: Apply the model-cardinality theorem

The Löwenheim–Skolem theorem states that for every signature \sigma, every infinite \sigma-structure M and every infinite cardinal number \kappa\geq|\sigma|, there is a \sigma-structure N such that |N|=\kappa and such that

  • if \kappa<|M| then N is an elementary substructure of M;
  • if \kappa\geq|M| then N is an elementary extension of M.

An elementary substructure satisfies exactly the same first-order sentences as the original structure (its elementary extension).

The statement follows immediately by taking M to be an infinite model of the theory. Since the signature is countable, every infinite \kappa satisfies \kappa\geq|\sigma|.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the application requires formal first-order signatures, structures, theories, elementary embeddings and arbitrary infinite cardinalities together. The supplied theorem makes the deduction immediate but does not remove that model-theoretic prerequisite burden.
