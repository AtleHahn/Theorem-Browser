Statement

Let Z\ge0 be a random variable with 0<\mathbb E[Z^2]<\infty, and put \mu=\mathbb E[Z] and \sigma^2=\operatorname{Var}(Z). Suppose \sigma>0 and 0\le t\le\mu/\sigma. Then \mathbb P(Z>\mu-t\sigma)\ge t^2/(1+t^2).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Real analysis supplies integration and nonnegative functions. The extra probability notions here are a real-valued quantity with a probability law, expectation as its weighted integral, and variance as the second centered moment. These closely related definitions and the given threshold range fit about 30 minutes; independence, stochastic convergence and probability-space construction are unnecessary.

Proof 1: Substitute a standardized threshold

Use the centered estimate \mathbb P(Z>\theta\mu)\ge(1-\theta)^2\mu^2/(\sigma^2+(1-\theta)^2\mu^2). Substitute \theta=1-t\sigma/\mu, which lies in [0,1] by the stated restriction. This gives the displayed standardized bound.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Expectations, variance and tail probabilities are ordinary undergraduate probability. The proof only substitutes a threshold into the explicitly supplied moment inequality; nonnegativity and positive second moment ensure mu>0, so the substitution is valid. No measure-theoretic convergence theorem is needed.
