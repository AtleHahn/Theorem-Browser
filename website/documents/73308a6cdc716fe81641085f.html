Statement

Let (X,\Sigma,\mu) be a Radon measure space: X is a Hausdorff topological space, \Sigma contains its Borel sets, and \mu is locally finite and inner regular by compact sets. Let Y be a second-countable topological space, equipped with its Borel sigma-algebra, and let f:X\to Y be measurable.

For every A\in\Sigma with \mu(A)<\infty and every \varepsilon>0, there is a closed set E\subseteq X such that

\mu(A\setminus E)<\varepsilon

and f|_E:E\to Y is continuous for the subspace topology.

Second countability means that the topology has a countable base. Measurability means that the inverse image of each Borel subset of Y belongs to \Sigma. Inner regularity means that the measure of a measurable set is the supremum of the measures of its compact subsets; local finiteness means that each point has a neighborhood of finite measure.
