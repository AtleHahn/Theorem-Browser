Statement

For an integer n\geq1, let \omega(n) be the number of distinct prime divisors of n. For every real \varepsilon>0,

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{1}{x}\#\left\{n\in\mathbb Z:3\leq n\leq x,\ \left|\frac{\omega(n)}{\log\log n}-1\right|\geq\varepsilon\right\}=0,

where \log denotes the natural logarithm and \# denotes the number of elements of a finite set. Thus \log\log n is the normal order of \omega(n): the proportion of integers whose relative deviation exceeds any prescribed positive tolerance tends to zero.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Prime factors and logarithms are elementary; the quantified limiting proportion is standard undergraduate analysis. No probability limit theorem or analytic-number-theory proof methods are required to understand the statement.
