Statement

For a simple bipartite graph with parts U,V of sizes m,n, write z(m,n;s,t) for the largest edge count when no s vertices of U and t vertices of V form a complete bipartite subgraph K_{s,t}. Let a projective plane of order q have q^2+q+1 points and as many lines, each point/line incident with q+1 lines/points, and any two distinct points on exactly one common line. Its point-line incidence graph has q^2+q+1 vertices in each part, (q^2+q+1)(q+1) edges and no K_{2,2}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The finite incidence counts and unique joining-line property are supplied; the graph construction and forbidden four-cycle are finite relations.

Proof 1: Unique joining line and incidence count

Take a projective plane of order {\displaystyle q}, a system of {\displaystyle q^{2}+q+1} points and {\displaystyle q^{2}+q+1} lines in which each two points determine a unique line, and each two lines intersect at a unique point. We construct a bipartite graph associated to this projective plane that has one vertex part as its points, the other vertex part as its lines, such that a point and a line is connected if and only if they are incident in the projective plane. This leads to a {\displaystyle K_{2,2}}-free graph with {\displaystyle q^{2}+q+1} vertices in each part and {\displaystyle (q^{2}+q+1)(q+1)} edges.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Count incidences from the supplied finite-plane parameters and exclude two common lines through the same two points. School finite counting and the explicitly supplied incidence rules suffice.
