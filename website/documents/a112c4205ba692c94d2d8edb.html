Statement

For points p=(p_1,p_2) and q=(q_1,q_2) in the Euclidean plane with Cartesian coordinates, their segment length is d(p,q)=\sqrt{(p_1-q_1)^2+(p_2-q_2)^2}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The formula expresses a planar segment length in ordinary real coordinates and square roots.

Proof 1: Use a horizontal-vertical right triangle — outline

This can be seen by applying the Pythagorean theorem to a right triangle with horizontal and vertical sides, having the line segment from {\displaystyle p} to {\displaystyle q} as its hypotenuse. The two squared formulas inside the square root give the areas of squares on the horizontal and vertical sides, and the outer square root converts the area of the square on the hypotenuse into the length of the hypotenuse.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The horizontal and vertical coordinate differences are the legs of a right triangle. Pythagoras and the nonnegative square root give the distance, using high-school coordinate geometry.
