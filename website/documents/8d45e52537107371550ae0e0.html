Simpler proof assessment

Likely alternative prerequisite level: High school only; lowest recorded proof level: Undergraduate level. Maintain integer flows with capacities one. Starting at zero, augment along residual x-y paths; each augmentation raises the integer value, bounded by the number of arcs. When none exists, residual reachability gives a cut whose arcs are saturated forward and unused backward, so cut size equals flow value. Remove cycles and decompose the remaining integral flow into arc-disjoint paths. All steps are finite path, counting and cancellation arguments, covering arbitrary finite directed graphs and the zero-path case without an imported max-flow theorem.

Statement

For distinct vertices x,y in a finite directed graph, the minimum number of arcs whose deletion destroys every directed x-to-y path equals the maximum number of pairwise arc-disjoint directed x-to-y paths.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement concerns finite vertices, paths and deletion sets, with disjointness and the required counting conditions specified locally.

Mathematical Field
Combinatorics

Review notes

Proof 1: Unit capacities and integral flow decomposition — outline

The directed edge version follows from its weighted variant, the max-flow min-cut theorem. In the integral form of that theorem, integer arc capacities admit an integer-valued maximum flow whose value equals the minimum cut capacity. Here each arc is given capacity 1; the conversion between such flows and disjoint paths uses integral flow decomposition into paths and cycles.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Set all capacities to one in integral max-flow/min-cut and decompose the integer flow into paths and cycles. Undergraduate graph algorithms suffice; flow conservation and decomposition add several concepts beyond the bare path statement.
