Statement

Let x,y\in\mathbb R^n have norm one, 0<\varepsilon\le1, and \|x-y\|\ge\varepsilon. For all \alpha,\beta\in[0,1], \|(\alpha x+\beta y)/2\|\le1-\varepsilon^2/8.

Proof 1: Express the contracted midpoint as a convex combination outline

Let \delta=\varepsilon^2/8. For the unit vectors x,y with \|x-y\|\geq\varepsilon, one can show, using

\frac12\|x+y\|^2=\|x\|^2+\|y\|^2-\frac12\|x-y\|^2

and \sqrt{1-t}\leq1-t/2 for 0\leq t\leq1, that \|(x+y)/2\|\leq1-\delta. In particular, (x+y)/2\in(1-\delta)B, where B is the closed unit ball.

Let \alpha,\beta\in[0,1], \bar x=\alpha x, \bar y=\beta y. The argument below will be symmetric in \bar x,\bar y, so we assume without loss of generality that \alpha\geq\beta and set \gamma=\beta/\alpha\leq1. (The case \alpha=\beta=0 is immediate.) Then,

\frac{\bar x+\bar y}{2} =\frac{\alpha x+\beta y}{2} =\alpha\frac{x+\gamma y}{2} =\alpha\left(\gamma\frac{x+y}{2}+(1-\gamma)\frac x2\right) =\alpha\gamma\frac{x+y}{2}+\alpha(1-\gamma)\frac x2.

Since \|x/2\|\leq1-\delta for \varepsilon\leq1, this implies that

\frac{\bar x+\bar y}{2}\in \alpha\gamma(1-\delta)B+\alpha(1-\gamma)(1-\delta)B =\alpha(1-\delta)B\subseteq(1-\delta)B.

Here we use that for any convex body K and \gamma\in[0,1], \gamma K+(1-\gamma)K=K.
