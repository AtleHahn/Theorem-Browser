Statement

Let r>0 and let f be holomorphic on a neighborhood of the closed disk \{z:|z|\le r\}, with Taylor series f(z)=\sum_{k\ge0}a_kz^k about zero. Put M_r=\sup_{|z|=r}|f(z)|. Then \sum_{k\ge0}|a_k|^2r^{2k}\le M_r^2.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex differentiability extends the calculus derivative. Power series, circle parametrization, bounds and convergence are familiar analysis; the hypotheses and formulas are explicit, with no abstract complex geometry or distribution theory.

Proof 1: Bound the circle integral by its supremum

Parseval–Gutzmer gives \sum_{k\ge0}|a_k|^2r^{2k}=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_0^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^2\,d\theta. By the definition of M_r, this integral is at most 2\pi M_r^2. Divide by 2\pi.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the explicit circle-integral identity and bound the continuous integrand by its circle supremum. Undergraduate complex analysis and calculus suffice.
