Statement

Let (S,d) and (S',d') be metric spaces, and let X_n,X be S-valued random elements on a common probability space. Let g:S\to S' be such that g(X_n),g(X) are random elements and the distances d(X_n,X) and d'(g(X_n),g(X)) are measurable. Let D_g be the discontinuity set of g and assume \Pr(X\in D_g)=0. If X_n\to X almost surely, then g(X_n)\to g(X) almost surely.

Proof 1: Intersect the convergence and continuity events

By definition of the continuity of the function g(\cdot),

\lim_{n\to\infty}X_n(\omega)=X(\omega)\quad\Rightarrow\quad \lim_{n\to\infty}g(X_n(\omega))=g(X(\omega))

at each point X(\omega) where g(\cdot) is continuous. Therefore,

\begin{aligned} \Pr\left(\lim_{n\to\infty}g(X_n)=g(X)\right) &\geq\Pr\left(\lim_{n\to\infty}g(X_n)=g(X),\ X\notin D_g\right)\\ &\geq\Pr\left(\lim_{n\to\infty}X_n=X,\ X\notin D_g\right)=1, \end{aligned}

because the intersection of two almost sure events is almost sure.

By definition, we conclude that g(X_n) converges to g(X) almost surely.

Level justification

Pointwise continuity preserves the limit outside the two null events, but the full claim uses metric-space random elements, measurability and almost-sure convergence. This measure-theoretic probability setting is not confidently a small extension; retain beyond undergraduate provisionally.
