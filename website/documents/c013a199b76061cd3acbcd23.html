Statement

For n\ge1, every continuous map s:S^{2n}\to S^{2n} has a point p with s(p)=p or s(p)=-p.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves continuous maps in finite-dimensional Euclidean space; unit spheres; dot products. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: An even-sphere map has a fixed or antipodal point — outline

This can be seen by transforming the function into a tangential vector field as follows.

Let s be the function mapping the sphere to itself, and let v be the tangential vector function to be constructed. For each point p, construct the stereographic projection of s(p) from -p onto the affine tangent plane at p. Then v(p) is the displacement vector of this projected point relative to p. If s(p)\ne-p for every p, this gives a continuous tangent vector field. According to the hairy ball theorem, there is a p such that v(p)=0, so that s(p)=p.

This argument breaks down only if there exists a point p for which s(p) is the antipodal point of p, since such a point is the only one that cannot be stereographically projected onto the tangent plane of p. In that case the other alternative in the statement holds.
