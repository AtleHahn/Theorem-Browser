Statement

On a measure space let 1\le p_0<p_1\le\infty, 0<\theta<1, and 1/p_\theta=(1-\theta)/p_0+\theta/p_1, with 1/\infty=0. Every f\in L^{p_\theta} can be written f=g+h with g\in L^{p_0} and h\in L^{p_1}.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The claim decomposes an arbitrary Lebesgue-space function into functions in two endpoint spaces.

Proof 1: Split at the level one

Split f=g+h with g=f\mathbf1_{\{|f|>1\}} and h=f\mathbf1_{\{|f|\le1\}}. On the first set, |f|^{p_0}\le|f|^{p_\theta}, so g\in L^{p_0}. On the second, |f|^{p_1}\le|f|^{p_\theta} if p_1<\infty, and |h|\le1 if p_1=\infty. Thus h\in L^{p_1}.

Level justification

Split the measurable function above and below one and compare the endpoint powers, including essential boundedness. The manipulation is short but relies on general measure-space Lp membership, so retain the advanced level.
