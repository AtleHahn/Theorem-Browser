Statement

Let X be a real Hausdorff locally convex topological vector space, and let \mu,\nu be Gaussian measures on X, using the Radon probability measure convention defined below. Exactly one of these alternatives holds:

  • \mu and \nu are equivalent: a Borel set has \mu-measure zero if and only if it has \nu-measure zero.
  • \mu and \nu are mutually singular: there is a Borel set E\subseteq X such that \mu(E)=1 and \nu(E)=0.

Here locally convex means that zero has a neighborhood basis of convex sets, and Hausdorff means distinct points have disjoint neighborhoods. A Gaussian measure is a Radon Borel probability measure whose image under every continuous real linear functional is a real normal distribution, allowing variance zero. Radon means that for every Borel set E, its measure is the supremum of the measures of compact subsets of E. The sigma-algebra in both conclusions is the Borel sigma-algebra of X.
