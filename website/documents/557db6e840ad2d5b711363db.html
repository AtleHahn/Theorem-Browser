Statement

Let n\geq2 be an integer. A smooth function f:\mathbb R^{n-1}\to\mathbb R has a minimal graph when it satisfies the minimal-surface equation everywhere:

\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_i} \left(\frac{\partial f/\partial x_i}{\sqrt{1+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(\partial f/\partial x_j)^2}}\right)=0.

If 2\leq n\leq8, every such graph is a hyperplane: f(x)=a\cdot x+b for some a\in\mathbb R^{n-1} and b\in\mathbb R.

For every n\geq9, there exists a smooth function f:\mathbb R^{n-1}\to\mathbb R satisfying the displayed equation whose graph is not a hyperplane.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The minimal-graph condition is explicitly given as a partial differential equation. Multivariable calculus and linear algebra suffice to understand all its terms, the global domain, affine graphs, and the existence/nonexistence quantifiers in every dimension. No minimal-surface theory or solution method is needed to understand this formulation.
