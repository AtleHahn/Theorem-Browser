Statement

Let n be a positive integer. If every prime factor q\equiv3\pmod4 occurs with an even exponent in the prime factorization of n, then n is a sum of two integer squares.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The hypothesis and conclusion use elementary integer arithmetic.

Proof 1: Multiply the prime-power representations — outline

Product identity

Write m=a^2+b^2 and n=c^2+d^2 with integers a,b,c,d. The Diophantus identity gives

mn=(ac+bd)^2+(ad-bc)^2,

which is a sum of two integer squares.

Applying the prime factorization

Fermat’s theorem represents every prime congruent to one modulo four by two squares, and 2=1^2+1^2. The even powers of primes congruent to three modulo four are squares. Applying the local product identity to the prime factorization represents n by two squares.
