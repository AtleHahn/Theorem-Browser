Statement

Every finite simple planar graph can be drawn without crossings using edges made from at most two circular arcs joined smoothly, so that all edges incident with each vertex leave that vertex in one common tangent direction. Straight segments are allowed as limiting circular arcs.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement describes finite graphs, circular arcs and tangent directions in elementary plane geometry.

Proof 1: Use perpendicular arcs from boundary vertex points — outline

Use a circle packing representing the graph. Place each vertex on its circle, choosing a point different from the finitely many tangency points. Draw each edge as two circular arcs, one inside each incident circle, perpendicular to the packing circles and meeting at their point of tangency. At a vertex all these arcs are perpendicular to the same circle, so they leave in the same direction. The arcs join smoothly at the common tangency point and give the prescribed pointed drawing.
