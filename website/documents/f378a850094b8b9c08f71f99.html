Statement

Let k\ge1 be an integer and let n_1,\ldots,n_k>1 be pairwise coprime integers, N=\prod_i n_i, and N_i=N/n_i. If a_i,M_i,m_i\in\mathbb Z and M_iN_i+m_in_i=1, then x=\sum_i a_iM_iN_i satisfies x\equiv a_i\pmod{n_i} for every i.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The explicit selector sum and each integer coefficient identity use elementary finite algebra and congruences.

Proof 1: Reduce the finite selector sum

Put N_i=N/n_i. Use the given identities M_iN_i+m_in_i=1. Set

x=\sum_{i=1}^k a_iM_iN_i.

For fixed i, every N_j with j\ne i is divisible by n_i, hence

x\equiv a_iM_iN_i\equiv a_i(1-m_in_i)\equiv a_i\pmod{n_i}.
Level
High school only
Level justification

For each modulus all but one summand are divisible by it; the remaining coefficient is congruent to one by the supplied identity. Only integer divisibility and finite sums are required.
