Statement

Proof 1: Wang’s eighth-power counterexample over the rationals — outline

It is clear that 16 is not a 2-adic 8th power, and hence not a rational 8th power, since the 2-adic valuation of 16 is 4 which is not divisible by 8.

Generally, 16 is an 8th power in a field K if and only if the polynomial {\displaystyle X^{8}-16} has a root in K. Write

{\displaystyle X^{8}-16=(X^{4}-4)(X^{4}+4)=(X^{2}-2)(X^{2}+2)(X^{2}-2X+2)(X^{2}+2X+2).}

Thus, 16 is an 8th power in K if and only if 2, −2 or −1 is a square in K. Let p be any odd prime. It follows from the multiplicativity of the Legendre symbol that 2, −2 or −1 is a square modulo p. Hence, by Hensel's lemma, 2, −2 or −1 is a square in {\displaystyle \mathbb {Q} _{p}}.

At the real place, 2 is a square.

Level justification

Combine p-adic valuations, quadratic factorization, residue symbols and Hensel lifting. The p-adic field and lifting framework is not confidently a small undergraduate extension.
