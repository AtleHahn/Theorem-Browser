Statement

Let n,r be nonnegative integers and let R=\{0,1\}^r be the set of binary strings of length r, so |R|=2^r. Bitwise exclusive-or u\oplus t adds each pair of bits modulo 2. For A\subseteq R, write A\oplus t=\{u\oplus t:u\in A\}. Empty unions are empty and empty products equal 1; R has one empty string when r=0. If |A|/|R|\le2^{-n}, then for any t_1,\ldots,t_r\in R, with S=\bigcup_{i=1}^r(A\oplus t_i), \frac{|S|}{|R|}\le r\frac{|A|}{|R|}\le r2^{-n}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement defines all binary-string operations and asks for an upper bound on the fraction covered by a finite union. Cardinality, a reversible bit operation, fractions and integer powers suffice to understand every hypothesis and the conclusion, including the supplied empty cases.

Proof 1: Bound the union by its translated cardinalities

Each A\oplus t_i has cardinality |A|. Hence the union-density estimate is \frac{|S|}{|R|}\le\sum_{i=1}^r\frac{|A\oplus t_i|}{|R|}=r\frac{|A|}{|R|}\le r2^{-n}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Exclusive-or translation is undone by the same translation and hence preserves cardinality. The union has at most the sum of the translated cardinalities. Dividing by the nonzero finite size of R and using the hypothesis is elementary counting and algebra, including the empty-union case.
