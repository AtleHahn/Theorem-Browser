Statement

For standard real Brownian motion B, T>0 and C>0, \Pr(\sup_{0\le t\le T}B_t\ge C)\le\exp(-C^2/(2T)).

Proof 1: Apply the exponential submartingale bound and optimize

Doob's inequality provides a direct way to bound the maximum of canonical one-dimensional Brownian motion, denoted here as {\displaystyle B}. Because the exponential function is monotonically increasing, for any positive {\displaystyle \lambda }:

{\displaystyle \left\{\sup _{0\leq t\leq T}B_{t}\geq C\right\}=\left\{\sup _{0\leq t\leq T}\exp(\lambda B_{t})\geq \exp(\lambda C)\right\}.}

By applying Doob's inequality, and noting that the exponential of Brownian motion is a positive submartingale, we obtain:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}P\left[\sup _{0\leq t\leq T}B_{t}\geq C\right]&=P\left[\sup _{0\leq t\leq T}\exp(\lambda B_{t})\geq \exp(\lambda C)\right]\\[8pt]&\leq {\frac {\operatorname {E} [\exp(\lambda B_{T})]}{\exp(\lambda C)}}\\[8pt]&=\exp \left({\tfrac {1}{2}}\lambda ^{2}T-\lambda C\right)&&\left({\text{since }}\operatorname {E} \left[\exp(\lambda B_{t})\right]=\exp \left({\tfrac {1}{2}}\lambda ^{2}t\right)\right)\end{aligned}}}

Since the left-hand side does not depend on {\displaystyle \lambda }, we can minimize the right-hand side by choosing {\displaystyle \lambda =C/T}. This provides the final bound:

{\displaystyle P\left[\sup _{0\leq t\leq T}B_{t}\geq C\right]\leq \exp \left(-{\frac {C^{2}}{2T}}\right).}

Level justification

The last quadratic minimization is elementary, but the proof applies a continuous-time submartingale maximal inequality to a Brownian exponential process. Filtrations, conditional expectations and continuous-time path maxima require substantial stochastic-process background.
