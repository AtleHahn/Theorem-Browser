Statement

A body starts from rest and moves in one direction with constant acceleration a>0. Under the kinematic law s(t)=\frac12at^2, for any \Delta>0 and integer n\ge0, the distance in [n\Delta,(n+1)\Delta] equals \frac12a\Delta^2(2n+1). Thus successive distances have ratios 1:3:5:\cdots.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement specifies a quadratic real function and distances over explicit equal intervals.

Proof 1: Apply consecutive squares to the quadratic motion law — outline

From the equation for uniform linear acceleration, the distance covered

{\displaystyle s=ut+{\tfrac {1}{2}}at^{2}}

for initial speed {\displaystyle u=0,} constant acceleration {\displaystyle a} (acceleration due to gravity without air resistance), and time elapsed {\displaystyle t,} it follows that the distance {\displaystyle s} is proportional to {\displaystyle t^{2}} (in symbols, {\displaystyle s\propto t^{2}}), thus the distances from the starting point are proportional to consecutive squares for integer values of time elapsed.

Equal successive time intervals therefore give consecutive square differences, which are the odd numbers.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Substitute consecutive multiples of the time step in the assumed quadratic law and subtract the squares. No derivation of the physical law by calculus is needed.
