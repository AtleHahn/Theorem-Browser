Statement

Let G be a finite simple undirected graph: its vertices form a finite set and its edges are unordered pairs of distinct vertices. There is a least positive integer r(G) such that, for every integer N\ge r(G), every assignment of red or blue to the edges of the complete graph on N vertices contains a copy of G with all its edges the same color. A complete graph has every pair as an edge; a copy means an injective assignment of the vertices preserving the edges, without requiring preservation of nonedges.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The finite graph and coloring notions are explicitly defined using finite sets and pairs.

Proof 1: Apply finite Ramsey to a complete graph containing the target — outline

Use the finite Ramsey theorem: for every positive integer k there is a finite R(k,k) such that every red-blue coloring of the complete graph on R(k,k) vertices has a monochromatic complete subgraph on k vertices. For a nonempty graph G, applied with k the number of vertices of G, this gives the existence of the Ramsey number of G. The least integer with the required property exists by well-ordering.
