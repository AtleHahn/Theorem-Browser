Statement

Define F:[0,1]\rightrightarrows[0,1] by F(x)=\{3/4\} for x<1/2, F(1/2)=[0,1], and F(x)=\{1/4\} for x>1/2. Its values are nonempty closed convex sets, but there is no continuous f:[0,1]\to[0,1] with f(x)\in F(x) for every x.

Proof 1: Compare the forced one-sided limits

The function

F(x)= \begin{cases} \{3/4\},&0\leq x<0.5,\\ [0,1],&x=0.5,\\ \{1/4\},&0.5<x\leq1 \end{cases}

is a set-valued function from the real interval [0,1] to itself. It has nonempty convex closed values. The function does not have a continuous selection: any selection is necessarily discontinuous at 0.5.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Any selection has the two distinct forced one-sided limits, so it cannot be continuous at one half. Undergraduate real-analysis continuity suffices.
