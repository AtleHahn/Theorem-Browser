Statement

Let L\subseteq\Sigma^* be a regular language over a finite alphabet, where \Sigma^* denotes all finite words and |w| the length of w. Then some integer p\ge1 has the property that every word w of length at least p in the language factors as w=xyz, |y|\ge1, |xy|\le p, with xy^nz in the language for every integer n\ge0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A regular language is recognized by a finite labeled-state machine. That finite transition-graph definition, together with words and concatenation, suffices for the explicitly quantified repetition property.

Proof 1: Repeat a loop between two equal states

For every regular language there is a finite-state automaton (FSA) that accepts the language. The number of states in such an FSA are counted and that count is used as the pumping length {\displaystyle p}. For a string of length at least {\displaystyle p}, let {\displaystyle q_{0}} be the start state and let {\displaystyle q_{1},...,q_{p}} be the sequence of the next {\displaystyle p} states visited as the string is read. Because the FSA has only {\displaystyle p} states, within this sequence of {\displaystyle p+1} visited states there must be at least one state that is repeated. Write {\displaystyle q_{s}} for such a state. The transitions that take the machine from the first encounter of state {\displaystyle q_{s}} to the second encounter of state {\displaystyle q_{s}} match some string. This string is called {\displaystyle y} in the lemma, and since the machine will match a string without the {\displaystyle y} portion, or with the string {\displaystyle y} repeated any number of times, the conditions of the lemma are satisfied.

Here x is the prefix before the first encounter and z the suffix after the second. The two encounters are distinct and within the first p transitions, so |y|\ge1 and |xy|\le p.
