Statement

Let X_1,\ldots,X_n be independent integrable real random variables. Suppose finite v_i\ge0 satisfy \mathbb E e^{s(X_i-\mathbb EX_i)}\le e^{v_i s^2/2} for every s\in\mathbb R, and let V=\sum_iv_i>0. Then for t>0, \mathbb P(|\sum_i(X_i-\mathbb EX_i)|\ge t)\le2e^{-t^2/(2V)}. The numbers v_i are variance proxies.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Variance proxies are explicitly defined exponential-moment bounds, not a separate statistical theory. The full statement needs finite independent random variables and expectations, including integrability, as averages/integrals of scalar functions. These linked definitions extend undergraduate analysis; no probability limit theorem or function-space construction is used.

Proof 1: Repeat exponential Markov with the supplied variance proxies — outline

Put S=\sum_i(X_i-\mathbb EX_i). For s>0, the source’s exponential Markov and independence argument gives \mathbb P(S\ge t)\le e^{-st}\prod_i\mathbb E e^{s(X_i-\mathbb EX_i)}\le\exp(-st+s^2V/2). Minimize the quadratic at s=t/V to obtain e^{-t^2/(2V)}. Applying the same argument to -S and adding the bounds gives the assertion.

Factor the exponential moment, use the assumed quadratic bound and optimize at s=t/V, then add the two tails. Undergraduate probability and quadratic algebra suffice.
