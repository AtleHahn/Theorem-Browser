Statement

Let A be an algebra with finitary operations, and let \Phi be an equivalence relation on A that is a subalgebra of A\times A with componentwise operations. The rule \omega^{A/\Phi}([a_1],\ldots,[a_r])=[\omega^A(a_1,\ldots,a_r)] defines each basic operation on the equivalence classes independently of representatives, making A/\Phi an algebra of the same type.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Equivalence classes, products and operations of finite arity are familiar algebra. Compatibility is explicitly expressed by a subalgebra of the product, directly generalizing quotient groups/rings without category theory.

Proof 1: Operations on a quotient by a congruence are well-defined — outline

If corresponding representatives are equivalent, the pairs (a_i,b_i) lie in \Phi. Because \Phi is closed under the componentwise basic operations, (\omega(a_1,\ldots,a_r),\omega(b_1,\ldots,b_r))\in\Phi. Thus the outputs have the same class. This is exactly why the source says the representative definition is well-defined.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply closure of the relation under each componentwise operation to equivalent representatives. Undergraduate quotient structures and equivalence relations suffice; an arbitrary finitary algebra is a set with a collection of finite-input operations, a small generalization of familiar group and ring quotients.
