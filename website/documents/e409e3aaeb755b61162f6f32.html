Statement

Equal balls in Euclidean three-space can be stacked in triangular layers in the periodic patterns ABAB\ldots and ABCABC\ldots. Here A,B,C denote the three horizontal layer positions: above a layer of type A, the positions B,C lie over its two families of triangular hollows.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Equal balls, triangular hollows and the two specified repeating layer patterns use elementary solid geometry.

Proof 1: Triangular-hollow stacking outline

One method for generating a close-packed structure is as follows. Consider a plane with a triangular arrangement of equal spheres tangent to their nearest neighbors. Call it A. For any three neighbouring spheres, a fourth sphere can be placed on top in the hollow between the three bottom spheres. If we do this for half of the holes in a second plane above the first, we create a new compact layer. There are two possible choices for doing this, call them B and C. Suppose that we chose B. Then one half of the hollows of B lies above the centres of the balls in A and one half lies above the hollows of A that were not used for B. Thus the balls of a third layer can be placed either directly above the balls of the first one, yielding a layer of type A, or above the holes of the first layer that were not occupied by the second layer, yielding a layer of type C. Combining layers of types A, B, and C produces various close-packed structures.

Two simple arrangements within the close-packed family correspond to regular arrangements. One is called cubic close packing (or face-centred cubic, "FCC", which is a lattice)—where the layers are alternated in the ABCABC... sequence. The other is called hexagonal close packing ("HCP", which, although being a regular arrangement, is not a lattice), where the layers are alternated in the ABAB... sequence.
