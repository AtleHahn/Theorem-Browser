Statement

Let f_0=0, f_1=1 and f_{n+2}=f_n+f_{n+1} for every integer n\ge0. Then for every n\ge0, (f_n^2+f_{n+1}^2+f_{n+2}^2)^2=2(f_n^4+f_{n+1}^4+f_{n+2}^4).

Level
High school only
Level justification

The sequence and recurrence are explicitly defined using elementary arithmetic.

Proof 1: Substitute the Fibonacci recurrence (outline)

Use Candido’s identity for real x,y: [x^2+y^2+(x+y)^2]^2=2[x^4+y^4+(x+y)^4]. Set x=f_n and y=f_{n+1}. The Fibonacci recurrence gives x+y=f_{n+2}, yielding the stated identity.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Substitute consecutive Fibonacci numbers into the displayed real polynomial identity and use their defining recurrence. Only high-school algebra is needed.
