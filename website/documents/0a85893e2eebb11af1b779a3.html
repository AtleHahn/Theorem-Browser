Statement

All graphs are finite, simple and undirected; containing a graph means containing an isomorphic subgraph, not necessarily induced. Write \operatorname{ex}(n;H) for the maximum number of edges in an n-vertex graph not containing H. The chromatic number \chi(H) is the least number of colors needed to color vertices so adjacent vertices have different colors. T(n,r-1) is the complete (r-1)-partite graph on n vertices with part sizes differing by at most one. For H with r=\chi(H)\geq2, \operatorname{ex}(n;H)\geq e(T(n,r-1))=\left(1-\frac1{r-1}+o(1)\right)\frac{n^2}{2}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves finite simple graphs and subgraphs; proper vertex colorings; extremal edge counts; limits of numerical densities. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Use the balanced graph with one fewer color — outline

The graph T(n,r-1) and all its subgraphs can be colored with r-1 colors, so it cannot contain H. Its number of edges is therefore a lower bound for \operatorname{ex}(n;H). The balanced multipartite edge count gives the stated asymptotic.
