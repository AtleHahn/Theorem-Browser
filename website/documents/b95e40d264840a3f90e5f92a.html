Statement

Write i^2=-1 and e^z=\sum_{m=0}^\infty z^m/m! for the complex exponential. For real x, \cos x=(e^{ix}+e^{-ix})/2=\operatorname{Re}(e^{ix}) and \sin x=(e^{ix}-e^{-ix})/(2i)=\operatorname{Im}(e^{ix}).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Complex exponential series; Real sine and cosine; Real and imaginary parts. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Add and subtract the opposite-angle Euler formulas

Euler’s formula and trigonometric parity give e^{ix}=\cos x+i\sin x and e^{-ix}=\cos x-i\sin x. Adding and subtracting these equations and dividing by 2 or 2i gives the formulas. Their real and imaginary parts give the other equalities.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Add and subtract the supplied Euler identities. The manipulation is elementary, but understanding the complex exponential as the specified infinite power series requires undergraduate calculus; no proof of Euler formula is used.
