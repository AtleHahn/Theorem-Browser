Statement

Write i^2=-1 and e^z=\sum_{m=0}^\infty z^m/m! for the complex exponential. For real x, \cos x=(e^{ix}+e^{-ix})/2=\operatorname{Re}(e^{ix}) and \sin x=(e^{ix}-e^{-ix})/(2i)=\operatorname{Im}(e^{ix}).

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Complex exponential series; Real sine and cosine; Real and imaginary parts. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. Proof 1: Add and subtract the opposite-angle Euler formulas

Euler’s formula and trigonometric parity give e^{ix}=\cos x+i\sin x and e^{-ix}=\cos x-i\sin x. Adding and subtracting these equations and dividing by 2 or 2i gives the formulas. Their real and imaginary parts give the other equalities.
