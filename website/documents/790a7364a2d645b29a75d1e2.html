Statement

For a positive integer n and z\in\mathbb C, define \sigma_z(n)=\sum_{d\mid n,\ d>0}d^z, using d^z=\exp(z\log d) with the real logarithm of the positive integer d. If a,b are relatively prime positive integers, then \sigma_z(ab)=\sigma_z(a)\sigma_z(b).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Coprime positive integers; Divisor sums; Complex powers; Finite products. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Split the finite product across disjoint prime factors

Finite-product formula

Let n=\prod_{i=1}^r p_i^{a_i} be its prime factorization, with distinct primes p_i and positive integer exponents a_i; for n=1 use the empty product.

The proof is combinatorial in nature. Distributing (expanding) the product

{\displaystyle \prod _{i=1}^{r}\left(1+p_{i}^{z}+p_{i}^{2z}+\cdots +p_{i}^{a_{i}z}\right)}

gives a sum in which each summand is a product. In the products there is one factor per parentheses, and each is one of the summands in the parentheses. Each such selection gives a summand labelled by a different divisor. Each such selection is equivalent to select, for each {\displaystyle i=1,2,\ldots ,r}, a value {\displaystyle 0\leq x_{i}\leq a_{i}} which will represent selecting the summand {\displaystyle p_{i}^{x_{i}z}} from the {\displaystyle i}-th parentheses. On the other hand, this is the same collection of choices needed to select a divisor of {\displaystyle n}, and write it as its prime factorization. This is because each divisor of {\displaystyle n} can be written uniquely in the form {\displaystyle \prod _{i=1}^{r}p_{i}^{x_{i}}}, for {\displaystyle 0\leq x_{i}\leq a_{i}}.

Split the prime factors

A direct consequence of the formula is that the function {\displaystyle n\mapsto \sigma _{z}(n)} is multiplicative. In fact, if {\displaystyle a,b} are relatively prime, then they have prime factorizations {\displaystyle \prod _{i=1}^{r}p_{i}^{a_{i}}} and {\displaystyle \prod _{i=r+1}^{s}p_{i}^{a_{i}}} in which the set of primes {\displaystyle \{p_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r}} and {\displaystyle \{p_{i}\}_{i=r+1}^{s}} are disjoint. Then

{\displaystyle \sigma _{z}(ab)=\prod _{i=1}^{s}\sum _{j=0}^{a_{i}}p_{i}^{jz}=\prod _{i=1}^{r}\sum _{j=0}^{a_{i}}p_{i}^{jz}\cdot \prod _{i=r+1}^{s}\sum _{j=0}^{a_{i}}p_{i}^{jz}=\sigma _{z}(a)\sigma _{z}(b)}

Level justification

Expand finite prime-factor products and split the disjoint prime sets of coprime inputs. The exponent may be any complex number, defined using the complex exponential; elementary undergraduate complex-number functions suffice, with no analytic number theory.
