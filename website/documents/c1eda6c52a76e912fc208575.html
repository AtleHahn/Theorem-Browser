Statement

For f(x)=(x^2-2)^2 on [0,2]\cap\mathbb Q, continuity and differentiation interpreted through rational increments give f(0)=f(2), but f' has no zero in (0,2)\cap\mathbb Q.

Level

Proof 1: Exclude the irrational derivative zero — outline

For example, consider {\displaystyle f:\mathbb {Q} \to \mathbb {Q} }, such that for all {\displaystyle x\in \mathbb {Q} }, {\displaystyle f(x)=(x^{2}-2)^{2}}. Then {\displaystyle f} is continuous on {\displaystyle [0,2]\cap \mathbb {Q} }, differentiable on {\displaystyle (0,2)\cap \mathbb {Q} }, {\displaystyle f(0)=4} and {\displaystyle f(2)=4}, but there exists no {\displaystyle x\in (0,2)\cap \mathbb {Q} } such that {\displaystyle f'(x)=0}. In the real numbers, this value would be {\displaystyle {\sqrt {2}}}, but {\displaystyle {\sqrt {2}}} is not in {\displaystyle (0,2)\cap \mathbb {Q} }.
