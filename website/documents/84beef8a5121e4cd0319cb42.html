Statement

Let D^n=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:|x|\le1\} and S^{n-1}=\partial D^n, for an integer n\ge1. There is no continuous retraction D^n\to S^{n-1}. Here a retraction means a map r satisfying r(x)=x for every x\in S^{n-1}.

Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Euclidean balls and spheres; continuous retractions. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. Proof 1: Connectedness, fundamental group and homology by dimension

Let D^n=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:|x|\le1\} and S^{n-1}=\partial D^n, for an integer n\ge1. A retraction r:D^n\to S^{n-1} means a continuous map with r(x)=x for every x\in S^{n-1}.

In the case n = 1, the impossibility is more basic, because S0 (i.e., the endpoints of the closed interval D1) is not even connected. The case n = 2 is less obvious, but can be proven by using basic arguments involving the fundamental groups of the respective spaces: the retraction would induce a surjective group homomorphism from the fundamental group of D2 to that of S1, but the latter group is isomorphic to Z while the first group is trivial, so this is impossible.

For n > 2, however, proving the impossibility of the retraction is more difficult. One way is to make use of homology groups: the homology Hn−1(Dn) is trivial, while Hn−1(Sn−1) is infinite cyclic. This shows that the retraction is impossible, because inclusion of the sphere, split by the retraction, would induce an injective group homomorphism from the latter group to the former.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Connectedness settles dimension one, but the supplied argument for arbitrary dimension uses fundamental groups, induced homomorphisms, singular homology and its functoriality. Learning these linked algebraic-topology constructions exceeds a short extension of basic point-set topology. The final contradiction about a split map from a nonzero group to zero is elementary, but does not replace that preparation.

Proof 2: Punctured-space cohomology obstruction — outline

Let D^n=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:|x|\le1\} and S^{n-1}=\partial D^n, for an integer n\ge1. A retraction r:D^n\to S^{n-1} means a continuous map with r(x)=x for every x\in S^{n-1}.

For n=1, connectedness excludes a retraction from the interval onto its two endpoints.

Use the de Rham cohomology of open subsets of Euclidean space En. For n ≥ 2, the de Rham cohomology of U = En – (0) is one-dimensional in degree 0 and n – 1, and vanishes otherwise. If a retraction existed, then U would have to be contractible and its de Rham cohomology in degree n – 1 would have to vanish, a contradiction.
