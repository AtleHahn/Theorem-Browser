Statement

For a finite family of line segments in \mathbb R^2, its intersection graph has one vertex per set and edges for intersecting pairs. There is no function c of clique size alone such that every such graph satisfies \chi(G)\le c(\omega(G)), where \chi is the minimum proper vertex-color count and \omega the largest pairwise adjacent vertex set.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Intersection, coloring and clique numbers are defined for finite plane segments. An unbounded sequence of integer color counts requires elementary quantifier reasoning, not analysis.

Proof 1: Intersection graphs of plane segments are not chromatically bounded

The source invokes families of triangle-free intersection graphs of line segments in \mathbb R^2 with arbitrarily large chromatic number. Triangle-free graphs have clique size at most two, whereas their chromatic numbers are unbounded. Thus no bound depending only on clique size can hold.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Use the supplied triangle-free examples with unbounded color count; their largest clique size is at most two, so no bound at that fixed size can work. School finite graph and unbounded-integer reasoning suffice.
