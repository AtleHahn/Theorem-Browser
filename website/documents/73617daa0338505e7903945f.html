Statement

Let n\ge1, let A\subseteq\mathbb R^n be a nonempty convex set, and let a_0\in\partial A. There exists v\ne0 such that \langle v,a\rangle\le\langle v,a_0\rangle for every a\in A. Thus the hyperplane through a_0 with normal v supports A.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Convex subsets of Euclidean space; Inner products; Boundary points; Supporting hyperplanes. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. Proof 1: Separate a boundary singleton or extend the affine span — outline

If the affine span of A is not all of \mathbb R^n, then extend the affine span to a supporting hyperplane. It contains a_0, since a finite-dimensional affine subspace is closed and a_0\in\overline A.

Else, \operatorname{relint}(A)=\operatorname{int}(A) is disjoint from \operatorname{relint}(\{a_0\})=\{a_0\}, so apply the separation theorem for convex sets with disjoint relative interiors. Here \operatorname{relint} denotes interior in the affine span. The separating hyperplane passes through a_0 because a_0\in\overline A.

Level justification

Handle a proper affine span by linear algebra and otherwise separate the boundary singleton from the relative interior. Undergraduate Euclidean topology and affine linear algebra suffice; relative interior is explicitly interior in the affine span.
