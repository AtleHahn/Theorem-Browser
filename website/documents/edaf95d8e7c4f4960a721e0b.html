Statement

Let X be a real random variable with finite mean \mu. For an integer n\ge2, assume 0<M_n=\mathbb E|X-\mu|^n<\infty. Then for every real k>0, \Pr(|X-\mu|\ge kM_n^{1/n})\le k^{-n}. For n=2 this is Chebyshev’s variance inequality.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The moment M_n is explicitly defined as the weighted integral of |X-mu|^n. From undergraduate integration, learning probability-weighted expectation and a tail event is a small coherent extension within about 30 minutes. The integer power and its root are familiar, so higher moment order adds no new probability theory.

Proof 1: Apply Markov to the absolute central power — outline

Mitzenmacher and Upfal note that by applying Markov's inequality to the nonnegative variable {\displaystyle |X-\operatorname {E} (X)|^{n}}, one can get a family of tail bounds

{\displaystyle \Pr \left(|X-\operatorname {E} (X)|\geq k\operatorname {E} (|X-\operatorname {E} (X)|^{n})^{\frac {1}{n}}\right)\leq {\frac {1}{k^{n}}},\qquad k>0,\ n\geq 2.}

For {\displaystyle n=2} we obtain Chebyshev's inequality. Here \operatorname E(X)=\mu and M_n=\operatorname E|X-\mu|^n is positive and finite by hypothesis.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply Markov to the nonnegative variable |X-mu|^n with threshold k^n M_n. Monotonicity of positive powers identifies the event, and the expectation cancels. This requires only ordinary undergraduate moments and probability inequalities.
