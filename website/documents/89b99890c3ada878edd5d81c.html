Statement

Let n\geq3 be an integer. In the Euclidean plane, suppose a unit disk is surrounded by a cyclic ring of n disks with positive finite radii r_1,\ldots,r_n. The disks have pairwise disjoint interiors, are externally tangent to the unit disk, occur in cyclic order around it, and each disk is tangent to the next, including the last to the first.

Then

\min_{1\leq j\leq n}r_j\geq\frac{1}{F_{2n-3}-1},

where F_0=0, F_1=1 and F_{j+2}=F_{j+1}+F_j are the Fibonacci numbers.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All objects are elementary circles, positive lengths and an explicitly defined integer recurrence.

Proof 1: Extremal flowers and the Fibonacci bend recurrence (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Section7, Theorem10 and Proposition12, pp.124–130; Lemma7, p.118).

The source permits half-plane petals in the extremal closure; its bound therefore applies to the finite-radius rings here. The extremal bends start with b_2=0, b_3=1 and satisfy b_{j+1}=3b_j-b_{j-1}+1, giving b_n=F_{2n-3}-1. Equivalently, F_{n-1}^2+F_{n-2}^2=F_{2n-3}. For n=3 the bound is 1.

The omitted radius calculation in Lemma14 can be supplied in the inverted coordinates of Lemma13. Put t=\sqrt{x}, H=\sqrt{h}, with the fixed quantities \rho\ge0, y\ge0 and \sigma>0 as in that parametrization. The added interstice disk has radius z=(tH/(t+H))^2 and horizontal center coordinate Y=y+2\sqrt\rho\,t+2t\sqrt z. Its bend after inversion is

\frac{\sigma^2+Y^2}{z}-2\sigma =\sigma^2(1/t+1/H)^2+ \bigl[y/t+y/H+2\sqrt\rho+(2\sqrt\rho/H+2)t\bigr]^2-2\sigma.

Expanding gives a strictly convex function of t>0: the coefficients of t^{-2},t^{-1},t,t^2 are nonnegative and that of t^{-2} is positive. It has no interior maximum, so the smallest re-inverted disk occurs at a parameter endpoint, where an additional nonneighboring tangency occurs.
