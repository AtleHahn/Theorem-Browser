Statement

Let ABC be a nondegenerate Euclidean triangle with circumcenter O, circumradius R, incenter I, and inradius r. Let L be the second intersection of ray AI with the circumcircle. Then LB\cdot IA=2Rr.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All objects are elementary triangle and circle constructions.

none

Proof 1: Compare the two right triangles

The point L is the midpoint of the arc BC not containing A. Draw the ray LO and denote its second intersection with the circumcircle by M.

Then LM is a diameter of the circumcircle. Drop the perpendicular ID from the point I to AB. Then ID=r.

Now observe that 2R=LM and r=ID, that is, the required equality can be rewritten as LB\cdot IA=LM\cdot ID. Rewriting it once more, we obtain LB/LM=ID/IA.

This equality follows from the similarity of triangles \triangle AID and \triangle MLB.

Indeed, the angles at B and D in these triangles are right angles, while the angles at A and M are equal because they both subtend the arc BL (moreover, the ratio LB/LM=ID/IA is equal to the sine of the angle \angle BAL).

Level
High school only
Level justification

Use the diameter LM=2R and incircle radius ID=r. The two right triangles have the same acute angle subtending BL, so their opposite-side-to-hypotenuse ratios agree. Cross multiplication yields LB times IA=2Rr. This uses standard circle geometry, triangle centers and similarity only.
