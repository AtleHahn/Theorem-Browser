Statement

If two real or complex sequences (a_i)_{i\ge0} and (b_i)_{i\ge0} agree for all sufficiently large indices, then \sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_i converges if and only if \sum_{i=0}^{\infty}b_i converges.

Proof 1: Ignoring the finite prefix in the tail criterion — outline

Use the Cauchy series criterion: convergence is equivalent to all sufficiently late finite tail sums being arbitrarily small. Since the two sequences agree after their finite initial portions, those late tail sums are the same for the two series. Hence the criterion, and therefore convergence, holds for either both or neither.
