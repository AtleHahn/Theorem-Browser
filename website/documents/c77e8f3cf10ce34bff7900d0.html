Statement

Let X be a set with binary operations \circ,\otimes:X\times X\to X and respective two-sided identities e_\circ,e_\otimes. Thus e_\circ\circ a=a=a\circ e_\circ and e_\otimes\otimes a=a=a\otimes e_\otimes for every a\in X. Suppose (a\otimes b)\circ(c\otimes d)=(a\circ c)\otimes(b\circ d) for all a,b,c,d\in X. Then e_\circ=e_\otimes, the two operations coincide, and their common operation is associative and commutative.

Proof 1: Insert identity elements into the interchange law

First, observe that the units of the two operations coincide: {\displaystyle e_{\circ }=e_{\circ }\circ e_{\circ }=(e_{\otimes }\otimes e_{\circ })\circ (e_{\circ }\otimes e_{\otimes })=(e_{\otimes }\circ e_{\circ })\otimes (e_{\circ }\circ e_{\otimes })=e_{\otimes }\otimes e_{\otimes }=e_{\otimes }}.

Write the common identity as 1. Now, let {\displaystyle a,b\in X}. Then {\displaystyle a\circ b=(1\otimes a)\circ (b\otimes 1)=(1\circ b)\otimes (a\circ 1)=b\otimes a=(b\circ 1)\otimes (1\circ a)=(b\otimes 1)\circ (1\otimes a)=b\circ a}. This establishes that the two operations coincide and are commutative.

For associativity, {\displaystyle (a\otimes b)\otimes c=(a\otimes b)\otimes (1\otimes c)=(a\otimes 1)\otimes (b\otimes c)=a\otimes (b\otimes c)}.
