Statement

For a continuous 2\pi-periodic function f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb C, put \widehat f(n)=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_0^{2\pi}f(t)e^{-int}\,dt and S_Nf(x)=\sum_{|n|\le N}\widehat f(n)e^{inx}. If f is continuously differentiable, then S_Nf(x)\to f(x) for every real x.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves continuously differentiable periodic functions; Fourier coefficients; complex trigonometric series; pointwise convergence. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Specialize bounded-variation convergence — outline

If a periodic function has bounded variation then its Fourier series converges everywhere to the local average of the function, by Dirichlet's convergence theorem. Here the local average is the average of the two one-sided limits. In particular, if a function is continuously differentiable then its Fourier series converges to it everywhere.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Fourier coefficients and partial sums are explicitly defined by ordinary integrals. Bounded variation is the single extra concept: C1 on a compact interval gives a bounded derivative and hence variation at most interval length times that bound. The imported convergence theorem then gives f because the one-sided limits agree.
