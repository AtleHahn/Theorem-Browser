Statement

Let a finite group G act on a finite set X. Put X^g=\{x\in X:g\cdot x=x\} and G_x=\{g\in G:g\cdot x=x\}. The orbit of x is G\cdot x=\{g\cdot x:g\in G\}, and X/G is the set of these orbits. Then |X/G|=|G|^{-1}\sum_{g\in G}|X^g|.

Proof 1: Double counting fixed pairs and summing over orbits

In the proof of Burnside's lemma, the first step is to re-express the sum over the group elements g ∈ G as an equivalent sum over the set of elements x ∈ X:

{\displaystyle \sum _{g\in G}|X^{g}|=\#\{(g,x)\in G\times X\mid g\cdot x=x\}=\sum _{x\in X}|G_{x}|.}

Here {\displaystyle X^{g}=\{x\in X:g\cdot x=x\}} is the set of points of {\displaystyle X} fixed by the element {\displaystyle g} of {\displaystyle G}, whereas {\displaystyle G_{x}=\{g\in G:g\cdot x=x\}} is the stabilizer subgroup of {\displaystyle G}, consisting of those symmetries that fix the point {\displaystyle x\in X}.

The orbit-stabilizer theorem says that for each {\displaystyle x\in X} there is a natural bijection between the orbit {\displaystyle G\cdot x=\{g\cdot x:g\in G\}} and the set of left cosets {\displaystyle G/G_{x}}. Lagrange's theorem implies that

{\displaystyle |G\cdot x|=[G:G_{x}]=|G|/|G_{x}|.}

The sum may therefore be rewritten as

{\displaystyle \sum _{x\in X}|G_{x}|=\sum _{x\in X}{\frac {|G|}{|G\cdot x|}}=|G|\sum _{x\in X}{\frac {1}{|G\cdot x|}}.}

Writing {\displaystyle X} as the disjoint union of its orbits in {\displaystyle X/G} gives

{\displaystyle |G|\sum _{x\in X}{\frac {1}{|G\cdot x|}}=|G|\sum _{A\in X/G}\sum _{x\in A}{\frac {1}{|A|}}=|G|\sum _{A\in X/G}1=|G|\cdot |X/G|.}

Putting everything together gives the desired result:

{\displaystyle \sum _{g\in G}|X^{g}|=|G|\cdot |X/G|.}
