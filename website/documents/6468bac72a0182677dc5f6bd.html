Statement

Let d\ge1 be an integer. Call (q,r) admissible if 2\le q,r\le\infty, 2/q+d/r=d/2, and (q,r,d)\ne(2,\infty,2), with 1/\infty=0. Let U(t)=e^{it\Delta/2} be the free Schrödinger group on \mathbb R^d, defined by \widehat{U(t)f}(\xi)=e^{-it|\xi|^2/2}\widehat f(\xi) using the Fourier transform with kernel e^{-ix\cdot\xi}. For any two admissible pairs (q,r) and (\widetilde q,\widetilde r) there exists C_{d,q,r,\widetilde q,\widetilde r} such that

\left\|\int_{s<t}U(t-s)F(s)\,ds\right\|_{L_t^qL_x^r}\le C_{d,q,r,\widetilde q,\widetilde r}\|F\|_{L_t^{\widetilde q'}L_x^{\widetilde r'}}.

Here 1/a+1/a'=1. The retarded integral, initially on smooth compactly supported functions, is interpreted as its bounded extension to the displayed domain. The mixed norm is \|v\|_{L_t^qL_x^r}=\left(\int_{\mathbb R}(\int_{\mathbb R^d}|v(t,x)|^r dx)^{q/r}dt\right)^{1/q}, replacing each integral norm of exponent \infty by essential supremum. All norms use Lebesgue measure.
