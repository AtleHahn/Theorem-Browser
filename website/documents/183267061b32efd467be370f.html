Statement

Start with C_0=[0,1]. Obtain C_{k+1} from C_k by removing the open middle third of each of its component intervals, and let C=\bigcap_{k=0}^{\infty}C_k.

Let \mu be the Cantor probability measure on the Borel subsets of \mathbb R: it is characterized by assigning mass 2^{-k} to each of the 2^k closed component intervals of C_k, for every k\geq0. Let \lambda be Lebesgue measure on \mathbb R. Then

\mu(\{x\})=0\quad(x\in\mathbb R),\qquad \mu(C)=1,\qquad\lambda(C)=0.

Thus \mu is atomless and singular with respect to \lambda. Its cumulative distribution function F(x)=\mu(( -\infty,x]) is continuous, while \mu is not absolutely continuous with respect to \lambda.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The example combines measure-theoretic probability, Borel/Lebesgue measure, null-set concentration and atomlessness. Introductory real analysis alone does not guarantee these concepts, and their cumulative burden exceeds the conservative short-reading allowance.
