Statement

For \alpha>0 and a positive integer n, with (2n-1)!!=1\cdot3\cdots(2n-1), \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x^{2n}e^{-\alpha x^2}\,dx=\sqrt{\frac\pi\alpha}\frac{(2n-1)!!}{(2\alpha)^n}. The corresponding integral over [0,\infty) is half this value.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Improper integrals; Integer powers; Double factorials. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Even moments of a Gaussian integral — outline

An easy way to derive the moments is by differentiating under the integral sign.

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\int _{-\infty }^{\infty }x^{2n}e^{-\alpha x^{2}}\,dx&=\left(-1\right)^{n}\int _{-\infty }^{\infty }{\frac {\partial ^{n}}{\partial \alpha ^{n}}}e^{-\alpha x^{2}}\,dx\\[1ex]&=\left(-1\right)^{n}{\frac {\partial ^{n}}{\partial \alpha ^{n}}}\int _{-\infty }^{\infty }e^{-\alpha x^{2}}\,dx\\[1ex]&={\sqrt {\pi }}\left(-1\right)^{n}{\frac {\partial ^{n}}{\partial \alpha ^{n}}}\alpha ^{-{\frac {1}{2}}}\\[1ex]&={\sqrt {\frac {\pi }{\alpha }}}{\frac {(2n-1)!!}{\left(2\alpha \right)^{n}}}\end{aligned}}}

Evenness gives the half-line integral.
