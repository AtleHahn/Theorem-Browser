Statement

A partition of a nonnegative integer is a multiset of positive integers whose sum is that integer; order of parts is ignored. For integers n\geq1 and k\geq1, the number of partitions of n into exactly k parts equals the number whose largest part equals k.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The objects and counting argument are explicit finite integer combinatorics.

Proof 1: Partitions with k parts and partitions with largest part k — proof

Represent a partition by left-aligned rows of boxes whose lengths are its parts. Conjugation turns rows into columns and is its own inverse. Thus exactly k rows become a longest row of length k, preserving the number of boxes.

Level justification

Transpose the rows and columns of a finite box diagram; the operation preserves size, exchanges row count with largest row length and is its own inverse. School finite counting suffices.
