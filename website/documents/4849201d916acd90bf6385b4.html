Statement

Let (M,g) be a time-oriented Lorentzian manifold with signature (-,+,\ldots,+). If a smooth real function \tau has past-directed timelike gradient, then it is a time function: it strictly increases on every nonconstant future-directed causal curve. Consequently M has no closed nonconstant future-directed causal curve.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Lorentzian gradients; Timelike and causal vectors; Time functions. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Pair the temporal gradient with future causal tangents — outline

The source uses the past-directed gradient condition to conclude strict increase on future-directed causal curves. This is the usual gradient pairing with the causal tangent. Such strict increase rules out a closed causal curve, as used in the source’s causality discussion.
