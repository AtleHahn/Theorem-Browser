Statement

Let S be a closed connected oriented surface of genus g\ge2, and let \rho:\pi_1(S)\to\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb C) be a discrete faithful representation with no parabolic elements in its image. Suppose it is singly degenerate: its domain of discontinuity has exactly one component, which is invariant and simply connected, and the quotient of that component is a marked compact Riemann surface X of the same topological type. Then [\rho] lies on the boundary of the Bers slice fixing X. More explicitly, there are marked quasi-Fuchsian representations \rho_j whose first conformal boundary component is X and whose conjugacy classes converge algebraically to [\rho], while \rho is not quasi-Fuchsian. Algebraic convergence of conjugacy classes means that representatives can be chosen, by conjugation in \operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb C), which converge on every element of \pi_1(S). The limit set of a discrete group is the accumulation set on the sphere at infinity of an orbit in hyperbolic three-space; the domain of discontinuity is its complement. A parabolic element is a nonidentity Möbius transformation with exactly one fixed point on that sphere. A marked quasi-Fuchsian surface representation is a discrete faithful surface-group representation with Jordan-curve limit set and two invariant complementary components; their quotients are its two marked conformal boundary surfaces. A marking identifies the fundamental group with that of S up to the usual isotopy convention. The Bers slice fixing X consists of these quasi-Fuchsian conjugacy classes with the first quotient marked conformally equivalent to X; the other quotient varies. Labels are chosen so the surviving component of \rho is the first one.
