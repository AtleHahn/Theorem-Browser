Statement

Let X be a real random variable with finite mean \mu and finite variance \sigma^2, and let \varphi:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R be twice differentiable and convex, with \varphi(X) integrable. Then \mathbb E\varphi(X)-\varphi(\mathbb EX)\ge0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Means, variances and expectations of a scalar function add linked integral definitions to undergraduate differentiability and convexity. No independence or stochastic convergence is present.

Proof 1: Nonnegative second derivative in the lower variance bound

Use the source-stated lower variance bound \mathbb E\varphi(X)-\varphi(\mu)\ge\sigma^2\inf_{x\in\mathbb R}\frac{\varphi^{\prime\prime}(x)}2. Convexity gives \varphi^{\prime\prime}(x)\ge0, so this lower bound is nonnegative and the claimed Jensen inequality follows.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use nonnegative second derivative in the explicit lower variance bound. Undergraduate probability, variance and calculus suffice; no proof of the quantitative bound is needed.
