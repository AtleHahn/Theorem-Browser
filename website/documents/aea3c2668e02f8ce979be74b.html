Statement

Let m,x,y,N,k\in\mathbb C with k\ne0. If Nx^2+k=y^2, then N((mx+y)/k)^2+(m^2-N)/k=((my+Nx)/k)^2. If also m^2-N\ne0, the implication is an equivalence. This includes real and integer inputs.

Level
High school only
Level justification

This is an explicit algebraic identity and its converse for complex numbers, with nonzero denominators stated. Understanding it requires only substitution and polynomial arithmetic. This rating concerns understanding the statement, independently of the difficulty of its proof.

Proof 1: Multiply, complete the two squares, and divide

The proof follows from simple algebraic manipulations as follows: multiply both sides of the equation by {\displaystyle m^{2}-N}, add {\displaystyle N^{2}x^{2}+2Nmxy+Ny^{2}}, factor, and divide by {\displaystyle k^{2}}.

{\displaystyle \,Nx^{2}+k=y^{2}\implies Nm^{2}x^{2}-N^{2}x^{2}+k(m^{2}-N)=m^{2}y^{2}-Ny^{2}}{\displaystyle \implies Nm^{2}x^{2}+2Nmxy+Ny^{2}+k(m^{2}-N)=m^{2}y^{2}+2Nmxy+N^{2}x^{2}}{\displaystyle \implies N(mx+y)^{2}+k(m^{2}-N)=(my+Nx)^{2}}{\displaystyle \implies \,N\left({\frac {mx+y}{k}}\right)^{2}+{\frac {m^{2}-N}{k}}=\left({\frac {my+Nx}{k}}\right)^{2}.}

So long as neither {\displaystyle k} nor {\displaystyle m^{2}-N} are zero, the implication goes in both directions. (The lemma holds for real or complex numbers as well as integers.)

Level
High school only
Level justification

Multiply, expand, complete the two squares and divide by the assumed nonzero k^2. These are polynomial identities valid for complex numbers by the same elementary algebra as for real numbers. Reversal additionally requires m^2-N nonzero because that was the initial multiplier. No complex-analysis or number-theoretic theorem is used.
