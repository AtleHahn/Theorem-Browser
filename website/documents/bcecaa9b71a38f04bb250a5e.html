In a finite ergodic measure-preserving system, the long-run mean return time is the reciprocal of the region's relative measure. This connects temporal recurrence along typical trajectories with ensemble size, clarifying one precise sense in which dynamical and statistical descriptions agree.

Statement

Let (X,\Sigma,\mu) be a measure space with 0<\mu(X)<\infty, and let T:X\to X be measure preserving and ergodic: every measurable set invariant modulo null sets has measure zero or full measure. Let A\in\Sigma have \mu(A)>0. For almost every x\in A, write its successive visit times as 0=k_0<k_1<\cdots, and put R_i=k_i-k_{i-1}. Then (R_1+\cdots+R_n)/n\to\mu(X)/\mu(A).

Proof 1: Invert the visit frequency along occurrence times — outline

The ergodic visit-frequency theorem gives frequency \mu(A)/\mu(X) almost everywhere.

The occurrence times of a measurable set A is defined as the set k1, k2, k3, ..., of times k such that Tk(x) is in A, sorted in increasing order. The differences between consecutive occurrence times Ri = ki − ki−1 are called the recurrence times of A. Another consequence of the ergodic theorem is that the average recurrence time of A is inversely proportional to the measure of A, assuming that the initial point x is in A, so that k0 = 0.

{\displaystyle {\frac {R_{1}+\cdots +R_{n}}{n}}\to {\frac {\mu (X)}{\mu (A)}}\quad {\text{(almost surely)}}}

That is, the smaller A is, the longer it takes to return to it.

Level justification

Inverting visit frequencies uses an ergodic measure-preserving transformation and almost-everywhere statements for arbitrary positive-measure sets. This is measure-theoretic dynamics beyond the baseline.
