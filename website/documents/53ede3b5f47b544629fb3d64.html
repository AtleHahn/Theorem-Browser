Statement

Put K=\{(s,t)\in\mathbb R^2:t>0\}\cup\{(s,0):s>0\} and F=\mathbb R\times\{0\}. The functional \phi(s,0)=s is nonnegative on F\cap K, but no real linear functional on \mathbb R^2 extending \phi is nonnegative on all of K.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Real linear functionals on the plane; Coordinate inequalities. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Check the explicit half-plane witness — outline

Already in two dimensions one obtains a counterexample. Let E=\mathbb R^2, K=\{(s,t):t>0\}\cup\{(s,0):s>0\}, and F be the horizontal axis. The positive functional \phi(s,0)=s cannot be extended to a positive functional on E.

The source states this counterexample without verifying the nonexistence of a positive extension.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

An extension has the form s+ct; taking t positive and s sufficiently negative defeats positivity. Undergraduate two-dimensional linear functionals suffice; the source leaves this elementary verification implicit.
