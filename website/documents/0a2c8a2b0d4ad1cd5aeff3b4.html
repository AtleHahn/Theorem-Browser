Statement

Let n\ge1 be an integer, a_1\le\cdots\le a_n and b_1\ge\cdots\ge b_n be real numbers. Then \frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n a_jb_j\le\left(\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^na_j\right)\left(\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^nb_j\right).

Level
High school only
Level justification

The assertion concerns only ordered real numbers and finite arithmetic.

Proof 1: Use opposite signs in the double sum — outline

Consider the sum

{\displaystyle S=\sum _{j=1}^{n}\sum _{k=1}^{n}(a_{j}-a_{k})(b_{j}-b_{k}).}

The two sequences have opposite monotonicity, therefore aj − ak and bj − bk have opposite signs or one is zero for any j, k. Hence S ≤ 0.

Opening the brackets, we deduce:

{\displaystyle 0\geq 2n\sum _{j=1}^{n}a_{j}b_{j}-2\sum _{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\,\sum _{j=1}^{n}b_{j},}

hence

{\displaystyle {\frac {1}{n}}\sum _{j=1}^{n}a_{j}b_{j}\leq \left({\frac {1}{n}}\sum _{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\right)\!\!\left({\frac {1}{n}}\sum _{j=1}^{n}b_{j}\right)\!.}
Level
High school only
Level justification

Each difference product is nonpositive; expanding the finite double sum and dividing by 2n² yields the claim. This is finite algebra and order comparison.
