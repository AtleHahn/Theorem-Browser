Statement

Let B be the dual closed unit ball of a real or complex normed space. If \mathcal C is a cover of B by convex weak-star closed subsets of the dual such that \{B\cap C:C\in\mathcal C\} has the finite intersection property, then B\cap\bigcap_{C\in\mathcal C}C\ne\varnothing.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement is an intersection property for weak-star closed sets in a dual unit ball.

Proof 1: Apply the closed-set compactness criterion

By Banach–Alaoglu, the ball B is weak-star compact. Compactness implies convex compactness because a topological space is compact if and only if every family of closed subsets having the finite intersection property (FIP) has non-empty intersection.

The definition of convex compactness is similar to this characterization of compact spaces in terms of the FIP, except that it only involves those closed subsets that are also convex (rather than all closed subsets). Apply this to the family \{B\cap C:C\in\mathcal C\} of closed subsets of B.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The compactness deduction is standard point-set topology, but its input is compactness of a continuous-dual ball in the weak-star topology. Understanding the dual norm, weak-star neighborhoods and their distinction from norm topology is a functional-analysis prerequisite package exceeding a brief extension of the undergraduate baseline. Convexity and the covering condition are stronger than needed for this deduction.
