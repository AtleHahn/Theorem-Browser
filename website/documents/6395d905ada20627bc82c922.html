Statement

Let K be a compact convex region in the Euclidean plane of constant width w>0: for every direction, the two parallel supporting lines on opposite sides of K have distance w. Then

\operatorname{area}(K)\geq\frac{\pi-\sqrt3}{2}\,w^2.

Equality holds if and only if K is a Reuleaux triangle of width w, up to congruence. Such a triangle is the intersection of the three closed disks of radius w centered at the vertices of an equilateral triangle of side length w.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All geometric objects have elementary local definitions using lines, disks and triangles. Understanding the extremal area claim does not require calculus or abstract convex geometry.

Review notes

  • The unique minimum-area constant-width body is a classical extremal theorem in convex geometry.

Proof 1: Inner parallel sets and the minimum inradius (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Section 3, Theorems 5–6 and Corollary 7, pp.6–10; equality argument following (3.7).).

Apply Bogosel Theorems 5–6 and Corollary 7, Section 3, pp.6–10 of arXiv:2303.15559. Theorem 6 proves the area comparison for inner parallel sets, extends by Reuleaux-polygon approximation, and obtains equality only for the Reuleaux triangle from its uniquely minimal inradius. That classical inradius result is an explicitly cited dependency (Exercise 7-14 of the cited geometry text); accept it without re-proving it. Set t=0. Rescale width 1 to w; area scales by w squared. Area of the unit Reuleaux triangle is three pi/3 sectors minus twice an equilateral triangle, namely (pi-sqrt(3))/2.
