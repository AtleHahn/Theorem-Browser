Statement

Let K be a compact convex region in the Euclidean plane of constant width w>0: for every direction, the two parallel supporting lines on opposite sides of K have distance w. Then

\operatorname{area}(K)\geq\frac{\pi-\sqrt3}{2}\,w^2.

Equality holds if and only if K is a Reuleaux triangle of width w, up to congruence. Such a triangle is the intersection of the three closed disks of radius w centered at the vertices of an equilateral triangle of side length w.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All geometric objects have elementary local definitions using lines, disks and triangles. Understanding the extremal area claim does not require calculus or abstract convex geometry.
