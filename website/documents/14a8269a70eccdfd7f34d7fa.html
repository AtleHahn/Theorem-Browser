Statement

Let a smooth convex closed oval enclose a region of positive area A, and let P lie in its interior. Take P as polar origin. Let r(θ), θ≥0, be the accumulated area swept from angle 0 to θ, counting each full turn again, so r(θ+2π)=r(θ)+A. The spiral with polar coordinates (r(θ),θ) is not contained in the zero set of any nonzero real polynomial in the Cartesian coordinates.

Proof 1: Intersect the accumulated-area spiral with lines — outline

Each additional turn increases the radius by the positive area A. Thus the spiral meets every line through P at infinitely many distinct points. A nonzero polynomial restricted to a line has only finitely many zeros unless it vanishes identically on that line; moreover a nonzero plane polynomial can contain only finitely many distinct lines in its zero set. Choose a line through P that is not such a component. Its infinitely many spiral intersections contradict the finiteness of the polynomial’s zeros there.

Level justification

Use accumulated area and polar coordinates to get infinitely many intersections on each radial line, then apply the polynomial restriction criterion. Undergraduate calculus and polynomial algebra suffice; algebraic curves here mean only zero sets of plane polynomials.
