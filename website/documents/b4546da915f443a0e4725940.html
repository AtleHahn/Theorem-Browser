Statement

Let ABC be a nondegenerate Euclidean triangle and let D lie strictly between B and C on the side BC. Then AD bisects the interior angle BAC if and only if

\frac{|BD|}{|CD|}=\frac{|AB|}{|AC|}.

Here |PQ| denotes the length of the segment joining P and Q, and bisecting means that \angle BAD=\angle DAC.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The complete statement involves only elementary triangle geometry and ratios, with local definitions.

  • A classical independent characterization of the internal angle bisector by side ratios.

Proof 1: Area ratios and uniqueness of side division (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Proof 1 (area ratios)).

For the converse, let E be where the internal bisector meets BC. The forward direction gives BE/EC=AB/AC=BD/DC. Writing L=BC, x=BE and y=BD, cross-multiplication gives x(L-y)=y(L-x) and hence x=y. Thus E=D. This converse is an additional elementary deduction from the linked forward proof.
