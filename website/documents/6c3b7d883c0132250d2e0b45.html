Statement

Let G,G' be groups with identities e,e'. Subgroups H\le G\times G' whose two coordinate projections are surjective are in bijection with triples (N,N',f) consisting of normal subgroups N\trianglelefteq G, N'\trianglelefteq G' and an isomorphism f:G/N\to G'/N'.

For a subgroup H, the corresponding normal subgroups are

N=\{g\in G:(g,e')\in H\},\qquad N'=\{g'\in G':(e,g')\in H\},

and f(gN)=g'N' whenever (g,g')\in H. Thus the image of H in G/N\times G'/N' is the graph of f. Conversely, the subgroup corresponding to a triple is

H=\{(g,g')\in G\times G':f(gN)=g'N'\}.

Proof 1: Identify the quotient relation as an isomorphism

Let p_1:H\to G and p_2:H\to G' be the coordinate projections. Write \widetilde N=\ker p_2=N\times\{e'\} and \widetilde N'=\ker p_1=\{e\}\times N'. Before proceeding with the proof, \widetilde N and \widetilde N' are shown to be normal in G\times\{e'\} and \{e\}\times G', respectively. It is in this sense that they can be identified with normal subgroups of G and G'.

Since p_2 is a homomorphism, its kernel \widetilde N is normal in H. Moreover, given g\in G, there exists h=(g,g')\in H, since p_1 is surjective. Therefore, p_1(\widetilde N)=N is normal in G, viz:

g p_1(\widetilde N) =p_1(h)p_1(\widetilde N) =p_1(h\widetilde N) =p_1(\widetilde N h) =p_1(\widetilde N)g.

It follows that \widetilde N is normal in G\times\{e'\} since

\begin{aligned} (g,e')\widetilde N &=(g,e')(N\times\{e'\})\\ &=gN\times\{e'\}\\ &=Ng\times\{e'\}\\ &=(N\times\{e'\})(g,e')\\ &=\widetilde N(g,e'). \end{aligned}

The proof that \widetilde N' is normal in \{e\}\times G' proceeds in a similar manner.

Given the identification of G with G\times\{e'\}, we use G/N and gN for the corresponding quotient and cosets; similarly for G'/N' and g'N'.

On to the proof. Consider the map H\to G/N\times G'/N' defined by

(g,g')\longmapsto(gN,g'N').

The image of H under this map is

\{(gN,g'N'):(g,g')\in H\}.

Since H\to G/N is surjective, this relation is the graph of a well-defined function G/N\to G'/N' provided

g_1N=g_2N\implies g_1'N'=g_2'N'

for every (g_1,g_1'),(g_2,g_2')\in H, essentially an application of the vertical line test.

Since g_1N=g_2N, we have (g_2^{-1}g_1,e')\in\widetilde N\subset H. Thus

(e,(g_2')^{-1}g_1') =(g_2,g_2')^{-1}(g_1,g_1')(g_2^{-1}g_1,e')^{-1}\in H,

whence (e,(g_2')^{-1}g_1')\in\widetilde N', that is, g_1'N'=g_2'N'.

Furthermore, for every (g_1,g_1'),(g_2,g_2')\in H we have (g_1g_2,g_1'g_2')\in H. It follows that this function is a group homomorphism.

By symmetry,

\{(g'N',gN):(g,g')\in H\}

is the graph of a well-defined homomorphism G'/N'\to G/N. These two homomorphisms are clearly inverse to each other and thus are indeed isomorphisms.

The source leaves the routine reverse-correspondence checks implicit: the inverse image of the graph of an isomorphism under the quotient map gives the subgroup associated with the triple, and the two constructions undo one another.
