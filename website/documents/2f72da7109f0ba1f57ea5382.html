Statement

Let \Sigma=\operatorname{Th}(\mathbb R;+,-,\cdot,0,1,<) be the set of all first-order ordered-field sentences true in the real numbers. There is a model K\models\Sigma containing an element \omega with \omega>n for every nonnegative integer n.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement concerns ordered-field models of an infinite first-order theory.

Proof 1: Adjoin finitely satisfiable largeness constraints

Add a new constant \omega and the axioms \omega>0,\omega>1,\ldots. Every finite subset is satisfied in \mathbb R by choosing a real number larger than the finitely many listed integers. Compactness gives a model of the whole expanded theory, containing the required infinitely large element.

Level justification

Expand the full first-order theory of the reals by a constant and use compactness to obtain a nonstandard model. Ordered-field model theory, language expansion and satisfaction require substantial logical background beyond a small undergraduate extension.
