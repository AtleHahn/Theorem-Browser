Statement

Let B_n be the number of unlabelled ordered full binary trees with n internal nodes: a tree is either a leaf, of size zero, or a root with an ordered pair of such subtrees. Then for every integer n\ge0, B_n=\frac1{n+1}\binom{2n}{n}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Recursively defined ordered full binary trees and counting their shapes require introductory undergraduate combinatorics or computer science. A bright US college student should be able to understand the statement after about 30 minutes of focused background research; this rating concerns understanding the claim, not proving it.

Proof 1: Root decomposition and Lagrange inversion

Consider the set {\displaystyle {\mathcal {B}}} of unlabelled ordered full binary trees. An element of {\displaystyle {\mathcal {B}}} is either a leaf of size zero, or a root node with two subtrees. Denote by {\displaystyle B_{n}} the number of binary trees on {\displaystyle n} internal nodes.

Removing the root splits a binary tree into two trees of smaller size. This yields the functional equation on the generating function {\displaystyle \textstyle B(z)=\sum _{n=0}^{\infty }B_{n}z^{n}{\text{:}}}

{\displaystyle B(z)=1+zB(z)^{2}.}

Letting {\displaystyle C(z)=B(z)-1}, one has thus {\displaystyle C(z)=z(C(z)+1)^{2}.} For n\ge1, applying formal Lagrange coefficient inversion with {\displaystyle \phi (w)=(w+1)^{2}} yields

{\displaystyle B_{n}=[z^{n}]C(z)={\frac {1}{n}}[w^{n-1}](w+1)^{2n}={\frac {1}{n}}{\binom {2n}{n-1}}={\frac {1}{n+1}}{\binom {2n}{n}}.}

This shows that {\displaystyle B_{n}} is the nth Catalan number.

For n=0 there is one leaf.

Formal generating functions and coefficient extraction are a small related extension of undergraduate discrete mathematics and polynomial algebra; applying the displayed Lagrange formula then requires only a binomial coefficient.
