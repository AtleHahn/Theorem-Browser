Statement

Let n\ge1 be an integer and let f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb C be Lebesgue measurable with \int_{\mathbb R^n}|f(x)|\,dx<\infty. Define \widehat f(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb R^n}f(x)e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\,dx. Then |\widehat f(\xi)|\to0 as |\xi|\to\infty.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement uses measure-theoretic L1 integrability and Fourier integrals in arbitrary dimension.

Proof 1: Translation cancellation and L1 approximation (outline)

We will focus on the one-dimensional case {\displaystyle n=1}, the proof in higher dimensions is similar. First, suppose that {\displaystyle f} is continuous and compactly supported. For {\displaystyle \xi \neq 0}, the substitution {\displaystyle \textstyle x\to x+{\frac {\pi }{\xi }}} leads to

{\displaystyle {\hat {f}}(\xi )=\int _{\mathbb {R} }f(x)\mathrm {e} ^{-\mathrm {i} x\xi }\mathrm {d} x=\int _{\mathbb {R} }f\left(x+{\frac {\pi }{\xi }}\right)\mathrm {e} ^{-\mathrm {i} x\xi }\mathrm {e} ^{-\mathrm {i} \pi }\mathrm {d} x=-\int _{\mathbb {R} }f\left(x+{\frac {\pi }{\xi }}\right)\mathrm {e} ^{-\mathrm {i} x\xi }\mathrm {d} x}.

This gives a second formula for {\displaystyle {\hat {f}}(\xi )}. Taking the mean of both formulas, we arrive at the following estimate:

{\displaystyle |{\hat {f}}(\xi )|\leq {\frac {1}{2}}\int _{\mathbb {R} }\left|f(x)-f\left(x+{\frac {\pi }{\xi }}\right)\right|\mathrm {d} x}.

Because {\displaystyle f} is continuous, {\displaystyle \left|f(x)-f\left(x+{\tfrac {\pi }{\xi }}\right)\right|} converges to {\displaystyle 0} as {\displaystyle |\xi |\to \infty } for all {\displaystyle x\in \mathbb {R} }. Thus, {\displaystyle |{\hat {f}}(\xi )|} converges to 0 as {\displaystyle |\xi |\to \infty } due to the dominated convergence theorem.

If {\displaystyle f} is an arbitrary integrable function, it may be approximated in the {\displaystyle L^{1}} norm by a compactly supported continuous function. For {\displaystyle \varepsilon >0}, pick a compactly supported continuous function {\displaystyle g} such that {\displaystyle \|f-g\|_{L^{1}}\leq \varepsilon }. Then

{\displaystyle \limsup _{\xi \rightarrow \pm \infty }|{\hat {f}}(\xi )|\leq \limsup _{\xi \to \pm \infty }\left|\int (f(x)-g(x))\mathrm {e} ^{-\mathrm {i} x\xi }\,\mathrm {d} x\right|+\limsup _{\xi \rightarrow \pm \infty }\left|\int g(x)\mathrm {e} ^{-\mathrm {i} x\xi }\,\mathrm {d} x\right|\leq \varepsilon +0=\varepsilon .}

Because this holds for any {\displaystyle \varepsilon >0}, it follows that {\displaystyle |{\hat {f}}(\xi )|\to 0} as {\displaystyle |\xi |\to \infty }.
