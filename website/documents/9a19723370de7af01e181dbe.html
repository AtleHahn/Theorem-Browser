Statement

Let n\ge1 and u_0,\ldots,u_{n-1}\in C^1(I) on an open interval I=(a,b). All functions are real-valued. Set f_0=u_0', f_j=u_{j-1}+u_j' for 1\le j<n, and f_n=u_{n-1}. Then \int_a^b\sum_{j=0}^nf_j(x)h^{(j)}(x)\,dx=0 for every smooth compactly supported h.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Continuous real functions; Smooth compactly supported test functions; Integration; Differentiation. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Write the integrand as a sum of total derivatives

The integrand is (u_0h)'+(u_1h')'+\cdots+(u_{n-1}h^{(n-1)})'. Its integral vanishes since h and all its derivatives vanish near the endpoints.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Expand a finite sum of product derivatives and integrate; compact support makes all boundary terms zero. Undergraduate calculus suffices and test functions here are ordinary smooth functions vanishing near the endpoints.
