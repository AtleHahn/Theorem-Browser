Statement

Let p be prime and n≥m≥0 integers. For a positive integer q let ν_p(q) be the exponent of p in q, and let S_p(r) be the sum of the base-p digits of a nonnegative integer r, with S_p(0)=0. Then \nu_p\binom nm=(S_p(m)+S_p(n-m)-S_p(n))/(p-1).

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement uses integer divisibility and positional notation.

Proof 1: Subtract factorial valuations using Legendre’s formula (outline)

Use Legendre’s factorial formula \nu_p(r!)=(r-S_p(r))/(p-1), including r=0. Writing \binom nm=n!/(m!(n-m)!) and subtracting factorial valuations gives \nu_p\binom nm=[n-S_p(n)-m+S_p(m)-(n-m)+S_p(n-m)]/(p-1). Cancellation of the integer terms yields the formula.

Level justification

Subtract the three supplied factorial valuations and cancel integer terms. Prime exponents, factorials and positional digits are elementary arithmetic.
