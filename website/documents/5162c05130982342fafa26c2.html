Statement

Suppose a straightedge construction scheme with a fixed circle and its center can construct the intersections of any line and any specified circle, and of any two specified circles, where a circle is specified by its center and a point on it. Then it can simulate every finite compass-and-straightedge construction of points.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Points, lines, circles, parallels and perpendiculars are elementary geometry.

Proof 1: Replace each basic operation

To prove the Poncelet–Steiner theorem, it suffices to show that each of the basic constructions of compass and straightedge is possible using a straightedge alone (provided that a circle and its center exist in the plane), as these are the foundations of all other constructions. All constructions can be written as a series of steps involving these five basic constructions:

  1. Creating the line through two existing points.
  2. Creating the circle through one point with centre another point.
  3. Creating the point which is the intersection of two existing, non-parallel lines.
  4. Creating the one or two points in the intersection of a line and a circle (if they intersect).
  5. Creating the one or two points in the intersection of two circles (if they intersect).

Constructions (1) and (3) can be done with a straightedge alone. For construction (2), a circle is considered to be given by any two points, one defining the center and one existing on the circumference at radius. It is understood that the arc of a circle cannot be drawn without a compass, so the remaining requirements are that constructions (4) and (5) be possible using only a straightedge, along with a fixed given circle and its center. Under the assumptions of the statement, these two constructions are available. Therefore, it follows that every compass-straightedge construction can be done under the restrictions of the theorem.

High school only
Level justification

Replace each of the five listed construction operations by the allowed straightedge operation or the assumed intersection procedure. School construction geometry and finite step substitution suffice.
