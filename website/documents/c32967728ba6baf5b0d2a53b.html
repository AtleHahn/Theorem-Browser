Statement

A ray is a one-way infinite simple path. Two rays are equivalent when some ray meets each in infinitely many vertices; an end is an equivalence class. A thick end contains infinitely many pairwise vertex-disjoint rays. If an infinite graph G contains a subdivision of the plane hexagonal-tiling graph, then G has a thick end. A subdivision replaces each edge by a finite path with new internal vertices disjoint from the other replacement paths.

Level justification

Hermite polynomials have a single defining recurrence, Jordan products and graph-end conditions are explicitly specified, and well-quasi-order is defined by a sequence comparison. These concrete additions do not require their advanced proof theories.

Proof 1: A hexagonal subdivision forces a thick end — outline

Use the source-stated thick end of the hexagonal-tiling graph as a prerequisite. Its infinitely many disjoint equivalent rays give such rays in the subdivision: replace each edge of a ray by its subdividing path. The rays witnessing their equivalence are subdivided in the same way. All these rays are also rays in G. Thus one end of G contains the indicated infinitely many disjoint rays, so it is thick.
