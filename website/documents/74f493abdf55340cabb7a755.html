Statement

Let X be a smooth connected projective curve over an algebraically closed field, D a divisor and K a canonical divisor. For a line bundle L, write h^j(L)=\dim_k H^j(X,L), where k is the base field. Put \ell(D)=\dim H^0(X,\mathcal O(D)) and \chi(\mathcal O(D))=h^0(\mathcal O(D))-h^1(\mathcal O(D)). Then \ell(D)-\ell(K-D)=\chi(\mathcal O(D)).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Smooth projective algebraic curves; Divisors and associated line bundles; Sheaf cohomology and Euler characteristic. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Proof 1: Identify the correction term by Serre duality

The integer {\displaystyle \ell (D)} is the dimension of the space of global sections of the line bundle {\displaystyle {\mathcal {L}}(D)} associated to D (here \mathcal L(D)=\mathcal O(D), \mathcal K_X=K, and \omega_X=\mathcal O(K)). In terms of sheaf cohomology, we therefore have {\displaystyle \ell (D)=\mathrm {dim} H^{0}(X,{\mathcal {L}}(D))}, and likewise {\displaystyle \ell ({\mathcal {K}}_{X}-D)=\dim H^{0}(X,\omega _{X}\otimes {\mathcal {L}}(D)^{\vee })}. But Serre duality for non-singular projective varieties in the particular case of a curve states that {\displaystyle H^{0}(X,\omega _{X}\otimes {\mathcal {L}}(D)^{\vee })} is isomorphic to the dual {\displaystyle H^{1}(X,{\mathcal {L}}(D))^{\vee }}. The difference \ell(D)-\ell(K-D) thus equals the Euler characteristic of the divisor D.
