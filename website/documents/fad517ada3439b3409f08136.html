Statement

Let f:I\to J and g=f^{-1}:J\to I be C^2 functions on open real intervals. For y=f(x), g^{\prime\prime}(y)=-\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(x)}{(f^{\prime}(x))^3}.

Proof 1: Second derivative of an inverse function

Put y=f(x), so x=g(y). Differentiating the identity g(f(x))=x twice with respect to x, one obtains

{\displaystyle {\frac {d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}}\,{\frac {dx}{dy}}+{\frac {d}{dx}}\left({\frac {dx}{dy}}\right)\,\left({\frac {dy}{dx}}\right)=0,}

that is simplified further by the chain rule as

{\displaystyle {\frac {d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}}\,{\frac {dx}{dy}}+{\frac {d^{2}x}{dy^{2}}}\,\left({\frac {dy}{dx}}\right)^{2}=0.}

Replacing the first derivative, using (dx/dy)(dy/dx)=1, we get

{\displaystyle {\frac {d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}}=-{\frac {d^{2}x}{dy^{2}}}\,\left({\frac {dy}{dx}}\right)^{3}}

which implies

{\displaystyle {\frac {d^{2}x}{dy^{2}}}=-{\frac {d^{2}y/dx^{2}}{\left(dy/dx\right)^{3}}}.}

Since d^2x/dy^2=g''(y) and d^2y/dx^2=f''(x), this is the stated formula.
