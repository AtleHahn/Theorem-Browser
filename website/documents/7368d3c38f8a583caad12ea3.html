Statement

Suppose real ground-state energies E_N satisfy E_N\ge-CN^{5/3} for all positive integers N, with C<\infty. Suppose the exponent 5/3 is optimal in the sense that no bound E_N\ge-C_\alpha N^\alpha holds uniformly in N with finite C_\alpha for any \alpha<5/3. Then each fixed N has a finite energy lower bound (stability of the first kind), but there is no uniform linear bound E_N\ge-C_1N (stability of the second kind fails).

Level
High school only
Level justification

The energies are a stipulated family of real numbers; both stability properties and exponent optimality are explicitly bounds.

Proof 1: Use fixed-size finiteness and optimality of the exponent

The bound -CN^{5/3} is finite for every fixed N. A uniform linear bound would have exponent 1<5/3, contrary to the assumed optimality. These are respectively stability of the first kind and failure of stability of the second kind.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The given lower bound is finite at fixed N, and exponent one contradicts the explicitly defined optimality condition. Elementary real powers and logical implication suffice; no operator or physical energy theory is used.
