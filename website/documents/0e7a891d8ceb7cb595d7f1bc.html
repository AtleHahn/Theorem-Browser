Statement

For real t, \int\sin t\,dt=-\cos t+C.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves real antiderivatives; sine and cosine. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Trigonometric substitution

Use u=\cos t. Under this substitution, the integral becomes -\int du, with result -u+C=-\cos t+C.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Substitute u=cos t and integrate the resulting constant differential. Undergraduate single-variable calculus suffices.
