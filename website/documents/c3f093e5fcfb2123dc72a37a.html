Statement

Let A be a countable admissible set and L\in A a relational language, with its relation symbols and finite arities coded as sets. Let L_A be the admissible fragment of L_{\omega_1,\omega} consisting of formula codes belonging to A. If \Gamma is a set of L_A-sentences definable over (A,\in) by a \Sigma_1 formula with parameters from A, and every subset \Gamma_0\subseteq\Gamma with \Gamma_0\in A has a model, then \Gamma has a model. Here L_{\omega_1,\omega} permits countable conjunctions and disjunctions and finite strings of quantifiers, with the usual finite-free-variable restriction. Sentences have no free variables; a model satisfies every sentence of the given set. Membership in A, not ordinary finiteness, is the meaning of A-finite, both for the coded language and for subsets of \Gamma. Admissible means transitive (x\in y\in A implies x\in A) and satisfying Kripke–Platek set theory: extensionality, empty set, pairing, union, set induction, and separation and collection for formulas with bounded quantifiers. A bounded quantifier is \forall x\in y or \exists x\in y. Separation forms a subset selected by such a formula; collection gathers witnesses for all elements of a set into one set. A \Sigma_1 formula is an existentially quantified bounded formula. The axiom of infinity is not required in this convention, so the set of all hereditarily finite sets is included as an admissible example. Formula coding uses the standard set-theoretic syntax of symbols, finite quantifiers and sets of conjuncts/disjuncts.
