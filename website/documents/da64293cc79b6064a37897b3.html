Statement

A finite simple graph has finitely many vertices, at most one edge per vertex pair, and no loops. A proper coloring assigns distinct colors to adjacent vertices; a plane graph is drawn without crossings. Every nonempty finite simple planar graph has a vertex incident to at most five edges.

Level
High school only
Level justification

A graph is a finite set of vertices joined by edges; planarity means it can be drawn without crossings, and proper coloring assigns different colors across each edge. These finite combinatorial and planar notions are explicit.

Proof 1: Positive total degree deficit — outline

Graphs with at most two vertices already satisfy the bound. For a graph with at least three vertices, use the planar edge-augmentation result: add edges without introducing new vertices to make every region triangular, including the unbounded outer region.

Suppose v,e,f are the number of vertices, edges, and regions (faces). Since each region is triangular and each edge is shared by two regions, we have 2e=3f. This together with Euler's formula, v-e+f=2, gives 6v-2e=12. Now, the degree of a vertex is the number of edges abutting it. If v_i is the number of vertices of degree i and D is the maximum degree of any vertex,

6v-2e =6\sum_{i=1}^D v_i-\sum_{i=1}^D i v_i =\sum_{i=1}^D(6-i)v_i =12.

This is \sum_{x\in V(G)}(6-d(x))=12, grouped by degree.

But since 12>0 and 6-i\leq0 for all i\geq6, this demonstrates that there is at least one vertex of degree 5 or less. Removing the added edges can only decrease degrees, so the original graph also has such a vertex.
