Statement

Let f be holomorphic on a neighborhood of the closed unit disk. For |z|<1, f(z)=\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_{|\zeta|=1}\frac{\zeta+z}{\zeta-z}\operatorname{Re}f(\zeta)\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta}+i\operatorname{Im}f(0), with the circle positively oriented.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex differentiability extends the calculus derivative; limits, compact sets, uniform approximation and zeros of functions are familiar analysis. Zero multiplicity is the order of the first nonzero Taylor coefficient, and circle integrals are parametrized ordinary integrals. These limited definitions suffice for the full claim.

Proof 1: Poisson formula reduction — outline

Put u=\operatorname{Re}f. Apply the Poisson formula u(z)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}u(e^{i\psi})\operatorname{Re}\frac{e^{i\psi}+z}{e^{i\psi}-z}\,d\psi. This gives the real part of the contour expression in the statement after parametrizing \zeta=e^{i\psi}. The holomorphic function is determined by its real part up to an imaginary constant, fixed by its value at zero.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the explicit disk Poisson formula to the real part and determine the imaginary constant. Undergraduate complex analysis and contour parametrization suffice.
