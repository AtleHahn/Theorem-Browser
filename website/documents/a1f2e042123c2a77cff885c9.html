Statement

Let f:M\to\mathbb K be a bounded linear functional on a subspace of a real or complex normed space X. There is a seminorm P on X such that P(m)=|f(m)| on M and P(x)\le\|f\|\|x\| for all x\in X.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra supplies scalar-valued linear maps, and analysis supplies norms and bounds. The operator norm is the supremum on the unit ball; a seminorm satisfies the norm inequalities but may vanish on nonzero vectors. These closely related norm definitions are a small extension sufficient for the extension claim.

Proof 1: Extending a bounded functional absolute value as a seminorm

Suppose that f is a bounded linear functional defined on a vector subspace M of a normed space X, so its operator norm \|f\| is a non-negative real number. Then the linear functional's absolute value p:=|f| is a seminorm on M and the map q:X\to\mathbb R defined by q(x)=\|f\|\,\|x\| is a seminorm on X that satisfies p\leq q|_M on M. The Hahn–Banach theorem for seminorms guarantees the existence of a seminorm P:X\to\mathbb R that is equal to |f| on M (since P|_M=p=|f|) and is bounded above by P(x)\leq\|f\|\,\|x\| everywhere on X (since P\leq q).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof checks that the absolute value of a linear functional and a constant multiple of a norm are seminorms, then applies the explicitly stated dominated seminorm extension theorem. Normed spaces, bounded functionals and operator norm are closely related extensions of undergraduate vector norms; no dual-space topology or proof of Hahn–Banach is required.
