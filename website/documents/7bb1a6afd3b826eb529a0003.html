No computable function bounds all total computable functions in the stated sense. Effective procedures admit an open-ended hierarchy of growth and difficulty, with no universal effective ceiling.

Statement

Fix a Gödel numbering (\varphi_i)_{i\in\mathbb N} of the partial computable functions \mathbb N\to\mathbb N and a Blum complexity measure (\Phi_i). For every total computable function b:\mathbb N\to\mathbb N, there exists a total computable function g:\mathbb N\to\mathbb N such that no program computing g has complexity at most b(x) on all but finitely many inputs x. Precisely, if

C(b)=\{g:\text{some }i\text{ satisfies }\varphi_i=g\text{ total and }\Phi_i(x)\le b(x)\text{ for all but finitely many }x\},

then C(b) is a proper subset of the total computable functions. Here a Gödel numbering is an effective enumeration of programs for the partial computable functions; total means the program halts on every input. A Blum measure assigns a partial natural-number-valued cost \Phi_i(x) to each program and input, with \operatorname{dom}\Phi_i=\operatorname{dom}\varphi_i and with the predicate \Phi_i(x)\le c decidable uniformly in (i,x,c). Undefined costs do not satisfy this predicate. Different programs computing the same function may have different costs.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Uniform decidability of a cost predicate, partial-program indexing and quantification over every implementation of a function form a cumulative computability burden beyond a confident short undergraduate extension.
