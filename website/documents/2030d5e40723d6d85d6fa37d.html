Statement

For every abelian group A and integer i>0, there is a pointed connected CW complex K(A,i) with \pi_i(K(A,i))\cong A and \pi_j(K(A,i))=0 for all positive integers j\ne i, representing degree-i singular cohomology: [X,K(A,i)]_*\cong H^i(X;A) naturally for every pointed connected CW complex X.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves CW complexes; homotopy groups; Eilenberg–MacLane spaces; singular cohomology with abelian coefficients. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Proof 1: Represent singular cohomology by Brown’s theorem (outline)

Write \mathrm{Hotc} for the homotopy category of pointed connected CW complexes, with pointed homotopy classes of maps as morphisms.

A contravariant functor F:\mathrm{Hotc}^{op}\to\mathrm{Set} satisfies the wedge axiom when F(\bigvee_\alpha X_\alpha)\cong\prod_\alpha F(X_\alpha) under the canonical restriction map. It satisfies the weak-pullback axiom when it sends a homotopy pushout square to a square for which the map to the set-theoretic pullback is surjective.

Use Brown representability: a contravariant set-valued functor on \mathrm{Hotc} satisfying the wedge and weak-pullback axioms is naturally represented by a pointed connected CW complex.

Take F(X)=H^i(X;A), singular cohomology with coefficients in the abelian group A, for fixed i>0. Its representing space is the Eilenberg–MacLane space K(A,i).
