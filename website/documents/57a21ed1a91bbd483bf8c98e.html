Statement

For an odd prime p, write (a/p)=0 if p\mid a, 1 if a is a nonzero square modulo p, and -1 otherwise. Let a,b be primes with a\equiv1\pmod4 and b\equiv3\pmod4. If (b/a)=1, then (a/b)=1.

High school only

Proof 1: Apply conic solvability and reduce modulo four — outline

Assume (b/a)=1 but (a/b)=-1. Use Legendre’s conic theorem: for pairwise coprime integer coefficients A,B,C not all of the same sign, solvability of u^2\equiv-BC\pmod A, v^2\equiv-CA\pmod B, and w^2\equiv-AB\pmod C implies a nonzero integer solution of Ax^2+By^2+Cz^2=0. Applied with coefficients 1,a,-b, the assumed characters and the first supplement give a nonzero solution of x^2+ay^2-bz^2=0. Reducing modulo4 yields a contradiction after choosing a primitive integer solution: the sum of the three square residues is0, forcing all three integers even.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Check conic solvability from the assumed symbols and the sign supplement, then reduce a primitive solution modulo four. Undergraduate number theory suffices; no algebraic-geometric theory of conics is used.
