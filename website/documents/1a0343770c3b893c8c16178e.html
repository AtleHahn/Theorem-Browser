Statement

Let G be a finite group with a Sylow 2-subgroup P isomorphic to the quasidihedral group

\langle a,b\mid a^{2^{n-1}}=1,\ b^2=1,\ b^{-1}ab=a^{2^{n-2}-1}\rangle,\qquad n\geq4.

Put F=P\cap[G,G]. Exactly one of the following types occurs:

  1. G has a normal subgroup of odd order and index a power of 2 (the 2-nilpotent type).
  2. F is generalized quaternion of index 2 in P (the Q type).
  3. F is dihedral of index 2 in P (the D type).
  4. F=P (the QD type).

The numbers of G-conjugacy classes represented by involutions in P and by cyclic subgroups of order 4 in P, respectively, are (2,2) in type 1, (2,1) in type 2, and (1,1) in type 4.

An involution is an element of order 2; two subgroups are G-conjugate if one is g^{-1}Hg for some g\in G. The commutator subgroup [G,G] is generated by x^{-1}y^{-1}xy. A Sylow 2-subgroup has order the largest power of 2 dividing |G|. A generalized quaternion group of order 2^r (r\geq3) has presentation

\langle u,v\mid u^{2^{r-1}}=1,\ v^2=u^{2^{r-2}},\ v^{-1}uv=u^{-1}\rangle.

The dihedral group of order 2^r has presentation \langle u,v\mid u^{2^{r-1}}=v^2=1,\ v^{-1}uv=u^{-1}\rangle.
