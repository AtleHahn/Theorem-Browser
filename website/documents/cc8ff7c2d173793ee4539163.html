Statement

For an odd prime p, write (a/p)=0 if p\mid a, 1 if a is a nonzero square modulo p, and -1 otherwise. For every odd prime p, (-2/p)=(-1)^{(p^2+4p-5)/8}, which is 1 for p\equiv1,3\pmod8 and -1 for p\equiv5,7\pmod8.

Proof 1: Multiply the two supplement signs

The two supplements give

\left(\frac{-1}{p}\right)=(-1)^{(p-1)/2}, \qquad \left(\frac{2}{p}\right)=(-1)^{(p^2-1)/8}.

For -2 to be a quadratic residue, -1 and 2 must either both be quadratic residues or both be non-residues modulo p, by multiplicativity of quadratic character.

So (p-1)/2 and (p^2-1)/8 are either both even or both odd. The sum of these two expressions is

\frac{p^2+4p-5}{8},

which is an integer. Therefore,

\left(\frac{-2}{p}\right)=(-1)^{(p^2+4p-5)/8} =\begin{cases} 1 & p\equiv1,3\pmod8,\\ -1 & p\equiv5,7\pmod8. \end{cases}

High school only
Level justification

Multiply the two supplied signs, add their integer exponents and check four odd residue classes modulo eight. School modular arithmetic suffices; the finite-field theory behind the supplements is not invoked.
