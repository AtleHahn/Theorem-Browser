Statement

Let n\ge2 and 1\le k<n be integers. For a real n-times differentiable function f on \mathbb R with f and f^{(n)} bounded,

\|f^{(k)}\|_\infty\le a_{n-k}\,a_n^{-1+k/n}\,\|f\|_\infty^{1-k/n}\|f^{(n)}\|_\infty^{k/n},

where \|g\|_\infty=\sup_{x\in\mathbb R}|g(x)| and

a_j=\frac4\pi\sum_{r=0}^\infty\frac{(-1)^{r(j+1)}}{(2r+1)^{j+1}}\qquad(j\ge1).

The displayed constant is the smallest possible one valid for all such f.

Proof 1: Euler-spline comparison and smooth sharpness (external)

The following editorial argument completes the stated scope. A supporting source is A. N. Kolmogorov and approximation theory (Section 1.3, Theorem 3 and following proof, printed pp.108–109; last-derivative and smooth-approximation bridges supplied here.).

Tikhomirov Section 1.3, Theorem 3, states the exact Euler-spline/Favard constant and supplies Cavaretta’s comparison proof for k<n-1. His K_j equals the stated a_j after shifting the summation index. For the last derivative use the sharp elementary whole-line Landau inequality ||h prime||^2<=2||h||||h double-prime||: centered Taylor estimates give |h prime(x)|<=||h||/t+||h double-prime||t/2, then optimize t. Apply this to h=f^(n-2) and use the proved k=n-2 inequality. Because 2 a_2=a_1^2 (a_1=pi/2,a_2=pi^2/8), the resulting constant is exactly a_1 a_n^(-1/n), not a weaker one. For n=2 use the same Landau argument directly. Regularity bridge: bounded everywhere f^(n) makes f^(n-1) Lipschitz by the mean value theorem, hence locally absolutely continuous. Taylor estimates at x,x+1,...,x+n-1 and inversion of a fixed Vandermonde matrix bound all intermediate derivatives uniformly using the bounded f and f^(n). Thus the stated class lies in the source Sobolev class and essential-supremum estimates yield the desired ordinary supremum for the continuous intermediate derivatives. Sharpness in the EVERYWHERE differentiable class: convolve the periodic Euler spline (nth derivative sign(sin t), lower derivative norms a_(n-k)) with nonnegative smooth periodic approximate identities. The lower derivatives converge uniformly, the nth derivative norm is at most one and equals one for sufficiently small compactly supported mollifiers on an interior plateau. Ratios tend to a_(n-k) a_n^(-1+k/n), so no smaller constant works.
