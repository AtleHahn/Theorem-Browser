Statement

Let a finite simple graph have n\ge1 vertices with degrees d_1\ge\cdots\ge d_n\ge0. Here a simple graph has at most one edge joining any two distinct vertices and no edge from a vertex to itself; a degree counts incident edges. Then \sum_i d_i is even, and for every integer 1\le k\le n, \sum_{i=1}^k d_i\le k(k-1)+\sum_{i=k+1}^n\min(d_i,k).

Level
High school only
Level justification

The local graph definition reduces the statement to finite vertices, pairs, and integer counts.

Proof 1: Double-count the incidences at the highest-degree vertices

It is not difficult to show that the conditions of the Erdős–Gallai theorem are necessary for a sequence of numbers to be graphic. The requirement that the sum of the degrees be even is the handshaking lemma. The inequality between the sum of the {\displaystyle k} largest degrees and the sum of the remaining degrees can be established by double counting: the left side gives the numbers of edge-vertex adjacencies among the {\displaystyle k} highest-degree vertices, each such adjacency must either be on an edge with one or two high-degree endpoints, the {\displaystyle k(k-1)} term on the right gives the maximum possible number of edge-vertex adjacencies in which both endpoints have high degree, and the remaining term on the right upper bounds the number of edges that have exactly one high degree endpoint.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Count incident edge ends inside and outside the k selected vertices, and count all edge ends twice for parity. Elementary graph counting suffices.
