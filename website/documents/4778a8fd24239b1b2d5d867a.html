Statement

For real a>b>0, z^2+2az+b^2 has exactly one zero in |z|<b, namely -a+\sqrt{a^2-b^2}; its other zero lies outside the disk.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The claim counts complex roots of a finite polynomial inside an explicitly specified disk using coefficient inequalities or an explicit quadratic root. Its meaning needs elementary polynomial algebra and complex absolute values. This rating concerns understanding the statement, independently of the difficulty of its proof.

Proof 1: Compare with the linear term on the radius-b circle

Use Rouché’s theorem: if F,G are holomorphic on a neighborhood of a closed Jordan domain and |G|<|F| on its boundary, then F+G and F have equal interior zero counts, counted with multiplicity.

Consider the polynomial {\displaystyle z^{2}+2az+b^{2}} with {\displaystyle a>b>0}. By the quadratic formula it has two zeros at {\displaystyle -a\pm {\sqrt {a^{2}-b^{2}}}}. Rouché's theorem can be used to obtain some hint about their positions. Since

{\displaystyle |z^{2}+b^{2}|\leq 2b^{2}<2a|z|{\text{ for all }}|z|=b,}

comparing with 2az, which has one zero in the disk, Rouché's theorem says that the polynomial has exactly one zero inside the disk {\displaystyle |z|<b}. Since {\displaystyle -a-{\sqrt {a^{2}-b^{2}}}} is clearly outside the disk, we conclude that the zero is {\displaystyle -a+{\sqrt {a^{2}-b^{2}}}}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof supplies Rouché’s theorem and checks it on polynomials using a circle and an explicit modulus inequality. The undergraduate baseline plus a brief explanation of holomorphicity and root multiplicity suffices. There is no need to learn contour integration or prove Rouché’s theorem; the remaining computation is elementary algebra. The application is therefore undergraduate despite the theorem’s complex-analysis origin.

Proof 1 — Compare with the linear term on the radius-b circle

Statement used:

If F and G are holomorphic near a closed disk and |G(z)|<|F(z)| on its boundary circle, F+G and F have the same number of zeros inside the disk, counted with multiplicity.

Application in proof:

Use F(z)=2az and G(z)=z^2+b^2 on |z|=b. Since a>b>0, |G|<=2b^2<2ab=|F|. Exactly one zero is inside; the explicit quadratic roots identify which one.
