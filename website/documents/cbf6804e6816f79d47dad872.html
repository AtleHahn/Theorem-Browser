Likely alternative prerequisite level: Undergraduate level; lowest recorded proof level: Beyond undergraduate level. Choose a small circle with f nonzero and enclosing only the zero of order m. Uniform convergence eventually gives |f_k-f|<|f| on that circle, so the explicitly stated Rouche comparison gives exactly m zeros. Applying this to each smaller circle shows all these zeros approach z_0. Only compactness, uniform convergence, local zero multiplicity and this concrete zero-count comparison are added; no winding integrals or derivative convergence are required. This is a likely undergraduate application under the same criterion used for the database's FTA Rouche proof.

Statement

Let G\subset\mathbb C be connected and open, and let holomorphic functions f_k converge uniformly on compact subsets of G to a holomorphic function f not identically zero. If f has a zero of order m at z_0, then for each sufficiently small \rho>0, all sufficiently large k have exactly m zeros, counted with multiplicity, in |z-z_0|<\rho. These zeros converge to z_0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Real analysis and calculus supply limits, derivatives, series and integration. Complex differentiability, primitives and logarithms are direct extensions, and circle integrals are parametrized ordinary integrals. The full claims use no Riemann surfaces, residues or abstract differential forms.

Proof 1: Contour zero count and integer stabilization

Let f be an analytic function on an open subset of the complex plane with a zero of order m at z0, and suppose that {fn} is a sequence of functions converging uniformly on compact subsets to f. Fix some ρ > 0 such that the closed disc centered at z_0 of radius \rho lies in G and f(z) ≠ 0 in 0 < |z − z0| ≤ ρ. Choose δ > 0 such that |f(z)| > δ for z on the circle |z − z0| = ρ. Since fk(z) converges uniformly on the disc we have chosen, we can find N such that |fk(z)| ≥ δ/2 for every k ≥ N and every z on the circle, ensuring that the quotient fk′(z)/fk(z) is well defined for all z on the circle |z − z0| = ρ. By Weierstrass's theorem we have {\displaystyle f_{k}'\to f'} uniformly on the disc, and hence we have another uniform convergence on the boundary circle:

{\displaystyle {\frac {f_{k}'(z)}{f_{k}(z)}}\to {\frac {f'(z)}{f(z)}}.}

Denoting the number of zeros of fk(z) in the disk, counted with multiplicity, by Nk, we may apply the argument principle to find

{\displaystyle m={\frac {1}{2\pi i}}\int _{\vert z-z_{0}\vert =\rho }{\frac {f'(z)}{f(z)}}\,dz=\lim _{k\to \infty }{\frac {1}{2\pi i}}\int _{\vert z-z_{0}\vert =\rho }{\frac {f'_{k}(z)}{f_{k}(z)}}\,dz=\lim _{k\to \infty }N_{k}}

In the above step, we were able to interchange the integral and the limit because of the uniform convergence of the integrand. We have shown that Nk → m as k → ∞. Since the Nk are integer valued, Nk must equal m for large enough k.
