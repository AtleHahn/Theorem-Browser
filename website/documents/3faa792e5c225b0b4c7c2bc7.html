Statement

Let (X_n)_{n\ge1} be real random variables, possibly on different probability spaces, and put \varphi_n(t)=\mathbb E[e^{itX_n}] for t\in\mathbb R. Suppose \varphi_n(t)\to\varphi(t) for every real t. The following are equivalent:

  1. There exists a real random variable X such that \mathbb P(X_n\le x)\to\mathbb P(X\le x) at every continuity point of the distribution function of X.
  2. The laws are tight: \lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_n\mathbb P(|X_n|>R)=0.
  3. \varphi is the characteristic function t\mapsto\mathbb E[e^{itX}] of a real random variable.
  4. \varphi is continuous on \mathbb R.
  5. \varphi is continuous at zero.

When these hold, the limiting law in (1) has characteristic function \varphi. Here i^2=-1 and expectations are integrals with respect to the relevant probability law.
