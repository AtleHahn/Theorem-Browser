Statement

For every nonnegative square matrix A=(a_{ij}), \min_i\sum_j a_{ij}\leq\rho(A)\leq\max_i\sum_j a_{ij}.

Proof 1: Source matrix argument

Any matrix induced norm satisfies the inequality \|A\|\geq|\lambda| for any eigenvalue \lambda, because, if x is a corresponding eigenvector,

\|A\|\geq\frac{|Ax|}{|x|} =\frac{|\lambda x|}{|x|}=|\lambda|.

The infinity norm of a matrix is the maximum of its absolute row sums:

\|A\|_\infty=\max_i\sum_j|a_{ij}|.

Hence the desired upper bound is exactly \|A\|_\infty\geq|\lambda| applied to the nonnegative matrix A.

For the lower bound, given that A is positive (not just nonnegative), there exists a positive eigenvector w such that Aw=rw and the smallest component of w (say w_i) is 1. Then r=(Aw)_i is at least the sum of the numbers in row i of A. Thus the minimum row sum gives a lower bound for r, and this observation extends to all nonnegative matrices by continuity of the spectral radius.
