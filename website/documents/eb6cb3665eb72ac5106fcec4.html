Statement

Let U\subset\mathbb R^n be open, \varepsilon>0, and F\in C^{\infty}(( -\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\times U). There exists G\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb R\times U) with \partial_t^kG(0,x)=\partial_t^kF(0,x) for every integer k\ge0 and every x\in U.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Euclidean smooth functions; higher partial derivatives; interval domains. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Multiply by a compactly supported cutoff

Choose a smooth function \psi(t) with compact support in (-\varepsilon,\varepsilon), identically one near zero. On that interval multiply F(t,x) by \psi(t), extending the product by zero to \mathbb R\times U. This gives a smooth solution with the same derivatives at t=0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Multiply by a smooth one-variable cutoff equal to one near zero and extend by zero outside its compact support. Undergraduate multivariable calculus suffices; the preserved jet means only the explicitly listed derivatives at t=0.
