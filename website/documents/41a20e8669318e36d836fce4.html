Let \mathbb N_0=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}, A_0=\mathbb N_0, and A_i=\{i-1\} for i≥1. Every subfamily has union cardinality at least its number of sets, but the whole indexed family has no injective representative assignment.

Proof 1: An infinite family satisfying cardinality Hall conditions without representatives — outline

The singleton sets force f(i)=i−1 for every i≥1 and thereby use every natural number, leaving no distinct value for f(0). The cardinality condition holds: a subfamily excluding A_0 has one distinct singleton element per index; a subfamily containing A_0 has countably infinite union, while it has at most countably many indices.
