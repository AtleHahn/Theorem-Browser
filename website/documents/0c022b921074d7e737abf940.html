Statement

Let n\ge1 be an integer, let \gamma_n be the standard Gaussian measure on \mathbb R^n, and put T_h(x)=x+h for h\in\mathbb R^n. Thus

\gamma_n(A)=(2\pi)^{-n/2}\int_A e^{-\lVert x\rVert^2/2}\,dx.

Then the pushforward measure (T_h)_*\gamma_n, assigning \gamma_n(A-h) to a Borel set A, has Radon–Nikodym derivative

\frac{d(T_h)_*\gamma_n}{d\gamma_n}(x)=e^{\langle h,x\rangle-\lVert h\rVert^2/2},

where \langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle and \lVert\cdot\rVert are the Euclidean inner product and norm.

Proof 1: Translation density of a finite-dimensional Gaussian measure

The Gaussian measure of the translation A-h of a Borel set A is

\begin{aligned} \gamma_n(A-h) &=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\int_A \exp\left(-\tfrac12\langle x-h,x-h\rangle\right)\,dx\\ &=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\int_A \exp\left(\frac{2\langle x,h\rangle-\langle h,h\rangle}{2}\right) \exp\left(-\tfrac12\langle x,x\rangle\right)\,dx. \end{aligned}

So under translation through h, the Gaussian measure scales by the density appearing in the last display:

\exp\left(\frac{2\langle x,h\rangle-\langle h,h\rangle}{2}\right) =\exp\left(\langle x,h\rangle-\tfrac12\|h\|^2\right).

The measure that associates to the set A the number \gamma_n(A-h) is the pushforward measure, denoted (T_h)_*\gamma_n. Here T_h:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^n refers to the translation map: T_h(x)=x+h. The above calculation shows that the Radon–Nikodym derivative of the pushforward measure with respect to the original Gaussian measure is given by

\frac{d(T_h)_*\gamma_n}{d\gamma_n}(x) =\exp\left(\langle h,x\rangle-\tfrac12\|h\|^2\right).
