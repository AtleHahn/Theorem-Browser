Statement

For nonzero complex numbers a_n satisfying \sum_n|a_n|^{-p-1}<\infty, p\ge0 an integer, let P(z)=\prod_nE_p(z/a_n) with E_p(w)=(1-w)\exp(\sum_{j=1}^p w^j/j), with the sum empty for p=0. Away from the zeros, \left(\frac d{dz}\right)^p\frac{P^{\prime}(z)}{P(z)}=-p!\sum_n(a_n-z)^{-p-1}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Real analysis supplies infinite products, series and derivatives, and complex arithmetic supplies complex exponentials. Holomorphic or entire functions and zero multiplicity are limited extensions; the canonical factors are defined explicitly, so understanding the product identity requires no general factorization theory.

Proof 1: Higher logarithmic derivative of a canonical product (outline)

For a single elementary factor,

{\displaystyle {\frac {d}{dz}}\log E_{p}(z/a)=-{\frac {1}{a-z}}+\sum _{j=1}^{p}{\frac {z^{j-1}}{a^{j}}}.}

After differentiating {\displaystyle p} times, the polynomial part vanishes, and hence

{\displaystyle {\frac {d^{p}}{dz^{p}}}\left({\frac {P'}{P}}\right)=-p!\sum _{n=1}^{\infty }{\frac {1}{(a_{n}-z)^{p+1}}}.}
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The finite-factor derivative is elementary, but the infinite product identity requires locally uniform convergence of canonical products and justification of repeated complex differentiation away from their zeros. This is substantial complex analysis.
