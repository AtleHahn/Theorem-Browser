Statement

Let K be a field and g,f_1,\ldots,f_s\in K[X_1,\ldots,X_n]. Given a finite degree bound D, existence of polynomials g_i of total degree at most D satisfying g=\sum_i f_i g_i reduces to solvability of a finite linear system over K. If a valid bound guarantees such degrees whenever g\in(f_1,\ldots,f_s), this decides ideal membership by linear algebra.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Polynomial ideals and degrees belong to introductory algebra, and solving coefficient equations is linear algebra. The supplied finite degree bound makes the unknown coefficients finite in number; understanding the reduction requires no Groebner-basis theory.

Proof 1: Use bounded coefficients as linear-system unknowns — outline

To compute the g_i in

g=f_1g_1+\cdots+f_sg_s

or to prove that they do not exist, it suffices to provide an upper bound on the total degree of the g_i: such a bound reduces the problem to a finite system of linear equations that may be solved by usual linear algebra techniques.

For the ideal membership problem, which consists in testing if a polynomial belongs to an ideal, a solution is likewise provided by a bound guaranteed to hold whenever the polynomial belongs to the ideal.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Write each unknown g_i as a linear combination of the finitely many monomials of degree at most D. Comparing coefficients in sum f_i g_i gives linear equations in those unknown coefficients. Introductory polynomial algebra and linear algebra suffice; a valid degree bound is assumed, not derived by commutative-algebra methods.
