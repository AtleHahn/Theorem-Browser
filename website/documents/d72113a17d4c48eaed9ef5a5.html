Statement

Let d\geq1, let \Omega\subset\mathbb R^d be bounded and open, and let f:\Omega\to\mathbb R be locally Lebesgue integrable. Suppose its distributional Laplacian is represented by a locally integrable real function g, meaning

\int_\Omega f\,\Delta\varphi\,dx=\int_\Omega g\varphi\,dx\qquad(\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)).

Put f^+(x)=\max(f(x),0). Then

\Delta f^+\geq\mathbf1_{\{f\geq0\}}g

in the sense of distributions: for every nonnegative \varphi\in C_c^\infty(\Omega),

\int_\Omega f^+\Delta\varphi\,dx\geq\int_{\{f\geq0\}}g\varphi\,dx.

Here C_c^\infty(\Omega) denotes smooth real functions with compact support in \Omega, \Delta\varphi=\sum_{j=1}^d\partial_j^2\varphi, and local integrability means integrability of the absolute value on every compact subset. The indicator includes the zero level set, as written.
