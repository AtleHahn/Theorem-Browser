Statement

For a homomorphism of commutative rings A\to B and an A-module M, the following are equivalent: (i) \operatorname{Tor}_1^A(X,M)=0 for every B-module X; (ii) B\otimes_A M is flat over B and \operatorname{Tor}_1^A(B,M)=0. If B=A/I for an ideal I, they are also equivalent to (iii) \operatorname{Tor}_1^A(X,M)=0 for every A-module X killed by some power of I.

Proof 1: Use Tor exact sequences and descend the ideal-power filtration

If (i) is valid and {\displaystyle N\hookrightarrow N'} is an injection of {\displaystyle B}-modules with cokernel C, then, as A-modules,

{\displaystyle \operatorname {Tor} _{1}^{A}(C,M)=0\to N\otimes _{A}M\to N'\otimes _{A}M}.

Since {\displaystyle N\otimes _{A}M\simeq N\otimes _{B}(B\otimes _{A}M)} and the same for {\displaystyle N'}, this proves the flatness in (ii); taking X=B gives the other condition. Conversely, assuming (ii) and considering {\displaystyle 0\to R\to F\to X\to 0} where F is B-free, we get:

{\displaystyle \operatorname {Tor} _{1}^{A}(F,M)=0\to \operatorname {Tor} _{1}^{A}(X,M)\to R\otimes _{A}M\to F\otimes _{A}M}.

Here, the last map is injective by flatness and that gives us (i). To see the "Moreover" part, if (i) is valid, then {\displaystyle \operatorname {Tor} _{1}^{A}(I^{n}X/I^{n+1}X,M)=0} and so

{\displaystyle \operatorname {Tor} _{1}^{A}(I^{n+1}X,M)\to \operatorname {Tor} _{1}^{A}(I^{n}X,M)\to 0.}

By descending induction, this implies (iii), starting with I^NX=0. The converse is trivial. {\displaystyle \square }
