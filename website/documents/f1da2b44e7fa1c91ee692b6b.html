Statement

Let X_1,X_2,\ldots be independent identically distributed real-valued random variables. For n\geq1 define the initial rank

R_n=\#\{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}:X_i\geq X_n\}.

For each integer k\geq1, form the ordered list of those X_n for which R_n=k, in increasing order of n. Regard this possibly finite list as an infinite sequence Y_k=(Y_{k,j})_{j\geq1} by placing a fixed extra symbol \partial\notin\mathbb R in every position after the list ends. Then the random sequences Y_1,Y_2,\ldots are independent and identically distributed.

Formally, Y_{k,j}=X_n when n is the jth index with R_n=k, and Y_{k,j}=\partial if fewer than j such indices exist. Equip \mathbb R\cup\{\partial\} with the Borel sets of \mathbb R together with the isolated extra point, and the sequence space with its product sigma-algebra. Independence means that for every finite set of distinct ranks k_1,\ldots,k_s and measurable sets B_1,\ldots,B_s of sequences,

\mathbb P(Y_{k_1}\in B_1,\ldots,Y_{k_s}\in B_s)=\prod_{a=1}^s\mathbb P(Y_{k_a}\in B_a).

Identically distributed means that \mathbb P(Y_k\in B) does not depend on k. The count defining R_n includes i=n and all earlier ties; no continuity assumption is imposed on the common distribution.
