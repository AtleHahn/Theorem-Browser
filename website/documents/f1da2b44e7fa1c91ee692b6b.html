Statement

Let X_1,X_2,\ldots be independent identically distributed real-valued random variables. For n\geq1 define the initial rank

R_n=\#\{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}:X_i\geq X_n\}.

For each integer k\geq1, form the ordered list of those X_n for which R_n=k, in increasing order of n. Regard this possibly finite list as an infinite sequence Y_k=(Y_{k,j})_{j\geq1} by placing a fixed extra symbol \partial\notin\mathbb R in every position after the list ends. Then the random sequences Y_1,Y_2,\ldots are independent and identically distributed.

Formally, Y_{k,j}=X_n when n is the jth index with R_n=k, and Y_{k,j}=\partial if fewer than j such indices exist. Equip \mathbb R\cup\{\partial\} with the Borel sets of \mathbb R together with the isolated extra point, and the sequence space with its product sigma-algebra. Independence means that for every finite set of distinct ranks k_1,\ldots,k_s and measurable sets B_1,\ldots,B_s of sequences,

\mathbb P(Y_{k_1}\in B_1,\ldots,Y_{k_s}\in B_s)=\prod_{a=1}^s\mathbb P(Y_{k_a}\in B_a).

Identically distributed means that \mathbb P(Y_k\in B) does not depend on k. The count defining R_n includes i=n and all earlier ties; no continuity assumption is imposed on the common distribution.

Proof 1: Record thinning and quantized limits (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (§3.6.5 Theorem 3.1 and proof, printed pp.157–158 (PDF pp.174–175); own general-law extension in adaptation.).

Ross proves the positive-integer case by successive thinning: the event that level i occurs in rank-k records is that the kth retained observation >=i equals i; the thinning indicators are independent across levels and positions and have probabilities independent of k. The following general-law extension is supplied here: choose monotone quantizers into a shifted nonnegative integer grid, truncating below -m with mesh 1/m, and decode their values back to real grid points. For each fixed finite sample prefix, all comparisons (including ties) eventually agree with the original comparisons. If the law has a top atom s, keep s in a distinct top bin. By the kth occurrence of s, all rank-k records have ended; finite-prefix agreement then proves convergence of every coordinate, including cemetery coordinates. If there is no top atom, every rank-k record list is infinite almost surely: start after any finite prefix at its maximum h, collect the next k observations exceeding h, and note that the last is a minimum among those k with conditional probability at least 1/k. In that event its full initial rank is k. Each such block ends almost surely; repeating with a new current maximum gives infinitely many successes (conditional probability of m consecutive failures <=(1-1/k)^m). Consequently every fixed record coordinate is determined at a finite time, and finite-prefix agreement proves convergence of all decoded record coordinates. The quantized rank lists are iid by Ross. Joint weak convergence on the Polish product sequence space preserves their finite-rank product laws and identical marginals. This proves the displayed independence for arbitrary measurable sequence events.
