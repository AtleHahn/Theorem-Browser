Statement

If finitely generated groups G and H are quasi-isometric, then G admits a nontrivial splitting over a finite subgroup if and only if H does. Such a splitting is either an amalgamated free product with the finite amalgam proper in both factors, or an HNN extension with finite associated subgroups.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Both the geometric equivalence and the algebraic splitting property belong to geometric group theory.

Proof 1: Transfer through the invariant number of ends

The number of ends is invariant under quasi-isometry, so e(G)=e(H). Stallings’ theorem says that a finitely generated group has more than one end exactly when it admits a nontrivial splitting over a finite subgroup. Applying this equivalence to both groups transfers the splitting property.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The two implications are immediate once the theorems are available, but their statements involve group ends, quasi-isometries, amalgamated products and HNN extensions. These constitute substantial geometric group theory.
