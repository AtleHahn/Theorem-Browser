Statement

Every propositional formula A provable without additional premises in Frege's classical propositional calculus or Łukasiewicz's classical propositional axiomatization has a proof of \vdash A in classical propositional sequent calculus. Here \vdash A is the sequent with no left assumptions and the single conclusion A.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The assertion compares two finite formal-proof formats for propositional formulas. The companion specifies the Hilbert systems’ relevant features and the sequent translation rules. Learning propositional truth assignments, axiom instances and finite derivations is a small extension of undergraduate symbolic reasoning; no first-order semantics or cut-elimination proof is needed.

Proof 1: Replace valid axioms and implement modus ponens by cut — outline

Use the standard facts that every axiom instance of either named Hilbert system is a propositional tautology and that its only inference rule is modus ponens, from B and B\to C to C. Also use propositional sequent completeness (every tautology has a sequent proof) and the cut rule, which implements modus ponens together with the implication rules.

Every proof in propositional calculus uses only axioms and the inference rules. Each use of an axiom scheme yields a true logical formula, and can thus be proven in sequent calculus. The only inference rule in the systems mentioned above is modus ponens, which is implemented by the cut rule.
