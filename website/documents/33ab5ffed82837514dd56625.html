Statement

Let k be an algebraically closed field. Let a connected algebraic group G over k act transitively on a variety X, and let f_i:V_i\to X be morphisms of varieties for i=1,2. Varieties here are integral separated schemes of finite type over k.

There exists a nonempty Zariski-open subset U\subseteq G such that for every g\in U(k) the scheme-theoretic fiber product

W_g=V_1\mathbin{\times}_{X}V_2, \qquad\text{formed using }g\circ f_1\text{ and }f_2,

is either empty or pure of dimension

\dim V_1+\dim V_2-\dim X.

Pure dimension means that all irreducible components have the displayed dimension. The map g\circ f_1 sends v to g\cdot f_1(v); the closed points of W_g are pairs (v,w) with g\cdot f_1(v)=f_2(w), with the scheme structure of the fiber product.

If \operatorname{char}k=0 and both V_1,V_2 are smooth over k, the open subset U can be chosen so that every W_g is also smooth over k.

Proof 1: Equivariant flatness and generic incidence fibers (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Lemma1 and Theorem2, pp.288–290).

Use q:G\times V_1\to X, q(g,v)=g\cdot f_1(v), and Z=(G\times V_1)\times_X V_2. The projection Z\to G has fiber W_g. Flatness of q is established before smoothness is invoked. The dimension calculation is \dim Z=\dim G+\dim V_1+\dim V_2-\dim X, and Lemma1 gives pure general fibers of dimension \dim Z-\dim G. In the usual reduced algebraic-group convention, connected G is integral over algebraically closed k. Regular finite-type schemes over this perfect field are smooth. Intersect the two open subsets from parts(i) and(ii) to obtain both conclusions on one nonempty open subset.
