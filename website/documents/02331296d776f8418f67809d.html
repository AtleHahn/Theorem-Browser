Statement

Independently draw with replacement from n\geq1 coupon types, each with probability 1/n, and let T be the number of draws until every type has appeared. Here n is an integer. For every integer k\geq1, \mathbb E\binom Tk=n\sum_{i=0}^n\binom ni(-1)^{n-i}i(i-1)^{k-1}/(n+1-i)^{k+1}. In the k=1 summand at i=1, the zero exponent has value one.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The sampling experiment is explicit and discrete. Expectation and variance are weighted sums over possible integer waiting times; analysis supplies infinite sums and asymptotic notation, and algebra supplies permutation cycles where used. These concrete additions do not require general stochastic-process theory.

Proof 1: Expand the rational probability generating function

Use the probability generating function G(z)=\operatorname E(z^T)=\binom{n/z}{n}^{-1}, analytic in a neighborhood of z=1. Here \binom un=u(u-1)\cdots(u-n+1)/n!, and o.g.f. means ordinary generating function.

Replacing {\displaystyle z} with {\displaystyle 1+z} in the probability generating function produces the o.g.f. for {\displaystyle E\left[{T \choose k}\right]}. Using the partial fraction decomposition {\displaystyle {{\frac {1}{x}}-1 \choose n}^{-1}=\sum _{k=0}^{n}{n \choose k}{\frac {(-1)^{n-k}}{1-kx}}}, we can take the expansion

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}&{{\frac {n}{x+1}} \choose n}^{-1}\\=&\sum _{i=0}^{n}{n \choose i}{\frac {(-1)^{n-i}}{1-i(1-{\frac {n}{x+1+n}})}}\\=&\sum _{i=0}^{n}{n \choose i}(-1)^{n-i}\left({\frac {1+n}{1+n-i}}+in\sum _{k=1}^{\infty }{\frac {(i-1)^{k-1}}{(n+1-i)^{k+1}}}x^{k}\right)\end{aligned}}}

revealing that for {\displaystyle k>0},

{\displaystyle E\left[{T \choose k}\right]=n\sum _{i=0}^{n}{n \choose i}(-1)^{n-i}i{\frac {(i-1)^{k-1}}{(n+1-i)^{k+1}}}}
