Statement

A Euclidean rectangle with adjacent side lengths a,b and diagonal length e satisfies a^2+b^2=e^2.

Proof 1: Use equal rectangle diagonals and divide by two

Use Euler's quadrilateral identity

a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2=e^2+f^2+4g^2,

where a,b,c,d are the consecutive side lengths, e,f the diagonal lengths, and g the distance between the diagonal midpoints.

If the quadrilateral is a parallelogram, then the midpoints of the diagonals coincide so that the connecting line segment {\displaystyle g} has length 0. In addition the parallel sides are of equal length, hence Euler's theorem reduces to

{\displaystyle 2a^{2}+2b^{2}=e^{2}+f^{2}}

which is the parallelogram law.

If the quadrilateral is a rectangle, then the equation simplifies further since now the two diagonals are of equal length as well:

{\displaystyle 2a^{2}+2b^{2}=2e^{2}}

Dividing by 2 yields:

{\displaystyle a^{2}+b^{2}=e^{2}}

In other words, in the case of a rectangle the relation of the quadrilateral's sides and its diagonals is described by the Pythagorean theorem.

High school only
Level justification

Use equal opposite sides, bisected diagonals and equal diagonal lengths in the supplied quadrilateral identity, then divide by two. School Euclidean geometry suffices.
