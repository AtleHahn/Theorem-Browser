Statement

The notation \mathrm K_{j=1}^{\infty}a/b means the limit of the finite nested fractions a/(b+a/(b+\cdots+a/b)). Finite convergents are evaluated as rational functions after simplification; convergence here means that they are eventually finite and tend to a finite complex value. Let a,b\in\mathbb C be nonzero and put z=a/b^2. Then x=\mathop{\mathrm K}_{j=1}^{\infty}a/b converges if and only if y=1+\mathop{\mathrm K}_{j=1}^{\infty}z/1 converges.

Proof 1: Rescale all repeated denominators — outline

Apply the equivalence transformation to the nonzero rescaling x/b, so that the repeated denominators become 1 and numerators become a/b^2. Adding 1 gives the displayed y. Nonzero scaling and translation preserve finite convergence.
