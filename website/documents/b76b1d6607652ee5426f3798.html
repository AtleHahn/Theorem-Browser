Statement

If a Lebesgue-measurable set A\subseteq\mathbb R^n has positive measure, then A-A=\{a-b:a,b\in A\} contains an open neighborhood of zero.

Proof 1: Positive convolution near zero

Let {\displaystyle A\subset \mathbb {R} ^{n}} be a measurable subset of positive Lebesgue measure. First, we consider the case where {\displaystyle m(A)<\infty }. In this case, it follows that the characteristic functions {\displaystyle \chi _{A}(x)} and {\displaystyle \chi _{-A}(x)} are contained in {\displaystyle L^{p}(\mathbb {R} ^{n})} for all {\displaystyle 1\leq p<\infty }. Then {\displaystyle h(x)=\chi _{A}\ast \chi _{-A}(x)} is continuous on {\displaystyle \mathbb {R} ^{n}} (where {\displaystyle \ast } denotes convolution) and

{\displaystyle h(0)=\int _{\mathbb {R} ^{n}}\chi _{-A}(-y)\chi _{A}(y)\;dy=\int _{\mathbb {R} ^{n}}\chi _{A}(s)\;ds=m(A)>0.}

Then since {\displaystyle h} is continuous and {\displaystyle h(0)>0}, there exists an open neighborhood {\displaystyle U} of 0 so that {\displaystyle h(x)>0} for all {\displaystyle x\in U}. But by definition of {\displaystyle h(x)}, {\displaystyle h(x)>0} only if {\displaystyle x\in A-A}. Hence, {\displaystyle 0\in U\subset A-A}.

Now suppose {\displaystyle m(A)=\infty }. We can write {\displaystyle A} as the following union:

{\displaystyle A=\bigcup _{R=0}^{\infty }(A\cap B_{R}(0)),}

where {\displaystyle B_{R}(0)} is the ball of radius {\displaystyle R} centered at 0. By countable subadditivity, there exists at least one {\displaystyle R_{0}} so that {\displaystyle m(A\cap B_{R_{0}}(0))>0}. Hence, since {\displaystyle A\cap B_{R_{0}}(0)} has finite Lebesgue measure, by the first part of the proof, there exists a neighborhood {\displaystyle U} contained in {\displaystyle (A\cap B_{R_{0}}(0))-(A\cap B_{R_{0}}(0))\subset A-A}. Hence, the proof concludes.
