Statement

Consider n\ge3 distinct lines in the Euclidean plane, with no parallel pair and no three concurrent. A triangular face means a bounded triangular region containing no other arrangement line in its interior. There is at least one triangular face.

Level
High school only
Level justification

A simple arrangement has no parallel pair and no three concurrent lines; its finite polygon faces are elementary plane geometry.

Proof 1: Preserve at least one triangle while adding lines

Adding another line while retaining simplicity cannot decrease the triangular-face count: each old triangle either survives or is cut into two pieces, one of which is again a triangle. Starting with three lines therefore shows that every such arrangement has a triangle.
