Statement

Let G be a group satisfying both the ascending and descending chain conditions on normal subgroups: every increasing or decreasing sequence of normal subgroups eventually becomes constant. Then G has a finite internal direct-product decomposition

G=G_1\times\cdots\times G_k

into nontrivial indecomposable subgroups. Indecomposable means that the group cannot itself be expressed as a direct product of two nontrivial groups. The trivial group has the empty decomposition.

If G=H_1\times\cdots\times H_l is another such decomposition, then k=l, and the factors H_i can be reordered so that G_i\cong H_i for every i and

G=G_1\times\cdots\times G_r\times H_{r+1}\times\cdots\times H_k \qquad(0\le r\le k).

An internal direct product means that multiplication gives an isomorphism from the external product of the displayed subgroups onto G.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

All group concepts are standard introductory abstract algebra. Chain stabilization and the exchange formula are explicit, small extensions of familiar subgroup inclusions and direct products.

Proof 1: Normal endomorphisms, Fitting decomposition and factor exchange (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorems2.13 and2.21 and Lemmas2.14–2.20, pp.41–45).

For one ordering valid for every r, first exchange G_1 with a suitable H_1. In G/G_1, apply induction to the decompositions induced by G_2,\ldots,G_k and H_2,\ldots,H_l. Lifting the resulting products gives all the required identities in G with this same ordering. Normal-subgroup chain conditions pass to this quotient. In Lemma2.19, read the exponent in (2.2) as m and the subsequent nilpotence condition as \mu^s=0. The trivial group has the empty decomposition.
