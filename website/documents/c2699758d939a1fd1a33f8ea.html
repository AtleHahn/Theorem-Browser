Statement

Independently draw with replacement from n\geq1 coupon types, each with probability 1/n, and let T be the number of draws until every type has appeared. Here n is an integer. Let H_n^{(r)}=\sum_{j=1}^n j^{-r} and set C_i(n)=\sum_{\pi\in S_i}\prod_{c\text{ cycle of }\pi}H_n^{(|c|)}, where S_i is the finite set of permutations of 1,\ldots,i and |c| is the length of the cycle. For every integer k\geq1, \mathbb E\binom Tk=\sum_{i=1}^k\binom{k-1}{i-1}(-1)^{k-i}n^iC_i(n)/i!. Equivalently, \mathbb E(T^{\underline{k}})=\sum_{i=1}^k L(k,i)(-1)^{k-i}n^i C_i(n), where T^{\underline{k}}=T(T-1)\cdots(T-k+1) and L(k,i)=\binom{k-1}{i-1}k!/i!.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The sampling experiment is explicit and discrete. Expectation and variance are weighted sums over possible integer waiting times; analysis supplies infinite sums and asymptotic notation, and algebra supplies permutation cycles where used. These concrete additions do not require general stochastic-process theory.

Proof 1: Compose the coefficient series and rescale by a factorial

Use the probability generating function G(z)=\operatorname E(z^T)=\binom{n/z}{n}^{-1}, analytic in a neighborhood of z=1. Replacing z with 1+z produces the ordinary generating function for \operatorname E\binom Tk. Here [x^k] denotes the coefficient of x^k, and \binom un=u(u-1)\cdots(u-n+1)/n!.

Given an ordinary generating function f, since

\left(\frac{x}{1-x}\right)^i =\sum_{k=i}^{\infty}\binom{k-1}{i-1}x^k\qquad(i\geq1),

a variation of the binomial transform is

[x^k]f\left(\frac{x}{1+x}\right) =\sum_{i=1}^k\binom{k-1}{i-1}(-1)^{k-i}[x^i]f(x) \qquad(k>0).

Specifically, if \binom{n/(x+1)}n^{-1}=f(x/(1+x)), f(x)=\binom{n-nx}n^{-1}.

Rewriting the binomial coefficient via the gamma function and expanding as the exponential of the polygamma series (in terms of generalized harmonic numbers), the source gives the coefficient identity

i![x^i]\binom{n-x}n^{-1} =\sum_{P\in S_i}\prod_{c\text{ cycle of }P}H_n^{(|c|)} =C_i(n).

The gamma/polygamma calculation is not supplied. Using this identity and scaling x by n, we obtain

\operatorname E\binom Tk =\sum_{i=1}^k\binom{k-1}{i-1}(-1)^{k-i}\frac{n^i}{i!} \sum_{P\in S_i}\prod_{c\text{ cycle of }P}H_n^{(|c|)},

which can also be written with the falling factorial and Lah numbers as

\operatorname E(T^{\underline k}) =\sum_{i=1}^k L(k,i)(-1)^{k-i}n^i \sum_{P\in S_i}\prod_{c\text{ cycle of }P}H_n^{(|c|)}.

Here T^{\underline k}=k!\binom Tk and L(k,i)=\binom{k-1}{i-1}k!/i!.

Level justification

Apply the supplied explicit coefficient identity and the power-series composition transform, then multiply by k factorial. The omitted gamma/polygamma derivation is not invoked as a method; only its concrete rational-series coefficient conclusion is needed. Calculus series, finite permutation cycles and discrete expectation suffice.
