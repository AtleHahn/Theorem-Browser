Statement

If a nondegenerate Euclidean triangle has area A, inradius r and semiperimeter s, then A=rs.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Elementary Euclidean geometry, trigonometry and algebra are within the high-school criterion.

Mathematical Field
High School Mathematics
Required topics
Elementary triangle geometryAlgebra
Review notes
none

Proof 1: Add the three triangles from the incenter

Let a,b,c be the side lengths. If r is the radius of the incircle of the triangle, then joining its center to the vertices breaks the triangle into three triangles of equal altitude r and bases a,b,c. Their combined area is

A=\tfrac12ar+\tfrac12br+\tfrac12cr=rs,

where s=\tfrac12(a+b+c) is the semiperimeter.

The same area sum can be made using the six smaller right triangles obtained by drawing radii to the points of tangency. These come in three pairs, with the triangles in each pair having the same area. The tangent lengths from the vertex opposite side a are s-a, and similarly at the other vertices. For example, the two triangles near that vertex, being right triangles of width s-a and height r, each have an area of \tfrac12r(s-a). So those two triangles together have an area of r(s-a), and the area of the whole triangle is therefore

\begin{aligned} A&=r(s-a)+r(s-b)+r(s-c)\\ &=r\bigl(3s-(a+b+c)\bigr)\\ &=r(3s-2s)\\ &=rs. \end{aligned}
Level
High school only
Level justification

Split at the incenter into three triangles of common height r and add their areas; the optional tangent-pair subdivision gives the same sum. School Euclidean geometry suffices.
