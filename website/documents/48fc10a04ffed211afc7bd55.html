Statement

Let X be a vector space over \mathbb K=\mathbb R or \mathbb C, and let Y be a subspace of its algebraic dual that separates points: for each nonzero x\in X some y\in Y has y(x)\ne0. Write \sigma(X,Y) for the topology of pointwise convergence against all y\in Y, and \sigma(Y,X) for pointwise convergence on X. Define \tau(X,Y) by the seminorms p_K(x)=\sup_{y\in K}|y(x)|, where K ranges over all nonempty convex, balanced, \sigma(Y,X)-compact subsets of Y. Balanced means aK\subseteq K whenever |a|\le1. A locally convex Hausdorff vector-space topology \mathcal T on X has continuous dual exactly Y if and only if

\sigma(X,Y)\subseteq\mathcal T\subseteq\tau(X,Y).

Topology inclusion means inclusion of open sets. Thus the weak topology is the coarsest and the Mackey topology the finest compatible locally convex Hausdorff topology. Locally convex means having a neighborhood base at zero of convex sets; a vector-space topology makes addition and scalar multiplication continuous.
