Statement

For finitely many nonatomic probability measures on a circle and an integer m\geq1, the set of common values attained by a union of at most m arcs is closed in [0,1].

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The set of common values depends on arbitrary nonatomic measures, beyond calculus integration.

Proof 1: Take the image of the compact equal-value locus — outline

Let W\subseteq[0,1] be the set of common values in the statement. W is a closed set. This is easy to prove, since the space of unions of at most m arcs is a compact set under a suitable topology.

The source gives this compactness outline without specifying the topology or expanding the deduction of closedness.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The compactness step is basic topology, but the outline also relies on continuity of arbitrary nonatomic measures under motion of arc endpoints. Checking that claim requires measure continuity and control of small neighborhoods, beyond the stated elementary analysis baseline. A precise realization is the compact parameter space (S^1 times [0,1])^m of starts and arc lengths; the equal-measure locus is closed and its continuous common-value image is compact. This specifies the omitted topology without changing the companion outline.

Compact endpoint parameterization of finite arc unions
Kind:

Theorem

Treatment:

Implicitly assumed

Statement used:

Every union of at most m circular arcs is represented by m pairs of a start point in S^1 and an oriented arc length in [0,1], allowing zero lengths and full circles; the parameter space is compact.

Application in proof:

Use this finite compact parameter space for all candidate arc unions.

Application level:

Undergraduate level

Level justification:

Finite products of a circle and compact intervals are basic topology; no hyperspace compactness theorem is necessary.

Review notes:

Use a fixed circle parameterization. Repeated or zero-length arcs allow fewer than m arcs, and length one represents the full circle. Endpoints have zero measure, so their inclusion does not affect values.

Continuity of nonatomic measure values under moving endpoints
Kind:

Theorem

Treatment:

Implicitly assumed

Statement used:

For a finite nonatomic Borel measure on the circle, the measure of a finite union of arcs depends continuously on their endpoint parameters.

Application in proof:

Make every measure-value map continuous; intersect their pairwise equality loci and take the compact image under one value map.

Application level:

Beyond undergraduate level

Level justification:

Checking this uses countable-additivity consequences and the fact that shrinking neighborhoods of a point have measure tending to its zero point mass.

Review notes:

Changes in the union lie in small neighborhoods of finitely many moving endpoints. The union bound and nonatomicity make their measures tend to zero, including degenerate and full-circle limits.
