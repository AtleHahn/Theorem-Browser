Statement

Let n\ge1 and 1\le p<\infty. Let u\ge0 belong to W^{1,p}(\mathbb R^n), the space of Lebesgue L^p functions whose first weak partial derivatives also belong to L^p. Let u^* be its symmetric decreasing rearrangement: the radial, radially nonincreasing function whose positive-threshold superlevel sets \{u^*>t\} are centered balls, possibly empty, with the same measures as \{u>t\}, interpreted up to null sets. Then u^*\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb R^n) and

\int_{\mathbb R^n}|\nabla u^*(x)|^p\,dx \le\int_{\mathbb R^n}|\nabla u(x)|^p\,dx.

Proof 1: Polarization and weak compactness of gradients (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Section4.2, Theorem4.7 and Lemma4.8, pp.36–37; Proposition2.13 and Exercise2.15).

In the polarization proof read g_k\to u^* and \|\nabla g_k\|_p=\|\nabla u\|_p. For p=1, use more than boundedness: Lemma4.8 preserves the entire distribution of |\nabla u|, so \sup_k\int_{\{|\nabla g_k|>M\}}|\nabla g_k|=\int_{\{|\nabla u|>M\}}|\nabla u|\to0. Thus the gradients are uniformly integrable on every ball. Dunford–Pettis, Theorem1 and a diagonal subsequence give local weak L^1 limits. Testing against compactly supported smooth functions identifies these limits as \nabla u^*; lower semicontinuity on balls and exhaustion give the global bound. Equimeasurability gives u^*\in L^p, so this also proves the asserted Sobolev membership.

The complete statement concerns Lebesgue Sobolev spaces and rearrangement. The proof uses weak derivatives of maxima and minima, convergence of iterated polarizations, and weak compactness of gradients. At p=1 it additionally uses uniform integrability and the Dunford–Pettis theorem, whose measure-theoretic proof uses Radon–Nikodym. These are beyond introductory undergraduate analysis.
