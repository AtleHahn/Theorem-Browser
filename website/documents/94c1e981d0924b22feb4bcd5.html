Statement

Let a<b, let g:[a,b]\to\mathbb R be continuous, and put S=\{x\in[a,b]:\text{there exists }y\in(x,b]\text{ with }g(y)>g(x)\}. If a\le c<d\le b, [c,d)\subseteq S and d\notin S, then g(c)<g(d).

Proof 1: Compare an interval maximum with a later larger value

To prove this, suppose g(c)\ge g(d). Then g achieves its maximum on [c,d] at some point z<d. Since z\in S, there is a y in (z,b] with g(z)<g(y). If y\le d, then g would not reach its maximum on [c,d] at z. Thus, y\in(d,b], and g(d)\le g(z)<g(y). This means that d\in S, which is a contradiction, thus establishing the lemma.

Level justification

Take a compact-interval maximum and contradict the existence of a later larger value in each of the two possible locations. Undergraduate real analysis and the extreme-value theorem suffice.
