Statement

For fixed prime power q, the number N_n of monic irreducible degree-n polynomials over \mathbb F_q satisfies N_n=q^n/n+O(q^{n/2}/n) and hence N_n\sim q^n/n as n\to\infty.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory algebra supplies finite fields, polynomial evaluation, irreducibility and vector subspaces. The Mobius function, asymptotic notation or uniform finite counting probability is a limited numerical definition where used. These statements require no field-extension norm or Galois theory.

Proof 1: Proper-divisor geometric-sum bound

The proof involves a short, combinatorial argument, summarised as follows: every element of the degree n extension of \mathbb F_q is a root of some monic irreducible polynomial whose degree d divides n; by counting these roots in two different ways one establishes that

q^n=\sum_{d\mid n}dN_d,

where the sum is over all divisors d of n. Möbius inversion then yields

N_n=\frac1n\sum_{d\mid n}\mu\left(\frac nd\right)q^d,

where \mu(k) is the Möbius function. The main term occurs for d=n, and it is not difficult to bound the remaining terms. The error bound

N_n=\frac{q^n}{n}+O\left(\frac{q^{n/2}}n\right)

depends on the fact that the largest proper divisor of n can be no larger than n/2.

Level justification

The final geometric bound is elementary analysis, but the supplied proof first derives the exact count from extension fields, minimal polynomials and their splitting degrees. Retain beyond undergraduate provisionally for that cumulative algebraic background.
