Statement

Let (a_n)_{n\ge0} be a real sequence with a_n\ge-K for every n, for some constant K\ge0. Suppose the power series A(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_nx^n converges for 0\le x<1 and

\lim_{x\uparrow1}(1-x)A(x)=1.

Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_k=1.

Equivalently, the hypothesis on the asymptotic behavior of the series can be written

\lim_{y\downarrow0}y\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_ne^{-ny}=1.

In particular the theorem applies to nonnegative coefficients, and to bounded real coefficient sequences.
