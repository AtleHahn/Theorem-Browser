Statement

Let (a_n)_{n\ge0} be a real sequence with a_n\ge-K for every n, for some constant K\ge0. Suppose the power series A(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_nx^n converges for 0\le x<1 and

\lim_{x\uparrow1}(1-x)A(x)=1.

Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_k=1.

Equivalently, the hypothesis on the asymptotic behavior of the series can be written

\lim_{y\downarrow0}y\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_ne^{-ny}=1.

In particular the theorem applies to nonnegative coefficients, and to bounded real coefficient sequences.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The complete statement uses familiar real-analysis limits and convergent power series; its one-sided bound and exponential substitution require no additional theory.

Proof 1: Polynomial cutoff sandwiches and a nonnegative shift (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorem4 and Lemma3).

First apply the nonnegative theorem to b_n=(a_n+K)/(1+K). Its Abel limit is 1, and undoing the normalization gives

\frac1N\sum_{n=0}^N a_n=(1+K)\frac1N\sum_{n=0}^N b_n-K\frac{N+1}{N}\longrightarrow1.

For Lemma3’s cutoff g(t)=t^{-1}\mathbf1_{[c,1]}(t), c=e^{-1}, use a continuous minorant that is zero through c, equals (t-c)/(\delta t) on [c,c+\delta], and equals 1/t afterward. Use a continuous majorant that is zero through c-\delta, equals (t-c+\delta)/(\delta t) on [c-\delta,c], and equals 1/t afterward. Their integral errors tend to zero. Uniform polynomial approximations shifted downward and upward give the required pointwise sandwich. Both cutoff endpoints are included: at x=e^{-1/N} exactly the indices 0\le n\le N survive. Finally (1-e^{-y})/y\to1 gives the equivalent exponential hypothesis.
