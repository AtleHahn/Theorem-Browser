Statement

Let A_3\le S_3 be the alternating subgroup, and let r:S_3\to\{\pm1\} be the sign homomorphism. The exact sequence 1\to A_3\xrightarrow{q}S_3\xrightarrow{r}\{\pm1\}\to1, where q is inclusion, has a right splitting but no left splitting; moreover S_3\not\cong A_3\times\{\pm1\}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Permutation groups, sign, kernels and products belong to introductory group theory; exactness and the two splitting identities are short related definitions.

Proof 1: Transpositions and element orders

Let A=A_3, B=S_3, and C=B/A\cong\{\pm1\}. The direct-product conclusion fails, because S_3 is not abelian, whereas A\times C is. But a right splitting exists: we may define u:C\to B by mapping the generator to any two-cycle.

Note for completeness that a left splitting fails: any homomorphism t:B\to A must map every two-cycle to the identity, because the order of its image must divide 2, while every element in A other than the identity has order 3, as A is the alternating subgroup of S_3, or namely the cyclic group of order 3. But every permutation is a product of two-cycles, so t is the trivial map, whence tq:A\to A is the trivial map, not the identity.
