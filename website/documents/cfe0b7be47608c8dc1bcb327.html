Statement

Let m\ge1 be an integer and let M:c_0(\mathbb C)^m\to\mathbb C be multilinear. Here c_0(\mathbb C) is the space of complex sequences tending to zero, with supremum norm, and e_i is its ith unit sequence. Define

\|M\|=\sup_{\|x_1\|_\infty,\ldots,\|x_m\|_\infty\le1}|M(x_1,\ldots,x_m)|.

Then

\left(\sum_{i_1,\ldots,i_m\ge1}|M(e_{i_1},\ldots,e_{i_m})|^{2m/(m+1)}\right)^{(m+1)/(2m)}\le 2^{(m-1)/2}\|M\|.

Multilinear means linear separately in every argument. Nonnegative sums and the norm are interpreted in [0,\infty].

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Multilinearity is a short extension of linear algebra, and the remaining explicit sequence/norm notions fit undergraduate analysis.

In Lemma 2.5 take singleton coordinate groups, q=2 and r_k=1. Formula (2.16) gives exponent 2m/(m+1) and weights 1/m; the scalar constant in (2.24) gives 2^{(m-1)/2}. Apply the finite-dimensional estimate on the first N coordinates and let N\to\infty. If \|M\|=\infty the inequality is automatic; for m=1 choose the phases of finitely many coefficients.
