Statement

Fix a hexagonally close-packed planar layer of equal spheres. Form successive layers by placing another congruent planar layer into either of the two types of lowest gaps above its predecessor, and continue for infinitely many layers. This procedure gives uncountably many distinct layer stackings.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The sphere-layer construction is explicit Euclidean geometry with an independent binary choice at each stage. Uncountability of infinite binary sequences is undergraduate set theory; no geometric-packing classification is needed.

Proof 1: Two choices at every layer — outline

At each step there are at least two choices of how to place the next layer, so this method of stacking the spheres, continued for infinitely many layers, creates an uncountably infinite number of packings.

The source leaves the coding by infinite binary sequences implicit.
