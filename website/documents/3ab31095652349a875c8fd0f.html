Statement

Let X be a connected compact Riemann surface of genus g. A divisor is a finite integer sum of points, its degree is the sum of its coefficients, and K is the divisor of a nonzero meromorphic one-form. For a divisor D, let L(D) consist of zero and the meromorphic functions h with (h)+D\ge0, and put \ell(D)=\dim_{\mathbb C}L(D). Then \ell(D)-\ell(K-D)=\deg(D)-g+1.

Proof 1: Use a Laurent quotient to add one point — outline

Let \mathcal O_D be the sheaf of zero and meromorphic functions h such that all coefficients of the divisor (h)+D are nonnegative. Write h^j(X,\mathcal F)=\dim_{\mathbb C}H^j(X,\mathcal F) and \chi(\mathcal F)=h^0(X,\mathcal F)-h^1(X,\mathcal F).

The integer \ell(D) is the dimension of the space of global sections of \mathcal O_D. In terms of sheaf cohomology, we therefore have \ell(D)=\dim H^0(X,\mathcal O_D), and likewise \ell(K-D)=\dim H^0(X,\mathcal O_{K-D}). But Serre duality for a compact Riemann surface states that H^0(X,\mathcal O_{K-D}) is isomorphic to the dual H^1(X,\mathcal O_D)^\vee. The left hand side \ell(D)-\ell(K-D) thus equals the Euler characteristic of the divisor D. When D=0, we find the Euler characteristic for the structure sheaf is 1-g. To prove the theorem for a general divisor, one can then proceed by adding or removing points one by one to the divisor and ensure that the Euler characteristic transforms accordingly to the right hand side.

Here the fact that the Euler characteristic transforms as desired when one adds a point to the divisor can be read off from the long exact sequence induced by the short exact sequence

0\to\mathcal O_D\to\mathcal O_{D+P}\to\mathbb C_P\to0,

where \mathbb C_P is the skyscraper sheaf at P, and the map \mathcal O_{D+P}\to\mathbb C_P returns the -k-1th Laurent coefficient in a local coordinate, where k=D(P).

Proof 2: Transfer algebraic Riemann–Roch using Chow and GAGA — outline

The theorem for compact Riemann surfaces can be deduced from the algebraic version using Chow's Theorem and the GAGA principle: in fact, every compact Riemann surface is defined by algebraic equations in some complex projective space. (Chow's Theorem says that any closed analytic subvariety of projective space is defined by algebraic equations, and the GAGA principle says that sheaf cohomology of an algebraic variety is the same as the sheaf cohomology of the analytic variety defined by the same equations).
