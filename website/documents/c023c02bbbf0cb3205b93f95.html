Statement

For an integer n\ge1, let \mathbb{CP}^n denote nonzero vectors (z_0,\ldots,z_n)\in\mathbb C^{n+1} modulo multiplication by a nonzero complex scalar. The formula \mu([z_0:\cdots:z_n])=(|z_1|^2,\ldots,|z_n|^2)/(\sum_{j=0}^n|z_j|^2) is well defined and has image \{(u_1,\ldots,u_n)\in\mathbb R^n:u_i\ge0,\ \sum_i u_i\le1\}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Finite complex coordinate vectors; Locally defined homogeneous-coordinate equivalence; Modulus squares and normalized ratios; A simplex given by linear inequalities. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Normalized coordinate-square recipe — outline

Use the coordinate formula

[z_0:\cdots:z_n]\longmapsto\frac{(|z_1|^2,\ldots,|z_n|^2)}{|z_0|^2+\cdots+|z_n|^2}.

Its image is the standard n-simplex.
