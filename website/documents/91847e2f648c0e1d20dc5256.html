Statement

Let M be a closed, oriented, smooth four-dimensional manifold, where closed means compact without boundary. Its integral intersection form is the symmetric bilinear form on the free abelian group

L=H^2(M;\mathbb Z)/\operatorname{Tor}H^2(M;\mathbb Z)

given by

Q_M(a,b)=\langle a\smile b,[M]\rangle.

Here H^2 is integral singular cohomology, \operatorname{Tor} is the subgroup of elements of finite order, \smile is the cup product, and [M] is the oriented fundamental class; representatives of a,b may be used in the displayed pairing.

If the extension of Q_M to L\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb R is positive definite, there is an integral basis of L in which its matrix is I_b, where b=\operatorname{rank}L. If it is negative definite, there is an integral basis in which its matrix is -I_b. No simply-connectedness assumption is required.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The complete statement requires smooth manifold topology, integral cohomology modulo torsion, cup products and evaluation on an oriented fundamental class before the integral diagonalization claim can be understood. These interdependent concepts substantially exceed a 30-minute extension of baseline undergraduate algebra and topology.

  • A fundamental restriction on smooth four-manifold topology: definite intersection forms must be standard over the integers.

Proof availability

The source gives an idea of proof only for the simply-connected case, using substantial gauge-theoretic inputs. The sketch prematurely states the number of reducible connections and later identifies rank with signature after choosing a negative-definite orientation; it also omits the lattice argument needed for diagonalization. It does not supply a complete proof of the full statement for arbitrary fundamental group. These gaps and sign issues have not been repaired editorially.
