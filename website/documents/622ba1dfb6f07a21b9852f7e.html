Statement

Let a group G of prime order p act on a finite set X of size k. The number of points fixed by every element of G is congruent to k modulo p, and is at least the least nonnegative residue of k modulo p.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory group theory supplies cyclic groups, actions, products, normal subgroups and commutators. Perfect means generated by commutators, and the counted fixed points/tuples are explicit; no character theory is required.

Proof 1: Fixed-point congruence for a prime-order action

Since each orbit has either 1 or p elements by the orbit–stabilizer theorem, there are at least k\bmod p orbits of length 1, which are G-invariant elements. More specifically, k and the number of G-invariant elements are congruent modulo p. Here k\bmod p denotes the least nonnegative residue, and a G-invariant element is one fixed by every element of G.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Partition the finite set into orbits of size one or p and count modulo p. Undergraduate groups and actions suffice.
