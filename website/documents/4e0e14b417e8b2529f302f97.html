Statement

There are positive real numbers x_1,\ldots,x_{20} such that \sum_{i=1}^{20}\frac{x_i}{x_{i+1}+x_{i+2}}<10, where subscripts are cyclic modulo 20.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The existential failure of a finite rational inequality requires only elementary real algebra.

Mathematical Field
High School Mathematics
Required topics
Positive real numbersFinite cyclic rational sums
Review notes
none

Proof 1: Lighthill small-parameter construction — outline

For a positive parameter \epsilon, take (x_1,\ldots,x_{20})=(1+5\epsilon,6\epsilon,1+4\epsilon,5\epsilon,1+3\epsilon,4\epsilon,1+2\epsilon,3\epsilon,1+\epsilon,2\epsilon,1+2\epsilon,\epsilon,1+3\epsilon,2\epsilon,1+4\epsilon,3\epsilon,1+5\epsilon,4\epsilon,1+6\epsilon,5\epsilon). The cyclic sum expands as 10-\epsilon^2+O(\epsilon^3). It is therefore below 10 for sufficiently small positive \epsilon, and all twenty coordinates are positive. The termwise rational expansion is left implicit.
