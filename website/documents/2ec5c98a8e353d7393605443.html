Statement

Let F_0=0, F_1=1, and F_n=F_{n-1}+F_{n-2} for n\ge2. A primitive prime divisor of F_n means a prime dividing F_n but none of F_m for 1\le m<n. For positive n, F_n has a primitive prime divisor if and only if n\notin\{1,2,6,12\}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The locally defined recurrence and prime-divisor condition use elementary arithmetic and functions.

Proof 1: Specialize Carmichael and check the exceptions

Use Carmichael’s real-discriminant Lucas-sequence theorem as an external prerequisite: for coprime nonzero integer parameters P,Q with P^2-4Q>0, the sequence U_0=0, U_1=1, U_n=PU_{n-1}-QU_{n-2} has a prime divisor new among positive-index terms for positive n\notin\{1,2,6\}, except for U_{12}(\pm1,-1).

For (P,Q)=(1,-1) the recurrence is Fibonacci and the discriminant is 5>0. The theorem therefore gives the required primitive divisor outside \{1,2,6,12\}. Conversely, F_1=F_2=1 have no prime divisors. The only prime divisor of F_6=8 is 2=F_3, and the only prime divisors of F_{12}=144 are 2=F_3 and 3=F_4. Thus none of these four terms has a primitive prime divisor.

Level justification

Substitute P=1,Q=-1 into the explicitly stated recurrence theorem and check its hypotheses and four exceptional terms. All applied objects are integer recurrences and new prime divisors; the proof of Carmichael theorem is not invoked.
