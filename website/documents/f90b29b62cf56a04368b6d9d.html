Statement

For a sentence \varphi in a relational language with equality, replace equality by a fresh binary relation Eq, obtaining \varphi'. Conjoin \varphi' with the universal closures of

Eq(x,x),\quad Eq(x,y)\to(Eq(x,z)\to Eq(y,z)),\quad Eq(x,y)\to(Eq(z,x)\to Eq(z,y)),

and, for every r-ary predicate A occurring in \varphi, the congruence condition

\bigwedge_{i=1}^r Eq(x_i,y_i)\ \to\ [A(x_1,\ldots,x_r)\leftrightarrow A(y_1,\ldots,y_r)].

Call the resulting equality-free sentence T_\varphi. Then satisfiability of T_\varphi implies satisfiability of \varphi with actual equality, and refutability of T_\varphi implies refutability of \varphi in the calculus with equality axioms. Domains are nonempty.

Proof 1: Congruence quotient and equality substitution — outline

If T_\varphi is refutable, substitute actual equality for Eq. Its added axioms are equality theorems, so \varphi is refutable. If T_\varphi has a model, quotient its domain by the equivalence relation interpreted by Eq. Predicate congruence makes all relations well defined on equivalence classes, and the quotient satisfies \varphi.
