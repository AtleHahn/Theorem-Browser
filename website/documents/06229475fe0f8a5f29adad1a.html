Statement

Assume a randomized polynomial-time procedure maps an n-variable Boolean formula F to formulas G_0,\ldots,G_n such that every satisfying assignment of any G_i satisfies F, and, whenever F is satisfiable, with probability at least 1/4 at least one G_i has exactly one satisfying assignment. Choosing an index I uniformly from \{0,\ldots,n\} independently of the list gives an output G_I that is always unsatisfiable if F is unsatisfiable, and is uniquely satisfiable with probability at least 1/[4(n+1)] if F is satisfiable.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Finite Boolean formulas, satisfying assignments and uniform selection from a finite list suffice. The probabilistic procedure is assumed with explicit guarantees; the conclusion is a finite probability bound, with no complexity-class equivalence included.

Proof 1: Conditioning and a uniform index

On every list containing a uniquely satisfiable member, a uniform index selects at least one such member with probability at least 1/(n+1). Such lists occur with probability at least 1/4, giving the product bound. If the original formula is unsatisfiable, every list member is unsatisfiable by the assumed assignment-preservation property.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Condition on lists with an isolated solution and multiply their probability by the reciprocal list length. Elementary probability and Boolean assignments suffice; the polynomial-time procedure is an explicit hypothesis.
