Statement

Let b\ge2 and n\ge0 be integers satisfying n\equiv1\pmod3,\quad n>1. Then (b+1)b^n+1 is composite.

Level
High school only

Proof 1: Use the stated polynomial divisor — outline

Use the stated divisibility b^2+b+1\mid (b+1)b^n+1 for this exponent class. The divisor is greater than one, and with n\ge4 the number is strictly larger than the divisor. Hence this is a proper nontrivial divisor, proving compositeness.
