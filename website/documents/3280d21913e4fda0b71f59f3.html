Statement

A topological space is called \omega-bounded if the closure of every countable subset is compact. A metric space is compact if and only if it is \omega-bounded. Here compact means that every open cover has a finite subcover, and countable includes finite sets.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

All concepts are basic point-set topology, with omega-boundedness explicitly defined. No ordinal or nonmetrizable-surface theory is needed for this specific statement.
