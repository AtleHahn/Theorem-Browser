Statement

Let X have density e^{-x} on (0,\infty) and put Y=e^X\sin X/X. Then \lim_{R\to\infty}\mathbb E\bigl[Y\mathbf1_{\{X\le R\}}\bigr]=\pi/2, but \mathbb E|Y|=\infty, so this conditional integral is not a finite Lebesgue expectation.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The density and random-variable transform are explicit. Truncated expectations are weighted integrals, while a finite Lebesgue expectation requires integrable absolute value. Distinguishing an improper limit from absolute integrability is a short extension of undergraduate real-analysis convergence.

Proof 1: Cancel the exponential density in truncated integrals

Density integration cancels the exponential factor: \mathbb E[Y\mathbf1_{\{X\le R\}}]=\int_0^R\frac{e^x\sin x}{x}e^{-x}\,dx=\int_0^R\frac{\sin x}{x}\,dx. The classical Dirichlet integral converges conditionally to \pi/2; its absolute integral diverges. The same cancellation with absolute values gives \mathbb E|Y|=\int_0^\infty|\sin x|/x\,dx=\infty.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

All expectations reduce to explicit one-dimensional improper integrals after cancellation of a smooth density. The supplied conditional/absolute convergence facts show why truncation has a limit but the absolute expectation is infinite. This distinction is accessible from introductory real analysis and density-based probability, without a general measure-space construction.
