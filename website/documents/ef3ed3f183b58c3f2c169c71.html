Identifies the precise foundational strength of two existence principles taken together: over ZF, the stated Krein–Milman assertion and the Boolean prime ideal theorem jointly recover the axiom of choice. This connects apparently geometric existence claims with commitments about arbitrary infinite selections; the equivalence concerns the conjunction, not either principle alone.

Statement

Work over Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory (ZF). Let AC denote the axiom of choice, BPI the Boolean prime ideal theorem (every proper ideal of a Boolean algebra is contained in a prime ideal), and KM the assertion that every nonempty compact convex subset of a real or complex Hausdorff locally convex topological vector space has an extreme point. Then \mathrm{AC}\iff(\mathrm{KM}\land\mathrm{BPI}).

Level justification

Proof 1: Combine the two stated directions

Under the Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory (ZF) axiomatic framework, the axiom of choice (AC) suffices to prove the Krein–Milman statement KM. The axiom of choice also implies the Boolean prime ideal theorem (BPI). Conversely, the Krein–Milman theorem KM together with the Boolean prime ideal theorem (BPI) imply the axiom of choice. In summary, AC holds if and only if both KM and BPI hold.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The final inference is propositional logic, but understanding its assertions requires choice principles over ZF, Boolean prime ideals, continuous duals and compact convex sets in locally convex topological vector spaces. This combination is substantially beyond a short extension of ordinary undergraduate algebra and analysis.
