Statement

Let (C,x_1,\ldots,x_n) be a stable connected complex genus-g curve with distinct marked smooth points, and let k_i\ge1 be integers. Attach a projective line at each x_i, identifying its coordinate z_i=0 with x_i. Map C constantly to 0\in\mathbb P^1, and its ith tail by z_i\mapsto z_i^{k_i}. This defines a stable map whose poles are the n tail points z_i=\infty of orders k_i, and which is unramified over \mathbb P^1\setminus\{0,\infty\}.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Stable marked curves, nodal attachment and stability of maps require substantial algebraic geometry. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Attach rational tails with power maps — outline

Send the stable marked curve to 0 and attach to its marked points n rational components on which the map is z\mapsto z^{k_1},\ldots,z\mapsto z^{k_n}. These are stable maps unramified outside 0 and \infty.
