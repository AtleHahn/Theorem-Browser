Statement

For a normal element a of a unital C*-algebra A with unit 1, there exists exactly one unital *-homomorphism \Phi_a:C(\sigma_A(a))\to A sending the coordinate function z\mapsto z to a.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves C*-algebras; Normal elements and spectra; Continuous functional calculus; Unital star-homomorphisms; Continuous function algebras. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Gelfand composition and polynomial density — outline

The existence and uniqueness of the continuous functional calculus are proven separately.

Existence: Since the spectrum of a in the C*-subalgebra C^*(a,1) generated by a and 1 is the same as it is in A, it suffices to show the statement for A=C^*(a,1). This algebra is commutative because a is normal. The actual construction is almost immediate from the Gelfand representation: it suffices to assume A is the C*-algebra of continuous functions on some compact space X and define

\Phi_a(f)=f\circ x,

where x\in C(X) is the function representing a, whose range is \sigma_A(a). Transport this map back to C^*(a,1)\subseteq A.

Uniqueness: Since \Phi_a(\boldsymbol1) and \Phi_a(\operatorname{Id}_{\sigma(a)}) are fixed, \Phi_a is already uniquely defined for all polynomials

p(z,\overline z)=\sum_{k,l=0}^N c_{k,l}z^k\overline z^{\,l} \qquad(c_{k,l}\in\mathbb C),

since \Phi_a is a *-homomorphism. These form a dense subalgebra of C(\sigma(a)) by the Stone–Weierstrass theorem. Thus \Phi_a is unique, since -homomorphisms of C-algebras are continuous.
