Statement

Let G=(V,E) be a finite directed graph, let s,t\in V be distinct, and assign each edge a finite real capacity c_{uv}\ge0. A feasible flow assigns real numbers 0\le f_{uv}\le c_{uv} to edges and satisfies

\sum_{u:(u,v)\in E}f_{uv}=\sum_{w:(v,w)\in E}f_{vw}\qquad(v\notin\{s,t\}).

Its value is the net outflow

|f|=\sum_{v:(s,v)\in E}f_{sv}-\sum_{v:(v,s)\in E}f_{vs}.

For S\subseteq V with s\in S and t\notin S, put

c(S,V\setminus S)=\sum_{(u,v)\in E,\ u\in S,\ v\notin S}c_{uv}.

A maximum feasible flow and a minimum-capacity cut exist, and

\max_f |f|=\min_{S:\ s\in S,\ t\notin S}c(S,V\setminus S).
Level
High school only
Level justification

The finite graph, constraints and optimization quantities are all defined through elementary sums, inequalities and sets. No algorithm or linear-programming duality is needed to understand the assertion.

The residual-graph argument assumes a final Ford–Fulkerson flow, whose termination is not guaranteed for arbitrary real capacities. The linear-programming discussion also omits the step converting the dual solution to a cut.
