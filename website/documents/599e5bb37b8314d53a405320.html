Statement

For a real or complex normed vector space X, the closed unit ball \{f\in X':\|f\|\le1\} of the continuous dual is compact in the weak-star topology of pointwise convergence on X.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement concerns weak-star compactness of the unit ball of an infinite-dimensional dual space.

Proof 1: Identify the dual ball as a neighborhood polar

For a zero neighborhood U, its polar is U^\circ=\{f\in X^\prime:\sup_{u\in U}|f(u)|\le1\}. Use the neighborhood-polar Alaoglu theorem: the polar of any zero neighborhood in a real or complex topological vector space is weak-star compact.

If {\displaystyle X} is a normed vector space, then the polar of a neighborhood is closed and norm-bounded in the dual space. In particular, if {\displaystyle U} is the open (or closed) unit ball in {\displaystyle X} then the polar of {\displaystyle U} is the closed unit ball in the continuous dual space {\displaystyle X^{\prime }} of {\displaystyle X} (with the usual dual norm).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The identification of the polar with a dual unit ball is direct once the definitions are known, but the supplied theorem is for general topological vector spaces, continuous duals, weak-star topology and neighborhood polars. Understanding those linked structures and the compactness assertion exceeds the short undergraduate extension, in accordance with the neighborhood-polar calibration case.
