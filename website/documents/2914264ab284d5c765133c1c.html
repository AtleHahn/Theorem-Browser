Statement

Let k,r\ge1 be integers, and color every k-element subset of the positive integers with one of r colors. There exists a sequence (a_n)_{n\ge0} of positive integers and a color c such that for every choice of nonempty finite sets F_1,\ldots,F_k\subseteq\{0,1,2,\ldots\} with \max F_j<\min F_{j+1} for 1\le j<k, the set

\left\{\sum_{t\in F_1}a_t,\ldots,\sum_{t\in F_k}a_t\right\}

has exactly k elements and has color c. Thus every set of sums over successive disjoint index blocks is monochromatic in the same color.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All objects are elementary integers, finite sums and color assignments; quantifiers are explicit and require no advanced structural theory.

Proof 1: Idempotent ultrafilters and recursive block choices (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorem 3.4 and proof, pp. 13–14 (PDF pages 13–14).).

Apply Theorem 3.4 and its inductive choice of x_(n+1) greater than the sum of earlier terms. Then sums over successive nonempty index blocks are strictly increasing and hence form a k-element set. The theorem gives their common color. The source constructs the sequence using idempotent ultrafilters and the extension-set argument of Bergelson–Hindman (1989, Theorem 2.3 and Corollary 2.4).
