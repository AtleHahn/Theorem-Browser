Statement

If nonempty open sets U\subseteq\mathbb R^n and V\subseteq\mathbb R^m are homeomorphic, then n=m. Here a homeomorphism is a continuous bijection with continuous inverse. In particular, \mathbb R^n and \mathbb R^m are not homeomorphic when n\ne m.

Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Euclidean open sets; continuous maps and inverses; dimension. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. Proof 1: Applying the injection obstruction in the larger-to-smaller direction

Use the continuous-injection obstruction: no nonempty open subset of \mathbb R^a has a continuous injection into \mathbb R^b when a>b. A homeomorphism between the stated open sets, in the direction from the larger dimension to the smaller (using its inverse if necessary), would be such an injection. This gives the open-set conclusion and hence the special case of the whole Euclidean spaces.
