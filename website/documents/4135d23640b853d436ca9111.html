Statement

Let D be a nonzero finite-dimensional associative unital division algebra over \mathbb R, with real scalars central, and put n=\dim_{\mathbb R}D. Write L_a for left multiplication by a\in D and \operatorname{tr}(a)=\operatorname{tr}(L_a). Set V=\{a\in D:a^2\in\mathbb R_{\le0}1\}. The formula B(a,b)=-(ab+ba)/2 defines a real positive-definite inner product on V.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A finite-dimensional associative algebra is a vector space with bilinear associative multiplication and identity; division means nonzero elements are invertible. Central real scalars, multiplication matrices, trace and minimal polynomials are direct linear-algebra/ring notions. These finite-dimensional definitions suffice without classification theory.

Proof 1: Polarize the negative square

Use the trace-zero decomposition lemma: V=\ker\operatorname{tr} is a real vector subspace of D. Identify \mathbb R1 with \mathbb R.

For a, b in V define B(a, b) = (−ab − ba)/2. Because of the identity (a + b)2 − a2 − b2 = ab + ba, it follows that B(a, b) is real. Furthermore, since a2 ≤ 0, we have: B(a, a) > 0 for a ≠ 0. Thus B is a positive-definite symmetric bilinear form, in other words, an inner product on V.
