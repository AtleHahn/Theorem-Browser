Statement

Let n\geq1 and k\geq0 be integers, let 1\leq p<\infty be real, and let \Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^n be nonempty and open. Integrals and almost-everywhere equivalence below use Lebesgue measure. For a multi-index \alpha=(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n) of nonnegative integers, put |\alpha|=\sum_i\alpha_i and D^\alpha=\partial_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots\partial_n^{\alpha_n}. A locally integrable v is the weak derivative D^\alpha u if

\int_\Omega uD^\alpha\psi\,dx=(-1)^{|\alpha|}\int_\Omega v\psi\,dx

for every infinitely differentiable function \psi with compact support in \Omega.

Define W^{k,p}(\Omega) to consist of almost-everywhere equivalence classes of functions u whose weak derivatives D^\alpha u belong to L^p(\Omega) for all |\alpha|\leq k, with D^0u=u and norm

\|u\|_{W^{k,p}}=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\left(\int_\Omega|D^\alpha u|^p\,dx\right)^{1/p}.

Then, for every u\in W^{k,p}(\Omega), there is a sequence u_j\in C^\infty(\Omega)\cap W^{k,p}(\Omega) such that

\|u_j-u\|_{W^{k,p}}\longrightarrow0.

Here C^\infty(\Omega) denotes the infinitely differentiable functions on \Omega; no regularity of the boundary of \Omega is required.
