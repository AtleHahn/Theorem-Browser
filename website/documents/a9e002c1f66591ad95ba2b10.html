Statement

Let F be a field, \overline F an algebraic closure, and a,b\in F[x]. There exist p,q\in F[x] with ap+bq=1 if and only if a and b have no common root in \overline F.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory field and polynomial algebra supplies roots and Bezout identities. An algebraic closure is a containing field in which polynomials split, and the claim uses only roots in that field, not extension degrees or Galois groups. This limited definition is a small addition to the baseline.

Proof 1: Polynomial gcd and root existence — outline

As the common roots of two polynomials are the roots of their greatest common divisor, Bézout's identity and the fact that every nonconstant polynomial over an algebraically closed field has a root imply the stated criterion.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof uses Euclidean polynomial gcds and their Bézout representation, standard introductory abstract algebra. The extra algebraic-closure notion is only a containing field in which every nonconstant polynomial splits; no construction of the closure, extension-degree calculations or Galois theory is used. A nonconstant gcd then supplies a common root, whereas gcd one supplies the identity. This is a small extension of polynomial algebra. If both polynomials are zero, both sides of the proposed equivalence are false, handled separately.

Convert absence of a nonconstant common factor into the unit identity.
