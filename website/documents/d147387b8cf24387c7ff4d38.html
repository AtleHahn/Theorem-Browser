Statement

For every w\in\mathbb C\setminus\mathbb Z, \pi\cot(\pi w)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{n=-N}^{N}\frac1{w-n}. Here \cot z=\cos z/\sin z, with \cos z=(e^{iz}+e^{-iz})/2 and \sin z=(e^{iz}-e^{-iz})/(2i).

Proof 1: Cancel the even contour integral

Let \Gamma_N be the rectangle that is the boundary of [-N-\tfrac12,N+\tfrac12]^2 with positive orientation, for a positive integer N. We use the facts that \pi\cot(\pi z) has simple poles with residue 1 at each integer, and that |\cot(\pi z)| is uniformly bounded on these contours.

Pick an arbitrary ⁠{\displaystyle w\in \mathbb {C} \setminus \mathbb {Z} }⁠. Define

{\displaystyle g(z):={\frac {1}{w-z}}\pi \cot(\pi z)}

By the Cauchy residue theorem, for all {\displaystyle N} large enough such that {\displaystyle \Gamma _{N}} encircles ⁠{\displaystyle w}⁠,

{\displaystyle {\frac {1}{2\pi i}}\oint _{\Gamma _{N}}g(z)dz=-\pi \cot(\pi w)+\sum _{n=-N}^{N}{\frac {1}{w-n}}}

It remains to prove the integral converges to zero. Since {\displaystyle \pi \cot(\pi z)/z} is an even function, and {\displaystyle \Gamma _{N}} is symmetric about the origin, we have ⁠{\displaystyle \oint _{\Gamma _{N}}\pi \cot(\pi z)/zdz=0}⁠, and so

{\displaystyle \oint _{\Gamma _{N}}g(z)dz=\oint _{\Gamma _{N}}\left({\frac {1}{z}}+{\frac {1}{w-z}}\right)\pi \cot(\pi z)dz=-w\oint _{\Gamma _{N}}{\frac {1}{z(z-w)}}\pi \cot(\pi z)dz=O(1/N)}

In the last estimate the denominator has order N^2 and the contour has length of order N. Letting N\to\infty gives the asserted expansion.
