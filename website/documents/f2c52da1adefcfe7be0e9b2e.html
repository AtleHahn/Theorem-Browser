Statement

On a measure space (S,\Sigma,\mu), write \|h\|_t=(\int_S|h|^t\,d\mu)^{1/t} for 0<t<\infty, and \|h\|_\infty=\operatorname{ess\,sup}|h|. Norms may be infinite; products with a zero factor are defined to be zero, including 0\cdot\infty. Let 1\le p\le\infty and q be conjugate, with f\in L^p(\mu). Then \kappa_f(g)=\int fg\,d\mu is a bounded linear functional on L^q(\mu) and \|\kappa_f\|\le\|f\|_p. Equality holds if p<\infty, and also for p=\infty when every infinite-measure measurable set contains a positive finite-measure subset. The operator norm is \sup_{\|g\|_q\le1}|\kappa_f(g)|.

Proof 1: Norm of the integration functional associated with an Lp function

Hölder gives |\kappa_f(g)|\le\|f\|_p\|g\|_q, which gives boundedness; linearity follows from the integral. Under the stated hypotheses, the extremal norm characterization says that the supremum over the unit ball equals \|f\|_p.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Use Holder for boundedness of the integral functional and the extremal characterization for its norm. General Lebesgue spaces, essential suprema and their dual functionals require advanced analysis.
