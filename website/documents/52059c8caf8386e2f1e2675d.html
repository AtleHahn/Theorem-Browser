Proof 1: Triangle altitudes are inversely proportional to their bases — source argument

Altitudes can be used in the computation of the area of a triangle: one-half of the product of an altitude's length and its base's length (symbol b) equals the triangle's area: A=hb/2. Thus, the longest altitude is perpendicular to the shortest side of the triangle.

Any two altitudes of a triangle are inversely proportional with the sides on which they fall.

Level
High school only
Level justification
