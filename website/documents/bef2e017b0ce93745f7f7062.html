Statement

Write i^2=-1 and e^z=\sum_{m=0}^\infty z^m/m! for the complex exponential. Define \cos z=\sum_{m\ge0}(-1)^m z^{2m}/(2m)! and \sin z=\sum_{m\ge0}(-1)^m z^{2m+1}/(2m+1)!. For every complex z, e^{iz}=\cos z+i\sin z.

Level justification

Proof 1: Separate even and odd complex exponential terms

Here is a proof of Euler's formula using power-series expansions, as well as basic facts about the powers of i:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}i^{0}&=1,&i^{1}&=i,&i^{2}&=-1,&i^{3}&=-i,\\i^{4}&=1,&i^{5}&=i,&i^{6}&=-1,&i^{7}&=-i\\&\vdots &&\vdots &&\vdots &&\vdots \end{aligned}}}

Using now the power-series definition in the statement, we see that for complex values of z

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}e^{iz}&=1+iz+{\frac {(iz)^{2}}{2!}}+{\frac {(iz)^{3}}{3!}}+{\frac {(iz)^{4}}{4!}}+{\frac {(iz)^{5}}{5!}}+{\frac {(iz)^{6}}{6!}}+{\frac {(iz)^{7}}{7!}}+{\frac {(iz)^{8}}{8!}}+\cdots \\[8pt]&=1+iz-{\frac {z^{2}}{2!}}-{\frac {iz^{3}}{3!}}+{\frac {z^{4}}{4!}}+{\frac {iz^{5}}{5!}}-{\frac {z^{6}}{6!}}-{\frac {iz^{7}}{7!}}+{\frac {z^{8}}{8!}}+\cdots \\[8pt]&=\left(1-{\frac {z^{2}}{2!}}+{\frac {z^{4}}{4!}}-{\frac {z^{6}}{6!}}+{\frac {z^{8}}{8!}}-\cdots \right)+i\left(z-{\frac {z^{3}}{3!}}+{\frac {z^{5}}{5!}}-{\frac {z^{7}}{7!}}+\cdots \right)\\[8pt]&=\cos z+i\sin z,\end{aligned}}}

where in the last step we recognize the two terms are the Maclaurin series for cos z and sin z. The splitting into two series is justified since we know the series for sine and cosine converge.
