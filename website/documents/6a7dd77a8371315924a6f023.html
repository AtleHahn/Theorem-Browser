Statement

Let H be a complex Hilbert space, B(H) its algebra of bounded linear operators, and A\subseteq B(H) an algebra closed under adjoints. No norm-closure or identity hypothesis is imposed on A. Write \overline A^{\mathrm s} for its strong-operator closure and A_1=\{a\in A:\|a\|\le1\}. Then

\overline{A_1}^{\mathrm s}=\{a\in\overline A^{\mathrm s}:\|a\|\le1\}.

Moreover every self-adjoint h\in\overline A^{\mathrm s} with \|h\|\le1 is a strong limit of a net of self-adjoint elements h_\alpha\in A with \|h_\alpha\|\le1.

The operator norm is \|a\|=\sup_{\|\xi\|\le1}\|a\xi\|. Strong convergence means a_\alpha\xi\to a\xi in H for every \xi\in H; a net is indexed by a directed set, in which every two indices have a common upper bound. The adjoint is determined by \langle a\xi,\eta\rangle=\langle\xi,a^*\eta\rangle, and self-adjoint means a=a^*.

Proof availability

The source sketches self-adjoint approximation using functional calculus and then gives a matrix reduction to the general case. It does not establish the self-adjoint approximation step for an arbitrary, possibly non-norm-closed algebra: the functional-calculus approximants need not belong to that algebra. The usable matrix reduction is preserved in the existing general-unit-ball entry, where self-adjoint density is explicitly a prerequisite; it does not prove both clauses here.
