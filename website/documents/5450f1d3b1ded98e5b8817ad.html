Statement

Let (B_n) be a finite or countable measurable partition of a probability space, and let A,C be events with P(C)>0. If C is independent of every B_n, then P(A\mid C)=\sum_{n:P(B_n)>0}P(A\mid C\cap B_n)P(B_n), where conditional probability is defined by the ratio of intersection mass to conditioning mass.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The conditional probabilities are defined as ratios of event probabilities, and the partition consists of disjoint events. Countable additivity and ordinary nonnegative series explain the sum. These closely related probability rules are a limited addition to undergraduate sets and analysis, without conditional expectations of functions.

Proof 1: Replace conditional partition weights by unconditional weights

The law of total probability for conditional probabilities gives

P(A\mid C)= \sum_{n:P(B_n\cap C)>0}P(A\mid B_n\cap C)P(B_n\mid C).

Taking the B_n to be the partition in the statement, and assuming C is an event independent of each of the B_n, we have P(B_n\mid C)=P(B_n) and therefore

P(A\mid C)= \sum_{n:P(B_n)>0}P(A\mid C\cap B_n)P(B_n).

Terms with zero conditioning probability are omitted.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the displayed conditional total-probability formula and independence to replace each weight by P(B_n). Positive P(C) makes the retained cells exactly the positive-probability cells. Elementary probability and countable nonnegative sums suffice; no sigma-algebra conditional expectation is used.
