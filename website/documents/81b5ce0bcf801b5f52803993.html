Statement

Let (X_t)_{t\in[a,b]} be a centered real process on a probability space (\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P), with a<b, X_t\in L^2(\Omega) and continuous covariance K(s,t)=\mathbb E(X_sX_t). Define (Tf)(t)=\int_a^b K(s,t)f(s)\,ds on real L^2([a,b]). Let (\lambda_k,e_k) denote its positive eigenvalues and real orthonormal eigenfunctions, repeated by multiplicity, and put Z_k=\int_a^b X_t e_k(t)\,dt, with the integral understood in L^2(\Omega). Finite lists are allowed. Order \lambda_k nonincreasingly and assume V=\mathbb E\|X\|_{L^2([a,b])}^2>0. For any real orthonormal basis (\varphi_k) with coefficients W_k=\int_a^b X_t\varphi_k(t)\,dt, set p_k=\lambda_k/V and q_k=\mathbb E|W_k|^2/V. Then -\sum_k p_k\log p_k\leq-\sum_k q_k\log q_k, allowing +\infty and using 0\log0=0.

Proof 1: Projection error implies majorization (outline)

Denote the coefficients obtained for the basis e_k(t) as p_k, and for \varphi_k(t) as q_k, as in the statement. Write \langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle and \|\cdot\| for the real L^2([a,b]) inner product and norm.

Choose N\geq1. If there are only r positive eigenvalues, sums involving Z_k e_k or |Z_k|^2 stop at \min(N,r), and p_k=0 for k>r. Use the KL minimum integrated mean-square error theorem as a prerequisite: since e_k minimizes the mean squared error, we have that

\mathbb E\left\|\sum_{k=1}^N Z_ke_k-X\right\|^2 \leq \mathbb E\left\|\sum_{k=1}^N W_k\varphi_k-X\right\|^2.

Expanding the right hand side, we get:

\begin{aligned} \mathbb E\left\|\sum_{k=1}^N W_k\varphi_k-X\right\|^2 ={}&\mathbb E\|X\|^2 +\sum_{k=1}^N\sum_{\ell=1}^N\mathbb E[W_\ell W_k]\langle\varphi_\ell,\varphi_k\rangle\\ &-\sum_{k=1}^N\mathbb E[W_k\langle\varphi_k,X\rangle] -\sum_{k=1}^N\mathbb E[W_k\langle X,\varphi_k\rangle]. \end{aligned}

Using the orthonormality of \varphi_k(t), and expanding X in the \varphi_k(t) basis, we get that the right hand side is equal to:

\mathbb E\|X\|^2-\sum_{k=1}^N\mathbb E[|W_k|^2].

We may perform identical analysis for the e_k(t), and so rewrite the above inequality as:

\mathbb E\|X\|^2-\sum_{k=1}^N\mathbb E[|Z_k|^2] \leq \mathbb E\|X\|^2-\sum_{k=1}^N\mathbb E[|W_k|^2].

Subtracting the common first term, and dividing by \mathbb E\|X\|^2=V>0, we obtain that:

\sum_{k=1}^Np_k\geq\sum_{k=1}^Nq_k.

For each N, place N largest q-values first by a finite reordering of the comparison basis; the same inequality holds for their sum. Both sequences sum to one, so p majorizes q. If the list of positive eigenvalues is finite, append zeros to p. By the Schur-concavity of countable Shannon entropy, this implies that:

-\sum_{k=1}^\infty p_k\log(p_k)\leq-\sum_{k=1}^\infty q_k\log(q_k).
