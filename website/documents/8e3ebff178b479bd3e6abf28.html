Statement

There is a map f:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R^6 that preserves every unit distance but does not preserve all Euclidean distances.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves finite-dimensional Euclidean spaces; distance-preserving functions. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Map colors to simplex vertices — outline

Use the known seven-coloring of the Euclidean plane in which no two points at unit distance have the same color.

The Euclidean plane can be colored with seven colors, using a tiling by hexagons of slightly less than unit diameter, so that no two points of the same color are a unit distance apart. Then the points of the plane can be mapped by their colors to the seven vertices of a unit-edge six-dimensional regular simplex.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Map the seven color classes to the unit-simplex vertices and note that the finite image cannot preserve every distance. Undergraduate Euclidean linear algebra and the explicit coloring theorem suffice.
