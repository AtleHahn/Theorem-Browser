Statement

A finite simple graph of maximum degree two admits a proper edge coloring with two colors if and only if all of its cycle components have even length. A proper edge coloring gives different colors to any two edges with a common endpoint.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement characterizes two-colorability by the parity of cycle lengths.

Proof 1: Alternating colors on paths and cycles

When \Delta=2, the graph G must be a disjoint union of paths and cycles (with isolated vertices allowed). If all cycles are even, they can be 2-edge-colored by alternating the two colors around each cycle. However, if there exists at least one odd cycle, then no 2-edge-coloring is possible.

Paths are colored by the same alternation. Here \Delta denotes the maximum degree.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Classify components and alternate two colors along each path or cycle, with odd cycles obstructing closure. Finite graph traversal and parity suffice.
