Statement

For a simple symmetric random walk of even length n=2r\ge0, the number of paths that remain nonnegative and return to zero is \frac1{r+1}\binom{2r}r. Their probability is 2^{-2r}\frac1{r+1}\binom{2r}r.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The finite path count and probability are given by factorial arithmetic.

Proof 1: Set the endpoint to zero in the path count

The number of random walks on the integers that consist of n steps of unit length, beginning at the origin and ending at the point m, that never become negative is, for n\geq m\geq0 of the same parity,

\binom n{(n+m)/2}-\binom n{(n+m)/2+1} =\frac{m+1}{(n+m)/2+1}\binom n{(n+m)/2}.

Here \binom Nk=0 for integers k<0 or k>N.

When m=0 and n is even, this gives the Catalan number

\frac1{n/2+1}\binom n{n/2}.

Thus the probability that a simple symmetric random walk is never negative and returns to the origin at time n is

2^{-n}\frac1{n/2+1}\binom n{n/2}.

Putting n=2r gives the stated formulas.

High school only
Level justification

Set the endpoint m to zero in the explicit finite path count and divide by the 2^n equally likely walks. School counting, binomial coefficients and finite probability suffice.
