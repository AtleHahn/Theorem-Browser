Statement

Let K be a number field with ring of integers \mathcal O_K, let \mathfrak p be a nonzero prime ideal, and let \zeta\in\mathcal O_K be a primitive nth root of unity, where n\ge1 and n\notin\mathfrak p. Put q=|\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak p|. Then q\equiv1\pmod n.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Proof 1: Prime ideal norm is one modulo the root-of-unity order — proof

Use the fact that no two distinct nth roots of unity can be congruent modulo \mathfrak p. Thus the residue classes of \mathcal O_K/\mathfrak p containing the powers of \zeta are a subgroup of order n of its multiplicative group of units,

(\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak p)^\times=\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak p\setminus\{0\}.

Therefore, by Lagrange's theorem, the order of (\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak p)^\times is a multiple of n, and

\begin{aligned} q&=|\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak p|\\ &=|(\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak p)^\times|+1\\ &\equiv1\pmod n. \end{aligned}

Level justification
