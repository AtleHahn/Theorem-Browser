Statement

Let a real cubic have one real root r and two nonreal roots g\pm ih, h>0. If these three complex points form an equilateral triangle, then its derivative has a repeated real root at the triangle’s center.

Proof 1: Specialize Marden’s ellipse to the incircle

Use Marden’s theorem: for a cubic whose three roots form a nondegenerate triangle, the roots of its derivative are the two foci, counted with multiplicity, of the Steiner inellipse, the ellipse tangent at the three side midpoints. For an equilateral triangle this ellipse is its incircle, and both foci coincide at the center. The center lies on the real axis by conjugate symmetry, so the derivative has a duplicate real root.
