Statement

For a linear bijection T:X\to Y between Fréchet spaces, T is continuous if and only if T^{-1} is continuous. In either case T is an open map and an isomorphism of topological vector spaces. This includes Banach spaces.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

General locally convex complete metrizable vector spaces are outside calculus/linear algebra.

Proof 1: Swapping the two graph coordinates

Use the closed graph theorem for Fréchet spaces: a linear map defined on the whole space is continuous exactly when its graph is closed. The coordinate-swap homeomorphism X\times Y\to Y\times X takes the graph of T to the graph of T^{-1}. Thus one graph is closed if and only if the other is. Applying the closed graph theorem in both directions gives the equivalence, and a continuous linear bijection with continuous inverse is a topological vector-space isomorphism. Consequently T is open, since the image under T of an open set is its preimage under the continuous inverse.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The graph swap is elementary topology, but the proof invokes the closed graph theorem in the full Fréchet-space setting. Complete metrizable locally convex spaces and their linear-map theory require substantial functional-analysis background.
