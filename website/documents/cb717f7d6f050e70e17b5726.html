Statement

For an irrational real algebraic number \alpha (a real root of a nonzero polynomial with integer coefficients), define \mu(\alpha) as the supremum of real \mu for which |\alpha-p/q|<q^{-\mu} holds for infinitely many coprime integers p,q with q>0. Then \mu(\alpha)=2.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Real algebraic numbers as polynomial roots; Supremum and rational approximation exponents. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Combine Dirichlet’s lower bound with Roth’s upper bound

Dirichlet’s approximation theorem gives infinitely many reduced approximations with |\alpha-p/q|<q^{-2}, so \mu(\alpha)\ge2. Roth’s finite-approximation theorem says that for every \varepsilon>0 only finitely many reduced fractions satisfy |\alpha-p/q|<q^{-(2+\varepsilon)}, so \mu(\alpha)\le2. Combining the two bounds gives equality.
