Statement

Let n\ge1 and 0<x_i\le1/2. Define A=n^{-1}\sum_i x_i, G=(\prod_i x_i)^{1/n}, A'=n^{-1}\sum_i(1-x_i) and G'=(\prod_i(1-x_i))^{1/n}. Then G/G'\le A/A'.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The stated inequality concerns finite positive real means only.

Proof 1: Logarithmic specialization of Levinson’s inequality — outline

Use Levinson’s inequality: if a>0, f^{(3)}(x)\ge0 on (0,2a), 0<x_i\le a, and p_i>0, write P=\sum_i p_i. Then

\frac{\sum_i p_i f(x_i)}P-f\!\left(\frac{\sum_i p_i x_i}P\right)\le \frac{\sum_i p_i f(2a-x_i)}P-f\!\left(\frac{\sum_i p_i(2a-x_i)}P\right).

The source specifies p_i=1, a=1/2, f(x)=\log x. This gives

\frac1n\sum_i\log x_i-\log A\le\frac1n\sum_i\log(1-x_i)-\log A',

where A=n^{-1}\sum_i x_i and A'=n^{-1}\sum_i(1-x_i). Exponentiating yields G/A\le G'/A', equivalently G/G'\le A/A'.
