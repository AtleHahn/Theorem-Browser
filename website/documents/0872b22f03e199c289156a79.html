Statement

Let n\geq1 be an integer and let \mu be Lebesgue measure on \mathbb R^n. If A,B\subseteq\mathbb R^n are nonempty Lebesgue-measurable sets and their Minkowski sum

A+B=\{a+b:a\in A,\ b\in B\}

is Lebesgue measurable, then

\mu(A+B)^{1/n}\geq\mu(A)^{1/n}+\mu(B)^{1/n}.

The sets need not be bounded, and the inequality is interpreted in the nonnegative extended real numbers when a measure is infinite.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The full arbitrary Lebesgue-measurable scope requires measurability, infinite measure and the distinction between measurable sets and potentially nonmeasurable sums. This is more than a confidently short extension of elementary volume and introductory analysis; the conservative classification is beyond undergraduate.

Proof availability

The geometric argument proves compact and bounded measurable cases, then truncates arbitrary measurable sets and applies the bounded case without establishing measurability of the truncated sums. Measurability of the original sum does not supply that missing hypothesis. The alternative Prékopa–Leindler proof also has an unsupported general one-dimensional reduction. No complete proof in the full stated generality is retained, and no substitute approximation argument has been invented.
