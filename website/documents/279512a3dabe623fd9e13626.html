Statement

For two circles with distinct centers and radical axis m, their common points are exactly the points where m meets either circle. Thus, given constructions for the radical axis and line-circle intersections, their intersection points can be constructed.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Points, lines, circles, parallels and perpendiculars are elementary geometry.

Proof 1: Reduce to the equal-power locus

Given constructions for the radical axis and line-circle intersections, the common points U,V are obtained as follows (there may be fewer than two).

  1. Construct the radical axis, line m, between the two circles.
  2. Construct the intersection points, U and V, between the radical axis, line m, and either one of the two circles arbitrarily chosen.
    • The radical axis is a line, so this construction is possible using the assumed line-circle intersection construction.
  3. These points are the desired points of intersection of the circles.
    • The two circles and the radical axis all intersect at the same loci of points: two distinct points, one point if they are tangent, or none if they do not intersect.
    • If the radical axis does not intersect one circle then it intersects neither, and neither do the two circles intersect.
Level
High school only
Level justification

On either circle the power is zero. Equal power on the radical axis therefore means membership in the other circle as well. This is elementary circle geometry and the stated constructions.
