Statement

Let Ω⊂R^n be open and g a positive-definite C4 Riemannian metric on Ω whose scalar curvature is not C3. Then no C5 immersion f:Ω→R^N, for any N, has induced metric g_{ij}=\sum_\alpha\partial_i f^\alpha\partial_j f^\alpha.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Riemannian scalar curvature and regularity of isometric immersions require substantial differential geometry. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Compare regularity in the Gauss equation

If f is an immersion with induced metric g, then

R^g=|H(f)|^2-|h(f)|_g^2,

where R^g is the scalar curvature of the Riemannian metric g, h(f) denotes its second fundamental form, and H(f) denotes its trace (the mean-curvature vector in this convention); the above equation is the Gauss equation. There is thus a second-order differential operator of g which coincides with a second-order differential operator applied to f.

If f were C^5, then |H|^2-|h|^2 would have to be at least C^3. This contradicts the hypothesis that R^g is not C^3.

The regularity count depends on scalar curvature, second fundamental forms and metric contractions for an isometric immersion. This requires a substantial differential-geometric framework beyond multivariable calculus.
