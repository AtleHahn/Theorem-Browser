Statement

Positive numbers a,b,c are the side lengths of a Euclidean triangle allowing a collinear degenerate triangle exactly when each is at most the sum of the other two. The triangle can be nondegenerate exactly when all three inequalities are strict.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement asks when three positive lengths form an ordinary or degenerate triangle.

Proof 1: Construct the third vertex from its height

Let a,b,c>0 satisfy all three non-strict triangle inequalities. Place A=(0,0) and B=(c,0), and attempt to place C=(d,h) with AC=b and BC=a. We need to prove that there exists a real number h consistent with the values a,b,c, in which case this triangle exists.

By the Pythagorean theorem we have b^2=h^2+d^2 and a^2=h^2+(c-d)^2. Subtracting these yields a^2-b^2=c^2-2cd. This equation allows us to express d in terms of the sides of the triangle:

d=\frac{-a^2+b^2+c^2}{2c}.

For the height of the triangle we have h^2=b^2-d^2. By replacing d with the formula given above, we have

h^2=b^2-\left(\frac{-a^2+b^2+c^2}{2c}\right)^2.

For a real number h to satisfy this, h^2 must be non-negative. Since 4c^2 and a+b+c are positive, the following inequalities are equivalent:

\begin{aligned} 0&\le b^2-\left(\frac{-a^2+b^2+c^2}{2c}\right)^2,\\ 0&\le\left(b-\frac{-a^2+b^2+c^2}{2c}\right) \left(b+\frac{-a^2+b^2+c^2}{2c}\right),\\ 0&\le\bigl(a^2-(b-c)^2\bigr)\bigl((b+c)^2-a^2\bigr),\\ 0&\le(a+b-c)(a-b+c)(b+c+a)(b+c-a),\\ 0&\le(a+b-c)(a+c-b)(b+c-a). \end{aligned}

The last inequality holds if the triangle inequality is satisfied for all sides. Therefore, there does exist a real number h\ge0 consistent with the sides a,b,c, and the triangle exists. If each triangle inequality holds strictly, h>0 and the triangle is nondegenerate (has positive area); but if one of the inequalities holds with equality, h=0 and the triangle is degenerate. Conversely, any triangle satisfies the non-strict triangle inequalities, and a nondegenerate triangle satisfies all three strictly.
