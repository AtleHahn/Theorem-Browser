Statement

On a measure space (S,\mu), let 1\le p\le\infty and let f,g be real- or complex-valued elements of L^p. Then f+g\in L^p and \|f+g\|_p\le\|f\|_p+\|g\|_p, where the finite-p norm is (\int|f|^p\,d\mu)^{1/p} and the infinity norm is the essential supremum.

Proof 1: Hölder estimate with integrability check — outline

For p=1, integrate |f+g|\leq|f|+|g|; for p=\infty, take essential suprema. Suppose 1<p<\infty.

First, we prove that {\textstyle f+g} has finite {\textstyle p}-norm if {\textstyle f} and {\textstyle g} both do, which follows by

{\displaystyle |f+g|^{p}\leq 2^{p-1}(|f|^{p}+|g|^{p}).}

Indeed, here we use the fact that {\textstyle h(x)=|x|^{p}} is convex over {\textstyle \mathbb {R} ^{+}} (for {\textstyle p>1}) and so, by the definition of convexity,

{\displaystyle \left|{\tfrac {1}{2}}f+{\tfrac {1}{2}}g\right|^{p}\leq \left|{\tfrac {1}{2}}|f|+{\tfrac {1}{2}}|g|\right|^{p}\leq {\tfrac {1}{2}}|f|^{p}+{\tfrac {1}{2}}|g|^{p}.}

This means that

{\displaystyle |f+g|^{p}\leq {\tfrac {1}{2}}|2f|^{p}+{\tfrac {1}{2}}|2g|^{p}=2^{p-1}|f|^{p}+2^{p-1}|g|^{p}.}

Now, we can legitimately talk about {\textstyle \|f+g\|_{p}}. If it is zero, then Minkowski's inequality holds. We now assume that {\textstyle \|f+g\|_{p}} is not zero. Using the triangle inequality and then Hölder's inequality, we find that

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\|f+g\|_{p}^{p}&=\int |f+g|^{p}\,\mathrm {d} \mu \\&=\int |f+g|\cdot |f+g|^{p-1}\,\mathrm {d} \mu \\&\leq \int (|f|+|g|)|f+g|^{p-1}\,\mathrm {d} \mu \\&=\int |f||f+g|^{p-1}\,\mathrm {d} \mu +\int |g||f+g|^{p-1}\,\mathrm {d} \mu \\&\leq \left(\left(\int |f|^{p}\,\mathrm {d} \mu \right)^{\frac {1}{p}}+\left(\int |g|^{p}\,\mathrm {d} \mu \right)^{\frac {1}{p}}\right)\left(\int |f+g|^{(p-1)\left({\frac {p}{p-1}}\right)}\,\mathrm {d} \mu \right)^{1-{\frac {1}{p}}}&&{\text{ Hölder's inequality}}\\&=\left(\|f\|_{p}+\|g\|_{p}\right){\frac {\|f+g\|_{p}^{p}}{\|f+g\|_{p}}}\end{aligned}}}

We obtain Minkowski's inequality by multiplying both sides by

{\displaystyle {\frac {\|f+g\|_{p}}{\|f+g\|_{p}^{p}}}.}

Proof 2: Norm-weighted convex combination — outline

For p=1, integrate |f+g|\leq|f|+|g|; for p=\infty, take essential suprema. Suppose 1<p<\infty.

If either input has zero norm, the inequality is immediate. Suppose both norms are positive.

Given {\displaystyle t\in (0,1)}, one has, by convexity (Jensen's inequality), for every {\displaystyle x\in S}

{\displaystyle |f(x)+g(x)|^{p}={\Bigl |}(1-t){\frac {f(x)}{1-t}}+t{\frac {g(x)}{t}}{\Bigr |}^{p}\leq (1-t){\Bigl |}{\frac {f(x)}{1-t}}{\Bigr |}^{p}+t{\Bigl |}{\frac {g(x)}{t}}{\Bigr |}^{p}={\frac {|f(x)|^{p}}{(1-t)^{p-1}}}+{\frac {|g(x)|^{p}}{t^{p-1}}}.}

By integration this leads to

{\displaystyle \int _{S}|f+g|^{p}\,\mathrm {d} \mu \leq {\frac {1}{(1-t)^{p-1}}}\int _{S}|f|^{p}\,\mathrm {d} \mu +{\frac {1}{t^{p-1}}}\int _{S}|g|^{p}\,\mathrm {d} \mu .}

One takes then

{\displaystyle t={\frac {\Vert g\Vert _{p}}{\Vert f\Vert _{p}+\Vert g\Vert _{p}}}}

to reach the conclusion.
