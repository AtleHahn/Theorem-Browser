Statement

Define \chi(X)=\sum_{j\ge0}(-1)^j b_j(X), where b_j(X)=\dim_{\mathbb Q}H_j(X;\mathbb Q); these dimensions are finite and vanish for all sufficiently large j. For every integer n\ge1, \chi(\mathbb{RP}^n)=(1+(-1)^n)/2, hence is 0 for odd n and 1 for even n. Here \mathbb{RP}^n is the antipodal quotient of S^n.

Proof 1: Use the two-sheeted antipodal cover

Use \chi(S^n)=1+(-1)^n and the covering formula \chi(\text{cover})=(\text{number of sheets})\,\chi(\text{base}).

The n-dimensional real projective space is the quotient of the n-sphere by the antipodal map. It follows that its Euler characteristic is exactly half that of the corresponding sphere – either 0 or 1.

Level justification

Apply finite-cover multiplicativity to the antipodal sphere covering. The statement defines Euler characteristic through rational homology, so homology and covering-space topology remain substantive prerequisites beyond the elementary final division by two.
