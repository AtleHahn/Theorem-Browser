Statement

Let E,N_0>0 and suppose a real statistic G has law N(0,N_0E/2) under H and N(E,N_0E/2) under K. For 0<\alpha<1, the rule G>G_0 has probability \alpha under H when G_0=\sqrt{N_0E/2}\,\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha), and its probability under K is \Phi(\sqrt{2E/N_0}-\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha)), where \Phi is the standard normal distribution function.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The standard normal law is given by its elementary Gaussian density, and independence gives finite products of such laws. Calculus and linear algebra then interpret quantiles or matrix norm inequalities. These limited finite-dimensional probability additions do not require general stochastic processes.

Proof 1: Standardize the two Gaussian tails

In the integrals below, N(m,v) denotes the normal density with mean m and variance v, evaluated at the integration variable G.

The false alarm error

{\displaystyle \alpha =\int _{G_{0}}^{\infty }N\left(0,{\tfrac {1}{2}}N_{0}E\right)\,dG\Rightarrow G_{0}={\sqrt {{\tfrac {1}{2}}N_{0}E}}\Phi ^{-1}(1-\alpha )}

And the probability of detection:

{\displaystyle \beta =\int _{G_{0}}^{\infty }N\left(E,{\tfrac {1}{2}}N_{0}E\right)\,dG=1-\Phi \left({\frac {G_{0}-E}{\sqrt {{\tfrac {1}{2}}N_{0}E}}}\right)=\Phi \left({\sqrt {\frac {2E}{N_{0}}}}-\Phi ^{-1}(1-\alpha )\right),}

where Φ is the cdf of standard normal, or Gaussian, variable.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Standardize each normal law, solve for the quantile threshold and use normal symmetry. This is ordinary undergraduate probability and statistics.
