Statement

Let k\ge1 be an integer. Let x_1,\ldots,x_k be distinct elements of a field K, Q(X)=\prod_i(X-x_i) and Q_i(X)=Q(X)/(X-x_i). Then in K(X) one has 1/Q(X)=\sum_i1/[Q_i(x_i)(X-x_i)].

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Polynomial substitution, ring centers, simple rings and rational partial fractions are introductory algebra. Invariance is explicitly a polynomial equality, and simple means no nontrivial two-sided ideals; no further structure is needed.

Proof 1: A low-degree numerator equal to one at every node

In fact, reducing the right-hand side to a common denominator one gets

{\displaystyle \sum _{i=1}^{k}{\frac {1}{Q_{i}(x_{i})(X-x_{i})}}={\frac {1}{Q(X)}}\sum _{i=1}^{k}{\frac {Q_{i}(X)}{Q_{i}(x_{i})}},}

and the numerator is equal to one, as being a polynomial of degree less than {\displaystyle k,} which takes the value one for {\displaystyle k} different values of {\displaystyle X.}

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Put the fractions over a common denominator and apply the root bound to the difference of the numerator and one. Undergraduate polynomial algebra over fields suffices.
