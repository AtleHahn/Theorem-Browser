Statement

Let C be a smooth projective geometrically connected curve over a field k, of genus g=\dim_k H^1(C,\mathcal O_C). If E is a vector bundle of rank n and degree d=\deg c_1(E), then

\dim_k H^0(C,E)-\dim_k H^1(C,E)=d+n(1-g).
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Vector-bundle degree, Chern classes and coherent cohomology of projective curves require substantial algebraic geometry. Proof 1: Riemann–Roch for vector bundles on a curve

Take X=C and Y=\operatorname{Spec}k a point, with f:C\to Y the structure map. Write h^i(C,E)=\dim_kH^i(C,E) and \chi(C,E)=h^0(C,E)-h^1(C,E). The Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch formula can be read as

\begin{aligned} \operatorname{ch}(f_!E)&=h^0(C,E)-h^1(C,E),\\ f_*(\operatorname{ch}(E)\operatorname{td}(X)) &=f_*((n+c_1(E))(1+(1/2)c_1(T_C)))\\ &=f_*(n+c_1(E)+(n/2)c_1(T_C))\\ &=f_*(c_1(E)+(n/2)c_1(T_C))\\ &=d+n(1-g); \end{aligned}

hence,

\chi(C,E)=d+n(1-g).

Here f_!E is the alternating sum of the cohomology spaces in the Grothendieck group, \operatorname{ch} is the Chern character, \operatorname{td}(X) is the Todd class of the tangent bundle, and f_* takes the degree of the codimension-one component. Products of higher codimension vanish on the curve. The last equality uses \deg c_1(T_C)=2-2g.
