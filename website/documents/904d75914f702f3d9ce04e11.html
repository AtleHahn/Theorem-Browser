Statement

Let X\subset\mathbb{CP}^N be a complex projective algebraic variety of complex dimension n\ge1, and let Y be a hyperplane section such that U=X\setminus Y is smooth. The inclusion Y\hookrightarrow X induces an isomorphism on \pi_k for 0\le k<n-1 and a surjection for k=n-1. Here \pi_0 denotes the set of path components, and for k\ge1 a basepoint in Y and its image in X are understood.

Proof 1: Cell attachment and the relative exact sequence (outline)

Thom and Bott interpret Y as the vanishing locus in X of a section of a line bundle. An application of Morse theory to this section implies that X can be constructed, up to homotopy relative to Y, from Y by adjoining cells of dimension n or more. We use this cell-attachment conclusion as an external theorem.

The standard relative cellular approximation theorem gives \pi_j(X,Y)=0 for j<n. The relative homotopy exact sequence therefore makes \pi_k(Y)\to\pi_k(X) an isomorphism for k<n-1 and a surjection for k=n-1. The degree-zero assertion has its usual path-component meaning: cells of dimension at least two do not change path components, while attachment of positive-dimensional cells introduces no new component disjoint from Y.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply relative cellular approximation to the geometric attachment theorem, then use the relative homotopy sequence, treating path components separately. Higher homotopy groups and complex projective geometry are graduate prerequisites.
