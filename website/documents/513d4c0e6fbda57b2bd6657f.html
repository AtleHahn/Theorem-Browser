Statement

Let X\subseteq\bar X be a menu, f:\bar X\times[0,1]\to\mathbb R, and let x^*(t)\in X maximize f(x,t) over X for every t. Suppose f(x,\cdot) is differentiable and absolutely continuous for all x\in\bar X, and b(t)=\sup_{x\in\bar X}|f_t(x,t)| is integrable. Then for V(t)=f(x^*(t),t), V(t)=V(0)+\int_0^t f_t(x^*(s),s)\,ds.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The chosen outcomes range over an arbitrary menu and the conclusion is a Lebesgue integral utility identity.

Proof 1: Restrict the derivative bound to the menu

We use the integral envelope theorem: if every objective is differentiable and absolutely continuous in the scalar parameter, their derivatives have a common integrable bound, the value is finite, and an optimum is attained almost everywhere, then the value is absolutely continuous and its change is the integral of the selected objective derivative.

Consider an agent whose utility function f(x,t) over outcomes x\in\bar X depends on his type t\in[0,1]. Let X\subseteq\bar X represent the “menu” of possible outcomes the agent could obtain in the mechanism by sending different messages. The agent's equilibrium utility in the mechanism is

V(t)=\max_{x\in X}f(x,t),

and the set of the mechanism's equilibrium outcomes is

X^*(t)=\mathop{\rm argmax}_{x\in X}f(x,t).

Any selection x^*(t)\in X^*(t) is a choice rule implemented by the mechanism. Suppose that the agent's utility function f(x,t) is differentiable and absolutely continuous in t for all x\in\bar X, and that \sup_{x\in\bar X}|f_t(x,t)| is integrable on [0,1]. Then the integral envelope theorem implies that the agent's equilibrium utility V in any mechanism implementing a given choice rule x^* must satisfy the integral condition

V(t)=V(0)+\int_0^t f_t(x^*(s),s)\,ds.

Level justification

Restrict the common derivative bound to the menu and invoke the integral envelope theorem. Absolute continuity, almost-everywhere selection and Lebesgue-integrable domination require advanced real analysis beyond the elementary differentiable envelope formula.
