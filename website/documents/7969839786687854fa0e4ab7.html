Statement

Let X be a normal topological space, meaning that any two disjoint closed subsets have disjoint open neighborhoods. Let A\subseteq X be closed and f:A\to\mathbb R continuous. Then there is a continuous function F:X\to\mathbb R with F|_A=f.

If f is bounded, F can be chosen to satisfy

\sup_{x\in X}|F(x)|=\sup_{a\in A}|f(a)|,

where the supremum of the absolute values on an empty domain is taken as zero. No boundedness assumption on f is required for the extension assertion.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Undergraduate point-set topology suffices, with normality defined explicitly; no advanced extension machinery is needed to read the statement.

Proof 1: Geometric corrections and an unbounded cutoff (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorem15.41 and Corollary15.42 p.148; Theorem7.4 pp.35–36; Urysohn proof pp.138–139,147–148).

The construction uses only the stated separation property: for closed C\subseteq U with U open, separating C and X\setminus U gives an open V with C\subseteq V\subseteq\overline V\subseteq U. This supplies all nested neighborhoods in Urysohn’s lemma without Hausdorffness. In Corollary15.42, extend \arctan f to G:X\to[-\pi/2,\pi/2], and choose h:X\to[0,1] equal to 1 on A and 0 where |G|=\pi/2. Then |hG|<\pi/2 everywhere and F=\tan(hG) extends f. For bounded f with s=\sup_A|f|>0, use Theorem15.41 on [-s,s]; restriction to A gives the opposite norm inequality. If s=0 or A is empty, take F=0.
