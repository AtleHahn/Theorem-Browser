Statement

For a finite triangulation T of the closed unit ball B_n⊆R^n, assume its boundary triangulation is antipodally symmetric. Label every vertex by a nonzero integer L(v), with L(−v)=−L(v) on the boundary. An edge is complementary if its labels are a and −a. An n-simplex is alternating if its n+1 labels have distinct absolute values and their signs alternate when ordered by absolute value. For n≥1, if every label belongs to {−n,…,−1,1,…,n}, then T has a complementary edge.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Arbitrary-dimensional ball triangulations and simplices require a limited geometric extension of undergraduate linear algebra/topology; all label conditions are explicitly finite.

Proof 1: Rule out alternating simplices with only n label sizes

We use Ky Fan's lemma: if a boundary-odd labeling of a boundary-antipodally-symmetric triangulation of B_n has no complementary edge, then it has an odd number of n-dimensional alternating simplices.

By definition, an n-dimensional alternating simplex must have labels with n+1 different sizes.

This means that, if the labeling L uses only n different sizes (i.e. L:V(T)\to\{+1,-1,+2,-2,\ldots,+n,-n\}), it cannot have an n-dimensional alternating simplex.

Hence, by Ky Fan's lemma, L must have a complementary edge.

Level justification

Apply the explicit parity lemma and exclude an alternating n-simplex by its required n+1 distinct label sizes. Finite-dimensional simplices and their triangulations are a small addition to linear algebra and finite counting; no equivariant topology is used in the application.
