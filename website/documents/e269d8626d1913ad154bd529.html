Statement

Let T:\Omega\to\Omega^\prime be a function, let (\Omega^\prime,\mathcal A^\prime) be a measurable space, and define \sigma(T)=\{T^{-1}(B):B\in\mathcal A^\prime\}. Let m\ge1, a_1,\ldots,a_m\ge0 be real numbers, A_i\in\sigma(T) and f=\sum_{i=1}^m a_i\mathbf1_{A_i}. There is a finite-valued nonnegative \mathcal A^\prime-measurable simple function g:\Omega^\prime\to[0,\infty) such that f=g\circ T.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A measurable space adds a family of sets closed under complements and countable unions, and measurable functions are tested by preimages. The pullback family and finite indicator sum are explicit. These connected definitions are a small extension of undergraduate sets/functions, with no measure or integration involved.

Proof 1: Extend the indicator recipe by linearity — outline

First, note that, by the definition of \sigma(T) as the set of preimages of \mathcal A^\prime-measurable sets under T, we know that if A\in\sigma(T), then there exists some A^\prime\in\mathcal A^\prime such that A=T^{-1}(A^\prime).

Now, assume that f=\mathbf1_A is an indicator of some set A\in\sigma(T). If we identify A^\prime\in\mathcal A^\prime such that A=T^{-1}(A^\prime), then the function g=\mathbf1_{A^\prime} suits the requirement, and since A\in\sigma(T), such a set A^\prime\in\mathcal A^\prime always exists. By linearity, the claim extends to any finite nonnegative simple measurable function f.

Level justification

Choose the measurable preimage witnesses and sum their indicator functions. Sigma-algebra closure and measurability by preimages are the only new definitions beyond sets and functions; no measure, integration or limiting argument is required.
