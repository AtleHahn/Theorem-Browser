Statement

For f\in L^2([ -\pi,\pi ];\mathbb C), define \widehat f(n)=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(x)e^{-inx}\,dx. Then \sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}|\widehat f(n)|^2=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|f(x)|^2\,dx.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The full L2 domain requires Lebesgue integration and infinite-dimensional function spaces.

Proof 1: Specialize the Hilbert-space identity to the Fourier basis

Use the general Hilbert-space Parseval identity: for an orthonormal basis (e_n), \sum_n|\langle f,e_n\rangle|^2=\|f\|^2. Take H=L^2[-\pi,\pi] with normalized inner product \langle f,g\rangle=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f\overline g, and its standard orthonormal Fourier basis e_n(x)=e^{inx}. Then \langle f,e_n\rangle=\widehat f(n) and the squared norm is the stated integral. Substitution gives the conclusion.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Specialize orthonormal-basis Parseval to the full Lebesgue L2 space and its complete Fourier basis. Interpreting arbitrary square-integrable equivalence classes and their integral Hilbert structure requires measure-theoretic analysis beyond the ordinary undergraduate allowance.
