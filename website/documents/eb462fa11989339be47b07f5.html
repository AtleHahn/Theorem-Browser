Statement

Let R be a commutative ring, I an ideal, and M=M_0\supseteq M_1\supseteq\cdots a sequence of finitely generated R-modules with IM_j\subseteq M_{j+1}. Put B_IR=\bigoplus_{j\ge0} I^j, with multiplication induced by ideal multiplication, and B_IM=\bigoplus_{j\ge0}M_j, a graded B_IR-module. Then B_IM is finitely generated over B_IR if and only if the filtration is stable: IM_j=M_{j+1} for all sufficiently large j.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement concerns modules over arbitrary commutative rings and graded algebra.

Proof 1: Generators in bounded degree

Indeed, if the filtration is I-stable, then {\displaystyle B_{I}M} is generated by the first {\displaystyle k+1} terms {\displaystyle M_{0},\dots ,M_{k}} and those terms are finitely generated; thus, {\displaystyle B_{I}M} is finitely generated. Conversely, if it is finitely generated, then it is generated by {\textstyle \bigoplus _{j=0}^{k}M_{j}} for some {\displaystyle k\geq 0}. Then, for {\displaystyle n>k}, each f in {\displaystyle M_{n}} can be written as

{\displaystyle f=\sum a_{j}g_{j},\quad a_{j}\in I^{n-j}}

with {\displaystyle g_{j}} in {\displaystyle M_{j},j\leq k}. That is, {\displaystyle f\in I^{n-k}M_{k}}.
