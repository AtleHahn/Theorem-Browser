Statement

For 0\le k<1, define K(k)=\int_0^1[(1-k^2t^2)(1-t^2)]^{-1/2}\,dt and E(k)=\int_0^1\sqrt{1-k^2t^2}/\sqrt{1-t^2}\,dt. Write \mathrm{Ti}_2(z)=\int_0^z\arctan(t)/t\,dt, and let C=\sum_{j\ge0}(-1)^j/(2j+1)^2 be Catalan’s constant. The source uses \mathrm{Ti}_2(1)=\beta(2)=C, with \beta(2) denoting that same series. \int_0^1K(x)\,dx=2C.

Proof 1: Integral of the complete elliptic integral of the first kind — outline

The improper integral of the complete elliptic integral of the first kind, {\displaystyle K(x)}, evaluates to twice the Catalan constant, {\displaystyle C}. This can be shown using Fubini's theorem to relate the expression to the arctangent integral:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\int _{0}^{1}K(x)\,\mathrm {d} x&=\int _{0}^{1}\int _{0}^{1}{\frac {1}{\sqrt {(1-x^{2}y^{2})(1-y^{2})}}}\,\mathrm {d} y\,\mathrm {d} x\\&=\int _{0}^{1}\int _{0}^{1}{\frac {1}{\sqrt {(1-x^{2}y^{2})(1-y^{2})}}}\,\mathrm {d} x\,\mathrm {d} y\\&=\int _{0}^{1}{\frac {\arcsin(y)}{y{\sqrt {1-y^{2}}}}}\,\mathrm {d} y\\&=\left\{2\,\mathrm {Ti} _{2}\left[y\left(1+{\sqrt {1-y^{2}}}\,\right)^{-1}\right]\right\}_{y=0}^{y=1}\\&=2\,\mathrm {Ti} _{2}(1)=2\beta (2)=2\,C\end{aligned}}}

Level justification

The elliptic integrals and arctangent integral are explicitly ordinary real integrals. Reverse the nonnegative double integral, integrate and evaluate the given Catalan identity. Calculus and real-series convergence suffice; algebraic elliptic curves are not involved.
