Statement

Let n\ge1 be an integer, \mu Lebesgue measure on \mathbb R^n, A+B=\{a+b:a\in A,b\in B\} and tA=\{ta:a\in A\}. For nonempty compact sets A,B\subseteq\mathbb R^n, \mu(A+B)^{1/n}\ge\mu(A)^{1/n}+\mu(B)^{1/n}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Undergraduate topology supplies compact sets, and vector addition defines the supplied Minkowski sum. Lebesgue volume here extends Euclidean box volume to compact sets, for example through shrinking open neighborhoods and box covers. Understanding that extension, including zero-volume compact sets, and the displayed inequality fits about 30 minutes from analysis and multivariable calculus; no measurable-function integration is needed for the statement.

Proof 1: Box induction and measure approximation outline

Axis-aligned boxes

By translation invariance of volumes, it suffices to take {\textstyle A=\prod _{i=1}^{n}[0,a_{i}],B=\prod _{i=1}^{n}[0,b_{i}]}. Then {\textstyle A+B=\prod _{i=1}^{n}[0,a_{i}+b_{i}]}. In this special case, the Brunn–Minkowski inequality asserts that {\textstyle \prod (a_{i}+b_{i})^{1/n}\geq \prod a_{i}^{1/n}+\prod b_{i}^{1/n}}. After dividing both sides by {\textstyle \prod (a_{i}+b_{i})^{1/n}} , this follows from the AM–GM inequality: {\textstyle (\prod {\frac {a_{i}}{a_{i}+b_{i}}})^{1/n}+(\prod {\frac {b_{i}}{a_{i}+b_{i}}})^{1/n}\leq \sum {\frac {1}{n}}{\frac {a_{i}+b_{i}}{a_{i}+b_{i}}}=1}.

Finite disjoint unions of boxes

We will use induction on the total number of boxes, where the previous calculation establishes the base case of two boxes. First, we observe that there is an axis aligned hyperplane H such that each side of H contains an entire box of A (interchange A and B if only B has more than one box). To see this, it suffices to reduce to the case where A consists of two boxes, and then calculate that the negation of this statement implies that the two boxes have a point in common. For a body X, we let {\textstyle X^{-},X^{+}} denote the intersections of X with the "right" and "left" halfspaces defined by H. Noting again that the statement of Brunn–Minkowski is translation invariant, we then translate B so that {\textstyle {\frac {\mu (A^{+})}{\mu (B^{+})}}={\frac {\mu (A^{-})}{\mu (B^{-})}}}; such a translation exists by the intermediate value theorem because {\textstyle t\to \mu ((B+tv)^{+})} is a continuous function, if v is perpendicular to H {\textstyle {\frac {\mu ((B+tv)^{+})}{\mu ((B+tv)^{-})}}} has limiting values 0 and {\textstyle \infty } as {\displaystyle t\to -\infty ,t\to \infty }, so takes on {\textstyle {\frac {\mu (A^{+})}{\mu (A^{-})}}} at some point. We now have the pieces in place to complete the induction step. First, observe that {\textstyle A^{+}+B^{+}} and {\displaystyle A^{-}+B^{-}} are disjoint up to their common boundary hyperplane and are subsets of {\textstyle A+B}, and so {\textstyle \mu (A+B)\geq \mu (A^{+}+B^{+})+\mu (A^{-}+B^{-}).} Now, {\textstyle A^{+},A^{-}} both have at least one fewer box than A, while {\textstyle B^{+},B^{-}} each have at most as many boxes as B. Thus, we can apply the induction hypothesis: {\textstyle \mu (A^{+}+B^{+})\geq (\mu (A^{+})^{1/n}+\mu (B^{+})^{1/n})^{n}} and {\textstyle \mu (A^{-}+B^{-})\geq (\mu (A^{-})^{1/n}+\mu (B^{-})^{1/n})^{n}}. Elementary algebra shows that if {\textstyle {\frac {\mu (A^{+})}{\mu (B^{+})}}={\frac {\mu (A^{-})}{\mu (B^{-})}}}, then also {\textstyle {\frac {\mu (A^{+})}{\mu (B^{+})}}={\frac {\mu (A^{-})}{\mu (B^{-})}}={\frac {\mu (A)}{\mu (B)}}}, so we can calculate: {\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\mu (A+B)\geq \mu (A^{+}+B^{+})+\mu (A^{-}+B^{-})\geq (\mu (A^{+})^{1/n}+\mu (B^{+})^{1/n})^{n}+(\mu (A^{-})^{1/n}+\mu (B^{-})^{1/n})^{n}\\=\mu (B^{+})(1+{\frac {\mu (A^{+})^{1/n}}{\mu (B^{+})^{1/n}}})^{n}+\mu (B^{-})(1+{\frac {\mu (A^{-})^{1/n}}{\mu (B^{-})^{1/n}}})^{n}=(1+{\frac {\mu (A)^{1/n}}{\mu (B)^{1/n}}})^{n}(\mu (B^{+})+\mu (B^{-}))=(\mu (B)^{1/n}+\mu (A)^{1/n})^{n}\end{aligned}}}

Bounded open sets

In this setting, both bodies can be approximated arbitrarily well by unions of disjoint axis aligned rectangles contained in their interior; this follows from general facts about the Lebesgue measure of open sets. That is, we have a sequence of bodies {\textstyle A_{k}\subseteq A}, which are disjoint unions of finitely many axis aligned rectangles, where {\textstyle \mu (A\setminus A_{k})\leq 1/k}, and likewise {\textstyle B_{k}\subseteq B}. Then we have that {\textstyle A+B\supseteq A_{k}+B_{k}}, so {\textstyle \mu (A+B)^{1/n}\geq \mu (A_{k}+B_{k})^{1/n}\geq \mu (A_{k})^{1/n}+\mu (B_{k})^{1/n}}. The right-hand side converges to {\textstyle \mu (A)^{1/n}+\mu (B)^{1/n}} as {\textstyle k\to \infty }, establishing this special case.

Compact sets

For a compact body X, define {\textstyle X_{\varepsilon }=X+B(0,\varepsilon )} to be the {\textstyle \varepsilon }-thickening of X. Here each {\textstyle B(0,\varepsilon )} is the open ball of radius {\textstyle \varepsilon }, so that {\textstyle X_{\varepsilon }} is a bounded, open set. {\textstyle \bigcap _{\varepsilon >0}X_{\varepsilon }={\text{cl}}(X)}, so that if X is compact, then {\textstyle \lim _{\varepsilon \to 0}\mu (X_{\varepsilon })=\mu (X)}. By using associativity and commutativity of Minkowski sum, along with the previous case, we can calculate that {\textstyle \mu ((A+B)_{2\varepsilon })^{1/n}=\mu (A_{\varepsilon }+B_{\varepsilon })^{1/n}\geq \mu (A_{\varepsilon })^{1/n}+\mu (B_{\varepsilon })^{1/n}}. Sending {\textstyle \varepsilon } to 0 establishes the result.

Proof 2: Indicator functions and optimized scaling from Prékopa–Leindler outline

Use the Prékopa–Leindler inequality: for 0<\lambda<1 and nonnegative measurable functions f,g,h on \mathbb R^n, if h(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y)\ge f(x)^\lambda g(y)^{1-\lambda} for every x,y, then \int h\ge(\int f)^\lambda(\int g)^{1-\lambda}. Apply this to f=1_A, g=1_B, h=1_{\lambda A+(1-\lambda)B}, where 1_E is the indicator function of E. This gives \mu(\lambda A+(1-\lambda)B)\ge\mu(A)^\lambda\mu(B)^{1-\lambda}.

Assume first that A and B have positive volume; the zero-volume cases are immediate.

Using this multiplicative inequality for the scaled sets A/\lambda and B/(1-\lambda), we find

{\textstyle \mu (A+B)=\mu (\lambda {\frac {A}{\lambda }}+(1-\lambda ){\frac {B}{1-\lambda }})\geq {\bigl (}{\frac {\mu (A)}{\lambda ^{n}}}{\bigr )}^{\lambda }{\bigl (}{\frac {\mu (B)}{(1-\lambda )^{n}}}{\bigr )}^{1-\lambda }.}

The right side is maximized at {\textstyle \lambda ={\frac {\mu (A)^{1/n}}{\mu (A)^{1/n}+\mu (B)^{1/n}}}}, which gives

{\textstyle \mu (A+B)\geq (\mu (A)^{1/n}+\mu (B)^{1/n})^{n}} concluding the proof.
