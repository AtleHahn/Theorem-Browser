Statement

There is no function of just the numbers of interior and boundary integer lattice points that gives the volume of every three-dimensional lattice polyhedron.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The claim compares counts of integer-coordinate points with ordinary three-dimensional volume.

Proof 1: Reeve tetrahedra with equal counts and unequal volumes

The Reeve tetrahedra in three dimensions have four integer points as vertices and contain no other integer points, but do not all have the same volume. These properties of the Reeve tetrahedra are used here without proof. Therefore, there does not exist an analogue of Pick's theorem in three dimensions that expresses the volume of a polyhedron as a function only of its numbers of interior and boundary points.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Use the supplied existence of tetrahedra with identical integer-point counts but different volumes. Elementary spatial geometry and the fact that equal inputs cannot give different function values suffice; no construction or lattice-volume theorem is required.
