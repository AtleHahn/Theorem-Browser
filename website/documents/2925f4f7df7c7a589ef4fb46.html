Statement

In the hyperbolic plane, let A,C,B',B form a nondegenerate Saccheri quadrilateral in that cyclic order: AB and CB' are equal perpendicular segments to the base line BB', with A,C on the same side of it. The line joining the midpoints of AC and BB' is perpendicular to both those lines.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The hyperbolic plane adds a non-Euclidean metric/geodesic interpretation to undergraduate geometry. Saccheri quadrilaterals are defined locally and the needed reflection property is supplied.

Proof 1: Saccheri symmetry

The quadrilateral ACB'B has equal perpendicular legs, so use the symmetry theorem for a Saccheri quadrilateral: reflection in its axis exchanges the endpoints of both base and summit. Its axis therefore joins their midpoints and is perpendicular to both. This is the desired common perpendicular.

Level justification

Apply the explicitly stated reflection symmetry of the hyperbolic quadrilateral. Elementary undergraduate non-Euclidean geometry suffices: only geodesic perpendicularity, midpoints and reflections are needed, not a differential-geometric model.
