Statement

Let h:X\to[0,\infty) and T:X\to\mathbb R^s be measurable on a fixed observation space X with reference measure \mu. For \eta\in\mathbb R^s let Z(\eta)=\int h(x)e^{\eta^TT(x)}\,d\mu(x), and wherever 0<Z(\eta)<\infty put A(\eta)=\log Z(\eta) and p_\eta(x)=h(x)e^{\eta^TT(x)-A(\eta)}. For \eta,u such that both Z(\eta) and Z(\eta+u) are positive and finite, M_T(u)=\mathbb E_\eta e^{u^TT(X)}=e^{A(\eta+u)-A(\eta)}. Consequently K_T(u):=\log M_T(u)=A(\eta+u)-A(\eta).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The expectation is for a vector statistic in a measure-theoretic probability model.

Proof 1: Absorb the moment-generating factor into the natural parameter

The moment-generating function of T(X) is

\begin{aligned} M_T(u)&=\mathbb E_\eta\left[\exp\left(u^{\mathsf T}T(X)\right)\right]\\ &=\int_X h(x)\exp\left[(\eta+u)^{\mathsf T}T(x)-A(\eta)\right]\,d\mu(x)\\ &=e^{A(\eta+u)-A(\eta)}. \end{aligned}

Thus

K_T(u)=\log M_T(u)=A(\eta+u)-A(\eta)

is the cumulant generating function for T(X).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The exponent manipulation is elementary, but the actual expectation is an integral of measurable statistics against a general reference-measure density. The full measure-theoretic setting remains beyond the ordinary undergraduate baseline.
