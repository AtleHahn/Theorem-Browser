Sign of the discriminant determines a real cubic’s distinct real roots

Statement

Let a,b,c,d\in\mathbb R, a\ne0, and let \Delta=18abcd-4b^3d+b^2c^2-4ac^3-27a^2d^2\ne0. If \Delta>0, ax^3+bx^2+cx+d has three distinct real roots. If \Delta<0, it has one real root and two nonreal conjugate roots.

Proof 1: Pair complex conjugate roots

First, if r is a root of a polynomial with real coefficients, then its complex conjugate is also a root. So the non-real roots, if any, occur as pairs of complex conjugate roots. As a cubic polynomial has three roots (not necessarily distinct) by the fundamental theorem of algebra, at least one root must be real.

By the cubic discriminant formula, if r1, r2, r3 are the three roots of the cubic {\displaystyle ax^{3}+bx^{2}+cx+d}, then the discriminant is

{\displaystyle \Delta =a^{4}(r_{1}-r_{2})^{2}(r_{1}-r_{3})^{2}(r_{2}-r_{3})^{2}}

If the three roots are real and distinct, the discriminant is a product of positive reals, that is {\displaystyle \Delta >0.}

If only one root, say r1, is real, then r2 and r3 are complex conjugates, which implies that r2 − r3 is a purely imaginary number, and thus that (r2 − r3)2 is real and negative. On the other hand, r1 − r2 and r1 − r3 are complex conjugates, and their product is real and positive. Thus the discriminant is the product of a single negative number and several positive ones. That is {\displaystyle \Delta <0.}
