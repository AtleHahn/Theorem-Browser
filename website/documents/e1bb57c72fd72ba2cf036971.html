Statement

Let X_k (k\ge1) be independent real random variables on a probability space, with finite means \mu_k and variances \sigma_k^2. Set s_n^2=\sum_{k=1}^n\sigma_k^2 and, for the indices with s_n>0, set

Z_n=\frac{\sum_{k=1}^n(X_k-\mu_k)}{s_n}.

All limits below concern these normalized expressions, so they are understood for eventually positive s_n. If for every \varepsilon>0,

\frac1{s_n^2}\sum_{k=1}^n\mathbb E\big[(X_k-\mu_k)^2\mathbf1_{\{|X_k-\mu_k|>\varepsilon s_n\}}\big]\longrightarrow0,

then Z_n converges in distribution to the standard normal law. Conversely, if

\max_{1\le k\le n}\frac{\sigma_k^2}{s_n^2}\longrightarrow0,

then convergence of Z_n to that law implies the displayed Lindeberg condition, so under this additional condition the two are equivalent.

The indicator \mathbf1_E is one on E and zero elsewhere. Convergence in distribution here means \Pr(Z_n\le t)\to\Phi(t) for every real t, where \Phi(t)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}\int_{-\infty}^t e^{-u^2/2}\,du.
