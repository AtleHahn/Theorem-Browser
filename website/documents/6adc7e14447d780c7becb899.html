There is no computable upper bound on the size of the smallest finite model of every finitely satisfiable sentence in the stated vocabulary. Thus restricting attention to finite objects does not remove fundamental algorithmic limits, and exhaustive search lacks a universal computable cutoff.

Statement

Let \tau be a finite relational first-order vocabulary, and use effectively coded sentences and finite nonempty \tau-structures. Assume \tau contains a binary relation symbol. There is no total recursive function b on sentence codes such that every finitely satisfiable sentence \varphi has a model of size at most b(\varphi).

Proof 1: A recursive bound would make the finite search decidable — outline

Trakhtenbrot's theorem states that satisfiability for finite structures is not decidable in first-order logic for the vocabulary in the statement. It follows that there is no recursive function b such that: if \varphi has a finite model, then it has a model of size at most b(\varphi). In other words, there is no effective analogue to the Löwenheim–Skolem theorem in the finite.

The source states this consequence without giving the bounded-search argument.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

A recursive size bound would permit exhaustive finite-model checking and contradict the supplied undecidability theorem. Effective coding, first-order relational structures and finite satisfiability together exceed a confidently small extension of ordinary undergraduate logic; retain beyond undergraduate in doubt.
