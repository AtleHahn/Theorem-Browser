Statement

Let n\ge1, 1\le p<\infty, and u\in C_c^1(\mathbb R^n) be nonnegative. With \mathcal H^{n-1} denoting Hausdorff surface measure, \int_{\mathbb R^n}|\nabla u|^p\,dx=\int_0^\infty\int_{u^{-1}(t)}|\nabla u|^{p-1}\,d\mathcal H^{n-1}\,dt. For p=1 the level-integrand is 1.

Proof 1: Coarea representation of the gradient energy — outline

If the function {\displaystyle u} is smooth enough, the coarea formula can be used to write

{\displaystyle \int _{\mathbb {R} ^{n}}|\nabla u|^{p}=\int _{0}^{+\infty }\int _{u^{-1}({t})}|\nabla u|^{p-1}\,d{\mathcal {H}}^{n-1}\,dt,}

where {\displaystyle {\mathcal {H}}^{n-1}} denotes the {\displaystyle (n-1)}–dimensional Hausdorff measure on the Euclidean space {\displaystyle \mathbb {R} ^{n}}.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The coarea application integrates over arbitrary level sets using Hausdorff measure and Lebesgue integration. Even for a C1 function these measure-theoretic objects are not ordinary multivariable calculus surfaces alone.
