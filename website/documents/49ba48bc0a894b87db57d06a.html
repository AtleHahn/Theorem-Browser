Statement

Assume every two NP-complete languages are polynomial-time isomorphic, meaning there is a bijection between them computable in polynomial time with polynomial-time inverse. Assume also the stated fact that the usual 3SAT language is not sparse. Then no NP-complete language is sparse; sparse means having only polynomially many words of length at most n.

Proof 1: Bound the lengths of distinct isomorphic images — outline

Since some NP-complete sets are not sparse (for example, the set of satisfiable 3SAT formulas), the conjecture that any two NP-complete sets are polynomial-time isomorphic implies the weaker conjecture that there are no NP-complete sparse sets. The source states this deduction without expanding the polynomial-length counting argument.
