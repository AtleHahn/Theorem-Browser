Simpler proof assessment

Likely alternative prerequisite level: High school only; lowest recorded proof level: Undergraduate level. For degrees deg(u)>=deg(v)+2, counting their neighbor sets gives a neighbor w of u, distinct from v, that is not adjacent to v. Replacing uw by vw makes a unit balancing transfer and preserves a simple graph. The finite integer majorization lemma follows by transferring one unit from a surplus earlier entry to a deficient later entry and resorting until the target is reached. This elementary finite procedure proves downward closure for equal-sum sequences, without importing the partition-order cover theorem.

Statement

Let n\ge1 be an integer. Let c_1\ge\cdots\ge c_n\ge0 and d_1\ge\cdots\ge d_n\ge0 be integer sequences with the same sum and with \sum_{i=1}^k c_i\ge\sum_{i=1}^k d_i for 1\le k\le n. If c is graphical, then d is graphical. Here graphical means that the sequence lists the degrees of a finite simple graph on n vertices; a simple graph has no loops and at most one edge between two distinct vertices.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The comparison and graph convention are fully given in finite integer terms.

Proof 1: Descend through graphicality-preserving partition covers — outline

Use the integer-partition order by prefix sums. Its descending cover changes subtract one from an entry and add one to an entry smaller by at least two, then sort. The stated Aigner–Triesch transfer result says that these changes preserve being graphical. Descending from c to d through this finite partition order therefore preserves graphicality, giving the result.

Level justification
