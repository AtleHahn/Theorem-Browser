Statement

Let \Gamma be a finitely generated discrete subgroup of \operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb C). There is a sequence of injective homomorphisms

\rho_j:\Gamma\to\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb C)

with discrete geometrically finite images, such that \rho_j(\gamma)\to\gamma for every \gamma\in\Gamma. Equivalently, the inclusion representation of \Gamma is an algebraic limit of marked geometrically finite Kleinian groups. Here \operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb C)=\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb C)/\{I,-I\} has its matrix-quotient topology and acts by orientation-preserving isometries of hyperbolic three-space. A Kleinian group is a discrete subgroup. Geometric finiteness means that the action admits a fundamental polyhedron with finitely many sides; translates of such a polyhedron cover hyperbolic space and have disjoint interiors. Algebraic convergence means convergence on each element of the fixed marked group, equivalently on a fixed finite generating set. No torsion-free or non-elementary restriction is imposed.

Proof 1: Ending-invariant approximation and fixed points (external)

A link to the proof outline can be found here (§9 proof of the density theorem; §12.1 Theorem 12.1, printed pp.387–388, with the explicit replacement of its last convergence paragraph given below.).

This is an expert proof exposition with ending-invariant and equivariant realization dependencies. For torsion, §12.1 passes to a finite-index normal torsion-free subgroup H and constructs equivariant geometrically finite approximations extending faithfully and discretely to \Gamma.

The following replaces the final root-uniqueness argument in that section. For non-elementary \Gamma, choose loxodromic a,b\in H with at least three distinct fixed points. For each \gamma\in\Gamma, its approximating image conjugates the images of a,b to those of \gamma a\gamma^{-1},\gamma b\gamma^{-1}. All four elements lie in H and converge. Their attracting and repelling fixed points vary continuously, so the image of \gamma carries a convergent triple to the triple converging to its \gamma-image. A Möbius transformation is uniquely and continuously determined by three distinct points and their images; thus these images converge to \gamma. Elementary finitely generated Kleinian groups are already geometrically finite, so use the constant sequence.
