Statement

Let n\geq1 be an integer and f:\mathbb Z^n\to\mathbb R^n a bounded map. Suppose h(x)=f(x)-x is SGDP for an integral triangulation of \mathbb R^n. An integral triangulation has integer vertices and every simplex lies in a unit axis-parallel integer cell. A map h on lattice points is simplicially gross direction preserving (SGDP) with respect to such a triangulation when h(x)\cdot h(y)\geq0 for any two vertices in a common simplex. Then f(x)=x for some x\in\mathbb Z^n.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra supplies dot products and simplices; a triangulation is a face-to-face subdivision by simplices. The integer-cell and SGDP conditions are explicitly given geometric restrictions. This small combinatorial-geometric extension suffices to understand the fixed-point assertion without topological degree theory.

Proof 1: Restrict to a finite lattice box bounding the range — outline

Use the finite discrete fixed-point theorem: if X\subset\mathbb Z^n is nonempty, finite and integrally convex, F:X\to\operatorname{conv}(X), and F(x)-x is SGDP, then F has a fixed point. Here integrally convex means that each y\in\operatorname{conv}(X) lies in the convex hull of those z\in X with |z_i-y_i|<1 in every coordinate.

Take a finite lattice box X bounding the range of f and apply this theorem to the restriction of f to X.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof bounds the range in an integer box and applies the explicitly stated finite theorem. Convex hulls, dot products and simplex subdivisions are concrete extensions of undergraduate linear algebra and Euclidean geometry; SGDP and integral convexity are given as explicit conditions. No proof of a general topological fixed-point theorem is required.
