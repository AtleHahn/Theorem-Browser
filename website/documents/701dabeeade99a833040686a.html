Statement

Let A_n(x,y)\to B_n(y,z) be a family of tautologies, indexed by a size parameter n, with pairwise disjoint variable lists x,y,z. Suppose a proof system has feasible interpolation: an algorithm extracts from any proof of such an implication an interpolant circuit C(y) in time polynomial in the proof length. If this family has proofs of length polynomial in n, it has interpolants of size polynomial in n. Thus superpolynomial lower bounds for every interpolant of the family exclude polynomial-size proofs in that system.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The interpolation algorithm and its polynomial dependence on proof length are specified, and the companion defines the two implications required of an interpolant. Boolean circuits are finite networks of truth operations and size is their encoding length. These limited algorithmic definitions extend undergraduate functions and polynomial bounds without assuming proof-complexity lower-bound theory.

Proof 1: Compose the short-proof bound with the interpolation algorithm

Here an interpolant is a circuit C(y) such that both A_n(x,y)\to C(y) and C(y)\to B_n(y,z) hold.

The following three statements cannot be simultaneously true: (a) the implications A_n(x,y)\to B_n(y,z) have proofs of length polynomial in n in the proof system; (b) the proof system has feasible interpolation; (c) every interpolant circuit for this family requires superpolynomial size.

Indeed, (a) and (b) imply that there are interpolant circuits of size polynomial in n: the interpolation algorithm takes time polynomial in the proof length, and polynomial time in a polynomial proof length is polynomial in n. This is in contradiction with (c).

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

All interpolation properties needed are stated explicitly in terms of Boolean implications and an extraction algorithm. Composing two polynomial resource bounds and bounding output size by runtime is a small undergraduate algorithms extension; proof-complexity lower-bound machinery is not invoked.
