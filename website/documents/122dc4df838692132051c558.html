Statement

Let V_1,V_2,V_3,V_4 be finite-dimensional subspaces of a vector space over an arbitrary field. Write d(U)=\dim U, V_{ij}=V_i+V_j, V_{ijk}=V_i+V_j+V_k, I_{ij}=V_i\cap V_j, I_{ijk}=V_i\cap V_j\cap V_k, and I_{1234}=V_1\cap V_2\cap V_3\cap V_4. Here sums are ordinary subspace sums. d(I_{123})\ge d(I_{12})+d(V_3)-d(V_{13})-d(V_{23})+d(V_{123}).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory group/ring theory and linear algebra supply the objects. The extra Sylow, normalizer, invariant-polynomial or pseudoreflection condition is explicitly defined in subgroup, coordinate or polynomial terms; no specialist extension or representation theory is required.

Proof 1: Use subspace inclusions and the dimension formula

We use the standard vector space identity

\dim U+\dim W=\dim(U+W)+\dim(U\cap W).

It is clear that (V_1\cap V_2)+V_3\subseteq(V_1+V_3)\cap(V_2+V_3), then

\dim((V_1\cap V_2)+V_3) \le\dim((V_1+V_3)\cap(V_2+V_3)),

hence

\begin{aligned} \dim(V_1\cap V_2\cap V_3) &=\dim(V_1\cap V_2)+\dim V_3-\dim((V_1\cap V_2)+V_3)\\ &\ge\dim(V_1\cap V_2)+\dim V_3-\dim((V_1+V_3)\cap(V_2+V_3))\\ &=\dim(V_1\cap V_2)+\dim V_3\\ &\quad-\{\dim(V_1+V_3)+\dim(V_2+V_3)-\dim(V_1+V_2+V_3)\}\\ &=\dim(V_1\cap V_2)+\dim V_3-\dim(V_1+V_3)-\dim(V_2+V_3)\\ &\quad+\dim(V_1+V_2+V_3). \end{aligned}

Level justification

Use the inclusion of (V1 intersect V2)+V3 in (V1+V3) intersect (V2+V3) and expand dimensions twice. Undergraduate subspace linear algebra suffices.
