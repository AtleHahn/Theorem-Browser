Statement

Let B>0, let x:\mathbb R\to\mathbb C be continuous and integrable, and let X(f)=\int_{\mathbb R}x(t)e^{-2\pi i ft}\,dt vanish for |f|\ge B. Let 0<T\le1/(2B). Write \operatorname{sinc}(u)=\sin(\pi u)/(\pi u) for u\ne0 and \operatorname{sinc}(0)=1. Then for every real t, x(t)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{n=-N}^{N}x(nT)\operatorname{sinc}((t-nT)/T).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Calculus supplies the explicitly given Fourier integral and sinc function; analysis supplies the symmetric partial-sum limit. The signal is continuous and integrable, so no L2 equivalence classes or distributional transform are needed to understand reconstruction.

Proof 1: Filter the periodized spectrum and invert — outline

Put f_s=1/T. Use Poisson summation in its Fourier-series form:

X_{1/T}(f):=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}X(f-k/T) =\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}T x(nT)e^{-i2\pi nTf}.

When there is no overlap of the copies (also known as “images”) of X(f), the k=0 term can be recovered by the product

X(f)=H(f)\cdot X_{1/T}(f),

where

H(f)= \begin{cases} 1&|f|<B,\\ 0&|f|>f_s-B. \end{cases}

All that remains is to derive the formula for reconstruction. H(f) need not be precisely defined in the region B\leq |f|\leq f_s-B because X_{1/T}(f) is zero in that region. However, the worst case is when B=f_s/2, the Nyquist frequency. A function that is sufficient for that and all less severe cases is

H(f)=\operatorname{rect}(f/f_s)= \begin{cases} 1&|f|<f_s/2,\\ 0&|f|>f_s/2. \end{cases}

Endpoint values are immaterial. Therefore,

\begin{aligned} X(f)&=\operatorname{rect}(f/f_s)\cdot X_{1/T}(f)\\ &=\operatorname{rect}(Tf)\cdot\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}T x(nT)e^{-i2\pi nTf}\\ &=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}x(nT)\, \underbrace{T\operatorname{rect}(Tf)e^{-i2\pi nTf}}_{\mathcal F\{\operatorname{sinc}((t-nT)/T)\}}. \end{aligned}

The inverse transform of both sides produces the Whittaker–Shannon interpolation formula:

x(t)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}x(nT)\operatorname{sinc}\left(\frac{t-nT}{T}\right),

which shows how the samples x(nT) can be combined to reconstruct x(t).

Here the Fourier series is interpreted through symmetric partial sums in L^2 on the finite frequency interval, and inversion gives the corresponding sinc partial sums. The standard compact-frequency L^2 inversion estimate and Fourier inversion for continuous integrable functions with integrable transform give convergence at every t. The source suppresses this convergence justification and quotes the sinc/rectangle transform pair.
