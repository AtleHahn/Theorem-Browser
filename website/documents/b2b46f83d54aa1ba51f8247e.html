Statement

Let two nonintersecting circles of radii 0<r_1,r_2<R, centered at O_1,O_2, lie inside a circle of radius R centered at O and touch it at distinct points K_1,K_2. Let t_{12} denote the length of a segment on an exterior common tangent between its two tangency points. Then t_{12}=\sqrt{(R-r_1)(R-r_2)}\,|K_1K_2|/R.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All objects are elementary Euclidean circles, lengths and real arithmetic.

Proof 1: Pythagoras, chord formula and cosine law

Note that from Pythagorean theorem,

{\displaystyle \,t_{12}^{2}={\overline {O_{1}O_{2}}}^{2}-(r_{1}-r_{2})^{2}.}

We will try to express this length in terms of the points {\displaystyle \,K_{1},K_{2}}. By the law of cosines in triangle {\displaystyle \,O_{1}OO_{2}},

{\displaystyle {\overline {O_{1}O_{2}}}^{2}={\overline {OO_{1}}}^{2}+{\overline {OO_{2}}}^{2}-2{\overline {OO_{1}}}\cdot {\overline {OO_{2}}}\cdot \cos \angle O_{1}OO_{2}}

Since the circles {\displaystyle \,O,O_{1}} tangent to each other:

{\displaystyle {\overline {OO_{1}}}=R-r_{1},\,\angle O_{1}OO_{2}=\angle K_{1}OK_{2}}

Let C be a point on the outer circle distinct from K_1 and K_2. According to the law of sines in triangle {\displaystyle \,K_{1}CK_{2}}:

{\displaystyle {\overline {K_{1}K_{2}}}=2R\cdot \sin \angle K_{1}CK_{2}=2R\cdot \sin {\frac {\angle K_{1}OK_{2}}{2}}}

Therefore,

{\displaystyle \cos \angle K_{1}OK_{2}=1-2\sin ^{2}{\frac {\angle K_{1}OK_{2}}{2}}=1-2\cdot \left({\frac {\overline {K_{1}K_{2}}}{2R}}\right)^{2}=1-{\frac {{\overline {K_{1}K_{2}}}^{2}}{2R^{2}}}}

and substituting these in the formula above:

{\displaystyle {\overline {O_{1}O_{2}}}^{2}=(R-r_{1})^{2}+(R-r_{2})^{2}-2(R-r_{1})(R-r_{2})\left(1-{\frac {{\overline {K_{1}K_{2}}}^{2}}{2R^{2}}}\right)}{\displaystyle {\overline {O_{1}O_{2}}}^{2}=(R-r_{1})^{2}+(R-r_{2})^{2}-2(R-r_{1})(R-r_{2})+(R-r_{1})(R-r_{2})\cdot {\frac {{\overline {K_{1}K_{2}}}^{2}}{R^{2}}}}{\displaystyle {\overline {O_{1}O_{2}}}^{2}=((R-r_{1})-(R-r_{2}))^{2}+(R-r_{1})(R-r_{2})\cdot {\frac {{\overline {K_{1}K_{2}}}^{2}}{R^{2}}}}

And finally, the length we seek is

{\displaystyle t_{12}={\sqrt {{\overline {O_{1}O_{2}}}^{2}-(r_{1}-r_{2})^{2}}}={\frac {{\sqrt {R-r_{1}}}\cdot {\sqrt {R-r_{2}}}\cdot {\overline {K_{1}K_{2}}}}{R}}}
Level
High school only
Level justification

Combine the tangent-segment right triangle, cosine law and chord half-angle formula, then simplify and take the positive square root. High-school geometry and trigonometry suffice.
