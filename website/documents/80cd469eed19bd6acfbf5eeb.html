Statement

Let T preserve a measure space (X,\Sigma,\mu) and let f\in L^2(\mu). The averages N^{-1}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}f\circ T^n converge in L^2 to the orthogonal projection of f onto \{g\in L^2:g\circ T=g\text{ almost everywhere}\}.

Proof 1: Specialize the Hilbert-space theorem to composition — outline

Use the Hilbert-space mean ergodic theorem: for an isometric linear operator U on a Hilbert space H, the averages N^{-1}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}U^nx converge in norm to Px, where P is the orthogonal projection onto \ker(I-U).

The Hilbert-space mean ergodic theorem for isometries specializes to the case in which the Hilbert space H consists of L2 functions on a measure space and U is an operator of the form

{\displaystyle Uf(x)=f(Tx)\,}

where T is a measure-preserving endomorphism of X, thought of in applications as representing a time-step of a discrete dynamical system. The ergodic theorem then asserts that the average behavior of a function ƒ over sufficiently large time-scales is approximated by the orthogonal component of ƒ which is time-invariant.

Level justification

Realize composition as a Hilbert-space isometry and identify its fixed functions modulo null sets. General measure preservation, L2 Hilbert spaces and norm ergodic convergence are advanced analysis.
