Statement

Let p be prime and u,x,y\in\mathbb Z with p\nmid u. If ux\equiv uy\pmod p, then x\equiv y\pmod p.

Proof 1: Apply Euclid’s lemma to the difference

We can prove the cancellation law easily using Euclid's lemma, which states that if a prime p divides a product ab (where a and b are integers), then p must divide a or b. Indeed, the assertion ux\equiv uy\pmod p simply means that p divides ux-uy=u(x-y). Since p is a prime which does not divide u, Euclid's lemma tells us that it must divide x-y instead; that is, x\equiv y\pmod p holds.
