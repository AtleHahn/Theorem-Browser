Statement

For each field \mathbb F\in\{\mathbb R,\mathbb C\} there is a finite constant K_{\mathbb F}>0 with the following property, independent of all matrix and Hilbert-space dimensions. Let n\ge1 and let M=(M_{ij})\in\mathbb F^{n\times n} satisfy

\left|\sum_{i,j=1}^n M_{ij}s_it_j\right|\le1 \quad\text{for all }s_i,t_j\in\mathbb F\text{ with }|s_i|,|t_j|\le1.

Then, for every Hilbert space H over \mathbb F and all vectors S_1,\ldots,S_n,T_1,\ldots,T_n\in H of norm at most one,

\left|\sum_{i,j=1}^n M_{ij}\langle S_i,T_j\rangle\right|\le K_{\mathbb F}.

A Hilbert space is an inner-product space complete for the norm \|v\|=\sqrt{\langle v,v\rangle}. In the complex case take the inner product linear in its first variable and conjugate-linear in its second.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra and introductory metric-space completeness suffice; a Hilbert space adds completeness to familiar inner-product geometry. Only finitely many vectors enter each test, and no operator spectral theory or tensor-product theory is needed.
