Statement

For each field \mathbb F\in\{\mathbb R,\mathbb C\} there is a finite constant K_{\mathbb F}>0 with the following property, independent of all matrix and Hilbert-space dimensions. Let n\ge1 and let M=(M_{ij})\in\mathbb F^{n\times n} satisfy

\left|\sum_{i,j=1}^n M_{ij}s_it_j\right|\le1 \quad\text{for all }s_i,t_j\in\mathbb F\text{ with }|s_i|,|t_j|\le1.

Then, for every Hilbert space H over \mathbb F and all vectors S_1,\ldots,S_n,T_1,\ldots,T_n\in H of norm at most one,

\left|\sum_{i,j=1}^n M_{ij}\langle S_i,T_j\rangle\right|\le K_{\mathbb F}.

A Hilbert space is an inner-product space complete for the norm \|v\|=\sqrt{\langle v,v\rangle}. In the complex case take the inner product linear in its first variable and conjugate-linear in its second.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra and introductory metric-space completeness suffice; a Hilbert space adds completeness to familiar inner-product geometry. Only finitely many vectors enter each test, and no operator spectral theory or tensor-product theory is needed.

Proof 1: Real Gaussian rounding with complexification (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Lemma2.1 and Corollary2.3, real cases; Section4 and Theorem4.1, Case I, pp.330–339).

Use the source’s real proof with K_{\mathbb R}=\pi/(2\log(1+\sqrt2)). The following independent complexification suffices for the stated existence claim. Rotate M by a scalar of modulus one so that the target sum is real and nonnegative, and write the rotated matrix as A+iB. With (x,y)_{\mathbb R}=\operatorname{Re}\langle x,y\rangle, the sum’s real part is

\sum A_{ij}(S_i,T_j)_{\mathbb R}-\sum B_{ij}(S_i,iT_j)_{\mathbb R}.

Each real matrix A,B satisfies the real scalar bound, so its absolute value is at most 2K_{\mathbb R}. Thus take K_{\mathbb C}=2K_{\mathbb R}; the sharper complex constant is unnecessary here. Restrict to the finite span of the given vectors, and pad each vector to unit norm using separate orthogonal coordinates for the two families, preserving all cross-inner-products.
