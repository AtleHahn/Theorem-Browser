Statement

Let A_1,\ldots,A_m be events in a probability space. Let N count those that occur, and put S_k=\sum_{J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\},\ |J|=k}\Pr(\cap_{j\in J}A_j), with S_0=1. Let R be a unital real or complex algebra and x a central indeterminate. In R[x], \sum_{n=0}^m\Pr(N=n)x^n=\sum_{k=0}^mS_k(x-1)^k, with scalar coefficients interpreted as multiples of the identity. For R=\mathbb R, evaluation at a real x gives \mathbb E[x^N]=\sum_{k=0}^mS_k(x-1)^k.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Only finitely many events and their intersections occur, so expectation is an explicit finite probability-weighted sum. Polynomial multiplication and shifts/differences of sequences are given by formulas. Elementary finite probability and linear algebra suffice without measure-theoretic integration.

Proof 1: Take coefficientwise expectations

Let A_1,\ldots,A_m\subseteq\Omega, m\ge0, and put N(\omega)=\sum_{j=1}^m1_{A_j}(\omega) and N_k(\omega)=\sum_{J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\},\ |J|=k}1_{\cap_{j\in J}A_j}(\omega). An empty intersection is \Omega, so N_0=1. The pointwise polynomial identity is x^{N(\omega)}=\sum_{k=0}^mN_k(\omega)(x-1)^k. Take the expectation of its finitely many coefficients. Since \mathbb E[N_k]=S_k, the right side becomes the claimed sum, while the left side is the generating polynomial of N.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Take expectations of finitely many scalar coefficients and regard those coefficients as multiples of an algebra identity. Undergraduate probability and elementary algebra suffice; no integration of algebra-valued random variables is required.
