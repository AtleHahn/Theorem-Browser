Statement

For a vector space X over its scalar field \mathbb K\in\{\mathbb R,\mathbb C\}, the algebraic dual X^{\#} with its pointwise topology \sigma(X^{\#},X) is a complete Hausdorff locally convex topological vector space.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Completeness for the weak-star uniformity on a general algebraic dual uses uniform-space concepts.

Proof 1: Closed subspace of the complete scalar product

Use the closed-dual lemma: For a vector space X over its scalar field \mathbb K\in\{\mathbb R,\mathbb C\}, the algebraic dual X^{\#}, consisting of all scalar-valued linear maps on X, is closed in \mathbb K^X with the topology of pointwise convergence.

Because the underlying field {\displaystyle \mathbb {K} } is a complete Hausdorff locally convex topological vector space, the same is true of the product space {\textstyle \mathbb {K} ^{X}=\prod _{x\in X}\mathbb {K} .} A closed subset of a complete space is complete, so by the stated closed-dual lemma, the space {\displaystyle \left(X^{\#},\sigma \left(X^{\#},X\right)\right)} is complete. {\displaystyle \blacksquare }

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Closedness of linearity equations alone is undergraduate, but the conclusion and argument additionally use completeness in the topological-vector-space uniformity and arbitrary products of complete Hausdorff locally convex spaces. These are interdependent concepts beyond basic point-set topology, and cannot be treated as merely metric completeness.
