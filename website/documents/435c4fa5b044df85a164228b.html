Statement

Let h be a real random variable of finite variance, w\in\mathbb R^d, and I a real symmetric positive-definite d\times d matrix. If \operatorname{Var}(h)\ge(v^Tw)^2/(v^TIv) for every nonzero v\in\mathbb R^d, then \operatorname{Var}(h)\ge w^TI^{-1}w.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The matrix notions are undergraduate linear algebra. The additional probabilistic content is a real random variable and its finite variance, the expected squared deviation from its mean; the assertion introduces no independence, stochastic limits or process structure. This limited mean-square definition fits brief preparation, but it must be understood rather than replacing variance by an unexplained scalar.

Proof 1: Take the quadratic-form supremum

Take the supremum over nonzero v in the assumed inequalities. The positive-definite quadratic-form identity \sup_{v\ne0}(v^Tw)^2/(v^TIv)=w^TI^{-1}w gives the conclusion.

This single probability definition is brief preparation from averages and integration. Then take the supremum of the assumed directional inequalities and apply the supplied quadratic-form identity, including w=0.
