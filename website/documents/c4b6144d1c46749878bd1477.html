Statement

Multiplication by 2 modulo 21 has permutation sign -1 on all residue classes and sign +1 on the invertible residue classes. Thus the two permutation signs need not coincide for an odd composite modulus.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Permutation signs or group translations require undergraduate abstract algebra, even for explicitly specified finite permutations. A bright US college student should be able to understand the statement after about 30 minutes of focused background research; this rating concerns understanding the claim, not proving it.

Proof 1: Explicit cycle decomposition

For example, multiplication by 2 on Z/21Z has cycle decomposition (0)(1,2,4,8,16,11)(3,6,12)(5,10,20,19,17,13)(7,14)(9,18,15), so the sign of this permutation is (1)(−1)(1)(−1)(−1)(1) = −1.

On the units modulo 21, multiplication by 2 is the product of the two displayed 6-cycles, whose signs multiply to 1.

Level justification

Check the listed cycles and multiply their signs, then retain the two unit cycles. Undergraduate finite permutations and parity suffice; no general Jacobi-symbol theorem is used.
