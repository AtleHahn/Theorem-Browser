Statement

For an odd prime p and integer a with p\nmid a, a^{(p-1)/2}\equiv1 or -1\pmod p.

Level

Proof 1: Apply the field quadratic-root bound to Fermat’s congruence — outline

Fermat’s theorem gives a^{p-1}\equiv1\pmod p. Thus a^{(p-1)/2} is a root of X^2-1 modulo p. Over the field of residues modulo p a quadratic has at most two roots, so this root is 1 or -1.
