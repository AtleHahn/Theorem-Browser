Statement

Graphs below are finite, undirected and simple. A clique is a set of pairwise adjacent vertices. Fix 0\le d\le1 and integer r\ge2. For positive integers N, on N vertices take a clique on q_N=\lfloor\sqrt d\,N\rfloor vertices and leave the rest isolated. Then its edge homomorphism density 2e/N^2 tends to d and its r-clique homomorphism density r!\,\#K_r/N^r tends to d^{r/2} as N\to\infty. Here e is the number of edges and \#K_r counts unordered r-vertex cliques.

Proof 1: Count ordered tuples in the chosen clique — outline

Take a number of vertices {\displaystyle N} approaching infinity. Pick a set of {\displaystyle \lfloor{\sqrt {d}}N\rfloor} of the vertices, and connect two vertices if and only if they are in the chosen set.

In the limit this gives a {\displaystyle K_{r}} density of {\displaystyle d^{r/2}.}

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Count ordered distinct tuples in the chosen clique and divide by N to the appropriate power. The floor error is bounded by one, so each fixed factor tends to sqrt(d). This is elementary sequence-limit analysis with finite counting.
