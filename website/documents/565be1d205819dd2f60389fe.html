Statement

There exists a function f:\mathbb N\to\mathbb N such that the following holds. Let G be a primitive permutation group on a finite set S, let \alpha\in S, and let H=\{g\in G:g(\alpha)=\alpha\}. If an H-orbit in S has length h>1, then |H|\le f(h). A permutation group is a subgroup of the bijections of S. It is primitive if it acts transitively and the only partitions of S that its permutations preserve are the partition into singletons and the one-part partition. An H-orbit of s is \{g(s):g\in H\}.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory finite group theory suffices; primitivity adds just the explicitly defined invariant-partition condition.
