Statement

For N\geq4, let Y be a uniform cyclic shift of f[n]=\delta_{n,0}-\delta_{n,1} in \mathbb R^N. Its complex Fourier basis is a covariance eigenbasis, but adaptive two-term approximation in that basis has positive error. Adaptive two-term approximation in the coordinate basis has zero error.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Finite cyclic shifts; Fourier covariance eigenbases; Adaptive sparse approximation. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Compare a two-sparse signal with its Fourier coefficients

The Fourier power of this random shift is

\mathbb E|\langle Y,g_m\rangle|^2=\frac4N\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\pi m}{N}\right),\qquad g_m[n]=N^{-1/2}e^{2\pi imn/N}.

A cyclic shift only changes Fourier coefficient phases. Hence all N-1 coefficients indexed by 1\leq m<N are nonzero for each realization. With N\geq4, two terms leave a nonzero residual. In contrast, every realization has exactly two nonzero coordinate coefficients, so selecting those two gives zero error.

Level justification

Count the nonzero Fourier coefficients of each shifted two-impulse vector and compare with its two coordinate coefficients. Undergraduate finite-dimensional linear algebra and finite probability suffice; no infinite stochastic KL expansion is used.
