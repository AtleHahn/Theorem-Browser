Statement

Let \sigma be a first-order signature, M an infinite \sigma-structure, and \kappa an infinite cardinal with \kappa\ge|\sigma|. There is a \sigma-structure N of cardinality \kappa such that N\preccurlyeq M if \kappa<|M|, and M\preccurlyeq N if \kappa\ge|M|. Here an elementary substructure preserves the truth of all first-order formulas with parameters from that substructure.

Proof 1: Close under witnesses and enlarge the elementary diagram — outline

Downward construction

For each first-order {\displaystyle \sigma }-formula {\displaystyle \varphi (y,x_{1},\ldots ,x_{n})}, the axiom of choice implies the existence of a function

{\displaystyle f_{\varphi }:M^{n}\to M}

such that, for all {\displaystyle a_{1},\ldots ,a_{n}\in M}, either

{\displaystyle M\models \varphi (f_{\varphi }(a_{1},\dots ,a_{n}),a_{1},\dots ,a_{n})}

or

{\displaystyle M\models \neg \exists y\,\varphi (y,a_{1},\dots ,a_{n}).}

Applying the axiom of choice again we get a function from the first-order formulas {\displaystyle \varphi } to such functions {\displaystyle f_{\varphi }}.

The family of functions {\displaystyle f_{\varphi }} gives rise to a preclosure operator {\displaystyle F} on the power set of {\displaystyle M}

{\displaystyle F(A)=\{f_{\varphi }(a_{1},\dots ,a_{n})\in M\mid \varphi \text{ a }\sigma\text{-formula} ;\,a_{1},\dots ,a_{n}\in A\}}

for {\displaystyle A\subseteq M}.

Here formulas of arity zero are allowed. The formula y=x_1 ensures A\subseteq F(A), and the formulas y=g(x_1,\ldots,x_n) ensure closure under the signature functions. The notation F^\omega(A) below means \bigcup_{j<\omega}F^j(A).

Iterating {\displaystyle F} countably many times results in a closure operator {\displaystyle F^{\omega }}. Taking an arbitrary subset {\displaystyle A\subseteq M} such that {\displaystyle \left\vert A\right\vert =\kappa }, and having defined {\displaystyle N=F^{\omega }(A)}, one can see that also {\displaystyle \left\vert N\right\vert =\kappa }. Then {\displaystyle N} is an elementary substructure of {\displaystyle M} by the Tarski–Vaught test: a substructure is elementary if every existential formula with parameters in it that has a witness in the larger structure has a witness in the substructure.

The trick used in this proof is essentially due to Skolem, who introduced function symbols for the Skolem functions {\displaystyle f_{\varphi }} into the language. One could also define the {\displaystyle f_{\varphi }} as partial functions such that {\displaystyle f_{\varphi }} is defined if and only if {\displaystyle M\models \exists y\,\varphi (y,a_{1},\ldots ,a_{n})}. The only important point is that {\displaystyle F} is a preclosure operator such that {\displaystyle F(A)} contains a solution for every formula with parameters in {\displaystyle A} which has a solution in {\displaystyle M} and that

{\displaystyle \left\vert F(A)\right\vert \leq \left\vert A\right\vert +\left\vert \sigma \right\vert +\aleph _{0}.}

Upward construction

First, one extends the signature by adding a new constant symbol for every element of {\displaystyle M}. The complete theory of {\displaystyle M} for the extended signature {\displaystyle \sigma '} is called the elementary diagram of {\displaystyle M}. In the next step one adds {\displaystyle \kappa } many new constant symbols to the signature and adds to the elementary diagram of {\displaystyle M} the sentences {\displaystyle c\neq c'} for any two distinct new constant symbols {\displaystyle c} and {\displaystyle c'}. Using the compactness theorem, the resulting theory is easily seen to be consistent: every finite part can be satisfied in the infinite structure M. Since its models must have cardinality at least {\displaystyle \kappa }, the downward part of this theorem guarantees the existence of a model {\displaystyle N} which has cardinality exactly {\displaystyle \kappa }. It contains an isomorphic copy of {\displaystyle M} as an elementary substructure.

In the downward step of the upward construction, retain the full expanded signature, so the named copy of M is retained. Identify that copy with M to obtain the elementary extension. When \kappa=|M|, one may simply take N=M.
