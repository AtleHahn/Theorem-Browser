Statement

Let n\ge1 be an integer. If a real function u\in L^1(\mathbb R^n) has weak gradient Du\in L^2(\mathbb R^n), then u\in L^2(\mathbb R^n) and \|u\|_2\le C_n\|u\|_1^{2/(n+2)}\|Du\|_2^{n/(n+2)}. The constant depends only on n.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The conclusion is membership and an estimate in a Lebesgue space under a distributional weak-gradient hypothesis.

Proof 1: Apply interpolation to obtain square integrability

Use the Gagliardo–Nirenberg theorem as an external prerequisite: for integers 0\le j<m, 1\le q,r\le\infty, 1\le P<\infty, and j/m\le\theta\le1 with

\frac1P=\frac jn+\theta\left(\frac1r-\frac mn\right)+\frac{1-\theta}{q},

it gives \|D^ju\|_P\le C\|D^mu\|_r^\theta\|u\|_q^{1-\theta} for real u\in L^q(\mathbb R^n) with weak derivatives D^mu\in L^r. If j=0,q=\infty,rm<n, additionally require decay at infinity or membership in some finite L^s. If r>1 and m-j-n/r is a nonnegative integer, require \theta<1. The constant is independent of u, and 1/\infty=0.

Indeed, choosing

j=0,\quad m=1,\quad n\geq1,\quad P=2,\quad q=1,\quad r=2,\quad \theta=\frac{n}{n+2},

one gets

\|u\|_{L^2(\mathbb R^n)} \leq C\|u\|_{L^1(\mathbb R^n)}^{2/(n+2)} \|\nabla u\|_{L^2(\mathbb R^n)}^{n/(n+2)}.
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the supplied interpolation theorem to obtain L2 membership and the norm bound. General Lebesgue integrability and weak gradients require graduate analysis even though checking the parameters is elementary.
