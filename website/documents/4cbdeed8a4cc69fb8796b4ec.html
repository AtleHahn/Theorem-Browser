Statement

For integers n\ge2r\ge2, the Kneser graph KG_{n,r}, whose vertices are r-subsets of an n-element ground set and whose edges join disjoint pairs, has fractional chromatic number n/r.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Basic graph coloring and finite sets supply the graph. Fractional coloring replaces a choice of independent sets by nonnegative weights with total weight at least one at each vertex; this finite system of inequalities is a small extension sufficient to understand the claimed minimum.

Proof 1: Use the vertex-transitive fractional-coloring formula — outline

Because Kneser graphs have symmetries taking any vertex to any other vertex (they are vertex-transitive graphs), their fractional chromatic number equals the ratio of their number of vertices to their independence number, so, using the Erdős–Ko–Rado independence number \binom{n-1}{r-1} and vertex count \binom nr, these graphs have fractional chromatic number exactly {\displaystyle n/r}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use symmetry to apply the vertex-transitive fractional-coloring formula, insert the EKR independence number and cancel binomial coefficients. Undergraduate finite graph theory suffices with fractional coloring defined by nonnegative weights on independent sets covering each vertex to weight at least one, a small linear-inequality extension.
