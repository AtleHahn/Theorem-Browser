Marsaglia hyperplane bound

A deterministic congruential generator confines successive output vectors to relatively few hyperplanes. Apparently random individual values can therefore hide strong collective structure, limiting what superficial randomness tests establish.

Statement

Let m\ge2 and k be integers, and let (r_i)_{i\ge1} be integers satisfying 0<r_i<m and r_{i+1}\equiv kr_i\pmod m. Put u_i=r_i/m. For every integer n\ge1, all successive vectors

(u_i,u_{i+1},\ldots,u_{i+n-1})\quad(i\ge1)

lie in the union of at most \lfloor(n!m)^{1/n}\rfloor hyperplanes in \mathbb R^n. A hyperplane is a set \{x:c_1x_1+\cdots+c_nx_n=b\} for a nonzero real coefficient vector c.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Hyperplanes are explicitly defined by elementary linear equations; modular arithmetic and the finite bound are accessible through elementary reasoning.
