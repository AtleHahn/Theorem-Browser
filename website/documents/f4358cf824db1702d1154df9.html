Statement

For s\in\mathbb C and real |x|<1, (1-x)^{-s}=\sum_{k=0}^\infty\binom{s+k-1}{k}x^k. Powers of the positive real base use its real logarithm, and \binom rk=r(r-1)\cdots(r-k+1)/k! with \binom r0=1.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Calculus and real analysis supply power series, finite differences and explicit growth estimates. Complex exponentials and complex differentiability extend familiar definitions, while all generalized coefficients are supplied by formulas. No abstract analytic continuation or Riemann surfaces are needed.

Proof 1: Substitute the negative exponent and change the sign

Use the generalized binomial series (1+x)^r=\sum_{k\ge0}\binom rk x^k for complex r and real |x|<1.

With r = −s, we have for |x| < 1:

{\displaystyle {\frac {1}{(1+x)^{s}}}=\sum _{k=0}^{\infty }{\binom {-s}{k}}x^{k}=\sum _{k=0}^{\infty }{\binom {s+k-1}{k}}(-1)^{k}x^{k}.}

Replacing x with -x yields:

{\displaystyle {\frac {1}{(1-x)^{s}}}=\sum _{k=0}^{\infty }{\binom {s+k-1}{k}}(-1)^{k}(-x)^{k}=\sum _{k=0}^{\infty }{\binom {s+k-1}{k}}x^{k}.}
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Substitute r=-s and then x to -x in the explicit convergent binomial series; extract signs from its finite coefficient product. Undergraduate series and complex exponentials suffice, with generalized binomial coefficients defined locally.
