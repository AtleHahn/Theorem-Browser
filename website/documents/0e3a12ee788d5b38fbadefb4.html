Statement

When Turing-machine space counts work-tape cells and the finite tape alphabet may be enlarged, fixed-size blocks of old tape symbols can be represented by single new symbols, compressing the represented work-tape contents by the fixed block length, up to rounding. Thus multiplicative constants do not distinguish the usual cell-space complexity classes.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The concrete tape/finite-control model adds a limited computational definition. Space is explicitly counted in cells with a freely enlarged finite alphabet.

Proof 1: Pack blocks into a larger alphabet — outline

Switch to a larger alphabet whose symbols encode fixed-size blocks of symbols of the original alphabet. One new tape cell then represents a fixed number of old tape cells, giving the stated linear compression. The finite control keeps the position within the encoded block.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Encode each fixed block as one symbol and keep a finite position counter in the control. Basic algorithms and finite-state representations suffice; the concrete tape model and cell count are a small extension.
