Statement

Let u:\mathbb Z\to\mathbb C have finite support and define its discrete-time Fourier transform U(\omega)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}u[k]e^{-ik\omega}. Define Hu[n]=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}U(\omega)(-i\operatorname{sgn}\omega)e^{in\omega}\,d\omega. Then Hu[n]=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}u[k]h[n-k],\qquad h[j]=\begin{cases}0,&j\text{ even},\\2/(\pi j),&j\text{ odd}.\end{cases} The sum is finite.

Proof 1: Apply the inverse-transform convolution theorem

The inverse DTFT, using the convolution theorem, is:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}Hu[n]&={\scriptstyle \mathrm {DTFT} ^{-1}}(U(\omega ))\ *\ {\scriptstyle \mathrm {DTFT} ^{-1}}(-i\cdot \operatorname {sgn}(\omega ))\\&=u[n]\ *\ {\frac {1}{2\pi }}\int _{-\pi }^{\pi }(-i\cdot \operatorname {sgn}(\omega ))\cdot e^{i\omega n}\,\mathrm {d} \omega \\&=u[n]\ *\ \underbrace {{\frac {1}{2\pi }}\left[\int _{-\pi }^{0}i\cdot e^{i\omega n}\,\mathrm {d} \omega -\int _{0}^{\pi }i\cdot e^{i\omega n}\,\mathrm {d} \omega \right]} _{h[n]},\end{aligned}}}

where

{\displaystyle h[n]\ \triangleq \ {\begin{cases}0,&{\text{if }}n{\text{ even}}\\{\frac {2}{\pi n}}&{\text{if }}n{\text{ odd}}\end{cases}}}

Here * denotes convolution: (u*h)[n]=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}u[k]h[n-k], a finite sum because u has finite support.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Finite support reduces the convolution identity to a finite interchange with a real integral. Split at zero and integrate complex exponentials, treating the zero frequency separately. Ordinary calculus suffices without singular-integral theory.
