Céa error estimate for a complex sesquilinear form

Statement

Let V be a complex Hilbert space and V_h\subseteq V a finite-dimensional subspace. Let a be linear in its first argument and conjugate-linear in its second, with |a(v,w)|\le\gamma\|v\|\|w\| and |a(v,v)|\ge\alpha\|v\|^2 for all v,w\in V, where \alpha,\gamma>0. Suppose a bounded conjugate-linear functional L and elements u\in V, u_h\in V_h satisfy a(u,w)=L(w) for all w\in V and a(u_h,w)=L(w) for all w\in V_h. Then \|u-u_h\|\le(\gamma/\alpha)\|u-v\| for every v\in V_h.

Proof 1: Sesquilinear absolute-value adaptation — outline

Céa's lemma holds on complex Hilbert spaces also; one then uses a sesquilinear form a(\cdot,\cdot) instead of a bilinear one. The coercivity assumption then becomes |a(v,v)|\geq\alpha\|v\|^2 for all v in V (notice the absolute value sign around a(v,v)).

The source gives this adaptation without repeating the real error calculation. With these absolute values, that calculation reads

\begin{aligned} \alpha\|u-u_h\|^2 &\leq |a(u-u_h,u-u_h)|\\ &=|a(u-u_h,u-v)+a(u-u_h,v-u_h)|\\ &=|a(u-u_h,u-v)|\\ &\leq\gamma\|u-u_h\|\,\|u-v\| \end{aligned}

for all v in V_h. We used the a-orthogonality of u-u_h and v-u_h\in V_h,

a(u-u_h,w)=0,\qquad w\in V_h,

which follows directly from V_h\subseteq V:

a(u,w)=L(w)=a(u_h,w)\qquad\text{for all }w\in V_h.

Dividing by \alpha\|u-u_h\| when u\ne u_h gives the estimate; when u=u_h it is immediate.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Existence of u and u_h is assumed here. Subtract their two given equations, use sesquilinear additivity, and apply the two stated norm bounds. Sesquilinearity is a small extension of inner-product linear algebra; no variational existence theorem or operator duality is needed.
