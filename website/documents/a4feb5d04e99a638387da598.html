Statement

Let n\geq1, let g be a norm on \mathbb R^n, let K=\{x:g(x)\leq1\}, and let \Gamma be a full-rank lattice in \mathbb R^n. For 1\leq j\leq n, define

\lambda_j=\inf\{\lambda>0:\lambda K\text{ contains }j\text{ linearly independent vectors of }\Gamma\}.

Then

\frac{2^n}{n!}\det\Gamma\ \leq\ (\lambda_1\cdots\lambda_n)\operatorname{vol}_n(K)\ \leq\ 2^n\det\Gamma.

A full-rank lattice is the integer span of a real basis b_1,\ldots,b_n; its covolume \det\Gamma is |\det(b_1,\ldots,b_n)|, independent of that lattice basis. The volume is Lebesgue volume. Equivalently, K is any closed convex body symmetric about zero with positive finite volume, using its norm unit-ball formulation.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra and undergraduate analysis suffice for norms, determinants, volume and infima. A lattice is explicitly an integer span, a small extension rather than an additional theory.

Proof availability

Source proof and its limitations

For the lower bound the source chooses independent lattice vectors b_j attaining the successive minima. The convex hull of \pm b_j/\lambda_j lies in K and has volume

\frac{2^n|\det(b_1,\ldots,b_n)|}{n!\lambda_1\cdots\lambda_n}.

The determinant is a positive integer multiple of the lattice covolume. Volume comparison gives the lower inequality.

For the upper bound the source proposes a centroid transformation with Jacobian J=\lambda_1\cdots\lambda_n/2^n. Its final volume relation is written as \operatorname{vol}(K)/J, incompatible with the stated Jacobian, and the asserted containment in a fixed primitive cell does not follow merely from absence of lattice differences. This section retains the valid lower-bound argument and records why the written upper-bound argument is not a complete proof of the two-sided theorem. No replacement proof is supplied.
