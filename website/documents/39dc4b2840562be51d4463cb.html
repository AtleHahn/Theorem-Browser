Statement

A Platonic solid is a convex polyhedron in Euclidean three-space whose faces are congruent regular p-gons and at each vertex of which q faces meet. Radial projection from its center onto a concentric sphere maps the faces to congruent regular spherical polygons whose interiors do not overlap and whose union is the sphere.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The claim describes projecting the faces of a regular solid radially onto a sphere to tile its surface. This is elementary spatial geometry with the construction explicitly described. This rating concerns understanding the statement, independently of the difficulty of its proof.

Proof 1: Project the convex boundary along rays — outline

Project every face along rays from the center to the sphere. The faces become regular spherical polygons and together cover the sphere: the convex solid surrounds the center, so each ray meets its boundary in exactly one point. Common boundaries project to common spherical edges, while distinct face interiors remain disjoint.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Follow rays from the center through the convex boundary, using unique intersection and preservation of face symmetries to obtain congruent spherical patches. Elementary spatial geometry suffices; no spherical integration or differential geometry is used.
