A smooth scalar differential equation can have finite-time blowup

A smooth differential equation can have a solution that blows up in finite time. Local uniqueness and smooth laws therefore do not by themselves guarantee a globally continuing deterministic history.

Statement

The smooth autonomous equation y'=y^2, y(0)=1, has a solution y(t)=1/(1-t) on (-\infty,1) that cannot be extended continuously through t=1. Thus local smoothness of the right side does not guarantee a solution for all real times.

Proof 1: The reciprocal solution becomes unbounded — outline

If the right-hand side is only locally Lipschitz, some solutions may not be defined for certain values of t, even if it is smooth. For instance, the differential equation dy/dt=y^2 with initial condition y(0)=1 has the solution y(t)=1/(1-t), which is not defined at t=1. Its unbounded values as t\to1^- rule out a finite continuous extension through that time.

Level justification

Differentiate the explicit reciprocal solution and observe its infinite one-sided limit. Undergraduate calculus and the elementary definition of an ODE solution suffice.
