Statement

For continuous real functions f_0,\ldots,f_3 on an open interval I=(a,b), the identity \int_a^b\sum_{j=0}^{3}f_jh^{(j)}=0 for every smooth compactly supported h holds if and only if f_3\in C^1(I), v_1=f_2-f_3'\in C^1(I), v_0=f_1-v_1'\in C^1(I), and v_0'=f_0.

Proof 1: Eliminate the chain variables in nested order

Use the source-stated higher fundamental lemma: the variational identity is equivalent to existence of C^1 functions u_0,\ldots,u_{n-1} with f_0=u_0', f_j=u_{j-1}+u_j' for 1\le j<n, and f_n=u_{n-1}. For n=3, eliminate the chain variables successively: u_2=f_3, u_1=f_2-f_3', and u_0=f_1-(f_2-f_3')'. The last chain equation is u_0'=f_0. Conversely these choices reconstruct the chain.

The necessary and sufficient condition may be written as f_0-(f_1-(f_2-f_3')')'=0; in general, the brackets cannot be opened because of non-differentiability.
