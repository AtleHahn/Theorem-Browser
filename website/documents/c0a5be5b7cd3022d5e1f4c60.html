Statement

Fix an integer d\ge1, let E\subseteq\mathbb R^d, and let \lambda_d denote Lebesgue measure. A family \mathcal V is a Vitali cover of E if for every x\in E and \delta>0 some U\in\mathcal V contains x and has 0<\operatorname{diam}(U)<\delta. It is regular if a fixed finite constant C satisfies \operatorname{diam}(U)^d\leq C\lambda_d(U) for every U\in\mathcal V. If E\subseteq\mathbb R^d is Lebesgue measurable and \mathcal V is a regular Vitali cover by closed sets, there is a countable disjoint selection covering E up to a Lebesgue-null set, even when \lambda_d(E)=\infty.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement extends the regular-family covering conclusion to sets of possibly infinite measure.

Proof 1: Annulus decomposition outline

Use the finite-measure Vitali covering theorem for regular closed families: a measurable set of finite Lebesgue measure has a finite or countable disjoint selection covering it up to a null set.

The result without assuming that E has finite measure is obtained by applying this covering result, for every integer n\geq0, to the portion of E contained in the open annulus \Omega_n of points x such that n<|x|<n+1. Restrict the cover to members contained in each open annulus; this remains a Vitali cover there. Selections in different annuli are disjoint. The integer-radius spheres omitted by the annuli are Lebesgue-null, as are the countably many exceptional sets.
