Statement

No finite extension K/\mathbb Q of degree greater than one is unramified at every finite prime.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Ramification at finite primes in arbitrary number-field extensions requires substantial algebraic number theory. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Use a prime divisor of the nonunit discriminant — outline

A number field of degree greater than one has discriminant different from 1 and -1, by the discriminant estimate \sqrt{|d_K|}\ge\frac{n^n}{n!}\left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right)^{n/2}>1,\qquad n=[K:\mathbb Q]>1. The discriminant–ramification criterion says that a rational prime ramifies in K exactly when it divides d_K. Consequently such a field cannot be unramified at every finite prime.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Combine the discriminant lower bound with the prime-divisor characterization of ramification. Understanding number-field discriminants, integer rings and ramified primes requires algebraic number theory despite the short integer-divisibility conclusion.
