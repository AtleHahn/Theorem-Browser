Statement

For a nonempty finite word s of length \ell and rational r=m/\ell\ge0, the fractional power s^r is the first m symbols of the infinite repetition of s. A word is e-power-free if it has no finite substring that is a power of exponent at least e. Let \operatorname{RT}(2) be the infimum of positive real thresholds e for which an infinite e-power-free binary word exists. Then \operatorname{RT}(2)=2.

Proof 1: Combine the binary square obstruction with Thue–Morse avoidance — outline

It is not possible for an infinite two-symbol word (or even a two-symbol word of length greater than three) to be square-free.

For alphabets of two symbols, however, there do exist infinite cube-free words, words with no substring of the form sss. One such example is the Thue–Morse sequence itself. More strongly, the Thue–Morse sequence contains no substring that is a power strictly greater than two.

These stated properties give the threshold \operatorname{RT}(2)=2; their proofs are not supplied here.

Level justification

Introductory real analysis and finite-word notation suffice to apply them; proving Thue–Morse avoidance is not part of the supplied argument.
