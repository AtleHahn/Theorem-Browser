Statement

Let M=(M_t)_{t\ge0} be a real-valued continuous local martingale with M_0=0 on a filtered probability space (\Omega,\mathcal F,(\mathcal F^0_t)_{t\ge0},\mathbb P). Use the usual augmentation of its given filtration and denote it by (\mathcal F_t): the filtration is completed by null sets and made right-continuous. A local martingale is an adapted process for which there are stopping times \sigma_n\uparrow\infty almost surely such that each stopped process M_{t\wedge\sigma_n} is a martingale. A martingale is integrable at each time and satisfies \mathbb E[M_t\mid\mathcal F_s]=M_s for s\le t; a stopping time S satisfies \{S\le t\}\in\mathcal F_t for every t. Write Q_t=\langle M\rangle_t for its continuous quadratic variation, the increasing process obtained as the limit in probability of sums of squared increments over deterministic partitions of [0,t] whose mesh tends to zero. Put Q_\infty=\lim_{t\to\infty}Q_t. Suppose Q_\infty=\infty almost surely, and for u\ge0 define

T_u=\inf\{s\ge0:Q_s>u\},\qquad B_u=M_{T_u}.

Then each T_u is almost surely finite, B is standard Brownian motion with respect to the time-changed filtration \mathcal G_u=\mathcal F_{T_u}, and almost surely

M_t=B_{Q_t}\quad\hbox{for every }t\ge0.

For a stopping time S, \mathcal F_S=\{E\in\mathcal F:E\cap\{S\le t\}\in\mathcal F_t\text{ for all }t\ge0\}. Being standard Brownian motion with respect to \mathcal G means that B is adapted and continuous, B_0=0, and B_v-B_u is independent of \mathcal G_u and normally distributed with mean zero and variance v-u for v\ge u.
