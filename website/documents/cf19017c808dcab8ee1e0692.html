Proof 1: Comparison with a telescoping series

For integers N\ge2,

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\sum _{n=1}^{N}{\frac {1}{n^{2}}}&<1+\sum _{n=2}^{N}{\frac {1}{n(n-1)}}\\&=1+\sum _{n=2}^{N}\left({\frac {1}{n-1}}-{\frac {1}{n}}\right)\\&=1+1-{\frac {1}{N}}\;{\stackrel {N\to \infty }{\longrightarrow }}\;2.\end{aligned}}}

This gives us the upper bound 2, and because the infinite sum contains no negative terms, it must converge to a value strictly between 0 and 2.
