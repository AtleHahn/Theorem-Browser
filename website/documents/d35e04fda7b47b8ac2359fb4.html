Statement

Let n\ge1 and 1\le i\le n be integers. The function F_i(A)=\operatorname{tr}(A^i) on complex n\times n matrices is polynomial in the matrix entries, satisfies F_i(SAS^{-1})=F_i(A) for invertible S, and restricts on diagonal matrices to x_1^i+\cdots+x_n^i.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Complex matrix powers; Trace; Conjugation; Polynomial functions. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Use the explicit trace-power polynomial — outline

The required polynomial function is A\mapsto\operatorname{tr}(A^i): it is invariant under conjugation and on a diagonal matrix its value is x_1^i+\cdots+x_n^i.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Matrix multiplication makes each entry polynomial, conjugation preserves the trace of powers, and diagonal powers give the stated sum. Standard linear algebra suffices.
