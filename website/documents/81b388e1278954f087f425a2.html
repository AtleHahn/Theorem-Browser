All uncountable Polish spaces become equivalent when only Borel measurable structure is retained. This demonstrates how mathematical identity depends on the chosen level of structure: spaces with very different topologies can be indistinguishable as measurable spaces.

Statement

Every Polish space is isomorphic as a Borel space to exactly one of the following types: a finite set, the integers \mathbb Z, or the real line \mathbb R. More precisely, a finite Polish space has its finite cardinality type, a countably infinite one is Borel isomorphic to \mathbb Z, and an uncountable one is Borel isomorphic to \mathbb R. A Polish space is a topological space admitting a complete separable metric inducing its topology. The Borel sets form the sigma-algebra generated by open sets. A Borel isomorphism is a bijection whose map and inverse are measurable for these sigma-algebras; finite sets and \mathbb Z carry the discrete topology, and \mathbb R its usual topology. The empty set is included among finite sets.
