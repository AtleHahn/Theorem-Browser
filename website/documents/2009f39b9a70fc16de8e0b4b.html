Statement

Let d\ge1 be an integer, \rho_1,\ldots,\rho_d>0, and c_\alpha\in\mathbb C for \alpha\in\mathbb N_0^d. Write |\alpha|=\sum_i\alpha_i and \rho^\alpha=\prod_i\rho_i^{\alpha_i}. For integers m\ge0, put M_m=\max_{|\alpha|=m}|c_\alpha|\rho^\alpha and S_m=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}|c_\alpha|\rho^\alpha. Then M_m\le S_m\le(m+1)^dM_m.

Level
High school only
Level justification

After unpacking the explicitly defined multi-index notation, this bounds a finite sum of nonnegative numbers by its maximum times an elementary bound on the number of summands. This rating concerns understanding the statement, independently of the difficulty of its proof.

Proof 1: Bound the nonnegative homogeneous sum by its largest term — outline

Choose an index attaining M_m=\max_{|\alpha|=m}|c_\alpha|\rho^\alpha. The source estimate is

M_m\le S_m\le(m+1)^dM_m.

Each summand is nonnegative and at most M_m; the indices have all coordinates between 0 and m.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Each nonnegative term is at most the maximum, and there are at most (m+1)^d possible index tuples. Elementary finite sums, complex modulus and counting suffice.
