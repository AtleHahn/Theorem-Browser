Statement

Let N,T be linear maps on a finite-dimensional complex inner-product space, with NN^*=N^*N. If TN=NT, then TN^*=N^*T.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Complex matrices; Normal matrices; Matrix adjoints; Commuting linear maps. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Commute with the polynomial spectral projections

Since the underlying inner-product space is finite-dimensional, the spectral theorem says that N is of the form

N=\sum_i\lambda_iP_i,

where the \lambda_i are the distinct eigenvalues and the P_i are pairwise orthogonal projections. One expects that TN=NT if and only if TP_i=P_iT.

Indeed, this follows by elementary arguments: all P_i are representable as polynomials of N, and for this reason, if T commutes with N, it has to commute with P_i. Therefore T must also commute with

N^*=\sum_i\overline{\lambda_i}P_i.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the spectral decomposition of a normal matrix and polynomial expressions for its projections. Commutation with N then gives commutation with each projection and the conjugate-eigenvalue combination N star. Standard spectral linear algebra suffices.
