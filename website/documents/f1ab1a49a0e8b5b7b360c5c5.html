Statement

Let X_i be independent random elements in measurable spaces \mathcal X_i, and let f:\prod_i\mathcal X_i\to\mathbb R be measurable. Suppose a measurable nonempty set Y has probability 1-p>0, f is conditionally integrable on Y, and for x,x^{\prime}\in Y, |f(x)-f(x^{\prime})|\le\sum_{i:x_i\ne x_i^{\prime}}c_i, where c_i\ge0 and S=\sum_i c_i^2>0. Set m=\mathbb E[f(X)\mid X\in Y] and C=\sum_i c_i. Then, for \varepsilon>0, \mathbb P(|f(X)-m|\ge\varepsilon)\le2p+2\exp(-2\max(0,\varepsilon-pC)^2/S).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The general event and conditional mean require measure-theoretic probability.

Proof 1: Apply the one-sided theorem to both signs

Use the one-sided high-probability bounded-differences theorem:

\mathbb P(f(X)-m\geq\varepsilon) \leq p+\exp\left(-\frac{2\max(0,\varepsilon-pC)^2}{S}\right),

and, as an immediate consequence,

\mathbb P(|f(X)-m|\geq\varepsilon) \leq2p+2\exp\left(-\frac{2\max(0,\varepsilon-pC)^2}{S}\right).

The source states the consequence without writing out the sign change and union bound.
