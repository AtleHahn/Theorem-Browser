Statement

Let f be a nonzero polynomial of positive degree over \mathbb R or \mathbb C, with f(a)=0. There is a polynomial g with f(X)=(X-a)g(X) and \deg g=\deg f-1. For x\ne a, f(x)=0 if and only if g(x)=0.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The assertion concerns finite polynomials over the real or complex numbers, their roots, factorization, absolute values or reciprocal substitutions. These are elementary algebraic concepts. This rating concerns understanding the statement, independently of the difficulty of its proof.

Proof 1: Divide out the known linear factor — outline

The factor theorem is used to remove known zeros from a polynomial while leaving all other zeros intact, thus producing a lower degree polynomial whose zeros may be easier to find. Starting with the known zero a of f, the method is as follows:

  1. Use the factor theorem to conclude that (X-a) is a factor of f(X).
  2. Compute the polynomial g(X)=f(X)/(X-a), for example using polynomial long division or synthetic division.
  3. Conclude that any root x\ne a of f(x)=0 is a root of g(x)=0. Since the polynomial degree of g is one less than that of f, it is “simpler” to find the remaining zeros by studying g.

Conversely, a zero of g is a zero of the product (X-a)g.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Divide out the known root factor and use the zero-product rule away from that root. School real/complex polynomial algebra suffices.
