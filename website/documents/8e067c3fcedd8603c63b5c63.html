Statement

For every integer k\ge1, the real number \zeta(2k)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-2k} is transcendental, meaning it is not algebraic over \mathbb Q.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Real analysis supplies the convergent series and introductory algebra supplies algebraic versus transcendental numbers. The statement defines the zeta values directly, so analytic continuation or transcendence theory is not needed to understand the assertion.

Proof 1: Rational multiples of powers of pi

By Euler's even-zeta formula, we can conclude that {\displaystyle \zeta (2k)} is always a nonzero rational multiple (nonzero because the defining series is positive) of {\displaystyle \pi ^{2k}}. In particular, since {\displaystyle \pi } and integer powers of it are transcendental, we can conclude at this point that {\displaystyle \zeta (2k)} is irrational, and more precisely, transcendental for all {\displaystyle k\geq 1}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the supplied even-zeta evaluation as a nonzero rational multiple of a power of pi. Algebraicity means satisfying a rational polynomial; substituting a rational multiple of a power into such a polynomial would make pi algebraic. Series analysis and this small polynomial argument suffice, without proving pi transcendental.
