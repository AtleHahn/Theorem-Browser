Statement

Let X_1,X_2,\ldots be independent identically distributed real random variables with density f. Write f_k for the density of X_1+\cdots+X_k. For positive integers m,n, f_{m+n}=f_m*f_n almost everywhere, where (u*v)(x)=\int_{\mathbb R}u(t)v(x-t)\,dt. In particular f_{n+1}=f_n*f.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Densities represent real probabilities by integrals; independence is product factorization. The convolution is explicitly given, and almost-everywhere equality allows changes on null sets. These related integral definitions form a small undergraduate analysis extension; only finite blocks are used.

Proof 1: Convolution recurrence for densities of independent sums — proof

The density of the sum of two independent real-valued random variables equals the convolution of the density functions of the original variables.

Thus, the density of the sum of m+n terms of a sequence of independent identically distributed variables equals the convolution of the densities of the sums of m terms and of n terms. In particular, the density of the sum of n+1 terms equals the convolution of the density of the sum of n terms with the original density (the "sum" of 1 term).

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Split the iid sum into two independent blocks and use the supplied convolution formula for their densities. Undergraduate continuous probability and real integrals suffice; no measure-space construction is used.
