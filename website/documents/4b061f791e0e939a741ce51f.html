Statement

Let n\geq 1 be an integer and let a_1,\ldots,a_n be nonnegative integers. In the formal Laurent polynomial in commuting indeterminates t_1,\ldots,t_n,

P(t_1,\ldots,t_n)=\prod_{\substack{1\leq i,j\leq n\\i\ne j}}\left(1-\frac{t_i}{t_j}\right)^{a_i},

the coefficient of t_1^0\cdots t_n^0 is

\frac{(a_1+\cdots+a_n)!}{a_1!\cdots a_n!}.

Here a Laurent polynomial is a finite sum of monomials whose exponents are integers, possibly negative; the indicated coefficient is its constant term. We use 0!=1 and take an empty product to be 1.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement concerns a coefficient in a finite algebraic expansion and a factorial ratio. The local Laurent-polynomial definition adds only negative integer powers to familiar polynomial algebra. Arbitrary finite tuples and a product over distinct indices require elementary reasoning, without abstract ring theory or analysis.
