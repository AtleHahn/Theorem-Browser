Statement

Let M be a von Neumann algebra with a faithful normal semifinite trace \tau. Let

M_0=\{a\in M:\tau(a^*a)<\infty\},\qquad H=L^2(M,\tau),

where H is the Hilbert-space completion of M_0 for the inner product \langle a,b\rangle=\tau(b^*a). Identify M with its representation on H by left multiplication. The map a\mapsto a^* on M_0 extends to a conjugate-linear isometry J:H\to H with J^2=I, and

JMJ=M',

where M'=\{T\in B(H):Ta=aT\text{ for every }a\in M\} is the commutant and B(H) denotes the bounded linear operators on H.

Here a trace is a nonnegative extended-valued additive, positively homogeneous functional on positive elements of M, invariant under unitary conjugation. Normality means complete additivity on orthogonal families of projections; semifiniteness means that every projection is an orthogonal sum of projections of finite trace; faithfulness means that every nonzero projection has positive trace. The expression \tau(b^*a) uses the linear extension of the trace to its integrable ideal.

Level justification

Operator algebras, traces on positive cones, normality, semifiniteness and noncommutative L2 construction have substantial cumulative prerequisites beyond undergraduate functional analysis or a short reading allowance.

Review notes

  • The trace commutation theorem identifies left and right operator actions and underlies the standard representation of semifinite von Neumann algebras.

Proof 1: Hilbert-algebra commutation and tracial specialization (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Proposition 3.4; Theorem 4.6, pp.34–35; Theorem 1.24(i), pp.11–12).

For \varphi=\tau, traciality gives \mathfrak n_\tau=\mathfrak n_\tau^*=M_0 and \|a^*\|_2=\|a\|_2. Hence the involution extends to the stated antiunitary J and has polar modulus I. Theorem 4.6 identifies its left von Neumann algebra with M, and the first commutant identity in Theorem 1.24(i) gives JMJ=M\prime. Finite-trace projections increasing to 1 provide the cutoffs: \|(1-e)a\|_2^2=\tau(a^*(1-e)a)\to0.
