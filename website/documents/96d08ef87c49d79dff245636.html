Statement

Let W_1,W_2,\ldots be infinite strings over an alphabet of q\ge2 symbols, with every symbol of every string independent and uniform. For a fixed nonempty finite word w, almost surely some W_k starts with w.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The independent coloring or symbol experiment is explicit. Elementary probability gives products for finitely specified choices, and almost surely means probability one. These concrete sequence/path events need no martingale or general stochastic-process machinery to understand.

Proof 1: Apply the block-failure product to independent typists

Write m=|w|. Each string begins with w with probability q^{-m}. Multiplying the failure probabilities for independent trials gives the same result whether we consider one monkey typing n consecutive blocks of text or n monkeys each typing one block, simultaneously and independently. In this case,

X_n=(1-q^{-m})^n

is the probability that none of the first n monkeys types w correctly on their first try. As n approaches infinity, X_n approaches zero. Therefore, at least one of infinitely many monkeys will, with probability equal to one, produce a text using the same number of keystrokes as a perfectly accurate human typist copying it from the original. In terms of the strings W_k, this says that w occurs as a prefix of one of them.

Level

Level justification

Multiply independent failure probabilities and pass to the decreasing-event limit. Ordinary undergraduate probability and geometric-sequence limits suffice.
