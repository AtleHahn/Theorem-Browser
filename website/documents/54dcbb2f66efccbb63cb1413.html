Statement

Let H be a complex inner-product space with inner product B linear in the first variable. Its anti-dual consists of the continuous conjugate-linear functionals, with operator norm. The map J:H\to\overline H' defined by J(x)(y)=B(x,y) is a linear isometric embedding.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex inner products and conjugate-linear scalar functions are linear algebra. The anti-dual here is explicitly the continuous conjugate-linear functionals, with norm the unit-ball supremum; the formula gives the embedding directly. No dual topology or completion is required.

Proof 1: Send a vector to its inner-product functional — outline

For h\in H, define the canonical map

B(h,\cdot):H\to\mathbb C,\qquad y\mapsto B(h,y).

The canonical map from H into its anti-dual \overline H' is the map

H\to\overline H',\qquad x\mapsto B(x,\cdot).

This canonical map is linear and continuous; it is an isometry onto a vector subspace of the anti-dual when (H,B) is an inner-product space.

The source asserts the continuity and isometry; the norm verification is left implicit.
