Statement

Let K be a field and P_1,\ldots,P_k\in K[X] be nonzero pairwise coprime polynomials, k\ge1. Put d_i=\deg P_i, Q=\prod_iP_i, and D=\deg Q. Given polynomials A_i with A_i=0 or \deg A_i<d_i, there is exactly one polynomial P with P=0 or \deg P<D satisfying P\equiv A_i\pmod{P_i} for every i. If D>0 and 1/Q=\sum_i S_i/P_i with S_i=0 or \deg S_i<d_i, put Q_i=Q/P_i and let B_i be the remainder of A_iS_i modulo P_i; then P=\sum_iB_iQ_i. If D=0, all residues and the representative are zero.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory polynomial and ring theory supplies degrees, divisibility, ideals and finite-field arithmetic. The radical consists of elements with a power in the ideal, and Krull dimension counts lengths of prime-ideal chains. These limited ideal-based definitions suffice without schemes, localization or field-extension arithmetic.

Proof 1: Partial fractions and reduced coefficient polynomials — outline

Assume first that D>0. We want to find a polynomial {\displaystyle P(X)}, which satisfies the congruences

{\displaystyle P(X)\equiv A_{i}(X){\pmod {P_{i}(X)}},}

for {\displaystyle i=1,\ldots ,k.}

Consider the polynomials

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}Q(X)&=\prod _{i=1}^{k}P_{i}(X)\\Q_{i}(X)&={\frac {Q(X)}{P_{i}(X)}}.\end{aligned}}}

The partial fraction decomposition of {\displaystyle 1/Q(X)} gives k polynomials {\displaystyle S_{i}(X)} with S_i=0 or \deg S_i<d_i, such that

{\displaystyle {\frac {1}{Q(X)}}=\sum _{i=1}^{k}{\frac {S_{i}(X)}{P_{i}(X)}},}

and thus

{\displaystyle 1=\sum _{i=1}^{k}S_{i}(X)Q_{i}(X).}

Then a solution of the simultaneous congruence system is given by the polynomial

{\displaystyle \sum _{i=1}^{k}A_{i}(X)S_{i}(X)Q_{i}(X).}

In fact, we have

{\displaystyle \sum _{i=1}^{k}A_{i}(X)S_{i}(X)Q_{i}(X)=A_{i}(X)+\sum _{j=1}^{k}(A_{j}(X)-A_{i}(X))S_{j}(X)Q_{j}(X)\equiv A_{i}(X){\pmod {P_{i}(X)}},}

for {\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq k.}

This solution may have a degree at least {\displaystyle D=\sum _{i=1}^{k}d_{i}.} The unique solution that is zero or has degree less than {\displaystyle D} may be deduced by considering the remainder {\displaystyle B_{i}(X)} of the Euclidean division of {\displaystyle A_{i}(X)S_{i}(X)} by {\displaystyle P_{i}(X).} This solution is

{\displaystyle P(X)=\sum _{i=1}^{k}B_{i}(X)Q_{i}(X).}

The source leaves the degree and uniqueness checks implicit. When D=0, all the moduli are nonzero constants, so all the specified remainders and the representative are zero.
