Statement

For any events A,B with probabilities a=\mathbb P(A) and b=\mathbb P(B), \max(0,a+b-1)\le\mathbb P(A\cap B)\le\min(a,b) and \max(a,b)\le\mathbb P(A\cup B)\le\min(1,a+b).

High school only
Level justification

Proof 1: Fréchet bounds for two events — outline

The proofs are elementary. Recall that P(A ∨ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A & B), which implies P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∨ B) = P(A & B). Because all probabilities are no bigger than 1, we know P(A ∨ B) ≤ 1, which implies that P(A) + P(B) − 1 ≤ P(A & B). Because all probabilities are also nonnegative we can similarly say 0 ≤ P(A & B), so max(0, P(A) + P(B) − 1) ≤ P(A & B). This gives the lower bound on the conjunction.

To get the upper bound, first assume both marginal probabilities are positive. Recall that P(A & B) = P(A|B) P(B) = P(B|A) P(A). Because P(A|B) ≤ 1 and P(B|A) ≤ 1, we know P(A & B) ≤ P(A) and P(A & B) ≤ P(B). Therefore, P(A & B) ≤ min(P(A), P(B)), which is the upper bound.

If either marginal probability is zero, the intersection, being a subset of that null event, also has probability zero. Comparable bounds on the disjunction are similarly derived from the same probability identity.
