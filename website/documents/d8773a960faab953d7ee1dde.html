Statement

For a real or complex normed vector space X, the following are equivalent:

  1. There is a Hausdorff locally convex vector topology \tau on X for which the closed norm unit ball B_X=\{x:\|x\|\leq1\} is compact.
  2. There is a Banach space Y over the same scalar field and a surjective linear isometry X\to Y^*, where Y^* is the space of continuous linear scalar-valued functionals on Y, with its operator norm.

A Banach space is a complete normed space. A locally convex vector topology makes addition and scalar multiplication continuous and has a neighborhood basis at zero consisting of convex sets. Hausdorff means that distinct points have disjoint neighborhoods. No equality of \tau with the norm topology is assumed.

Proof 1: Mixed topology and its norm-closed predual (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (§3, Remarks 3.4(d), 3.6(c),(e), and Proposition 3.7 with proof).

Compactness of B_X implies every \tau-continuous seminorm is bounded on B_X, hence bounded by a constant times the norm; therefore \tau is weaker than the norm topology. With the source’s mixed topology \gamma, take Y=(X,\gamma)\prime with its norm-dual norm. Proposition 3.7 makes Y Banach and the evaluation map x\mapsto[y\mapsto y(x)] a surjective isometry. This starts from a normed space over either scalar field, without assuming completeness of X.

Source proof sketch and missing converse construction

For (2) implies (1), identify X with Y^* and take the weak-star topology, the topology of pointwise convergence on Y. It is Hausdorff and locally convex; Banach–Alaoglu makes the closed unit ball compact.

For (1) implies (2), the source says only that this is an application of the bipolar theorem. It does not construct the predual Y or verify the surjective isometry. Thus one direction is supplied, but this section is not a complete proof of the equivalence.
