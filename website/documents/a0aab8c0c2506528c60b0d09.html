Statement

Let A_1,\ldots,A_m\subseteq\Omega, m\ge0, and put N(\omega)=\sum_{j=1}^m1_{A_j}(\omega) and N_k(\omega)=\sum_{J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\},\ |J|=k}1_{\cap_{j\in J}A_j}(\omega). An empty intersection is \Omega, so N_0=1. For a unital ring R and a central indeterminate x, the following identity holds in R[x]: x^{N(\omega)}=\sum_{k=0}^mN_k(\omega)(x-1)^k.

Proof 1: Substitute monomials into the combinatorial identity

Use the combinatorial Schuette–Nesbitt identity c_{N(\omega)}=\sum_{k=0}^mN_k(\omega)\sum_{l=0}^k(-1)^{k-l}\binom kl c_l. As a special case, take for V the polynomial ring R[x] with the indeterminate x.

Substituting cn = xn for n ∈ {0, ..., m} into the combinatorial Schuette–Nesbitt identity and using the binomial formula shows that

{\displaystyle \sum _{n=0}^{m}1_{\{N=n\}}x^{n}=\sum _{k=0}^{m}N_{k}\underbrace {\sum _{l=0}^{k}{\binom {k}{l}}(-1)^{k-l}x^{l}} _{=\,(x-1)^{k}},}

which proves the stated identity, since the left side is x^{N(\omega)}.
