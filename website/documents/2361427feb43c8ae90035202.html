Statement

Let \to be a rewriting relation on proof trees and let o(T) assign an ordinal to each tree. If T\to T' implies o(T)>o(T'), then there is no infinite sequence T_0\to T_1\to\cdots.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Ordinal-valued rankings and arbitrary rewriting relations require set theory beyond calculus and linear algebra.

Mathematical logic

Proof 1: Use well-foundedness of the assigned ordinals

An infinite rewrite sequence would give an infinite strictly decreasing sequence of ordinal values. Ordinals are well-founded, so such a sequence cannot exist.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Translate an infinite rewrite into a strictly decreasing ordinal sequence. The implication itself is elementary, but the ordinal-valued proof-tree ranking framework is not supplied beyond its name and is not confidently a small undergraduate extension; retain beyond undergraduate in doubt.
