Statement

Let 0\le a<b and 0\le g(x)\le f(x) be continuous on [a,b], with A=\int_a^b(f-g)\,dx>0. Let \bar x=A^{-1}\int_a^bx(f-g)\,dx be the centroid coordinate of the region between these graphs. Rotation about the y-axis produces volume V=2\pi\bar x A.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Definite integrals; Centroids; Surfaces and solids of revolution. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Pappus volume formula for a region between graphs by integration

{\displaystyle A=\int _{a}^{b}dA=\int _{a}^{b}[f(x)-g(x)]\,dx}

The {\displaystyle x} component of the centroid of this area is given by:

{\displaystyle {\bar {x}}={\frac {1}{A}}\,\int _{a}^{b}x\,[f(x)-g(x)]\,dx}

If this area is rotated about the y-axis, the volume generated can be calculated using the shell method. It is given by:

{\displaystyle V=2\pi \int _{a}^{b}x\,[f(x)-g(x)]\,dx}

Using the last two equations to eliminate the integral we have:

{\displaystyle V=2\pi {\bar {x}}A}

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the cylindrical-shell integral and the given centroid integral to identify volume as 2 pi times centroid radius times area. Undergraduate calculus suffices.
