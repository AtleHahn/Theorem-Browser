Statement

Let p be a positive prime with p\equiv1\pmod3. Let C be the nonzero cube residue classes modulo p, and let e be a nonzero class not in C. Then C,eC,e^2C partition the nonzero classes, each with (p-1)/3 elements. Here eC denotes multiplication of every element of C by e.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All sets are finite classes modulo the stated integer prime, and their multiplication is specified locally.

Proof 1: Partition primitive-root exponents modulo three — outline

For p\equiv1\pmod3, the non-zero residue classes (mod p) can be divided into three sets, each containing (p−1)/3 numbers. Let e be a cubic non-residue. The first set is the cubic residues; the second one is e times the numbers in the first set, and the third is e2 times the numbers in the first set. Another way to describe this division is to choose a primitive root g (mod p); then the sets C,gC,g^2C consist, respectively, of the numbers whose indices with respect to g (the exponents of its powers) are congruent to 0 (resp. 1, 2) (mod 3). For an arbitrary noncube e, the two nonzero exponent classes may be interchanged. In the vocabulary of group theory, the cubic residues form a subgroup of index 3 of the multiplicative group {\displaystyle (\mathbb {Z} /p\mathbb {Z} )^{\times }} and the three sets are its cosets.

Level justification

Use a primitive root to identify nonzero residues with cyclic exponents and partition those exponents modulo three. Cyclic groups and cosets are introductory algebra; the final group terminology describes the same construction.
