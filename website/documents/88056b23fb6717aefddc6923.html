Statement

Let d\ge2 be an integer. A density matrix is a positive semidefinite Hermitian d\times d matrix of trace one. For its eigenvalues \lambda_i, define S(\rho)=-\sum_i\lambda_i\log\lambda_i, using natural logarithms and 0\log0=0. Put T(\rho,\sigma)=\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}\sqrt{(\rho-\sigma)^*(\rho-\sigma)}. For every 0\le T\le1 there are such matrices at trace distance T with |S(\rho)-S(\sigma)|=T\log(d-1)-T\log T-(1-T)\log(1-T).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Finite-dimensional Hermitian matrices; Positive semidefiniteness and eigenvalues; Trace norm; Finite entropy sums and logarithms. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Use a pure state and a diagonal mixture — outline

Choose the commuting diagonal matrices \rho=\operatorname{Diag}\left(1-T,\frac{T}{d-1},\ldots,\frac{T}{d-1}\right),\qquad\sigma=\operatorname{Diag}(1,0,\ldots,0). These saturate the stated entropy bound at the prescribed distance.
