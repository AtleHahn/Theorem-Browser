Statement

Let P\in\mathbb R[x] be nonconstant. Form the nonzero Sturm sequence P_0=P, P_1=P' and P_{j+1}=-\operatorname{rem}(P_{j-1},P_j), stopping before the first zero remainder. For real t, let V(t) be the number of sign changes in (P_0(t),P_1(t),\ldots) after zero terms are omitted.

If a<b and neither endpoint is a multiple root of P, then the number of distinct real roots of P in (a,b] is V(a)-V(b). In particular, for square-free P no restriction on finite endpoints is needed. One may also take a=-\infty or b=+\infty: evaluate signs there by each polynomial's leading term, and count roots in the corresponding unbounded interval.

Proof 1: Signed remainders and common-factor reduction (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Section1.1, Theorems1.1–1.2, pp.5–6; infinity discussion p.7).

Put g=P_K and Q_j=P_j/g. If c is a root of P of multiplicity m, write P=(x-c)^m h and g=(x-c)^{m-1}k, with h(c)k(c)\ne0. Then Q_0'(c)=h(c)/k(c) and Q_1(c)=m h(c)/k(c), so the reduced sequence loses one variation across c. It is not necessary that Q_1=Q_0'. At a simple endpoint, g is nonzero and deleting P_0=0 gives the right-hand variation. This converts the source’s nonroot-endpoint formula to (a,b]. At either infinity, each nonzero polynomial has the sign of its leading term for sufficiently large absolute argument.
