Statement

Let C be a compact connected Riemann surface, and let D be an effective special divisor on C. Then

2(\ell(D)-1)\le\deg D.

If equality holds, then D=0, or D is a canonical divisor, or C is hyperelliptic and D is linearly equivalent to an integral multiple of a hyperelliptic divisor. A divisor is a finite sum D=\sum_P m_PP with m_P\in\mathbb Z; its degree is \sum_Pm_P, and effective means all m_P\ge0. For a nonzero meromorphic function f, (f) is its zero divisor minus its pole divisor, with orders counted. Put L(D)=\{0\}\cup\{f:(f)+D\ge0\} and \ell(D)=\dim_{\mathbb C}L(D). A canonical divisor K is the divisor of a nonzero meromorphic differential; special means \ell(K-D)>0. Divisors are linearly equivalent if their difference is (f) for some nonzero meromorphic f. Hyperelliptic means that C has genus at least two and admits a holomorphic map of degree two to \mathbb{CP}^1. A hyperelliptic divisor is a fiber of such a map, counted with multiplicity; it has degree two.
