Statement

Let (Y_n)_{n\ge1} be independent identically distributed real random variables with \mathbb E Y_n=0 and \operatorname{Var}(Y_n)=1, and put S_n=\sum_{j=1}^nY_j. Then almost surely \limsup_{n\to\infty}S_n/\sqrt n=+\infty and \liminf_{n\to\infty}S_n/\sqrt n=-\infty. In particular S_n/\sqrt n does not converge almost surely.

Proof 1: Normal tails and the zero–one law

The central limit theorem states that the sums Sn scaled by the factor n−1/2 converge in distribution to a standard normal distribution. Removing finitely many summands changes the normalized sums by a quantity tending to zero, so the limsup events are tail events. By Kolmogorov's zero–one law, for any fixed M, the probability that the event {\displaystyle \limsup _{n}{\frac {S_{n}}{\sqrt {n}}}\geq M} occurs is 0 or 1. Then

{\displaystyle \Pr \left(\limsup _{n}{\frac {S_{n}}{\sqrt {n}}}\geq M\right)\geqslant \limsup _{n}\Pr \left({\frac {S_{n}}{\sqrt {n}}}\geq M\right)=\Pr \left({\mathcal {N}}(0,1)\geq M\right)>0}

so, taking a countable intersection over positive integer thresholds,

{\displaystyle \limsup _{n}{\frac {S_{n}}{\sqrt {n}}}=\infty \qquad {\text{with probability 1.}}}

An identical argument applied to the increments -Y_n shows that

{\displaystyle \liminf _{n}{\frac {S_{n}}{\sqrt {n}}}=-\infty \qquad {\text{with probability 1.}}}

This implies that these quantities cannot converge almost surely.

Level justification

Identify limsup events in the tail sigma algebra, apply the zero-one law and use CLT normal tails to force probability one at all thresholds. Tail sigma algebras and invariance under removing finite coordinates are not developed locally and cannot confidently be treated as a small extension; retain beyond undergraduate provisionally.
