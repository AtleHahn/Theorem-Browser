Statement

Independently draw with replacement from n\geq1 coupon types, each with probability 1/n, and let T be the number of draws until every type has appeared. Here n is an integer. Write H_m=\sum_{j=1}^m1/j, H_m^{(r)}=\sum_{j=1}^m j^{-r} and H_0=0. Then \operatorname{Var}(T)=n^2H_n^{(2)}-nH_n<\pi^2n^2/6.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The sampling experiment is explicit and discrete. Expectation and variance are weighted sums over possible integer waiting times; analysis supplies infinite sums and asymptotic notation, and algebra supplies permutation cycles where used. These concrete additions do not require general stochastic-process theory.

Proof 1: Add independent geometric variances

Let t_i be the time to collect the i-th new coupon after i-1 distinct coupons have been collected. Then T=t_1+\cdots+t_n, and t_i has geometric distribution with parameter p_i=(n-i+1)/n.

Using the independence of random variables ti, we obtain:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\operatorname {Var} (T)&{}=\operatorname {Var} (t_{1}+\cdots +t_{n})\\&{}=\operatorname {Var} (t_{1})+\operatorname {Var} (t_{2})+\cdots +\operatorname {Var} (t_{n})\\&{}={\frac {1-p_{1}}{p_{1}^{2}}}+{\frac {1-p_{2}}{p_{2}^{2}}}+\cdots +{\frac {1-p_{n}}{p_{n}^{2}}}\\&{}=\left({\frac {n^{2}}{n^{2}}}+{\frac {n^{2}}{(n-1)^{2}}}+\cdots +{\frac {n^{2}}{1^{2}}}\right)-\left({\frac {n}{n}}+{\frac {n}{n-1}}+\cdots +{\frac {n}{1}}\right)\\&{}=n^{2}\cdot \left({\frac {1}{1^{2}}}+{\frac {1}{2^{2}}}+\cdots +{\frac {1}{n^{2}}}\right)-n\cdot \left({\frac {1}{1}}+{\frac {1}{2}}+\cdots +{\frac {1}{n}}\right)\\&{}<{\frac {\pi ^{2}}{6}}n^{2}\end{aligned}}}

since {\displaystyle {\frac {\pi ^{2}}{6}}={\frac {1}{1^{2}}}+{\frac {1}{2^{2}}}+\cdots +{\frac {1}{n^{2}}}+\cdots } (see Basel problem).

Level justification

Use independence of successive geometric stages to add their explicit variances, then compare the reciprocal-square sum with the supplied Basel value. Discrete probability and real series suffice.
