Statement

There exist sequences of complex coefficients a_m and positive real numbers b_m=2^m such that, for every integer j\ge0,

\sum_{m=1}^\infty a_m b_m^j=(-1)^j,\qquad \sum_{m=1}^\infty|a_m|b_m^j<\infty.
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Complex sequences; Absolutely convergent series; Powers. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Entire interpolation at powers of two — outline

A solution to this system of equations can be obtained by taking b_m=2^m and seeking an entire function

g(z)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}a_mz^m

such that g(2^j)=(-1)^j for j\geq0, with g(0)=0. That such a function can be constructed follows from the Weierstrass theorem and Mittag-Leffler theorem. Its power series converges absolutely at every 2^j, giving the stated moment equations and absolute convergence.
