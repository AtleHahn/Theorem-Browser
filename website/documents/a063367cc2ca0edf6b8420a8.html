Statement

For a nonempty bounded S\subset\mathbb R^D, define V(S)=\sup_{x\in\operatorname{conv}S}\inf\{\operatorname{Var}(X):X\text{ is finitely supported in }S,\ E X=x\}, where \operatorname{Var}(X)=E\|X-E X\|^2. Then d_H(S,\operatorname{conv}S)^2\le V(S), where d_H(S,\operatorname{conv}S)=\sup_{x\in\operatorname{conv}S}\inf_{s\in S}\|x-s\|.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra and real analysis supply convex combinations, suprema and infima. The finite-support variance is a weighted sum of squared distances, and all other set operations are explicitly defined. These are limited extensions of familiar Euclidean convexity.

Proof 1: Expand the distance and variance definitions — outline

d_H(S,\operatorname{conv}S)^2\leq V(S): Expand their definitions.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

For a finite distribution with mean x, each squared distance to a support point is at least the squared distance from x to S. Average, then take the infimum over such distributions and supremum over x. Euclidean vector norms and real infima/suprema suffice.
