Statement

Let \Omega be a set and D\subseteq\mathcal P(\Omega). Call D a Dynkin system when it contains \Omega, is closed under B\setminus A for A,B\in D with A\subseteq B, and contains the union of each increasing sequence of its members. If A,B\in D and A\cap B=\varnothing, then A\cup B\in D.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The Dynkin-system axioms are explicit closure conditions on a family of subsets. Sigma-algebra means closure under complements and countable unions; ordinary set operations and sequences suffice, with no measure or integration theory.

Proof 1: Complement the difference of two nested members

The Dynkin axioms give \Omega\setminus A\in D. Disjointness gives B\subseteq\Omega\setminus A, so (\Omega\setminus A)\setminus B=\Omega\setminus(A\cup B) belongs to D. Complementing again gives A\cup B\in D.
