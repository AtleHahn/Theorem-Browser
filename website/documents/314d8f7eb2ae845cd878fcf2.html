Statement

Let G be a finite group of order p^a q^b, where p\ne q are primes, a\ge1,b\ge0 are integers, and Z(G)=\{1\}. Then some g\ne1 has a conjugacy class of size q^d for an integer d>0.

Proof 1: Choose an element in a Sylow subgroup center

By the first statement of Sylow's theorem, {\displaystyle G} has a subgroup {\displaystyle S} of order {\displaystyle p^{a}}. Because {\displaystyle S} is a nontrivial {\displaystyle p}-group, its center {\displaystyle Z(S)} is nontrivial. Fix a nontrivial element {\displaystyle g\in Z(S)}. The number of conjugates of {\displaystyle g} is equal to the index of its centralizer G_g=\{h\in G:hg=gh\}, which divides the index {\displaystyle q^{b}} of {\displaystyle S} (because {\displaystyle S} is a subgroup of {\displaystyle G_{g}}). Hence this number is of the form {\displaystyle q^{d}}. Moreover, the integer {\displaystyle d} is strictly positive, since {\displaystyle g} is nontrivial and therefore not central in {\displaystyle G}.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Choose a nonidentity central element of a Sylow p-subgroup and compare its centralizer index with the subgroup index. Introductory finite group theory suffices.
