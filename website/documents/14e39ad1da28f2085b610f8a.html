Statement

For every real x, \arctan x=\frac1{2i}\operatorname{Log}\left(\frac{1+ix}{1-ix}\right), where \arctan x\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2) and \operatorname{Log} is the principal complex logarithm with argument in (-\pi,\pi).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Calculus supplies arctangent and logarithms, and complex polar form supplies the principal argument. The principal complex logarithm is log modulus plus i times this argument; this single definition makes the stated identity and branch restriction accessible.

Proof 1: Take the principal logarithm of the Euler quotient

Let \theta=\arctan x, so -\pi/2<\theta<\pi/2. A calculation with Euler's formula e^{i\theta}=\cos\theta+i\sin\theta gives

\begin{aligned} e^{2i\theta}&=\frac{e^{i\theta}}{e^{-i\theta}} =\frac{\cos\theta+i\sin\theta}{\cos\theta-i\sin\theta} =\frac{1+i\tan\theta}{1-i\tan\theta},\\ \theta&=\frac1{2i}\operatorname{Log}\left(\frac{1+i\tan\theta}{1-i\tan\theta}\right),\\ \arctan x&=\frac1{2i}\operatorname{Log}\left(\frac{1+ix}{1-ix}\right). \end{aligned}

Here 2\theta\in(-\pi,\pi), so the principal logarithm inverts the exponential in the second line.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof uses complex polar notation and the principal logarithm, defined by log modulus plus i times the argument in (-pi,pi). From elementary complex arithmetic and trigonometry this is a small extension. The explicit bound on theta gives 2theta in that argument interval, so no branch crossing occurs. No holomorphic logarithm theory or analytic continuation is required.
