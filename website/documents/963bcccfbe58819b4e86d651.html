Statement

Let H be a complex Hilbert space. A bounded operator P is an orthogonal projection of rank one if and only if P^2=P=P^* and \operatorname{tr}(P)=1. The trace here is the trace of the positive projection P.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement concerns possibly infinite-dimensional Hilbert-space operators and their trace.

Proof 1: Idempotency, orthogonality and trace

These conditions can be understood as {\displaystyle P=P^{2}} means P is idempotent (projecting twice is the same as projecting once), {\displaystyle P=P^{*}} means {\displaystyle Px} and {\displaystyle Px-x} are orthogonal for any {\displaystyle x\in H}. Orthogonal projections have only 0 or 1 as possible eigenvalues, and in this context {\displaystyle \mathbb {tr} (P)=1} means the eigenspace for eigenvalue 1 is one-dimensional, implying P is a projection onto a one-dimensional subspace.

Interpret self-adjoint bounded projections and their trace on an arbitrary complex Hilbert space. The infinite-dimensional operator trace and adjoint framework exceed ordinary linear algebra even though the final rank inference is short.
