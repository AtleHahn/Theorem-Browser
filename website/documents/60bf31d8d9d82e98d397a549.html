Statement

Let A be a finite nonempty alphabet and let a group G act on A by permutations. For integers r\ge1 and 0\le k<m, there exists an integer n\ge m such that every assignment of r colors to the k-dimensional combinatorial cubes in A^n admits an m-dimensional combinatorial cube all of whose k-dimensional subcubes have the same color.

Here a d-dimensional combinatorial cube is a set of words obtained as follows. Choose d disjoint nonempty blocks of coordinate positions. In each remaining position fix a letter of A. For every position i in block j, choose g_i\in G and put g_i(a_j) in that position, where a_1,\ldots,a_d vary independently over A. For d=0 this gives a single fixed word. A k-dimensional subcube is a cube of this form that is contained as a set of words in the larger cube. A coloring assigns a color to each such set, and monochromatic means that these assigned colors agree. A group action here satisfies 1(a)=a and (gh)(a)=g(h(a)).

Level justification

Finite sets, functions and elementary group actions suffice. The explicitly supplied coordinate-block definition is a small extension of undergraduate combinatorics; no structural Ramsey theory is needed to understand the claim.
