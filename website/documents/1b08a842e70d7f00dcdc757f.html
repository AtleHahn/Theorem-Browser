Statement

Let U\subset\mathbb R^d be a bounded domain with piecewise smooth boundary and outward unit normal n. Functions are defined on a neighborhood of \overline U. Write \Delta=\nabla\cdot\nabla and \partial_n f=\nabla f\cdot n. For C^1 scalar \psi and C^1 vector field \Gamma, \int_U(\psi\nabla\cdot\Gamma+\Gamma\cdot\nabla\psi)\,dV=\int_{\partial U}\psi\Gamma\cdot n\,dS.

Proof 1: Apply the divergence theorem to the scalar-vector product

The identity is derived from the divergence theorem by substituting \mathbf F=\psi\mathbf\Gamma, where \psi is a scalar function and \mathbf\Gamma is a vector field:

\int_U(\psi\,\nabla\cdot\mathbf\Gamma+\mathbf\Gamma\cdot\nabla\psi)\,dV =\oint_{\partial U}\psi(\mathbf\Gamma\cdot\mathbf n)\,dS =\oint_{\partial U}\psi\mathbf\Gamma\cdot d\mathbf S.

Here d\mathbf S=\mathbf n\,dS is the oriented surface element.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the Euclidean divergence theorem to the product field and expand its divergence. Undergraduate multivariable calculus suffices.
