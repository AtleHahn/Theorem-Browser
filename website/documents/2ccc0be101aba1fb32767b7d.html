Statement

Let S,P be nonempty subsets of \mathbb R and let U:S\times P\to\mathbb R satisfy U(s',p')-U(s,p')\le U(s',p)-U(s,p) whenever s<s' in S and p<p' in P. For each p, let A(p)=\{s\in S: U(s,p)\ge U(t,p)\text{ for all }t\in S\}. Assume A(p) is nonempty and has both a least and a greatest element. Then both p\mapsto\min A(p) and p\mapsto\max A(p) are nonincreasing. In particular, if every maximizer is unique, the maximizing function is nonincreasing.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The claim concerns parameter-dependent sets of optimizers and monotonicity under a decreasing-differences hypothesis; interpreting the nested quantifiers requires undergraduate analysis or optimization. A bright US college student should be able to understand the statement after about 30 minutes of focused background research; this rating concerns understanding the claim, not proving it.

Proof 1: Apply the order-dual monotonicity theorem

Use the order-dual scalar form of Topkis’s theorem: on a fixed real choice set, decreasing differences imply that the greatest and least maximizing selections, when they exist, are nonincreasing in the parameter. The displayed inequality is precisely decreasing differences. Thus the theorem applies to U on S. When the maximizer is unique, the two extremal selections coincide, giving the final assertion.
