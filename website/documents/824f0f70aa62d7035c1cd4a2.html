Statement

Let 0<p<1 and let M be a nonzero finite-dimensional vector subspace of L^p([0,1]), with its usual topological vector space topology, over \mathbb R or \mathbb C. Every nonzero linear functional on M is continuous but has no continuous linear extension to L^p([0,1]).

Proof 1: Failure of continuous extension in Lp below one

The continuous dual of L^p([0,1]) for 0<p<1 consists only of zero. Since this space is Hausdorff, the finite-dimensional-space theorem of F. Riesz makes M topologically isomorphic to the corresponding Euclidean space; hence every linear functional on M is continuous. An extension of a nonzero functional cannot be zero, so no continuous extension exists.

Level justification

Combine triviality of the continuous dual of Lp below one with Euclidean topology of finite-dimensional subspaces. Nonlocally convex function spaces and continuous duals require advanced functional analysis.
