Statement

Every self-adjoint element a of a C*-algebra A has positive elements a_+,a_-\in A such that a=a_+-a_-, a_+a_-=a_-a_+=0, and |a|=a_++a_-, where |a|=\sqrt{a^*a}.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves C*-algebras; Self-adjoint elements; Positivity; Absolute value. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Max and min scalar functions

The functions {\displaystyle f_{+}(z)=\max(z,0)} and {\displaystyle f_{-}(z)=-\min(z,0)} are continuous functions on {\displaystyle \sigma (a)\subseteq \mathbb {R} } with {\displaystyle \operatorname {Id} (z)=z=f_{+}(z)-f_{-}(z)} and {\displaystyle f_{+}(z)f_{-}(z)=f_{-}(z)f_{+}(z)=0}. Put {\displaystyle a_{+}=f_{+}(a)} and {\displaystyle a_{-}=f_{-}(a)}. According to the spectral mapping theorem, {\displaystyle a_{+}} and {\displaystyle a_{-}} are positive elements for which {\displaystyle a=\operatorname {Id} (a)=(f_{+}-f_{-})(a)=f_{+}(a)-f_{-}(a)=a_{+}-a_{-}} and {\displaystyle a_{+}a_{-}=f_{+}(a)f_{-}(a)=(f_{+}f_{-})(a)=0=(f_{-}f_{+})(a)=f_{-}(a)f_{+}(a)=a_{-}a_{+}} holds. Furthermore, {\textstyle f_{+}(z)+f_{-}(z)=|z|={\sqrt {z^{*}z}}={\sqrt {z^{2}}}}, such that {\textstyle a_{+}+a_{-}=f_{+}(a)+f_{-}(a)=|a|={\sqrt {a^{*}a}}={\sqrt {a^{2}}}} holds.

The functions f_+ and f_- vanish at zero, so their functional calculus values belong to A also when A is nonunital.
