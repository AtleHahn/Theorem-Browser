Statement

The direct-sum functor F:\mathbf{Ab}^2\to\mathbf{Ab} is left adjoint to G(Y)=(Y,Y).

Here F\dashv G means a bijection \operatorname{Hom}(FY,X)\cong\operatorname{Hom}(Y,GX) natural in X and Y.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full assertion requires abelian groups, direct sums, adjunctions.

Proof 1: Source construction outline

If F : Ab2 → Ab assigns to every pair (X1, X2) of abelian groups their direct sum, and if G : Ab → Ab2 is the functor which assigns to every abelian group Y the pair (Y, Y), then F is left adjoint to G, a consequence of the universal property of direct sums. The unit of this adjoint pair is the defining pair of inclusion maps from X1 and X2 into the direct sum, and the counit is the additive map from the direct sum of (X,X) to back to X (sending an element (a,b) of the direct sum to the element a+b of X).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The underlying homomorphism bijection for two abelian groups is accessible from undergraduate algebra. However, the supplied argument also identifies an adjunction of categories through its unit and counit, which requires functors, naturality and the adjunction framework. It is not clear that a student without category theory can learn enough of that framework within the allowance. Keep Beyond undergraduate provisionally rather than silently assessing only the elementwise sum map.

Definition

Treatment:

Implicitly assumed

Statement used:

Given homomorphisms f1:X1->Y and f2:X2->Y, there is a unique homomorphism f:X1 direct-sum X2->Y restricting to f1 and f2, namely f(a,b)=f1(a)+f2(b).

Application in proof:

The correspondence between f and its restrictions gives Hom(X1 direct-sum X2,Y) as a pair of Hom sets; the inclusions give the unit and summation gives the counit.

Application level:

Undergraduate level

Level justification:

The group-homomorphism universal property is a small extension of elementary group algebra. The uncertainty concerns the categorical interpretation of the complete proof, not this concrete property.
