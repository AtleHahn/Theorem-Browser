Statement

Let n\ge1 and let A,B\subseteq\mathbb R^n be nonempty convex sets with disjoint relative interiors. The relative interior is the interior in the affine span. Then there are a unit vector v and c\in\mathbb R with \langle v,a\rangle\le c\le\langle v,b\rangle for all a\in A,b\in B.

Proof 1: Apply the same compact exhaustion to disjoint relative interiors — outline

Approach both {\displaystyle A,B} from the inside by {\displaystyle A_{1}\subseteq A_{2}\subseteq \cdots \subseteq A} and {\displaystyle B_{1}\subseteq B_{2}\subseteq \cdots \subseteq B}, such that each {\displaystyle A_{k},B_{k}} is nonempty, convex, closed and compact, and the unions are the relative interiors {\displaystyle \mathrm {relint} (A),\mathrm {relint} (B)}. Use the standard relative-interior facts: these exhaustions exist, and each set is contained in the closure of its relative interior.

Now by strict separation of disjoint compact convex sets, for each pair {\displaystyle A_{k},B_{k}} there exists some unit vector {\displaystyle v_{k}} and real number {\displaystyle c_{k}}, such that {\displaystyle \langle v_{k},A_{k}\rangle <c_{k}<\langle v_{k},B_{k}\rangle }.

Since the unit sphere is compact, we can take a convergent subsequence, so that {\displaystyle v_{k}\to v}. Let {\displaystyle c_{A}:=\sup _{a\in \mathrm {relint}(A)}\langle v,a\rangle ,c_{B}:=\inf _{b\in \mathrm {relint}(B)}\langle v,b\rangle }. We claim that {\displaystyle c_{A}\leq c_{B}}, thus separating the relative interiors.

Assume not, then there exists some {\displaystyle a\in\mathrm {relint}(A),b\in\mathrm {relint}(B)} such that {\displaystyle \langle v,a\rangle >\langle v,b\rangle }, then since {\displaystyle v_{k}\to v}, for large enough {\displaystyle k}, we have {\displaystyle \langle v_{k},a\rangle >\langle v_{k},b\rangle }, a contradiction since a\in A_k and b\in B_k for all sufficiently large k.

By relative-interior density and continuity, the limiting inequality holds for all a\in A,b\in B. The numbers c_A,c_B are finite: any fixed point of B bounds the first above, and any fixed point of A bounds the second below. Choose c\in[c_A,c_B].
