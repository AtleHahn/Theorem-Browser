Statement

Let G be finite and p prime. Every p-subgroup H is conjugate to a subgroup of any Sylow p-subgroup P; all Sylow p-subgroups are conjugate. Here a Sylow subgroup has order the largest power of p dividing |G|.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Proof 1: Fixed points in the coset action

First, let H be a finite p-group, let \Omega be a finite set acted on by H, and let \Omega_0 denote the set of points of \Omega fixed under the action of H. Any element x\in\Omega not fixed by H will lie in an orbit of order |H|/|H_x| (where H_x denotes the stabilizer), which is a multiple of p. Writing |\Omega| as the sum of |Hx| over all distinct orbits Hx and reducing modulo p gives

|\Omega|\equiv|\Omega_0|\pmod p.

Now let \Omega be the set of left cosets of P in G and let H act on \Omega by left multiplication. Applying this fixed-point congruence to H on \Omega, we see that

|\Omega_0|\equiv|\Omega|=[G:P]\pmod p.

Now p\nmid[G:P] by definition, so p\nmid|\Omega_0|, hence in particular |\Omega_0|\ne0, so there exists some gP\in\Omega_0. With this gP, we have hgP=gP for all h\in H, so g^{-1}HgP=P and therefore g^{-1}Hg\le P. Furthermore, if H is a Sylow p-subgroup, then |g^{-1}Hg|=|H|=|P|, so that g^{-1}Hg=P.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Count p-group orbits modulo p in the coset action and use a fixed coset to obtain subgroup containment. Introductory finite group theory suffices.
