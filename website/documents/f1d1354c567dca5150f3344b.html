Statement

Let \sigma:(X,\mu)\to(Y,\nu) be a measurable map of probability spaces with \sigma_\#\mu=\nu. For every integrable f:Y\to\mathbb R, \int_X f\circ\sigma\,d\mu=\int_Y f\,d\nu.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The stated maps and integral identities use probability measures or Lebesgue measure theory, beyond the calculus/linear-algebra baseline.

Mathematical Field
Measure theory
Required topics

Review notes
none

Proof 1: Substitute the pushforward measure

Writing \sigma^*f=f\circ\sigma for the pullback, the pushforward integration formula and \sigma_\#\mu=\nu give

\int_X(f\circ\sigma)(x)\,\mu(dx) =\int_X(\sigma^*f)(x)\,\mu(dx) =\int_Y f(y)\,(\sigma_\#\mu)(dy) =\int_Y f(y)\,\nu(dy),

where f is \nu-integrable and f\circ\sigma is \mu-integrable.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the pushforward integration formula and replace the pushforward by nu. The deduction is short but general measurable spaces, integrable functions and pushforward measures are cumulative measure-theoretic prerequisites.
