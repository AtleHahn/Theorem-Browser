Statement

Let G=(V,E) be a finite simple bipartite graph with nonnegative integer edge weights w_e. A matching has weight \sum_{e\in M}w_e. A weighted vertex cover is a multiset of vertices, with multiplicities y_v\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0} satisfying y_u+y_v\ge w_{uv} for every edge uv. The maximum matching weight equals the minimum cover cardinality \sum_vy_v.

Proof 1: Use weighted linear-programming duality and integrality

Let A_G be the vertex-edge incidence matrix; \mathbf1_V and \mathbf0_V,\mathbf0_E are the all-one and zero vectors on the indicated index sets.

The maximum ***w-***weight of a fractional matching is given by the LP:

Maximize \mathbf w\cdot\mathbf x

Subject to: \mathbf x\ge\mathbf0_E

A_G\mathbf x\le\mathbf1_V.

And the minimum number of vertices in a fractional ***w-***vertex-cover is given by the dual LP:

Minimize \mathbf1_V\cdot\mathbf y

Subject to: \mathbf y\ge\mathbf0_V

A_G^T\mathbf y\ge\mathbf w.

The linear-programming duality theorem implies that the optimal values are equal (for any graph), and the fact that the graph is bipartite implies that these programs have optimal solutions in which all values are integers.

Level justification

The primal and dual programs are displayed explicitly. Linear programming duality and integer optimality for these bipartite programs are a modest extension of linear algebra and finite optimization; their proofs and polyhedral theory are not needed.
