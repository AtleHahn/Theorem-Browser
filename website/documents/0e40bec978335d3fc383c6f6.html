Statement

In a nondegenerate Euclidean triangle, let a,b,c be the side lengths opposite angles \alpha,\beta,\gamma. Then \frac{a-b}{a+b}=\frac{\tan((\alpha-\beta)/2)}{\tan((\alpha+\beta)/2)}.

Proof 1: Sine law and sum-to-product factorization

To prove the law of tangents one can start with the law of sines:

{\displaystyle {\frac {a}{\sin \alpha }}={\frac {b}{\sin \beta }}=d,}

where ⁠{\displaystyle d}⁠ is the diameter of the circumcircle, so that ⁠{\displaystyle a=d\sin \alpha }⁠ and ⁠{\displaystyle b=d\sin \beta }⁠.

It follows that

{\displaystyle {\frac {a-b}{a+b}}={\frac {d\sin \alpha -d\sin \beta }{d\sin \alpha +d\sin \beta }}={\frac {\sin \alpha -\sin \beta }{\sin \alpha +\sin \beta }}.}

Using the trigonometric identity, the factor formula for sines specifically

{\displaystyle \sin \alpha \pm \sin \beta =2\sin {\tfrac {1}{2}}(\alpha \pm \beta )\,\cos {\tfrac {1}{2}}(\alpha \mp \beta ),}

we get

{\displaystyle {\frac {a-b}{a+b}}={\frac {2\sin {\tfrac {1}{2}}(\alpha -\beta )\,\cos {\tfrac {1}{2}}(\alpha +\beta )}{2\sin {\tfrac {1}{2}}(\alpha +\beta )\,\cos {\tfrac {1}{2}}(\alpha -\beta )}}={\frac {\sin {\tfrac {1}{2}}(\alpha -\beta )}{\cos {\tfrac {1}{2}}(\alpha -\beta )}}{\Bigg /}{\frac {\sin {\tfrac {1}{2}}(\alpha +\beta )}{\cos {\tfrac {1}{2}}(\alpha +\beta )}}={\frac {\tan {\tfrac {1}{2}}(\alpha -\beta )}{\tan {\tfrac {1}{2}}(\alpha +\beta )}}.}

All denominators are nonzero because 0<\alpha+\beta<\pi and |\alpha-\beta|<\pi.

Proof 2: Divide the Mollweide sum and difference identities

Mollweide's stated identities, using \gamma=\pi-\alpha-\beta, read

\frac{a+b}{c}=\frac{\cos((\alpha-\beta)/2)}{\cos((\alpha+\beta)/2)},\qquad \frac{a-b}{c}=\frac{\sin((\alpha-\beta)/2)}{\sin((\alpha+\beta)/2)}.

Divide the second by the first to obtain the assertion. All divisors are positive, since 0<(\alpha+\beta)/2<\pi/2 and |\alpha-\beta|/2<\pi/2. This also includes a=b, as in the source's later nonsingular form.
