Statement

Let M=(M_{ij})_{i,j=1}^k be a complex Hermitian positive-semidefinite matrix, with each block M_{ij} of size m\times m. Then \det M\le\det[\det(M_{ij})]_{i,j=1}^k.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Complex Hermitian matrices; Positive semidefiniteness; Determinants; Eigenvalues; Characteristic polynomial coefficients. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Take the top characteristic coefficient

The source writes the characteristic polynomial as p_A(t)=\sum_{r=0}^m(-1)^re_r(A)t^{m-r}, with e_r(A)=\operatorname{tr}(\Lambda^r A), and imports the coefficient inequality \det M\le\left(\frac{\det[e_r(M_{ij})]}{\binom mr}\right)^{m/r},\qquad 1\le r\le m. As the source notes, set r=m. The constant characteristic coefficient is e_m(A)=\det A, while \binom mm=1 and m/m=1, so the displayed inequality becomes Thompson’s inequality.

Level justification

Set r=m in the explicit coefficient inequality and identify the constant characteristic coefficient with determinant. Finite Hermitian matrix theory suffices; exterior powers are an optional description of the coefficients, not needed for the application.
