Statement

Let a<b be integers and f:(a,b]\to\mathbb R differentiable, with monotone derivative that does not change sign and |f'(x)|\le\delta for a real 0<\delta<1. Define f(a)=\lim_{x\downarrow a}f(x), the finite limit supplied by the bounded derivative. Then

\left|\sum_{a<k\le b}e^{2\pi if(k)}-\int_a^b e^{2\pi if(x)}\,dx-\frac12e^{2\pi if(b)}+\frac12e^{2\pi if(a)}\right|\le\frac{2\delta}{1-\delta}.

The sum is over integers k, and i^2=-1.

Proof 1: Paired Poisson terms and explicit endpoint bounds (external)

The following editorial argument completes the stated scope. A supporting source is Topics in Analytic Number Theory (Assing Proposition 2, pp.54–55, for the Poisson summation method; explicit refinement and endpoint treatment given below.).

Here is an explicit-constant refinement of the source’s summation method: bounded monotone derivative, together with Darboux, gives a continuous derivative in the interior and one-sided limits at both endpoints, hence a C1 extension to the closed interval. For integer endpoints the symmetric Poisson formula identifies the trapezoidal error with the sum over nonzero integers nu of I_nu=integral_a^b exp(2 pi i(f(x)-nu*x)) dx. Integrate by parts in the Lebesgue-Stieltjes sense, valid because f prime is continuous of bounded variation. Pair nu=m and -m. Since endpoints are integers, their boundary terms combine; absolute boundary bound is 2 delta/[pi(m^2-delta^2)]. The total variation bound for the two remaining integrals is delta/[pi(m^2-delta^2)], using that f prime is monotone and lies either in [0,delta] or [-delta,0]. Thus the pair is at most 3 delta/[pi(m^2-delta^2)]. Summing and using m^2-delta^2 >= (1-delta)m^2 gives at most (pi/2)delta/(1-delta), which is smaller than the claimed 2 delta/(1-delta). Paired absolute convergence justifies the symmetric Poisson limit. The endpoint-weighted sum is exactly the expression stated in the statement.
