Statement

In an absolute (neutral) geometry plane, let ABC be a nondegenerate triangle and extend side BC through C to a point D. Then

\angle ACD>\angle CAB,\qquad \angle ACD>\angle ABC.

The exterior angle \angle ACD is formed by the extended side and side CA; the two angles on the right are the remote interior angles, at the other vertices. An absolute geometry plane satisfies the usual incidence, order and congruence axioms of plane geometry without assuming the parallel postulate. In particular, the assertion applies in both Euclidean and hyperbolic geometry.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

High-school geometry explains the configuration and inequalities; the complete claim also quantifies over neutral geometries. Introductory axiomatic reasoning plus a short explanation of omitting the parallel postulate suffices, without learning hyperbolic trigonometry or differential geometry.

Proof 1: Midpoint congruence with explicit half-plane placement (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (III.2, Theorem1, p.99; neutral supplement, TheoremIII.2.1).

Let E be the midpoint of AC and choose F beyond E on BE with EF=EB. SAS gives \angle BAE=\angle ECF=\angle ACF; the printed \angle CFE=\angle ACF is a label error. For the crucial placement, A,E lie on the same side of BC, and E,F do too because the line BEF meets BC only at B, outside EF. Hence A,F lie on the same side of BC. Meanwhile B,F are on opposite sides of AC because BF crosses it at E, and B,D are on opposite sides because BD crosses it at C. Thus D,F lie on the same side of AC. These two half-plane conditions put F strictly inside \angle ACD, proving the first strict inequality. Relabeling and vertical angles give the other. The neutral-geometry supplement confirms this proof uses no parallel postulate.

The source presents Euclid’s midpoint-and-congruence argument but explicitly identifies an unproved betweenness assertion: the constructed point must lie inside the exterior angle. It says this follows from a complete axiom system but does not supply that argument. A full usable proof in absolute geometry is therefore not retained.
