Statement

In an absolute (neutral) geometry plane, let ABC be a nondegenerate triangle and extend side BC through C to a point D. Then

\angle ACD>\angle CAB,\qquad \angle ACD>\angle ABC.

The exterior angle \angle ACD is formed by the extended side and side CA; the two angles on the right are the remote interior angles, at the other vertices. An absolute geometry plane satisfies the usual incidence, order and congruence axioms of plane geometry without assuming the parallel postulate. In particular, the assertion applies in both Euclidean and hyperbolic geometry.

Level justification

High-school geometry explains the configuration and inequalities; the complete claim also quantifies over neutral geometries. Introductory axiomatic reasoning plus a short explanation of omitting the parallel postulate suffices, without learning hyperbolic trigonometry or differential geometry.

Proof availability

The source presents Euclid’s midpoint-and-congruence argument but explicitly identifies an unproved betweenness assertion: the constructed point must lie inside the exterior angle. It says this follows from a complete axiom system but does not supply that argument. A full usable proof in absolute geometry is therefore not retained. The Euclidean angle-sum proof concerns the separate stronger theorem.
