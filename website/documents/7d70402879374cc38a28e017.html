Statement

Let 1<p<\infty.

For every sequence (a_n)_{n\ge1} of nonnegative real numbers,

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{a_1+\cdots+a_n}{n}\right)^p \le \left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)^p\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n^p.

If the right side is finite, equality holds if and only if every a_n is zero.

For every nonnegative measurable function f:(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty),

\int_0^\infty\left(\frac1x\int_0^x f(t)\,dt\right)^p\,dx \le\left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)^p\int_0^\infty f(x)^p\,dx.

The integrals are Lebesgue integrals. If the right side is finite, equality holds if and only if f=0 almost everywhere. Both inequalities allow infinite nonnegative sums or integrals.

Level

Level justification

The discrete form is elementary to state, but the complete measurable-function formulation uses Lebesgue integration and almost-everywhere equality. Their cumulative burden is not confidently a small extension of the assumed introductory analysis background.

Proof 1: Summation and integration by parts with equality defects (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorem6, pp.726–727; Elliott proof in Section10, pp.728–729; Remark7).

The following equality arguments complete the linked bounds. Put b_n=(a_1+\cdots+a_n)/n and b_0=0. Elliott’s Young step has the nonnegative defect

D_n=(n-1)\bigl(b_{n-1}^p+(p-1)b_n^p-pb_{n-1}b_n^{p-1}\bigr),

and summation gives p\sum_{n\le N}a_nb_n^{p-1}=(p-1)\sum_{n\le N}b_n^p+Nb_N^p+\sum_{n\le N}D_n. Equality with finite right side forces every D_n=0, hence all b_n equal for n\ge1; summability then forces every a_n=0.

For the integral, set F(x)=\int_0^x f and q=p/(p-1). The endpoint terms x^{1-p}F(x)^p tend to zero at both ends when f\in L^p. Integration by parts and equality in Hölder then force f=F/(qx) almost everywhere. Absolute continuity yields F(x)=Cx^{1/q}; for C\ne0, its derivative has pth power proportional to 1/x, which is not integrable. Thus equality forces f=0 almost everywhere. Infinite right sides satisfy the extended nonnegative inequalities automatically.
