Statement

Let I\subseteq\mathbb R be a nondegenerate interval and let f:I\to\mathbb R be continuous and injective. Write g:f(I)\to I for its inverse. If f is differentiable at x_0\in I and f'(x_0)\ne0, then g is differentiable at y_0=f(x_0) and

g'(y_0)=\frac{1}{f'(x_0)}=\frac{1}{f'(g(y_0))}.

At an included endpoint of the intervals, derivatives are understood as the corresponding one-sided derivatives.
