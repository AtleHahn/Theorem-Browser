Statement

Let n\geq1 be an integer and let \mu be a Gaussian probability measure on \mathbb R^n centered at the origin. Thus \mu is the distribution of AZ, where A is a real n\times n matrix and the coordinates of Z are independent standard normal random variables; singular A is allowed.

For any \mu-measurable convex sets E,F\subseteq\mathbb R^n symmetric about the origin,

\mu(E\cap F)\geq\mu(E)\mu(F).

Convex means that the line segment between any two points of the set lies in the set, and symmetry means E=-E and F=-F.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Matrix linear transformations and convexity are undergraduate concepts. Independent standard normal coordinates and probabilities give a concrete understanding of the measure, including degenerate cases, as a small extension of elementary probability and calculus; no measure-theoretic proof machinery is needed.
