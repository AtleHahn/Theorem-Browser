Statement

Let 0<T\le\infty, \lambda>-1, and let \varphi:(0,T)\to\mathbb R be measurable with \varphi(t)=t^\lambda g(t), where g is infinitely continuously differentiable on [0,\delta] for some 0<\delta<T and g(0)\ne0. Suppose either

|\varphi(t)|\le Ke^{bt}\quad(\delta\le t<T)

for constants K>0 and b\in\mathbb R, or

\int_0^T|\varphi(t)|\,dt<\infty.

Then the Laplace integral exists for all sufficiently large positive x, and, for every integer N\ge0,

\int_0^T e^{-xt}\varphi(t)\,dt =\sum_{n=0}^{N}\frac{g^{(n)}(0)\Gamma(\lambda+n+1)}{n!x^{\lambda+n+1}} +O(x^{-\lambda-N-2})\qquad(x\to+\infty).

Here \Gamma(s)=\int_0^\infty u^{s-1}e^{-u}\,du for s>0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Undergraduate analysis and calculus suffice; gamma and asymptotic remainder are explicit, and the additional integration condition is direct.

Proof 1: Taylor expansion with a weighted tail estimate (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Section1.2b, Lemma1.69 and its proof, printed pp.13–14).

Set \alpha=\lambda+1 and \beta=1. Choose 0<a<\delta. Under the exponential-tail hypothesis choose \nu>\max(b,0); under absolute integrability take \nu=0. In either case W=\int_a^T e^{-\nu t}|\varphi(t)|\,dt<\infty, and the tail is bounded by We^{-(x-\nu)a}. This also proves convergence for sufficiently large x. On [0,a], Taylor’s remainder is bounded by Ct^{N+1}; multiplying by t^\lambda e^{-xt} and extending its bound to infinity gives C\Gamma(\lambda+N+2)x^{-\lambda-N-2}. Extending each Taylor monomial integral to infinity changes it only by an exponentially small quantity. For finite T the amplitude can equivalently be extended by zero.
