Statement

For every sequence (a_n)_{n\geq1} of nonnegative real numbers,

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(a_1a_2\cdots a_n)^{1/n}\leq e\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n,

where e=\exp(1) and sums are interpreted in [0,\infty]. The constant e is optimal: replacing it by any smaller real constant makes the inequality false for some nonnegative sequence. If 0<\sum_{n\geq1}a_n<\infty, the inequality is strict.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Infinite series and convergence require introductory analysis; optimality means failure for a smaller constant and adds no specialized prerequisite.

Proof 1: Weighted AM–GM and finite-support sharpness (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (§4, Theorem 4.2 and Pólya specialization, pp.252–253).

The weighted AM–GM proof also allows zero terms. Its coefficients (1+1/n)^n<e make the inequality strict whenever the finite total is positive. For sharpness, take a_n=1/n for n\le N and zero thereafter. The ratio is \sum_{n\le N}(n!)^{-1/n}/H_N\to e: integral comparison gives \log(n!)=n\log n-n+O(\log n), hence n(n!)^{-1/n}\to e, while H_N\to\infty. Infinite right-hand sides make the inequality automatic.
