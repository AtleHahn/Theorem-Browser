Statement

Let U,V\subset\mathbb R be bounded open sets. If U=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^m A_j and V=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^m B_j are finite disjoint partitions with each A_j congruent to B_j, then U,V have equal Lebesgue length.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Basic topology supplies bounded open subsets of the line or plane, and their length or area can be understood as the usual countably additive extension of elementary length or area. The pieces are arbitrary sets, explicitly not required to have individual measures; the assertion compares only the open sets. This is a small set-geometric extension, with no general integration theory needed.

Proof 1: Apply Banach length to the finite partitions — outline

As shown by Banach, it is possible to define a "length" for all bounded subsets of the real line in such a way that congruent sets will have equal "length". This Banach measure, however, is only finitely additive, so it is not a measure in the full sense, but it equals the Lebesgue measure on sets for which the latter exists. This implies that if two bounded open subsets of the real line are equi-decomposable then they have equal length.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the explicitly stated finitely additive length to the finitely many paired pieces, whose values agree by congruence. Their sums agree and equal the usual lengths of the two open sets. The application requires only these finite set-function axioms, a small extension of undergraduate sets and length; it does not require construction of Banach length.
