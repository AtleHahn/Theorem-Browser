Statement

Let k be a commutative unital ring, H\le G a subgroup and N a kH-module. Define \operatorname{Coind}_H^G N=\operatorname{Hom}_{kH}(kG,N), with (g\cdot f)(s)=f(sg). For every integer n\ge0, H^n(G,\operatorname{Coind}_H^G N)\cong H^n(H,N). Here H^n(G,V)=\operatorname{Ext}_{kG}^n(k,V) uses the trivial module k.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Group cohomology as Ext; Group algebras; Coinduced modules; Trivial group representations. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Specialize the first Ext argument to the trivial module

Use the projective change-of-rings theorem \operatorname{Ext}_{R}^n({}_RM,N)\cong\operatorname{Ext}_{S}^n(M,\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(S,N)). Take R=kH, S=kG and specialize M to the trivial module k. The group algebra kG is projective as a left kH-module. Interpreting the Ext groups with trivial first argument as group cohomology gives the displayed isomorphism.
