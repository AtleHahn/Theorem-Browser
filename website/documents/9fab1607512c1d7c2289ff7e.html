Statement

Let M\in\mathbb C^{n\times n}, and let \sigma_1\ge\cdots\ge\sigma_n\ge0 be its singular values, the square roots of the eigenvalues of M^*M. For 1\le k\le n,

\sigma_k=\max_{\dim S=k}\min_{x\in S,\|x\|=1}\|Mx\| =\min_{\dim S=n-k+1}\max_{x\in S,\|x\|=1}\|Mx\|.

Proof 1: Apply Courant–Fischer to the squared norm

The singular values {σk} of a square matrix M are the square roots of the eigenvalues of M*M (equivalently MM*). An immediate consequence of the first Courant–Fischer equality for the Hermitian matrix M^*M is:

{\displaystyle \sigma _{k}^{\downarrow }=\max _{S:\dim(S)=k}\min _{x\in S,\|x\|=1}(M^{*}Mx,x)^{\frac {1}{2}}=\max _{S:\dim(S)=k}\min _{x\in S,\|x\|=1}\|Mx\|.}

Similarly,

{\displaystyle \sigma _{k}^{\downarrow }=\min _{S:\dim(S)=n-k+1}\max _{x\in S,\|x\|=1}\|Mx\|.}

Here {\displaystyle \sigma _{k}^{\downarrow }} denotes the kth entry in the decreasing sequence of the singular values, so that {\displaystyle \sigma _{1}^{\downarrow }\geq \sigma _{2}^{\downarrow }\geq \cdots }.
