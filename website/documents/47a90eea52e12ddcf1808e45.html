Statement

For a positive integer n, let \varphi(n) count the integers 1\le k\le n with \gcd(k,n)=1. Define the multiplicative Möbius function by \mu(1)=1, \mu(p)=-1 for primes p, and \mu(p^k)=0 for k\ge2; multiplicative means values multiply on coprime positive arguments. For every positive integer n, \varphi(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)n/d=n\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)/d.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All quantities are finite integer arithmetic with μ specified by its prime-factor values.

Proof 1: Apply finite Möbius inversion to the divisor sum

Apply Möbius inversion to \sum_{d\mid n}\varphi(d)=n. This gives \varphi(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)(n/d)=n\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)/d.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Apply the explicitly stated finite inversion formula to the divisor sum and factor out n. The Mobius function is defined locally through prime factors, so elementary divisors and finite sums suffice.

Proof 2: Expand the finite Euler product — outline

The formula may also be derived from Euler's product formula

\varphi(n)=n\prod_{p\mid n}\left(1-\frac1p\right)

by multiplying out \prod_{p\mid n}(1-\frac1p) to get \sum_{d\mid n}\frac{\mu(d)}d. The source leaves the term-by-term identification implicit.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Expand the finite product; squarefree divisors contribute the sign prescribed by the locally defined Mobius function. School factorization and finite sums suffice.
