Statement

Let n\ge1 be an integer and let f_i:[0,1]^n\to\mathbb R be continuous, 1\le i\le n. If \sup_{x_i=0}f_i\le a_i\le\inf_{x_i=1}f_i for each i, then some x\in[0,1]^n satisfies f_i(x)=a_i for all i.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Continuous real functions on a finite-dimensional cube; Suprema and infima on compact faces. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Translate the target values to zero

This statement can be reduced to the Poincaré–Miranda simultaneous-zero theorem by a simple translation of axes,

x_i'=x_i,\qquad y_i'=y_i-a_i \qquad\text{for all }i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

where x_i are the coordinates in the domain and y_i are the coordinates in the codomain.

Thus put g_i(x)=f_i(x)-a_i. The hypotheses make g_i nonpositive on x_i=0 and nonnegative on x_i=1. The Poincaré–Miranda theorem in this unit-cube convention gives a common zero of the g_i, exactly a simultaneous solution f_i(x)=a_i.
