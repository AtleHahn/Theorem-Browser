Statement

For every positive integer k, there exist positive integers a,d such that a,a+d,\ldots,a+(k-1)d are all prime.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement uses elementary integer arithmetic; the argument invokes the advanced relative-density Green–Tao theorem.

Proof 1: Apply the relative-density theorem to all primes

Use the relative-density Green–Tao theorem: if a subset A of the primes satisfies \limsup_{N\to\infty}|A\cap[1,N]|/\pi(N)>0, where \pi(N) counts primes at most N, then A contains arithmetic progressions of every finite length. Take A to be all primes. The ratio is identically 1 for N\ge2, so the theorem applies.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the supplied relative-density theorem to all primes, whose density ratio is one. Undergraduate sequence limits suffice to understand the limsup hypothesis; no transference proof of Green–Tao is required.
