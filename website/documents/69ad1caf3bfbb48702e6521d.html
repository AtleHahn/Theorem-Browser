Statement

Put \omega=(-1+i\sqrt3)/2 and E=\mathbb Z[\omega]=\{a+b\omega:a,b\in\mathbb Z\}. For z=a+b\omega put N(z)=a^2-ab+b^2. Congruence modulo \lambda\in E means that the difference is divisible by \lambda in E. An element is called primary if it is coprime to 3 and congruent to an ordinary integer modulo (1-\omega)^2 (equivalently, congruent to 1 or -1 modulo 3 in E).

For a prime element \pi\in E with N(\pi)\ne3 and \pi\nmid\gamma, define (\gamma/\pi)_3 to be the unique element of \{1,\omega,\omega^2\} satisfying

(\gamma/\pi)_3\equiv\gamma^{(N(\pi)-1)/3}\pmod\pi.

Extend the symbol multiplicatively in a nonzero denominator coprime to 3: if \lambda=u\prod_j\pi_j^{e_j} with u a unit and \gamma coprime to \lambda, put

(\gamma/\lambda)_3=\prod_j(\gamma/\pi_j)_3^{e_j},

with the empty product equal to 1.

For every pair of coprime primary elements \alpha,\beta\in E,

\left(\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\right)_3=\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)_3.

Proof 1: Cubic character sums and conjugate-prime reduction (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Section2 pp.396–398; equal-norm repair: https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~revans/errata/ item1, with explicit reconstructed trace argument here.).

Supporting passages: Berndt–Evans–Williams errata, item 1.

The linked Section 2 proves the distinct-norm prime case using Gauss and Jacobi sums. The following trace argument supplies the conjugate-prime case and the extension to composite arguments. For coprime primary conjugate primes pi,barpi of norm p, put t=pi+barpi, a nonzero rational integer prime to3 and p. The primary normalization gives t≡±2 mod3; and |t|<=2sqrt(p)<p for p>=7, so every prime factor of t has norm different from p. Distinct-norm reciprocity extended multiplicatively therefore gives (barpi/pi)_3=(t/pi)_3=(pi/t)_3 and (pi/barpi)_3=(t/barpi)_3=(barpi/t)_3. Since barpi≡-pi mod t and -1 is a cube, the right sides coincide. Thus the equal-norm prime case follows. Normalize each prime factor to -1 mod3; a primary element then has only a sign as residual unit, and signs contribute1 to cubic symbols. Multiplication over prime factors proves the full statement, with unit cases automatic.
