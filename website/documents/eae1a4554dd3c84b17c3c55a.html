Statement

Let K be a finite extension of \mathbb Q_p, let A be its ring of integers, and fix a uniformizer \pi of A. Every nonzero f(z)\in A[[z]] has a unique factorization f(z)=\pi^n u(z)F(z) with integer n\ge0, a unit u(z)\in A[[z]], and a distinguished polynomial F(z)\in A[z]: F is monic and all its nonleading coefficients lie in \pi A. The constant polynomial 1 is allowed.

Proof 1: Specialize formal preparation after extracting the uniformizer power outline

Use the Weierstrass preparation theorem for formal power series over a complete local ring A: for any power series f=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}a_jz^j\in A[[z]] such that not all a_j are in the maximal ideal \mathfrak m of A, there is a unique unit u in A[[z]] and a polynomial F of the form

F=z^s+b_{s-1}z^{s-1}+\cdots+b_0

with b_i\in\mathfrak m (a distinguished polynomial) such that

f=uF.

This applies to the ring of integers in a p-adic field. In this case the theorem says that a nonzero power series f(z) can always be uniquely factored as \pi^n u(z)F(z), where u(z) is a unit in the ring of power series, F(z) is a distinguished polynomial (monic, with the coefficients of the non-leading terms each in the maximal ideal), and \pi is a fixed uniformizer.
