Statement

Let n\ge1, let \mathcal X_i be nonempty sets, and let f:\prod_{i=1}^n\mathcal X_i\to\mathbb R. Suppose c_i\ge0 and changing only coordinate i changes f by at most c_i. Then |f(x)-f(y)|\le\sum_i c_i for every x,y, so f is bounded.

Level
High school only
Level justification

A point of the finite product is just a tuple choosing one element from each set. The hypothesis bounds the change in a real output when one coordinate is replaced; the conclusion adds these finitely many bounds. Finite tuples, functions and absolute-value inequalities suffice to understand the complete claim, even when the coordinate sets are arbitrary.

Proof 1: Replace the coordinates one at a time

Write x_{i\rightharpoondown j} for the tuple (x_i,\ldots,x_j), with an empty tuple when i>j. Pick any x'_1,x'_2,\ldots,x'_n. Then, for any x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_n, by triangle inequality,

\begin{aligned} &|f(x_{1\rightharpoondown n})-f(x'_{1\rightharpoondown n})|\\ \leq{}&|f(x_{1\rightharpoondown n})-f(x'_{1\rightharpoondown(n-1)},x_n)|+c_n\\ \leq{}&|f(x_{1\rightharpoondown n})-f(x'_{1\rightharpoondown(n-2)},x_{(n-1)\rightharpoondown n})|+c_{n-1}+c_n\\ \leq{}&\cdots\\ \leq{}&\sum_{i=1}^n c_i, \end{aligned}

where the second step is used when n\geq2. Thus f is bounded.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Replace the coordinates one at a time and add the n change bounds by the triangle inequality. Fixing one tuple then bounds every value within the finite sum of constants of its value. This is a finite elementary inequality argument.
