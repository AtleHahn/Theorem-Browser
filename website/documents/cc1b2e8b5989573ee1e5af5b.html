Statement

Let G be a group with identity e acting on a set X from the left, so ex=x and g(hx)=(gh)x. For x\in X write G_x=\{h\in G:hx=x\}. If y=gx, then G_y=gG_xg^{-1}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory group/ring theory and linear algebra supply the objects. The extra Sylow, normalizer, invariant-polynomial or pseudoreflection condition is explicitly defined in subgroup, coordinate or polynomial terms; no specialist extension or representation theory is required.

Proof 1: Stabilizers are conjugate along an orbit

By definition, {\displaystyle h\in G_{y}} if and only if {\displaystyle h\cdot (g\cdot x)=g\cdot x}. Applying {\displaystyle g^{-1}} to both sides of this equality yields {\displaystyle (g^{-1}hg)\cdot x=x}; that is, {\displaystyle g^{-1}hg\in G_{x}}.

An opposite inclusion follows similarly by taking {\displaystyle h\in G_{x}} and {\displaystyle x=g^{-1}\cdot y}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the inverse action and regroup the group products to characterize each stabilizer. Introductory groups and group actions suffice.
