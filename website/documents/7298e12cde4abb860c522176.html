Statement

A propositional formula is a finite expression in true/false variables and the usual Boolean connectives; \top,\bot denote true and false. Write \operatorname{atoms}(F) for the variables occurring in F, and F\models G if every assignment making F true makes G true. An interpolant for F,G is a formula H with F\models H\models G and \operatorname{atoms}(H)\subseteq\operatorname{atoms}(F)\cap\operatorname{atoms}(G). Let q\in\operatorname{atoms}(\varphi)\setminus\operatorname{atoms}(\psi), and define \varphi^{\prime}=\varphi[\top/q]\lor\varphi[\bot/q], where brackets mean substitution at every occurrence of q. If \rho is an interpolant for \varphi^{\prime},\psi, then it is also an interpolant for \varphi,\psi.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Boolean formulas, entailment, variable sets and interpolants are all defined locally using truth assignments. Their combination requires elementary finite logic.

Proof 1: Transferring an interpolant after Boolean substitution (outline)

The substituted formula has no occurrence of q and has no new variable: \operatorname{atoms}(\varphi^{\prime})\subseteq\operatorname{atoms}(\varphi). We have the one-way entailment \varphi\models\varphi^{\prime}. Hence \varphi\models\rho\models\psi whenever \rho interpolates \varphi^{\prime},\psi. Its variables are common to \varphi^{\prime},\psi, and therefore common to \varphi,\psi, as required.
