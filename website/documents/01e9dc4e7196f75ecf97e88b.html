Statement

For every odd prime p, (p-1)!\equiv-1\pmod p.

Level

Level justification

The statement is a finite integer product congruence for an odd prime.

Proof 1: Pairing inverse residue classes

Let p be an odd prime. Firstly it follows from Euclid's Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic that {\displaystyle ab\equiv 0{\pmod {p}}\iff a\equiv 0{\pmod {p}}\ \ {\hbox{or}}\ \ b\equiv 0{\pmod {p}}}. Consequently, {\displaystyle a^{2}\equiv 1{\pmod {p}}\iff a\equiv \pm 1{\pmod {p}}}: that is, {\displaystyle \pm 1} are their own inverses modulo {\displaystyle p} and this property is unique to them. It then follows from the validity of Euclidean division in the integers, and the fact that {\displaystyle p} is prime, that for every {\displaystyle 2\leq a\leq p-2} the gcd of {\displaystyle a} and {\displaystyle p} may be expressed via the Euclidean algorithm yielding a unique and distinct inverse {\displaystyle a^{-1}\neq a} of {\displaystyle a} modulo {\displaystyle p}. In particular therefore the product of all non-zero residues modulo {\displaystyle p} is {\displaystyle -1}.
