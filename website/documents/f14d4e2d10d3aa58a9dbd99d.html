Statement

Let R be a commutative ring with identity, I an ideal and M a finitely generated R-module. If IM=M, there is r\in R with r\equiv1\pmod I and rM=0. Equivalently, there is i\in I acting as the identity on M.

Proof 1: Apply the determinant trick to the identity

We first prove the following assertion. Let M be an R-module generated by n elements, and let \varphi:M\to M be an R-linear map. If there is an ideal I of R such that \varphi(M)\subseteq IM, then there is a monic polynomial

p(t)=t^n+p_1t^{n-1}+\cdots+p_n,\qquad p_k\in I^k,

such that p(\varphi)=0 as an endomorphism of M.

On the generators x_i of M, one has a relation of the form

\varphi(x_i)=\sum_{j=1}^n a_{ij}x_j

where a_{ij}\in I. Thus

\sum_{j=1}^n(\varphi\delta_{ij}-a_{ij})x_j=0,

where \delta_{ij} is the Kronecker delta, and we are now regarding M as an R[\varphi]-module with \varphi x_i=\varphi(x_i). Here R[\varphi] is the commutative ring of endomorphisms generated by scalar multiplication and \varphi. The required result follows by multiplying by the adjugate of the matrix [\varphi\delta_{ij}-a_{ij}] and using the adjugate identity. One finds that \det(\varphi\delta_{ij}-a_{ij}) annihilates every generator, and hence is zero as an endomorphism of M, so the polynomial

p(t)=\det(t\delta_{ij}-a_{ij})

has the required properties. This proves the assertion.

To complete the proof, assume that IM=M and take \varphi to be the identity on M. Then define the polynomial p as above. A generating list may contain the zero element if M=0. Then

r=p(1)=1+p_1+p_2+\cdots+p_n

has the required properties, r\equiv1\pmod I and rM=0. For the equivalent formulation, take i=1-r. Conversely, such an i gives r=1-i.
