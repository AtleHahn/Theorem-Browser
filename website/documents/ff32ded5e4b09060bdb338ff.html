Statement

Let U\subseteq\mathbb C be open, let f:U\to\mathbb C be holomorphic, and suppose the closed disk \overline D=\{z:|z-z_0|\le r\}, with r>0, lies in U. Let \gamma be its counterclockwise boundary circle. Then f(z_0)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}f(z_0+re^{i\theta})\,d\theta.

Proof 1: Parametrize the centered circle

Let U\subseteq\mathbb C be open, let f:U\to\mathbb C be holomorphic, and suppose the closed disk \overline D=\{z:|z-z_0|\le r\}, with r>0, lies in U. Let \gamma be its counterclockwise boundary circle. Cauchy’s integral formula states, for every a with |a-z_0|<r, that f(a)=(2\pi i)^{-1}\oint_\gamma f(z)/(z-a)\,dz. Apply the formula at a=z_0 and parametrize z=z_0+re^{i\theta}, dz=ire^{i\theta}\,d\theta. The factor ire^{i\theta}/(z-z_0)=i cancels, leaving f(z_0)=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_0^{2\pi}f(z_0+re^{i\theta})\,d\theta.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the stated disk Cauchy formula at its center and substitute the circle parametrization. Undergraduate multivariable calculus and complex arithmetic suffice with complex differentiability and this explicit contour formula as a small extension.
