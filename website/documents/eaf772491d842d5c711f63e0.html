Statement

If a satisfiable first-order theory T has exactly one model up to isomorphism, then for every sentence \varphi, either T\vdash\varphi or T\vdash\neg\varphi.

Level

Level justification

Proof 1: All models have the same first-order sentences

All models of T are isomorphic, and so are all first-order equivalent. Therefore, the theory is complete as all models are equivalent, by the completeness theorem.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The semantic argument is short: all models agree on a sentence, so completeness turns its common truth value into a formal proof from T. However, understanding this as a statement about arbitrary first-order theories requires the syntax/semantics distinction, structures, satisfaction and a deductive calculus. Without prior mathematical logic it is not clear that the full interpretation fits the allowance. Keep Beyond undergraduate provisionally rather than equating formal completeness with informal proof by cases.
