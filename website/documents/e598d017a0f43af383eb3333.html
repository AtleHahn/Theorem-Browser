Statement

Let H be a complex normed space. Write H' for its continuous linear functionals and \overline H' for its continuous antilinear functionals, both with norm \|f\|=\sup_{\|x\|\le1}|f(x)|. The map C:H'\to\overline H', (Cf)(x)=\overline{f(x)}, is a conjugate-linear surjective isometry.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra supplies conjugate-linear functionals and analysis supplies continuity and sup norms. The norm is explicitly defined and the map is pointwise conjugation, so no weak topology or bidual theory is needed to understand the isometry.

Proof 1: Conjugate the values of each functional — outline

Use the source’s explicit formula f\mapsto\overline f, where \overline f(x)=\overline{f(x)}. Conjugation changes linear scalar dependence into conjugate-linear dependence and preserves absolute values. Applying it twice recovers the functional, so the map is onto and preserves the displayed norm.
