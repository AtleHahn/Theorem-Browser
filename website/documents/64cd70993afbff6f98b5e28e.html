Statement

Let B be a complex Banach space with the approximation property, and let A:B\to B admit a representation

Ax=\sum_{k=1}^\infty f_k(x)v_k,\qquad v_k\in B,\quad f_k\in B',\qquad\sum_{k=1}^\infty\|v_k\|^{2/3}\|f_k\|^{2/3}<\infty.

Let (\lambda_j) be its nonzero eigenvalues counted with algebraic multiplicity, and assume \sum_j|\lambda_j|<\infty. Then its nuclear Fredholm determinant satisfies

\det(I+A)=\prod_j(1+\lambda_j).

The product is taken over all these eigenvalues, with the empty product equal to 1.

A Banach space is a complete normed vector space; B' is its space of bounded complex linear functionals with the operator norm. The approximation property means that for every compact subset K\subseteq B and every \varepsilon>0 there is a bounded finite-rank linear map S:B\to B with \sup_{x\in K}\|Sx-x\|<\varepsilon. Algebraic multiplicity of a nonzero eigenvalue \lambda is the dimension of its generalized eigenspace \bigcup_{m\geq1}\ker(A-\lambda I)^m.

For the displayed nuclear representation, the Fredholm determinant can be defined by

\det(I+A)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\det\left(\delta_{ij}+f_i(v_j)\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq N},

the representation-independent limit of the finite-rank determinants. Here I is the identity on B and \delta_{ij} is 1 if i=j and 0 otherwise. The given summability is the definition of two-thirds nuclearity in this statement.

Proof 1: Hilbert-space factorization of the determinant (external)

A supporting source can be found here (Section 3, equations (3.1)–(3.2), and Theorem 5.1). The deduction below supplies the argument for the stored statement.

The following Banach-to-Hilbert factorization is supplied here; the links support its trace-class determinant dependencies: delete zero summands and write A=sum a_i phi_i(.)u_i with a_i=||f_i||||v_i||>0, ||phi_i||=||u_i||=1 and sum a_i^(2/3)<infinity. Define U:B->ell2 by (Ux)_i=a_i^(1/3)phi_i(x), and V:ell2->B by Vy=sum a_i^(2/3)y_i u_i. Both are bounded by Cauchy–Schwarz and VU=A. On ell2, T=UV has matrix a_i^(1/3)a_j^(2/3)phi_i(u_j). Factor T=HD, where H_ij=a_i^(1/3)a_j^(1/3)phi_i(u_j) and D_jj=a_j^(1/3). Both H and D are Hilbert–Schmidt: sum_ij |H_ij|^2 <=(sum a_i^(2/3))^2 and sum_j |D_jj|^2=sum a_j^(2/3). Thus T is trace class. The nonzero eigenvalues of VU and UV have identical algebraic multiplicities: U,V intertwine their generalized eigenspaces, and on a nonzero generalized eigenspace their compositions are invertible. For the coordinate projections P_N, the determinant of I+P_N T P_N equals the determinant of the original N-by-N matrix delta_ij+f_i(v_j), by finite-dimensional Sylvester identity (or diagonal similarity after normalization). The Hilbert-space Fredholm determinant theorem and trace-norm convergence P_NTP_N->T show that their limit is det_ell2(I+T)=product_j(1+lambda_j(T)). By the determinant definition in the target and the multiplicity correspondence, this is exactly det_B(I+A)=product_j(1+lambda_j(A)). The empty spectrum and an eigenvalue -1 are covered by the same Hilbert theorem.
