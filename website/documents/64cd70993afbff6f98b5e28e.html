Statement

Let B be a complex Banach space with the approximation property, and let A:B\to B admit a representation

Ax=\sum_{k=1}^\infty f_k(x)v_k,\qquad v_k\in B,\quad f_k\in B',\qquad\sum_{k=1}^\infty\|v_k\|^{2/3}\|f_k\|^{2/3}<\infty.

Let (\lambda_j) be its nonzero eigenvalues counted with algebraic multiplicity, and assume \sum_j|\lambda_j|<\infty. Then its nuclear Fredholm determinant satisfies

\det(I+A)=\prod_j(1+\lambda_j).

The product is taken over all these eigenvalues, with the empty product equal to 1.

A Banach space is a complete normed vector space; B' is its space of bounded complex linear functionals with the operator norm. The approximation property means that for every compact subset K\subseteq B and every \varepsilon>0 there is a bounded finite-rank linear map S:B\to B with \sup_{x\in K}\|Sx-x\|<\varepsilon. Algebraic multiplicity of a nonzero eigenvalue \lambda is the dimension of its generalized eigenspace \bigcup_{m\geq1}\ker(A-\lambda I)^m.

For the displayed nuclear representation, the Fredholm determinant can be defined by

\det(I+A)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\det\left(\delta_{ij}+f_i(v_j)\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq N},

the representation-independent limit of the finite-rank determinants. Here I is the identity on B and \delta_{ij} is 1 if i=j and 0 otherwise. The given summability is the definition of two-thirds nuclearity in this statement.
