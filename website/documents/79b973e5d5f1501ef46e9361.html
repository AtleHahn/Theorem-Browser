Statement

On [0,1], let u(0)=0 and u(t)=1/t for t>0. With Lebesgue measure and \alpha=0, the inequality u(t)\le\int_{[0,t)}u(s)\,ds holds in the extended sense for every t, but u is not zero and is not integrable near 0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The functions and sums are explicit, and calculus supplies their convergence or divergence. The additional meromorphic label in the rational-function example means complex analytic except at its pole; discrete expectations reduce to the displayed weighted series. No broader theory is needed for these particular examples.

Proof 1: Use the divergent reciprocal integral (outline)

The source supplies u(0)=0, u(t)=1/t as its counterexample. At 0 both sides are zero. For every t>0, the integral of 1/s from 0 to t is infinite, so the inequality holds although u(t)>0. The omitted integrability hypothesis therefore cannot be dropped.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The reciprocal function has divergent integral at zero, so each positive-t right side is infinity, while both sides vanish at zero. Undergraduate improper integration suffices; the nonnegative Lebesgue integral here agrees with that explicit divergent improper integral.
