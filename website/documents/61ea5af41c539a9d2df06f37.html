Statement

Let A be a subset of the positive prime numbers, and let \pi(N) be the number of primes at most N. Suppose

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{|A\cap\{1,\ldots,N\}|}{\pi(N)}>0.

Then, for every positive integer k, A contains infinitely many distinct arithmetic progressions of length k: sequences a,a+d,\ldots,a+(k-1)d with positive integers a,d and all terms in A. In particular, the primes contain arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions.

The density hypothesis means that some \delta>0 satisfies |A\cap\{1,\ldots,N\}|\ge\delta\pi(N) for arbitrarily large integers N.

High school only
Level justification

The density condition is expanded into elementary counting and arbitrarily-large-integer quantifiers. The full statement uses no analytic machinery beyond that elementary formulation.
