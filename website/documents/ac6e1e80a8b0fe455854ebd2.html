Statement

Let H:\mathbb R^m\times\mathbb R^n\to[0,\infty) be a measurable log-concave density, meaning H((1-\lambda)u+\lambda v)\ge H(u)^{1-\lambda}H(v)^\lambda for 0<\lambda<1. Its marginal M(y)=\int_{\mathbb R^m}H(x,y)\,dx satisfies M((1-\lambda)y_1+\lambda y_2)\ge M(y_1)^{1-\lambda}M(y_2)^\lambda whenever these marginal values are finite.

Proof 1: Apply the integral inequality to three fixed slices

Suppose that H(x,y) is a log-concave density for (x,y)\in\mathbb R^m\times\mathbb R^n, so that by definition we have

H\left((1-\lambda)(x_1,y_1)+\lambda(x_2,y_2)\right) \ge H(x_1,y_1)^{1-\lambda}H(x_2,y_2)^\lambda,

and let M(y) denote the marginal density obtained by integrating over x:

M(y)=\int_{\mathbb R^m}H(x,y)\,dx.

Let y_1,y_2\in\mathbb R^n and 0<\lambda<1 be given. Then the displayed log-concavity inequality satisfies the condition

h((1-\lambda)x_1+\lambda x_2)\ge f(x_1)^{1-\lambda}g(x_2)^\lambda

with h(x)=H(x,(1-\lambda)y_1+\lambda y_2), f(x)=H(x,y_1) and g(x)=H(x,y_2), so the Prékopa–Leindler inequality applies. It can be written in terms of M as

M((1-\lambda)y_1+\lambda y_2)\ge M(y_1)^{1-\lambda}M(y_2)^\lambda,

which is the definition of log-concavity for M.
