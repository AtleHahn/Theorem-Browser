Statement

Let a<b be real. For an open set U\subseteq(a,b), let \ell(U) be the sum of the lengths of its disjoint open-interval components. Call A\subseteq(a,b) null if for every \varepsilon>0 it is contained in such an open U with \ell(U)<\varepsilon. If A and B are null, then A\cup B is null.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Nullity is fully defined by open interval covers and their lengths. Countable sums and set unions are introductory real analysis, with no general measurable integration needed.

Proof 1: Apply the open-set length identity to small covers — outline

For open sets U,V\subseteq(a,b), the length identity is \ell(U)+\ell(V)=\ell(U\cup V)+\ell(U\cap V). Together with the arbitrarily small open covers in the definition of null sets, this implies that the union of two null sets is null.
