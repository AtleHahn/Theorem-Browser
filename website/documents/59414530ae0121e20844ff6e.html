Statement

Let a rectangle have side lengths 0<S<L and L/S=\sqrt2. Cutting its long side in half produces rectangles with side lengths S and L/2 having the same long-to-short aspect ratio \sqrt2.

Proof 1: Compute the new aspect ratio

Let {\displaystyle S=} shorter length and {\displaystyle L=} longer length of the sides of a sheet of paper, with

{\displaystyle R={\frac {L}{S}}={\sqrt {2}}} as in the hypothesis.

Let {\displaystyle R'={\frac {L'}{S'}}} be the analogous ratio of the halved sheet, then

{\displaystyle R'={\frac {S}{L/2}}={\frac {2S}{L}}={\frac {2}{(L/S)}}={\frac {2}{\sqrt {2}}}={\sqrt {2}}=R.}

Compute the new long-to-short ratio as 2/sqrt2. School fractions and radicals suffice.
