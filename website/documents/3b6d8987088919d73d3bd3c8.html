Statement

If f,g:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C are entire and |f(z)|\le|g(z)| everywhere, then f=\alpha g for some \alpha\in\mathbb C.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex differentiability extends familiar calculus; entire means differentiable throughout the plane. Density, power-series convergence and local continuation across a boundary are direct analytic/topological notions, with the continuation condition explicitly described. No additional complex-geometric structures are needed.

Proof 1: Remove the singularities of a bounded quotient

Consider that for {\displaystyle g\equiv0} the theorem is trivial so we assume {\displaystyle g\not\equiv0}. Consider the function {\displaystyle h=f/g}. It is enough to prove that {\displaystyle h} can be extended to an entire function, in which case the result follows by Liouville's theorem. The holomorphy of {\displaystyle h} is clear except at points in {\displaystyle g^{-1}(0)}. But since {\displaystyle h} is bounded and all the zeroes of {\displaystyle g} are isolated, any singularities must be removable. Thus {\displaystyle h} can be extended to an entire bounded function which by Liouville's theorem implies it is constant.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The quotient argument needs isolated analytic zeros, removable singularities and Liouville together. Retain beyond undergraduate provisionally because these linked complex-analysis concepts exceed the single entire-function definition needed for a direct boundedness application.
