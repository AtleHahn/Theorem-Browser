The spectrum of a non-unitary linear isometry on a complex Hilbert space is the closed unit disk.

Proof 1: Spectral consequence of the local decomposition — outline

Local decomposition

Let H be a complex Hilbert space and V:H\to H a linear isometry. Then H admits an orthogonal decomposition on which V is unitarily equivalent to

\left(\bigoplus_{\alpha\in A}S\right)\oplus U,

where U is unitary and S is the unilateral shift on \ell^2(\mathbb N_0), S(x_0,x_1,\ldots)=(0,x_0,x_1,\ldots). Either summand may be absent.

A proof can be sketched as follows. Successive applications of V give a descending sequence of copies of H isomorphically embedded in itself:

H=H_0\supset H_1\supset H_2\supset\cdots, \qquad H_i=V^i(H).

Here V(H) denotes the range of V. If one defines

{\displaystyle M_{i}=H_{i}\ominus H_{i+1}=V^{i}(H\ominus V(H))\quad {\text{for}}\quad i\geq 0\;,}

then

{\displaystyle H=\left(\bigoplus _{i\geq 0}M_{i}\right)\oplus \left(\bigcap _{i\geq 0}H_{i}\right)=K_{1}\oplus K_{2}.}

It is clear that K1 and K2 are invariant subspaces of V.

So V(K2) = K2. In other words, V restricted to K2 is a surjective isometry, i.e., a unitary operator U.

Furthermore, each Mi is isomorphic to another, with V being an isomorphism between Mi and Mi+1: V "shifts" Mi to Mi+1. Let A be an index set whose cardinality is the Hilbert-space dimension of M_0. We see that K_1 can be written as an orthogonal direct sum of Hilbert spaces

{\displaystyle K_{1}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in A} H_{\alpha }}

where each Hα is an invariant subspace of V and V restricted to each Hα is the unilateral shift S. Therefore

{\displaystyle V=V\vert _{K_{1}}\oplus V\vert _{K_{2}}=\left(\bigoplus _{\alpha \in A}S\right)\oplus U,}

which is a Wold decomposition of V.

Spectral consequence

It is immediate from the Wold decomposition that the spectrum of any proper, i.e. non-unitary, isometry is the unit disk in the complex plane. The spectral facts used here are that the unilateral shift has spectrum the closed unit disk and an isometry has spectrum contained in that disk.
