Statement

Let V be a Hausdorff locally convex topological vector space over \mathbb R or \mathbb C, and let X\subseteq V be a nonempty compact convex set. Suppose a correspondence F assigns to each x\in X a nonempty convex subset F(x)\subseteq X, and its graph

\{(x,y)\in X\times X:y\in F(x)\}

is closed in the product topology. Then some x\in X satisfies x\in F(x).

The topology on X is the subspace topology. A topological vector space has continuous addition and scalar multiplication; locally convex means that zero has a basis of convex neighborhoods, and Hausdorff means distinct points have disjoint neighborhoods. A set is convex when it contains (1-t)x+ty for each pair of its points and every real t\in[0,1]. Compact means every open cover has a finite subcover. A correspondence here is a set-valued function; its fixed point is a point belonging to its own value.
