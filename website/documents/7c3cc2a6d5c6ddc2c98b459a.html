Statement

A rooted tree is a finite connected graph without cycles with a specified vertex called its root. A vertex w is a descendant of v when the path from the root to w contains v; it is a child when it is adjacent to v and is a descendant. An inf-embedding is an injective vertex map preserving descendants such that, for any two distinct children w_1,w_2 of v, the path joining their images contains the image of v. The root need not map to the root. Let (X,\le_X) be a well-quasi-ordered partially ordered set: in every infinite sequence x_1,x_2,\ldots there are i<j with x_i\le_X x_j. Give each vertex of each rooted tree a label in X, and require an inf-embedding also to satisfy \operatorname{label}(v)\le_X\operatorname{label}(F(v)). Then in every infinite sequence T_1,T_2,\ldots of such labeled rooted trees there exist i<j and a label-respecting inf-embedding of T_i into T_j.
