Statement

Let A be a finite classical propositional formula. Replace \neg A by a logically equivalent negation-normal formula and exhaustively expand its set-labelled tableau by conjunction replacement, disjunction branching, and opposite-literal closure. Then A is a tautology exactly when the tableau closes.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The finite propositional formula and tableau expansion rules are supplied as Boolean tree operations.

Proof 1: Apply the decision theorem to the negation

Use the propositional tableau decision theorem: an exhaustively expanded tableau closes exactly when its initial formulas are jointly unsatisfiable. Replacing a formula by its logically equivalent negation-normal form preserves satisfiability.

By setting X=\{\neg A\} one can test whether the formula A is a tautology of classical logic. If the tableau for \{\neg A\} closes then \neg A is unsatisfiable and so A is a tautology, since no assignment of truth values will ever make A false. Otherwise an open completed branch of the tableau gives an assignment that falsifies A.

Level justification

Apply the stated finite tableau decision criterion to the negation and interpret absence of a falsifying truth assignment. The propositional formula and branching rules are explicit; elementary finite logic suffices.
