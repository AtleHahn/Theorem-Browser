Statement

The real circle constant \pi is transcendental: no nonzero polynomial with rational coefficients vanishes at \pi.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Transcendence is explicitly defined as failure to satisfy a rational polynomial, a statement understandable using real numbers and polynomials.

Mathematical Field
High School Mathematics

Review notes

Proof 1: Contradict Euler’s identity under algebraicity

Use the Hermite–Lindemann theorem: e^\alpha is transcendental for every nonzero algebraic number \alpha.

To prove that \pi is transcendental, we prove that it is not algebraic. If \pi were algebraic, \pi i would be a nonzero algebraic number as well, and then by this theorem e^{\pi i}=-1 (Euler's identity) would be transcendental, a contradiction. Therefore \pi is not algebraic, which means that it is transcendental.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Assume pi algebraic, multiply by i and apply the explicitly stated exponential transcendence theorem, contradicting Euler. Undergraduate complex exponentials and the elementary algebraic-number closure property suffice; no transcendence proof is required.
