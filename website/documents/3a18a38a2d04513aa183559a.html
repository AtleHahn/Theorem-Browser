Statement

For a prime p and a nonzero integer u, let \nu_p(u) be the greatest integer k\ge0 such that p^k divides u. Let x,y\in\mathbb Z with p\nmid xy, and let n\ge1 be an integer. In each formula below all arguments of \nu_p are required to be nonzero.

For odd p:

\begin{aligned} p\mid x-y&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\nu_p(x^n-y^n)=\nu_p(x-y)+\nu_p(n),\\ p\mid x+y,\ n\text{ odd}&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\nu_p(x^n+y^n)=\nu_p(x+y)+\nu_p(n),\\ p\mid x+y,\ n\text{ even}&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\nu_p(x^n+y^n)=0. \end{aligned}

For p=2 (so x,y are odd):

\nu_2(x^n-y^n)= \begin{cases} \nu_2(x-y),&n\text{ odd},\\ \nu_2(x-y)+\nu_2(x+y)+\nu_2(n)-1,&n\text{ even}. \end{cases}

For any prime p, if p\nmid n, then

\begin{aligned} p\mid x-y&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\nu_p(x^n-y^n)=\nu_p(x-y),\\ p\mid x+y,\ n\text{ odd}&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\nu_p(x^n+y^n)=\nu_p(x+y). \end{aligned}

Proof 1: Factorization and prime-power induction — outline

First suppose p\mid x-y and p\nmid n. The source uses

x^{n-1}+x^{n-2}y+\cdots+y^{n-1}\equiv nx^{n-1}\not\equiv0\pmod p.

Together with x^n-y^n=(x-y)(x^{n-1}+\cdots+y^{n-1}), this proves the coprime-exponent difference formula. Replacing y by -y gives the odd-exponent sum formula.

For odd p, binomial expansion with y=x+kp shows that the quotient (x^p-y^p)/(x-y) is divisible by p but not by p^2. Hence \nu_p(x^p-y^p)=\nu_p(x-y)+1. Write n=p^ab with p\nmid b. The base case removes the exponent b, and induction on a gives the asserted difference valuation. The substitution y\mapsto-y gives the odd-exponent sum case.

For p=2, write n=2^ab with b odd. The base case again removes b. For a\ge1, if 4\mid x-y, repeated difference-of-squares factorization gives

x^{2^a}-y^{2^a}=(x-y)(x+y)(x^2+y^2)\cdots(x^{2^{a-1}}+y^{2^{a-1}}).

Each sum factor is congruent to 2 modulo 4, so its valuation is 1. The source states that the more general even-exponent formula with the separate \nu_2(x+y) term follows analogously. It leaves that parity bookkeeping and the odd-prime even-exponent zero-valuation case implicit. These routine omissions are recorded rather than expanded into a new argument.
