Statement

For real x>-1 and real r, (1+x)^r\ge1+rx if r\le0 or r\ge1, while (1+x)^r\le1+rx if 0\le r\le1. Each inequality is strict when x\ne0 and r\notin\{0,1\}. The nonstrict assertions for r\ge0 also hold at x=-1, with the convention 0^0=1.

High school only

Proof 1: Strict convexity as a function of the exponent

For {\displaystyle 0\neq x>-1} the function {\displaystyle h(\alpha )=(1+x)^{\alpha }} is strictly convex. Therefore, for {\displaystyle 0<\alpha <1} holds {\displaystyle (1+x)^{\alpha }=h(\alpha )=h((1-\alpha )\cdot 0+\alpha \cdot 1)<(1-\alpha )h(0)+\alpha h(1)=1+\alpha x} and the reversed inequality is valid for {\displaystyle \alpha <0} and {\displaystyle \alpha >1}.

For x=0 or r\in\{0,1\} there is equality. At x=-1 and r>0, the left side is zero and the stated inequalities follow from the sign of 1-r; r=0 follows from the adopted convention.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof treats the exponent as a real variable and uses strict convexity relative to the chord through exponents zero and one, including its continuation outside that interval. These are undergraduate calculus concepts; the second derivative is (1+x)^alpha times log(1+x)^2>0 for x nonzero. The remaining equality and zero-base cases are direct sign checks.
