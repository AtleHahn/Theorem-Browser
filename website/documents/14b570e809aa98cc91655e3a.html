Statement

Let (\Omega,\Sigma,\mu) be a measure space, 1<p<\infty, and q satisfy 1/p+1/q=1. Every norm-bounded sequence (f_n) in L^p(\mu) has a subsequence (f_{n_k}) and f\in L^p(\mu) such that \int f_{n_k}g\,d\mu\to\int fg\,d\mu for every g\in L^q(\mu).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement uses Lebesgue function spaces and their integral pairings on general measure spaces.

Proof 1: Specialize the reflexive subsequence theorem

Use the source’s preceding consequence: a bounded sequence in a reflexive Banach space has a weakly convergent subsequence. Apply it to X=L^p(\mu), 1<p<\infty, which is reflexive. Under the duality X'=L^q(\mu) stated in the source, weak convergence is precisely the displayed convergence of integrals.

Level justification
