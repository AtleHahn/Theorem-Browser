Statement

On a two-outcome probability space with equal outcome probabilities, let the sequence (X_n) be identically zero on one outcome and identically one on the other. Then \mathbb P(\sum_nX_n\text{ converges in }\mathbb R)=1/2, so the zero–one conclusion fails without independence.

Proof 1: Compare zero and unit partial sums

Without independence we can consider a sequence that's either {\displaystyle (0,0,0,\dots )} or {\displaystyle (1,1,1,\dots )} with probability {\displaystyle {\frac {1}{2}}} each. In this case the sum converges with probability {\displaystyle {\frac {1}{2}}}.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

On one outcome all partial sums are zero; on the other they are n and diverge. The convergence event therefore has one of the two equal probabilities. Only elementary real-series convergence and finite probability are needed.
