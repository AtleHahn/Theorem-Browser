Statement

Let V_1,V_2,V_3,V_4 be finite-dimensional subspaces of a vector space over an arbitrary field. Write d(U)=\dim U, V_{ij}=V_i+V_j, V_{ijk}=V_i+V_j+V_k, I_{ij}=V_i\cap V_j, I_{ijk}=V_i\cap V_j\cap V_k, and I_{1234}=V_1\cap V_2\cap V_3\cap V_4. Here sums are ordinary subspace sums. d(I_{1234})\ge d(I_{12})+d(V_3)+d(V_4)-d(V_{13})-d(V_{23})-d(V_{14})-d(V_{24})+d(V_{123})+d(V_{124}).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Matrices, determinants, kernels, linear independence and bases are standard undergraduate linear algebra. Over an algebraically closed field, the extra condition simply guarantees polynomial roots; infinite dimensions mean cardinalities of bases. The specific matrix or dimension claim adds no specialist construction.

Proof 1: Use subspace inclusions and the dimension formula

We will use frequently the standard vector space identity: dim(U) + dim(W) = dim(U+W) + dim(U∩W).

  1. It is clear that (V1∩V2) + V3 ⊆ (V1+ V3) ∩ (V2+V3), then
dim((V1∩V2)+V3) ≤ dim((V1+V3)∩(V2+V3)), hence
dim(V1∩V2∩V3) = dim(V1∩V2) + dim(V3) − dim((V1∩V2)+V3)
≥ dim(V1∩V2) + dim(V3) − dim((V1+V3)∩(V2+V3))
= dim(V1∩V2) + dim(V3) – {dim(V1+V3) + dim(V2+V3) – dim(V1+V2+V3)}
= dim(V1∩V2) + dim(V3) – dim(V1+V3) − dim(V2+V3) + dim(V1+V2+V3)
  1. It is clear that (V1∩V2∩V3) + (V1∩V2∩V4) ⊆ (V1∩V2), then
dim{(V1∩V2∩V3)+(V1∩V2∩V4)} ≤ dim(V1∩V2), hence
dim(V1∩V2∩V3∩V4) = dim(V1∩V2∩V3) + dim(V1∩V2∩V4) − dim{(V1∩V2∩V3) + (V1∩V2∩V4)}
≥ dim(V1∩V2∩V3) + dim(V1∩V2∩V4) − dim(V1∩V2)
  1. From (1) and (2) we have:
dim(V1∩V2∩V3∩V4) ≥ dim(V1∩V2∩V3) + dim(V1∩V2∩V4) − dim(V1∩V2)
≥ dim(V1∩V2) + dim(V3) − dim(V1+V3) − dim(V2+V3) + dim(V1+V2+V3) + dim(V1∩V2) + dim(V4) − dim(V1+V4) − dim(V2+V4) + dim(V1+V2+V4) − dim(V1∩V2)
= dim(V1∩V2) + dim(V3) + dim(V4) − dim(V1+V3) − dim(V2+V3) − dim(V1+V4) − dim(V2+V4) + dim(V1+V2+V3) + dim(V1+V2+V4)
