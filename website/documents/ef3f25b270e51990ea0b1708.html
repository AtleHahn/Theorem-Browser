Statement

A finite hypergraph H=(X\sqcup Y,E) is bipartite here if every edge contains exactly one vertex of Y. A matching consists of pairwise disjoint edges and is Y-perfect if it covers Y. For S\subseteq Y, put N(S)=\{e\setminus Y:e\in E,\ e\cap S\ne\varnothing\}. For a finite family F of nonempty sets, a subfamily K pins M\subseteq F if every member of M meets a member of K. Its width w(F) is the minimum size of K\subseteq F pinning all of F; its matching width mw(F) is the maximum over disjoint subfamilies M of their minimum pinning size using members of F. Take Y=\{1,2\} and edges \{1,A,a\},\{1,B,b\},\{2,A,b\},\{2,B,a\}. Each N(S) has a matching of size at least |S|, but H has no Y-perfect matching. Also mw(N(Y))=1.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All matching and pinning quantities are defined by counting intersecting or disjoint finite subfamilies; nested minima and maxima require elementary finite choices.

Proof 1: List all two-edge matchings and their pinners

Every vertex in Y has a neighbor, and Y itself has four neighbors:

N(Y)=\{\{A,a\},\{B,b\},\{A,b\},\{B,a\}\}.

Moreover, N(Y) admits a matching of size 2, e.g. \{\{A,a\},\{B,b\}\} or \{\{A,b\},\{B,a\}\}. However, H does not admit a Y-perfect matching, since every hyperedge that contains 1 overlaps every hyperedge that contains 2.

The matching-width of N(Y) is 1: it contains a matching of size 2, e.g. \{\{A,a\},\{B,b\}\}, but this matching can be pinned by a single edge, e.g. \{A,b\}. The other matching of size 2 is \{\{A,b\},\{B,a\}\}, but it too can be pinned by the single edge \{A,a\}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Enumerate the two possible disjoint pairs and their single pinners, while checking that every differently tagged original pair intersects. Elementary finite set enumeration suffices.
