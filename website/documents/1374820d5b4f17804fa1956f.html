Statement

Let G=(V,E) be a finite simple undirected graph. A matching is a subset of edges with no shared endpoints. A vertex is unmatched if no edge of the matching meets it. A path has distinct vertices; its length is its number of edges. An alternating path or cycle has successive edges alternately in and outside the matching (including the closing adjacency for a cycle). For two matchings M,M', the graph with vertex set V and edge set M\triangle M'=(M\setminus M')\cup(M'\setminus M) has components that are isolated vertices, even cycles alternating between the two matchings, or paths alternating between them.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Matching, alternating paths and symmetric difference are explicitly finite edge-set notions. Components and parity require elementary graph traversal.

Proof 1: Degree at most two and alternating edges

Let G'=(V,M\triangle M'). Observe that each vertex in G′ can be incident to at most 2 edges: one from M and one from M′. Graphs where every vertex has degree less than or equal to 2 must consist of either isolated vertices, cycles, and paths. Furthermore, each path and cycle in G′ must alternate between M and M′. In order for a cycle to do this, it must have an equal number of edges from M and M′, and therefore be of even length.
