Statement

Let X be Banach and Y normed. If continuous linear operators T_n:X\to Y have a limit Tx=\lim_nT_nx in Y for every x, then T is bounded linear and \|T\|\le\liminf_n\|T_n\|<\infty.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A Banach space is a complete normed vector space. Linear algebra and metric analysis supply bounded linear maps, pointwise/uniform convergence and compact sets; the operator norm is a unit-ball supremum. These related definitions suffice without dual or spectral theory.

Proof 1: Pointwise limits under a common operator bound — outline

Pointwise convergence makes each orbit bounded, so the uniform boundedness principle gives a common finite bound on the operator norms. Taking pointwise limits gives a bounded linear map and the stated limit-inferior norm bound.

Level

Level justification

Apply the explicit uniform-boundedness principle to convergent orbits and pass limits through vector operations and norm inequalities. Undergraduate complete metric spaces, normed linear algebra and liminf suffice with this bounded-operator theorem as a small related extension; no dual-space theory is used.
