Statement

Let n\ge1, let A\subset\mathbb Z^n be a nonempty finite set, and let L_A=\{\sum_{\alpha\in A}c_\alpha x^\alpha:c_\alpha\in\mathbb C\}, where x^\alpha=\prod_{j=1}^n x_j^{\alpha_j}. Write \Delta=\operatorname{conv}(A). For a generic choice of f_1,\ldots,f_n\in L_A, the number of solutions of f_1(x)=\cdots=f_n(x)=0 in (\mathbb C\setminus\{0\})^n equals n!\operatorname{vol}_n(\Delta). Here generic means outside an exceptional proper algebraic subset of the coefficient space.

Proof 1: Equal-support specialization of the mixed-volume theorem — outline

For finite nonempty supports A_1,\ldots,A_n\subset\mathbb Z^n, the Bernstein–Kushnirenko theorem states that the number of solutions x\in(\mathbb C\setminus\{0\})^n of a generic system f_1(x)=\cdots=f_n(x)=0, with f_i\in L_{A_i}, is equal to

n!V(\Delta_1,\ldots,\Delta_n),

where V denotes the Minkowski mixed volume and for each i, \Delta_i is the convex hull of the finite set of points A_i.

In particular, if all the sets A_i are the same, A=A_1=\cdots=A_n, then the number of solutions of a generic system of Laurent polynomials from L_A is equal to

n!\operatorname{vol}\Delta,

where \Delta is the convex hull of A and \operatorname{vol} is the usual n-dimensional Euclidean volume.
