Statement

Let A,B be events with P(A)>0 and P(B)>0. For events E,F with P(F)>0, conditional probability means P(E\mid F)=P(E\cap F)/P(F). Then P(A\mid B)=P(B\mid A)P(A)/P(B).

Level
High school only
Level justification

Proof 1: Equate the two joint-probability products

Bayes' theorem may be derived from the relation between joint and conditional probabilities. The joint probability of the events {\displaystyle A} and {\displaystyle B} both happening, written {\displaystyle P(A\cap B)}, is equal to the conditional probability of {\displaystyle A} given {\displaystyle B} times the probability of {\displaystyle B}:

{\displaystyle P(A\cap B)=P(A\vert B)P(B).}

Likewise,

{\displaystyle P(A\cap B)=P(B\vert A)P(A).}

The two products must therefore be equal to each other:

{\displaystyle P(A\vert B)P(B)=P(B\vert A)P(A),}

and dividing both sides by {\displaystyle P(B)} gives Bayes' theorem:

{\displaystyle P(A\vert B)={\frac {P(B\vert A)P(A)}{P(B)}},{\text{ if }}P(B)\neq 0.}

Level justification

The ratio definition gives P(A|B)P(B)=P(A intersect B)=P(B|A)P(A). Dividing by the assumed positive P(B) proves the result. Only elementary algebra and the supplied probability definition are needed.
