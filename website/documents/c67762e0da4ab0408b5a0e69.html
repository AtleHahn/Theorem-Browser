Statement

In M=\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z, an automorphism can fail to preserve the displayed free summand while preserving the torsion summand.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Matrix groups, quotient groups and abelian-group direct sums are introductory algebra. The scalar/central subgroups and concrete summands are explicit; no projective geometry or general module classification is needed.

Proof 1: Change the free generator by a torsion element — outline

In M=\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z, the generators

(1,\bar0),(0,\bar1)

may be replaced by

(1,\bar1),(0,\bar1).

The corresponding generator change

\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix}, \qquad (a,\bar b)\longmapsto(a,\bar a+\bar b),

is invertible and does not preserve the summand \mathbb Z\oplus0. However, it does preserve the 0\oplus\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z summand, as this is the torsion submodule (equivalently here, the 2-torsion elements).

Level justification

Check the explicit additive automorphism on Z direct sum Z/2 and its action on the free and torsion summands. Undergraduate abelian-group algebra suffices; the general PID decomposition theorem is not invoked.
