Statement

Let \zeta denote the meromorphic continuation of \sum_{n\ge1}n^{-s} from \operatorname{Re}s>1. Define H(\lambda,z)=\int_0^\infty e^{\lambda u^2}\Phi(u)\cos(zu)\,du, where \Phi(u)=\sum_{n\ge1}(2\pi^2n^4e^{9u}-3\pi n^2e^{5u})e^{-\pi n^2e^{4u}}. Let \Lambda be the real threshold such that H(\lambda,\cdot) has only real zeros precisely when \lambda\ge\Lambda. Then RH, the assertion that every nontrivial zero of \zeta has real part 1/2, is equivalent to \Lambda=0.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The threshold is defined by zeros of a parameterized entire function given by an improper integral and series.

Proof 1: The Riemann hypothesis is equivalent to zero de Bruijn–Newman constant

The known correspondence between zeta zeros and zeros of H(0,z) says RH holds precisely when all zeros of H(0,z) are real. The threshold characterization therefore makes RH equivalent to \Lambda\le0. Rodgers–Tao give \Lambda\ge0, so the criterion becomes \Lambda=0.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Combine the zeta-zero correspondence, the real-zero threshold of the parameterized entire integral and its nonnegativity. Although the final inequality step is elementary, understanding this combined analytic family and zeta correspondence is not confidently a small extension; retain beyond undergraduate provisionally.
