Statement

Let G be a finite group, and let \{\rho_\lambda:G\to U(d_\lambda)\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda} contain one representative of each equivalence class of irreducible complex representations, written in orthonormal bases. A representation is a homomorphism to invertible matrices; irreducible means that its only invariant subspaces are 0 and the whole nonzero space. Equivalent representations are related by a change of basis. The matrices can be chosen unitary, meaning \rho_\lambda(g)^*\rho_\lambda(g)=I.

For all allowable matrix indices,

\sum_{g\in G}\overline{\rho_\lambda(g)_{ij}}\,\rho_\mu(g)_{kl} =\frac{|G|}{d_\lambda}\,\delta_{\lambda\mu}\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}.

Here \delta is 1 when its two indices agree and 0 otherwise.

Put \chi_\lambda(g)=\operatorname{tr}(\rho_\lambda(g)). These characters form an orthonormal basis of the complex vector space of class functions (functions constant on conjugacy classes), with inner product

\langle a,b\rangle=\frac1{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}a(g)\overline{b(g)}.

In particular, the row and column orthogonality relations are

\frac1{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\chi_\lambda(g)\overline{\chi_\mu(g)}=\delta_{\lambda\mu},\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\chi_\lambda(g)\overline{\chi_\lambda(h)}= \begin{cases}|C_G(g)|,&g\text{ and }h\text{ are conjugate},\\0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where C_G(g)=\{x\in G:xg=gx\} and conjugate means h=xgx^{-1} for some x\in G.

All representations are finite-dimensional and the assertions are finite sums and basis statements; no compact-group representation theory or Haar measure is required.
