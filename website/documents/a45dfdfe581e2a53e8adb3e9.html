Statement

Let G be a finite group, and let \{\rho_\lambda:G\to U(d_\lambda)\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda} contain one representative of each equivalence class of irreducible complex representations, written in orthonormal bases. A representation is a homomorphism to invertible matrices; irreducible means that its only invariant subspaces are 0 and the whole nonzero space. Equivalent representations are related by a change of basis. The matrices can be chosen unitary, meaning \rho_\lambda(g)^*\rho_\lambda(g)=I.

For all allowable matrix indices,

\sum_{g\in G}\overline{\rho_\lambda(g)_{ij}}\,\rho_\mu(g)_{kl} =\frac{|G|}{d_\lambda}\,\delta_{\lambda\mu}\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}.

Here \delta is 1 when its two indices agree and 0 otherwise.

Put \chi_\lambda(g)=\operatorname{tr}(\rho_\lambda(g)). These characters form an orthonormal basis of the complex vector space of class functions (functions constant on conjugacy classes), with inner product

\langle a,b\rangle=\frac1{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}a(g)\overline{b(g)}.

In particular, the row and column orthogonality relations are

\frac1{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\chi_\lambda(g)\overline{\chi_\mu(g)}=\delta_{\lambda\mu},\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\chi_\lambda(g)\overline{\chi_\lambda(h)}= \begin{cases}|C_G(g)|,&g\text{ and }h\text{ are conjugate},\\0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where C_G(g)=\{x\in G:xg=gx\} and conjugate means h=xgx^{-1} for some x\in G.

Level justification

Undergraduate linear algebra and introductory group theory suffice after learning the closely related representation, irreducibility and character definitions given here. All representations are finite-dimensional and the assertions are finite sums and basis statements; no compact-group representation theory or Haar measure is required.

Proof 1: Invariant inner products and the character table (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorem2.4.5; Theorem2.5.5, Corollary2.5.6 and Theorem2.5.8, pp.41–50).

Multiply the normalized matrix-coefficient inner product by |G| to obtain the displayed matrix relation. Complex conjugating the inner-product convention leaves its real Kronecker-delta value unchanged. For columns, normalize the character-table column of a conjugacy class C by \sqrt{|C|/|G|}; orthonormal rows make the square table unitary, and |G|/|C|=|C_G(g)|.

In the coefficient-space decomposition used in Theorem2.4.3, justify directness by orthogonality of inequivalent irreducible G\times G subrepresentations (Lemma2.5.4). This replaces the reversed containment in that theorem’s printed directness argument. Over \mathbb C, Propositions2.5.1–2 give the required complete reducibility directly by an averaged inner product and invariant orthogonal complements.
