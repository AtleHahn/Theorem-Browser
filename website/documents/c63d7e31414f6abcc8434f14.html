Cauchy’s equality need not give a nonzero tangent vector

Statement

The curve \gamma(t)=(t^3,1-t^2) on [-1,1] has distinct endpoints (-1,0),(1,0), but no nonzero horizontal derivative vector. Its derivative vanishes at t=0, where the Cauchy mean-value equality is satisfied.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Polynomial plane curves; Vector derivatives; Horizontal directions. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. Proof 1: Inspect the polynomial curve derivative

Consider the curve given by

{\displaystyle t\mapsto \left(t^{3},1-t^{2}\right),}

which on the interval {\displaystyle [-1,1]} goes from the point {\displaystyle (-1,0)} to {\displaystyle (1,0)}, yet never has a nonzero horizontal derivative vector; however it has a stationary point (in fact a cusp) at {\displaystyle t=0}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The derivative is (3t²,-2t). Its second coordinate vanishes only at zero, where the first also vanishes; endpoints are found by substitution. Ordinary calculus of a plane-parametrized curve suffices.
