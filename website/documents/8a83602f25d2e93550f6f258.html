Statement

Among plane convex bodies of a fixed constant width w>0, the disk of diameter w has maximum area. In particular each such body has area at most \pi w^2/4. Constant width means that, in every direction, the two parallel supporting lines on opposite sides of the body are separated by w.

Level
High school only
Level justification

With constant width defined by supporting lines, the statement compares plane areas with that of a circle.

Proof 1: Combine Barbier’s theorem with the isoperimetric inequality

By the isoperimetric inequality, the curve of constant width in the Euclidean plane with the largest area is a circle. The perimeter of a curve of constant width w is \pi w, regardless of its shape; this is Barbier's theorem.

Here the isoperimetric inequality says that a circle encloses the largest area among curves of a fixed perimeter. The circle with perimeter \pi w has diameter w and enclosed area \pi w^2/4.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Barbier gives the same perimeter pi w to every body, and the supplied planar isoperimetric theorem bounds its area by that of the circle with that perimeter. Convex supporting lines and curve perimeter are a modest extension of plane geometry and calculus; no measure-theoretic convex-body machinery is used.
