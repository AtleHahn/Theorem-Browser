Statement

Let P\subset\mathbb R^n be an n-dimensional convex polytope, n\ge1. For each nonempty face F of P, including P itself, choose a point x in the relative interior of F and a sufficiently small sphere S(x,r) of radius r>0. Define its normalized interior solid angle by

\alpha(F)=\frac{\operatorname{area}_{n-1}(S(x,r)\cap P)}{\operatorname{area}_{n-1}(S(x,r))}.

For n=1, zero-dimensional area counts points. The relative interior is the interior within the affine span of the face. Then

\sum_{\substack{F\text{ a nonempty face of }P\\\text{including }P}}(-1)^{\dim F}\alpha(F)=0.

In particular, the whole-polytope term uses \alpha(P)=1.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Finite-dimensional affine geometry and surface measure extend standard linear algebra and multivariable calculus. Relative interior and normalized solid angle are explicitly described and form a small extension of those concepts.

  • The dimension-independent alternating angle relation is a classical structural identity for convex polytopes.

Proof 1: Directional projection and integrated face counts (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (pp.1214–1217).

At a relative-interior point of a face, the active facet inequalities define its tangent cone. On a sufficiently small sphere this cone cuts out exactly the solid angle used here, so the source’s normalized spherical measure is \alpha(F). The directional count gives the sum over proper faces as (-1)^{n-1}; adjoining the whole-polytope term (-1)^n\alpha(P)=(-1)^n yields zero. For n=1, the two endpoint angles are each 1/2.
