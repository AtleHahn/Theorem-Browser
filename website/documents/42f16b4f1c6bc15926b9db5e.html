Statement

Let k be a field, let n\ge1, and write x=(x_1,\ldots,x_n) and y=(y_1,\ldots,y_n). Let f_1,\ldots,f_s\in k[x,y] be a finite system of polynomials, and suppose \widehat y\in k[[x]]^n satisfies f_j(x,\widehat y(x))=0 for every j. For every integer c\ge1 there is y(x)\in k[[x]]^n whose components are algebraic over the rational function field k(x), such that f_j(x,y(x))=0 for every j and y-\widehat y\in(x_1,\ldots,x_n)^c k[[x]]^n. Here k[[x]] is the ring of formal power series, and the congruence means that corresponding coefficients of total degree less than c agree.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Formal multivariate series, substitution into polynomial systems, algebraicity over a rational-function field and ideal-adic accuracy together are beyond a confidently small extension of introductory algebra. Borderline conservatism applies to the cumulative burden.
