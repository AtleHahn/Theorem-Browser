Statement

Let X be compact Hausdorff and let A\subseteq C(X,\mathbb R) be a real subalgebra containing a nonzero constant function and separating points. Then A is dense in the uniform norm.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Compact Hausdorff spaces and uniform convergence are basic topology/analysis. A function subalgebra is closed under pointwise sums, products and scalar multiples, and separating points means some function distinguishes each pair. These direct definitions suffice for the density claim.

Proof 1: Take real parts of complex approximants — outline

The complex Stone–Weierstrass theorem makes the complex unital self-adjoint algebra generated by A dense in C(X,\mathbb C). For real-valued generators, taking real parts of this algebra yields exactly the real unital algebra generated by them, namely A. Real parts of uniformly approximating functions converge uniformly to the real part of their target. Hence each real continuous target is uniformly approximated by elements of A.

Level justification

Complexify the real function algebra and take real parts of uniform approximants. Basic compact topology, uniform convergence and elementary algebra of functions suffice; no abstract C-star representations are used.

Proof 2: Specialize the locally compact theorem

The locally compact Stone–Weierstrass theorem says that a real subalgebra of C_0(X,\mathbb R) is dense precisely when it separates points and vanishes nowhere. On compact X, C_0(X,\mathbb R)=C(X,\mathbb R). The nonzero constant in A ensures it vanishes nowhere. Thus the stated compact theorem follows.

Proof 3: Use absolute-value approximation and the lattice theorem — outline

Use the lattice density theorem: on a compact Hausdorff space with at least two points, a lattice of continuous real functions is dense if it interpolates arbitrary values at any two distinct points. Lattice operations can be expressed using absolute values, and absolute values can be uniformly approximated by polynomials.

Apply this to the uniform closure of A: polynomial approximation of |t| on bounded intervals makes it closed under f\mapsto|f|, hence under maximum and minimum. Point separation and constants give arbitrary two-point interpolation by affine transformations. The lattice theorem then gives density. The zero- or one-point cases follow from constants.
