Statement

For real or complex n×n matrix A and conformable U,B,V of sizes n×k,k×k,k×n, assume A and B+BVA^{-1}UB are nonsingular. Then (A+UBV)^{-1}=A^{-1}-A^{-1}UB(B+BVA^{-1}UB)^{-1}BVA^{-1}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves real or complex matrices; matrix inversion; finite-dimensional linear algebra. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Rank and factor the middle inverse

Local Woodbury calculation

Let A,U,C,V be real or complex matrices, of sizes n×n,n×k,k×k,k×n respectively. Assume A,C,A+UCV and C^{-1}+VA^{-1}U are invertible. Then (A+UCV)^{-1}=A^{-1}-A^{-1}U(C^{-1}+VA^{-1}U)^{-1}VA^{-1}.

The formula can be proven by checking that {\displaystyle (A+UCV)} times its alleged inverse on the right side of the Woodbury identity gives the identity matrix:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}&\left(A+UCV\right)\left[A^{-1}-A^{-1}U\left(C^{-1}+VA^{-1}U\right)^{-1}VA^{-1}\right]\\={}&\left\{I-U\left(C^{-1}+VA^{-1}U\right)^{-1}VA^{-1}\right\}+\left\{UCVA^{-1}-UCVA^{-1}U\left(C^{-1}+VA^{-1}U\right)^{-1}VA^{-1}\right\}\\={}&\left\{I+UCVA^{-1}\right\}-\left\{U\left(C^{-1}+VA^{-1}U\right)^{-1}VA^{-1}+UCVA^{-1}U\left(C^{-1}+VA^{-1}U\right)^{-1}VA^{-1}\right\}\\={}&I+UCVA^{-1}-\left(U+UCVA^{-1}U\right)\left(C^{-1}+VA^{-1}U\right)^{-1}VA^{-1}\\={}&I+UCVA^{-1}-UC\left(C^{-1}+VA^{-1}U\right)\left(C^{-1}+VA^{-1}U\right)^{-1}VA^{-1}\\={}&I+UCVA^{-1}-UCVA^{-1}\\={}&I.\end{aligned}}}

Binomial form

If A, B, U, V are matrices of sizes n×n, k×k, n×k, k×n, respectively, then

{\displaystyle \left(A+UBV\right)^{-1}=A^{-1}-A^{-1}UB\left(B+BVA^{-1}UB\right)^{-1}BVA^{-1}}

provided A and B + BVA−1UB are nonsingular. Nonsingularity of the latter requires that B−1 exist since it equals B(I + VA−1UB) and the rank of the latter cannot exceed the rank of B.

Since B is invertible, the two B terms flanking the parenthetical quantity inverse in the right-hand side can be replaced with (B−1)−1, which results in the original Woodbury identity.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof checks a matrix inverse by multiplication and uses a rank bound to deduce that B is invertible. Factoring B+BVA^{-1}UB as B(B^{-1}+VA^{-1}U)B converts the locally proved Woodbury expression into the claimed one. These are standard finite-dimensional linear algebra operations. The displayed right-inverse calculation also establishes invertibility of A+UBV, without needing to assume it separately.
