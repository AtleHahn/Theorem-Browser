Statement

Define \chi(X)=\sum_{j\ge0}(-1)^j b_j(X), where b_j(X)=\dim_{\mathbb Q}H_j(X;\mathbb Q); these dimensions are finite and vanish for all sufficiently large j. For X=R, M={0} and N=R{0}, χ(X)=1, χ(M)=1, χ(N)=2 and χ(M∩N)=0. Thus χ(M∪N)≠χ(M)+χ(N)−χ(M∩N).

Proof 1: Use a point and its complement in the real line — outline

Take the real line, a one-point subset, and its complement. This gives a counterexample to unrestricted inclusion–exclusion.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Compute the ordinary homological Euler characteristic of the line, point and two complementary intervals. Singular homology and homotopy invariance are substantial algebraic topology prerequisites, even though the resulting arithmetic is 1 versus 3.
