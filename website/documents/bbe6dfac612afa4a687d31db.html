Statement

For a positive integer n, let \Lambda(n)=\log p if n=p^j for a prime p and j\geq1, and 0 otherwise. Let N\geq6, \alpha\in\mathbb R, and coprime integers a,q satisfy 1\leq q\leq N and |\alpha-a/q|\leq q^{-2}. For S(\alpha)=\sum_{n\leq N}\Lambda(n)e^{2\pi i n\alpha}, one has |S(\alpha)|\ll (N/\sqrt q+N^{4/5}+\sqrt{Nq})(\log N)^4, with an absolute implied constant.

Proof 1: Choose the two cutoffs (outline)

The argument for this estimate follows from Vaughan's identity by proving by a somewhat intricate argument that, for U,V\geq2 and UV\leq N,

|S(\alpha)|\ll\left(UV+q+\frac{N}{\sqrt U}+\frac{N}{\sqrt V} +\frac{N}{\sqrt q}+\sqrt{Nq}\right)(\log(qN))^4,

and then deducing the stated formula in the non-trivial cases when q\leq N and with U=V=N^{2/5}. The intermediate estimate is an external prerequisite: the source does not supply its intricate estimation argument. The cutoff and logarithm comparisons are left implicit.
