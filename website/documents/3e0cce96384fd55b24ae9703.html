Statement

There are sequences with X_n\Rightarrow X and Y_n\Rightarrow Y marginally, whose sums do not converge in distribution to the sum of independent variables having the limiting marginal laws.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Distributional convergence can be read through scalar distribution functions and their limits; independence and marginal distributions add linked basic probability definitions. Only one convergence mode occurs, and the witness has fixed uniform laws.

Proof 1: Opposite dependent uniform variables — outline

Take X_n uniform on (0,1) and Y_n=-X_n. The marginal laws are fixed, uniform on (0,1) and (-1,0), respectively, while X_n+Y_n=0 for every n. Independent random variables with these two uniform laws have a nonconstant sum, so their sum law is not the point mass at zero. Thus the displayed marginal convergence cannot determine that independent sum limit.

Level justification

Compare the identically zero dependent sum with a sum of independent uniforms. Undergraduate elementary probability and the definition of convergence in distribution suffice.
