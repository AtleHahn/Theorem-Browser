Statement

Let P be a three-dimensional simple convex polyhedron, meaning that exactly three edges meet at every vertex. For i\ge3 let p_i count its faces with i sides; only finitely many p_i are nonzero. Then \sum_{i\ge3}(6-i)p_i=12.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The finite face and incidence counts use a locally defined simple convex polyhedron and elementary integer arithmetic.

Proof 1: Euler constraint on faces of a simple polyhedron

In a simple polyhedron, every vertex is incident to three angles of faces, and every edge is incident to two sides of faces. Since the numbers of angles and sides of the faces are given, one can calculate the three numbers {\displaystyle v} (the total number of vertices), {\displaystyle e} (the total number of edges), and {\displaystyle f} (the total number of faces), by summing over all faces and multiplying by an appropriate factor:

{\displaystyle v={\frac {1}{3}}\sum _{i}i\,p_{i},\qquad e={\frac {1}{2}}\sum _{i}i\,p_{i},\qquad f=\sum _{i}p_{i}.}

Plugging these values into Euler's polyhedral formula {\displaystyle v-e+f=2} and clearing denominators leads to the equation

{\displaystyle \sum _{i}(6-i)p_{i}=12,}

which must be satisfied by the face counts of every simple polyhedron.
