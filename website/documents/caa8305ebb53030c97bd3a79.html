Statement

Let \gamma be a smooth regular simple closed curve on a sphere of positive radius in \mathbb R^3. If \gamma is not contained in any closed hemisphere, then at least four distinct points of \gamma have osculating planes passing through the center of the sphere.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Undergraduate level, with regularity now explicit in the statement. For center o and radius R, differentiating |gamma-o|^2=R^2 gives (gamma-o) dot gamma prime=0 and (gamma-o) dot gamma double-prime=-|gamma prime|^2. Since gamma prime is nonzero, velocity and acceleration are linearly independent, so the osculating plane is well-defined everywhere; no separate nonzero-curvature hypothesis is missing. The plane is gamma(t)+span{gamma prime(t),gamma double-prime(t)}. Sphere, hemisphere, derivatives and this plane definition are a small extension of multivariable calculus and linear algebra within the preparation allowance.

Proof 1: Specialize Segre’s theorem to the sphere center — outline

Apply Segre’s theorem: if a point o lies in the convex hull of a smooth regular simple closed spherical curve but does not lie on the curve, then the osculating planes at at least four distinct points of the curve pass through o. Take o to be the sphere center. If o were outside the convex hull, strict separation would place the curve in an open hemisphere, contrary to the hypothesis. The sphere has positive radius, so o is not on the curve. Segre’s theorem therefore gives the conclusion.

Level justification

Undergraduate level. The explicit regularity assumption and sphere identity ensure a well-defined osculating plane at every point. If the center were outside the compact convex hull, strict separation would place the curve in an open hemisphere, contradicting the hypothesis. The center is off the curve because the radius is positive. The correctly quoted off-curve case of Segre’s theorem therefore gives four distinct curve points; its proof is not needed. The derivative and plane definitions are a small extension of multivariable calculus and linear algebra, and the supplied separation result is a concrete finite-dimensional convex-geometric implication.
