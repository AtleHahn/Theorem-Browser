Statement

Here a formal term is an expression built from variable, constant and function symbols, compared as written without evaluating it. A listed substitution simultaneously replaces each occurrence of its listed variables by the assigned terms, fixes all other variables, and leaves constant and function symbols unchanged; replacements are not repeated inside the inserted terms in the same application. There are idempotent substitutions \sigma,\tau with \sigma\tau\ne\tau\sigma, where composition applies the left substitution first. Idempotent means that applying the substitution twice gives the same replacements as applying it once.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Composition and idempotence are explicitly defined for finite simultaneous symbol replacements.

Proof 1: Compute the compositions in both orders

For distinct x,y,z, let \sigma=\{x\mapsto y\} and \tau=\{y\mapsto z\}. Each is idempotent. Direct composition gives \sigma\tau=\{x\mapsto z,y\mapsto z\} but \tau\sigma=\{x\mapsto y,y\mapsto z\}. Their images of x differ.
