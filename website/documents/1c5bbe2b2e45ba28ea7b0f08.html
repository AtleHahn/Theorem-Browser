Statement

Let y_1,\ldots,y_n be real observations with n\ge1, partitioned into nonempty groups I_1,\ldots,I_k. Put n_j=|I_j|, \bar y=n^{-1}\sum_i y_i, and \bar y_j=n_j^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_j}y_i. Then

\frac1n\sum_i(y_i-\bar y)^2=\sum_j\frac{n_j}{n}\left(\frac1{n_j}\sum_{i\in I_j}(y_i-\bar y_j)^2\right)+\sum_j\frac{n_j}{n}(\bar y_j-\bar y)^2.
Level
High school only
Level justification

All groups, means and sums are explicitly finite real arithmetic.

Proof 1: Cancel centered sums within each group

For i\in I_j, write y_i-\bar y=(y_i-\bar y_j)+(\bar y_j-\bar y) and expand the square. In each group the cross terms sum to

2(\bar y_j-\bar y)\sum_{i\in I_j}(y_i-\bar y_j)=0,

because \sum_{i\in I_j}y_i=n_j\bar y_j. The remaining within-group squares and n_j copies of the between-group square give the claimed identity after division by n.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Expand each centered square by adding and subtracting the group mean. The within-group deviations sum to zero, leaving the two stated finite sums.
