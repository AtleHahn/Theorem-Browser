Statement

Assuming the continuum hypothesis 2^{\aleph_0}=\aleph_1, there is a two-coloring of the unordered pairs of a set of cardinality \aleph_1 with no uncountable homogeneous subset.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Undergraduate cardinality and finite coloring concepts suffice after learning aleph-one as the least uncountable cardinal. CH is supplied, and a homogeneous subset means all its pairs have one color. No ordinal recursion or forcing is needed to understand the existence assertion.

Proof 1: Substitute the continuum hypothesis

Wacław Sierpiński showed that

2^{\aleph_0}\nrightarrow(\aleph_1)^2_2.

In particular, the continuum hypothesis implies that

\aleph_1\nrightarrow(\aleph_1)^2_2.

Here \kappa\nrightarrow(\lambda)^2_2 means that some two-coloring of the unordered pairs of a set of cardinality \kappa has no homogeneous subset of cardinality \lambda. The substitution uses 2^{\aleph_0}=\aleph_1.

Level justification

Substitute the assumed continuum equality into the supplied coloring theorem. Partition notation is fully explained as pair coloring and absence of an uncountable homogeneous set. Only elementary infinite cardinality and set coloring are needed, not ordinal combinatorics.
