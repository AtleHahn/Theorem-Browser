Statement

Let (\Omega_0,\mathcal A_0,P_0) be a probability space and let (\Omega_i,\mathcal A_i), i\ge1, be measurable spaces. For each i\ge1, let \kappa_i be a probability kernel from \prod_{j=0}^{i-1}\Omega_j to \Omega_i: for each history x, \kappa_i(x,\cdot) is a probability measure on \mathcal A_i, and for each B\in\mathcal A_i, x\mapsto\kappa_i(x,B) is measurable for the product sigma-algebra. There is a unique probability measure P on (\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}\Omega_i,\bigotimes_{i=0}^{\infty}\mathcal A_i) such that for every n\ge0 and measurable B_i\in\mathcal A_i, P(B_0\times\cdots\times B_n\times\prod_{i>n}\Omega_i)=\int_{B_0}P_0(dx_0)\int_{B_1}\kappa_1(x_0,dx_1)\cdots\int_{B_n}\kappa_n(x_0,\ldots,x_{n-1},dx_n). The integrals are iterated from right to left; for n=0 the right side is P_0(B_0). The product sigma-algebra is generated by these finite-coordinate cylinders.
