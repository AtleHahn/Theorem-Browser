Statement

Let k,x be positive integers and p_j the jth prime. Let M_x be the positive integers at most x all of whose prime divisors belong to \{p_1,\ldots,p_k\}. Then |M_x|\le2^k\sqrt x.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The objects and counting bound use elementary divisibility and finite choices.

Proof 1: Count the square-free factor and square root separately

Every n in Mx can be written as n = m2r with positive integers m and r, where r is square-free. Since only the k primes p1, ..., pk can show up (with exponent 1) in the prime factorization of r, there are at most 2k different possibilities for r. Furthermore, there are at most √x possible values for m. This gives us the upper estimate

{\displaystyle |M_{x}|\leq 2^{k}{\sqrt {x}}}
