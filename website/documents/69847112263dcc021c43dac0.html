Statement

Let n,m\geq1 and let f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^m be a nonconstant smooth map. There is a regular value y\in\mathbb R^m, meaning that every x with f(x)=y has derivative of rank m; a value outside the image is allowed.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Smooth real multivariable functions; Jacobian matrices and rank; Fibers and the locally defined regular-value condition. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Exclude the null set of critical values

Sard’s theorem says the critical values have measure zero. Thus not every value is critical, giving at least one regular value.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the supplied Euclidean Sard conclusion and choose a point outside its null set. Undergraduate derivative rank and the interval/box-cover meaning of nullity suffice for this application; no proof of Sard or manifold geometry is needed.
