Statement

Let (\Omega,\Sigma) be a measurable space, \mu a nonnegative sigma-finite measure, and \lambda a complex measure on \Sigma. There are unique complex measures \lambda_a,\lambda_s with

\lambda=\lambda_a+\lambda_s,\qquad \lambda_a\ll\mu,\qquad\lambda_s\perp\mu.

Moreover, there is h\in L^1(\mu;\mathbb C), unique up to equality \mu-almost everywhere, such that \lambda_a(E)=\int_Eh\,d\mu for every E\in\Sigma. If \lambda is nonnegative and finite, so are both components.

A complex measure is a countably additive complex-valued measure (of finite total variation). Absolute continuity means \mu(E)=0 implies |\lambda_a|(E)=0. Singularity means that some measurable N has \mu(N)=0 and |\lambda_s|(\Omega\setminus N)=0. Here |\lambda| is total variation, obtained by taking the supremum of sums of absolute values over measurable partitions. Sigma-finite means a countable cover by sets of finite measure, and L^1 means measurable functions of integrable absolute value.
