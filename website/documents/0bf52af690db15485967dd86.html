Statement

Let X be a real-valued random variable on a probability space with finite second moment. Write \mu=\mathbb E[X] for its mean and \sigma^2=\mathbb E[(X-\mu)^2] for its variance, which may be zero. For every real \lambda>0,

\Pr(X-\mu\ge\lambda)\le\frac{\sigma^2}{\sigma^2+\lambda^2}.

Here \Pr denotes probability and \mathbb E denotes expectation, or integration against the probability law.

Level justification

From undergraduate integration, a probability law and its weighted integrals supply the mean and variance in the displayed formulas. The inequality concerns one real random quantity and one threshold. These related definitions fit the short reading allowance; no convergence of random variables or construction of measure theory is needed. General probability laws go beyond the high-school finite weighted-sum case.
