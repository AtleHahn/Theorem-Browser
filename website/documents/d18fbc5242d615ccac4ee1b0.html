Statement

A Euclidean Hurwitz algebra is a nonzero finite-dimensional real unital algebra A (not assumed associative), with a positive-definite quadratic form q satisfying q(ab)=q(a)q(b). Write (a,b)=\frac12(q(a+b)-q(a)-q(b)), \|a\|^2=q(a), a^*=-a+2(a,1)1, L(a)b=ab, R(a)b=ba, and \operatorname{Re}a=(a,1)1. Up to real algebra isomorphism, A is \mathbb R, \mathbb C, \mathbb H or \mathbb O, where \mathbb H is the quaternion algebra and \mathbb O the octonion algebra.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Undergraduate level. The statement defines a finite-dimensional real vector space with a bilinear unital multiplication, not necessarily associative, and a positive-definite multiplicative quadratic form. Linear algebra and introductory algebra supply those notions and algebra isomorphism. Quaternion and octonion names can be understood concretely through the supplied successive pair/doubling construction: real dimensions 1, 2, 4 and 8 with explicit multiplication and conjugation. Learning these related finite-dimensional constructions is a small extension within approximately 30 minutes; general nonassociative-algebra structure theory and a proof of Hurwitz’s theorem are not prerequisites for understanding the claim.

Proof 1: Successive real, complex, quaternion and octonion subalgebras

It is routine to check that the real numbers R, the complex numbers C and the quaternions H are examples of associative Euclidean Hurwitz algebras with their standard norms and involutions. There are moreover natural inclusions R ⊂ C ⊂ H.

Use the following doubling result: if B is a proper unital subalgebra of A and j a unit vector orthogonal to B, then B is associative and the real subalgebra generated by B and j is the orthogonal direct sum B\oplus Bj, with

(a+bj)^*=a^*-bj,\qquad (a+bj)(c+dj)=(ac-d^*b)+(bc^*+da)j.

The doubling result applies to the inclusion of R in C and C in H. Taking O = H ⊕ H with the displayed product and the sum inner product gives a noncommutative nonassociative real algebra generated by the embedded copy H ⊕ {0} and J = (0, 1). This recovers the usual definition of the octonions or Cayley numbers. If A is a Euclidean Hurwitz algebra, it must contain R. If it is strictly larger than R, the doubling result shows that it contains C. If it is larger than C, it contains H. If it is larger still, it must contain O. But there the process must stop, because O is not associative. In fact, choose a,b\in\mathbb H with ab\ne ba. Then a(bj) = (ba)j ≠ (ab)j in O.
