Proof 1: Set r equal to two

Turán’s theorem states that every graph G with n vertices that does not contain K_{r+1} as a subgraph has at most as many edges as the Turán graph T(n,r). This graph is formed by partitioning the vertices into r parts of equal or nearly equal size, and connecting two vertices by an edge whenever they belong to two different parts.

The special case of Turán’s theorem for r=2 is Mantel’s theorem: the maximum number of edges in an n-vertex triangle-free graph is \lfloor n^2/4\rfloor. Here T(n,2) has \lfloor n/2\rfloor\lceil n/2\rceil=\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor edges.

Proof 2: Combine the degree-square bound with finite Cauchy–Schwarz — outline

Let G=(V,E) be a finite simple triangle-free graph on n vertices.

Consider any two adjacent vertices {\displaystyle x} and {\displaystyle y} in the graph with degrees {\displaystyle d(x)} and {\displaystyle d(y)} respectively. Since the graph is triangle-free, the neighbourhoods of {\displaystyle x} and {\displaystyle y} are disjoint, which gives {\displaystyle d(x)+d(y)\leq n}. Summing this inequality over all the edges gives the following.

{\displaystyle \sum _{xy\in E(G)}(d(x)+d(y))\leq \sum _{xy\in E(G)}n}

We then sum over vertices instead of edges.

{\displaystyle \sum _{x\in V(G)}d(x)^{2}\leq n|E|}

Finite sum inequality

For any two real sequences u_1,\ldots,u_n and v_1,\ldots,v_n, the difference between the products of square sums and the squared product sum is

\left(\sum_i u_i^2\right)\left(\sum_i v_i^2\right)-\left(\sum_i u_iv_i\right)^2=\frac12\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{j=1}^n(u_iv_j-u_jv_i)^2\ge0.

Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives the following inequality relating the sum of squares of degrees to the sum of degrees.

{\displaystyle (\sum _{x\in V(G)}d(x))^{2}\leq n(\sum _{x\in V(G)}d(x)^{2})}

Chaining the previous two inequalities, we get:

{\displaystyle (\sum _{x\in V(G)}d(x))^{2}\leq n^{2}|E|}

And then by applying the handshaking lemma and then rearranging, we have that:

{\displaystyle |E|\leq {\frac {n^{2}}{4}}}

If |E|=0, the bound is immediate; otherwise the rearrangement divides by |E|. Since |E| is an integer, |E|\leq\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor.

For attainment, partition the vertices into two parts of equal or nearly equal size, and connect two vertices by an edge whenever they belong to two different parts. This is the Turán graph T(n,2); it is triangle-free and has \lfloor n/2\rfloor\lceil n/2\rceil=\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor edges.
