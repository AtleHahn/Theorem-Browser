Statement

Let A,B,C be events with P(A\cap C)>0 and P(B\cap C)>0. For events E,F with P(F)>0, conditional probability means P(E\mid F)=P(E\cap F)/P(F). Then

P(A\mid B\cap C)=\frac{P(B\mid A\cap C)P(A\mid C)}{P(B\mid C)}.
Level
High school only
Level justification

The finite-event notation is explained by the ratio definition; the statement uses elementary real-number arithmetic.

Proof 1: Equate the two three-event products

This can be deduced as follows. Using the chain rule (obtained by multiplying the defining conditional-probability ratios)

{\displaystyle P(A\cap B\cap C)=P(A\vert B\cap C)\,P(B\vert C)\,P(C)}

And, on the other hand

{\displaystyle P(A\cap B\cap C)=P(B\cap A\cap C)=P(B\vert A\cap C)\,P(A\vert C)\,P(C)}

The desired result is obtained by identifying both expressions and solving for {\displaystyle P(A\vert B\cap C)}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Multiply the conditional-probability ratios to express the same triple-intersection probability in two orders, then cancel P(C) and divide by P(B|C). The stated positive intersection probabilities make all these denominators positive. The proof is elementary fraction algebra.
