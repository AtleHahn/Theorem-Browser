Statement

On a measure space (S,\Sigma,\mu), write \|h\|_t=(\int_S|h|^t\,d\mu)^{1/t} for 0<t<\infty, and \|h\|_\infty=\operatorname{ess\,sup}|h|. Norms may be infinite; products with a zero factor are defined to be zero, including 0\cdot\infty. Let n\ge2, p_k\in(0,\infty], \theta_k\in(0,1), \sum_k\theta_k=1, and 1/p=\sum_k\theta_k/p_k. Then \|\prod_k|f_k|^{\theta_k}\|_p\le\prod_k\|f_k\|_{p_k}^{\theta_k} for measurable real or complex f_k.

Proof 1: Weighted product interpolation inequality

Given measurable real- or complex-valued functions {\displaystyle f_{k}} on S, then the generalized Hölder inequality for a product of n functions, with exponents p_k/\theta_k gives

{\displaystyle \left\||f_{1}|^{\theta _{1}}\cdots |f_{n}|^{\theta _{n}}\right\|_{p}\leq \left\||f_{1}|^{\theta _{1}}\right\|_{\frac {p_{1}}{\theta _{1}}}\cdots \left\||f_{n}|^{\theta _{n}}\right\|_{\frac {p_{n}}{\theta _{n}}}=\|f_{1}\|_{p_{1}}^{\theta _{1}}\cdots \|f_{n}\|_{p_{n}}^{\theta _{n}}.}
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply generalized Holder with scaled exponents and simplify the norms of powers, including essential-supremum cases. General measurable-function norms and integration are essential.
