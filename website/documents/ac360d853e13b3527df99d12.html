Statement

Let a finite graph have a proper vertex coloring, meaning an assignment of colors to its vertices for which endpoints of every edge have different colors. Fix two colors a and b, and form the subgraph containing exactly the vertices of these colors and all edges between them. Interchanging a and b on one connected component of this subgraph gives another proper coloring. Here connected means joined by an edge path.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All objects and the swap are defined as finite combinatorial rules.

Proof 1: Swap the two colors on one component — outline

Consider the subgraph consisting of vertices colored a or b and the edges connecting them. Each edge joins a vertex of color a to one of color b. Swap a and b throughout one connected component; this preserves the coloring outside the component and gives a proper coloring.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Swapping the two colors preserves edges inside the component; no edge to another vertex of either color leaves that component, and other colors remain distinct. Elementary finite graph reasoning suffices.
