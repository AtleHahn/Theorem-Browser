Statement

Let N\ge1 be an integer and x[0],\ldots,x[N-1]\in\mathbb C. Define X[k]=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x[n]e^{-2\pi i kn/N}. Then \sum_{n=0}^{N-1}|x[n]|^2=N^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}|X[k]|^2.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves finite complex sequences; complex exponentials; discrete Fourier transform. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Expand one transform factor and use inverse DFT

Use the inverse DFT formula x[n]=N^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}X[k]e^{2\pi i kn/N}. By using the definition of inverse DFT of {\displaystyle X[k]}, we can derive

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}{\frac {1}{N}}\sum _{k=0}^{N-1}{\bigl |}X[k]{\bigr |}^{2}&={\frac {1}{N}}\sum _{k=0}^{N-1}X[k]\cdot X^{*}[k]={\frac {1}{N}}\sum _{k=0}^{N-1}{\Biggl (}\sum _{n=0}^{N-1}x[n]\,\exp \left(-i{\frac {2\pi }{N}}k\,n\right){\Biggr )}\,X^{*}[k]\\[5pt]&={\frac {1}{N}}\sum _{n=0}^{N-1}x[n]{\Biggl (}\sum _{k=0}^{N-1}X^{*}[k]\,\exp \left(-i{\frac {2\pi }{N}}k\,n\right){\Biggr )}={\frac {1}{N}}\sum _{n=0}^{N-1}x[n]{\bigl (}N\cdot x^{*}[n]{\bigr )}\\[5pt]&=\sum _{n=0}^{N-1}{\bigl |}x[n]{\bigr |}^{2},\end{aligned}}}

where {\displaystyle *} represents complex conjugate.
