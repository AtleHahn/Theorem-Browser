Statement

A partial order is a reflexive, antisymmetric, transitive relation \le. Distinct elements are comparable when one is below the other. A chain has every pair comparable; an antichain has no distinct comparable pair. A partition consists of disjoint subsets whose union is the whole set. If a partially ordered set has a largest antichain of finite size w, its minimum chain-partition size is w. Conversely, if its minimum chain-partition size is finite w, its largest antichain has size w.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Partial orders, chains, antichains and countability are undergraduate set/order concepts. A chain means pairwise comparable and an antichain pairwise incomparable; selection principles, where assumed, are explicit hypotheses. These short definitions suffice for the quantified existence or partition claim.

Proof 1: Compactness of the incomparability coloring — outline

Every chain meets an antichain in at most one element, so the size of any antichain is at most the number of chains in any chain partition.

Use the finite Dilworth theorem and the De Bruijn–Erdős coloring theorem: a graph whose every finite induced subgraph can be colored with at most w colors is itself w-colorable.

Suppose that an infinite partial order P has width w, meaning that there are at most a finite number w of elements in any antichain. For any subset S of P, a decomposition into w chains (if it exists) may be described as a coloring of the incomparability graph of S (a graph that has the elements of S as vertices, with an edge between every two incomparable elements) using w colors; every color class in a proper coloring of the incomparability graph must be a chain. By the assumption that P has width w, and by the finite version of Dilworth's theorem, every finite subset S of P has a w-colorable incomparability graph. Therefore, by the De Bruijn–Erdős theorem, P itself also has a w-colorable incomparability graph, and thus has the desired partition into chains.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Form the incomparability graph and apply the explicitly supplied finite-color compactness conclusion to its finite induced subgraphs. Elementary set theory and graph coloring suffice to understand this application; no logical or topological proof of compactness is used.

The reverse direction uses the elementary chain-antichain bound and the forward direction at the actual maximum antichain size; its finite maximum exists when chain-partition size is finite. The source leaves this standard converse implicit.
