Statement

Let X be a choice set and let f(x,t) be differentiable in t on a parameter domain T\subseteq\mathbb R^K, with \|\nabla_t f(x,t)\|\le B for all x,t. Let x^*(t) be a maximizing choice and V(t)=f(x^*(t),t). Let \gamma:[0,1]\to T be a differentiable path with bounded derivative, joining t_0 to t_1. Derivatives are the ordinary local multivariable derivatives on the parameter domain. Then V(t_1)-V(t_0)=\int_0^1\nabla_t f(x^*(\gamma(s)),\gamma(s))\cdot\gamma^\prime(s)\,ds. The integral is a Lebesgue integral along the path.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement concerns an arbitrary family of multivariable objectives, optimizing selections and a Lebesgue path integral; no envelope theorem is required just to understand it.

Proof 1: Apply the scalar integral formula along a path — outline

Use the finite-valued integral envelope theorem: if every objective is differentiable and absolutely continuous in the scalar parameter, their derivatives have a common integrable bound, and an optimum is attained almost everywhere, then the value is absolutely continuous and its change is the integral of the selected objective derivative. Apply it to the objectives along the path \gamma. The chain rule gives the parameter derivative \nabla_t f(x,\gamma(s))\cdot\gamma^\prime(s); the stated gradient and path-derivative bounds give the common bound needed for the theorem. This gives the path integral formula.

Level justification

The chain rule is standard, but the invoked envelope theorem requires absolute continuity, almost-everywhere maximizers and Lebesgue integrable domination. These linked real-measure-analysis prerequisites exceed the baseline.
