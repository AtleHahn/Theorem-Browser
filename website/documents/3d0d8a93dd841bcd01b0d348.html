Statement

For 0\le h\le1, let Y be sampled from N(\mu_h,\sigma_h^2) with weight h and from N(\mu_t,\sigma_t^2) with weight 1-h, where means are real and variances finite and nonnegative. Then \operatorname{Var}(Y)=h\sigma_h^2+(1-h)\sigma_t^2+h(1-h)(\mu_h-\mu_t)^2.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The experiment is an explicit mixture of two normal distributions. Mean and variance are weighted integrals and mixture weights are finite probabilities; these closely related elementary probability definitions suffice without general conditional expectation.

Proof 1: Add within-component and between-component variances

For 0<h<1, use the law of total variance \operatorname{Var}(Y)=\operatorname E[\operatorname{Var}(Y\mid X)]+\operatorname{Var}(\operatorname E[Y\mid X]).

Let X be a coin flip taking values Heads with probability h and Tails with probability 1−h. Given Heads, {\displaystyle Y\sim \mathrm {Normal} (\mu _{h},\sigma _{h}^{2})}; given Tails, {\displaystyle Y\sim \mathrm {Normal} (\mu _{t},\sigma _{t}^{2})}. Then {\displaystyle \operatorname {E} [\operatorname {Var} (Y\mid X)]=h\,\sigma _{h}^{2}+(1-h)\,\sigma _{t}^{2},} {\displaystyle \operatorname {Var} (\operatorname {E} [Y\mid X])=h\,(1-h)\,(\mu _{h}-\mu _{t})^{2},} so {\displaystyle \operatorname {Var} (Y)=h\,\sigma _{h}^{2}+(1-h)\,\sigma _{t}^{2}\;+\;h\,(1-h)\,(\mu _{h}-\mu _{t})^{2}.}

For h=0 or h=1, only the positive-weight component is sampled, and the formula reduces to its variance.

Level justification

Condition on the two-valued coin, evaluate two finite weighted averages and add the component and mean variances. Elementary undergraduate probability suffices; no general conditional-expectation construction is used.
