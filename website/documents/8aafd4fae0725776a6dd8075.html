Statement

Let p be prime. On V=\mathbb F_p^2, the representation of \mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z given by \rho(n)=\begin{pmatrix}1&n\\0&1\end{pmatrix} has invariant line W=\mathbb F_p(1,0), and W has no invariant direct complement.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Group theory and linear algebra suffice after defining a linear representation as an action by invertible linear maps. Invariant subspaces and direct complements are familiar, and irreducibility is explicitly the absence of a proper nonzero invariant subspace. No character or module classification theory is needed.

Proof 1: The unipotent matrix has only one eigenline

Consider a prime p, the group \mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z, the field K=\mathbb F_p, and the representation

\rho:\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z\to\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_p),\qquad \rho(n)=\begin{bmatrix}1&n\\0&1\end{bmatrix}.

Simple calculations show that there is only one eigenline for \begin{bmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{bmatrix} here, namely W. Any one-dimensional subspace preserved by \rho has to be spanned by an eigenvector of this matrix, so the one-dimensional subrepresentation is unique, and \mathbb F_p^2 cannot be decomposed into the direct sum of two one-dimensional subrepresentations.
