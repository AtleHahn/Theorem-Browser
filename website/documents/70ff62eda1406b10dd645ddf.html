Statement

A finite tiling of a sphere by pentagonal and hexagonal disk faces has exactly three faces meeting at each vertex and two faces at each edge. Then it has exactly 12 pentagons.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement describes finite elementary polygon incidences on a sphere.

Proof 1: Count face incidences and substitute in Euler’s formula

If P pentagons and H hexagons are used, then there are F=P+H faces, V=\tfrac13(5P+6H) vertices, and E=\tfrac12(5P+6H) edges. The Euler characteristic is thus

V-E+F=\tfrac13(5P+6H)-\tfrac12(5P+6H)+P+H=\tfrac16P.

Because the sphere has Euler characteristic 2, it follows that P=12. That is, a spherical tiling constructed in this way always has 12 pentagons.
