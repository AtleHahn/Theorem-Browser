Simpler proof assessment

Likely alternative prerequisite level: High school only; lowest recorded proof level: Undergraduate level. For D translated to zero, define X'=X/|X|^2 for each nonzero point. Expanding squared coordinate distances gives |X'-Y'|=|X-Y|/(|X||Y|) in every finite dimension. Apply the ordinary triangle inequality to A',B',C' and multiply denominators to obtain Ptolemy. Coincident points are checked directly. This uses finite sums of coordinate squares, not affine-span dimension or spatial flattening; the formula covers the full R^n statement.

Statement

For any four points A,B,C,D in Euclidean space \mathbb R^n, AB\cdot CD+BC\cdot DA\ge AC\cdot BD, where juxtaposed point names denote Euclidean distances.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement involves finite Euclidean distances, products and elementary geometric configurations.

Proof 1: Complex identity and flattening to a plane (outline)

First suppose the points lie in a plane and identify them with complex numbers. The identity

(A-B)(C-D)+(A-D)(B-C)=(A-C)(B-D)

and the complex triangle inequality give the result after taking moduli.

For four noncoplanar points, rotate one vertex about the diagonal joining its two neighbors until the four points are planar and the other diagonal has maximal length. The four side lengths and the rotation-axis diagonal remain fixed, while the other diagonal increases. Apply the planar inequality to the resulting configuration; its right-hand product is at least the original one. Four points in any higher-dimensional Euclidean space lie in an affine subspace of dimension at most three, so the same reduction applies.

Proof 2: Inversion triangle inequality and spatial flattening (outline)

First take four distinct coplanar points and invert with center D and radius r>0. The standard inversion distance formula is X'Y'=r^2XY/(DX\,DY). Applying the triangle inequality to the three images gives

A'B'+B'C'\ge A'C'.

Substitute the distance formula and multiply by DA\,DB\,DC/r^2 to obtain

AB\cdot DC+BC\cdot DA\ge AC\cdot DB.

Coincident-point cases follow immediately by substituting the zero distance and identifying the repeated point.

For noncoplanar points, use the source's spatial reduction: rotate B about AC into the plane ACD to maximize BD. The other five distances remain fixed and BD increases, so the planar inequality implies the original inequality. Four points in any Euclidean space have affine span of dimension at most three.
