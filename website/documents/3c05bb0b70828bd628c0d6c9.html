Statement

For a complex Hilbert space H, write \mathbf P(H) for its nonzero one-dimensional complex subspaces. For nonzero representatives v,w, put c([v],[w])=|\langle v,w\rangle|/(\|v\|\|w\|). A ray symmetry is a bijection of ray spaces preserving c. If U:H\to K is unitary or antiunitary, then T_U([v])=[Uv] is a ray symmetry. Unitary maps are bijective complex-linear inner-product isometries; antiunitary maps are bijective conjugate-linear maps satisfying \langle Uv,Uw\rangle=\overline{\langle v,w\rangle}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Rays are explicitly one-dimensional subspaces. Unitary and antiunitary maps are defined by linearity/conjugate linearity and the inner-product identity, direct extensions of complex linear algebra. Hilbert completeness adds no further structure used by the preservation claim.

Proof 1: Preservation of the normalized absolute inner product

In the proof write \underline{\Psi}=[\Psi] and \underline{\Psi}\cdot\underline{\Phi}=c([\Psi],[\Phi]).

Given a unitary transformation {\displaystyle U:H\to K} between Hilbert spaces, define

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}T_{U}:\mathbf {P} (H)&\to \mathbf {P} (K)\\{\underline {\Psi }}&\mapsto {\underline {U\Psi }}\\\end{aligned}}}

This is a symmetry transformation since

{\displaystyle T_{U}{\underline {\Psi }}\cdot T_{U}{\underline {\Phi }}={\frac {\left|\langle U\Psi ,U\Phi \rangle \right|}{\|U\Psi \|\|U\Phi \|}}={\frac {\left|\langle \Psi ,\Phi \rangle \right|}{\|\Psi \|\|\Phi \|}}={\underline {\Psi }}\cdot {\underline {\Phi }}.}

In the same way, an antiunitary transformation between Hilbert spaces induces a symmetry transformation.
