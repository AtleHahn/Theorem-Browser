Statement

If \theta/\pi\in\mathbb Q and \cos\theta\in\mathbb Q, then \cos\theta\in\{0,\pm\tfrac12,\pm1\}. Every value in this set occurs for a rational multiple of \pi.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Only rationality and elementary real trigonometric values are needed to understand the statements.

Proof 1: Lehmer’s algebraic-degree restriction outline

Write \theta=2\pi k/n with n\ge1 and \gcd(k,n)=1. If n>2, Lehmer’s degree theorem says that 2\cos\theta has algebraic degree \varphi(n)/2, where \varphi is Euler’s totient. If the cosine is rational this degree is one. Thus n\le2 or \varphi(n)=2, so the only possible denominators are n=1,2,3,4,6. Their usual cosine values are 1,-1,-1/2,0,1/2; all occur at rational multiples of \pi.

Level justification

Apply the supplied degree formula and enumerate the totient-two integers. Undergraduate polynomial algebra and elementary number theory suffice: algebraic degree is the least degree of a rational polynomial annihilating the number, a small extension; no cyclotomic extension structure is used.
