Arbitrarily long finite arithmetic patterns are forced by any finite classification of sufficiently many integers. Thus the presence of such a pattern alone cannot establish design or a special causal mechanism; some order is mathematically unavoidable even in arbitrary colorings.

Statement

For every pair of positive integers r,k, there is a positive integer N such that every coloring c:\{1,\ldots,N\}\to\{1,\ldots,r\} has a monochromatic arithmetic progression of length k. Explicitly, there are positive integers a,d with a+(k-1)d\le N and

c(a)=c(a+d)=\cdots=c(a+(k-1)d).

Proof 1: Finite color focusing by double induction (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorem2 and its complete proof, pp.1–2).

For k=1 take N=1 and d=1. In the inner induction, a (k-1)-term progression in the first W positions has its next term within 2W, since k\geq3 and its positive difference is at most W-1. The same bound applies to the progression of blocks, so the final common focus remains inside the finite interval being colored.

The argument uses only positive integers, pigeonhole counting and two finite inductions. Repeated color patterns in blocks produce one more focused color, and after all colors are focused the next term completes a monochromatic progression.
