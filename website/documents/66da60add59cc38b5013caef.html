Statement

Let integers A,B,D,x and a nonnegative integer n satisfy x^2+D=AB^n. Write n=3m+r with r\in\{0,1,2\} and set y=B^m. Then Y=AB^r x and X=AB^r y satisfy Y^2=X^3-A^2B^{2r}D.

Proof 1: Separate exponent residue classes and rescale

By representing {\displaystyle n=3m+r} with {\displaystyle r\in \{0,1,2\}} and {\displaystyle B^{n}=B^{r}y^{3}} with {\displaystyle y=B^{m}}, the equation of Ramanujan–Nagell type is reduced to the following three cubic equations (indexed by {\displaystyle r}):

{\displaystyle r=0:\qquad (Ax)^{2}=(Ay)^{3}-A^{2}D},{\displaystyle r=1:\qquad (ABx)^{2}=(ABy)^{3}-A^{2}B^{2}D},{\displaystyle r=2:\qquad (AB^{2}x)^{2}=(AB^{2}y)^{3}-A^{2}B^{4}D}.

In each case, put Y=AB^r x and X=AB^r y.

Separate the exponent modulo three, substitute y=B^m and multiply by the indicated square factor. School integer powers and polynomial algebra suffice.
