Statement

There exists an absolute constant C>0 such that the following holds for every integer n\geq1. Let X_1,\ldots,X_n be independent real random variables with \mathbb E X_i=0, \sigma_i^2=\mathbb E X_i^2>0, and \rho_i=\mathbb E|X_i|^3<\infty. Put

S_n=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^n X_i}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^n\sigma_i^2}},\qquad F_n(x)=\Pr(S_n\leq x),\qquad \Phi(x)=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^x e^{-t^2/2}\,dt.

Then

\sup_{x\in\mathbb R}|F_n(x)-\Phi(x)|\leq C\frac{\sum_{i=1}^n\rho_i}{(\sum_{i=1}^n\sigma_i^2)^{3/2}}.

The constant is independent of n and of the distributions of the random variables.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Real analysis and calculus provide integrals and supremum bounds. Probability distributions, independence and the three specified moments form a closely related small extension, with the normal cdf and normalization supplied explicitly; no characteristic-function proof theory is required.
