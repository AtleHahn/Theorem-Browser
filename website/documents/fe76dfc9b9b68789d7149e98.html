Completely positive transformations admit a representation as a larger algebraic representation followed by compression. In quantum theory this supports describing effective operations through a larger system, while leaving open which physical environment or interpretation realizes that representation.

Statement

For a unital completely positive map \Phi:A\to B(H) on a unital C-star algebra A, where H is a complex Hilbert space, there is a unital star representation \pi:A\to B(K) on a Hilbert space containing H such that \Phi(a)=P_H\pi(a)|_H.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Proof 1: Identify the dilation isometry with its range

By Stinespring's theorem, \Phi(a)=V^*\pi(a)V, where \pi:A\to B(K) is a unital star representation and V:H\to K is bounded. In particular V^*V=\Phi(1) holds so that V is an isometry if and only if \Phi(1)=1. In this case H can be embedded, in the Hilbert space sense, into K and V^*, acting on K, becomes the projection onto H. Symbolically, we can write

\Phi(a)=P_H\;\pi(a)\Big|_H.

In the language of dilation theory, this is to say that \Phi(a) is a compression of \pi(a). It is therefore a corollary of Stinespring's theorem that every unital completely positive map is the compression of some star homomorphism.

Level justification

Use Stinespring for a completely positive C-star algebra map, identify its isometry and compress the representation. C-star algebras, complete positivity and Hilbert-space dilation require operator algebra.
