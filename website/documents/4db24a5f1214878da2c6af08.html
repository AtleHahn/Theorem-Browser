Merging colors bounds multicolor Ramsey numbers

Statement

Write R(n_1,\ldots,n_c) for the least positive integer N such that every assignment of c colors to all unordered pairs of an N-element vertex set contains, for some i, a subset of size n_i whose pairs all have color i. A one-vertex subset is monochromatic in every color. For c>2 and positive integers n_1,\ldots,n_c, R(n_1,\ldots,n_c)\le R(n_1,\ldots,n_{c-2},R(n_{c-1},n_c)).

Level
High school only
Level justification

Finite graph terminology is defined by pairs of vertices; the statements require only finite counting and elementary logic.

Proof 1: Merge the last two colors and then separate them

Write K_j for the complete graph on j vertices. Use existence of the Ramsey numbers for fewer colours.

Consider a complete graph of {\displaystyle R(n_{1},\dots ,n_{c-2},R(n_{c-1},n_{c}))} vertices and colour its edges with c colours. Now 'go colour-blind' and pretend that c − 1 and c are the same colour. Thus the graph is now (c − 1)-coloured. Due to the definition of {\displaystyle R(n_{1},\dots ,n_{c-2},R(n_{c-1},n_{c})),} such a graph contains either a Kni mono-chromatically coloured with colour i for some 1 ≤ i ≤ c − 2 or a KR(nc − 1, nc)-coloured in the 'blurred colour'. In the former case we are finished. In the latter case, we recover our sight again and see from the definition of R(nc − 1, nc) we must have either a (c − 1)-monochrome Knc − 1 or a c-monochrome Knc. In either case the proof is complete.

Level justification

Merge the last two colors, apply the smaller-color threshold, and if necessary separate the two colors inside the resulting subset. School finite set reasoning suffices.
