Statement

Let 0\le r<R\le\infty, c\in\mathbb C, and let f be holomorphic on r<|z-c|<R. If two Laurent series \sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}a_n(z-c)^n and \sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}b_n(z-c)^n converge there to f (each nonnegative and negative part converging separately), then a_k=b_k for every integer k.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex differentiability and Laurent series extend the derivative and power-series definitions. The contour integrals are parametrized ordinary integrals on an annulus, and coefficient uniqueness is an equality of convergent series. These limited additions suffice without Riemann surfaces.

Proof 1: Extract each coefficient by contour integration

Suppose a function {\displaystyle f(z)} holomorphic on the annulus {\displaystyle r<|z-c|<R} has two Laurent series:

{\displaystyle f(z)=\sum _{n=-\infty }^{\infty }a_{n}(z-c)^{n}=\sum _{n=-\infty }^{\infty }b_{n}(z-c)^{n}.}

Multiply both sides by {\displaystyle (z-c)^{-k-1}}, where k is an arbitrary integer, and integrate on a positively oriented circle \gamma:|z-c|=\rho with r<\rho<R,

{\displaystyle \oint _{\gamma }\,\sum _{n=-\infty }^{\infty }a_{n}(z-c)^{n-k-1}\,dz=\oint _{\gamma }\,\sum _{n=-\infty }^{\infty }b_{n}(z-c)^{n-k-1}\,dz.}

The series converges uniformly on compact subannuli inside the convergence annulus, including one containing \gamma, so the integration and summation can be interchanged. Substituting the identity

{\displaystyle \oint _{\gamma }\,(z-c)^{n-k-1}\,dz=2\pi i\delta _{nk}}

where \delta_{nk}=1 if n=k and 0 otherwise, into the summation yields

{\displaystyle a_{k}=b_{k}.}

Hence the Laurent series is unique.

Level justification

Integrate the uniformly convergent Laurent series on a circle and use the integer-power integral to isolate one coefficient. Undergraduate complex analysis and uniform convergence suffice.
