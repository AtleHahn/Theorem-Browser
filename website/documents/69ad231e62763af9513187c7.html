Statement

Let Z_1,\ldots,Z_N be random variables and let G be a dependency graph for them: whenever disjoint index sets have no edge between them, the corresponding two random vectors are independent. Suppose the maximum degree of G is \Delta. Then the variables can be partitioned into \Delta+1 classes of mutually independent variables, with class sizes differing by at most one.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The dependency graph condition is supplied. Independence means joint probabilities of simultaneous conditions factor, including for finite vectors; learning this finite probabilistic meaning is a small extension of elementary probability and undergraduate sets. No stochastic convergence or general process theory is needed.

Proof 1: Color the dependency graph and use its independent classes — outline

Apply the Hajnal–Szemerédi theorem to obtain an equitable coloring of the dependency graph with \Delta+1 colors. Its color classes partition the variables into independent subsets, and equitability balances their sizes.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the supplied balanced-coloring theorem and the stated dependency condition to each edge-free class. Joint independence is elementary undergraduate probability; no stochastic-process or measure-theoretic construction is involved.
