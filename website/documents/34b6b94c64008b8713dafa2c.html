Likely alternative prerequisite level: High school only; lowest recorded proof level: Undergraduate level. Apply the finite Hales-Jewett color-focusing proof to the alphabet {0,...,m-1}. Encode a word (a_1,...,a_N) as 1+sum a_i m^(i-1) and color it by the given integer coloring. A monochromatic combinatorial line gives an arithmetic progression of length m with positive step sum_(variable positions) m^(i-1). For m=1 any singleton suffices. This explicit finite-combinatorial route avoids rational column spans, with no calculus, topology or infinite recurrence.

Statement

For every positive integer m, every finite colouring of the positive integers contains an arithmetic progression of length m in one colour, with positive common difference.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The conclusion asks for a finite equally spaced list of positive integers of one colour.

Mathematical Field

Review notes

Proof 1: Encode the progression by the displayed matrix — outline

Use Rado's columns theorem: an integer matrix A has a positive integer solution to Ax=0 in one colour under every finite colouring if its columns can be partitioned into nonempty blocks C_1,\ldots,C_t such that the first block sums to zero and each later block sum is a rational linear combination of columns in earlier blocks.

For Van der Waerden's theorem with m chosen to be the length of the monochromatic arithmetic progression, one can for example consider the following matrix:

\begin{pmatrix} 1&1&-1&0&\cdots&0&0\\ 1&2&0&-1&\cdots&0&0\\ \vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\ 1&m-1&0&0&\cdots&-1&0\\ 1&m&0&0&\cdots&0&-1 \end{pmatrix}.

Here row j is (1,j) followed by the negative of the jth standard basis vector, for 1\leq j\leq m, which also specifies the matrix for small m. A positive solution (a,d,y_1,\ldots,y_m) has y_j=a+jd, giving the progression. The source leaves the columns-condition check implicit.
