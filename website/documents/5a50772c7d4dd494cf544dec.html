Statement

Let f be a complex analytic function near a\in\mathbb C with f'(a)\ne0. There is a local analytic inverse g near f(a), with g(f(a))=a and f(g(z))=z, and

g(z)=a+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(z-f(a))^n}{n!}\left.\frac{d^{n-1}}{dw^{n-1}}\left(\frac{w-a}{f(w)-f(a)}\right)^n\right|_{w=a}.

The quotient in the coefficient is evaluated by its removable analytic extension at a. This series has positive radius of convergence.

The same coefficient identity holds formally: for F(t)\in t\mathbb C[[t]] with nonzero coefficient of t, its compositional inverse G(s)\in s\mathbb C[[s]] satisfies

G(s)=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{s^n}{n!}\left.\frac{d^{n-1}}{dt^{n-1}}\left(\frac{t}{F(t)}\right)^n\right|_{t=0}.

Here formal power series are manipulated coefficient by coefficient without a convergence requirement; the derivative is formal and evaluation at zero extracts the constant coefficient. Analytic means locally represented by a convergent power series.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Taylor series and inverse functions are calculus material; introductory complex analyticity and coefficientwise formal series are a modest cumulative reading extension. No contour theory is needed to understand the statement.

Proof 1: Formal Laurent coefficients with a convergence majorant (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Inversion of Formal Power Series, pp.1–2; analytic version on p.2).

Set F(t)=f(a+t)-f(a) and \phi(t)=t/F(t), using the removable extension. The formal proof gives [s^n]G(s)=[t^{n-1}]\phi(t)^n/n, which is the displayed derivative divided by n!. The following convergence supplement also proves analytic inverse existence: choose r>0 with M=\sum_{j\ge0}|[t^j]\phi|r^j<\infty. Then |[s^n]G|\le r(M/r)^n/n, so G converges near zero. The formal identities F(G(s))=s and G(F(t))=t become analytic identities on sufficiently small disks, and g(z)=a+G(z-f(a)).
