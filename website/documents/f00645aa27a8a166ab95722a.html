Statement

Let (X,A) be a CW pair and n\ge1 an integer. Suppose that every cell of X not in A has dimension greater than n. Then (X,A) is n-connected: every path component of X meets A, and for any base vertex a\in A all relative homotopy classes in \pi_i(X,A,a) vanish for 1\le i\le n (with the pointed-set interpretation for i=1).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves CW pairs; Relative homotopy groups and pointed sets; Connectivity. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Connectivity from dimensions of relative cells — outline

Use cellular approximation for pairs: a map of CW pairs is homotopic through maps of pairs to a cellular map.

Under the stated cell-dimension hypothesis, if {\displaystyle i\leq n\,}, then any map {\displaystyle (D^{i},\partial D^{i})\,}→(X,A) is homotopic to a cellular map of pairs, and since the n-skeleton of X sits inside A, any such map is homotopic to a map whose image is in A, and hence it is 0 in the relative homotopy group {\displaystyle \pi _{i}(X,A)\,}.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The argument uses maps of CW pairs, relative homotopy groups and cellular approximation with low-dimensional pointed-set conventions. These linked algebraic-topology concepts exceed the baseline.
