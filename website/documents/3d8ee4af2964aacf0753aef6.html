Statement

Let E be a real separable Banach space and let X_1,X_2,\ldots be independent symmetric Borel-measurable E-valued random variables on one probability space. Write S_n=\sum_{j=1}^nX_j and let \mu_n be its law. The following conditions are equivalent:

  1. There exists an E-valued random variable S on this probability space such that S_n\to S almost surely in norm.
  2. There exists such an S with S_n\to S in probability in norm.
  3. The laws \mu_n converge weakly to a Borel probability measure on E.
  4. The family (\mu_n) is uniformly tight.
  5. There is a Borel probability measure \mu on E such that for every continuous linear functional z:E\to\mathbb R,
\mathbb E e^{i z(S_n)}\longrightarrow\int_E e^{i z(x)}\,\mu(dx).

A separable Banach space is a complete normed real vector space with a countable dense subset. Symmetry means X_j and -X_j have the same law. Weak convergence means convergence of integrals of every bounded continuous real function on E. Uniform tightness means that for every \varepsilon>0 some compact K\subset E satisfies \mu_n(K)\geq1-\varepsilon for all n. Norm convergence in probability means \mathbb P(\|S_n-S\|>\varepsilon)\to0 for every \varepsilon>0; almost surely means outside an event of probability zero. Here i^2=-1. These conditions assert existence of a limit, not convergence to an arbitrary preselected random variable.
