Statement

Let L be a nonempty compact subset of an open set G\subset\mathbb C^n, where n\ge2. Every connected component of \mathbb C^n\setminus L meets G\setminus L in a nonempty open set.

Proof 1: Follow a segment toward the compact set

To see the nonemptiness, connect an arbitrary point p of \mathbb C^n\setminus L to some point of L via a line. The intersection of the line with \mathbb C^n\setminus L may have many connected components, but the component containing p gives a continuous path from p into G\setminus L. The intersection is open, since components of the open Euclidean set \mathbb C^n\setminus L are open.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A segment toward a point of L reaches its first boundary point, with the preceding segment in the same complement component and eventually in G. Components of an open Euclidean set are open. This uses elementary connectedness and Euclidean topology, not complex analysis.
