Statement

Let n\ge1 be an integer and S a finite set of rational primes. Define \mathbb Q(n,S)=\{x\in\mathbb Q:x\text{ is an nth power in }\mathbb Q_p\text{ for every }p\notin S\}. Then \mathbb Q(n,S)=\mathbb Q^n unless 8\mid n and 2\in S. In that exceptional case the kernel of \mathbb Q^\times/\mathbb Q^{\times n}\to\prod_{p\notin S}\mathbb Q_p^\times/\mathbb Q_p^{\times n} has order two.

Proof 1: Exceptional local-power pattern over the rationals

Use the Grunwald–Wang theorem: local nth powers outside S are global unless the field is s-special, 2^{s+1}\mid n, and S contains the special set S_0; in that case the multiplicative kernel has order two. Here \eta_s=2\cos(2\pi/2^s), and s-special means containing \eta_s but none of i,\eta_{s+1},i\eta_{s+1}.

The source gives that \mathbb Q is 2-special: it contains \eta_2=0 but not i,\eta_3=\sqrt2, or i\eta_3=\sqrt{-2}. Its stated special set is S_0=\{2\}. Substituting s=2 and this set into the imported Grunwald–Wang theorem gives exactly the two exceptional conditions 8\mid n and 2\in S, with its stated order-two kernel.
