Statement

For a nonzero irreducible nonnegative square matrix A, put r=\rho(A). For x\geq0, x\ne0, define f(x)=\min_{i:x_i>0}(Ax)_i/x_i. Then \max_{x\geq0,\,x\ne0}f(x)=r.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves finite-dimensional matrices; eigenvalues and spectral radius; irreducibility and positive directed walks. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Source matrix argument

For the proof we denote the supremum of f by the value R. The proof requires us to show R=r and that it is attained. Inserting the Perron–Frobenius eigenvector v into f, we obtain f(v)=r and conclude r\leq R. For the opposite inequality, we consider an arbitrary nonnegative nonzero vector x and let \xi=f(x). The definition of f gives 0\leq\xi x\leq Ax (componentwise). Now, we use the positive left eigenvector w for A for the Perron–Frobenius eigenvalue r, then

\xi w^T x=w^T\xi x\leq w^T(Ax)=(w^TA)x=r w^Tx.

Since w^Tx>0, we have f(x)=\xi\leq r, which implies R\leq r. The value r is attained at v.

Level justification

Use the supplied positive right eigenvector to attain r and the positive left eigenvector to bound every componentwise ratio from above. This is finite matrix linear algebra and inequalities; Perron-Frobenius is applied, not proved.
