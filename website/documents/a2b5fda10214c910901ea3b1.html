Statement

Fix a>0 and write f(z)=e^{iaz}g(z). Suppose f is holomorphic in the upper half-plane except at finitely many isolated points z_1,\ldots,z_n, and extends continuously to the real axis. Assume M_R=\max_{0\le\theta\le\pi}|g(Re^{i\theta})|\to0 as R\to\infty. Then the Cauchy principal value exists and \lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{-R}^R f(x)\,dx=2\pi i\sum_{k=1}^n\operatorname{Res}(f,z_k).

Proof 1: Closing the symmetric segment by an upper semicircle

Jordan's lemma yields a simple way to calculate the integral along the real axis of functions f(z) = ei a z g(z) holomorphic on the upper half-plane and continuous on the closed upper half-plane, except possibly at a finite number of non-real points z1, z2, …, zn. Let C_1 be the upper semicircle from R to -R and C_2 the real segment from -R to R. Consider the positively oriented closed contour C, which is the concatenation of these paths. By definition,

{\displaystyle \oint _{C}f(z)\,dz=\int _{C_{1}}f(z)\,dz+\int _{C_{2}}f(z)\,dz\,.}

Since on C2 the variable z is real, the second integral is over a real interval:

{\displaystyle \int _{C_{2}}f(z)\,dz=\int _{-R}^{R}f(x)\,dx\,.}

The left-hand side may be computed using the residue theorem to get, for all R larger than the maximum of |z1|, |z2|, …, |zn|,

{\displaystyle \oint _{C}f(z)\,dz=2\pi i\sum _{k=1}^{n}\operatorname {Res} (f,z_{k})\,,}

where Res(f, zk) denotes the residue of f at the singularity zk. Hence, if M_R\to0, then taking the limit as R tends to infinity, the contour integral over C1 vanishes by Jordan's lemma and we get the value of the Cauchy principal value

{\displaystyle \lim_{R\to\infty}\int _{-R}^{R}f(x)\,dx=2\pi i\sum _{k=1}^{n}\operatorname {Res} (f,z_{k})\,.}
