Statement

Let \mathbb H=\{x+iy\in\mathbb C:y>0\} have the hyperbolic metric ds^2=(dx^2+dy^2)/y^2. Let \Gamma be a discrete subgroup of \operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbb R), the group of real determinant-one 2\times2 matrices modulo \{I,-I\}, acting by

\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}z=\frac{az+b}{cz+d}.

Suppose the quotient hyperbolic orbifold M=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb H has finite hyperbolic area. Torsion in \Gamma is allowed; thus M need not be a smooth surface everywhere. Let \pi:\mathbb H\to M be the quotient map.

A horocycle in this model is either a horizontal line y=c>0 or a Euclidean circle internally contained in \{y\geq0\} and tangent to the real axis, with its tangency point omitted. For every horocycle C\subset\mathbb H, its image \pi(C) is either a closed curve in M or is dense in M.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Although the upper-half-plane curves are explicit, understanding finite-area orbifolds, discrete group quotients and their orbit geometry requires cumulative preparation beyond the undergraduate allowance.
