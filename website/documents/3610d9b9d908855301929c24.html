Statement

Write \Gamma(t)=\int_0^\infty u^{t-1}e^{-u}\,du for real t>0, \zeta(t)=\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-t} for real t>1, and \gamma=\lim_{N\to\infty}(\sum_{n=1}^N1/n-\log N). Then \int_0^\infty(\gamma x+\log\Gamma(1+x))x^{-5/2}\,dx=\frac{2\pi}{3}\zeta(3/2).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Improper integrals; Gamma integral; Zeta series; Logarithms; Euler–Mascheroni constant. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Specialize the Mellin integral identity

Use the Mellin identity \int_0^\infty x^{s-1}\frac{\gamma x+\log\Gamma(1+x)}{x^2}\,dx=\frac\pi{\sin(\pi s)}\frac{\zeta(2-s)}{2-s} for 0<\operatorname{Re}s<1. Substitute s=1/2 and evaluate the elementary sine and rational factors.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Substitute s=1/2 in the supplied scalar integral formula and simplify. The functions and constants have explicit integral or series definitions; undergraduate improper integration suffices without learning Mellin-transform theory.
