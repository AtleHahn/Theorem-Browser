Statement

Let X be a compact complex surface of general type, e(X) its topological Euler characteristic, and \sigma(X) the signature of the intersection form on H^2(X;\mathbb R). Then \sigma(X)\le e(X)/3. If equality holds, the universal cover of X is the unit ball in \mathbb C^2.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves compact complex surfaces of general type; Euler characteristic; intersection form signature; universal covers. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Signature formula substitution

Let X be a compact complex surface of general type. Use the Bogomolov–Miyaoka–Yau inequality c_1^2(X)\le3c_2(X) and its equality theorem: equality implies that X is a quotient of the unit ball in \mathbb C^2.

Since {\displaystyle c_{2}(X)=e(X)} is the topological Euler characteristic and by the Thom–Hirzebruch signature theorem {\displaystyle c_{1}^{2}(X)=2e(X)+3\sigma (X)} where {\displaystyle \sigma (X)} is the signature of the intersection form on the second cohomology, the Bogomolov–Miyaoka–Yau inequality can also be written as a restriction on the topological type of the surface of general type:

{\displaystyle \sigma (X)\leq {\frac {1}{3}}e(X),}

moreover if {\displaystyle \sigma (X)=(1/3)e(X)} then the universal covering is a ball.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Substitution gives the inequality, but applying the prerequisites requires complex surfaces of general type, Chern numbers, cohomological intersection forms and ball uniformization. These are several graduate theories; the short algebraic deduction does not remove their prerequisite burden.
