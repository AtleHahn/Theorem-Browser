Statement

Let G,H be groups, let f:G\to H be a group homomorphism, and let N be a normal subgroup of G contained in \ker f=\{g\in G:f(g)=e_H\}. Let \varphi:G\to G/N be the quotient homomorphism g\mapsto gN. There is a unique group homomorphism h:G/N\to H such that f=h\circ\varphi. Moreover, h is injective if and only if N=\ker f. In that case the induced map G/\ker f\to\operatorname{im}f is an isomorphism.

Proof 1: The universal property of quotient cosets (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Universal Property theorem and its proof).

For the final clauses, the displayed map satisfies h(gN)=e_H exactly when g\in\ker f. Hence its kernel is trivial precisely when N=\ker f. In that case restrict the codomain to \operatorname{im}f: the induced map is both injective and surjective, and therefore is an isomorphism.
