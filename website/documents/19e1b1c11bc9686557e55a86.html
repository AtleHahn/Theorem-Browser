Statement

For a smooth compactly supported complex function f on \mathbb R, -\int_{\mathbb R}f''(x)\overline{f(x)}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb R}|f'(x)|^2\,dx\geq0. Here compact support means that f vanishes outside a bounded interval.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves complex conjugation; compactly supported smooth functions; First and second derivatives; Definite integrals. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Nonnegativity of the negative second derivative energy — proof

If f is smooth and compactly supported then, using integration by parts, we have

\begin{aligned} -\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f''(x)\overline{f(x)}\,dx &=-\left[f'(x)\overline{f(x)}\right]_{-\infty}^{\infty} +\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f'(x)\overline{f'(x)}\,dx\\ &=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f'(x)|^2\,dx\geq0. \end{aligned}

The boundary term vanishes by compact support.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Integrate by parts over an interval containing the support and use conjugation to identify a squared modulus. Undergraduate calculus and complex arithmetic suffice; these are smooth compactly supported functions, so general Lebesgue or operator theory is unnecessary.
