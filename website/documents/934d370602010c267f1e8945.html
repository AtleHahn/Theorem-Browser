Statement

Let M be a C^2 manifold, let F\subseteq M be closed, and let X be a locally Lipschitz vector field on M. An integral curve of X is a differentiable curve c satisfying c'(t)=X(c(t)). The following conditions are equivalent:

  1. Every integral curve with c(0)\in F remains in F for every nonnegative time in its interval of definition.
  2. For every x\in F and every real C^1 function g defined on a neighborhood of x whose restriction to F has a local maximum at x, one has dg_x(X(x))\leq 0.

Here dg_x is the differential of g at x; the second condition is the exterior-normal condition expressed as a differential paired with the vector field.

Level

Level justification

Understanding the general manifold formulation requires tangent vectors, vector fields, differentials and their pairing, and integral curves in charts. This cumulative preparation exceeds the conservative undergraduate reading allowance even though its Euclidean interpretation is elementary.

Proof 1: Squared-distance comparison in local charts (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Section6.1, Theorem6.1.2 and Lemma6.1.3, pp.72–73).

In the necessity argument the contradictory derivative must be strictly positive. For sufficiency, in a chart let h(t)=d(c(t),F)^2 and let z be a nearest point. The test function w\mapsto |c(t)-z|^2-|c(t)-w|^2 has a maximum on F at z, so \langle c(t)-z,X(z)\rangle\le0. Comparing the next distance with this fixed z yields D^+h(t)\le2C h(t), where C is a local Lipschitz constant. Consequently D^+(e^{-2Ct}h(t))\le0, and h(0)=0 implies h(t)=0. This corrects the missing derivative in the last display. In Lemma6.1.3 a minimizing point satisfies |z|\le\sqrt{f(0)}+2|y|; use q_{2\varepsilon} instead of q_\varepsilon when |y|\le\varepsilon. Localize in nested chart balls so nearest points avoid the artificial boundary, and continue along each compact time segment.
