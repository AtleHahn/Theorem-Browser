Statement

Let \ell be a line in a Euclidean plane.

  1. Let C be a simple rectifiable plane curve of finite positive length s, lying entirely on one side of \ell, with \ell external to C. Let G_C=s^{-1}\int_C x\,ds be its arc-length centroid and R_C its distance from \ell. The surface obtained by revolving C once about \ell has area
S=2\pi R_Cs.
  1. Let F be a bounded measurable plane region of positive area A, lying entirely on one side of the external axis \ell. Let G_F=A^{-1}\int_F x\,dA be its area centroid and R_F its distance from \ell. The solid obtained by revolving F once about \ell has volume
V=2\pi R_FA.

The vector x denotes the position in the plane. In each formula 2\pi R is the distance traveled by the corresponding centroid in one revolution.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

General rectifiable curves and measurable plane regions require measure-theoretic length and surface area beyond ordinary calculus parametrizations; this complete formulation is conservatively beyond undergraduate.

Proof 1: Revolution Jacobians and the area formula (external)

The following editorial argument completes the stated scope. A supporting source is The Pappus Centroid Theorems (UCR Section 5.E for smooth special cases; Simon Chapter 2 Section 3, pp.53–54, area formula, plus explicit editorial parametrization proof given below.). Supporting sources: Leon Simon, Introduction to Geometric Measure Theory, Chapter 2 §3.

Choose coordinates (r,z) in the plane with axis r=0 and the set on r>0. A simple rectifiable curve has an arclength Lipschitz parametrization (r(t),z(t)), 0<=t<=s, with r_prime squared+z_prime squared=1 almost everywhere; omit endpoints if the curve is open. The revolution map Phi(t,theta)=(r(t)cos theta,r(t)sin theta,z(t)) has two-dimensional Jacobian r(t). Simplicity and r>0 make it one-to-one apart from the angular seam and possibly identified curve endpoints, whose images have area zero. The Lipschitz area formula gives S=integral_0^s integral_0^(2pi) r(t) dtheta dt=2pi integral_C r ds=2pi R_C s. For the region apply the same formula to Psi(r,z,theta)=(r cos theta,r sin theta,z) on F times [0,2pi); its three-dimensional Jacobian is r and it is one-to-one away from the seam. Boundedness gives Lipschitzness, and the area formula applies to Lebesgue-measurable sets. Thus V=2pi integral_F r dA=2pi R_F A. See Leon Simon, https://web.stanford.edu/class/math285/ts-gmt.pdf, Chapter 2 Section 3 equations (3.2)–(3.4), printed pp.53–54, for the area formula. The parametrization computations here extend the smooth cases in the UCR notes using Simon’s area formula.
