Statement

Let \ell be a line in a Euclidean plane.

  1. Let C be a simple rectifiable plane curve of finite positive length s, lying entirely on one side of \ell, with \ell external to C. Let G_C=s^{-1}\int_C x\,ds be its arc-length centroid and R_C its distance from \ell. The surface obtained by revolving C once about \ell has area
S=2\pi R_Cs.
  1. Let F be a bounded measurable plane region of positive area A, lying entirely on one side of the external axis \ell. Let G_F=A^{-1}\int_F x\,dA be its area centroid and R_F its distance from \ell. The solid obtained by revolving F once about \ell has volume
V=2\pi R_FA.

The vector x denotes the position in the plane. In each formula 2\pi R is the distance traveled by the corresponding centroid in one revolution.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

General rectifiable curves and measurable plane regions require measure-theoretic length and surface area beyond ordinary calculus parametrizations; this complete formulation is conservatively beyond undergraduate.
