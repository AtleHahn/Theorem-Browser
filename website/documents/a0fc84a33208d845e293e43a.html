Statement

Let A,S be finite subsets of an abelian group, with S\ne\varnothing, and let K>0. If |A+S|\le K|S|, there is T\subseteq A with |T|\le K and A\subseteq T+S-S. Set sums and differences mean all sums and differences of elements from the indicated sets.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Set comprehension and finite subsets of an abelian group are undergraduate foundations. Sum/difference sets are explicitly defined, so no advanced set or additive-combinatorial theory is needed to understand the claim.

Proof 1: Choose a maximal disjoint family of translates

Let {\displaystyle T} be a maximal subset of {\displaystyle A} such that the sets {\displaystyle t+S} for t\in T are all disjoint. Then {\displaystyle |T+S|=|T|\cdot |S|}, and also {\displaystyle |T+S|\leq |A+S|\leq K|S|}, so {\displaystyle |T|\leq K}. Furthermore, for any {\displaystyle a\in A}, there is some {\displaystyle t\in T} such that {\displaystyle t+S} intersects {\displaystyle a+S}, as otherwise adding {\displaystyle a} to {\displaystyle T} contradicts the maximality of {\displaystyle T}. Thus {\displaystyle a\in T+S-S}, so {\displaystyle A\subseteq T+S-S}.
