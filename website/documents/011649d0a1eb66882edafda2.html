Statement

The set of transcendental elements of \mathbb C (those satisfying no nonzero polynomial with integer coefficients) has cardinality \mathfrak c=|\mathbb R|.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Undergraduate set theory supplies bijections and countability; complex numbers are pairs of reals. Transcendence is explicitly defined by failure to satisfy an integer polynomial. Understanding the cardinality comparison needs no transcendence proof techniques.

Proof 1: Remove the countable algebraic part — outline

The source's countable-removal argument for real transcendental numbers applies also to complex transcendental numbers, once |\mathbb C|=\mathfrak c is known. The set of complex algebraic numbers is countably infinite, so its cardinality is \aleph_0. Furthermore, the complex algebraic numbers and the complex transcendental numbers are disjoint sets whose union is \mathbb C. Thus, since the cardinality of \mathbb C is \mathfrak c, the cardinality of the complex transcendental numbers is \mathfrak c: removing a countable subset from this uncountable set leaves its cardinality unchanged.
