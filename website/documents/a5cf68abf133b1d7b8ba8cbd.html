Statement

Let X,Y be topological spaces and R a principal ideal domain. Write H_i(-;R) for singular homology with coefficients in R, with zero groups in negative degrees. For every integer k there is a natural short exact sequence of R-modules

0\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{i+j=k}H_i(X;R)\otimes_R H_j(Y;R) \xrightarrow{\ \times\ } H_k(X\times Y;R) \longrightarrow\bigoplus_{i+j=k-1}\operatorname{Tor}_1^R(H_i(X;R),H_j(Y;R)) \longrightarrow0.

The first map is the homology cross product. Every such sequence splits, but not canonically: the surjection onto the Tor sum admits an R-linear right inverse, without a specified natural choice. Consequently the middle module is isomorphic to the direct sum of the two outer modules.

Here \otimes_R is the module tensor product, and \operatorname{Tor}_1^R(M,N) is the first homology of P_*\otimes_R N for a projective resolution P_*\to M. Naturality means compatibility with continuous maps in X and Y. In particular, if R is a field, the cross product is a natural isomorphism

\bigoplus_{i+j=k}H_i(X;R)\otimes_R H_j(Y;R)\cong H_k(X\times Y;R).
