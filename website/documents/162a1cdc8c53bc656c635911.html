Statement

At every y>0 at which \log is differentiable, (\log)^{\prime}(y)=1/y.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves real derivatives; positive-domain logarithm. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. Proof 1: Insert the exponential derivative in the inverse identity

Consider the function g(x)=e^x. It has an inverse f(y)=\ln y. Because g'(x)=e^x, the differentiable-inverse formula

f'(y)=\frac1{g'(f(y))}

says that

\frac{d}{dy}\ln y=\frac1{e^{\ln y}}=\frac1y.

This formula is used where g and its inverse f are differentiable, as assumed in the statement.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Substitute the derivative of exp into the differentiable-inverse identity. The statement assumes differentiability, so no inverse function existence theory is needed.
