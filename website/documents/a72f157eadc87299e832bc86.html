Statement

For every nonzero complex algebraic number \alpha, the number e^\alpha is transcendental, meaning it is not a root of a nonzero polynomial with rational coefficients.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Undergraduate algebra and analysis supply the objects. Hermite polynomials have a single defining recurrence, Jordan products and graph-end conditions are explicitly specified, and well-quasi-order is defined by a sequence comparison. These concrete additions do not require their advanced proof theories.

Proof 1: Apply algebraic independence to a singleton

Use algebraic independence of exponentials: If \alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n are complex algebraic numbers linearly independent over \mathbb Q, then e^{\alpha_1},\ldots,e^{\alpha_n} are algebraically independent over \mathbb Q: no nonzero polynomial in \mathbb Q[X_1,\ldots,X_n] vanishes at that tuple.

Suppose α is a non-zero algebraic number; then {α} is a linearly independent set over the rationals, and therefore by the algebraic-independence formulation of Lindemann–Weierstrass stated above {eα} is an algebraically independent set; or in other words eα is transcendental.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the fully stated algebraic-independence theorem to a singleton. Undergraduate complex exponentials and linear algebra suffice; algebraic independence is explicitly a polynomial nonvanishing condition and reduces here to transcendence.

Proof 2: Apply linear independence to zero and alpha

Use the linear formulation: If \alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n are distinct complex algebraic numbers and a_1,\ldots,a_n are algebraic numbers not all zero, then \sum_{i=1}^n a_i e^{\alpha_i}\ne0.

By this linear-independence formulation of Lindemann–Weierstrass, if α is a non-zero algebraic number, then {0, α} is a set of distinct algebraic numbers, and so the set {e0, eα} = {1, eα} is linearly independent over the algebraic numbers and in particular eα cannot be algebraic and so it is transcendental.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the supplied linear formulation to zero and alpha. If exp(alpha) were algebraic its relation with 1 would contradict the theorem. Undergraduate complex arithmetic and linear independence suffice.
