Statement

Let A,B be linear operators on a finite-dimensional complex vector space of dimension n. Suppose \alpha A+\beta B is diagonalizable for every \alpha,\beta\in\mathbb C. Then the eigenvalues of A and B can be listed, with algebraic multiplicity, as \lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n and \mu_1,\ldots,\mu_n so that for every \alpha,\beta, the eigenvalues of \alpha A+\beta B are

\alpha\lambda_1+\beta\mu_1,\ldots,\alpha\lambda_n+\beta\mu_n.

The two lists are fixed independently of \alpha,\beta. Diagonalizable means admitting a basis of eigenvectors, and algebraic multiplicity is multiplicity as a root of the characteristic polynomial.
