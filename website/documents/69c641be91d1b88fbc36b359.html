Statement

Let I be [a,b], [a,b) or [a,\infty), with a<b in the finite-endpoint cases. Let \alpha\ge0 and u be measurable real functions and let measurable \beta satisfy 0\le\beta\le c for a constant c\ge0. Assume \int_a^t|u(s)|\beta(s)\,ds<\infty and u(t)\le\alpha(t)+\int_a^t\beta(s)u(s)\,ds for every t. Then u(t)\le\alpha(t)+c\int_a^t\alpha(s)e^{c(t-s)}\,ds. When c=0 the added term is defined as zero.

Proof 1: Bound the density and exponential kernel

Use the density form u(t)\le\alpha(t)+\int_a^t\alpha(s)\beta(s)e^{\int_s^t\beta(r)\,dr}\,ds. Since \alpha\ge0, \beta\le c and \int_s^t\beta\le c(t-s), its integral is bounded by the asserted one. If c=0, \beta=0 and the original inequality already gives u\le\alpha.

Level justification

Bound the density and exponential kernel in the measurable Gronwall estimate. Retain beyond undergraduate provisionally because the exact claim concerns arbitrary measurable functions and weighted Lebesgue integrals, rather than only continuous calculus.
