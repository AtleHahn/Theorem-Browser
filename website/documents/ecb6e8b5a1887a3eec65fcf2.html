Statement

Let M,N be bounded normal operators on a complex Hilbert space. If a bounded invertible S satisfies MS=SN, then there is a unitary V with N=VMV^*.

Proof 1: Use the polar decomposition of the similarity operator

Suppose MS = SN where S is a bounded invertible operator. The Fuglede–Putnam theorem implies M*S = SN*, i.e.

{\displaystyle S^{-1}M^{*}S=N^{*}.}

Take the adjoint of the above equation and we have

{\displaystyle S^{*}M(S^{-1})^{*}=N.}

So

{\displaystyle S^{*}M(S^{-1})^{*}=S^{-1}MS\quad \Rightarrow \quad SS^{*}M(SS^{*})^{-1}=M.}

By polar decomposition, let S*=VR, with V a unitary (since S is invertible) and R the positive square root of SS*. As R is a limit of polynomials on SS*, the above implies that R commutes with M. It is also invertible. Then

{\displaystyle N=S^{*}M(S^{*})^{-1}=VRMR^{-1}V^{*}=VMV^{*}.}
