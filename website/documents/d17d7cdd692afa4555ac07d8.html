Statement

For n≥1 and variables x_1,\ldots,x_n, let p_k=\sum_jx_j^k for k≥1, let e_0=1, and let e_k=\sum_{j_1<\cdots<j_k}x_{j_1}\cdots x_{j_k}, with e_k=0 for k>n. For m≥1, p_m=(-1)^m m\sum_{\sum_{i=1}^m i r_i=m}\frac{(R-1)!}{\prod_i r_i!}\prod_i(-e_i)^{r_i},\qquad R=\sum_i r_i. The r_i are nonnegative integers.

Proof 1: Induct on monomial coefficients in Newton’s recurrence outline

Use Newton's identities,

ke_k=\sum_{i=1}^k(-1)^{i-1}e_{k-i}p_i,

solved for p_k. Write R=r_1+\cdots+r_m, with \sum_{i=1}^m i r_i=m, and let f(m;r_1,\ldots,r_m) denote the magnitude of the coefficient of the corresponding monomial. The case R=1 is the direct term (-1)^{m-1}me_m.

The multiple summation formula can be proved by considering the following inductive step, for R\ge2:

\begin{aligned} f(m;r_1,\ldots,r_m) &=\sum_{\substack{1\le i\le m\\r_i>0}} f(m-i;r_1,\ldots,r_i-1,\ldots,r_m)\\[8pt] &=\sum_{\substack{1\le i\le m\\r_i>0}} \frac{(m-i)(r_1+\cdots+r_m-2)!} {r_1!\cdots(r_i-1)!\cdots r_m!}\\[8pt] &=\frac{1}{r_1!\cdots r_m!} \left[r_1(m-1)+\cdots+r_m(m-m)\right] \left[r_1+\cdots+r_m-2\right]!\\[8pt] &=\frac{1}{r_1!\cdots r_m!} \left[m(r_1+\cdots+r_m)-m\right] \left[r_1+\cdots+r_m-2\right]!\\[8pt] &=\frac{m(r_1+\cdots+r_m-1)!}{r_1!\cdots r_m!}. \end{aligned}

Only indices with r_i>0 enter the decrement sums, so no negative factorial is used. The Newton signs give (-1)^{m+R} for the monomial, as in the formula. This proves the coefficient induction.
