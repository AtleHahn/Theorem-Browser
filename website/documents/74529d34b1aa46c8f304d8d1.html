Statement

Fix C>0. For an integer N\ge1, write [N]=\{1,\ldots,N\}. Suppose for each progression-free set A\subseteq[N] of density \alpha>0 with N>C\alpha^{-12} one can pass to a subprogression of length at least N^{1/3} on which its density is at least \alpha+\alpha^2/40, and the same assertion can be reapplied after rescaling that progression. Then every such initial set satisfies \alpha=O(1/\log\log N) as N\to\infty, with an implied constant depending on C. Here progression-free means no three distinct terms in arithmetic progression.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Density increments; Logarithmic asymptotic bounds; Finite arithmetic progressions. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Double the density and track cubed lengths — outline

We now iterate the density-increment assumption. Let {\displaystyle \alpha_{t}} be the density of {\displaystyle A} after the {\displaystyle t}th iteration. We have that {\displaystyle \alpha _{0}=\alpha ,} and {\displaystyle \alpha _{t+1}\geq \alpha_t +\alpha_t^{2}/40.} First, see that {\displaystyle \alpha } doubles (i.e. reach {\displaystyle T} such that {\displaystyle \alpha _{T}\geq 2\alpha _{0}}) after at most {\displaystyle 40/\alpha +1} steps. We double {\displaystyle \alpha } again (i.e reach {\displaystyle \alpha _{T}\geq 4\alpha _{0}}) after at most {\displaystyle 20/\alpha +1} steps. Since {\displaystyle \alpha \leq 1}, this process must terminate after at most {\displaystyle O(1/\alpha )} steps.

Let {\displaystyle N_{t}} be the size of our current progression after {\displaystyle t} iterations. By the density-increment assumption, we can always continue the process whenever {\displaystyle N_{t}>C\alpha _{t}^{-12},} and thus when the process terminates we have that {\displaystyle N_{t}\leq C\alpha _{t}^{-12}\leq C\alpha ^{-12}.} Also, note that when we pass to a subprogression, the progression length is at least the cube root of its previous length. Therefore

{\displaystyle N\leq N_{t}^{3^{t}}\leq (C\alpha ^{-12})^{3^{O(1/\alpha )}}=e^{e^{O(1/\alpha )}}.}

Therefore {\displaystyle \alpha =O(1/\log \log N),} so {\displaystyle |A|=O\left({\frac {N}{\log \log N}}\right),} as desired. {\displaystyle \blacksquare }
