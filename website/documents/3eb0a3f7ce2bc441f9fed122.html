Alexander’s closed braid theorem

Statement

Every tame link in \mathbb R^3 is ambient isotopic to the closure of a braid on finitely many strands. A link is a finite union of disjoint embedded circles; tame means ambient isotopic to a polygonal link. Ambient isotopy means a continuous deformation of the ambient space by homeomorphisms starting at the identity. A braid consists of disjoint strands running monotonically between two parallel planes, with the same finite set of endpoint positions in each plane; its closure joins each endpoint position to the corresponding position in the other plane outside the braid. Knots are the one-component case.

Level justification

Basic topology supplies embeddings and homeomorphisms. Polygonal links, ambient deformation, monotone strands and closure have concrete definitions and simple examples; these are a small extension within the reading allowance, without knot invariants or braid-group theory.
