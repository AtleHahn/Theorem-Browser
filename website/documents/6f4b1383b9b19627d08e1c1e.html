Statement

Let P(w)=\alpha\prod_{i=1}^n(w-a_i), where n\ge1, \alpha\in\mathbb C\setminus\{0\} and a_i\in\mathbb C. If P(z)\ne0 and P'(z)=0, then z=\sum_i\lambda_i a_i, where \lambda_i=|z-a_i|^{-2}/\sum_j|z-a_j|^{-2}. In particular all \lambda_i>0 and \sum_i\lambda_i=1.

Proof 1: Normalize the inverse-square distances

For the given root z of P' with P(z)\ne0, we have for the logarithmic derivative

0=\frac{P'(z)}{P(z)} =\sum_{i=1}^n\frac1{z-a_i} =\sum_{i=1}^n\frac{\overline z-\overline{a_i}}{|z-a_i|^2}.

Hence

\sum_{i=1}^n\frac{\overline z}{|z-a_i|^2} =\sum_{i=1}^n\frac{\overline{a_i}}{|z-a_i|^2}.

Taking their conjugates, and dividing, we obtain z as a convex sum of the roots of P:

z=\sum_{i=1}^n \frac{|z-a_i|^{-2}}{\sum_{j=1}^n|z-a_j|^{-2}}a_i.

The denominator is positive, and the displayed coefficients \lambda_i are positive and sum to 1.
