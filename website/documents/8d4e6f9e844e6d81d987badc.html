Statement

Every holomorphic function f:M\to\mathbb C on a compact connected Riemann surface M is constant.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The hypotheses require Riemann surfaces and holomorphic functions in their charts, beyond real multivariable calculus.

Proof 1: Use a maximum point in a coordinate chart

Let f be holomorphic on a compact connected Riemann surface M. By compactness, there is a point p_0\in M where |f(p)| attains its maximum. Then we can find a chart \varphi from a neighborhood of p_0 to the unit disk \mathbb D such that f(\varphi^{-1}(z)) is holomorphic on the unit disk and its modulus has a maximum at \varphi(p_0)\in\mathbb D, so it is constant, by the maximum modulus principle. The identity theorem on the connected surface then gives the same constant on all of M.

Level justification

Find a maximum using compactness, apply the complex maximum principle in a chart, and extend the constant by the identity theorem. Riemann surface charts and holomorphic function theory exceed the baseline.
