Statement

Let n\ge2 and let S be a convex body in \mathbb R^n. Suppose every orthogonal projection of S onto an (n-1)-dimensional linear subspace has the same (n-1)-dimensional volume as the unit ball in \mathbb R^{n-1}. Then the surface area of S equals that of the unit sphere in \mathbb R^n. In dimension three this is the unit projected-area case of bodies of constant brightness.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Multivariable calculus and linear algebra supply convex bodies, orthogonal projections and volume. Surface area is the familiar geometric quantity, explicitly specified by a volume derivative in the latter formulation; this is a small extension without Riemannian geometry.

Proof 1: Equal projections in the general Crofton formula (outline)

If S is a convex body in \mathbb R^n, for which every (n-1)-dimensional projection has the volume of the unit ball in \mathbb R^{n-1}, then the surface area of S is equal to that of the unit sphere in \mathbb R^n. This follows from the general form of Crofton formula: surface area of a convex body is determined by its hyperplane projection-volume function.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply a general projection-volume formula to arbitrary convex bodies in all dimensions. Surface measure and integral geometry for nonsmooth bodies exceed ordinary solid geometry or multivariable calculus; retain beyond undergraduate provisionally.
