Statement

Let (A_i)_{i\in I} be a finite indexed family of arbitrary sets, possibly infinite. If f:I\to\bigcup_{i\in I}A_i is injective and f(i)\in A_i, then every J\subseteq I has at least |J| distinct elements in \bigcup_{i\in J}A_i.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The index family is finite even when its member sets are infinite. The conclusion merely requires a stated finite number of distinct elements in a union, so elementary membership and counting suffice.

Proof 1: Distinct representatives force the marriage condition

If a transversal exists then the marriage condition must be true: the injective function f used to define the transversal maps J to a subset of \bigcup_{i\in J}A_i, of size equal to |J|, so the whole union must be at least as large.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Restrict the given injective selection to the finite index subset J. Its |J| distinct values all lie in the union, even when the individual sets are infinite. Elementary finite counting and subset membership suffice.
