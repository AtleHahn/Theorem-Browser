Statement

Let

a_0+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigl(a_n\cos(nx)+b_n\sin(nx)\bigr)

be a trigonometric series with real coefficients. Suppose there is a Lebesgue-measurable set E\subseteq\mathbb R of positive measure such that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_n\cos(nx)+b_n\sin(nx)|<\infty\qquad\text{for every }x\in E.

Then

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(|a_n|+|b_n|)<\infty.

In particular, the trigonometric series converges absolutely at every real x.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Series and trigonometry are undergraduate analysis. Understanding only the positive-measure-set condition is a modest measure-theory addition; no integration theorem or Fourier-transform machinery is needed for this statement.
