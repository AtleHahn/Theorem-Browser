Statement

For every Riemannian metric on \mathbb {CP}^2,

\operatorname{stsys}_2(\mathbb {CP}^2)^2\le2\operatorname{vol}_4(\mathbb {CP}^2).

Here the stable two-systole is the infimum of areas of rational two-cycles representing the homology class of a projective line.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Riemannian geometry; Stable systoles; Homology of complex projective space. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Set the complex dimension to two

Gromov's stable 2-systolic inequality is

\operatorname{stsys}_2(\mathbb{CP}^n)^n \leq n!\operatorname{vol}_{2n}(\mathbb{CP}^n),

valid for an arbitrary Riemannian metric on complex projective space. In the special case n=2, Gromov's inequality becomes

\operatorname{stsys}_2(\mathbb{CP}^2)^2 \leq2\operatorname{vol}_4(\mathbb{CP}^2).
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Setting n=2 is immediate, but stable systoles are defined through rational homology cycles and Riemannian volumes on complex projective manifolds. Those geometric and topological objects require advanced background.
