Statement

Let C\subset\mathbb{CP}^2 be a smooth algebraic curve of degree d\geq1. If \Sigma\subset\mathbb{CP}^2 is a smoothly embedded closed connected oriented surface representing [C]\in H_2(\mathbb{CP}^2;\mathbb Z), then

g(\Sigma)\geq\frac{(d-1)(d-2)}2.

Thus C minimizes genus in its homology class.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Smoothly embedded surfaces in complex projective space and their integral homology classes require substantial four-dimensional topology. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Specialization of the symplectic Thom theorem (outline)

Use the external symplectic Thom theorem: an embedded symplectic surface in a symplectic four-manifold minimizes genus within its homology class. As the source points out, \mathbb{CP}^2 is a symplectic four-manifold and its smooth complex algebraic curves are symplectic surfaces. Apply that theorem to C. The external degree–genus formula gives g(C)=(d-1)(d-2)/2, yielding the stated lower bound for \Sigma.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply symplectic genus minimization to a projective algebraic curve and then the degree-genus formula. Understanding the hypotheses requires symplectic four-manifolds, embedded surfaces and homology together with projective curves.
