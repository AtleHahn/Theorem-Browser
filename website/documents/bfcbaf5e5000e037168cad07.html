Statement

Let R be a commutative unital ring containing \mathbb Q, A\in M_3(R), and s_j=\operatorname{tr}(A^j). Then A^3-s_1A^2+(s_1^2-s_2)A/2-\det(A)I_3=0.

Proof 1: The 3-by-3 Cayley–Hamilton trace identity

Put n=3 and write \det(tI_n-A)=\sum_{i=0}^n c_it^i, with c_n=1.

Use the characteristic-coefficient identity c_{n-k}=(-1)^k B_k(s_1,-1!s_2,2!s_3,\ldots,(-1)^{k-1}(k-1)!s_k)/k!, where B_k is the complete exponential Bell polynomial and B_0=1.

For the first terms, B_1(x_1)=x_1 and B_2(x_1,x_2)=x_1^2+x_2.

It is apparent from the general formula for cn−k, expressed in terms of Bell polynomials, that the expressions

{\displaystyle -\operatorname {tr} (A)\quad {\text{and}}\quad {\tfrac {1}{2}}(\operatorname {tr} (A)^{2}-\operatorname {tr} (A^{2}))}

always give the coefficients cn−1 of λn−1 and cn−2 of λn−2 in the characteristic polynomial of any {\displaystyle n\times n} matrix, respectively. So, for a {\displaystyle 3\times 3} matrix A, the statement of the Cayley–Hamilton theorem can also be written as

{\displaystyle A^{3}-(\operatorname {tr} A)A^{2}+{\frac {1}{2}}\left((\operatorname {tr} A)^{2}-\operatorname {tr} (A^{2})\right)A-\det(A)I_{3}=O,}

where the right-hand side designates a {\displaystyle 3\times 3} matrix with all entries reduced to zero.
