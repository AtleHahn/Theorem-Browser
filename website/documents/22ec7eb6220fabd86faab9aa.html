Statement

Let \kappa be a regular uncountable cardinal, let S\subseteq\kappa be stationary, and let f:S\to\kappa satisfy f(\alpha)<\alpha for every nonzero \alpha\in S. Then there are \gamma<\kappa and a stationary S_0\subseteq S such that f(\alpha)=\gamma for every \alpha\in S_0. A subset of \kappa is stationary if it meets every closed unbounded (club) subset.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Regular uncountable cardinals, stationary sets and closed unbounded sets require substantial ordinal/set-theoretic preparation. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Diagonal club intersection contradicts regressivity

We can assume that 0\notin S (by removing 0, if necessary). If Fodor's lemma is false, for every \alpha<\kappa there is some club set C_\alpha such that C_\alpha\cap f^{-1}(\{\alpha\})=\varnothing. Let

C=\mathop{\Delta}_{\alpha<\kappa}C_\alpha =\{\xi<\kappa:\xi\in C_\alpha\text{ for every }\alpha<\xi\}.

The club sets are closed under diagonal intersection, so C is also club and therefore there is some \alpha\in S\cap C. Then \alpha\in C_\beta for each \beta<\alpha, and so there can be no \beta<\alpha such that \alpha\in f^{-1}(\{\beta\}), so f(\alpha)\geq\alpha, a contradiction.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Construct a diagonal club intersection and contradict regressivity at a stationary point. Regular uncountable cardinals, ordinal-indexed clubs and stationarity require advanced set theory.
