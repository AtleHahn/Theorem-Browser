Points of a locale can be characterized algebraically as suitable truth-valued morphisms or prime filters. This supports point-free foundations of topology, where regions and their logical relations are primary and points are recovered from that structure rather than assumed at the outset.

Statement

For a frame L, frame morphisms p:L\to2=\{0<1\} correspond to proper principal prime ideals, to meet-prime elements a<1, and to completely prime filters. Here meet-prime means x\wedge y\le a implies x\le a or y\le a; a completely prime filter contains a join only if it contains one of its summands.

Level justification

The claim concerns arbitrary complete lattices, their prime ideals and completely prime filters.

Proof 1: Recover a point from its zero and one fibres — outline

Consider a point of a locale L as a frame morphism p from L to 2. These morphisms are characterized equivalently by the inverse images of the two elements of 2. From the properties of frame morphisms, one can derive that p^{-1}(0) is a lower set (since p is monotone), which contains a greatest element a_p=\bigvee p^{-1}(0) (since p preserves arbitrary suprema). In addition, the principal ideal p^{-1}(0) is a prime ideal since p preserves finite infima and thus a_p is a meet-prime element. Now the complement of p^{-1}(0), given by p^{-1}(1), is a completely prime filter because p^{-1}(0) is a principal prime ideal. It turns out that all of these descriptions uniquely determine the initial frame morphism.

Level justification

Identify the zero fiber as a principal prime ideal via arbitrary joins and recover the completely prime complementary filter. Complete lattices, frame morphisms and several infinite-primality notions are a substantial cumulative order-theory extension.
