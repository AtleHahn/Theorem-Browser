Statement

The function f:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R defined by

f(x,y)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{2x^2y+y^3}{x^2+y^2},&(x,y)\ne(0,0),\\0,&(x,y)=(0,0),\end{cases}

is continuous everywhere and has both partial derivatives everywhere, but is not differentiable at the origin. Differentiability here means the existence of a linear map L:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R such that f(h)-f(0)-L(h)=o(\|h\|) as h\to0.

Review notes

  • Directly |f(x,y)|<=2|y|, the origin partials are 0 and 1, and f(t,t)-t=t/2. The absolute remainder divided by sqrt(2)|t| is 1/(2sqrt(2)), so no total derivative exists. Away from zero the denominator is positive and ordinary quotient differentiation applies.
