Statement

Let a be a normal element of a unital C*-algebra A with unit 1, and let \Phi_a:C(\sigma(a))\to A be its continuous functional calculus, writing f(a)=\Phi_a(f). For f\in C(\sigma(a)), f(a) is invertible if and only if 0\notin f(\sigma(a)). In that case f(a)^{-1}=(1/f)(a).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves C*-algebras; Normal elements and spectra; Continuous functional calculus; Invertibility and reciprocal functions. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Zero in the mapped spectrum — outline

Invertibility means that zero is absent from the spectrum. Spectral mapping gives the asserted equivalence; the homomorphism rules give the inverse formula.
