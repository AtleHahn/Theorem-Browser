Statement

In the Euclidean plane, suppose a single circle of positive radius has been drawn and its center is given. Every point constructible from given geometric data by finitely many compass-and-straightedge operations can be constructed from the same data, this fixed circle and its center using an unmarked straightedge alone. Lines may be drawn through constructed points, their intersections found, and intersections with the given circle used. A circle needed as intermediate data is specified by its center and a point on it; the assertion concerns constructing points and lines, not drawing new circular arcs.

Proof availability

The source describes a constructive proof, but its exceptional line-circle construction defines P-prime and Q-prime both as midpoints of PT, making the auxiliary circle degenerate. Other preliminary instructions also contain inconsistent point labels (EH where A,C were constructed, and M(C) where M(D) is intended). The general simulation reduction is usable and remains in the existing straightedge-basic-intersection-reduction entry; a full proof of all required constructions cannot be retained without repairing the source instructions.
