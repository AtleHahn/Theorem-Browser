Statement

Let eight distinct points of \mathbb P^2(K), for an algebraically closed field K, have no four collinear and no seven on a nondegenerate conic. The K-vector space of homogeneous cubic polynomials vanishing at all eight points has dimension two.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Projective points are homogeneous triples modulo scale and conics/cubics are homogeneous polynomial zero sets. Degenerate conics over the algebraically closed field have line components. These concrete coordinate and factorization notions extend undergraduate algebra; the conclusion is the dimension of a polynomial solution space.

Proof 1: Exclude a degenerate seven-point conic, then apply the dimension theorem

If seven of the points lay on a degenerate conic, its at most two component lines would contain four of the points on one line. This contradicts the hypothesis. Thus no seven lie on any conic. Apply the stated eight-point cubic dimension theorem: absence of seven co-conic points gives a two-dimensional vanishing space.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A degenerate conic has at most two line components, so seven points on it force four on one line. Apply the supplied cubic-form dimension conclusion. Homogeneous polynomial coordinates and elementary linear algebra suffice to understand the application.
