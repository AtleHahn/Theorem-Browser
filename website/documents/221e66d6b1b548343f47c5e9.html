Statement

Let H be a real or complex RKHS on X, with f(x)=\langle f,K_x\rangle. If S\subset X and \sup_{x\in S}\|K_x\|\le B<\infty, then for every f,g\in H, \sup_{x\in S}|f(x)-g(x)|\le B\|f-g\|. Thus norm convergence in H implies uniform convergence on S. In the complex case the inner product is linear in its first argument.

Level justification

RKHS means a complete inner-product space of functions with continuous evaluations. With the evaluation sections supplied, these linked definitions extend undergraduate metric and inner-product analysis; uniform convergence is baseline.

Proof 1: Bound point differences by Cauchy–Schwarz — outline

The reproducing identity expresses f(x)-g(x) as \langle f-g,K_x\rangle. Cauchy–Schwarz bounds its absolute value by \|f-g\|\|K_x\|. The bounded-section hypothesis gives the uniform bound and the convergence implication.
