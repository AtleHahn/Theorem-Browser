Statement

A closed disk of positive radius in \mathbb R^2 cannot be partitioned into finitely many arbitrary point sets and reassembled by planar isometries into two disjoint congruent copies of itself.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Plane isometries are distance-preserving maps, and the partition may use arbitrary subsets. Undergraduate set language suffices to understand the reassembly impossibility, without measure or group-amenability theory used in its proof.

Proof 1: Invariant finitely additive planar measure (outline)

The group of Euclidean motions of the plane is solvable. Use the source's consequence that there exists a nonnegative finitely additive measure on all planar sets, invariant under all planar isometries, including translations, rotations and reflections, which assigns a disk its usual positive finite area. Such a measure rules out a paradoxical decomposition of the disk: finite additivity and invariance preserve its measure under a finite equidecomposition, whereas two disjoint congruent copies have twice its positive measure.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the supplied finitely additive isometry-invariant area on all planar sets and compare one disk with two copies. Undergraduate elementary group theory and set functions suffice; no countably additive measure construction is used.
