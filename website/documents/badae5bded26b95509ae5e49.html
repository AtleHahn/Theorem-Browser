Statement

On a measure space (S,\Sigma,\mu), write \|h\|_t=(\int_S|h|^t\,d\mu)^{1/t} for 0<t<\infty, and \|h\|_\infty=\operatorname{ess\,sup}|h|. Norms may be infinite; products with a zero factor are defined to be zero, including 0\cdot\infty. For measurable real or complex f,g with \|f\|_2,\|g\|_2<\infty, |\int_S f\overline g\,d\mu|\le\|f\|_2\|g\|_2.

Proof 1: Cauchy–Schwarz for square-integrable functions

Let f and g denote measurable real- or complex-valued functions defined on S. If ‖fg‖1 is finite, then the pointwise products of f with g and its complex conjugate function are μ-integrable, the estimate

{\displaystyle {\biggl |}\int _{S}f{\bar {g}}\,\mathrm {d} \mu {\biggr |}\leq \int _{S}|fg|\,\mathrm {d} \mu =\|fg\|_{1}}

and the similar one for fg hold, and Hölder's inequality can be applied to the right-hand side. In particular, if f and g are in the Hilbert space L2(μ), then Hölder's inequality for p = q = 2 implies

{\displaystyle |\langle f,g\rangle |\leq \|f\|_{2}\|g\|_{2},}

where \langle f,g\rangle=\int_S f\overline g\,d\mu is the inner product of L2(μ). This is also called Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, but requires for its statement that ‖f ‖2 and ‖g‖2 are finite to make sure that the inner product of f and g is well defined.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

This formulation concerns arbitrary measure spaces, measurable complex functions and Lebesgue L2 norms. Applying Holder both establishes product integrability and bounds the inner product; that measure-theoretic framework is beyond the ordinary real-analysis baseline.
