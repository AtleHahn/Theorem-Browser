Statement

Let g be C^n on an open real interval, with n\ge0. Then D^ne^{g(x)}=e^{g(x)}B_n(g'(x),\ldots,g^{(n)}(x)). For 1\le k\le n, define B_{n,k}(z_1,\ldots,z_{n-k+1})=\sum \frac{n!}{m_1!\cdots m_n!}\prod_{j=1}^{n-k+1}(z_j/j!)^{m_j}, summed over nonnegative integers with \sum m_j=k and \sum j m_j=n. Put B_{0,0}=1 and B_{n,0}=0 for n>0; B_n=\sum_{k=0}^nB_{n,k}.

Proof 1: Factor the common exponential derivative

In the partial-Bell Faà di Bruno identity D^n(f\circ g)=\sum_kf^{(k)}(g)B_{n,k}(g',\ldots), take f(u)=e^u. All outer derivatives equal e^u, so factor out e^{g(x)}. The remaining sum of the partial Bell polynomials is the complete polynomial B_n.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the supplied higher chain rule to the exponential and factor its common derivative from a finite sum. The Bell polynomials are explicitly defined finite expressions, so ordinary repeated differentiation suffices.
