Statement

Let A be a commutative Noetherian ring and B\subset C commutative A-algebras. If C is finitely generated as an A-algebra and as a B-module, then B is finitely generated as an A-algebra.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement uses abstract algebras and modules over a general commutative ring.

Proof 1: A finite coefficient algebra

Let {\displaystyle x_{1},\ldots ,x_{m}} generate {\displaystyle C} as an {\displaystyle A}-algebra and let {\displaystyle y_{1},\ldots ,y_{n}} generate {\displaystyle C} as a {\displaystyle B}-module. We may include 1 among the module generators. Then we can write

{\displaystyle x_{i}=\sum _{j}b_{ij}y_{j}\quad {\text{and}}\quad y_{i}y_{j}=\sum _{k}b_{ijk}y_{k}}

with {\displaystyle b_{ij},b_{ijk}\in B}. Then {\displaystyle C} is finite over the {\displaystyle A}-algebra {\displaystyle B_{0}} generated by the {\displaystyle b_{ij},b_{ijk}}. Using that {\displaystyle A} and hence {\displaystyle B_{0}} is Noetherian, also {\displaystyle B} is finite over {\displaystyle B_{0}}. Since {\displaystyle B_{0}} is a finitely generated {\displaystyle A}-algebra, also {\displaystyle B} is a finitely generated {\displaystyle A}-algebra.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Retain Beyond undergraduate provisionally. The coefficient calculation is explicit, but following the general proof requires distinguishing algebra generation from module generation over several nested scalar rings and applying Noetherian module theory after a change of scalars. Without assuming a module-theory elective, that cumulative preparation cannot confidently fit the small-extension allowance. The elementary square-zero example can be assessed separately using its coordinates.
