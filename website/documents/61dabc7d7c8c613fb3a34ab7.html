Statement

For an integer p\ge1 and nonnegative integers n_1,\ldots,n_p,m, \binom{n_1+\cdots+n_p}{m}=\sum_{k_1+\cdots+k_p=m,\ k_i\ge0}\prod_{i=1}^p\binom{n_i}{k_i}, with binomial coefficients zero outside their usual range.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The identity is a finite sum of binomial coefficients of nonnegative integers.

Proof 1: Counting subsets across several groups

Take the p sets to be pairwise disjoint.

On the one hand, one chooses {\displaystyle \textstyle k_{1}} elements out of a first set of {\displaystyle \textstyle n_{1}} elements; then {\displaystyle \textstyle k_{2}} out of another set, and so on, through {\displaystyle \textstyle p} such sets, until a total of {\displaystyle \textstyle m} elements have been chosen from the {\displaystyle \textstyle p} sets. One therefore chooses {\displaystyle \textstyle m} elements out of {\displaystyle \textstyle n_{1}+\dots +n_{p}} in the left-hand side, which is also exactly what is done in the right-hand side.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Count m-element subsets of a disjoint union by their intersection sizes with each group. Elementary finite counting and binomial coefficients suffice.

Proof 2: Polynomial product coefficient comparison

Expand \prod_{i=1}^p(1+x)^{n_i}=(1+x)^{\sum_i n_i}. Comparing the coefficient of x^m gives exactly the stated sum. If m>\sum_i n_i, both coefficients and every summand are zero.
