Proof 1: At most two real intersections of distinct circles (outline)

Use Bézout’s theorem: two complex projective conics without a common component have total intersection multiplicity four. Distinct positive-radius circles have distinct irreducible conic closures.

Two distinct circles never intersect in more than two points in the plane, while Bézout's theorem predicts four. The discrepancy comes from the fact that every circle passes through the same two complex points on the line at infinity. Writing the circle

{\displaystyle (x-a)^{2}+(y-b)^{2}=r^{2}}

in homogeneous coordinates, we get

{\displaystyle (x-az)^{2}+(y-bz)^{2}-r^{2}z^{2}=0,}

from which it is clear that the two points (1 : i : 0) and (1 : –i : 0) lie on every circle. When two circles do not meet at all in the real plane, the two other intersections have non-real coordinates, or if the circles are concentric then they meet at exactly the two points on the line at infinity with an intersection multiplicity of two.
