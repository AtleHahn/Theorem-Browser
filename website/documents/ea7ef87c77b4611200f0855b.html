Statement

Let K\subseteq\mathbb R^2 be compact and totally disconnected, meaning that every connected subset of K has at most one point. Then there is a continuous injective map \gamma:[0,1]\to\mathbb R^2 such that

K\subseteq\gamma([0,1]).

The image of such a map is called a Jordan arc. All topological notions use the usual Euclidean topology and its subspace topology on K.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Compactness, connectedness and continuous maps are standard undergraduate topology/analysis; total disconnectedness and Jordan arc are explicitly defined. No dimension theory is required.

Proof 1: Clopen coordinates and planar extension (external)

A supporting source can be found here (Chapter 13, pp.91–96, planar extension theorem). The deduction below supplies the argument for the stored statement.

The following reduction is supplied here. Its substantive named dependency is: every homeomorphism between compact totally disconnected subsets of R2 extends to a homeomorphism of the plane. For the reduction, a compact totally disconnected metric space is zero-dimensional, so K has a countable family of clopen sets U_j separating points (standard compact-space component/quasicomponent theorem plus second countability). Define e(x)=sum over j>=1 of 2*3^(-j)1_{U_j}(x). The series converges uniformly, so e is continuous. If x and y first differ at index j, the difference of their jth terms has absolute value 23^(-j), while the tail difference is at most 3^(-j); hence e(x) differs from e(y). Thus x maps to (e(x),0) homeomorphically onto a compact subset D of [0,1] times {0}. Apply Moise’s planar extension theorem to this homeomorphism to obtain H:R2 to R2. Then gamma(t)=H^{-1}(t,0), 0<=t<=1, is continuous and injective and its image contains K. Empty K is covered by any straight segment. The construction already includes all finite sets and sets with isolated points; no perfect-set assumption is imposed.

Level justification

The clopen-coordinate reduction uses compact metric spaces and uniform convergence. Its planar extension dependency can be proved with shrinking finite polygonal disk covers, matching clopen partitions, and homeomorphisms of disks with finitely many holes. Compactness and the shrinking diameters establish continuity of the resulting map and inverse. These use undergraduate topology and elementary planar geometry; the additional partition and polygonal gluing terminology fits the short preparation allowance.
