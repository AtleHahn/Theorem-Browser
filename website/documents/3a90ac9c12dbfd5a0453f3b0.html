Statement

Let A\in\mathbb C^{n\times n}, n\ge1, be normal (AA^*=A^*A), and use the Euclidean vector norm and its induced matrix 2-norm. For any \delta A\in\mathbb C^{n\times n} and any eigenvalue \mu of A+\delta A, some \lambda\in\Lambda(A) satisfies |\lambda-\mu|\le\|\delta A\|_2, where \Lambda(A) denotes the spectrum.

Proof 1: Eigenvalue perturbation bound for normal matrices proof

We use the Bauer–Fike bound: if M=V D V^{-1} with D diagonal and \nu is an eigenvalue of M+E, then some \eta\in\Lambda(M) satisfies |\nu-\eta|\le\kappa_2(V)\|E\|_2, where \kappa_2(V)=\|V\|_2\|V^{-1}\|_2.

If A is normal, choose the eigenvector matrix V to be a unitary matrix, therefore:

{\displaystyle \|V\|_{2}=\left\|V^{-1}\right\|_{2}=1,}

so that κ2(V) = 1. The Bauer–Fike theorem then becomes:

{\displaystyle \exists \lambda \in \Lambda (A):\quad |\lambda -\mu |\leq \|\delta A\|_{2}}
