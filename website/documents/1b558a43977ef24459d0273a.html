Statement

All smooth immersions S^6\to\mathbb R^7 are regularly homotopic. In particular, the embedded six-sphere admits eversion.

Proof 1: Apply Smale’s classification and the exact-sequence calculation — outline

Smale's classification identifies the regular homotopy classes of immersions of S^6 in \mathbb R^7 with

\pi_6\left(V_6(\mathbb R^7)\right).

Here V_6(\mathbb R^7) is the Stiefel manifold of orthonormal six-frames, and V_6(\mathbb R^7)\cong SO(7).

The fibration SO(6)\to SO(7)\to S^6 gives the exact sequence

\pi_6(SO(6))\to\pi_6(SO(7))\to\pi_6(S^6) \to\pi_5(SO(6))\to\pi_5(SO(7)).

Due to Bott periodicity we have

\pi_6(SO(6))\cong\pi_6(\operatorname{Spin}(6)) \cong\pi_6(SU(4))\cong\pi_6(U(4))\cong0,

and since

\pi_6(S^6)\cong\mathbb Z,\qquad \pi_5(SO(6))\cong\mathbb Z,\qquad \pi_5(SO(7))\cong0,

we have \pi_6(SO(7))\cong0. The source leaves implicit the elementary exactness step: the resulting map \mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z is surjective and hence injective.

Therefore all immersions of S^6 in Euclidean space of one more dimension are regularly homotopic. In particular, S^6 embedded in \mathbb R^7 admits eversion, i.e. one can turn this sphere “inside-out”.
