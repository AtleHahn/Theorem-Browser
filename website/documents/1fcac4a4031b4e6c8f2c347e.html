Statement

Let U,V\subset\mathbb R^2 be bounded open sets. If U=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^m A_j and V=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^m B_j are finite disjoint partitions with each A_j congruent to B_j, then U,V have equal Lebesgue area.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Basic topology supplies bounded open subsets of the line or plane, and their length or area can be understood as the usual countably additive extension of elementary length or area. The pieces are arbitrary sets, explicitly not required to have individual measures; the assertion compares only the open sets. This is a small set-geometric extension, with no general integration theory needed.

Proof 1: Apply Banach area to the finite partitions — outline

As shown by Banach, it is possible to define an "area" for all bounded subsets of the Euclidean plane in such a way that congruent sets will have equal "area". This Banach measure, however, is only finitely additive, so it is not a measure in the full sense, but it equals the Lebesgue measure on sets for which the latter exists. This implies that if two bounded open subsets of the Euclidean plane are equi-decomposable then they have equal area.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The imported Banach-area theorem is used only as an area assignment on all bounded sets that is additive for finite disjoint unions, invariant under congruence and agrees with usual area on open sets. Sum corresponding equal piece areas to get the result. These axioms are a small extension of undergraduate set and area reasoning; constructing the assignment or studying nonmeasurable sets is not required.
