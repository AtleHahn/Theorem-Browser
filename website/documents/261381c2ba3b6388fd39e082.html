Statement

Let a<b be real and let K:[a,b]^2\to\mathbb R be continuous and symmetric. Suppose it is positive definite in the non-strict sense: \sum_{i,j}K(x_i,x_j)c_ic_j\ge0 for all finite point lists and real coefficients. On real L^2[a,b], put (T_Kf)(t)=\int_a^bK(t,s)f(s)\,ds. Every eigenvalue of T_K is nonnegative.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement quantifies over eigenvalues of an infinite-dimensional integral operator.

Proof 1: Positive Riemann sums for continuous eigenfunctions — outline

To show non-negativity of the eigenvalues one can write \lambda\langle f,f\rangle=\langle f,T_Kf\rangle and express the right hand side as an integral well-approximated by its Riemann sums, which are non-negative by positive-definiteness of K, implying \lambda\langle f,f\rangle\geq0, implying \lambda\geq0. For a nonzero eigenvalue the eigenfunction has a continuous representative by the integral equation; a zero eigenvalue already satisfies the inequality.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Obtain a continuous representative for a nonzero L2 eigenfunction and use positive double Riemann sums. The final positivity check is elementary, but the L2 operator and almost-everywhere representative framework requires measure-theoretic functional analysis.
