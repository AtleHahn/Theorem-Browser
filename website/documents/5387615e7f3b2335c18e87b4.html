Statement

For t\in\mathbb C, put x=t^2-1 and y=t^3-t. Then y^2=x^3+x^2, and t=\pm1 both map to (0,0).

Level
High school only
Level justification

The claim is an explicit substitution into a cubic equation and evaluation at two values, using elementary polynomial and complex-number algebra. This rating concerns understanding the statement, independently of the difficulty of its proof.

Proof 1: Substitution of the polynomial map — outline

The source gives the map

t\longmapsto(t^2-1,t^3-t)

from a line to the curve y^2=x^3+x^2, with the two points t=+1 and t=-1 corresponding to its double point (0,0). The polynomial substitution verifying these assertions is not written out in the source.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Substitute the two polynomials and expand, then evaluate at plus and minus one. Ordinary polynomial algebra works equally for complex numbers; no curve singularity or algebraic-geometry theory is used.
