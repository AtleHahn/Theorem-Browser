Statement

Let X be a set, U\subseteq X, and B a closed subset of a topological space Y. In Y^X with the topology of pointwise convergence, the subset U_B=\{f:X\to Y:f(U)\subseteq B\} is closed.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Basic topology supplies products, closed sets and evaluation coordinates. Pointwise convergence is the product topology on the function set, a direct familiar construction; the value constraint introduces no functional analysis.

Proof 1: Pass to the limit at each constrained point

Let {\displaystyle f\in Y^{X}} and suppose that {\displaystyle \left(f_{i}\right)_{i\in I}} is a net in {\displaystyle U_{B}} that converges pointwise to {\displaystyle f.} It remains to show that {\displaystyle f\in U_{B},} which by definition means {\displaystyle f(U)\subseteq B.} For any {\displaystyle u\in U,} because {\displaystyle \left(f_{i}(u)\right)_{i\in I}\to f(u)} in {\displaystyle Y} and every value {\displaystyle f_{i}(u)\in f_{i}(U)\subseteq B} belongs to the closed (in {\displaystyle Y}) subset {\displaystyle B,} so too must this net's limit belong to this closed set; thus {\displaystyle f(u)\in B,} which completes the proof. {\displaystyle \blacksquare }

Level justification

Pass each coordinate net into the closed target set, then use the net criterion for closedness. Undergraduate point-set topology suffices with nets as the small directed-sequence extension of convergence; no additional advanced function-space theory is used.
