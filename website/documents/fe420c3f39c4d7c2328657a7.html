Statement

Write \mathbb N=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}. Let k,\ell\ge2 be integers with k^p\ne\ell^q for all positive integers p,q. For r\ge2, define V_r(0)=1 and, for x>0, let V_r(x) be the largest power of r dividing x. A subset of \mathbb N is definable in (\mathbb N,+,V_r) if a first-order formula in equality, addition and V_r describes membership. A set S is ultimately periodic if there are integers N\ge0,d\ge1 such that, for all x\ge N, x\in S if and only if x+d\in S. For S\subseteq\mathbb N, simultaneous definability in (\mathbb N,+,V_k) and (\mathbb N,+,V_\ell) is equivalent to ultimate periodicity.

Proof 1: Combine Büchi’s correspondence with Cobham’s theorem

Cobham's theorem can be formulated in first-order logic using a theorem proven by Büchi in 1960.

Use Büchi’s recognizability–definability theorem: for each base r\ge2, a subset of \mathbb N is definable in (\mathbb N,+,V_r) exactly when its base-r representations are recognized by a finite automaton. This translates the two definability conditions to simultaneous k- and \ell-recognizability. Cobham’s theorem says that these two recognizability conditions are equivalent to ultimate periodicity.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The application combines first-order definability in arithmetic structures with automata recognizability in different bases. Both equivalences are supplied, but learning the two distinct formal frameworks together is not confidently a small extension of the agreed baseline. Retain beyond undergraduate provisionally.
