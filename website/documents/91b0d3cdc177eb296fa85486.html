Statement

For 1<p<\infty, H denotes the Hilbert transform on L^p(\mathbb R), with sign convention Hf(t)=\pi^{-1}\operatorname{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb R}f(\tau)/(t-\tau)\,d\tau. Let m\in L^2(\mathbb R) be real-valued with Fourier support in [-B,B], where 0\le B<|\omega|/(2\pi), and let \varphi\in\mathbb R, \omega\ne0. Then H[m(t)\cos(\omega t+\varphi)]=\operatorname{sgn}(\omega)m(t)\sin(\omega t+\varphi). Fourier support uses \widehat m(\xi)=\int m(t)e^{-2\pi it\xi}\,dt in the L^2 sense.

Proof 1: Hilbert transform of an amplitude-modulated carrier — proof

Bedrosian's theorem states that the Hilbert transform of the product of a low-pass and a high-pass signal with non-overlapping spectra is given by the product of the low-pass signal and the Hilbert transform of the high-pass signal, or

{\displaystyle \operatorname {H} \left(f_{\text{LP}}(t)\cdot f_{\text{HP}}(t)\right)=f_{\text{LP}}(t)\cdot \operatorname {H} \left(f_{\text{HP}}(t)\right),}

where fLP and fHP are the low- and high-pass signals respectively. A category of communication signals to which this applies is called the narrowband signal model. A member of that category is amplitude modulation of a high-frequency sinusoidal "carrier":

{\displaystyle u(t)=m(t)\cdot \cos(\omega t+\varphi ),}

where m(t) is the narrow bandwidth "message" waveform, such as voice or music. Then by Bedrosian's theorem:

{\displaystyle \operatorname {H} (u)(t)={\begin{cases}+m(t)\cdot \sin(\omega t+\varphi )&{\text{if }}\omega >0\\-m(t)\cdot \sin(\omega t+\varphi )&{\text{if }}\omega <0\end{cases}}}

Level justification

Use spectral separation in Bedrosian and the Hilbert transform of the carrier. General L2 Fourier support, principal-value transforms and multiplier action require graduate harmonic analysis.
