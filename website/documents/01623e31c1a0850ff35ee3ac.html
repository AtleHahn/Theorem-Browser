Statement

Let X be a complex vector space, let F:X\to\mathbb C satisfy F(\lambda x)=\lambda F(x) for all complex \lambda, and let p:X\to\mathbb R satisfy p(ux)=p(x) whenever |u|=1. Then |F|\le p if and only if \operatorname{Re}F\le p.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra supplies norms, inner products and scalar-valued linear maps; analysis supplies suprema and norm closure. Seminorm or cyclic-vector conditions are explicitly given by norm inequalities or the closed span of iterates. These direct definitions suffice without spectral or weak-topology theory.

Proof 1: Real-part domination for a complex homogeneous map

If {\displaystyle |F|\leq p} then {\displaystyle \operatorname {Re} F\leq |\operatorname {Re} F|\leq |F|\leq p.} For the converse, assume {\displaystyle \operatorname {Re} F\leq p} and fix {\displaystyle x\in X.} Let {\displaystyle r=|F(x)|} and pick any {\displaystyle \theta \in \mathbb {R} } such that {\displaystyle F(x)=re^{i\theta };} it remains to show {\displaystyle r\leq p(x).} Homogeneity of {\displaystyle F} implies {\displaystyle F\left(e^{-i\theta }x\right)=r} is real so that {\displaystyle \operatorname {Re} F\left(e^{-i\theta }x\right)=F\left(e^{-i\theta }x\right).} By assumption, {\displaystyle \operatorname {Re} F\leq p} and {\displaystyle p\left(e^{-i\theta }x\right)=p(x),} so that {\displaystyle r=\operatorname {Re} F\left(e^{-i\theta }x\right)\leq p\left(e^{-i\theta }x\right)=p(x),} as desired. {\displaystyle \blacksquare }

Level justification

Rotate x by the phase that makes F(x) nonnegative real, then apply the assumed real-part bound and phase invariance. Undergraduate complex vectors and polar form suffice.
