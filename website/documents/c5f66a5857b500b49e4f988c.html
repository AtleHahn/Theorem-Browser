Statement

Every infinite tree (a connected acyclic undirected graph) has either a vertex with infinitely many neighbors or a ray. A ray is an infinite path with no repeated vertices.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Graphs, finite subset colorings and infinite sequences are elementary discrete mathematics. Matchings are disjoint edges and a ray is an infinite nonrepeating path; the conditions are explicitly given. No graph-minor or set-theoretic independence machinery is needed.

Proof 1: Split according to local finiteness

If the tree is not locally finite, some vertex has infinitely many neighbors. Otherwise it is connected, infinite and locally finite, so Kőnig’s infinity lemma—every connected locally finite infinite graph has a ray—applies.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Separate the infinite-degree case and apply the explicitly stated infinity lemma to the locally finite case. Undergraduate infinite sets and graph definitions suffice, without proving the compactness principle.
