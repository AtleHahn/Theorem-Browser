Statement

Let X be a topological space, k a commutative ring with identity, \alpha\in C^p(X;k) and \beta\in C^q(X;k) singular cochains, and \sigma:\Delta^{p+q}\to X a singular simplex. In the Alexander–Whitney cup formula, (\alpha\smile\beta)(\sigma)=\alpha(\sigma|_{[0,\ldots,p]})\beta(\sigma|_{[p,\ldots,p+q]}), where restrictions are to the indicated ordered front and back faces.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Singular cochains and the Alexander-Whitney cup product require substantial algebraic-topology preparation. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Use the cochain degrees to select the single term

The standard cup product on cochains is given explicitly by

(\alpha\smile\beta)(\sigma)=\sum_{r=0}^{\dim\sigma}\alpha(\sigma|_{[0,\ldots,r]})\cdot\beta(\sigma|_{[r,\ldots,\dim\sigma]}).

Since cochain evaluation C^a(X;k)\otimes C_b(X;k)\to k vanishes unless a=b, this reduces to the stated expression, with r=p.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Use homogeneous degree to retain only the pth split in Alexander–Whitney. Singular simplices, cochains and their cup product are substantial algebraic-topology prerequisites despite the simple selection.
