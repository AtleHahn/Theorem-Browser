Statement

Let K\subseteq\mathbb R^2 be compact with connected complement \mathbb R^2\setminus K. For every continuous f:\partial K\to\mathbb R and every \varepsilon>0, there exists a polynomial P(x,y) with real coefficients such that

\frac{\partial^2P}{\partial x^2}+\frac{\partial^2P}{\partial y^2}=0,\qquad |f(x,y)-P(x,y)|<\varepsilon\quad\text{for all }(x,y)\in\partial K.

Here \partial K is the topological boundary, and the differential equation defines a harmonic polynomial.

Level justification

Proof 1: Dirichlet extensions and Runge approximation (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Chapter 3, Theorem 3.19 and Lemma 3.20, pp. 46–47.).

Use smooth enclosing neighborhoods whose filled components are finitely many disjoint Jordan disks. They can shrink to K: paths to infinity in the connected complement prevent their filled hulls from retaining points outside any prescribed neighborhood of K. Approximate the datum by a smooth function and solve the Dirichlet problem on each disk. At every boundary point of K, the connected unbounded complement meets every centered circle, so Lemma 3.20 applies. Choose harmonic conjugates separately on the disks, apply Runge approximation on K, and take real parts. Divide the error between smoothing, boundary convergence and polynomial approximation.
