Statement

Let R be an integral domain with irreducible nonassociate elements p,q and elements a,b such that pa=qb and q\nmid a. Then p+qX and a+qX each have no nonunit common divisor of their coefficients, but their product does.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Integral domains, irreducibles, associates and divisibility are introductory algebra. The polynomial coefficients and their common-divisor property are explicit, without extension fields or ideal-class theory.

Proof 1: A primitive product can fail over a non-UFD — outline

The coefficient pairs (p,q) and (a,q) have no nonunit common divisor by irreducibility and the divisibility assumptions. Yet (p+qX)(a+qX)=pa+(p+a)qX+q^2X^2=q\bigl(b+(p+a)X+qX^2\bigr), so the nonunit q divides every coefficient of their product.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use irreducibility to check coefficient coprimality and factor q out of the expanded product. Undergraduate integral-domain and polynomial algebra suffice.
