Statement

For a finite group G, let M(G) be the isomorphism classes of finite G-sets with addition induced by disjoint union. Its Grothendieck group A(G) has a commutative ring structure induced by Cartesian product with diagonal G-action, whose multiplicative identity is the one-point G-set.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory abstract algebra supplies finite group actions, isomorphisms and rings. The extra Grothendieck group here is the group of formal differences of finite G-set classes under disjoint union, analogous to extending nonnegative integers to integers. Product has the explicit diagonal action. This single group-completion construction and its meaning fit about 30 minutes; no category theory or K-theory is required.

Proof 1: Disjoint union, product and group completion — outline

The Burnside ring of a finite group G is constructed from the category of finite G-sets as a Grothendieck group. More precisely, let M(G) be the commutative monoid of isomorphism classes of finite G-sets, with addition the disjoint union of G-sets and identity element the empty set (which is a G-set in a unique way). Then A(G), the Grothendieck group of M(G), is an abelian group. The ring structure on A(G) is induced by the direct product of G-sets; the multiplicative identity is the (isomorphism class of any) one-point set, which becomes a G-set in a unique way.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Finite group actions and rings suffice after learning formal group completion: represent elements as differences of finite G-set classes and extend products distributively. This is one concrete algebraic construction analogous to forming integers from natural numbers, not a prerequisite in K-theory or category theory.
