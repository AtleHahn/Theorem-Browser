Statement

Let H be a complex inner-product space, with positive definite Hermitian inner product B(x,y) linear in x and conjugate-linear in y, and norm \|x\|=\sqrt{B(x,x)}. Let H^{\mathrm{anti}} be its space of continuous conjugate-linear functionals f:H\to\mathbb C, with norm

\|f\|=\sup_{\|x\|\leq1}|f(x)|.

Conjugate-linearity means f(x+y)=f(x)+f(y) and f(cx)=\overline c\,f(x).

The canonical linear map

J:H\longrightarrow H^{\mathrm{anti}},\qquad J(x)(y)=B(x,y),

is surjective if and only if H is complete in its norm, that is, is a Hilbert space. In this case J is a surjective linear isometry: \|J(x)\|=\|x\| for every x\in H.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Inner products, conjugation, norms and completeness come from linear algebra and introductory analysis. The antidual adds only bounded conjugate-linear scalar functionals and their explicitly given norm; this is a small extension with familiar finite-dimensional examples.
