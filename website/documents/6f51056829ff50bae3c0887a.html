Statement

On (S,\Sigma,\mu) let extended-real measurable f_n\ge-g for an integrable real g, and suppose f_n converge in measure to a measurable f. Then \int_Sf\,d\mu\le\liminf_n\int_S f_n\,d\mu.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement concerns measurable functions and measure integrals, beyond the calculus/linear-algebra baseline.

Proof 1: Choose an integral-minimizing subsequence and an almost-everywhere subsubsequence

There exists a subsequence such that

{\displaystyle \lim _{k\to \infty }\int _{S}f_{n_{k}}\,d\mu =\liminf _{n\to \infty }\int _{S}f_{n}\,d\mu .}

Since this subsequence also converges in measure to {\displaystyle f}, there exists a further subsequence, which converges pointwise to {\displaystyle f} almost everywhere, hence the Fatou inequality for almost-everywhere limits with a common integrable lower bound is applicable to this subsubsequence.
