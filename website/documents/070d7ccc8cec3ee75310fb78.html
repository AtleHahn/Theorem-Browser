Statement

Let (S,\Sigma,\mu) be a measure space, let 1\le p\le\infty, and let f,g be real- or complex-valued members of L^p(S,\mu). Then f+g\in L^p(S,\mu) and

\|f+g\|_p\le\|f\|_p+\|g\|_p,

where \|f\|_p=(\int_S|f|^p\,d\mu)^{1/p} for finite p and \|f\|_\infty=\operatorname{ess\,sup}_S|f|.

For 1<p<\infty, equality holds if and only if f=\lambda g almost everywhere for some real \lambda\ge0, or g=0 almost everywhere. Functions in L^p are identified when equal almost everywhere; L^p consists of those measurable functions with finite displayed norm.

Level justification

General measure spaces, integrability and almost-everywhere equivalence are cumulative prerequisites beyond the assumed undergraduate baseline. The finite-vector special case does not lower the level of this full statement.
