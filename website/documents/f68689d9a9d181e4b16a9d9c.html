Statement

Let d\ge2 and 1\le q<d. Let E\subset\mathbb R^d be Lebesgue measurable and suppose all its nonempty proper coordinate projections are measurable and have finite measure. For J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}, let \pi_J retain exactly the coordinates in J. Then |E|\le\prod_{|J|=q}|\pi_J(E)|^{1/\binom{d-1}{q-1}}.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The objects are general measurable sets and projected Lebesgue measures; finite multiplicity counting alone does not remove those prerequisites.

Proof 1: Iterate the hyperplane inequality and count multiplicities — outline

Use the coordinate-hyperplane Loomis–Whitney inequality

|F|^{r-1}\le\prod_{j=1}^r|\pi_j(F)|

in each dimension r\ge2, where \pi_j deletes the jth coordinate. Iterating the theorem yields, more generally,

|E|\le\prod_{|J|=q}|\pi_J(E)|^{1/\binom{d-1}{q-1}},

where \pi_J retains the coordinates in J.

The source gives the instruction to iterate, without displaying the iteration or its multiplicity count. Here q=d-k translates its indexing, and the projections are onto coordinate subspaces.
