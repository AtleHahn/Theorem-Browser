Statement

Let X be a vector space over \mathbb K\in\{\mathbb R,\mathbb C\}, M\subseteq X a linear subspace, and p:X\to[0,\infty) a seminorm, so p(x+y)\le p(x)+p(y) and p(\lambda x)=|\lambda|p(x). Let f:M\to\mathbb K be linear with |f(m)|\le p(m) for m\in M. Then there is a linear extension F:X\to\mathbb K of f satisfying |F(x)|\le p(x) for every x\in X.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra supplies subspaces and linear functionals; analysis supplies norm bounds and countability. Sublinear, convex or seminorm conditions are explicitly given inequalities, and the functional norm is a unit-ball supremum. These direct extensions suffice without locally convex or dual-topology theory.

Proof 1: Real-part extension and phase domination — outline

For real scalars, apply real Hahn–Banach to obtain F\leq p; applying the bound to -x gives |F|\leq p.

The following observations allow the Hahn–Banach theorem for real vector spaces to be applied to (complex-valued) linear functionals on complex vector spaces.

Every linear functional {\displaystyle F:X\to \mathbb {C} } on a complex vector space is completely determined by its real part {\displaystyle \;\operatorname {Re} F:X\to \mathbb {R} \;} through the formula

{\displaystyle F(x)\;=\;\operatorname {Re} F(x)-i\operatorname {Re} F(ix)\qquad {\text{ for all }}x\in X}

If {\displaystyle z=a+ib\in \mathbb {C} } has real part {\displaystyle \operatorname {Re} z=a} then {\displaystyle -\operatorname {Re} (iz)=b,} which proves that {\displaystyle z=\operatorname {Re} z-i\operatorname {Re} (iz).} Substituting {\displaystyle F(x)} in for {\displaystyle z} and using {\displaystyle iF(x)=F(ix)} gives {\displaystyle F(x)=\operatorname {Re} F(x)-i\operatorname {Re} F(ix).} {\displaystyle \blacksquare }

In particular, a linear functional on {\displaystyle X} extends another one defined on {\displaystyle M\subseteq X} if and only if their real parts are equal on {\displaystyle M} (in other words, a linear functional {\displaystyle F} extends {\displaystyle f} if and only if {\displaystyle \operatorname {Re} F} extends {\displaystyle \operatorname {Re} f}). The real part of a linear functional on {\displaystyle X} is always a real-linear functional (meaning that it is linear when {\displaystyle X} is considered as a real vector space) and if {\displaystyle R:X\to \mathbb {R} } is a real-linear functional on a complex vector space then {\displaystyle x\mapsto R(x)-iR(ix)} defines the unique linear functional on {\displaystyle X} whose real part is {\displaystyle R.}

If {\displaystyle F} is a linear functional on a (complex or real) vector space {\displaystyle X} and if {\displaystyle p:X\to \mathbb {R} } is a seminorm then

{\displaystyle |F|\,\leq \,p\quad {\text{ if and only if }}\quad \operatorname {Re} F\,\leq \,p.}

Let {\displaystyle F} be any homogeneous scalar-valued map on {\displaystyle X} (such as a linear functional) and let {\displaystyle p:X\to \mathbb {R} } be any map that satisfies {\displaystyle p(ux)=p(x)} for all {\displaystyle x} and unit length scalars {\displaystyle u} (such as a seminorm). If {\displaystyle |F|\leq p} then {\displaystyle \operatorname {Re} F\leq |\operatorname {Re} F|\leq |F|\leq p.} For the converse, assume {\displaystyle \operatorname {Re} F\leq p} and fix {\displaystyle x\in X.} Let {\displaystyle r=|F(x)|} and pick any {\displaystyle \theta \in \mathbb {R} } such that {\displaystyle F(x)=re^{i\theta };} it remains to show {\displaystyle r\leq p(x).} Homogeneity of {\displaystyle F} implies {\displaystyle F\left(e^{-i\theta }x\right)=r} is real so that {\displaystyle \operatorname {Re} F\left(e^{-i\theta }x\right)=F\left(e^{-i\theta }x\right).} By assumption, {\displaystyle \operatorname {Re} F\leq p} and {\displaystyle p\left(e^{-i\theta }x\right)=p(x),} so that {\displaystyle r=\operatorname {Re} F\left(e^{-i\theta }x\right)\leq p\left(e^{-i\theta }x\right)=p(x),} as desired. {\displaystyle \blacksquare }

Stated in simpler language, a linear functional is dominated by a seminorm {\displaystyle p} if and only if its real part is dominated above by {\displaystyle p.}

Proof of Hahn–Banach for complex vector spaces by reduction to real vector spaces

Suppose {\displaystyle p:X\to \mathbb {R} } is a seminorm on a complex vector space {\displaystyle X} and let {\displaystyle f:M\to \mathbb {C} } be a linear functional defined on a vector subspace {\displaystyle M} of {\displaystyle X} that satisfies {\displaystyle |f|\leq p} on {\displaystyle M.} Consider {\displaystyle X} as a real vector space and apply the Hahn–Banach theorem for real vector spaces to the real-linear functional {\displaystyle \;\operatorname {Re} f:M\to \mathbb {R} \;} to obtain a real-linear extension {\displaystyle R:X\to \mathbb {R} } that is also dominated above by {\displaystyle p,} so that it satisfies {\displaystyle R\leq p} on {\displaystyle X} and {\displaystyle R=\operatorname {Re} f} on {\displaystyle M.} The map {\displaystyle F:X\to \mathbb {C} } defined by {\displaystyle F(x)\;=\;R(x)-iR(ix)} is a linear functional on {\displaystyle X} that extends {\displaystyle f} (because their real parts agree on {\displaystyle M}) and satisfies {\displaystyle |F|\leq p} on {\displaystyle X} (because {\displaystyle \operatorname {Re} F\leq p} and {\displaystyle p} is a seminorm). {\displaystyle \blacksquare }

Proof 2: Compact gluing of finite-dimensional extensions — outline

Compact consistency lemma

Tychonoff's theorem in the Hausdorff case is used to deduce the following variant of the axiom of choice. Given a family of nonempty compact Hausdorff spaces {\displaystyle K_{x},\,x\in X} parametrized by a set {\displaystyle X}, by a choice function for it, we shall mean a function

{\displaystyle c:X\to U:=\cup _{x\in X}K_{x}}

such that {\displaystyle c(x)\in K_{x}} for each {\displaystyle x}. Also, for a relation {\displaystyle R} on {\displaystyle U}; i.e., a subset of {\displaystyle U\times U}, we say such {\displaystyle c} is {\displaystyle R}-consistent on a set {\displaystyle S\subset X} if {\displaystyle (c(x),c(y))\in R} for each {\displaystyle x,y\in S}.

Lemma—Assume, for each {\displaystyle x,y} in {\displaystyle X}, that {\displaystyle R\cap (K_{x}\times K_{y})} is closed. Also, assume that for each finite subset {\displaystyle S\subset X}, there is a choice function for the family {\displaystyle K_{x},\,x\in X} that is {\displaystyle R}-consistent on {\displaystyle S}.

Then there exists a choice function for the family {\displaystyle K_{x}} that is {\displaystyle R}-consistent on {\displaystyle X}.

Proof of lemma: let {\displaystyle P=\prod _{x\in X}K_{x}}, which is nonempty and compact (also Hausdorff). Consider the family {\displaystyle P^{S}} parametrized by finite subsets {\displaystyle S\subset X} and given by

{\displaystyle P^{S}=} the set of all {\displaystyle c\in P} that are {\displaystyle R}-consistent on {\displaystyle S}.

Each {\displaystyle P^{S}} is the intersection of {\displaystyle p_{x,y}^{-1}(R\cap (K_{x}\times K_{y}))} over {\displaystyle x,y\in S}, where {\displaystyle p_{x,y}(c)=(p_{x}(c),p_{y}(c))}. Thus, it is closed and nonempty by assumption. For finitely many finite sets S_1,\ldots,S_n, the nonempty set P^{S_1\cup\cdots\cup S_n} is contained in P^{S_1}\cap\cdots\cap P^{S_n}. By compactness, the intersection {\displaystyle \cap _{S}P^{S}} is nonempty and any element of it (which we can pick without the axiom of choice) is a required choice function. {\displaystyle \square }

Application to finite-dimensional extensions

For this application write V=X and V_0=M for the ambient space and subspace in the statement. Now, the Hahn–Banach theorem is proved as follows. Given a linear functional {\displaystyle f} on a subspace {\displaystyle V_{0}\subset V} dominated by a seminorm {\displaystyle p}, consider

  • {\displaystyle \mathcal I} = the set of all finite-dimensional subspaces of {\displaystyle V}.
  • For each {\displaystyle W\in \mathcal I}, {\displaystyle W'} = its dual and {\displaystyle K_{W}=\{g\in W'\mid g|_{W\cap V_{0}}=f|_{W\cap V_{0}},\,|g|\leq p\}.}
  • {\displaystyle R=\{(g,h)\in K_{W_{1}}\times K_{W_{2}}\mid W_{i}\in \mathcal I,\,g|_{W_{1}\cap W_{2}}=h|_{W_{1}\cap W_{2}}\}}.

The hypotheses here use finite-dimensional dominated extension, compactness of closed bounded subsets of finite-dimensional dual spaces, and closedness of the agreement constraints. Finite-dimensional extension gives compatible choices on any finite collection of subspaces; nonemptiness of the full product, supplied by Hausdorff Tychonoff, supplies all other coordinates. Thus the assumptions of the lemma are satisfied and thus we find an {\displaystyle R}-consistent choice function {\displaystyle c}. Then the functional {\displaystyle g(x)=c(W)(x),\,W=\operatorname {span} \{x\}} is a required extension.
