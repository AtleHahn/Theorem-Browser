Simpler proof assessment

Likely alternative prerequisite level: Undergraduate level; lowest recorded proof level: Beyond undergraduate level. Sum the Euclidean angle totals of all faces: a k-gon contributes (k-2)pi. Every edge has two incident faces, so total defect is 2pi V-2pi E+2pi F. Interpreting chi as this finite cell count proves the general closed-surface formula; chi=2 and 0 give the sphere and torus cases. This needs elementary polygon geometry and basic topology, not curvature measures or differential geometry. Target undergraduate respects the statement level.

Statement

Let P be a closed polyhedral surface and \chi(P) its Euler characteristic. At a vertex v let \delta_v=2\pi-\sum_{F\ni v}\theta_{F,v}, where \theta_{F,v} is the interior angle of face F at v. Then \sum_v\delta_v=2\pi\chi(P). In particular, a polyhedron homeomorphic to a sphere has total defect 4\pi, and a toroidal polyhedron has total defect zero.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

For a finite polyhedral surface the Euler characteristic is the combinatorial number vertices minus edges plus faces. The remaining quantities are face angles and their finite sums. These definitions suffice for the full polyhedral formula without curvature tensors or homology.

Proof 1: Curvature concentrated at the vertices

A generalization says the number of circles in the total defect equals the Euler characteristic of the polyhedron. This is a special case of the Gauss–Bonnet theorem, which relates the integral of the Gaussian curvature to the Euler characteristic. Here, the Gaussian curvature is concentrated at the vertices: on the faces and edges the curvature is zero (the surface is locally isometric to a Euclidean plane) and the integral of curvature at a vertex is equal to the defect there (by definition).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Interpret polyhedral Gaussian curvature as an atomic measure and apply Gauss-Bonnet. Curvature measures and their geometric theorem require graduate background, even though the final sum is finite.
