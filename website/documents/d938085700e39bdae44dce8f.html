Statement

Let 0\le x\le\pi/2, with x/\pi\in\mathbb Q and \sin x\in\mathbb Q. Then x\in\{0,\pi/6,\pi/2\} and the corresponding sine values are 0,1/2,1.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Proof 1: Lehmer degree theorem and first-quadrant specialization outline

Lehmer showed that for relatively prime integers k and n with n>2, the number 2\cos(2\pi k/n) is an algebraic number of degree \varphi(n)/2, where \varphi denotes Euler's totient function. Because rational numbers have degree 1, a rational cosine requires n\le2 or \varphi(n)=2, and therefore the only possibilities are n=1,2,3,4,6.

The corresponding result for the sine follows using the trigonometric identity \sin\theta=\cos(\theta-\pi/2). The cosine values for these denominators are 1,-1,-1/2,0,1/2, so a rational sine at a rational multiple of \pi must be one of 0,\pm1/2,\pm1. On [0,\pi/2] the sine is nonnegative and strictly increasing, with \sin0=0, \sin(\pi/6)=1/2, and \sin(\pi/2)=1. These give exactly the stated possibilities.
