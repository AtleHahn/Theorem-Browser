Statement

Let A,B be real or complex n×n matrices with A invertible and spectral radius \rho(A^{-1}B)<1. Then (A-B)^{-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(A^{-1}B)^kA^{-1}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves real or complex matrices; matrix inverses; spectral radius; convergent matrix geometric series. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Iterate the resolvent identity — outline

Use the resolvent identity

{\displaystyle \left({A}-{B}\right)^{-1}={A}^{-1}+{A}^{-1}{B}\left({A}-{B}\right)^{-1},}

This is valid even if {\displaystyle B} is singular, and has a recursive structure that yields

{\displaystyle \left({A}-{B}\right)^{-1}=\sum _{k=0}^{\infty }\left({A}^{-1}{B}\right)^{k}{A}^{-1}}

if the spectral radius of {\displaystyle A^{-1}B} is less than one. That is, if the above sum converges then it is equal to {\displaystyle (A-B)^{-1}}.
