Statement

For a finitely generated module M over a PID R, let tM=\{m\in M:rm=0\text{ for some }0\ne r\in R\}. Then M=tM\oplus F for a finite-rank free submodule F.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement distinguishes torsion and free submodules over a general PID.

Proof 1: Lift a free quotient basis and split the projection

Denote by tM the torsion submodule of M. It can be embedded as a submodule of M, and this gives a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow tM\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow M/tM\longrightarrow0,

where the map M\longrightarrow M/tM is a projection. The quotient M/tM is a finitely generated torsion-free module, and such a module over a PID is a free module of finite rank. Thus M/tM is isomorphic to R^n for a nonnegative integer n.

Since every free module is a projective module, there exists a right inverse of the projection map (it suffices to lift each of the basis elements of M/tM into M). By the splitting lemma, M splits as an internal direct sum of tM and some submodule F of M. The image F of the right inverse is isomorphic to the free quotient M/tM.

Level justification

Use freeness of the torsion-free quotient and split a module exact sequence by lifting a basis. The general PID-module, quotient and splitting framework is not developed locally and cumulatively exceeds a confidently small extension; retain beyond undergraduate provisionally.
