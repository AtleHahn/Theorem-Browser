Statement

If a real or complex sequence (c_n)_{n\geq1} converges to C, then its arithmetic means d_N=(c_1+\cdots+c_N)/N also converge to C.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves convergent scalar sequences; arithmetic means. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Initial segment and small tail outline

To see that, subtract C everywhere to reduce to the case C=0. Then divide the sequence into an initial segment, and a tail of small terms: given any \varepsilon>0, choose m so that each term after c_m is bounded in absolute value by \varepsilon/2. We can take N large enough to make the contribution of the initial segment c_1,\ldots,c_m to the average at most \varepsilon/2 in absolute value, while each term in the tail is bounded by \varepsilon/2 so that the average is also necessarily bounded by \varepsilon.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Subtract the limit, split the average into a fixed finite initial segment and an epsilon-small tail, and bound their contributions separately. This is the standard epsilon definition of sequence convergence from undergraduate analysis.
