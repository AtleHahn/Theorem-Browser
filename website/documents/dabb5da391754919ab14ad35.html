Statement

Assume the axiom of choice. If there are surjections f:A\to B and g:B\to A, then there is a bijection A\to B.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Sets, maps, equivalence relations and partial orders are undergraduate foundations. All conditions are explicit, and choice is a stated assumption where needed; no ordinal or model-theoretic structure occurs.

Proof 1: Choose sections of the two surjections

We construct an injective function h:B\to A by picking a single element from the inverse image under f of each point in B. The surjectivity of f guarantees the existence of at least one element in each such inverse image; the choices use the assumed axiom of choice. We do the same to obtain an injective function k:A\to B from the inverse images under g. The Schröder–Bernstein theorem then can be applied to the injections h and k.

Level justification

Choose one point from each nonempty fiber to get injections in both directions, then apply Schroder-Bernstein. Undergraduate sets, cardinality and the elementary choice principle suffice.
