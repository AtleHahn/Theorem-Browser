Statement

Let \sigma:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R be continuous with finite limits \alpha=\sigma(-\infty)<\beta=\sigma(+\infty). The clipped ramp r(x)=\max(-1,\min(1,x)) can be approximated uniformly on \mathbb R by finite real linear combinations of functions \sigma(ax+b).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves continuous real functions with finite limits at infinity; clipped ramp function; uniform approximation on the real line; finite linear combinations of affine rescalings. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Compressed-step staircase — outline

If {\displaystyle \sigma } is "squashing", that is, it has limits {\displaystyle \sigma (-\infty )<\sigma (+\infty )}, then one can first affinely scale down its x-axis so that its graph looks like a step-function with two sharp "overshoots", then make a linear sum of enough of them to make a "staircase" approximation of the ramp function. With more steps of the staircase, the overshoots smooth out and we get arbitrarily good approximation of the ramp function.

The source gives this staircase construction qualitatively. It leaves the normalization, control of overshoots and tails, and choices of step size and compression implicit.
