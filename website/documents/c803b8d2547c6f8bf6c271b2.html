Statement

Let P be a real homogeneous polynomial in n variables such that \int_{\mathbb R^n}e^{P(x)}\,d^nx is finite. If T is a real n\times n matrix with \det T=1, then \int_{\mathbb R^n}e^{P(Tx)}\,d^nx=\int_{\mathbb R^n}e^{P(x)}\,d^nx.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Multiple integrals; Homogeneous polynomials; Matrix determinants. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. Proof 1: Unimodular invariance of homogeneous exponential integrals — outline

Applying a linear change of basis shows that the integral of the exponential of a homogeneous polynomial in n variables may depend only on \operatorname{SL}(n,\mathbb R)-invariants of the polynomial. Here the change of basis is x\mapsto Tx, with determinant one.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Change variables by the invertible matrix and use determinant one to leave the volume factor unchanged. Undergraduate multivariable integration and determinants suffice for this convergent smooth integral.
