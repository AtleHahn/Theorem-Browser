Statement

Let Q_0 be a convex cyclic quadrilateral and Q any convex quadrilateral with the same successive positive side lengths a,b,c,d. Then \operatorname{area}(Q)\le\operatorname{area}(Q_0).

Proof 1: The angle correction is nonnegative outline

Use Bretschneider’s formula for a convex quadrilateral with side lengths a,b,c,d, semiperimeter s, and \theta half the sum of two opposite angles:

{\displaystyle K={\sqrt {(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)(s-d)-abcd\cos ^{2}\theta }}}

(The choice of which pair of opposite angles is irrelevant: if the other two angles are taken, half their sum is 180° − θ. Since cos(180° − θ) = −cos θ, we have cos2(180° − θ) = cos2 θ.)

It is a property of cyclic quadrilaterals (and ultimately of inscribed angles) that opposite angles of a quadrilateral sum to 180°. Consequently, in the case of an inscribed quadrilateral, θ is 90°, whence the term

{\displaystyle abcd\cos ^{2}\theta =abcd\cos ^{2}\left(90^{\circ }\right)=abcd\cdot 0=0,}

giving the basic form of Brahmagupta's formula. It follows from the latter equation that the area of a cyclic quadrilateral is the maximum possible area for any quadrilateral with the given side lengths.

Level justification

In the supplied area formula the squared-cosine correction is nonnegative and vanishes for supplementary opposite angles. Monotonicity of square root gives the bound.
