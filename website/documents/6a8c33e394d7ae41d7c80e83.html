Philosophical significance

An angle can be trisected to any prescribed accuracy with classical tools even though exact trisection is not generally possible. This distinguishes exact mathematical impossibility from practical approximation and highlights the role of ideal precision in construction problems.

Statement

Using repeated straightedge-and-compass angle bisections and angle addition/subtraction, the third of any given angle can be approximated to any prescribed positive accuracy in finitely many steps.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement asks for a finite sequence of elementary angle constructions achieving any prescribed positive accuracy. Understanding it requires elementary Euclidean geometry and numerical approximation; infinite-series convergence is used in the supplied proof, not required to understand the statement.

Proof 1: Geometric-series bisection outline

Trisection can be approximated by repetition of the compass and straightedge method for bisecting an angle. The geometric series ⁠1/3⁠ = ⁠1/4⁠ + ⁠1/16⁠ + ⁠1/64⁠ + ⁠1/256⁠ + ⋯ or ⁠1/3⁠ = ⁠1/2⁠ − ⁠1/4⁠ + ⁠1/8⁠ − ⁠1/16⁠ + ⋯ can be used as a basis for the bisections. An approximation to any degree of accuracy can be obtained in a finite number of steps.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Represent one third by the given geometric series and use its vanishing tail to obtain any prescribed finite accuracy. Angle construction is elementary, while the written convergence and accuracy argument uses basic undergraduate limits.
