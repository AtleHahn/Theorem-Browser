Statement

Let f:S^1\to S^1 be a smooth orientation-preserving diffeomorphism, with f(e^{i\theta})=e^{ig(\theta)} and g(\theta+2\pi)=g(\theta)+2\pi. On L^2(S^1) let P be the orthogonal projection onto the closed span of e^{in\theta} for n\geq0, let T=2P-I, and let (V(f)h)(\zeta)=h(f^{-1}(\zeta)). Set K_f=V(f)PV(f)^{-1}-P. The operator I-K_f is Fredholm of index zero.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Fredholm and Hardy-space notions are outside calculus/linear algebra.

Proof 1: Compact perturbation of the identity

The operator

K_f=V(f)PV(f)^{-1}-P

is compact, indeed it is given by an operator with smooth kernel because P and T are given by singular integral operators. The smooth-kernel assertion is used here as an external prerequisite.

The operator I-K_f is a Fredholm operator of index zero, by the theorem that a compact perturbation of the identity is Fredholm of index zero.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Use smooth-kernel compactness and the compact-perturbation Fredholm theorem for the conjugated Hardy projection. Singular integral operators, L2 compactness and Fredholm index require functional analysis.
