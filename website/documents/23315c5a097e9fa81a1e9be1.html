Statement

Fix an integer d\ge1. For every E\subseteq\mathbb R^d and every Vitali cover \mathcal F of E by closed balls, some countable disjoint subfamily covers E up to a Lebesgue-null set. A Vitali cover means that every x\in E belongs to members of arbitrarily small positive diameter.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Metric balls, diameter, disjoint families and separability are basic topology. A Vitali cover is explicitly arbitrarily small balls at every point; nullity means box covers of arbitrarily small total volume. These limited geometric definitions suffice without general integration.

Proof 1: Closed initial segments and a vanishing tail

Write \lambda_d^* for Lebesgue outer measure and 5C for the ball with the same center and five times the radius. Without loss of generality, one can assume that all balls in F are nondegenerate and have radius less than or equal to 1. By the infinite form of the covering lemma, there exists a countable disjoint subcollection {\displaystyle \mathbf {G} } of F such that every ball B ∈ F intersects a ball C ∈ G for which B ⊂ 5 C. Let r > 0 be given, and let Z denote the set of points z ∈ E that are not contained in any ball from G and belong to the open ball B(r) of radius r, centered at 0. It is enough to show that Z is Lebesgue-negligible, for every given r.

Let {\displaystyle \mathbf {G} _{r}=\{C_{n}\}_{n}} denote the subcollection of those balls in G that meet B(r). Note that {\displaystyle \mathbf {G} _{r}} may be finite or countably infinite. Let z ∈ Z be fixed. For each N, z does not belong to the closed set {\displaystyle K=\bigcup _{n\leq N}C_{n}} by the definition of Z. But by the Vitali cover property, one can find a ball B ∈ F containing z, contained in B(r), and disjoint from K. By the property of G, the ball B intersects some ball {\displaystyle C_{i}\in \mathbf {G} } and is contained in {\displaystyle 5C_{i}}. The ball C_i meets B(r) because it meets B, and so belongs to the enumerated subcollection. But because K and B are disjoint, we must have i > N. So {\displaystyle z\in 5C_{i}} for some i > N, and therefore

{\displaystyle Z\subset \bigcup _{n>N}5C_{n}.}

This gives for every N the inequality

{\displaystyle \lambda _{d}^{*}(Z)\leq \sum _{n>N}\lambda _{d}(5C_{n})=5^{d}\sum _{n>N}\lambda _{d}(C_{n}).}

But since the balls of {\displaystyle \mathbf {G} _{r}} are contained in B(r+2), and these balls are disjoint we see

{\displaystyle \sum _{n}\lambda _{d}(C_{n})<\infty .}

Therefore, the term on the right side of the above inequality converges to 0 as N goes to infinity, which shows that Z is negligible as needed.

If the selected subcollection meeting B(r) is finite, take K to be its entire union; the same argument forces Z to be empty. Taking positive integer r covers the whole uncovered set.
