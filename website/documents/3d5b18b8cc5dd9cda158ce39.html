Statement

Let A be a finite-dimensional Jordan algebra over a field F of characteristic different from 2: its bilinear multiplication is commutative and satisfies (xx)(xy)=x((xx)y). Let e\in A satisfy e^2=e, and put A_\lambda(e)=\{x\in A:ex=\lambda x\}. Then A=A_0(e)\oplus A_{1/2}(e)\oplus A_1(e).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A Jordan algebra is explicitly a vector space with bilinear commutative multiplication satisfying the displayed identity. Multiplication by the idempotent is a linear map, and the asserted pieces are its eigenspaces. This is a small extension of ordinary eigenspace decomposition.

Proof 1: Spectral consequence of the idempotent multiplication identity

Let R:A\to A be multiplication by e. Use the source-stated idempotent multiplication identity R(2R-I)(R-I)=0, where I is the identity operator. Thus the only possible eigenvalues of R are 0,1/2,1. These roots are distinct in characteristic different from 2, so the finite-dimensional operator is diagonalizable by the square-free annihilating-polynomial criterion. Its eigenspaces give A=A_0(e)\oplus A_{1/2}(e)\oplus A_1(e).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the supplied polynomial identity to multiplication by the idempotent and use its three distinct roots to diagonalize. Undergraduate linear algebra and bilinear multiplication suffice with the locally defined Jordan algebra as a small extension.
