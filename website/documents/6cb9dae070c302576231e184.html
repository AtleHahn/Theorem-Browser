Statement

Let G be a sigma-compact locally compact unimodular group with bi-invariant Haar measure \mu. Let 1\le P,Q<\infty and 1<R\le\infty satisfy 1/P+1/Q=1+1/R. If F\in L^P(G) and H\in L^Q(G) are real or complex measurable functions, then

\|F*H\|_R\le\|F\|_P\|H\|_Q,\qquad (F*H)(x)=\int_G F(y)H(y^{-1}x)\,d\mu(y).

Here \|u\|_s=(\int_G|u|^s\,d\mu)^{1/s} for finite s, and \|u\|_\infty is the essential supremum.

Proof 1: Three-factor Hölder argument and norm duality — outline

Trilinear form

Let G be a sigma-compact locally compact unimodular group with bi-invariant Haar measure \mu. Let 1\le p,q,r<\infty satisfy 1/p+1/q+1/r=2. For measurable nonnegative functions f\in L^p(G), g\in L^q(G), and h\in L^r(G) that are also integrable,

\int_G\int_G f(x)g(y^{-1}x)h(y)\,d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y)\le\|f\|_p\|g\|_q\|h\|_r.

The norms are \|u\|_s=(\int_G|u|^s\,d\mu)^{1/s}. A factor with exponent zero in the Hölder product is omitted.

We assume that the functions {\displaystyle f,g,h:G\to \mathbb {R} } are nonnegative and integrable, where {\displaystyle G} is a {\displaystyle \sigma }-compact unimodular group endowed with a bi-invariant {\displaystyle \sigma }-finite Haar measure {\displaystyle \mu .} We use the fact that {\displaystyle \mu (S)=\mu (S^{-1})} for any measurable {\displaystyle S\subseteq G.} Since {\textstyle p(2-{\tfrac {1}{q}}-{\tfrac {1}{r}})=q(2-{\tfrac {1}{p}}-{\tfrac {1}{r}})=r(2-{\tfrac {1}{p}}-{\tfrac {1}{q}})=1}

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}&\int _{G}\int _{G}f(x)g(y^{-1}x)h(y)\,\mathrm {d} \mu (x)\,\mathrm {d} \mu (y)\\={}&\int _{G}\int _{G}\left(f(x)^{p}g(y^{-1}x)^{q}\right)^{1-{\frac {1}{r}}}\left(f(x)^{p}h(y)^{r}\right)^{1-{\frac {1}{q}}}\left(g(y^{-1}x)^{q}h(y)^{r}\right)^{1-{\frac {1}{p}}}\,\mathrm {d} \mu (x)\,\mathrm {d} \mu (y)\end{aligned}}}

By the Hölder inequality for three functions we deduce that

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}&\int _{G}\int _{G}f(x)g(y^{-1}x)h(y)\,\mathrm {d} \mu (x)\,\mathrm {d} \mu (y)\\&\leq \left(\int _{G}\int _{G}f(x)^{p}g(y^{-1}x)^{q}\,\mathrm {d} \mu (x)\,\mathrm {d} \mu (y)\right)^{1-{\frac {1}{r}}}\left(\int _{G}\int _{G}f(x)^{p}h(y)^{r}\,\mathrm {d} \mu (x)\,\mathrm {d} \mu (y)\right)^{1-{\frac {1}{q}}}\left(\int _{G}\int _{G}g(y^{-1}x)^{q}h(y)^{r}\,\mathrm {d} \mu (x)\,\mathrm {d} \mu (y)\right)^{1-{\frac {1}{p}}}.\end{aligned}}}

The conclusion follows then by left-invariance of the Haar measure, the fact that integrals are preserved by inversion of the domain, and by Fubini's theorem.

Equivalent norm form

The source identifies the trilinear inequality with the convolution norm inequality. The background used for this equivalence is L^R norm duality with conjugate exponent R'=R/(R-1) (and R'=1 when R=\infty), together with taking absolute values and truncation/monotone convergence. In the trilinear formula, the test function is f with exponent p=R', while g=|H|, q=Q, and h=|F|, r=P.
