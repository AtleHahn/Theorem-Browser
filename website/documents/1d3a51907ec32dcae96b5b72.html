Statement

For a complex topological vector space X and complex-linear F:X\to\mathbb C, F is continuous if and only if \operatorname{Re}F is continuous.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A topological vector space adds continuity of addition and scalar multiplication to undergraduate topology and linear algebra. Complex linear functionals and their real parts then have their ordinary meanings; no dual topology or functional-analytic structure theorem is needed.

Proof 1: Continuity of a complex functional and its real part — outline

Every linear functional F:X\to\mathbb C on a complex vector space is completely determined by its real part through the formula

F(x)=\operatorname{Re}F(x)-i\operatorname{Re}F(ix) \qquad\text{for all }x\in X.

A linear functional F on a topological vector space is continuous if and only if this is true of its real part \operatorname{Re}F. Here continuity in the reverse direction follows from the displayed formula and continuity of x\mapsto ix; in the forward direction it follows by taking real parts.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use F(x)=Re F(x)-i Re F(ix). Continuity follows by composition and addition, with multiplication by i continuous by the topological-vector-space definition. This adds only a compatibility definition to basic topology and linear algebra.
