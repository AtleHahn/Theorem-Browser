Statement

For n\geq1, the nth positive integer that is not of the form m(m+1), m\geq1, is n+\lfloor\sqrt{n-1}\rfloor.

Proof 1: Apply the counting inverse to squares

Use the Lambek–Moser theorem: if f is a nondecreasing unbounded function from positive integers to nonnegative integers and f^*(n) is the number of positive integers x for which f(x)<n, then F(n)=f(n)+n and F^*(n)=f^*(n)+n give the nth members of two complementary sets of positive integers.

Let f(n)=n^2, the function that squares its argument. Then its inverse is the square root function, whose closest integer approximation (in the sense used for the Lambek–Moser theorem) is

f^*(n)=\lfloor\sqrt{n-1}\rfloor.

These two functions give F(n)=n^2+n and F^*(n)=\lfloor\sqrt{n-1}\rfloor+n. For n=1,2,3,\dots, the values of F are the pronic numbers

2,\ 6,\ 12,\ 20,\ 30,\ 42,\ 56,\ 72,\ 90,\ 110,\ \dots,

while the values of F^* are

1,\ 3,\ 4,\ 5,\ 7,\ 8,\ 9,\ 10,\ 11,\ 13,\ 14,\ \dots.

These two sequences are complementary: each positive integer belongs to exactly one of them.
