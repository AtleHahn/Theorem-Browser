Statement

Every finite simple group is isomorphic to one of the groups listed below. A group is simple if it is nontrivial and has no normal subgroups other than itself and the identity subgroup.

  • A cyclic group C_p of prime order p.
  • An alternating group \operatorname{Alt}(n), the group of even permutations on n letters, with n\geq5.
  • A group in the following families of finite simple groups of Lie type, or the Tits group {}^2F_4(2)':\begin{gathered} A_n(q)\ (n\geq1),\quad B_n(q)\ (n\geq2),\quad C_n(q)\ (n\geq3),\quad D_n(q)\ (n\geq4),\\ E_6(q),\ E_7(q),\ E_8(q),\ F_4(q),\ G_2(q),\\ {}^2A_n(q^2)\ (n\geq2),\quad {}^2D_n(q^2)\ (n\geq4),\quad {}^2E_6(q^2),\quad {}^3D_4(q^3),\\ {}^2B_2(2^{2m+1}),\quad {}^2F_4(2^{2m+1}),\quad {}^2G_2(3^{2m+1})\quad(m\geq1). \end{gathered}Here q ranges over prime powers. The nonsimple members A_1(2), A_1(3), B_2(2), G_2(2) and {}^2A_2(2^2) are excluded. The symbols denote the standard finite groups of Chevalley, Steinberg, Suzuki and Ree type in their simple-group conventions; the superscript on a twisted type denotes the twisting, and the displayed field argument uses the q^2 or q^3 convention. A prime on a group denotes its commutator subgroup; in particular {}^2F_4(2)' is the Tits group.
  • One of the twenty-six sporadic simple groups:\begin{gathered} M_{11},M_{12},M_{22},M_{23},M_{24},\quad J_1,J_2,J_3,J_4,\\ \mathrm{Co}_1,\mathrm{Co}_2,\mathrm{Co}_3,\quad \mathrm{Fi}_{22},\mathrm{Fi}_{23},\mathrm{Fi}_{24}',\\ \mathrm{HS},\mathrm{McL},\mathrm{He},\mathrm{Ru},\mathrm{Suz},\mathrm{O'N},\mathrm{HN},\mathrm{Ly},\mathrm{Th},\mathrm{B},\mathrm{M}. \end{gathered}These are respectively the Mathieu, Janko, Conway and Fischer groups, followed by the Higman–Sims, McLaughlin, Held, Rudvalis, Suzuki, O'Nan, Harada–Norton, Lyons, Thompson, Baby Monster and Monster groups.

The list allows coincidences between differently named groups.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Although simplicity itself is undergraduate algebra, understanding all named Lie-type families, twists and sporadic groups in the complete classification cumulatively exceeds the conservative undergraduate reading allowance.
