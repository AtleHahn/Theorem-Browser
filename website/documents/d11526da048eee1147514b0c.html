Statement

Let X,Y,Z be real normed vector spaces, B:X\times Y\to Z a continuous bilinear map, and f:\mathbb R\to X, g:\mathbb R\to Y differentiable at x. Then x\mapsto B(f(x),g(x)) is differentiable there with derivative B(f'(x),g(x))+B(f(x),g'(x)).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra and analysis supply bilinearity, norms, derivatives, sequences and closure. Banach completeness or boundedness is a direct metric condition, and the maps are explicitly given. No weak topology, duality or spectral theory is needed to understand these assertions.

Proof 1: Split the bilinear difference quotient

Let B:X\times Y\to Z be a continuous bilinear map between vector spaces, and let f and g be differentiable functions into X and Y, respectively. The only properties of multiplication used in the proof using the limit definition of derivative are that multiplication is continuous and bilinear.

Let h(x)=B(f(x),g(x)). As in that proof, add B(f(x),g(x+\Delta x))-B(f(x),g(x+\Delta x)), which is zero, to the numerator to permit its factoring, and then use continuity:

\begin{aligned} \lim_{\Delta x\to0}\frac{h(x+\Delta x)-h(x)}{\Delta x} &=\lim_{\Delta x\to0} \frac{B(f(x+\Delta x),g(x+\Delta x))-B(f(x),g(x))}{\Delta x}\\ &=\lim_{\Delta x\to0} \frac{B(f(x+\Delta x),g(x+\Delta x))-B(f(x),g(x+\Delta x)) +B(f(x),g(x+\Delta x))-B(f(x),g(x))}{\Delta x}\\ &=\lim_{\Delta x\to0} \frac{B(f(x+\Delta x)-f(x),g(x+\Delta x)) +B(f(x),g(x+\Delta x)-g(x))}{\Delta x}\\ &=B\left(\lim_{\Delta x\to0}\frac{f(x+\Delta x)-f(x)}{\Delta x}, \lim_{\Delta x\to0}g(x+\Delta x)\right) +B\left(f(x),\lim_{\Delta x\to0}\frac{g(x+\Delta x)-g(x)}{\Delta x}\right)\\ &=B(f'(x),g(x))+B(f(x),g'(x)). \end{aligned}

The fact that \lim_{\Delta x\to0}g(x+\Delta x)=g(x) follows from the fact that differentiable functions are continuous. Thus h is differentiable at x with the stated derivative.
