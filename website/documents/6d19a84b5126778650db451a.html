Statement

For real random variables X,Y on a probability space with finite second moments, |\mathbb E(XY)|^2\le\mathbb E(X^2)\mathbb E(Y^2).

Proof 1: Use the expectation inner product — outline

After defining an inner product on the square-integrable real random variables modulo almost-sure equality using the expectation of their product,

{\displaystyle \langle X,Y\rangle :=\operatorname {E} (XY),}

the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality becomes

{\displaystyle {\bigl |}\operatorname {E} (XY){\bigr |}^{2}\leq \operatorname {E} (X^{2})\operatorname {E} (Y^{2}).}
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Form the inner-product space of general square-integrable random variables modulo almost-sure equality and apply abstract Cauchy-Schwarz. The actual proof invokes the measure-theoretic L2 quotient construction rather than a finite or elementary quadratic expectation argument; retain beyond undergraduate provisionally because that prerequisite is not developed locally.
