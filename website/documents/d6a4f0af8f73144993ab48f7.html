Statement

Let U\subseteq\mathbb C be open, and let f:U\to\mathbb C be holomorphic (complex differentiable at every point of U). If a smooth closed curve \gamma:[a,b]\to U is homotopic within U to a constant curve, then

\int_\gamma f(z)\,dz=0.

Here the integral is \int_a^b f(\gamma(t))\gamma'(t)\,dt. A homotopy to a constant curve is a smooth deformation H:[0,1]\times[a,b]\to U through closed curves, with H(0,t)=\gamma(t), H(1,t)=p for some p\in U, and H(s,a)=H(s,b). In particular the conclusion holds for every smooth closed curve in a simply connected open set, since every such loop contracts there.

Proof 1: General Cauchy formula and winding numbers (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Volker Runde and John C. Bowman, Math 411: Honours Complex Variables, Chapter 11, Theorem 11.2 and its proof, printed p.70 / PDF page 70; supporting Theorem 11.1, printed pp.68–70).

To pass from the source's homology hypothesis to the statement above, fix w\notin U. The winding number

n(H(s,\cdot),w)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_a^b\frac{\partial_tH(s,t)}{H(s,t)-w}\,dt

is integer-valued and continuous in s: the smooth homotopy has compact image avoiding w, so the denominator stays bounded away from zero. It is therefore constant, and equals zero at the constant loop. Thus \gamma has winding number zero about every point outside U, which is the source's definition of being homologous to zero. Theorem 11.2 applies, including the simply connected specialization. This explanatory bridge is supplied here; it is not part of the cited proof.

Original Wikipedia article
Show proof availability notes

The original Wikipedia article gives a Green's-theorem argument for a simple contour enclosing a region on which the real and imaginary parts are continuously differentiable. It does not give the reduction needed for arbitrary null-homotopic curves. That special-case argument and the simply connected specialization remain in their existing dedicated entries; no full proof of the statement above is retained here.

The article's Discussion section also asserts a boundary version allowing any rectifiable simple closed curve in \overline U, with U simply connected and f holomorphic on U and continuous on \overline U. This assertion is false as stated and has been removed from this entry's Statement. For a counterexample, take U=\{re^{i\theta}:r>1,\ -\pi<\theta<\pi\} and f(z)=1/z. The logarithm maps U homeomorphically onto a convex strip, so U is simply connected, and f extends continuously to \overline U=\{z:|z|\geq1\}. Nevertheless, the positively oriented unit circle lies in \overline U and has integral 2\pi i. Simple connectivity of U does not ensure that a loop in its closure encloses a region contained in U. This error is present in the archived Wikipedia article; it does not affect the open-domain theorem stated above.
