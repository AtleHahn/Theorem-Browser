Statement

Let U\subseteq\mathbb C be open, and let f:U\to\mathbb C be holomorphic (complex differentiable at every point of U). If a smooth closed curve \gamma:[a,b]\to U is homotopic within U to a constant curve, then

\int_\gamma f(z)\,dz=0.

Here the integral is \int_a^b f(\gamma(t))\gamma'(t)\,dt. A homotopy to a constant curve is a smooth deformation H:[0,1]\times[a,b]\to U through closed curves, with H(0,t)=\gamma(t), H(1,t)=p for some p\in U, and H(s,a)=H(s,b). In particular the conclusion holds for every smooth closed curve in a simply connected open set, since every such loop contracts there.

The boundary formulation also holds: if U\subseteq\mathbb C is simply connected and open, f is holomorphic on U and extends continuously to \overline U, and \gamma is a rectifiable simple closed curve in \overline U, then \int_\gamma f(z)\,dz=0. Rectifiable means finite length; simple means no self-intersections apart from the coinciding endpoints.

Proof availability

The article gives a Green's-theorem argument for a simple contour enclosing a region on which the real and imaginary parts are continuously differentiable. It does not give the reduction needed for arbitrary null-homotopic curves or the rectifiable boundary formulation. That special-case argument and the simply connected specialization remain in their existing dedicated entries; no full proof of the statement above is retained here.
