Statement

A simple undirected graph has a vertex set V and edges that are unordered pairs of distinct vertices. A proper k-coloring is a map V\to\{1,\ldots,k\} assigning different values to adjacent vertices. Assume the axiom of choice and let k\ge1 be an integer. An arbitrary graph G has a proper k-coloring if and only if every finite subgraph does.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The graph and proper-coloring definitions are explicit, and undergraduate sets suffice for infinite graphs. The axiom of choice is an identified selection principle; the finite-to-infinite existence claim needs no proof machinery for comprehension.

Proof 1: Compactness of the space of color assignments — outline

A proper coloring restricts to each finite subgraph. Conversely:

Suppose that, for the given graph {\displaystyle G}, every finite subgraph is {\displaystyle k}-colorable, and let {\displaystyle X} be the space of all assignments of the {\displaystyle k} colors to the vertices of {\displaystyle G} (regardless of whether they form a valid coloring). Then {\displaystyle X} may be given a topology as a product space {\displaystyle k^{V(G)}}, with the discrete topology on the color set, where {\displaystyle V(G)} denotes the set of vertices of the graph. By Tychonoff's theorem this topological space is compact. For each finite subgraph {\displaystyle F} of {\displaystyle G}, let {\displaystyle X_{F}} be the subset of {\displaystyle X} consisting of assignments of colors that validly color {\displaystyle F}. Then the system of sets {\displaystyle X_{F}} is a family of closed sets with the finite intersection property, so by compactness it has a nonempty intersection. Every member of this intersection is a valid coloring of {\displaystyle G}.

Proof 2: Logical compactness of finite-color inequality structures — outline

The graph-coloring assertion is a special case of the compactness theorem of Kurt Gödel, stating that a set of first-order sentences has a model if and only if every finite subset of it has a model. More specifically, the De Bruijn–Erdős theorem can be interpreted as the compactness of the first-order structures whose non-logical values are any finite set of colors and whose only predicate on these values is inequality.

The colorings of finite subgraphs satisfy the finite collections of coloring constraints. Compactness gives a simultaneous coloring of the whole graph. Conversely, a coloring restricts to every finite subgraph.
