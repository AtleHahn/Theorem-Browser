Removing a cut whose formula was introduced by weakening

Statement

Contexts \Gamma,\Delta,\Pi,\Lambda,\Sigma,\Theta are finite multisets of formulas (finite unordered lists, repetitions allowed). A sequent \Gamma\vdash\Delta is a pair of such multisets. A derivation is a finite rooted tree of sequents, whose child labels are premises of the rule at their parent. Concatenation of contexts denotes multiset union. The Cut rule takes \Gamma\vdash\Delta,A and A,\Pi\vdash\Lambda to \Gamma,\Pi\vdash\Delta,\Lambda. Left or right weakening adds one formula to the corresponding context. Given derivations of \Gamma\vdash\Delta,A and \Pi\vdash\Lambda, the cut against A,\Pi\vdash\Lambda obtained by left weakening can be replaced by finitely many weakenings of the second derivation. The endsequent is \Gamma,\Pi\vdash\Delta,\Lambda.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Multisets, finite derivation trees, Cut and weakening are all locally defined. The assertion replaces a tree fragment by adding finitely many listed formulas, requiring only finite symbolic reasoning.

Proof 1: Discard the unused premise and weaken the other

{\displaystyle {\frac {\Gamma \vdash \Delta ,A\qquad {\frac {\Pi \vdash \Lambda }{A,\Pi \vdash \Lambda }}\operatorname {Weak} _{L}}{\Gamma ,\Pi \vdash \Delta ,\Lambda }}\operatorname {Cut} \quad \implies \quad {\frac {\Pi \vdash \Lambda }{\Gamma ,\Pi \vdash \Delta ,\Lambda }}\operatorname {Weak} ^{*}.}

Here {\displaystyle \operatorname {Weak} ^{*}} denotes finitely many applications of left and right weakening. The cut formula {\displaystyle A} is introduced by weakening in the right premiss, so that occurrence of {\displaystyle A} is unused. The left premiss is discarded, and the original endsequent is recovered from {\displaystyle \Pi \vdash \Lambda } by weakening.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Discard the premise supplying the unused cut formula and add its other contexts by finitely many weakenings. The endsequent is identical.
