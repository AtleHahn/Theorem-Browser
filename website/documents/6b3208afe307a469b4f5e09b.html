Statement

For K=\mathbb Q, write (a)=a\mathbb Z=\{ak:k\in\mathbb Z\} for the principal fractional ideal generated by a nonzero rational number a. Let P_4 consist of ideals (u/v) with odd nonzero integers u,v satisfying u\equiv v\pmod4. Let P_{4\infty} impose also u/v>0. Then (3)\in P_4 but (3)\notin P_{4\infty}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Fractional ideals are explicitly sets of integer multiples, and both named classes are defined by signs and integer congruences.

Proof 1: Compare the two integer generators — outline

For instance, taking K to be the rational numbers, the ideal (3) lies in P4 because (3) = (−3) and −3 fits the necessary conditions. But (3) is not in P4∞ since here it is required that the positive generator of the ideal is 1 mod 4, which is not so.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Compare the two possible generators 3 and -3 and their signs and remainders modulo four. Principal fractional ideals are explicitly described as sets of multiples; no ideal theory is needed.
