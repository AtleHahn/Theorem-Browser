Statement

Let p\equiv1\pmod4 be prime, let K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt p), and let h be its ideal class number, the order of its ideal class group. Write its fundamental unit \varepsilon>1 in the real embedding \sqrt p>0 as \varepsilon=(t+u\sqrt p)/2 with integers t,u. Then

h\frac{u}{t}\equiv B_{(p-1)/2}\pmod p,

where the Bernoulli numbers are defined by z/(e^z-1)=\sum_{j\ge0}B_j z^j/j!. Congruence of these rational numbers means equality after reducing their denominators invertible modulo p.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Ideal class groups and fundamental units require algebraic number theory; combined with rational reductions and the Bernoulli convention they exceed a small extension of introductory abstract algebra.
