Statement

Let f:\mathbb N_{>0}\to(0,\infty) be any function, and let

\varphi(q)=\#\{a\in\{1,\ldots,q\}:\gcd(a,q)=1\}

be Euler’s totient function. For Lebesgue-almost every real number \alpha, the inequality

\left|\alpha-\frac pq\right|<\frac{f(q)}q

has infinitely many solutions in integers p,q with q>0 and \gcd(p,q)=1 if and only if

\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi(q)f(q)}q=\infty.

Thus divergence gives infinitely many reduced rational approximations for almost every \alpha, while convergence gives only finitely many for almost every \alpha.
