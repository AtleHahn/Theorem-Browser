Statement

Let (X_n)_{n\ge1} be independent real random variables with finite means \mu_n and variances \sigma_n^2. If \sum_n\mu_n converges in \mathbb R and \sum_n\sigma_n^2<\infty, then \sum_nX_n converges in \mathbb R almost surely.

Proof 1: Centering and control of tail oscillations

Subtracting the means reduces to \mu_n=0, since the series of means converges. Put S_N=\sum_{n=1}^N X_n. Express the oscillation of the partial sums by

D=\lim_{m\to\infty}\sup_{p,q\ge m}|S_p-S_q|\in[0,\infty].

For every m the tail diameter is bounded by twice the largest displacement from S_m, so

D\le2\sup_{k\ge1}\left|\sum_{i=1}^kX_{m+i}\right|.

Thus, for every m\in\mathbb N and \epsilon>0,

\begin{aligned} \mathbb P(D\geq\epsilon) &\leq\mathbb P\left(2\sup_{k\geq1}\left|\sum_{i=1}^kX_{m+i}\right|\geq\epsilon\right)\\ &=\mathbb P\left(\sup_{k\geq1}\left|\sum_{i=1}^kX_{m+i}\right|\geq\frac\epsilon2\right)\\ &\leq\limsup_{N\to\infty}4\epsilon^{-2}\sum_{i=m+1}^{m+N}\sigma_i^2\\ &=4\epsilon^{-2}\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{i=m+1}^{m+N}\sigma_i^2. \end{aligned}

The second inequality is due to Kolmogorov's maximal inequality, first for finite maxima and then by increasing their length and taking smaller positive thresholds up to \epsilon/2.

By the assumption that \sum_{n=1}^\infty\sigma_n^2 converges, the last term tends to zero as m\to\infty. Taking \epsilon through the positive reciprocals of integers shows D=0 almost surely. Thus the real partial sums are Cauchy almost surely and converge to a finite real limit. Adding back the convergent series of means proves the result.
