Statement

Let v_1,\ldots,v_n be vectors in a finite-dimensional real or complex vector space V, let A\subseteq V, and put t=\frac12\sum_{i=1}^n v_i. The number of subsets I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\} with \sum_{i\in I}v_i\in A equals the number of choices \varepsilon_i\in\{-\tfrac12,\tfrac12\} with \sum_i\varepsilon_i v_i\in A-t. It also equals the number of choices \delta_i\in\{-1,1\} with \sum_i\delta_i v_i\in2(A-t). For a disk target in \mathbb R^2 or \mathbb C, this last scaling doubles its diameter.

Proof 1: Subtract half the total and double the coefficients

Consider the indexed vectors v_1,\ldots,v_n. By subtracting

t=\frac12\sum_{i=1}^n v_i

from each possible subsum (that is, by changing the origin), the problem is equivalent to determining the number of sums of the form

\sum_{i=1}^n\varepsilon_i v_i

that fall in the translated target set A-t, where \varepsilon_i takes the value \tfrac12 or -\tfrac12. The coefficient is \tfrac12 for an index in the subset and -\tfrac12 otherwise.

If the coefficients take values 1 or -1, instead of \tfrac12 or -\tfrac12, the target is 2(A-t) to account for the doubling of the coefficients. In particular, a disk of diameter one is changed to a disk of radius one.

Level justification

Subtract half the total vector sum to obtain coefficients plus/minus one-half and then double. Undergraduate vector addition and scalar multiplication suffice; the correspondence counts subsets, not distinct sum values.
