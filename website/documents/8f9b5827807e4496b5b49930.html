Statement

Let V be a Hausdorff locally convex topological vector space over \mathbb R or \mathbb C, and let X\subseteq V be nonempty, compact and convex. Every continuous map f:X\to X has a fixed point: there exists x\in X with f(x)=x.

A topological vector space has continuous vector addition and scalar multiplication. Locally convex means that zero has a neighborhood basis of convex sets. Hausdorff means that distinct points have disjoint neighborhoods. Convexity means that (1-t)x+ty belongs to the set whenever x,y do and 0\leq t\leq1 is real. Compactness means that every open cover has a finite subcover, and X carries the subspace topology.
