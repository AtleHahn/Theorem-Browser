Statement

Let n\geq1 be an integer, and let V\subset\mathbb R^n be a compact region with piecewise smooth boundary \partial V. Let F=(F_1,\ldots,F_n) be a continuously differentiable vector field on an open neighborhood of V. With \nu the outward unit normal, defined at almost every boundary point, the divergence theorem states

\int_V\operatorname{div}F\,dV=\int_{\partial V}F\cdot\nu\,dS,\qquad \operatorname{div}F=\sum_{i=1}^n\frac{\partial F_i}{\partial x_i}.

Here dV is n-dimensional volume measure and dS is (n-1)-dimensional surface measure. The right side is the outward flux of F through the boundary.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement extends the standard multivariable-calculus flux formula to arbitrary finite dimension; the objects are coordinate vectors, derivatives and volume and surface integrals, a small extension of undergraduate calculus.

Proof 1: Local flux identities and seam cutoffs (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (§15.1 smooth local proof; §15.2, Theorem 15.9 and §15.2.2 cutoff construction, pp.136–143.).

For the usual finite piecewise smooth decomposition, the seams have dimension at most n-2, so their \varepsilon-neighborhoods have volume O(\varepsilon^2). They satisfy the negligibility condition of Theorem 15.9. Its cutoff \psi_\varepsilon is one near the seams and has \int|\nabla\psi_\varepsilon|\to0. Apply the smooth local identity to (1-\psi_\varepsilon)F. The extra gradient term is bounded by \sup|F|\int|\nabla\psi_\varepsilon| and tends to zero; the face integrals converge by dominated convergence. In dimension one, use the fundamental theorem of calculus on the interval components.
