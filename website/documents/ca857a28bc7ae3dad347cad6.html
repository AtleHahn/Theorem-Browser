The normal-mean example realizes a sharp information bound on unbiased estimation. It connects sample size and observational noise with a limit on inferential precision, within the specified statistical model.

Statement

For n\ge1 independent observations with distribution N(\theta,\sigma^2), where \sigma^2>0 is known, the sample Fisher information for the unknown mean \theta\in\mathbb R is I(\theta)=n/\sigma^2. Every unbiased estimator to which the scalar Cramér–Rao regularity conditions apply has variance at least \sigma^2/n.

Level justification

Proof 1: Substitute a common mean and constant normal covariance

For a normal observation vector with differentiable mean \mu(\theta) and covariance C(\theta), use the Fisher information formula

I(\theta)=\mu'(\theta)^TC^{-1}\mu'(\theta) +\frac12\operatorname{tr}(C^{-1}C' C^{-1}C'),

where \operatorname{tr} is the trace.

For the sample of n independent observations with unknown mean \theta and known variance \sigma^2, the observation vector has distribution

N_n(\theta\mathbf1,\sigma^2I_n),

where \mathbf1 has every entry equal to one. Then the Fisher information is a scalar given by

I(\theta) =\left(\frac{\partial\mu(\theta)}{\partial\theta}\right)^T C^{-1}\left(\frac{\partial\mu(\theta)}{\partial\theta}\right) =\sum_{i=1}^n\frac1{\sigma^2} =\frac n{\sigma^2},

since C=\sigma^2I_n is constant. Thus the scalar Cramér–Rao bound for an unbiased estimator satisfying its regularity conditions is

\operatorname{Var}(\hat\theta)\geq\frac{\sigma^2}{n}.
