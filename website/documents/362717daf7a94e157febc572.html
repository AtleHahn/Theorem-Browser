Statement

Let X be a homology n-sphere, for an integer n\geq1: an n-dimensional topological manifold whose singular homology groups with integer coefficients are

H_0(X;\mathbb Z)\cong H_n(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z,\qquad H_i(X;\mathbb Z)=0\quad(i\ne0,n).

For a topological space Y, let SY denote its unreduced suspension, obtained from Y\times[0,1] by collapsing Y\times\{0\} to one point and Y\times\{1\} to another point. Then

S(SX)\cong S^{n+2}

by a homeomorphism, where S^{n+2} is the usual (n+2)-dimensional sphere.

Review notes

  • The classical double-suspension theorem turns every homology sphere into a topological sphere after two suspensions.

Proof 1: Cell-like approximation of the double suspension (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Bourbaki exposé 515, pp.169–186; Section 2 approximation theorem and Section 3 proof, pp.172–175 (PDF pages 4–7).).

For n=1,2, classification of closed one- and two-dimensional manifolds identifies X with S^n, and suspending twice gives S^{n+2}. For n\ge3, use the cell-like approximation construction in Section 3. Under the usual manifold conventions, the nonzero top integral homology forces compactness and orientability.
