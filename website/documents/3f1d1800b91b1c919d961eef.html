Statement

Let M,N be Hermitian positive definite complex n\times n matrices. Their entrywise product (M\circ N)_{ij}=M_{ij}N_{ij} is positive definite.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Positive definite matrices; Hermitian matrices; Hadamard product. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Trace of a squared matrix norm

For any matrices {\displaystyle M} and {\displaystyle N}, the Hadamard product {\displaystyle M\circ N} considered as a sesquilinear form acts on vectors {\displaystyle a,b} as

{\displaystyle a^{*}(M\circ N)b=\operatorname {tr} \left(M^{\textsf {T}}\operatorname {diag} \left(a^{*}\right)N\operatorname {diag} (b)\right)}

where {\displaystyle \operatorname {tr} } is the matrix trace and {\displaystyle \operatorname {diag} (a)} is the diagonal matrix having as diagonal entries the elements of {\displaystyle a}.

Suppose {\displaystyle M} and {\displaystyle N} are positive definite, and so Hermitian. We can consider their square-roots {\displaystyle M^{\frac {1}{2}}} and {\displaystyle N^{\frac {1}{2}}}, which are also Hermitian, and write

{\displaystyle \operatorname {tr} \left(M^{\textsf {T}}\operatorname {diag} \left(a^{*}\right)N\operatorname {diag} (b)\right)=\operatorname {tr} \left({\overline {M}}^{\frac {1}{2}}{\overline {M}}^{\frac {1}{2}}\operatorname {diag} \left(a^{*}\right)N^{\frac {1}{2}}N^{\frac {1}{2}}\operatorname {diag} (b)\right)=\operatorname {tr} \left({\overline {M}}^{\frac {1}{2}}\operatorname {diag} \left(a^{*}\right)N^{\frac {1}{2}}N^{\frac {1}{2}}\operatorname {diag} (b){\overline {M}}^{\frac {1}{2}}\right)}

Then, for {\displaystyle a=b}, this is written as {\displaystyle \operatorname {tr} \left(A^{*}A\right)} for {\displaystyle A=N^{\frac {1}{2}}\operatorname {diag} (a){\overline {M}}^{\frac {1}{2}}} and thus is strictly positive for {\displaystyle A\neq 0}, which occurs if and only if {\displaystyle a\neq 0}. This shows that {\displaystyle (M\circ N)} is a positive definite matrix.
