Statement

For square matrices A,B\in R^{n\times n} over a commutative ring with identity, \det(AB)=\det(A)\det(B).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory group/ring theory and linear algebra supply the objects. The extra Sylow, normalizer, invariant-polynomial or pseudoreflection condition is explicitly defined in subgroup, coordinate or polynomial terms; no specialist extension or representation theory is required.

Proof 1: Only the full subset contributes

Let R be a commutative ring with identity, let m,n\ge0 be integers, and let A\in R^{m\times n} and B\in R^{n\times m}. Write [n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}. For an m-element subset S\subseteq[n], A_{[m],S} and B_{S,[m]} select the indicated columns and rows in increasing order. Empty determinants are 1 and empty sums are 0. The external Cauchy–Binet identity is \det(AB)=\sum_{\substack{S\subseteq[n]\\|S|=m}}\det(A_{[m],S})\det(B_{S,[m]}). When m=n, the only subset of size n is [n]. Thus the sum has one term, \det(A)\det(B).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Specialize the explicitly stated Cauchy-Binet formula to equal dimensions, leaving one subset. Undergraduate determinants and commutative-ring arithmetic suffice.
