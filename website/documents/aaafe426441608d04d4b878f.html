Statement

Let a\in\mathbb R\setminus\mathbb Q. The fractional parts \{ka\}, k=1,2,\ldots, are equidistributed in [0,1): for 0\le u<v\le1, \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\#\{1\le k\le N:u\le\{ka\}<v\}}{N}=v-u.

Proof 1: Weyl criterion and geometric sums — outline

Use Weyl’s criterion: for a real sequence (x_k), equidistribution of its fractional parts is equivalent to

\frac1N\sum_{k=1}^N e^{2\pi i m x_k}\longrightarrow0\qquad(m\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}).

For the sequence of multiples of a, this reduces to summing finite geometric series. The geometric-series prerequisite is \sum_{k=1}^N z^k=z(1-z^N)/(1-z) for z\ne1.

Level justification

Use the supplied exponential-average criterion and sum the finite geometric progression. Irrationality makes its denominator nonzero, while its bounded numerator divided by N tends to zero. Elementary complex arithmetic and real limits suffice.
