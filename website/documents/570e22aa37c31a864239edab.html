A monoid homomorphism criterion from its image

Statement

A semigroup is a set with an associative operation xy; a monoid is a semigroup with a two-sided identity. A homomorphism satisfies f(xy)=f(x)f(y). Let f:S_0\to S_1 be a semigroup homomorphism between monoids with identities e_0,e_1. If e_1\in f(S_0), then f(e_0)=e_1. In particular every surjective semigroup homomorphism between monoids preserves identity.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Semigroups and monoids are direct variants of group axioms, and their definitions are supplied. Congruences, fibers and quotients use familiar equivalence relations and multiplication compatibility, so no specialist semigroup theory is required.

Proof 1: Use the identity of the image — outline

The image of {\displaystyle f} is also a semigroup. If {\displaystyle S_{0}} is a monoid with an identity element {\displaystyle e_{0}}, then {\displaystyle f(e_{0})} is the identity element in the image of {\displaystyle f}. If {\displaystyle S_{1}} is also a monoid with an identity element {\displaystyle e_{1}} and {\displaystyle e_{1}} belongs to the image of {\displaystyle f}, then {\displaystyle f(e_{0})=e_{1}}, i.e. {\displaystyle f} is a monoid homomorphism. Particularly, if {\displaystyle f} is surjective, then it is a monoid homomorphism.

The identity of a semigroup is unique.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Recognize f(e0) as the identity of the image and use uniqueness when the target identity is also in that image. Introductory undergraduate monoids and homomorphisms suffice.
