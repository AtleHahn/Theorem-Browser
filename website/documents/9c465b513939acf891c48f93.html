Statement

In a connected plane graph, a simple cycle drawn in its geometric dual separates the primal vertices into those inside and outside it. The primal edges crossed by that dual cycle are exactly the edges joining the two sets. A plane graph is a planar graph with a fixed crossing-free drawing; its geometric dual has a vertex in each face and a dual edge crossing each primal edge once, with no other crossings between primal and dual edges.

Proof 1: A simple dual cycle determines a primal edge cut (outline)

The drawn dual cycle is a simple closed curve crossing precisely the primal edges corresponding to its dual edges. Primal vertices lie off this curve. The Jordan separation property places the endpoints of each crossed primal edge on opposite sides, while an uncrossed primal edge stays on one side. Thus the crossed edges form the cut.
