Statement

For real x\ge-1 and 0\le r\le1, (1+x)^r\le1+rx, with 0^0=1.

Proof 1: Weighted arithmetic–geometric mean

Let {\displaystyle \lambda _{1},\lambda _{2}} be two non-negative real constants with \lambda_1+\lambda_2>0. By weighted AM-GM on {\displaystyle 1,1+x} with weights {\displaystyle \lambda _{1},\lambda _{2}} respectively, we get

{\displaystyle {\dfrac {\lambda _{1}\cdot 1+\lambda _{2}\cdot (1+x)}{\lambda _{1}+\lambda _{2}}}\geq {\sqrt[{\lambda _{1}+\lambda _{2}}]{(1+x)^{\lambda _{2}}}}.}

Note that

{\displaystyle {\dfrac {\lambda _{1}\cdot 1+\lambda _{2}\cdot (1+x)}{\lambda _{1}+\lambda _{2}}}={\dfrac {\lambda _{1}+\lambda _{2}+\lambda _{2}x}{\lambda _{1}+\lambda _{2}}}=1+{\dfrac {\lambda _{2}}{\lambda _{1}+\lambda _{2}}}x}

and

{\displaystyle {\sqrt[{\lambda _{1}+\lambda _{2}}]{(1+x)^{\lambda _{2}}}}=(1+x)^{\frac {\lambda _{2}}{\lambda _{1}+\lambda _{2}}},}

so our inequality is equivalent to

{\displaystyle 1+{\dfrac {\lambda _{2}}{\lambda _{1}+\lambda _{2}}}x\geq (1+x)^{\frac {\lambda _{2}}{\lambda _{1}+\lambda _{2}}}.}

After substituting {\displaystyle r={\dfrac {\lambda _{2}}{\lambda _{1}+\lambda _{2}}}} (bearing in mind that this implies {\displaystyle 0\leq r\leq 1}) our inequality turns into

{\displaystyle 1+rx\geq (1+x)^{r}}

which is Bernoulli's inequality for {\displaystyle 0\leq r\leq 1}.

Every r\in[0,1] is obtained by taking \lambda_1=1-r, \lambda_2=r. Zero weights and the zero base are interpreted with the stated zeroth-power convention.

The real exponential/power expressions and the zero conventions are explicit; no limit argument or proof of weighted AM–GM is needed.

Proof 2: Reciprocal exponent from the local convexity inequality

Local inequality for exponents at least one

For real y\ge-1 and R\ge1, (1+y)^R\ge1+Ry.

For y>-1 and y\ne0, the function h(\alpha)=(1+y)^\alpha is strictly convex. At R>1 it lies above the continuation of the secant line through (0,h(0))=(0,1) and (1,h(1))=(1,1+y), giving h(R)>1+Ry. At R=1 or y=0 there is equality. At y=-1, the left side is zero and 1-R\le0.

Reciprocal exponent

The case {\displaystyle 0\leq r\leq 1} can also be derived from the case {\displaystyle R\geq 1} by noting that (using the local inequality above) {\displaystyle \left(1+{\frac {x}{R}}\right)^{R}\geq 1+x=\left[(1+x)^{\frac {1}{R}}\right]^{R}} and by using the fact that {\displaystyle f(x)=x^{R}} is increasing on nonnegative arguments. We can conclude that {\displaystyle 1+x/R\geq (1+x)^{\frac {1}{R}}} for {\displaystyle R\geq 1}, therefore {\displaystyle (1+x)^{r}\leq 1+rx} for {\displaystyle 0<r=1/R\leq 1}. The leftover case {\displaystyle r=0} is equality under the stated convention.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Unlike the direct weighted-AM–GM application, this proof derives its auxiliary inequality from strict convexity and secant-line continuation, then uses monotonicity of real powers and reciprocal exponents. Convexity of the exponential can be checked by a positive second derivative. These are undergraduate calculus and elementary convex-function concepts; no advanced convex analysis is needed.
