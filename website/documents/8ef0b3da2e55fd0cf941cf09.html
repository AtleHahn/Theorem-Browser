Statement

Let P\in\mathbb C[z] have degree n\ge1, let z_0\in\mathbb C, and write P(z+z_0)=\sum_{j=0}^n b_jz^j with b_j=P^{(j)}(z_0)/j!. For a finite complex vector v, write \|v\|_p=(\sum_j|v_j|^p)^{1/p} for 1\le p<\infty and \|v\|_\infty=\max_j|v_j|. For conjugate exponents 1\le p,q\le\infty, with 1/p+1/q=1, put R_p(v)=\|(1,\|v\|_p)\|_q. Thus R_1(v)=\max(1,\|v\|_1) and R_\infty(v)=1+\|v\|_\infty. Every root \zeta of P obeys |\zeta-z_0|\le R_p((b_0/b_n,\ldots,b_{n-1}/b_n)). If P(z_0)\ne0, it also obeys |\zeta-z_0|\ge1/R_p((b_n/b_0,\ldots,b_1/b_0)), for every 1\le p\le\infty.

Proof 1: Shift the variable and apply upper and reciprocal bounds

The difference \zeta-z_0 is a root of P(z+z_0). Its coefficients are the displayed finite Taylor coefficients. Apply the coefficient norm upper bound, and when the constant coefficient b_0=P(z_0) is nonzero apply the reciprocal lower bound.
