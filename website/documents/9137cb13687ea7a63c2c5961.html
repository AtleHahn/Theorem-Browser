Statement

For m,n\ge0, the binomial coefficient \binom mn is odd exactly when every position occupied by a 1 in the binary expansion of n is also occupied by a 1 in the binary expansion of m. Use \binom mn=0 for n>m.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement concerns elementary integer arithmetic and finite binomial coefficients.

Proof 1: Specialize the digit criterion to base two

One consequence of Lucas's theorem is that the binomial coefficient {\displaystyle {\tbinom {m}{n}}} is divisible by the prime p if and only if at least one of the digits of the base-p representation of n is greater than the corresponding digit of m. In particular, {\displaystyle {\tbinom {m}{n}}} is odd if and only if the positions of the ones in the binary expansion of n are a subset of the positions of the ones in that of m.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Set the prime p equal to two in the supplied digit criterion. A binary digit of n exceeds the matching digit of m precisely when it is one and the latter is zero. Divisibility by two is evenness, so the complementary condition is exactly the claimed containment.
