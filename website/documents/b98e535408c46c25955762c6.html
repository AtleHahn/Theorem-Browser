Proof 1: Interleave decimal digits and apply Schröder–Bernstein — outline

A near-bijection from the unit square to the unit interval can be obtained by interleaving the digits of the decimal representations of the Cartesian coordinates of points in the square. To resolve the decimal ambiguity, choose the expansion not eventually equal to 9 for each coordinate less than 1, and represent 1 by 0.999\ldots. The interleaved expansion is not eventually 9 unless both coordinates are 1, so its odd and even digits recover the coordinates. This is an injection rather than a bijection, but this issue can be repaired by using the Schröder–Bernstein theorem, together with the reverse injection t\mapsto(t,0).
