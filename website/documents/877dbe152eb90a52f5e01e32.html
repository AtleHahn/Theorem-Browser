Statement

Let three pairwise externally tangent circles have common curvature k>0. Any fourth circle tangent to all three at distinct tangencies has signed curvature (3\pm2\sqrt3)k, where the enclosing circle has negative curvature.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Congruence and the signed radius convention are elementary geometry.

Proof 1: Equal-curvature substitution

For four mutually tangent finite circles with distinct tangencies, signed curvatures satisfy (k_1+k_2+k_3+k_4)^2=2(k_1^2+k_2^2+k_3^2+k_4^2); an enclosing circle has curvature -1/r, and the other circles have curvature 1/r. Solving for the fourth curvature gives k_4=k_1+k_2+k_3\pm2\sqrt{k_1k_2+k_2k_3+k_3k_1}.

When three of the four circles are congruent, their centers form an equilateral triangle, as do their points of tangency. The two possibilities for a fourth circle tangent to all three are concentric, and the fourth-curvature formula reduces to

{\displaystyle k_{4}=(3\pm 2{\sqrt {3}})k.}

Level justification

Set the first three curvatures equal in the supplied fourth-curvature formula and simplify the square root.
