Proof 1: Two inclusions by membership and contradiction

Here we use {\displaystyle {\overline {A}}} to denote the complement of A, relative to the universe U in the statement. The proof that {\displaystyle {\overline {A\cap B}}={\overline {A}}\cup {\overline {B}}} is completed in 2 steps by proving both {\displaystyle {\overline {A\cap B}}\subseteq {\overline {A}}\cup {\overline {B}}} and {\displaystyle {\overline {A}}\cup {\overline {B}}\subseteq {\overline {A\cap B}}}.

Part 1

Let {\displaystyle x\in {\overline {A\cap B}}}. Then, {\displaystyle x\not \in A\cap B}.

Because {\displaystyle A\cap B=\{\,y\ |\ y\in A\wedge y\in B\,\}}, it must be the case that {\displaystyle x\not \in A} or {\displaystyle x\not \in B}.

If {\displaystyle x\not \in A}, then {\displaystyle x\in {\overline {A}}}, so {\displaystyle x\in {\overline {A}}\cup {\overline {B}}}.

Similarly, if {\displaystyle x\not \in B}, then {\displaystyle x\in {\overline {B}}}, so {\displaystyle x\in {\overline {A}}\cup {\overline {B}}}.

Thus, {\displaystyle \forall x{\Big (}x\in {\overline {A\cap B}}\implies x\in {\overline {A}}\cup {\overline {B}}{\Big )}};

that is, {\displaystyle {\overline {A\cap B}}\subseteq {\overline {A}}\cup {\overline {B}}}.

Part 2

To prove the reverse direction, let {\displaystyle x\in {\overline {A}}\cup {\overline {B}}}, and for contradiction assume {\displaystyle x\not \in {\overline {A\cap B}}}.

Under that assumption, it must be the case that {\displaystyle x\in A\cap B},

so it follows that {\displaystyle x\in A} and {\displaystyle x\in B}, and thus {\displaystyle x\not \in {\overline {A}}} and {\displaystyle x\not \in {\overline {B}}}.

However, that means {\displaystyle x\not \in {\overline {A}}\cup {\overline {B}}}, in contradiction to the hypothesis that {\displaystyle x\in {\overline {A}}\cup {\overline {B}}},

therefore, the assumption {\displaystyle x\not \in {\overline {A\cap B}}} must not be the case, meaning that {\displaystyle x\in {\overline {A\cap B}}}.

Hence, {\displaystyle \forall x{\Big (}x\in {\overline {A}}\cup {\overline {B}}\implies x\in {\overline {A\cap B}}{\Big )}},

that is, {\displaystyle {\overline {A}}\cup {\overline {B}}\subseteq {\overline {A\cap B}}}.
