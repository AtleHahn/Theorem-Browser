Statement

For nonzero complex t, let C_t=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb C^2:xy=t\} and f_t:C_t\to\mathbb C be f_t(x,y)=x+y. There are two branch values of f_t, both tending to 0 as t\to0, the value of f_0 at the node (0,0) of C_0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The curves and maps are explicit equations; branch values are critical values in a local coordinate, a small extension of undergraduate differentiation and complex arithmetic. The node here is the crossing xy=0.

Proof 1: Inspect the smoothing and its sum map — outline

Consider the family of curves xy=t and maps f_t(x,y)=x+y. As t\to0, the two branch values of f_t tend toward the value of f_0 at the node of C_0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

For nonzero t the curve can be parametrized by x nonzero with y=t/x; critical values of x+t/x are plus and minus 2sqrt(t), tending to zero. Undergraduate one-variable complex differentiation and limits suffice with branch value as the locally relevant critical-value definition. The source only outlines this calculation.
