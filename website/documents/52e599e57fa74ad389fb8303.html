Statement

Let X be a real or complex topological vector space with a compact neighborhood C of zero. There is a finite set \{c_1,\ldots,c_n\}\subseteq C whose linear span is dense in X.

Proof 1: Iterate a finite cover by half-neighborhoods — outline

Let C be a compact neighborhood of the origin in X. By compactness, there are c_1,\ldots,c_n\in C such that

C\subseteq\left(c_1+\tfrac12C\right)\cup\cdots\cup\left(c_n+\tfrac12C\right).

We claim that the finite dimensional subspace Y spanned by \{c_1,\ldots,c_n\} is dense in X, or equivalently, its closure is X. Since X is the union of scalar multiples of C, it is sufficient to show that C\subseteq\overline Y. By induction, for every m,

C\subseteq Y+\frac{1}{2^m}C.

But compact sets are bounded, so C lies in the closure of Y. This proves the result.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The finite-cover iteration uses boundedness and absorption in an arbitrary topological vector space, not a normed or metrizable setting. These specialized notions are only implicit, so an undergraduate extension is not confidently justified; retain beyond undergraduate provisionally.
