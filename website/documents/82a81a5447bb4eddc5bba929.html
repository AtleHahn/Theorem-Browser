Statement

For a finite simple graph G=(V,E) with n vertices, a planar framework is a tuple p=(p_v)_{v\in V}\in(\mathbb R^2)^V. Define its rigidity matrix R(p) by (R(p)u)_{ij}=(p_i-p_j)\cdot(u_i-u_j) for each edge ij. Suppose \dim\ker R(p)=3. Add a new vertex v, joined only to two old vertices a,b, and place it so that p_v,p_a,p_b are noncollinear. Then the new rigidity matrix also has kernel dimension three.

Proof 1: Solve the two new independent velocity equations

Adding the vertex at p_v is equivalent to adjoining rows corresponding to the equations

\begin{aligned} (p_v-p_a)\cdot(u_v-u_a)&=0,\\ (p_v-p_b)\cdot(u_v-u_b)&=0 \end{aligned}

to the old rigidity matrix, together with the two columns for the velocity u_v. Here a and b are the neighbors of v, and u_v is the velocity assigned to p_v by an infinitesimal motion.

Our goal is that the dimension of the space of infinitesimal motions of the new framework is the same as that of the old framework. The point p_v is not collinear with p_a and p_b, which ensures that there is exactly one solution u_v to these equations for each old infinitesimal motion. Hence restriction identifies the two motion spaces, and the kernel of the new rigidity matrix has dimension 3.

Level justification

For each old kernel vector, solve the two independent equations for the new velocity. Noncollinearity gives an invertible two-by-two matrix, so restriction is a linear isomorphism. Undergraduate linear algebra suffices; rigidity is explicitly defined by the matrix.
