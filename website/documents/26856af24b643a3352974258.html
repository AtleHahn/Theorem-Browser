Statement

Let n\ge1, let \mathcal X_i be nonempty sets, and let f:\prod_{i=1}^n\mathcal X_i\to\mathbb R. Suppose c_i\ge0 and changing only coordinate i changes f by at most c_i. Equip the sets with measurable structures, assume f is measurable, and let X_i be independent random elements of \mathcal X_i. Put S=\sum_i c_i^2>0 and \mu=\mathbb E f(X_1,\ldots,X_n). For \varepsilon>0, each one-sided deviation probability \mathbb P(f(X)-\mu\ge\varepsilon) and \mathbb P(f(X)-\mu\le-\varepsilon) is at most e^{-2\varepsilon^2/S}, and \mathbb P(|f(X)-\mu|\ge\varepsilon)\le2e^{-2\varepsilon^2/S}. If all c_i=0, f is constant and these deviation probabilities vanish.

Proof 1: Doob conditional expectations and Azuma range bounds

For better readability, we will introduce a notational shorthand: {\displaystyle z_{i\rightharpoondown j}} will denote {\displaystyle z_{i},\dots ,z_{j}} for any {\displaystyle z\in\prod_{k=1}^n{\mathcal X}_k} and integers {\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq j\leq n}, so that, for example,

{\displaystyle f(X_{1\rightharpoondown (i-1)},y,x_{(i+1)\rightharpoondown n}):=f(X_{1},\ldots ,X_{i-1},y,x_{i+1},\ldots ,x_{n}).}

An index block with its lower endpoint greater than its upper endpoint is empty. For n=1, boundedness follows directly from |f(x_1)-f(x'_1)|\le c_1; the following displayed chain applies for n\ge2.

Pick any {\displaystyle x_{1}',x_{2}',\ldots ,x_{n}'}. Then, for any {\displaystyle x_{1},x_{2},\ldots ,x_{n}}, by triangle inequality,

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}&|f(x_{1\rightharpoondown n})-f(x'_{1\rightharpoondown n})|\\[6pt]\leq {}&|f(x_{1\rightharpoondown \,n})-f(x'_{1\rightharpoondown (n-1)},x_{n})|+c_{n}\\\leq {}&|f(x_{1\rightharpoondown n})-f(x'_{1\rightharpoondown (n-2)},x_{(n-1)\rightharpoondown n})|+c_{n-1}+c_{n}\\\leq {}&\ldots \\\leq {}&\sum _{i=1}^{n}c_{i},\end{aligned}}}

and thus {\displaystyle f} is bounded.

Since {\displaystyle f} is bounded, define the Doob martingale {\displaystyle \{Z_{i}\}} (each {\displaystyle Z_{i}} being a random variable depending on the random values of {\displaystyle X_{1},\ldots ,X_{i}}) as

{\displaystyle Z_{i}:=\mathbb {E} [f(X_{1\rightharpoondown n})\mid X_{1\rightharpoondown i}]}

for all 1\le i\le n and {\displaystyle Z_{0}:=\mathbb {E} [f(X_{1\rightharpoondown n})]}, so that {\displaystyle Z_{n}=f(X_{1\rightharpoondown n})}.

For a fixed past a=(x_1,\ldots,x_{i-1}), write

g_i(a,u)=\mathbb E_{X_{i+1},\ldots,X_n} [f(a,u,X_{(i+1)\rightharpoondown n})],\qquad h_i(a)=\mathbb E_{X_i}[g_i(a,X_i)].

Since X_i,\ldots,X_n are independent of each other, these product expectations express the conditional expectations without conditioning on a possibly null event X_i=u. In particular, Z_i=g_i(X_{1\rightharpoondown(i-1)},X_i) and Z_{i-1}=h_i(X_{1\rightharpoondown(i-1)}).

For each fixed past define the numerical bounds

\begin{aligned} U_i(a)&=\sup_{u\in\mathcal X_i}g_i(a,u)-h_i(a),\\ L_i(a)&=\inf_{u\in\mathcal X_i}g_i(a,u)-h_i(a). \end{aligned}

Equivalently,

\begin{aligned} U_i(a)&=\sup_{u\in\mathcal X_i}\mathbb E[ f(a,u,X_{(i+1)\rightharpoondown n})-f(a,X_{i\rightharpoondown n})],\\ L_i(a)&=\inf_{u\in\mathcal X_i}\mathbb E[ f(a,u,X_{(i+1)\rightharpoondown n})-f(a,X_{i\rightharpoondown n})]. \end{aligned}

Note that the conditional range of Z_i-Z_{i-1} is bounded between these two numbers. In addition,

\begin{aligned} U_i(a)-L_i(a) &=\sup_{u,\ell\in\mathcal X_i}\bigl(g_i(a,u)-g_i(a,\ell)\bigr)\\ &=\sup_{u,\ell\in\mathcal X_i}\mathbb E[ f(a,u,X_{(i+1)\rightharpoondown n})- f(a,\ell,X_{(i+1)\rightharpoondown n})]\\ &\leq\sup_{u,\ell\in\mathcal X_i}\mathbb E[c_i]\\ &\leq c_i. \end{aligned}

Only this conditional range bound is needed; no measurability of the pointwise suprema as functions of the past is asserted.

Use the general form of Azuma’s inequality: a martingale whose conditional increment ranges have lengths at most c_i satisfies the upper-tail bound \exp(-2\varepsilon^2/\sum_i c_i^2). Then, applying the general form of Azuma's inequality to {\displaystyle \left\{Z_{i}\right\}}, we have

{\displaystyle {\text{P}}(f(X_{1},\ldots ,X_{n})-\mathbb {E} [f(X_{1},\ldots ,X_{n})]\geq \varepsilon )=\operatorname {P} (Z_{n}-Z_{0}\geq \varepsilon )\leq \exp \left(-{\frac {2\varepsilon ^{2}}{\sum _{i=1}^{n}c_{i}^{2}}}\right).}

The one-sided bound in the other direction is obtained by applying Azuma's inequality to {\displaystyle \left\{-Z_{i}\right\}} and the two-sided bound follows from a union bound. {\displaystyle \square }

If all c_i=0, the coordinate-replacement calculation makes f constant, and the deviation probabilities vanish.
