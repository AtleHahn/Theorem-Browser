Statement

Let \Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^3 be open. There is a constant C>0, independent of u, such that every u\in H^2(\Omega)\cap H_0^1(\Omega) satisfies

\|u\|_{L^\infty(\Omega)}\le C\|u\|_{H^1(\Omega)}^{1/2}\|u\|_{H^2(\Omega)}^{1/2},\qquad \|u\|_{L^\infty(\Omega)}\le C\|u\|_{L^2(\Omega)}^{1/4}\|u\|_{H^2(\Omega)}^{3/4}.

Here H^j(\Omega) consists of the functions whose weak derivatives D^\alpha u of orders |\alpha|\le j lie in L^2(\Omega), with norm (\sum_{|\alpha|\le j}\int_\Omega|D^\alpha u|^2)^{1/2}. A weak derivative satisfies the integration-by-parts identity against every smooth compactly supported test function. H_0^1(\Omega) is the closure in H^1 of smooth compactly supported functions. The L^2 norm is (\int_\Omega |u|^2)^{1/2} and the L^\infty norm is the essential supremum of |u|, with Lebesgue measure throughout.

Proof 1: Pointwise Laplacian estimate and integration by parts (proof outline) (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorem 1, Section 2; Section 3 proof Steps 1–4, PDF pp.2–3 (Bulletin AMS 26 (1992), pp.294–298).). The linked exposition is a proof sketch with the indicated dependencies.

Xie Theorem 1 and Section 3 proof Steps 1–4 apply to homogeneous H_0^1 with distributional Laplacian in L2 on every open subset of R3, hence to the stated H2 intersect H_0^1 class. The bound is ||u||infinity <= (2pi)^(-1/2)||grad u||2^(1/2)||Delta u||2^(1/2). Bounding these by H1 and sqrt(3)H2 proves the first displayed inequality. Weak integration by parts (density in H_0^1) gives ||grad u||2 squared = -Re integral conjugate(u)Delta u <= ||u||2||Delta u||2, which gives the second. Enlarge the constant if treating complex u by real and imaginary parts. No boundary regularity is required.
