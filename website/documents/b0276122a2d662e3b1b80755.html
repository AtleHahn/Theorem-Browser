Statement

Let G be a finite group and let BG be a CW model of its classifying space. Write BG^{(k)} for its k-skeleton and BG^{(k)}_+ for that skeleton with a disjoint basepoint. Let \pi_S^0(Y) denote degree-zero stable cohomotopy of a based space Y, that is, maps from its suspension spectrum to the sphere spectrum in the stable homotopy category, with its usual cohomotopy ring structure.

Let A(G) be the Burnside ring: the Grothendieck group of finite G-sets under disjoint union, with multiplication induced by Cartesian product with diagonal G-action. Its augmentation \epsilon:A(G)\to\mathbb Z sends a finite G-set to its cardinality. Put I=\ker\epsilon and

\widehat{A(G)}_I=\varprojlim_{r\ge1}A(G)/I^r.

There is an isomorphism of rings

\varprojlim_{k\ge0}\pi_S^0(BG^{(k)}_+)\ \cong\ \widehat{A(G)}_I,

where the maps in the left inverse system are restrictions along skeleton inclusions. This is the skeletal, weak formulation of Segal’s conjecture explicitly stated in the source.

Proof 1: Equivariant completion and skeletal limits (external)

A link to the proof outline can be found here (May pp.620–628: Theorem 3, Lemma 4, Theorem 6, Proposition 7, Propositions 8–9 and Theorems 12–16; proof discussion pp.629ff. Caruso–May–Priddy pp.413–433, Sections 1–8.).

Supporting sources: Caruso–May–Priddy, p-adic completion proof.

The linked exposition and p-group proof use the Adams–Gunawardena–Miller Ext calculation as a substantive dependency. May's Theorem 6 and Proposition 7 supply the passage from p-group p-adic completion to finite-group I-adic completion.

Here is the skeletal-limit bridge. Use the finite-type bar model of BG. Each finite skeleton has finite degree-minus-one stable cohomotopy: it is finitely generated and has zero rational part. This inverse system is Mittag–Leffler, so the \varprojlim^1 term in Milnor's exact sequence vanishes. The natural restriction map therefore identifies degree-zero cohomotopy with the skeletal inverse limit, preserving products. For any other CW model, cellular homotopy equivalences preserve skeleta and cellular homotopies shift the filtration by at most one. They induce inverse maps on the pro-systems and hence on their inverse limits.
