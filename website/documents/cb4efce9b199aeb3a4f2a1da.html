Statement

For a convex Euclidean quadrilateral with consecutive side lengths a,b,c,d, diagonal lengths e,f, and distance g between the diagonal midpoints, a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2=e^2+f^2+4g^2.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All quantities are explicitly specified lengths, diagonal midpoints and elementary squares in a convex plane quadrilateral.

Proof 1: Use the auxiliary parallelogram and the diagonal midpoints

For a given convex quadrilateral {\displaystyle ABCD} introduce an additional point {\displaystyle E} such that {\displaystyle ABED} forms a parallelogram and use the following auxiliary identity:

{\displaystyle |AB|^{2}+|BC|^{2}+|CD|^{2}+|AD|^{2}=|AC|^{2}+|BD|^{2}+|CE|^{2}}

Let M,N be the midpoints of AC,BD. If C=E, both CE and MN vanish and the identity immediately gives the result. Otherwise:

{\displaystyle M} being the midpoint of {\displaystyle AC} yields {\displaystyle {\tfrac {|AC|}{|AM|}}=2}. Since {\displaystyle N} is the midpoint of {\displaystyle BD} it is also the midpoint of {\displaystyle AE}, as {\displaystyle AE} and {\displaystyle BD} are both diagonals of the parallelogram {\displaystyle ABED}. This yields {\displaystyle {\tfrac {|AE|}{|AN|}}=2} and hence {\displaystyle {\tfrac {|AC|}{|AM|}}={\tfrac {|AE|}{|AN|}}}. Therefore, it follows from the intercept theorem (and its converse) that {\displaystyle CE} and {\displaystyle NM} are parallel and {\displaystyle |CE|^{2}=(2|NM|)^{2}=4|NM|^{2}}, which yields Euler's theorem.
