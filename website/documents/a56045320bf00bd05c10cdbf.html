Statement

Let M be a compact Kähler manifold of complex dimension n, let L be a positive holomorphic line bundle on M, and let K_M=\bigwedge^n T^{*(1,0)}M be its canonical line bundle. Then

H^q(M,K_M\otimes L)=0\qquad(q>0).

Here a Kähler manifold is a complex manifold with a Hermitian metric whose associated real (1,1)-form is closed. Positivity of L means that it admits a smooth Hermitian metric with positive-definite Chern curvature form. The canonical bundle is the bundle of holomorphic top-degree forms, \otimes is tensor product, and H^q denotes sheaf cohomology of the sheaf of holomorphic sections of the indicated bundle.

In algebraic language, if k is any field of characteristic zero, X is a smooth projective k-scheme of dimension d, and L is an ample invertible sheaf, the canonical-twist assertion is

H^q(X,\Omega^d_{X/k}\otimes L)=0\qquad(q>0).

An invertible sheaf is a locally free sheaf of rank one; ample here means that some positive tensor power defines a projective embedding. The sheaf \Omega^d_{X/k} is the dth exterior power of the sheaf of relative Kähler differentials. Cohomology in this formulation is algebraic sheaf cohomology.

Proof 1: Curvature positivity, harmonic forms and algebraic descent (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Proposition47 and Theorems48 and50, printed pp.24–26).

Apply Theorem48 to E=L^{-1} and form degree zero. For 0<q\le n, Serre duality gives H^q(M,K_M\otimes L)\cong H^{n-q}(M,L^{-1})^*=0; higher degrees vanish by the Dolbeault resolution. Theorem50 transfers the result to characteristic-zero fields. A smooth projective scheme has finitely many disjoint regular components. On a component of dimension d, apply the canonical-twist result; on one of smaller dimension, \Omega^d_{X/k} restricts to zero. Thus the algebraic assertion also covers schemes that are disconnected or not of pure dimension.
