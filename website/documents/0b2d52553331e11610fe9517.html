Statement

For integers m\ge1, n\ge0 and k_i\ge0 with \sum_i k_i=n, write \binom n{k_1,\ldots,k_m}=n!/(k_1!\cdots k_m!), with 0!=1. Then \sum_{k_1+\cdots+k_m=n}\binom n{k_1,\ldots,k_m}=m^n.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement uses polynomial algebra and finite integer factorials.

Proof 1: Set every variable equal to one

The substitution of x_i=1 for all i into the multinomial theorem

\sum_{k_1+\cdots+k_m=n} \binom{n}{k_1,k_2,\ldots,k_m}x_1^{k_1}x_2^{k_2}\cdots x_m^{k_m} =(x_1+x_2+\cdots+x_m)^n

gives immediately that

\sum_{k_1+\cdots+k_m=n}\binom{n}{k_1,k_2,\ldots,k_m}=m^n.
Level
High school only
Level justification

Set every variable equal to one in the stated finite expansion. Elementary algebra and powers suffice.
