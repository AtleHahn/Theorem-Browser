Statement

Define polynomials B_0(x)=1, B_j^{\prime}(x)=jB_{j-1}(x) and \int_0^1B_j(x)\,dx=0 for j\ge1. Write b_j=B_j(1), so b_1=1/2, and P_j(x)=B_j(x-\lfloor x\rfloor). An asymptotic series below means that truncating after any displayed power leaves a remainder of smaller order than that last power. Let H_n=\sum_{k=1}^n1/k and \gamma=\lim_{n\to\infty}(H_n-\log n). Then H_n\sim\log n+\gamma+1/(2n)-\sum_{k\ge1}b_{2k}/(2kn^{2k}) as n\to\infty.

Proof 1: Split the convergent sum-integral difference — outline

For f(x)=1/x, the sum and the integral go to infinity but the difference between them goes to a limit, the Euler–Mascheroni constant, γ ≈ 0.5772... This gives:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\int _{1}^{n}\left({\frac {1}{\lfloor x\rfloor }}-{\frac {1}{x}}\right)dx&=\int _{1}^{\infty }\left({\frac {1}{\lfloor x\rfloor }}-{\frac {1}{x}}\right)dx-\int _{n}^{\infty }\left({\frac {1}{\lfloor x\rfloor }}-{\frac {1}{x}}\right)dx\\&=\gamma -\int _{n}^{\infty }\left({\frac {1}{\lfloor x\rfloor }}-{\frac {1}{x}}\right)dx\\&\approx \gamma -{\frac {1}{2n}}-\sum _{k=1}{\frac {b_{2k}}{2kn^{2k}}}\\\end{aligned}}}

from which we have the asymptotic expansion:

{\displaystyle \sum _{k=1}^{n}{\frac {1}{k}}\sim \ln n+\gamma +{\frac {1}{2n}}-\sum _{k=1}{\frac {b_{2k}}{2kn^{2k}}},}

The expansion uses the Euler–Maclaurin formula. The source leaves the finite-order tail estimates implicit.
