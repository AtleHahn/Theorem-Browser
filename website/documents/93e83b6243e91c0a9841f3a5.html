Statement

Let \sum_{n\ge1}(a_n\cos nx+b_n\sin nx) be a real trigonometric series. If it converges absolutely on a Lebesgue-measurable subset of \mathbb R of positive measure, then it converges absolutely at every real x.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The hypothesis refers to positive Lebesgue measure and absolute convergence of an infinite trigonometric series.

Proof 1: Apply coefficient summability and bounded trigonometry — outline

Use the Denjoy–Luzin coefficient theorem: absolute convergence on a set of positive measure implies \sum_{n\ge1}(|a_n|+|b_n|)<\infty. In particular, the trigonometric series converges absolutely everywhere, by comparison using the bounds |\cos nx|\le1 and |\sin nx|\le1.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The comparison step is basic, but the hypothesis and coefficient theorem concern absolute convergence on an arbitrary Lebesgue-measurable set of positive measure. Retain beyond undergraduate provisionally under the conservative rule for the added measure-theoretic setting.
