Statement

For an integer n\ge1 and square-integrable complex functions f,g on \mathbb R^n with Lebesgue measure, |\int f(x)\overline{g(x)}\,dx|^2\le(\int|f(x)|^2\,dx)(\int|g(x)|^2\,dx). Functions in L^2 are identified when equal almost everywhere.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The hypotheses use measure-theoretic integration and an infinite-dimensional function space.

Proof 1: Specialize the inner-product inequality to L2 — outline

Use the standard L^2 inner product \langle f,g\rangle=\int_{\mathbb R^n}f\overline g. Applying the inner-product Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and substituting \langle f,f\rangle=\int|f|^2 and \langle g,g\rangle=\int|g|^2 gives the displayed integral inequality.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the abstract inner-product inequality after identifying the general Lebesgue L2 inner product on almost-everywhere classes. The full L2 construction and integrability of products require advanced measure theory despite the elementary final substitution.
