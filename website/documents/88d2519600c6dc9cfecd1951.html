Statement

Let I=(a,b) be a nonempty open interval and f:I\to\mathbb R continuous. If \int_a^b f(x)h(x)\,dx=0 for every smooth compactly supported real function h on I, then f vanishes identically. Compact support here means that h=0 outside some [c,d]\subset I.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Continuous real functions; Smooth compactly supported test functions; Integration; Differentiation. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Choose a positive bump where the function keeps its sign

Suppose {\displaystyle f({\bar {x}})\neq 0} for some {\displaystyle {\bar {x}}\in (a,b)}. Since {\displaystyle f} is continuous, it is nonzero with the same sign for some {\displaystyle c,d} such that {\displaystyle a<c<{\bar {x}}<d<b}. Without loss of generality, assume {\displaystyle f({\bar {x}})>0}. Then take an {\displaystyle h} that is positive on {\displaystyle (c,d)} and zero elsewhere, for example

{\displaystyle h(x)={\begin{cases}\exp \left(-{\frac {1}{(x-c)(d-x)}}\right),&c<x<d\\0,&\mathrm {otherwise} \end{cases}}}.

Note this bump function satisfies the properties in the statement, including {\displaystyle C^{\infty }}. Since

{\displaystyle \int _{a}^{b}f(x)h(x)dx>0,}

we reach a contradiction.

Level justification

Continuity gives an interval of constant nonzero sign; integrate against the supplied positive smooth bump to contradict the zero pairing. Smooth endpoint behavior and ordinary integration are undergraduate real analysis.
