Statement

For a finite numerical set A, write A+A=\{a+b:a,b\in A\} and A\cdot A=\{ab:a,b\in A\}. There are an absolute constant c>0 and arbitrarily large finite sets A\subset\mathbb C such that \max(|A+A|,|A\cdot A|)\leq |A|^{2-c}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The sum and product sets are explicitly defined finite numerical sets, and the bound uses only their sizes and a real power. Understanding the assertion needs elementary algebra and counting. This rating concerns understanding the statement, independently of the difficulty of its proof.

Proof 1: Real sum-product counterexamples are complex counterexamples — outline

Use the stated result of Bloom, Sawin, Schildkraut and Zhelezov: for an absolute c>0 there are arbitrarily large finite real sets A with \max(|A+A|,|A\cdot A|)\leq |A|^{2-c}. These real counterexamples are hence also counterexamples over the complex numbers.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Use the explicitly stated finite real-set theorem and include the same sets in C. Finite counting, sums, products and real powers suffice for the application; the advanced proof of the imported theorem is not used.
