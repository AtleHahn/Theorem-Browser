Statement

Let f:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R be continuously differentiable and assume |\partial_i f(x+te_i)-\partial_i f(x)|\leq L|t| for all x,t,i. Fix x, \eta>0, and sample i uniformly from \{1,\ldots,d\}. Put x^+=x-\eta\partial_i f(x)e_i. Then f(x^+)\leq f(x)+(L\eta^2/2-\eta)(\partial_i f(x))^2 and \mathbb E_i f(x^+)\leq f(x)+(L\eta^2/2-\eta)\|\nabla f(x)\|^2/d.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves partial derivatives; finite uniform averaging; Coordinatewise Lipschitz bounds; Euclidean gradient and norm. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Source argument

For each coordinate, apply the one-dimensional upper-bound argument:

Set {\textstyle g(t)=f(x+t(y-x))} for {\textstyle t\in [0,1]} and use the {\textstyle L} -Lipschitz continuity to show that {\textstyle f(y)-f(x)=g(1)-g(0)=\int _{0}^{1}g'(t)=\langle \int _{0}^{1}\nabla f(x+t(y-x))dt,y-x\rangle \leq \langle \nabla f(x),y-x\rangle +{\frac {L}{2}}\|y-x\|^{2}}.

Here the two points differ only in coordinate i, so the stated coordinate Lipschitz bound is the bound used in that integral.

{\displaystyle f(x^+)\leq f(x)+(L\eta ^{2}/2-\eta )(\partial _{i}f(x))^{2}}

Take expectation with respect to {\textstyle i}, we have

{\displaystyle \mathbb {E} [f(x^+)]\leq f(x)+{\frac {L\eta ^{2}/2-\eta }{d}}\|\nabla f(x)\|^{2}}

Level justification

Integrate the partial derivative along one coordinate segment, bound the remainder using its Lipschitz constant, and substitute the update. Uniform finite averaging gives the gradient norm bound. Multivariable calculus and finite probability suffice.
