Statement

Define f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R by f(x)=x/2 for x\ne0 and f(0)=1. For every x_0\in\mathbb R, the iterates x_{n+1}=f(x_n) converge to 0, although f has no fixed point.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Real sequences; Function iteration; Limits. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Halve every nonzero iterate — outline

The iteration

{\displaystyle x_{n+1}={\begin{cases}{\frac {x_{n}}{2}},&x_{n}\neq 0\\1,&x_{n}=0\end{cases}}}

converges to 0 for all values of {\displaystyle x_{0}}. However, 0 is not a fixed point of the function

{\displaystyle f(x)={\begin{cases}{\frac {x}{2}},&x\neq 0\\1,&x=0\end{cases}}}

as this function is not continuous at {\displaystyle x=0}, and in fact has no fixed points.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Every nonzero orbit is a geometric sequence; an orbit beginning at zero first moves to one. Solve the fixed-point equation separately at zero and away from zero. Basic real sequence limits suffice.
