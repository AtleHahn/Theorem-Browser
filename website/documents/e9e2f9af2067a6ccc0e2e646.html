Statement

Any d+2 points in \mathbb R^d admit a partition into two nonempty parts with intersecting convex hulls. The convex hull consists of all convex combinations, i.e. weighted sums with nonnegative weights totaling one.

Proof 1: Normalize a nonzero affine dependence

Consider any set {\displaystyle X=\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots ,x_{d+2}\}\subset \mathbf {R} ^{d}} of d + 2 points in d-dimensional space. Then there exists a set of multipliers a1, ..., ad + 2, not all of which are zero, solving the system of linear equations

{\displaystyle \sum _{i=1}^{d+2}a_{i}x_{i}=0,\quad \sum _{i=1}^{d+2}a_{i}=0,}

because there are d + 2 unknowns (the multipliers) but only d + 1 equations that they must satisfy (one for each coordinate of the points, together with a final equation requiring the sum of the multipliers to be zero). Fix some particular nonzero solution a1, ..., ad + 2. Let {\displaystyle I\subseteq X} be the set of points with positive multipliers, and let {\displaystyle J=X\setminus I} be the set of points with multipliers that are negative or zero. Since the multipliers sum to zero and are not all zero, both parts are nonempty. Then {\displaystyle I} and {\displaystyle J} form the required partition of the points into two subsets with intersecting convex hulls.

The convex hulls of {\displaystyle I} and {\displaystyle J} must intersect, because they both contain the point

{\displaystyle p=\sum _{x_{i}\in I}{\frac {a_{i}}{A}}x_{i}=\sum _{x_{j}\in J}{\frac {-a_{j}}{A}}x_{j},}

where

{\displaystyle A=\sum _{x_{i}\in I}a_{i}=-\sum _{x_{j}\in J}a_{j}.}

Here A>0. The left hand side of the formula for {\displaystyle p} expresses this point as a convex combination of the points in {\displaystyle I}, and the right hand side expresses it as a convex combination of the points in {\displaystyle J}. Therefore, {\displaystyle p} belongs to both convex hulls, completing the proof.
