Statement

Let k be a field, H\le G a subgroup of finite index, M a finite-dimensional kG-module and N a finite-dimensional kH-module. Write \operatorname{Res}_H^G M for restriction and \operatorname{Ind}_H^G N=kG\otimes_{kH}N for induction. For each integer n\ge0, \operatorname{Ext}_{kG}^n(M,\operatorname{Ind}_H^G N)\cong\operatorname{Ext}_{kH}^n(\operatorname{Res}_H^G M,N).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Group representations over a field; Group algebras; Induced modules; Restriction of scalars; Derived Ext functors. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Specialize change of rings to group algebras

Use the change-of-rings theorem: if R\to S makes S projective as a left R-module, then \operatorname{Ext}_{R}^n({}_RM,N)\cong\operatorname{Ext}_{S}^n(M,\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(S,N)). Apply it to R=kH and S=kG. The latter is finitely generated projective as a left kH-module. For finite-index subgroups the induced and coinduced modules agree, so replace \operatorname{Hom}_{kH}(kG,N) by kG\otimes_{kH}N. This gives the asserted isomorphism.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply change of rings for Ext and identify induction with coinduction at finite index. Derived functors, group-algebra modules and tensor-Hom constructions require substantial homological algebra.
