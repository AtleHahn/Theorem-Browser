Statement

Let r,s\ge1 be integers. Every sequence a_1,\ldots,a_{rs+1} in a totally ordered set has a weakly increasing subsequence of length r+1 or a strictly decreasing subsequence of length s+1.

Level
High school only
Level justification

A total order supplies consistent comparisons between all elements; the assertion uses only the comparisons among finitely many listed values. Weak and strict subsequences and their lengths require elementary order reasoning, not a theory of infinite orders.

Proof 1: Apply the product order to sequence indices — outline

Use the source's two-order application: order indices by i\preceq j when i\le j and a_i\le a_j. A chain is a weakly increasing subsequence. For an antichain, listing its indices increasingly gives strictly decreasing values, since for i<j incomparability forces a_i>a_j. The finite-poset chain–antichain bound supplies a chain of r+1 indices or an antichain of s+1 indices, as required.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Order the finite index set by both index and value. Its chains and antichains give the two kinds of subsequence; apply the supplied finite cardinality bound.
