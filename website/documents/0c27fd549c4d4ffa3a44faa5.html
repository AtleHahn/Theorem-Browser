Statement

Let A\subseteq B be commutative rings with the same identity. If B is finite as an A-module and is a Noetherian ring, then A is Noetherian.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement concerns abstract rings and finite generation of modules.

Proof 1: Specialize Formanek’s module criterion to the overring — outline

Use Formanek’s criterion: a commutative ring A is Noetherian when a faithful finite A-module M has the ascending chain condition on submodules IM for ideals I of A. Apply this criterion with M=B. The inclusion of rings makes B faithful, and the modules IB are ideals of the Noetherian ring B, so their increasing chains stabilize. The criterion gives the conclusion.

Level justification

The argument uses finite faithful modules, submodules of the form IM, Noetherian rings and a module chain-condition criterion. Retain beyond undergraduate provisionally because the combined module-theoretic prerequisites exceed a confidently small extension of introductory rings.
