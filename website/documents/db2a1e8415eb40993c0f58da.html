Statement

Given distinct Euclidean points A,B and an integer n\ge1, one can construct with a fixed-aperture compass the point Q on the ray AB with AQ=n\,AB.

Level

Proof 1: Three equilateral triangles and repeated extension

Write U(V) for the circle centered at U and passing through V. Given the line AB determined by A and B, construct the point C on the line such that B is the midpoint of line segment AC.

  1. Construct point D as an intersection of circles A(B) and B(A). (ABD is an equilateral triangle.)
  2. Construct point E\ne A as the intersection of circles D(B) and B(D). (DBE is an equilateral triangle.)
  3. Finally, construct point C\ne D as the intersection of circles B(E) and E(B). (EBC is an equilateral triangle, and the three angles at B show that A,B,C are collinear.)

This construction can be repeated as often as necessary to find a point Q so that the length of line segment AQ is n times the length of line segment AB for any positive integer n.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Three successive equilateral triangles make a straight angle at B and reproduce the segment length. Repeat the construction finitely many times. Only elementary circle geometry and angle addition are needed.
