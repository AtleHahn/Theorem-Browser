Statement

Let F be a continuous real function on an open set U\subseteq\mathbb R^2. If its iterated Riemann integral is zero on every closed rectangle contained in U, then F is identically zero.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Continuous functions on open subsets of the plane; Iterated Riemann integrals. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Contradict a locally constant sign

If the iterated integral of a continuous function F vanishes for all rectangles, then F must be identically zero; for otherwise F or -F would be strictly positive at some point and therefore by continuity on a rectangle contained in U, which is not possible: the iterated integral is positive if the function is positive.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A nonzero value gives a small rectangle with a strict uniform sign by continuity, contradicting its zero integral. This is ordinary multivariable Riemann integration.
