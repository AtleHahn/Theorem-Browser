Statement

Fix n\ge1 and let B be an invertible real n\times n matrix. If every symmetric convex set in \mathbb R^n of volume greater than 2^n contains a nonzero integer point, then every full-rank lattice L=B\mathbb Z^n has the corresponding property with threshold 2^n|\det B|.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Full-rank lattices; Invertible linear maps; Determinants; Volume; Convexity and symmetry. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Pull back by an invertible lattice basis matrix

To obtain a proof for general lattices, it suffices to prove Minkowski's theorem only for \mathbb Z^n; this is because every full-rank lattice can be written as B\mathbb Z^n for some invertible linear transformation B, and the properties of being convex and symmetric about the origin are preserved by linear transformations, while the covolume of B\mathbb Z^n is |\det B| and volume of a body scales by exactly 1/|\det B| under an application of B^{-1}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Pull the body back by the invertible basis matrix, scale volume by its determinant and push the integer point forward. Undergraduate linear algebra and multivariable volume transformation suffice.
