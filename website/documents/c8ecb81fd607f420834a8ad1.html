Statement

There is no total algorithm which, given an integer-coefficient polynomial in finitely many variables, decides whether it has a zero with all coordinates in \mathbb Z.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Integer polynomials and integer solutions are familiar, and a total algorithm means one that always halts with a correct answer. This single computational notion suffices to understand the impossibility claim without its logic-based proof.

Proof 1: Combine MRDP with an undecidable enumerable set — outline

Use MRDP: a computably enumerable set of integers is represented by an existential integer-polynomial equation. There are computably enumerable sets which are not decidable. The conjunction of these two facts rules out a general algorithm deciding the solvability of integer Diophantine equations.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A polynomial-zero decision algorithm would decide every enumerable set through its supplied MRDP representation, contradicting an undecidable example. Undergraduate discrete mathematics and the basic distinction between enumeration and decision suffice; no proof of MRDP is required.
