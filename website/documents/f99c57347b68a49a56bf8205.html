Statement

The self-map T:[1,\infty)\to[1,\infty) given by T(x)=x+1/x strictly decreases distances between distinct points in the usual metric, but has no fixed point.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The explicit rational function and distance inequality use elementary real algebra.

Proof 1: A rational-function counterexample outline

The condition {\displaystyle d(T(x),T(y))<d(x,y)} for all {\displaystyle x\neq y} is in general not enough to ensure the existence of a fixed point, as is shown by the map

{\displaystyle T:[1,\infty )\to [1,\infty ),\,\,T(x)=x+{\tfrac {1}{x}}\,,}

which lacks a fixed point.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Subtract T(x)-T(y) and factor (x-y)(1-1/(xy)); for distinct x,y>=1 the multiplier lies strictly between zero and one. T(x)>x excludes a fixed point. School algebra suffices.
