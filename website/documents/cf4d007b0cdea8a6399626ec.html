Statement

Let (U,\Sigma) be a measurable space and let V_1,\ldots,V_n be nonatomic countably additive probability measures on it, where n\ge1 is an integer. There exists a measurable partition X=(X_1,\ldots,X_n) maximizing \sum_{i=1}^nV_i(X_i) among all such partitions.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Although the objective is a finite sum, the candidate partitions involve arbitrary nonatomic measures.

Proof 1: Maximize the diagonal sum on a compact set

For a measurable partition X=(X_1,\ldots,X_k) of U (empty pieces allowed), put M_X[i,j]=V_i(X_j). The external Dubins–Spanier theorem says that, for fixed k, the set \mathbb M of all these real n\times k matrices is compact. Take k=n. Compactness of \mathbb M implies existence of a utilitarian-optimal partition: the objective is the sum of the diagonal entries of M_X.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Maximizing the diagonal sum is finite-dimensional, but applying the compactness theorem requires general nonatomic measures and measurable partitions. That measure-theoretic framework exceeds the ordinary undergraduate baseline.
