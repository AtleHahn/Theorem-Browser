Statement

Define \chi(X)=\sum_{j\ge0}(-1)^j b_j(X), where b_j(X)=\dim_{\mathbb Q}H_j(X;\mathbb Q); these dimensions are finite and vanish for all sufficiently large j. For every integer n≥1, T^n=(S^1)^n has χ(T^n)=0.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

This is a topological product invariant.

Proof 1: Multiply the circle Euler characteristics

The n-dimensional torus is the product space of n circles. Its Euler characteristic is 0 by the product property

\chi(M\times N)=\chi(M)\chi(N),

using \chi(S^1)=0.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the product rule to the homologically defined Euler characteristic. The arithmetic is elementary, but the statement explicitly uses rational homology and Betti numbers, whose framework is not confidently a small extension of basic topology.
