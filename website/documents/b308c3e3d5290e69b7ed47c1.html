Statement

Let L be a finite distributive lattice: a finite partially ordered set in which every pair x,y has a least upper bound x\vee y and a greatest lower bound x\wedge y, and both operations distribute over the other. Let f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4:L\to[0,\infty) satisfy

f_1(x)f_2(y)\le f_3(x\vee y)f_4(x\wedge y)\qquad(x,y\in L).

For S\subseteq L write f_j(S)=\sum_{x\in S}f_j(x). For X,Y\subseteq L define X\vee Y=\{x\vee y:x\in X,\ y\in Y\} and X\wedge Y=\{x\wedge y:x\in X,\ y\in Y\}. Then for all X,Y\subseteq L,

f_1(X)f_2(Y)\le f_3(X\vee Y)f_4(X\wedge Y).
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

An undergraduate can learn finite lattice joins/meets and distributivity as a small extension of familiar order and set operations, with subsets under union/intersection as a basic example. Understanding arbitrary partial orders and the two induced operations on families calls for undergraduate abstraction, but no probability or statistical mechanics is needed.
