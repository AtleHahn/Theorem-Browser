Statement

The map \alpha(t)=(1/(1+t^2),\ t-2t/(1+t^2),\ 0) from \mathbb R to \mathbb R^3 is the a=0 member of the smooth family

\beta(t,a)=\left(\frac1{(1+t^2)(1+a^2)},\ t-\frac{2t}{(1+t^2)(1+a^2)},\ \frac{ta}{(1+t^2)(1+a^2)}\right).

For each fixed a\ne0, t\mapsto\beta(t,a) is a smooth embedding: it is injective, has nonzero derivative and has a continuous inverse on its image.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Smooth Euclidean curves; Injectivity and nonzero derivative; Continuity of an inverse on the image. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Rational one-parameter family — outline

The explicit family is

{\displaystyle \beta (t,a)=\left({\frac {1}{(1+t^{2})(1+a^{2})}},\ t-{\frac {2t}{(1+t^{2})(1+a^{2})}},\ {\frac {ta}{(1+t^{2})(1+a^{2})}}\right).}

Notice β(t, 0) = α(t) and for a ≠ 0 then as a function of t, β(t, a) is an embedding.
