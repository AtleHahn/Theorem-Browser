Statement

Let N\ge1 be an integer and let (q_{r1},q_{r2},q_{r3})\in\{-1,1\}^3 for 1\le r\le N. Define C_{ij}=N^{-1}\sum_{r=1}^Nq_{ri}q_{rj}. Then C_{12}+C_{23}-C_{13}\le1.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All correlations are explicitly finite arithmetic averages of products of signs, so no probability theory or quantum mechanics is required.

Proof 1: Average the pointwise sign inequality

For every triple of signs, q_1q_2+q_2q_3-q_1q_3\le1. Indeed, if q_1=-q_3 the expression is 1, and if q_1=q_3 it is 2q_1q_2-1, hence either 1 or -3. Averaging this pointwise inequality gives

C_{12}+C_{23}-C_{13}=\frac1N\sum_{r=1}^N(q_{r1}q_{r2}+q_{r2}q_{r3}-q_{r1}q_{r3})\le1.
Level
High school only
Level justification

Check the two endpoint-sign cases and average the resulting bound. Elementary arithmetic of signs suffices.
