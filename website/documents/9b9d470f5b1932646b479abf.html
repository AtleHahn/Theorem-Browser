Every completely positive map has a representation as a compression of a star representation on a larger Hilbert space. In quantum theory this supports describing operations on a subsystem through an enlarged system, distinguishing effective open-system behavior from the structure of the larger representation.

Statement

Let A be a unital C-star algebra, let H be a complex Hilbert space, and let \Phi:A\to B(H) be completely positive. There exist a Hilbert space K, a unital star homomorphism \pi:A\to B(K) and a bounded V:H\to K such that \Phi(a)=V^*\pi(a)V and \|V\|^2=\|\Phi(1)\|.

Proof 1: Form the tensor quotient — outline

We now briefly sketch the proof. Let {\displaystyle K=A\otimes H} be the algebraic tensor product. For {\displaystyle a\otimes h,\ b\otimes g\in K}, define

{\displaystyle \langle a\otimes h,b\otimes g\rangle _{K}:=\langle \Phi (b^{*}a)h,g\rangle _{H}=\langle h,\Phi (a^{*}b)g\rangle _{H}}

and extend by semi-linearity to all of K. This is a Hermitian sesquilinear form because {\displaystyle \Phi } is compatible with the * operation. Complete positivity of {\displaystyle \Phi } is then used to show that this sesquilinear form is in fact positive semidefinite. Since positive semidefinite Hermitian sesquilinear forms satisfy the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, the subset

{\displaystyle K'=\{x\in K\mid \langle x,x\rangle _{K}=0\}\subset K}

is a subspace. We can remove degeneracy by considering the quotient space {\displaystyle K/K'}. The completion of this quotient space is then a Hilbert space, also denoted by {\displaystyle K}. Next define {\displaystyle \pi (a)(b\otimes g)=ab\otimes g} and {\displaystyle Vh=1_{A}\otimes h}. One can check that {\displaystyle \pi } and {\displaystyle V} have the desired properties.

One can verify that V^{*}(a\otimes h)=\Phi(a)h holds. In particular V^{*}V=\Phi(1) holds, so \|V\|^{2}=\|\Phi(1)\|.
