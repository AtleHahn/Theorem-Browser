Statement

For a prime p and a\in\mathbb F_p, the nonzero polynomial X^p-a has formal derivative zero. Here \mathbb F_p=\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z uses arithmetic modulo p, and the formal derivative is (\sum_j c_jX^j)^\prime=\sum_{j\ge1}jc_jX^{j-1}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The field notation is explicitly reduced to arithmetic modulo p, and formal differentiation is explicitly defined by a coefficient rule. Understanding the claim requires integer remainders and polynomial coefficients; no calculus or abstract finite-field theory is necessary. Nonzero refers to the original leading coefficient one.

Proof 1: The formal derivative of Xp−a in characteristic p — proof

Formal differentiation gives

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}{\frac {d}{dX}}(X^{p}-a)&=p\cdot X^{p-1}\\&\equiv 0\cdot X^{p-1}{\bmod {p}}\\&\equiv 0{\bmod {p}}\end{aligned}}}
Level
High school only
Level justification

The formal derivative rule is explicitly defined as a coefficient operation, so no calculus is required. It gives pX^(p-1), whose coefficient is zero modulo p, while the original leading coefficient is one.
