Statement

Let H be a separable Hilbert space and T>0. For a function u:[0,T]\to H, define

\operatorname{Var}(u)=\sup_{0=t_0<\cdots<t_m=T}\sum_{j=1}^m\|u(t_j)-u(t_{j-1})\|,

where the supremum is over all finite partitions. Suppose (f_n)_{n\geq1} is a sequence of such functions with

\sup_{n\geq1}\bigl(\|f_n(0)\|+\operatorname{Var}(f_n)\bigr)<\infty.

Then there are strictly increasing positive integers n_k and a function f:[0,T]\to H of finite variation such that f_{n_k}(t) converges weakly to f(t) for every t\in[0,T]: for every continuous linear functional \ell on H and every t,

\ell(f_{n_k}(t))\longrightarrow\ell(f(t)).

A Hilbert space is a complete inner-product space, separable means it has a countable dense subset, and the norm is the inner-product norm. The bounded-variation norm in this statement is \|u(0)\|+\operatorname{Var}(u), not variation alone.

Proof 1: Weak compactness and bounded-variation selection (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Section 3, Helly theorem, pp. 116–117; metric selection proof, pp. 114–116).

The given bound implies \|f_n(t)\|\le M for all n,t; the Hilbert ball is weakly compact. A repair of the metric selection step is to select also the scalar increasing variation functions v_n(t)=\operatorname{Var}(f_n;[0,t]) on a countable dense set. Their monotone limit has only countably many discontinuities. At its continuity points the inequality d(f_n(s),f_n(t))\le v_n(t)-v_n(s) forces convergence from nearby selected times; select once more at the exceptional points and endpoints. This avoids inferring simultaneous inequalities from separate limsup inequalities. Weak lower semicontinuity of the norm bounds every partition sum of the limit.
