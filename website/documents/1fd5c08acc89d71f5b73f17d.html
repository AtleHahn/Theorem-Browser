Statement

A genus-g handlebody is a compact three-manifold homeomorphic to a regular neighborhood of a bouquet of g circles; a Heegaard splitting is a decomposition into two such handlebodies along their common boundary. In S^3=\{(z_1,z_2)\in\mathbb C^2:|z_1|^2+|z_2|^2=1\}, the torus |z_1|=|z_2|=1/\sqrt2 is a genus-one Heegaard surface.

Proof 1: Clifford-torus splitting of the three-sphere — outline

Under the usual identification of {\displaystyle \mathbb {R} ^{4}} with {\displaystyle \mathbb {C} ^{2}} we may view {\displaystyle S^{3}} as living in {\displaystyle \mathbb {C} ^{2}}. Then the set of points where each coordinate has norm {\displaystyle 1/{\sqrt {2}}} forms a Clifford torus, {\displaystyle T^{2}}. This is the standard genus one splitting of {\displaystyle S^{3}}.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The coordinate torus is explicit, but the outline merely identifies it as a Heegaard splitting without giving the solid-torus parametrizations or explaining regular neighborhoods and handlebodies. Given that three-manifold framework, an undergraduate classification is not confidently justified; retain beyond undergraduate provisionally.
