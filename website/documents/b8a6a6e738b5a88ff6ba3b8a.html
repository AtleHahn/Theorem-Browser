Statement

A subset Q of a real vector space is convex if and only if it contains every finite convex combination \sum_{i=1}^m\lambda_iq_i of its points, where \lambda_i\ge0 and \sum_i\lambda_i=1. Convex means closure under the combinations (1-t)x+ty with 0\le t\le1.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra and real analysis supply convex combinations, suprema and infima. The finite-support variance is a weighted sum of squared distances, and all other set operations are explicitly defined. These are limited extensions of familiar Euclidean convexity.

Proof 1: Induction on the number of points — outline

By mathematical induction, a set Q is convex if and only if every convex combination of members of Q also belongs to Q.

The source specifies induction but does not expand the grouping of weights or the zero-weight cases.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Group all but one nonzero weight, normalize that group, and apply the two-point convexity condition after induction. A zero grouped weight leaves a single point; the converse is the two-term case. This is elementary vector-space algebra.
