For every positive integer n, 2\sqrt n\int_0^1(1-t^2)^n\,dt\le\int_{-\sqrt n}^{\sqrt n}e^{-x^2}\,dx\le2\sqrt n\int_0^1(1+t^2)^{-n}\,dt.

Proof 1: Finite-interval bounds for the Gaussian integral — proof

Since {\displaystyle (1+t)e^{-t}\leq 1} for all {\displaystyle t}, we have the exact bounds:

{\displaystyle 1-x^{2}\leq e^{-x^{2}}\leq (1+x^{2})^{-1}}

On [-1,1] all terms are nonnegative. Raising to the positive integer power n and integrating, we can do the bound at Laplace approximation limit:

{\displaystyle \int _{[-1,1]}(1-x^{2})^{n}dx\leq \int _{[-1,1]}e^{-nx^{2}}dx\leq \int _{[-1,1]}(1+x^{2})^{-n}dx}

That is, by rescaling and evenness,

{\displaystyle 2{\sqrt {n}}\int _{[0,1]}(1-x^{2})^{n}dx\leq \int _{[-{\sqrt {n}},{\sqrt {n}}]}e^{-x^{2}}dx\leq 2{\sqrt {n}}\int _{[0,1]}(1+x^{2})^{-n}dx}

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Raise the nonnegative pointwise bounds to the nth power, integrate and rescale the finite interval. This is ordinary calculus and elementary inequalities.
