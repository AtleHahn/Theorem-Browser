Statement

Let f:U\to\mathbb R^r be a smooth map from an open subset U of \mathbb R^n, let r\le n, and let Df(a) have rank r. There is a smooth change of source coordinates G near a such that f(G(z,w))=z for (z,w) near (f(a),w_0)\in\mathbb R^r\times\mathbb R^{n-r}.

Level justification

Proof 1: A full-rank smooth map is locally a coordinate projection

Put b=f(a) and, by permuting the coordinates on \mathbb R^n, assume the matrix

\left[\frac{\partial f_i}{\partial x_j}(a)\right]_{1\le i,j\le r}

has rank r. Then the map

F:U\to\mathbb R^r\times\mathbb R^{n-r}=\mathbb R^n, \qquad x\mapsto(f(x),x_{r+1},\ldots,x_n)

is such that F'(a) has rank n. Hence, by the inverse function theorem, we find the smooth inverse G of F defined in a neighborhood V\times W of (b,a_{r+1},\ldots,a_n). We then have

x=(F\circ G)(x)=(f(G(x)),G_{r+1}(x),\ldots,G_n(x)).

That is, after the change of coordinates by G, f is a coordinate projection: writing x=(z,w) gives f(G(z,w))=z.
