Statement

Let A\subseteq B be commutative rings with the same identity, and suppose that B is integral over A: every b\in B satisfies a monic polynomial with coefficients in A. A prime ideal \mathfrak q of B lies over a prime ideal \mathfrak p of A when \mathfrak q\cap A=\mathfrak p.

  1. Going up, lying over, and incomparability. Every prime ideal of A has a prime ideal of B lying over it. For integers 1\le m<n, if

    \mathfrak p_1\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathfrak p_n

    is a chain of prime ideals of A and

    \mathfrak q_1\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathfrak q_m

    is a chain of prime ideals of B with \mathfrak q_i\cap A=\mathfrak p_i for 1\le i\le m, then the latter chain extends to \mathfrak q_1\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathfrak q_n with \mathfrak q_i\cap A=\mathfrak p_i for every i. Moreover, distinct prime ideals of B lying over the same prime ideal of A are incomparable under inclusion.

  2. Going down. Suppose additionally that B is an integral domain and A is integrally closed in its field of fractions: every element of \operatorname{Frac}(A) satisfying a monic polynomial over A belongs to A. For integers 1\le m<n, if

    \mathfrak p_1\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\mathfrak p_n

    is a chain of prime ideals of A and

    \mathfrak q_1\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\mathfrak q_m

    is a chain of prime ideals of B with \mathfrak q_i\cap A=\mathfrak p_i for 1\le i\le m, then the latter chain extends to \mathfrak q_1\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\mathfrak q_n with \mathfrak q_i\cap A=\mathfrak p_i for every i.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Prime ideals and inclusion chains are familiar undergraduate algebra, but integral extensions, fraction fields and integral closure must be understood together to interpret both chain-lifting assertions. Their cumulative burden is not confidently within the conservative 30-minute extension of introductory algebra; classified beyond undergraduate rather than assuming a commutative-algebra elective. Flatness and localization belong only to the excluded secondary proof and are not statement prerequisites.
