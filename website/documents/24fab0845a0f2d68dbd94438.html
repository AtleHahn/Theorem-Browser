Statement

Let (\Omega,\mathcal F,P) be a probability space, 1\le p<\infty, and q satisfy 1/p+1/q=1 (with q=\infty if p=1). Let K be a convex subset of the real space L^p(P) containing 0, and let B_+ consist of nonnegative bounded random variables. Set K-B_+=\{Y-b:Y\in K,b\in B_+\}, and take its closure in the L^p norm. The following are equivalent:

  1. For every nonzero f\in L^p(P) with f\ge0 almost surely, there exists c>0 such that cf\notin\overline{K-B_+}.
  2. For every A\in\mathcal F with P(A)>0, there exists c>0 such that c\mathbf1_A\notin\overline{K-B_+}.
  3. There exists Z\in L^q(P) with Z>0 almost surely and \sup_{Y\in K}\mathbb E[ZY]<\infty. Here L^p consists of measurable functions modulo almost-sure equality with finite \mathbb E|Y|^p, equipped with norm (\mathbb E|Y|^p)^{1/p}; L^\infty means essentially bounded. The symbol \mathbf1_A is the indicator of A.
