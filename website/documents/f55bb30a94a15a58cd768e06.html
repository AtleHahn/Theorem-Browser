Statement

Let m,n\ge1 and 1\le p_i<\infty satisfy \sum_i1/p_i=1. For nonnegative measurable f_i\in L^{p_i}(\mathbb R^n), \int_{\mathbb R^n}\prod_{i=1}^m f_i(x)\,dx\le\prod_{i=1}^m\|f_i\|_{p_i},\qquad \|f_i\|_{p_i}=\left(\int_{\mathbb R^n}f_i^{p_i}\right)^{1/p_i}. All integrals are Lebesgue integrals.

Proof 1: Identity maps and determinant concavity — outline

Use the geometric Brascamp–Lieb inequality with nonnegative integrable inputs g_i, positive weights c_i and surjective maps B_i satisfying \sum_i c_i n_i=n:

\int_{\mathbb R^n}\prod_i g_i(B_ix)^{c_i}\,dx\le D^{-1/2}\prod_i\left(\int_{\mathbb R^{n_i}}g_i\right)^{c_i},

where

D=\inf_{A_i>0}\frac{\det(\sum_i c_iB_i^*A_iB_i)}{\prod_i(\det A_i)^{c_i}}.

Take n_i=n, B_i=\mathrm{id}, c_i=1/p_i, and replace the input by g_i=f_i^{1/c_i}. Then \sum_i1/p_i=1, and log-concavity of the determinant on positive-definite matrices implies D=1. This yields the stated Hölder inequality.
