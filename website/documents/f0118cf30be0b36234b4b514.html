Statement

Let X_1,X_2,\ldots be independent integrable real variables with mean zero, S_n=\sum_{i=1}^nX_i and \mathcal F_n=\sigma(X_1,\ldots,X_n). Then (S_n) is a martingale.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The assertion involves independent random variables, their generated filtration and the martingale property.

Proof 1: Condition the next independent increment

The partial sums are adapted and integrable. By independence and the zero means, it is clear that:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\operatorname {E} \left[X_{1}+\cdots +X_{n}+X_{n+1}\mid X_{1},\ldots ,X_{n}\right]&=X_{1}+\cdots +X_{n}+\operatorname {E} \left[X_{n+1}\mid X_{1},\ldots ,X_{n}\right]\\&=X_{1}+\cdots +X_{n},\end{aligned}}}

Consequently, the partial sums {\displaystyle S_{n}=X_{1}+\dots +X_{n}} form a martingale.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Condition the next independent increment on the generated filtration and use its zero expectation. Sigma-algebra conditional expectation, adaptedness and martingales require advanced probability.
