Statement

Let k be a field and let G be a connected k-split solvable algebraic group. Here k-split means that G has a finite series from the identity subgroup to G of algebraic subgroups defined over k, each normal in the next, whose successive quotients are isomorphic over k to the additive group \mathbb G_a or the multiplicative group \mathbb G_m. Let V be a complete algebraic variety over k with a k-rational point, and suppose G acts regularly on V, meaning the action map G\times V\to V is a morphism. Complete means proper over k. Then V has a k-rational point fixed by G.

In particular, over an algebraically closed field a connected solvable linear algebraic group acting regularly on a nonempty complete variety has a fixed point. A linear algebraic group is an algebraic subgroup of some \mathrm{GL}_n, connectedness uses the Zariski topology, and solvability means that the derived series terminates at the identity.
