Statement

Let ABC be any nondegenerate Euclidean triangle. Trisect each interior angle into three equal angles. Let X be the intersection of the trisector at B adjacent to side BC with the trisector at C adjacent to side BC. Define Y analogously using the trisectors adjacent to CA, and Z using those adjacent to AB. Then XYZ is equilateral: |XY|=|YZ|=|ZX|.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Only elementary plane geometry and division of angles into three equal parts are required.

Proof 1: Conway’s seven-triangle construction (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Seven-triangle construction and edge matching, with the linked remarks).

Here is the placement without relying on the figure. Write the given angles as 3a,3b,3c, so a+b+c=60^\circ. Start with an equilateral PQR of side1. Externally on QR, RP, PQ attach triangles with angle triples (a,c+60^\circ,b+60^\circ) at (A,Q,R), (b,a+60^\circ,c+60^\circ) at (B,R,P), and (c,b+60^\circ,a+60^\circ) at (C,P,Q). The remaining triangles BPC,CQA,ARB have angle triples (b,a+120^\circ,c), (c,b+120^\circ,a), and (a,c+120^\circ,b) respectively. Edge matching can also be checked by the sine-rule calculation in the remarks. The outer triangle has angles 3a,3b,3c; its internal trisectors meet at P,Q,R, which correspond to X,Y,Z in the statement.
