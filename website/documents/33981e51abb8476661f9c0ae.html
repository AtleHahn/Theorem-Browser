Statement

Let A be an m\times n matrix with integer entries, where m,n\geq1, and write c_1,\ldots,c_n\in\mathbb Q^m for its columns. Say that Ax=0 is regular if, for every integer r\geq1 and every coloring \kappa:\{1,2,3,\ldots\}\to\{1,\ldots,r\}, there is x=(x_1,\ldots,x_n) with all x_j positive integers, Ax=0, and \kappa(x_1)=\cdots=\kappa(x_n). Coordinates need not be distinct.

The system is regular if and only if there is an ordered partition C_1,\ldots,C_t of \{1,\ldots,n\} into nonempty blocks such that, on writing s_i=\sum_{j\in C_i}c_j,

s_1=0,\qquad s_i\in\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Q}\{c_j:j\in C_1\cup\cdots\cup C_{i-1}\}\quad(2\leq i\leq t).

Level justification

Finite-dimensional linear algebra and elementary integer colorings suffice; no Ramsey-theory machinery is needed to understand the explicit criterion.
