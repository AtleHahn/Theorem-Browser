Statement

The additive group \mathbb Z does not satisfy the descending chain condition on subgroups.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory group theory supplies subgroups, quotients, actions, products and homomorphisms. Perfect means generated by commutators, torsion means finite element orders, and the chain conditions are explicit sequences of subgroups. The relevant one of these closely related definitions is a small extension, without representation theory.

Proof 1: Powers-of-two subgroup chain

The subgroups 2\mathbb Z\supsetneq 2^2\mathbb Z\supsetneq 2^3\mathbb Z\supsetneq\cdots form an infinite strictly decreasing sequence.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the strict chain of subgroups divisible by successive powers of two. Undergraduate subgroup definitions suffice with descending chain condition defined as stabilization of every descending subgroup chain.
