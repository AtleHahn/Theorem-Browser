Statement

For a finite-dimensional vector space V of dimension n over any field K and Lie subalgebra \mathfrak g\subseteq\operatorname{End}_K(V), all its elements are nilpotent if and only if there is a flag V=V_0\supset V_1\supset\cdots\supset V_n=0, \operatorname{codim}V_i=i, with \mathfrak gV_i\subseteq V_{i+1}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra supplies nilpotent maps, flags and codimension. A Lie subalgebra here is a linear space closed under the commutator; a Lie ideal is closed under brackets with the ambient algebra. These explicit finite-dimensional algebraic conditions, and the supplied lower-central sequence, are a small extension without Lie groups or root systems.

Proof 1: Iterate the common-annihilator theorem — outline

Use Engel’s common-annihilator theorem: on any nonzero finite-dimensional space, a Lie algebra consisting of nilpotent operators kills a common nonzero vector. Applying this successively constructs the required flag. Conversely, operators lowering the complete flag are nilpotent.

Level justification

A Lie subalgebra here is only a vector subspace of endomorphisms closed under the commutator. Apply the explicitly stated common-kernel theorem successively on quotient spaces and reverse the resulting invariant flag. These are limited extensions of undergraduate linear algebra; no abstract Lie structure theory is needed.
