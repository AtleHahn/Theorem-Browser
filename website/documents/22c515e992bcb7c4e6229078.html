Statement

Every finite connected simple planar graph G=(V,E) has a circle packing in the Euclidean plane: a positive-radius circle C_v for each v\in V, with pairwise disjoint open disks, such that C_u and C_v are tangent if and only if \{u,v\}\in E. If a planar embedding of G is prescribed, the packing may be chosen so that the cyclic order of tangencies on each circle agrees with the cyclic order of edges at that vertex.

If G is maximal planar, the packing is unique up to Möbius transformations and reflections: any two packings representing G are related by such a transformation (with the corresponding circle labels). Simple means no loops or multiple edges; planar means drawable without edge crossings, and maximal planar means that no further edge between existing vertices can be added while preserving simplicity and planarity.

A Möbius transformation is z\mapsto(az+b)/(cz+d) on \mathbb C\cup\{\infty\}, with a,b,c,d\in\mathbb C and ad-bc\ne0; reflection includes complex conjugation. The equivalence is applied to circle configurations on this extended plane, with the given packings represented by finite circles.

Level justification

The full uniqueness clause adds complex fractional linear maps and the point at infinity to elementary graph and circle geometry. These can be understood from undergraduate complex numbers and topology within a short reading extension; no hyperbolic geometry is needed for the statement.

Proof availability

Source sketches and limitations

For existence, the article describes a limiting construction from Koebe's circle-domain uniformization theorem, and an alternative method that deforms packings while flipping diagonals of triangulations. It also explains reduction from a planar graph to a maximal planar graph containing it as an induced subgraph, followed by removal of added circles.

For uniqueness, it lifts the circles and circles through triangular gaps to hyperbolic planes, constructs a reflection group, and invokes Mostow rigidity to obtain a boundary Möbius transformation.

The limiting/deformation arguments are not supplied. The source's own note 8 warns that adding just one vertex per face is not a valid augmentation for every graph. The reflection-group argument omits the precise finite-volume/manifold or orbifold construction needed to apply rigidity. These are recorded as sketches with substantive missing steps, rather than a complete existence-and-uniqueness proof.
