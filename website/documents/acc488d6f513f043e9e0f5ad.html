Statement

Fix a nondegenerate minor-arc triangle ABC on the unit sphere, of area E>0. For P in the open hemisphere containing A bounded by the great circle BC, define its first area coordinate u(P)=\operatorname{area}(PBC)/E. For any such P_0, the locus u(P)=u(P_0) is the arc through P_0 of the small circle through B^*,C^*,P_0. The other coordinates have the corresponding cyclic descriptions. A star denotes the antipodal point, for example B^*=-B.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Great circles, antipodes, spherical areas and the area-coordinate ratio are concrete spatial geometric notions defined locally.

Proof 1: Apply Lexell’s theorem to the coordinate numerator

The denominator E is fixed, so a fixed first coordinate is exactly a fixed area for triangles with base BC. Lexell’s theorem identifies their same-side apex locus with the indicated circle arc. Cycling the labels gives the other two coordinates.

Level justification

A fixed denominator turns a coordinate level into an equal-area condition; apply the explicitly stated same-side spherical circle-locus theorem. Only concrete sphere, circle and area geometry is needed.
