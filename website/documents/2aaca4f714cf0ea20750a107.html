Statement

For a permutation class (a collection closed under taking permutation patterns), let a_n be its number of length-n permutations and let \gamma=\limsup_{n\to\infty}a_n^{1/n}. Then \gamma\notin(1,\varphi), where \varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Permutations and subsequences are discrete undergraduate objects. A pattern records the relative order in a subsequence; a class is closed under taking these patterns. The growth rate uses the familiar limsup, so the excluded interval adds only one combinatorial definition.

Proof 1: Fibonacci threshold and polynomial growth — outline

Use the Kaiser–Klazar theorem: if a_n is ever less than the nth Fibonacci number F_n, the enumeration is eventually polynomial. If \gamma<\varphi, the standard asymptotic growth F_n^{1/n}\to\varphi forces such an inequality for some n. The eventually polynomial enumeration then has upper exponential growth at most 1, excluding 1<\gamma<\varphi.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Compare nth-root growth with the Fibonacci limit and apply the explicitly stated eventual-polynomial criterion. Undergraduate sequences, limsup and elementary permutation patterns suffice; the structural theorem is not proved.
