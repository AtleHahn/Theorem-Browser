Statement

Let \mathbf A be a graph of groups on a connected graph A with vertex set V and oriented edge set E. Edge reversal e\mapsto\bar e is a fixed-point-free involution, o(e) and t(e)=o(\bar e) are the endpoints, and E^+ chooses one orientation of each geometric edge. Each vertex has a group A_v, each edge has a group A_e=A_{\bar e}, and there are injective boundary maps \alpha_e:A_e\to A_{o(e)}, \omega_e=\alpha_{\bar e}:A_e\to A_{t(e)}. Choose a spanning tree T. Write F(S) for the free group on a set S and \langle\!\langle R\rangle\!\rangle for the normal subgroup generated by relators R. Define G=\pi_1(\mathbf A,T) as the quotient of (\ast_{v\in V}A_v)\ast F(E) by \bar e\alpha_e(c)e=\omega_e(c), e\bar e=1 for all e\in E,c\in A_e, and e=1 for edges of T. If every edge group is trivial, then G\cong(\ast_{v\in V}A_v)\ast F(S), where S=E^+\setminus E(T).

Proof 1: Source presentation calculation

The defining presentation in the statement has only the inverse-edge relations and the relations setting tree edges to one, since all edge groups are trivial.

Algebraically, a splitting of G with trivial edge groups corresponds to a free product decomposition

{\displaystyle G=(\ast A_{v})\ast F(X)}

where F(X) is a free group with free basis X = E+(A−T) consisting of all positively oriented edges (with respect to some orientation on A) in the complement of some spanning tree T of A.

Level

Level justification

Remove the edge-group relations and redundant inverse/tree generators from the given presentation. Understanding the resulting isomorphism still requires free groups, free products, normal-closure presentations and graph-of-groups constructions together. This is not confidently a small extension.
