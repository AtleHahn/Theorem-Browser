Statement

Let R be an integral domain, S\subseteq R finite nonempty, n\ge1 and 0\le d\le|S|. There exists a degree-d nonzero polynomial in R[x_1,\ldots,x_n] whose probability of vanishing on independent uniform samples from S is exactly d/|S|.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Polynomial degree and integral-domain arithmetic are introductory algebra. Uniform independent sampling from a finite set is equivalent to the explicitly given finite zero count, so no general probability foundations are needed.

Proof 1: Product of distinct linear factors

Choose any {\displaystyle d} distinct elements {\displaystyle s_{1},\ldots ,s_{d}} from {\displaystyle S}. Then define {\displaystyle P\in R[x_{1},\ldots ,x_{n}]} by {\displaystyle P(x_{1},\ldots ,x_{n})=\prod _{i=1}^{d}(x_{1}-s_{i})}. Let {\displaystyle r_{1},\ldots ,r_{n}} be selected uniformly at random from {\displaystyle S}. Then {\displaystyle \Pr[P(r_{1},\ldots ,r_{n})=0]=\Pr[r_{1}\in \{s_{1},\ldots ,s_{d}\}]={\frac {d}{|S|}}} so that the bound stated in the Schwartz-Zippel lemma is exactly matched.

For d=0 the empty product is 1. The product is monic of degree d; the absence of zero divisors justifies its stated zero set.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use a monic product of distinct linear factors and the absence of zero divisors, then count the allowed first coordinates. Undergraduate integral-domain algebra suffices.
