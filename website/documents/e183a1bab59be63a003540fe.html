Statement

Let I be a set, let (M_i)_{i\in I} be sets, and let \mathcal F be a proper filter on I: it contains I, not \varnothing, and is closed under finite intersections and supersets in I. On \prod_iM_i, set a\sim b if \{i:a_i=b_i\}\in\mathcal F. Then \sim is an equivalence relation.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Undergraduate sets, relations and finite algorithms suffice. The generation rules, finite incidence configuration or filter closure axioms are explicitly defined; only a small concrete set-based extension is needed, without model theory or ultraproduct semantics.

Proof 1: Filter closure and equality

Throughout, let {\displaystyle a_{\bullet }=\left(a_{i}\right)_{i\in I},b_{\bullet }=\left(b_{i}\right)_{i\in I},} and {\displaystyle c_{\bullet }=\left(c_{i}\right)_{i\in I}} be elements of {\displaystyle {\textstyle \prod \limits _{i\in I}}M_{i}.} The relation {\displaystyle a_{\bullet }\,\sim \,a_{\bullet }} always holds since {\displaystyle \{i\in I:a_{i}=a_{i}\}=I} is an element of filter {\displaystyle {\mathcal {F}}.} Thus the reflexivity of {\displaystyle \,\sim \,} follows from that of equality {\displaystyle \,=.\,} Similarly, {\displaystyle \,\sim \,} is symmetric since equality is symmetric. For transitivity, assume that {\displaystyle R=\{i:a_{i}=b_{i}\}} and {\displaystyle S:=\{i:b_{i}=c_{i}\}} are elements of {\displaystyle {\mathcal {F}};} it remains to show that {\displaystyle T:=\{i:a_{i}=c_{i}\}} also belongs to {\displaystyle {\mathcal {F}}.} The transitivity of equality guarantees {\displaystyle R\cap S\subseteq T} (since if {\displaystyle i\in R\cap S} then {\displaystyle a_{i}=b_{i}} and {\displaystyle b_{i}=c_{i}}). Because {\displaystyle {\mathcal {F}}} is closed under binary intersections, {\displaystyle R\cap S\in {\mathcal {F}}.} Since {\displaystyle {\mathcal {F}}} is upward closed in {\displaystyle I,} it contains every superset of {\displaystyle R\cap S} (that consists of indices); in particular, {\displaystyle {\mathcal {F}}} contains {\displaystyle T.} {\displaystyle \blacksquare }
