Statement

On \mathbb R^3, the plane field E=\ker(dz-y\,dx) is not tangent to a regular foliation by surfaces on any nonempty open set.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Here the differential form specifies the concrete plane equation dz=y dx in R3. A regular foliation by surfaces means a local decomposition into smooth surface slices with those tangent planes. These local coordinate definitions extend undergraduate multivariable calculus and tangent planes; no general tangent-bundle or foliation theorem is needed.

Proof 1: Lift a small square (outline)

The planes defined by the 1-form dz-y\,dx on \mathbb R^3 appear to twist along the y-axis. The plane field is not integrable, as can be verified by drawing a small square in the xy-plane and following a path tangent to the planes. The path would not return to the same z-coordinate after one circuit. Along such a path dz=y\,dx. The vertical sides contribute zero to the change in z, and the two horizontal sides, traversed in opposite directions at different y coordinates, contribute a nonzero signed area. Thus the lift does not return to its initial z coordinate.

A sufficiently small surface tangent to E would project locally diffeomorphically to the xy-plane, since projection restricts to an isomorphism on E. In that local graph, lifting a small closed square would return to its initial height. This contradicts the supplied square-lift obstruction.
