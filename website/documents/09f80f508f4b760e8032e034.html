Statement

Let M\subseteq B(H) be a unital von Neumann algebra on a complex Hilbert space H, and let \Omega be a unit vector such that M\Omega and M^{\prime}\Omega are dense in H and (ab\Omega,\Omega)=(ba\Omega,\Omega) for all a,b\in M. Here M^{\prime} is the commutant and * denotes operator adjoint. Let J(a\Omega)=a^*\Omega be the modular conjugation and let K=\overline{M_{\rm sa}\Omega}, with closure as a real linear subspace. Then H=K\oplus iK orthogonally for \operatorname{Re}(\cdot,\cdot), and K,iK are the +1,-1 real eigenspaces of J.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The real closed subspace of self-adjoint operator vectors and its Hilbert-space orthogonal decomposition exceed finite-dimensional linear algebra.

Proof 1: Real eigenspaces of the conjugation — outline

Let K be the closure of the real subspace M_{\rm sa}\Omega, where M_{\rm sa} denotes the self-adjoint elements in M. It follows that

H=K\oplus iK,

an orthogonal direct sum for the real part of the inner product. This is just the real orthogonal decomposition for the \pm1 eigenspaces of J.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Identify the closure of the self-adjoint operator orbit with a real eigenspace of modular conjugation. The algebra action, trace vector and dense Hilbert-space orbit are substantial prerequisites even though the eigenspace splitting is elementary once J is known.
