Statement

Let (M,\mu) and (N,\nu) be sigma-finite measure spaces, let 1\le p<q\le\infty, and let T:L^p(M,\mu)\to L^q(N,\nu) be bounded and linear. Suppose measurable sets (A_\alpha)_{\alpha\in\mathbb R} increase with \alpha, with union M and intersection empty, and satisfy

\mu(A_\beta\setminus A_\alpha)<\infty\quad(\beta>\alpha),\qquad \lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\mu(A_{\alpha+\varepsilon}\setminus A_\alpha)=0\quad(\alpha\in\mathbb R).

For f\in L^p(M,\mu) define the maximal function as the essential supremum

T^*f=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{\alpha\in\mathbb R}|T(f\mathbf1_{A_\alpha})|.

Then

\|T^*f\|_{L^q(N)}\le\frac{2^{-(1/p-1/q)}}{1-2^{-(1/p-1/q)}}\,\|T\|\,\|f\|_{L^p(M)},

where 1/q=0 when q=\infty. The spaces L^p consist of measurable functions modulo almost-everywhere equality, with the integral norm (or essential-supremum norm for p=\infty). The essential supremum of this family is the least measurable almost-everywhere upper bound of its members, as an equivalence class; it does not depend on arbitrary pointwise representatives. The symbol \mathbf1_A denotes the indicator of A, and \|T\| is the operator norm.

Proof 1: Balanced dyadic prefixes with the exact constant (external)

A supporting source can be found here (Christ–Kiselev Theorem 1.1 and §2 dyadic proof; exact-constant adaptation given below.). The deduction below supplies the argument for the stored statement.

The following exact-constant adaptation of the linked dyadic argument is supplied here. Normalize ||f||p=1. For any finite nested subfamily, partition M into its successive layers. Enlarge to M×[0,1] and lift f constantly; define T′h=T(integral_0^1 h(.,s)ds), so ||T′||≤||T||. Order layers and subdivide them using the auxiliary interval to form an increasing continuous chain for the probability |f|^p dmu ds. Each original set is a prefix. Balanced dyadic cells E_n,j have f-mass 2^-n; each prefix is a disjoint union of at most one cell per level n≥1 (use the infinite binary expansion at the upper endpoint). Thus its image is dominated by sum_n max_j |T′(f 1_E_n,j)|. The Lq norm of the level-n maximum is at most ||T|| 2^{n/q}2^{-n/p}; for q=infinity use the maximum directly. Summing n≥1 gives exactly 2^-delta/(1-2^-delta). Lp convergence of prefix approximations and the summable Lq majorant justify the domination. Apply this to rational parameters; right continuity in the assumptions implies f1_A_r tends in Lp to f1_A_alpha as rational r decreases to alpha. The rational supremum therefore dominates every member almost everywhere and equals the essential supremum. For each finite rational subfamily the bound is uniform. Increasing these finite families and using monotone convergence gives the bound for the countable rational supremum; the right-continuity argument then identifies it with the stated essential supremum.
