Statement

Let (M,\mu) and (N,\nu) be sigma-finite measure spaces, let 1\le p<q\le\infty, and let T:L^p(M,\mu)\to L^q(N,\nu) be bounded and linear. Suppose measurable sets (A_\alpha)_{\alpha\in\mathbb R} increase with \alpha, with union M and intersection empty, and satisfy

\mu(A_\beta\setminus A_\alpha)<\infty\quad(\beta>\alpha),\qquad \lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\mu(A_{\alpha+\varepsilon}\setminus A_\alpha)=0\quad(\alpha\in\mathbb R).

For f\in L^p(M,\mu) define the maximal function as the essential supremum

T^*f=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{\alpha\in\mathbb R}|T(f\mathbf1_{A_\alpha})|.

Then

\|T^*f\|_{L^q(N)}\le\frac{2^{-(1/p-1/q)}}{1-2^{-(1/p-1/q)}}\,\|T\|\,\|f\|_{L^p(M)},

where 1/q=0 when q=\infty. The spaces L^p consist of measurable functions modulo almost-everywhere equality, with the integral norm (or essential-supremum norm for p=\infty). The essential supremum of this family is the least measurable almost-everywhere upper bound of its members, as an equivalence class; it does not depend on arbitrary pointwise representatives. The symbol \mathbf1_A denotes the indicator of A, and \|T\| is the operator norm.
