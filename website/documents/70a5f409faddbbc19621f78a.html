Statement

On [0,1] with Lebesgue measure, let f_n(x)=n1_{(0,1/n)}(x) for n\ge1. Then f_n\to0 pointwise but \int_0^1f_n\,dx=1 for every n, so Fatou’s inequality is strict.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The functions are explicit step functions with elementary rectangle integrals. Real analysis supplies pointwise/uniform convergence and liminf; no general Lebesgue integration is needed to understand this particular counterexample.

Proof 1: Compare the shrinking support with constant spike area — outline

The specified spikes converge pointwise to zero, whose integral is zero, while each spike has height n and interval length 1/n, hence integral one.

Level

Level justification

Check pointwise eventual vanishing of the shrinking spike and compare its constant rectangular area with the zero limiting area. Only ordinary function-sequence convergence and elementary integrals are needed, despite the Lebesgue label.
