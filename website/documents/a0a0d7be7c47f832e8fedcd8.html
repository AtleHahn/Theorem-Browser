Statement

Graphs below are finite, undirected and simple. A clique is a set of pairwise adjacent vertices; an independent set has no edges within it. The complete r-partite graph joins all vertices in different parts and none in the same part. Write T(n,r) for this graph on n vertices with r part sizes differing by at most 1 (empty parts allowed). For integers n\ge0,r\ge1, every complete r-partite graph on n vertices has at most e(T(n,r)) edges, with equality exactly when its part sizes differ by at most 1; moreover e(T(n,r))\le(1-1/r)n^2/2. Here e(G) denotes the number of edges of G.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The locally defined multipartite graph and balance condition require only finite counting and integer comparisons.

Proof 1: Move a vertex between unequal parts

Balance the parts

Let {\displaystyle S_{1},S_{2},\ldots ,S_{r}} be the independent sets of the multipartite graph. Since two vertices have an edge between them if and only if they are not in the same independent set, the number of edges is

{\displaystyle \sum _{i<j}\left|S_{i}\right|\left|S_{j}\right|={\frac {1}{2}}\left(n^{2}-\sum _{i}\left|S_{i}\right|^{2}\right),}

where the left hand side follows from direct counting, and the right hand side follows from complementary counting. To show the {\displaystyle \left(1-{\frac {1}{r}}\right){\frac {n^{2}}{2}}} bound, applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality to the {\textstyle \sum \limits _{i}\left|S_{i}\right|^{2}} term on the right hand side suffices, since {\textstyle \sum \limits _{i}\left|S_{i}\right|=n}.

To prove the Turán Graph is optimal, one can argue that no two {\displaystyle S_{i}} differ by more than one in size. In particular, supposing that we have {\displaystyle \left|S_{i}\right|\geq \left|S_{j}\right|+2} for some {\displaystyle i\neq j}, moving one vertex from {\displaystyle S_{i}} to {\displaystyle S_{j}} (and adjusting edges accordingly) would increase the value of the sum. This can be seen by examining the changes to either side of the above expression for the number of edges, or by noting that the degree of the moved vertex increases.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The edge formula is finite counting and expansion of a square. If part sizes a and b satisfy a>=b+2, moving one vertex decreases the sum of their squares by 2(a-b-1)>0, hence increases the edge count. Repeating terminates among finitely many size vectors, yielding exactly balanced sizes; all such vectors have the same edge count. The stated upper bound uses the elementary finite-sum Cauchy–Schwarz inequality.
