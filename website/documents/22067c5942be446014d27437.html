Statement

For five bounded Lebesgue-measurable sets A_1,\ldots,A_5\subset\mathbb R^2, there is a nonzero real polynomial P(x,y) of total degree at most two such that \{P>0\} and \{P<0\} each contain half the area of every A_i.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement uses Lebesgue measure on arbitrary bounded measurable subsets, beyond the undergraduate calculus baseline.

Proof 1: Pull back a hyperplane under the quadratic lift — outline

Use the lift \Phi(x,y)=(x,y,x^2,y^2,xy)\in\mathbb R^5. An affine hyperplane in this space pulls back to a_0+a_1x+a_2y+a_3x^2+a_4y^2+a_5xy=0. Apply the measure-theoretic ham-sandwich theorem to the five area measures transported by \Phi, as required by the source lifting argument. The needed hyperplane-null condition follows from the standard null-zero-set fact for a nonzero polynomial, equivalently linear independence of 1,x,y,x^2,y^2,xy on every positive-area subset in the source independent-functions formulation. The resulting hyperplane bisects all five transported measures, so its displayed pullback polynomial bisects the five original areas.

Transport five area measures through a nonlinear lift, verify all hyperplanes are null and apply the measure-theoretic ham-sandwich theorem. Pushforward measures and null sets require measure theory beyond elementary Euclidean lifting.
