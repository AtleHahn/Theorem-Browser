Statement

Let A and B be associative algebras over a field F. If both satisfy polynomial identities over F, then A\otimes_F B satisfies a polynomial identity over F. An algebra satisfies a polynomial identity if some nonzero polynomial in finitely many noncommuting variables, with coefficients in F, evaluates to zero under every substitution from the algebra. The tensor product is over the common field, with multiplication (a\otimes b)(a'\otimes b')=aa'\otimes bb'.

Proof 1: Exponential codimension growth (proof sketch) (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorem 3.2; Section 4.1, first proof of Theorem 4.3; Section 4.1.1, Theorem 4.4 and proof.). The linked exposition is a proof sketch with the indicated dependencies.

The characteristic-free route in Theorems 4.3–4.4 uses Shirshov–Latyshev word reduction and Dilworth’s theorem. The following additional clarification handles arbitrary fields: choose a monomial of maximal total degree in a nonzero identity, with multidegree (d_i). Set unused variables to zero and replace each x_i by a sum of d_i distinct new variables, taking the inclusion-exclusion difference over all subsets to retain only terms using every new variable. Terms of smaller total degree disappear; surviving terms have the selected multidegree, and the coefficient of a suitably ordered fully labelled word is the original nonzero coefficient. Thus one obtains a nonzero multilinear identity without division by factorials or an infinite-field argument. The combinatorial reduction to d-good permutations and their exponential bound use only this normalized identity and combinatorics. For the tensor inequality, the diagonal evaluation map V_n -> (V_n/Id(A)) tensor (V_n/Id(B)) has kernel contained in Id(A tensor B), since multilinearity expands arbitrary tensor substitutions into pure ones. Its target dimension bounds the codimension; exponential growth is eventually below n!. Zero algebras and degree-one identities are immediate.
