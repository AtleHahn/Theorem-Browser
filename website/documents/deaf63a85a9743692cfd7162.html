Irreducible strongly continuous representations of the finite-degree Heisenberg group agree up to unitary equivalence once their nontrivial central character agrees. This supports equivalence of canonical quantum descriptions under specific hypotheses, without extending that uniqueness to arbitrary field theories.

Statement

Let n\ge1 be an integer. Write M(a,b,c)=\begin{pmatrix}1&a&c\\0&I_n&b\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix} for the real Heisenberg group, with a,b\in\mathbb R^n and c\in\mathbb R; in the block matrix, a is written as a row and b as a column with those vector coordinates. Two irreducible strongly continuous unitary representations nontrivial on the center are equivalent if and only if they have the same central character: the scalar by which each M(0,0,c) acts is identical for the two representations, for every real c.

Proof 1: Identify the scalar central character in the classification

By the stated Stone–von Neumann classification, each such representation is equivalent to exactly one U_h, h\ne0. On the center, U_h(M(0,0,c))=e^{ihc}I, and this character determines h. Hence equal central characters give the same representation class; equivalence conversely preserves these scalar actions.

Level justification

Read the scalar character exp(ihc) in the supplied classification and recover h. Strong continuity, irreducibility and equivalence of infinite-dimensional unitary representations remain graduate prerequisites.
