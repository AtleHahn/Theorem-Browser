Statement

Let X_1,X_2,\ldots be independent real random variables, each discrete (its law is concentrated on a countable subset of \mathbb R). Suppose \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}X_n converges almost surely to S. Then the law of S has exactly one of these three types: discrete; absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure; or singular continuous. Absolutely continuous means every Lebesgue-null Borel set has probability zero. Singular continuous means that the law has no atoms (\mathbb P(S=x)=0 for every x) and is concentrated on a Lebesgue-null Borel set.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The cumulative measure-theoretic distinction between atomic, singular and absolutely continuous laws plus random-series convergence is beyond the conservative baseline allowance.
