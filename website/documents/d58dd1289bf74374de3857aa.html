Statement

Let C\subset[0,1] be the standard ternary Cantor set, with its subspace topology. There exists a continuous surjection C\to[0,1]^2.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Basic point-set topology and real analysis supply compactness, closed sets, continuity, metrics and completeness. The Cantor-set construction or cofinite topology, where present, is a direct set-based example learnable briefly. The complete assertion needs no algebraic or differential topology.

Proof 1: Compose the product Cantor-function map with a homeomorphism

We start with a continuous function {\displaystyle h} from the Cantor space {\displaystyle C} onto the entire unit interval {\displaystyle [0,\,1]}. (The restriction of the Cantor function to the Cantor set is an example of such a function.) From it, we get a continuous function {\displaystyle H} from the topological product {\displaystyle C\;\times \;C} onto the entire unit square {\displaystyle [0,\,1]\;\times \;[0,\,1]} by setting

{\displaystyle H(x,y)=(h(x),h(y)).\,}

Since the Cantor set {\displaystyle C} is homeomorphic to its cartesian product with itself {\displaystyle C\times C}, there is a continuous bijection {\displaystyle g} from the Cantor set onto {\displaystyle C\;\times \;C}. The composition {\displaystyle f} of {\displaystyle H} and {\displaystyle g} is a continuous function mapping the Cantor set onto the entire unit square.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof composes two supplied maps and checks product continuity and surjectivity. The Cantor set, homeomorphism and product topology are ordinary undergraduate real-analysis/topology concepts; proofs of the imported Cantor-map constructions are not required.
