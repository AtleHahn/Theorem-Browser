Statement

For each integer i\geq3 with i\ne6, prescribe a nonnegative integer p_i, with only finitely many p_i nonzero. There exists a nonnegative integer p_6 and a simple convex three-dimensional polyhedron having exactly p_i faces with i sides for every i\geq3 if and only if

\sum_{\substack{i\geq3\\i\ne6}}(6-i)p_i=12.

Here simple means that exactly three edges meet at each vertex. A convex polyhedron in this statement is a bounded three-dimensional convex polytope, with its polygonal faces.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement requires only elementary solid geometry, counting faces by their number of sides, and a finite linear equation. The term simple is explicitly defined.

Proof 1: Spherical maps, triangular refinement and duality (external)

A supporting proof source can be found here (Mathematica Slovaca27(1977),383–407: §§2–3 constructive elements; §4.3 pp.401–406; final remark p.406 explicitly includes sphere when the two vertex parity sums vanish.). The additional argument below supplies the stated scope and is not attributed to that source.

The following refinement and realization argument supplements the source's construction. Set g=0 and every prescribed v_j=0 for j\ge4. Condition (a) of the final remark is automatic, so the construction gives a cubic spherical cell map with the prescribed nonhexagonal faces.

Dualize this map and trisect each triangular face in the triangular lattice into nine triangles. Original vertex degrees are unchanged; new interior and edge vertices have degree six. Distinct edge-interior vertices distinguish repeated original edges, and face-interior vertices distinguish original faces; corner triangles have distinct incident edge points. Thus the refined map is a simplicial sphere. Its dual is a simple 3-connected planar cubic graph, so Steinitz's theorem realizes it as a simple convex 3-polytope. The new degree-six vertices correspond only to additional hexagons. Conversely, 3V=2E, \sum_i i p_i=2E and Euler's formula imply \sum_i(6-i)p_i=12.
