Statement

Let \Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{v_1,\ldots,v_n\} be an (n-1)-simplex. For each nonempty J\subseteq[n] let D_J\subseteq\Delta be closed. Suppose \operatorname{conv}\{v_i:i\in I\}\subseteq\bigcup_{\varnothing\ne J\subseteq I}D_J for every nonempty I\subseteq[n]. A collection B of nonempty subsets of [n]=\{1,\ldots,n\} is balanced if there are weights w_J\ge0 such that \sum_{J\in B:i\in J}w_J=1 for every i\in[n]. There exists a balanced collection B such that \bigcap_{J\in B}D_J\ne\varnothing.

Proof 1: Choose the barycenters in Komiya’s theorem — outline

Use Komiya’s theorem: if the closed sets D_F on a compact convex polytope P cover every face F by the sets indexed by its subfaces, and points b^F\in F are chosen, then some collection B satisfies \bigcap_{F\in B}D_F\ne\varnothing and b^P\in\operatorname{conv}\{b^F:F\in B\}. Take P=\Delta and choose b^F to be the barycenter of each face. In standard simplex coordinates, b^\Delta=(1/n,\ldots,1/n). This is the source’s specified specialization to KKMS: the face-cover conditions agree, and barycentric balance is the stated weight balance. The elementary conversion between the convex-combination coefficients and the balancing weights is left implicit.
