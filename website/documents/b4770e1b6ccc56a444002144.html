Statement

Let three distinct circles lie in a Euclidean plane, and suppose each pair has two distinct common external tangents. In the projective completion of the plane, let H_{12},H_{23},H_{31} be the intersection points of these respective pairs of tangents (points at infinity when the tangents are parallel). Then the three points are collinear.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The assertion includes parallel tangent pairs through projective points at infinity. Projective completion and spatial plane incidence are a limited extension of undergraduate geometry, needed even in the equal-radius cases.

Proof 1: Similar cones and two planes — outline

Use cones of equal apex angle with the three circles as bases, creating three similar cones. Take them to be right circular cones on the same side of the base plane. Any pair of these similarly oriented three-dimensional objects has a center of similarity, about which one can scale either object to coincide with the other; the center is understood to be at infinity in the equal-radius case. Their centers of similarity are the external homothetic centers of the circular bases.

Further, the line connecting any two apex points must also contain their center of similarity. Take a plane through the three apex points, distinct from the base plane. All three centers of similarity lie in this plane, and all three must also lie in the plane containing the circular bases. Hence, the three centers must lie on the intersection of the two planes, which must be a line in projective three-dimensional space.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Lift the circles to similar cones and intersect the apex plane with the base plane, including centers at infinity. Undergraduate spatial/projective geometry suffices for this incidence argument; the Euclidean special case alone would not cover the full projective statement.
