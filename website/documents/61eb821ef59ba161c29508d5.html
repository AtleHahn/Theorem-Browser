Statement

Let Z_1,Z_2,\ldots be independent random variables. Every event A in the tail sigma algebra \bigcap_{n\ge1}\sigma(Z_n,Z_{n+1},\ldots) has probability zero or one.

Proof 1: Compare constant conditional tail probabilities with their indicator limit

For a tail event A of an independent sequence and \mathcal F_k=\sigma(Z_1,\ldots,Z_k), independence gives \Pr(A\mid\mathcal F_k)=\Pr(A). The event belongs to \mathcal F_\infty=\sigma(Z_1,Z_2,\ldots), so Levy’s event-indicator specialization gives limit 1_A. Hence \Pr(A)=1_A almost surely and the probability is zero or one.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Compare the constant conditional probabilities with Levy convergence to an event indicator. The argument presupposes generated sigma algebras, conditional expectations and almost-sure martingale convergence, a substantial measure-theoretic framework.

Proof 2: Specialize the independent-sigma-algebra law

Take F_n=\sigma(Z_n). Independence of the random variables gives independence of these sigma-algebras. The terminal sigma-algebra is defined as \bigcap_n\sigma(\bigcup_{k\ge n}F_k).
