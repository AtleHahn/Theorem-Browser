Statement

If m,r\ge1 and m(r-1)+1 objects are assigned to m boxes, some box contains at least r objects.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Understanding the claim requires finite counts, elementary arithmetic and assignment of objects to finitely many values or classes.

Mathematical Field
Combinatorics
Required topics
finite counting
Review notes
none

Proof 1: Equal thresholds in the strong form

Use the strong pigeonhole principle as an external premise: if positive integers q_1,\ldots,q_m are prescribed, then distributing \sum_iq_i-m+1 objects into m boxes forces some box i to contain at least q_i objects. Put q_1=\cdots=q_m=r. Then \sum_iq_i-m+1=mr-m+1=m(r-1)+1, giving the conclusion.

Level

Level justification

Set every threshold equal to r in the explicit counting bound and simplify. School counting and algebra suffice.
