Statement

For every real p>0 there are constants A_p,B_p>0, depending only on p, such that for every complex square-summable sequence (x_n)_{n\ge1} and independent random signs \varepsilon_n with \Pr(\varepsilon_n=1)=\Pr(\varepsilon_n=-1)=1/2,

A_p\Big(\sum_{n\ge1}|x_n|^2\Big)^{1/2}\le\left(\mathbb E\left|\sum_{n\ge1}\varepsilon_nx_n\right|^p\right)^{1/p}\le B_p\Big(\sum_{n\ge1}|x_n|^2\Big)^{1/2}.

The random series is interpreted as the limit of its partial sums in mean square. This includes finite sequences by setting subsequent coefficients to zero. The expectation is taken over the independent signs; square-summable means \sum_n|x_n|^2<\infty.
