Statement

For a real v>0, give the values 1 and -v weights v/(1+v) and 1/(1+v). Mean is their weighted sum, variance is their weighted squared deviation from the mean, and event probability is the sum of its weights. This distribution has mean 0, variance v, and \Pr(X\ge1)=v/(1+v), attaining the one-sided bound at threshold 1.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All probability and moment definitions are explicit finite weighted sums.

Proof 1: Compute the two weighted moments — outline

The bound on the one tailed variant is known to be sharp. To see this consider the random variable X that takes the values

X=1 with probability \dfrac{v}{1+v},

X=-v with probability \dfrac{1}{1+v}.

Then \mathbb E(X)=0 and \mathbb E(X^2)=v and \Pr(X<1)=1/(1+v). Thus \Pr(X\ge1)=v/(1+v).

Level
High school only
Level justification

The given weighted definitions reduce everything to two-term arithmetic: the mean is (v-v)/(1+v)=0 and the second moment is (v+v²)/(1+v)=v. Only the value 1 lies in the event X>=1.
