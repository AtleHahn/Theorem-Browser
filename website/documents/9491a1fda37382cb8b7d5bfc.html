Statement

Let \gamma=\lim_{N\to\infty}(\sum_{n=1}^N1/n-\log N) and E_1(x)=e^{-x}\int_0^\infty e^{-xy}/(1+y)\,dy for x>0. Then \gamma=\int_0^\infty-e^{-x}\log x\,dx=\int_0^\infty x^{-1}[(1+x)^{-1}-e^{-x}]\,dx.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Improper real integration; Logarithms; Exponential integral defined locally; Euler constant. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. Proof 1: Two integral representations of Euler’s constant — outline

Use the known identity \gamma=\int_0^\infty-e^{-x}\log x\,dx. The equivalence of the two integrals can be shown by executing Fubini’s theorem twice, relating both to the complementary exponential integral function E_1 defined in the statement.

Applying Fubini's theorem to the first integral form yields:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\gamma &=\int _{0}^{\infty }-\exp(-y)\ln(y)\,\mathrm {d} y\\&=\int _{0}^{\infty }\int _{0}^{\infty }\exp(-y)\left({\frac {1}{x+y}}-{\frac {1}{x+1}}\right)\,\mathrm {d} x\,\mathrm {d} y\\&=\int _{0}^{\infty }\int _{0}^{\infty }\exp(-y)\left({\frac {1}{x+y}}-{\frac {1}{x+1}}\right)\,\mathrm {d} y\,\mathrm {d} x\\&=\int _{0}^{\infty }\left[\exp(x)\,\mathrm {E} _{1}(x)-{\frac {1}{x+1}}\right]\,\mathrm {d} x\end{aligned}}}

Applying Fubini's theorem to the intermediate integral connects it to the second form:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\gamma &=\int _{0}^{\infty }\left[\exp(x)\,\mathrm {E} _{1}(x)-{\frac {1}{x+1}}\right]\,\mathrm {d} x\\&=\int _{0}^{\infty }\int _{0}^{\infty }\exp(-xz)\left[{\frac {1}{z+1}}-\exp(-z)\right]\,\mathrm {d} z\,\mathrm {d} x\\&=\int _{0}^{\infty }\int _{0}^{\infty }\exp(-xz)\left[{\frac {1}{z+1}}-\exp(-z)\right]\,\mathrm {d} x\,\mathrm {d} z\\&=\int _{0}^{\infty }{\frac {1}{z}}\left[{\frac {1}{z+1}}-\exp(-z)\right]\,\mathrm {d} z\end{aligned}}}

Level justification

The proof interchanges explicit absolutely integrable real-variable integrals and integrates elementary exponential kernels. E1 is defined by an ordinary improper integral; calculus and introductory analysis suffice for these concrete applications.
