Statement

For an integer n\ge1 and real numbers a_1,\ldots,a_n,b_1,\ldots,b_n,

\sum_{j=1}^n\sum_{k=1}^n(a_j-a_k)(b_j-b_k)=2n\sum_{j=1}^n a_jb_j-2\left(\sum_{j=1}^na_j\right)\left(\sum_{k=1}^nb_k\right).
Level
High school only
Level justification

The identity involves finitely many real numbers, products and sums.

Proof 1: Expand the double sum

Expand the brackets in the double sum and collect its terms. This gives

\sum_{j=1}^n\sum_{k=1}^n(a_j-a_k)(b_j-b_k)=2n\sum_{j=1}^n a_jb_j-2\left(\sum_{j=1}^na_j\right)\left(\sum_{k=1}^nb_k\right).
Level
High school only
Level justification

Expand four terms in each summand and collect repeated diagonal terms and two separated products of sums. Elementary finite algebra suffices.
