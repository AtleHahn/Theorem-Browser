Statement

Let n\ge2, let \mu be Lebesgue measure on \mathbb R^n, let B be the closed Euclidean unit ball, and let K be a convex body of positive volume. Define S(K)=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}[\mu(K+\varepsilon B)-\mu(K)]/\varepsilon, the surface area given by the Minkowski–Steiner formula. Then S(K)\ge n\mu(B)^{1/n}\mu(K)^{(n-1)/n}; equivalently, \mu(K)^{1/n}/S(K)^{1/(n-1)}\le\mu(B)^{1/n}/S(B)^{1/(n-1)}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Multivariable calculus and linear algebra supply convex bodies, orthogonal projections and volume. Surface area is the familiar geometric quantity, explicitly specified by a volume derivative in the latter formulation; this is a small extension without Riemannian geometry.

Proof 1: Take the first variation of the Brunn–Minkowski lower bound

First, observe that Brunn–Minkowski implies {\textstyle \mu (K+\varepsilon B)\geq (\mu (K)^{1/n}+\varepsilon \mu(B)^{1/n})^{n}=\mu (K)(1+\varepsilon ({\frac {\mu (B)}{\mu (K)}})^{1/n})^{n}\geq \mu (K)(1+n\varepsilon ({\frac {\mu (B)}{\mu (K)}})^{1/n}),} where in the last inequality we used that {\textstyle (1+x)^{n}\geq 1+nx} for {\textstyle x\geq 0}. We use this calculation to lower bound the surface area of {\textstyle K} via {\textstyle S(K)=\lim _{\varepsilon \to 0}{\frac {\mu (K+\varepsilon B)-\mu (K)}{\varepsilon }}\geq n\mu (K)({\frac {\mu (B)}{\mu (K)}})^{1/n}.} Next, we use the fact that {\textstyle S(B)=n\mu (B)}, which follows from the Minkowski-Steiner formula, to calculate {\textstyle {\frac {S(K)}{S(B)}}={\frac {S(K)}{n\mu (B)}}\geq {\frac {\mu (K)({\frac {\mu (B)}{\mu (K)}})^{1/n}}{\mu (B)}}=\mu (K)^{\frac {n-1}{n}}\mu (B)^{\frac {1-n}{n}}.} Rearranging this yields the isoperimetric inequality: {\textstyle {\frac {\mu (B)^{1/n}}{S(B)^{1/(n-1)}}}\geq {\frac {\mu (K)^{1/n}}{S(K)^{1/(n-1)}}}.}
