Statement

Let r>0 and let f be holomorphic near |z-c|\le r, and put M_r=\max_{|w-c|=r}|f(w)|. Let k\ge0 be an integer and 0\le\beta<1. Then for |z-c|\le\beta r, \left|f(z)-\sum_{j=0}^k\frac{f^{(j)}(c)}{j!}(z-c)^j\right|\le\frac{M_r\beta^{k+1}}{1-\beta}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Calculus and complex arithmetic supply the formulas, curves and estimates. Complex differentiability, holomorphic logarithm branches or conformal bijections are closely related extensions; the contour integral can be understood by parametrization and the branch is explicitly fixed where needed. No Riemann surfaces, residues or differential forms are required by these statements.

Proof 1: Sum the geometric majorant

The holomorphic Taylor expansion holds in the form

f(z)=P_k(z)+R_k(z),\qquad P_k(z)=\sum_{j=0}^k\frac{f^{(j)}(c)}{j!}(z-c)^j,

where the remainder term R_k is the tail of the convergent Taylor series. Cauchy's estimates give

|f^{(j)}(c)|\leq\frac{j!M_r}{r^j}, \qquad M_r=\max_{|w-c|=r}|f(w)|.

Applying Cauchy's estimates to the series expression for the remainder, one obtains the uniform estimates

\begin{aligned} |R_k(z)| &\leq\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}\frac{M_r|z-c|^j}{r^j}\\ &=\frac{M_r}{r^{k+1}}\frac{|z-c|^{k+1}}{1-\frac{|z-c|}{r}}\\ &\leq\frac{M_r\beta^{k+1}}{1-\beta}, \qquad\frac{|z-c|}{r}\leq\beta<1. \end{aligned}
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Bound the tail using holomorphic Taylor expansion and Cauchy derivative estimates. The geometric sum is calculus, but the input statements concern general holomorphic functions and their circle bounds.
