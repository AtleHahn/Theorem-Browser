Statement

For nonnegative real sequences (a_n) and (b_n):

  • If a_n\leq b_n for all sufficiently large n and \sum_{n=1}^\infty b_n converges, then \sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n converges.
  • If b_n\leq a_n for all sufficiently large n and \sum_{n=1}^\infty b_n diverges, then \sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n diverges.

For complex sequences (a_n) and (b_n):

  • If |a_n|\leq|b_n| eventually and \sum b_n converges absolutely, then \sum a_n converges absolutely.
  • If |b_n|\leq|a_n| eventually and \sum b_n does not converge absolutely, then \sum a_n does not converge absolutely.

Absolute convergence means convergence of the series of absolute values; failure of absolute convergence does not exclude conditional convergence.

For continuous real functions f,g on [a,b), where a<b and b=+\infty or b is a finite endpoint at which both functions have a vertical asymptote:

  • If 0\leq f(x)\leq g(x) throughout [a,b) and \int_a^b g(x)\,dx converges, then \int_a^b f(x)\,dx converges and is at most \int_a^b g(x)\,dx.
  • If 0\leq g(x)\leq f(x) throughout [a,b) and \int_a^b g(x)\,dx diverges, then \int_a^b f(x)\,dx diverges.

Here the improper integral is the limit of \int_a^t as t\uparrow b.
