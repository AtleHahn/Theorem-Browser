Statement

Let X_1,X_2,\ldots be independent identically distributed real random variables with distribution function F(x)=\mathbb P(X_1\le x). Suppose there are real sequences a_n>0, b_n for which

\frac{\max(X_1,\ldots,X_n)-b_n}{a_n}

converges in distribution to a nondegenerate distribution with distribution function G. Nondegenerate means not concentrated at a single point; convergence in distribution means convergence of the distribution functions at every continuity point of G.

Then there exist \mu\in\mathbb R, \sigma>0 and \gamma\in\mathbb R such that G(x)=G_\gamma((x-\mu)/\sigma), where

G_0(s)=\exp(-e^{-s}),

and for \gamma\ne0,

G_\gamma(s)= \begin{cases} \exp\bigl(-(1+\gamma s)^{-1/\gamma}\bigr),&1+\gamma s>0,\\ 0,&\gamma>0,\ s\le-1/\gamma,\\ 1,&\gamma<0,\ s\ge-1/\gamma. \end{cases}

These are respectively the Gumbel (\gamma=0), Fréchet (\gamma>0) and reversed Weibull (\gamma<0) families for maxima, allowing location and positive scale changes. The theorem classifies a nondegenerate limit if one exists; it does not assert convergence for every F.

Level justification

Undergraduate probability and real analysis suffice. Convergence in distribution and nondegeneracy are explicitly defined using familiar distribution functions and limits, without measure-valued weak convergence machinery.
