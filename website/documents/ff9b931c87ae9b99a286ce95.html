Statement

Let A,B be Hermitian positive semidefinite matrices of the same finite size and n\ge1 an integer. Then for 0\le j<n, \operatorname{tr}((A^{2^j}B^{2^j})^{2^{n-j}})\le\operatorname{tr}((A^{2^{j+1}}B^{2^{j+1}})^{2^{n-j-1}}). In particular \operatorname{tr}((AB)^{2^n})\le\operatorname{tr}(A^{2^n}B^{2^n}).

Proof 1: Iterate Cauchy–Schwarz through positive matrix products (outline)

Use the Frobenius inner product \langle X,Y\rangle=\operatorname{tr}(XY^*) and norm \|X\|_F^2=\operatorname{tr}(XX^*).

If

\operatorname{tr}((AB)^{2^n}) \le\operatorname{tr}((A^2B^2)^{2^{n-1}})

for all n\ge1, then all the other inequalities are also proved as special cases of it. So it suffices to prove that inequality, by induction on n.

For the n=1 case, since A,B are Hermitian and positive semidefinite, we can split A into (\sqrt A)^2, which allows us to write

\operatorname{tr}(ABAB)=\|\sqrt A B\sqrt A\|_F^2,

meaning it is a nonnegative real number.

Now by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

\begin{aligned} \operatorname{tr}(ABAB) &=\langle AB,BA\rangle\\ &\le\|AB\|_F\|BA\|_F\\ &=\|AB\|_F^2\\ &=\operatorname{tr}(ABBA)\\ &=\operatorname{tr}(AABB). \end{aligned}

For the n\ge2 case, define two sequences of matrices

a_k:=(AB)^{2^{n-k}}(BA)^{2^{n-k}},\qquad b_k:=(BA)^{2^{n-k}}(AB)^{2^{n-k}} \quad(1\le k\le n),

which, by construction, are Hermitian and positive semidefinite.

For any positive integer N, by the cyclic property of trace,

\begin{cases} \operatorname{tr}(a_k^N) =\operatorname{tr}((a_{k+1}b_{k+1})^N) =\operatorname{tr}(b_k^N),&1\le k<n,\\ \operatorname{tr}(a_n^N) =\operatorname{tr}((AABB)^N) =\operatorname{tr}(b_n^N). \end{cases}

By the same Cauchy–Schwarz argument as in the n=1 case,

\operatorname{tr}((AB)^{2^n})\le\operatorname{tr}(a_1).

Apply Cauchy–Schwarz and the cyclic equalities:

\begin{aligned} \operatorname{tr}(a_1) &=\operatorname{tr}(a_2b_2)\\ &=\langle a_2,b_2\rangle\\ &\le\|a_2\|_F\|b_2\|_F\\ &=\|a_2\|_F^2\\ &=\operatorname{tr}(a_2a_2). \end{aligned}

If n=2, then \operatorname{tr}(a_2a_2)=\operatorname{tr}((AABB)^2).

Otherwise, by the lower-exponent induction hypothesis,

\operatorname{tr}(a_2a_2) =\operatorname{tr}((a_3b_3)^2) \le\operatorname{tr}(a_3a_3b_3b_3),

and continuing the same Cauchy–Schwarz argument,

\operatorname{tr}(a_3a_3b_3b_3)\le\operatorname{tr}(a_3^4).

This continues until we obtain the upper bound

\operatorname{tr}(a_n^{2^{n-1}}) =\operatorname{tr}((AABB)^{2^{n-1}}).

Applying the first-step inequality to successive powers of A,B gives the whole chain. The source leaves the repetitive intermediate induction steps implicit.
