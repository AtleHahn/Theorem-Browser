Statement

Let k be any field, let K be an algebraically closed field extension of k, and let A=k[X_1,\ldots,X_n] for an integer n\ge1. For an ideal J\subseteq A, define

V_K(J)=\{a\in K^n:f(a)=0\text{ for every }f\in J\},\qquad I_k(S)=\{p\in A:p(a)=0\text{ for every }a\in S\}.

Then

I_k(V_K(J))=\sqrt J,\qquad \sqrt J=\{p\in A:p^r\in J\text{ for some integer }r\ge1\}.

In particular a polynomial over k that vanishes at every common zero in K^n has a positive integer power in J; the converse also holds.

An ideal is an additive subgroup closed under multiplication by elements of its ring. A field is algebraically closed if every nonconstant polynomial over it has a root in it. When k=K, this is the usual vanishing-ideal/zero-locus equality over an algebraically closed field.

Level justification

Introductory abstract algebra supplies fields, polynomials and ideals. The statement defines zero loci and radical by evaluation and powers; an algebraically closed extension is a small extension of those notions without Galois or scheme theory.
