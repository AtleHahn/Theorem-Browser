Statement

The open unit disk with its Euclidean metric has empty manifold boundary, Gaussian curvature zero, and Euler characteristic one. Thus \int_DK\,dA=0\ne2\pi\chi(D), so the compact closed-surface Gauss–Bonnet formula cannot be applied to it.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Curvature of arbitrary Riemannian surfaces and Euler characteristic require differential geometry and topology beyond calculus/linear algebra.

Proof 1: The open disk shows compactness is needed in the closed-surface formula — proof

Compactness of the surface is of crucial importance. Consider for instance the open unit disc, a non-compact surface without boundary, with its Euclidean metric, curvature 0 and Euler characteristic 1: the Gauss–Bonnet formula does not work, since its two sides would be 0 and 2\pi.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Compare the supplied curvature and Euler-characteristic values for a noncompact manifold without manifold boundary. Curvature, manifold boundary and topological Euler characteristic together are not confidently a small extension of ordinary undergraduate calculus, despite the final numerical mismatch.
