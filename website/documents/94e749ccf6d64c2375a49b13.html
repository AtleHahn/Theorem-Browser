Statement

For every nonconstant complex polynomial p, there is z_0\in\mathbb C such that |p(z_0)|\le |p(z)| for all z\in\mathbb C.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The assertion concerns finite polynomials over the real or complex numbers, their roots, factorization, absolute values or reciprocal substitutions. These are elementary algebraic concepts. This rating concerns understanding the statement, independently of the difficulty of its proof.

Proof 1: Restrict the minimum to a sufficiently large disk

By polynomial growth at infinity, find a closed disk D of radius r centered at the origin such that |p(z)|>|p(0)| whenever |z|\ge r. The minimum of |p(z)| on D, which must exist since |p| is continuous and D is compact, is therefore achieved at some point z_0 in the interior of D, but not at any point of its boundary. The same inequality outside D makes this a global minimum.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use leading-term growth to exclude the exterior of a large disk and the extreme value theorem on that compact disk. Undergraduate real analysis of the complex plane suffices.
