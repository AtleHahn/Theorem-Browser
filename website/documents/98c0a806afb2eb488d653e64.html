Statement

Let T=\{0,1\}^{\mathbb N_{>0}} be the set of all sequences of zeros and ones indexed by positive integers. The map f:T\to\mathbb R defined by f(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}t(n)10^{-n} is injective.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves binary sequence space; real decimal expansions; infinite series; injective maps. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Use only zero and one as decimal digits (outline)

An injection from T to R is given by mapping binary strings in T to decimal fractions, such as mapping t = 0111... to the decimal 0.0111.... This function, defined by f (t) = 0.t, is an injection because it maps different strings to different numbers.

Here 0.t is the series specified in the statement. Decimal expansions are unique except for the terminating/eventually-9 ambiguity, which cannot occur when only the digits 0 and 1 are used.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The map is defined by a convergent geometric-dominated series. Applying decimal uniqueness excludes the only ambiguity because digit 9 never occurs. Introductory real analysis suffices; the first differing digit also dominates the entire remaining binary-digit tail by a geometric-series bound.
