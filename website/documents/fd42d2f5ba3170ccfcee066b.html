Statement

If f is real-valued and continuous on a real compact rectangle [a,b]\times[c,d] with a<b, then \varphi(\alpha)=\int_a^bf(x,\alpha)\,dx is continuous on [c,d].

Proof 1: Integrate the uniform continuity bound

By the Heine–Cantor theorem f is uniformly continuous on the compact rectangle [a,b]\times[c,d]. In other words, for any \varepsilon>0 there exists \delta>0 such that, whenever |\Delta\alpha|<\delta and \alpha,\alpha+\Delta\alpha\in[c,d], for all values of x in [a,b],

|f(x,\alpha+\Delta\alpha)-f(x,\alpha)|<\varepsilon.

On the other hand,

\begin{aligned} \Delta\varphi&=\varphi(\alpha+\Delta\alpha)-\varphi(\alpha)\\ &=\int_a^b f(x,\alpha+\Delta\alpha)\,dx-\int_a^b f(x,\alpha)\,dx\\ &=\int_a^b\left(f(x,\alpha+\Delta\alpha)-f(x,\alpha)\right)\,dx, \end{aligned}

and consequently

|\Delta\varphi| \leq\int_a^b|f(x,\alpha+\Delta\alpha)-f(x,\alpha)|\,dx \leq\varepsilon(b-a).

Hence \varphi(\alpha) is a continuous function.
