Statement

Let H=\ell^2(\mathbb N_0;\mathbb F) for \mathbb F=\mathbb R or \mathbb C, with norm \|x\|_2=(\sum_{n\ge0}|x_n|^2)^{1/2}. On its closed unit ball define f(x)=y by y_0=\sqrt{1-\|x\|_2^2} and y_n=x_{n-1} for n\ge1. This map is continuous, takes values in the unit sphere, and has no fixed point.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Basic topology and real analysis supply compactness, completeness, continuity and sequence convergence; linear algebra supplies inner products and orthogonality. Hilbert completeness and the explicitly specified bounds or maps are direct extensions, with no spectral or weak-topology theory needed.

Proof 1: Insert the missing norm and shift — outline

For x in the closed unit ball of \ell^2, define

{\displaystyle y_{0}={\sqrt {1-\|x\|_{2}^{2}}}\quad {\text{ and}}\quad y_{n}=x_{n-1}{\text{ for }}n\geq 1.}

It is not difficult to check that this map is continuous, has its image in the unit sphere of ℓ2, but does not have a fixed point.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the explicit sum-of-squares norm to check the image norm, norm continuity for the first coordinate and the isometric shift for the rest. A fixed sequence would be constant and hence zero by square summability, contradicting the first coordinate. Undergraduate series and metric analysis suffice for this explicit sequence space.
