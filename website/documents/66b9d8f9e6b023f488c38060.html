Statement

Let C\subseteq\mathbb R^d be a rational polyhedral cone, that is, the solution set of finitely many homogeneous linear inequalities with rational coefficients. There are finitely many u_1,\ldots,u_r\in C\cap\mathbb Z^d such that every x\in C\cap\mathbb Z^d is a sum \sum_{i=1}^r n_i u_i with nonnegative integers n_i.

Proof 1: Split coefficients into integer and bounded fractional parts

Let \sigma=C^\vee be the dual cone of the given rational polyhedral cone C, where

C^\vee=\{y:\langle x,y\rangle\ge0\text{ for all }x\in C\}.

By rational polyhedral cone duality, there are integral vectors u_1,\ldots,u_r such that

\sigma=\{y:\langle u_i,y\rangle\ge0,\ 1\le i\le r\}.

Then the u_i generate the dual cone \sigma^\vee=C: indeed, writing C_0 for the cone generated by the u_i, we have \sigma=C_0^\vee, and taking duals gives \sigma^\vee=C_0.

Now, if x is in the semigroup

S_\sigma=\sigma^\vee\cap\mathbb Z^d,

then, by splitting its nonnegative real coefficients into integer and fractional parts, it can be written as

x=\sum_i n_i u_i+\sum_i r_i u_i,

where the n_i are nonnegative integers and 0\le r_i\le1. But since x and the first sum on the right-hand side are integral, the second sum is a lattice point in a bounded region, and so there are only finitely many possibilities for the second sum. Hence S_\sigma is finitely generated by these remainders together with the u_i.
