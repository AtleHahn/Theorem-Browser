Statement

For n≥1 and variables x_1,\ldots,x_n, let p_k=\sum_jx_j^k for k≥1, let e_0=1, and let e_k=\sum_{j_1<\cdots<j_k}x_{j_1}\cdots x_{j_k}, with e_k=0 for k>n. Let h_0=1 and h_k=\sum_{a_1+\cdots+a_n=k,\ a_j\ge0}x_1^{a_1}\cdots x_n^{a_n}. For k≥1 let M be k×k with M_{i1}=i h_i, M_{ij}=h_{i-j+1} for 2≤j≤i, M_{i,i+1}=1, and other entries zero. Then p_k=(-1)^{k-1}det M.

Proof 1: Symmetric-polynomial determinant: p from h

Consider the first k complete homogeneous counterparts of Newton's identities,

j h_j=\sum_{i=1}^j h_{j-i}p_i,\qquad 1\le j\le k,

as linear equations in which the complete homogeneous symmetric functions are known and the power sums are unknown, and apply Cramer's rule to find the solution for the final unknown. The analogous computation gives

p_k=(-1)^{k-1} \begin{vmatrix} h_1&1&0&\cdots\\ 2h_2&h_1&1&0&\cdots\\ 3h_3&h_2&h_1&1\\ \vdots&&&\ddots&\ddots\\ kh_k&h_{k-1}&\cdots&&h_1 \end{vmatrix}.

The source supplies this determinant and leaves the intermediate Cramer computation implicit.
