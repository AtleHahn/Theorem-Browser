Statement

Let G be finite and let K=\mathbb C. Then K[G] is isomorphic as a K-algebra to a finite product \prod_i M_{n_i}(\mathbb C), one factor for each isomorphism class of irreducible complex representations of G.

Proof 1: Maschke followed by Wedderburn–Artin

Representations of a group G are replaced by modules over its group algebra K[G]. Irreducible representations correspond to simple modules.

Maschke's theorem states: Let G be a finite group and K a field whose characteristic does not divide the order of G. Then K[G], the group algebra of G, is semisimple. This applies to K=\mathbb C, which has characteristic zero; also K[G] has finite dimension |G| over K.

The importance of this result stems from the well developed theory of semisimple rings, in particular, their classification as given by the Wedderburn–Artin theorem. In the needed form, a finite-dimensional semisimple algebra over an algebraically closed field is a product of full matrix algebras over that field, indexed by its simple-module classes. When K is the field of complex numbers, this shows that the algebra K[G] is a product of several copies of complex matrix algebras, one for each irreducible representation.
