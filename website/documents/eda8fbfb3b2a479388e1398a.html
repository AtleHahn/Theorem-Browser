Every first-order consequence of an infinite theory already follows from finitely many of its premises. This links semantic entailment with finite reasoning and reveals both the power and expressive limitations of first-order compactness.

Statement

Let \Gamma be a set of sentences in a well-orderable first-order language, and let \varphi be a sentence. If \Gamma\models\varphi, then some finite \Gamma_0\subseteq\Gamma satisfies \Gamma_0\models\varphi.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The result concerns formal syntax and semantic structures in mathematical logic, beyond undergraduate calculus and linear algebra.

Proof 1: Collect the finite premises in a proof

This is an immediate consequence of the completeness theorem, because only a finite number of axioms from \Gamma can be mentioned in a formal deduction of \varphi, and the soundness of the deductive system then implies that \varphi is a logical consequence of this finite set.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Use completeness to obtain a derivation and soundness to transfer its finite premise set back to semantic consequence. Retain beyond undergraduate provisionally for first-order models, formal consequence and the syntax-semantics relationship.
