Statement

The first uncountable ordinal \omega_1, with its order topology, and the two-sided long line L, with its order topology, are both \omega-bounded but not compact. A space is \omega-bounded if the closure of each countable subset is compact. Compactness means that every open cover has a finite subcover. The set \omega_1 consists of all countable ordinals, ordered by ordinal comparison; ordinals represent order types of well-ordered sets. To define L, first give L_+=\omega_1\times[0,1) the lexicographic order: compare the ordinal coordinates first and then the real coordinates. Take a reversed-order copy of L_+\setminus\{(0,0)\} followed by a copy of L_+; their ordered disjoint union is L. The order topology is generated by open intervals and open initial and final rays. No endpoint at infinity is adjoined. Thus \omega-boundedness does not imply compactness in general.

Level justification
