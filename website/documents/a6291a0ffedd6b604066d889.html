Proof 1: Apply Riemann–Roch to the canonical bundle

Write h^0(X,L) for the complex dimension of holomorphic sections of a line bundle L. The trivial bundle has h^0=1, since holomorphic functions on a compact connected Riemann surface are constant.

Since the canonical bundle {\displaystyle K} has {\displaystyle h^{0}(X,K)=g}, applying Riemann–Roch to {\displaystyle L=K} gives

{\displaystyle h^{0}(X,K)-h^{0}(X,K^{-1}\otimes K)=\deg(K)+1-g}

which can be rewritten as

{\displaystyle g-1=\deg(K)+1-g}

hence the degree of the canonical bundle is {\displaystyle \deg(K)=2g-2}.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Solving the last scalar equation is elementary, but Riemann–Roch here concerns holomorphic line bundles, their sections and degree on compact Riemann surfaces. Those linked geometric prerequisites are substantial.
