Statement

Let A be a Noetherian integral domain and let A\to B be a unital homomorphism of commutative rings making B a finitely generated A-algebra. There is a nonzero g\in A such that B[g^{-1}] is free as an A[g^{-1}]-module.

Proof 1: Normalization and prime-filtration induction outline

To prove this, let {\displaystyle F} be the fraction field of {\displaystyle A}. We argue by induction on the Krull dimension of {\displaystyle F\otimes _{A}B}. The base case is when the Krull dimension is {\displaystyle -\infty }; i.e., {\displaystyle F\otimes _{A}B=0}; that is, when there is some {\displaystyle 0\neq g\in A} such that {\displaystyle gB=0} , so that {\displaystyle B[g^{-1}]} is free as an {\displaystyle A[g^{-1}]}-module. For the inductive step, note that {\displaystyle F\otimes _{A}B} is a finitely generated {\displaystyle F}-algebra. Hence by the Noether normalization lemma (a finite-type algebra over a field is module-finite over a polynomial subalgebra), {\displaystyle F\otimes _{A}B} contains algebraically independent elements {\displaystyle x_{1},\dots ,x_{d}} such that {\displaystyle F\otimes _{A}B} is finite over the polynomial ring {\displaystyle F[x_{1},\dots ,x_{d}]}. Multiplying each {\displaystyle x_{i}} by elements of {\displaystyle A}, we can assume {\displaystyle x_{i}} are in {\displaystyle B}. We now consider:

{\displaystyle A':=A[x_{1},\dots ,x_{d}]\to B.}

The algebra {\displaystyle B} may not be finite over {\displaystyle A'}, but it will become finite after inverting a single element as follows. If {\displaystyle b} is an element of {\displaystyle B}, then, as an element of {\displaystyle F\otimes _{A}B}, it is integral over {\displaystyle F[x_{1},\dots ,x_{d}]}; i.e., {\displaystyle b^{n}+a_{1}b^{n-1}+\dots +a_{n}=0} for some {\displaystyle a_{i}} in {\displaystyle F[x_{1},\dots ,x_{d}]}. Thus, some {\displaystyle 0\neq g\in A} kills all the denominators of the coefficients of {\displaystyle a_{i}} and so {\displaystyle b} is integral over {\displaystyle A'[g^{-1}]}. Choosing some finitely many generators of {\displaystyle B} as an {\displaystyle A'}-algebra and applying this observation to each generator, we find some {\displaystyle 0\neq g\in A} such that {\displaystyle B[g^{-1}]} is integral (thus finite) over {\displaystyle A'[g^{-1}]}. Replace {\displaystyle B,A} by {\displaystyle B[g^{-1}],A[g^{-1}]} and then we can assume {\displaystyle B} is finite over {\displaystyle A':=A[x_{1},\dots ,x_{d}]}. To finish, use the standard finite prime filtration ending in zero, and write it as a finite filtration {\displaystyle B=B_{0}\supset B_{1}\supset B_{2}\supset \cdots \supset B_{r}=0} by {\displaystyle A'}-submodules such that {\displaystyle B_{i}/B_{i+1}\simeq A'/{\mathfrak {p}}_{i}} for prime ideals {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {p}}_{i}} (such a filtration exists by the theory of associated primes). For 0\le i<r, if {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {p}}_{i}\neq 0}, by inductive hypothesis, we can choose some {\displaystyle g_{i}\neq 0} in {\displaystyle A} such that {\displaystyle A'/{\mathfrak {p}}_{i}[g_{i}^{-1}]} is free as an {\displaystyle A[g_{i}^{-1}]}-module, while {\displaystyle A'} is a polynomial ring and thus free. Hence, with a product g of the finitely many chosen nonzero localization elements, {\displaystyle B[g^{-1}]} is a free module over {\displaystyle A[g^{-1}]}. {\displaystyle \square }

The argument uses the standard localization criterion for equality (so denominator clearing includes the finitely many relations), the decrease of generic-fiber dimension for nonzero prime quotients of a polynomial ring, and splitting of extensions with free quotient. These are the algebraic prerequisites to the source’s compressed induction.
