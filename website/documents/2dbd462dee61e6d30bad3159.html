Statement

For real |x|<1, \sqrt{1+x}=\sum_{k=0}^\infty\binom{1/2}{k}x^k=1+x/2-x^2/8+x^3/16-5x^4/128+\cdots, where \binom rk=r(r-1)\cdots(r-k+1)/k! for k\ge1 and \binom r0=1.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves real powers; convergent power series; generalized binomial coefficients. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Specialize the generalized binomial series — outline

Use (1+x)^r=\sum_{k=0}^\infty\binom rk x^k for real r and |x|<1, where \binom rk=r(r-1)\cdots(r-k+1)/k! and \binom r0=1.

For example, r = 1/2 gives the following series for the square root:

{\displaystyle {\sqrt {1+x}}=1+{\frac {1}{2}}x-{\frac {1}{8}}x^{2}+{\frac {1}{16}}x^{3}-{\frac {5}{128}}x^{4}+{\frac {7}{256}}x^{5}-\cdots .}
