Statement

In a finite simple graph, let d(U,V) be the number of ordered adjacent pairs in U\times V divided by |U||V|, for nonempty vertex subsets U,V. The pair (U,V) is \varepsilon-regular if |d(U',V')-d(U,V)|\leq\varepsilon whenever U'\subseteq U, V'\subseteq V, |U'|\geq\varepsilon|U| and |V'|\geq\varepsilon|V|. If 0<\varepsilon\leq1 and (X,Y) is \varepsilon-regular, fewer than \varepsilon|X| vertices in X have fewer than (d(X,Y)-\varepsilon)|Y| neighbors in Y.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Density and regularity are explicitly ratios of finite edge counts with subset-size inequalities.

Proof 1: A low-degree subset contradicts regularity

If at least \varepsilon|X| vertices of X had fewer than (d(X,Y)-\varepsilon)|Y| neighbors in Y, take their set B. Averaging those degrees into Y gives d(B,Y)<d(X,Y)-\varepsilon, witnessing irregularity of (X,Y). Thus fewer than \varepsilon|X| vertices have such a low degree.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Average the low degrees over their vertex subset and contradict the explicitly defined density condition. Finite counting and real inequalities suffice; no regularity lemma is used.
