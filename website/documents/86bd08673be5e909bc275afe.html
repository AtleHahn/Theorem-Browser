Statement

On a smooth manifold M, let \Omega^k(M) denote smooth real differential forms and let C_k^{\rm sm}(M;\mathbb Z) be smooth singular chains. Put C^k_{\rm sm}(M;\mathbb R)=\operatorname{Hom}(C_k^{\rm sm}(M;\mathbb Z),\mathbb R), with coboundary (\delta\lambda)(c)=\lambda(\partial c). Integration I(\omega)(c)=\int_c\omega induces a linear map from de Rham cohomology to the real cohomology of smooth singular cochains.

Proof 1: Use Stokes as the cochain-map identity

Differential k-forms can be integrated over a k-simplex in a natural way, by pulling back to \mathbb R^k. Extending by linearity allows one to integrate over chains. This gives a linear map from the space of k-forms to the kth group of smooth singular cochains with real coefficients. In other words, a k-form \omega defines a functional

I(\omega)(c)=\int_c\omega

on the k-chains. Stokes' theorem says that this is a cochain map; the exterior derivative, d, behaves like the dual of \partial on forms. Explicitly, for a smooth (k+1)-chain c,

(\delta I(\omega))(c)=I(\omega)(\partial c) =\int_{\partial c}\omega=\int_c d\omega=I(d\omega)(c).

This gives a homomorphism from de Rham cohomology to the real cohomology of smooth singular cochains: closed forms yield cocycles and exact forms yield coboundaries.

Level justification

Use Stokes to identify integration as a cochain map, hence pass from closed and exact forms to cocycles and coboundaries. Differential forms on manifolds and singular cohomology require advanced topology.
