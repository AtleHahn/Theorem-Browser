Statement

Let K be an algebraically closed field, n\ge1 an integer, and A\in K^{n\times n}. Let B\in K^{n\times n} and let v_1,\ldots,v_n be linearly independent with Av_i=\lambda_iBv_i. Set P=[v_1\ \cdots\ v_n] and D=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n). Then A=BPD P^{-1}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Matrices, determinants, kernels, linear independence and bases are standard undergraduate linear algebra. Over an algebraically closed field, the extra condition simply guarantees polynomial roots; infinite dimensions mean cardinalities of bases. The specific matrix or dimension claim adds no specialist construction.

Proof 1: Assemble and invert the generalized eigenvector matrix

The proof is

{\displaystyle \mathbf {A} \mathbf {P} =\mathbf {A} {\begin{bmatrix}|&&|\\\mathbf {v} _{1}&\cdots &\mathbf {v} _{n}\\|&&|\end{bmatrix}}={\begin{bmatrix}|&&|\\A\mathbf {v} _{1}&\cdots &A\mathbf {v} _{n}\\|&&|\end{bmatrix}}={\begin{bmatrix}|&&|\\\lambda _{1}B\mathbf {v} _{1}&\cdots &\lambda _{n}B\mathbf {v} _{n}\\|&&|\end{bmatrix}}={\begin{bmatrix}|&&|\\B\mathbf {v} _{1}&\cdots &B\mathbf {v} _{n}\\|&&|\end{bmatrix}}\mathbf {D} =\mathbf {B} \mathbf {P} \mathbf {D} }

And since P is invertible, we multiply the equation from the right by its inverse, finishing the proof.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Assemble the given vector equations columnwise as AP=BPD and invert the basis matrix. Undergraduate linear algebra suffices; the algebraically closed ambient field introduces no additional method.
