Suitable neural networks can approximate continuous functions arbitrarily well as their capacity grows. This establishes representational expressiveness, while leaving learning from finite data, computational feasibility, and generalization as separate questions.

Statement

Let \sigma:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R be continuous. Then \sigma is not a polynomial if and only if the following universal approximation property holds:

For every pair of positive integers n,m, every nonempty compact set K\subseteq\mathbb R^n, every continuous map f:K\to\mathbb R^m, and every \varepsilon>0, there exist a positive integer k, a matrix A\in\mathbb R^{k\times n}, a vector b\in\mathbb R^k, and a matrix C\in\mathbb R^{m\times k} such that

\sup_{x\in K}\|f(x)-C\sigma(Ax+b)\|_2<\varepsilon.

Here \sigma acts on each coordinate, and \|\cdot\|_2 is the Euclidean norm. The expression C\sigma(Ax+b) is a feedforward network with one hidden layer of width k, activation \sigma, and linear output. The width and all matrix/vector parameters may depend on f,K,\varepsilon,n,m. On the empty compact set the approximation requirement is vacuous.

Proof 1: Smoothing and extraction of ridge monomials (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorem1, Section6 Steps1–5, with the continuous-activation specialization below).

For the stated continuous case, the smoothing argument can be specialized as follows. Let V be the locally uniform closure of the span of affine copies of \sigma. Smooth convolutions g_\delta=\sigma*\rho_\delta belong to V by Riemann sums and converge locally uniformly to \sigma. For each r\geq1, some g_\delta has g_\delta^{(r)}(b)\ne0: otherwise all these smoothings would be polynomials of degree at most r-1, and interpolation at r fixed points would force their limit \sigma to have the same degree bound. Finite differences in a at a=0 applied to g_\delta(at+b) therefore put t^r in V; a nonzero value of \sigma gives constants. This argument, supplied here for the continuous case, avoids the general discontinuous-activation Steps6–7.

Consequently every (w\cdot x)^r is approximable on a compact set. The polarization identity

y_1\cdots y_r=\frac1{r!}\sum_{S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,r\}}(-1)^{r-|S|}\Bigl(\sum_{j\in S}y_j\Bigr)^r

gives every multivariate monomial. Stone–Weierstrass applied directly on K gives scalar approximation, without extending the target. Approximate each of the m coordinates within \varepsilon/\sqrt m and concatenate the hidden units, assigning zero output weights to the other coordinates. This remains one hidden layer. A polynomial activation of degree d cannot approximate t^{d+1} uniformly on an interval, since the space of degree-at-most-d polynomials is closed.

Source proof sketch and limitations

The source reduces vector outputs to scalar outputs and observes that a polynomial activation bounds the degree of every network output, obstructing density on a compact interval. For the converse it sketches approximation of a ramp by linear combinations of a squashing activation, followed by a proposed bump-function construction.

The passage does not cover every continuous nonpolynomial activation: it directs the reader elsewhere for that case. It also does not show that the proposed multidimensional bump intersections stay within the one-hidden-layer architecture. Thus the supplied sketch does not prove the full if-and-only-if statement. The statement retains arbitrary nonpolynomial activations and arbitrary compact domains; it has not been narrowed to sigmoids or scalar inputs.
