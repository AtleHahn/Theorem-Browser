Statement

Let \sigma:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R be continuous. Then \sigma is not a polynomial if and only if the following universal approximation property holds:

For every pair of positive integers n,m, every nonempty compact set K\subseteq\mathbb R^n, every continuous map f:K\to\mathbb R^m, and every \varepsilon>0, there exist a positive integer k, a matrix A\in\mathbb R^{k\times n}, a vector b\in\mathbb R^k, and a matrix C\in\mathbb R^{m\times k} such that

\sup_{x\in K}\|f(x)-C\sigma(Ax+b)\|_2<\varepsilon.

Here \sigma acts on each coordinate, and \|\cdot\|_2 is the Euclidean norm. The expression C\sigma(Ax+b) is a feedforward network with one hidden layer of width k, activation \sigma, and linear output. The width and all matrix/vector parameters may depend on f,K,\varepsilon,n,m. On the empty compact set the approximation requirement is vacuous.

Proof availability

Source proof sketch and limitations

The source reduces vector outputs to scalar outputs and observes that a polynomial activation bounds the degree of every network output, obstructing density on a compact interval. For the converse it sketches approximation of a ramp by linear combinations of a squashing activation, followed by a proposed bump-function construction.

The passage does not cover every continuous nonpolynomial activation: it directs the reader elsewhere for that case. It also does not show that the proposed multidimensional bump intersections stay within the one-hidden-layer architecture. Thus the supplied sketch does not prove the full if-and-only-if statement. The statement retains arbitrary nonpolynomial activations and arbitrary compact domains; it has not been narrowed to sigmoids or scalar inputs.
