Statement

Define \chi(X)=\sum_{j\ge0}(-1)^j b_j(X), where b_j(X)=\dim_{\mathbb Q}H_j(X;\mathbb Q); these dimensions are finite and vanish for all sufficiently large j. For spaces M and N satisfying this finite-type condition, χ(M⊔N)=χ(M)+χ(N).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

These are arbitrary topological spaces with homology.

Proof 1: Add homology ranks degree by degree

If M and N are two topological spaces satisfying the finite-type condition in the statement, then the Euler characteristic of their disjoint union is the sum of their Euler characteristics, since homology is additive under disjoint union:

{\displaystyle \chi (M\sqcup N)=\chi (M)+\chi (N).}
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Although the final alternating-sum addition is elementary, the statement and invoked theorem concern singular homology and Betti numbers of general spaces. That algebraic topology is beyond a small extension of introductory topology.
