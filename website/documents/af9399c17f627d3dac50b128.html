Statement

Let f be real analytic on an open interval containing p. There are a neighborhood W of p, c>0 and 0<\theta<1 such that |f(x)-f(p)|^\theta\leq c|f'(x)| for x\in W. If f(x)-f(p) has finite order k\geq2 at p, one can use \theta=(k-1)/k; for k=1, one can use \theta=1/2.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves real analytic functions in one variable; order of vanishing; differentiation and continuity. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Order of vanishing — outline

The proof for the one-dimensional case uses the order of vanishing: if f(x) − f(p) = (x − p)k · g(x) with g(p) ≠ 0, then θ = (k − 1)/k works for k ≥ 2, and θ = 1/2 works for k = 1.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Factor the first nonzero Taylor power and bound the remaining factor and derivative by continuity near p. Undergraduate power series and differentiation suffice; no multidimensional analytic geometry is required.
