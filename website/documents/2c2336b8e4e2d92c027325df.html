Statement

For integer n\ge1 and nonnegative real numbers a_1,\ldots,a_n,b_1,\ldots,b_n, \bigl(\prod_{i=1}^n(a_i+b_i)\bigr)^{1/n}\ge\bigl(\prod_{i=1}^na_i\bigr)^{1/n}+\bigl(\prod_{i=1}^nb_i\bigr)^{1/n}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

This is an elementary finite algebraic inequality.

Review notes
none

Proof 1: Divide by the sum product and use AM–GM outline

If some a_i+b_i=0, both sides of the desired inequality are zero. Otherwise, after dividing by \prod_{i=1}^n(a_i+b_i)^{1/n}, the inequality follows from AM–GM:

{\textstyle (\prod {\frac {a_{i}}{a_{i}+b_{i}}})^{1/n}+(\prod {\frac {b_{i}}{a_{i}+b_{i}}})^{1/n}\leq \sum {\frac {1}{n}}{\frac {a_{i}+b_{i}}{a_{i}+b_{i}}}=1}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

If a sum a_i+b_i vanishes both products vanish. Otherwise divide by the positive product of sums and apply AM–GM separately to the two lists of fractions. Their arithmetic means add to one. This is elementary algebra and inequalities.
