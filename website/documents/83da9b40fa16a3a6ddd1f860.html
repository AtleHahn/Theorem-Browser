Statement

Let m\ge1 be an integer, and let x_1,\ldots,x_m>-1 be real numbers, either all nonnegative or all nonpositive. Then \prod_{i=1}^m(1+x_i)\ge1+\sum_{i=1}^m x_i.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement concerns finite sums and products of real increments sharing a sign.

Proof 1: Induction using nonnegative pair products

In the first step we take {\displaystyle m=1}. In this case the inequality {\displaystyle 1+x_{1}\geq 1+x_{1}} is obviously true. In the second step we assume validity of the inequality for {\displaystyle m} numbers and deduce validity for {\displaystyle m+1} numbers. We assume that

{\displaystyle (1+x_{1})(1+x_{2})\dots (1+x_{m})\geq 1+x_{1}+x_{2}+\dots +x_{m}}

is valid. After multiplying both sides with a positive number {\displaystyle (x_{m+1}+1)} we get: {\displaystyle {\begin{alignedat}{2}(1+x_{1})(1+x_{2})\dots (1+x_{m})(1+x_{m+1})\geq &(1+x_{1}+x_{2}+\dots +x_{m})(1+x_{m+1})\\\geq &(1+x_{1}+x_{2}+\dots +x_{m})\cdot 1+(1+x_{1}+x_{2}+\dots +x_{m})\cdot x_{m+1}\\\geq &(1+x_{1}+x_{2}+\dots +x_{m})+x_{m+1}+x_{1}x_{m+1}+x_{2}x_{m+1}+\dots +x_{m}x_{m+1}\\\end{alignedat}}} As {\displaystyle x_{1},x_{2},\dots x_{m},x_{m+1}} all have the same sign, the products {\displaystyle x_{1}x_{m+1},x_{2}x_{m+1},\dots x_{m}x_{m+1}} are all nonnegative numbers. So the quantity on the right-hand side can be bounded as follows:

{\displaystyle (1+x_{1}+x_{2}+\dots +x_{m})+x_{m+1}+x_{1}x_{m+1}+x_{2}x_{m+1}+\dots +x_{m}x_{m+1}\geq 1+x_{1}+x_{2}+\dots +x_{m}+x_{m+1},}

what was to be shown.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Multiply the induction hypothesis by the positive factor 1+x_(m+1), expand, and discard the pair products x_i x_(m+1), which are nonnegative because all increments have one sign. Only finite sums, products and elementary induction are needed; the sum of increments itself need not be positive.
