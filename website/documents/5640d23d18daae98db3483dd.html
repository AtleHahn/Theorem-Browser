Statement

Let a,b,c be the positive side lengths of a nondegenerate Euclidean triangle, opposite interior angles \alpha,\beta,\gamma. If \Delta is the area, then \Delta=ab\sin\gamma/2.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement relates triangle sides, area and sines of interior angles using elementary trigonometry.

Proof 1: Multiply two squared-side area identities

Use the stated planar identities

\frac{a^2}{2\Delta}=\frac{\sin\alpha}{\sin\beta\sin\gamma},\qquad \frac{b^2}{2\Delta}=\frac{\sin\beta}{\sin\alpha\sin\gamma}.

Their product is a^2b^2/(4\Delta^2)=1/\sin^2\gamma. Taking positive square roots gives ab/(2\Delta)=1/\sin\gamma, equivalent to the assertion.
