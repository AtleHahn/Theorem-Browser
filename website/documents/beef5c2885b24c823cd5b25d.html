Statement

For a prime power q, let N_n count monic irreducible polynomials of degree n\ge1 over \mathbb F_q. Then N_n=\frac1n\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)q^d, where \mu is the Möbius function.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory algebra supplies finite fields, polynomial evaluation, irreducibility and vector subspaces. The Mobius function, asymptotic notation or uniform finite counting probability is a limited numerical definition where used. These statements require no field-extension norm or Galois theory.

Proof 1: Minimal-polynomial root count and Möbius inversion

Every element of the degree n extension of \mathbb F_q is a root of some monic irreducible polynomial whose degree d divides n; by counting these roots in two different ways one establishes that

q^n=\sum_{d\mid n}dN_d,

where the sum is over all divisors d of n. Möbius inversion then yields

N_n=\frac1n\sum_{d\mid n}\mu\left(\frac nd\right)q^d,

where \mu(k) is the Möbius function.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The counting step relies on degree-n field extensions, minimal polynomials and the precise splitting/divisor classification of irreducibles. Retain beyond undergraduate provisionally because this combined finite-field extension theory plus inversion is not confidently a small extension of introductory ring algebra.
