Statement

Consider a crease pattern whose creases are distinct rays from one interior vertex of a sheet, each assigned to be a mountain fold or a valley fold relative to a fixed side of the sheet. Suppose it admits a flat folding respecting those assignments: the sectors between creases can be folded into one plane without stretching, tearing or passing the paper through itself, with layering allowed. List the positive sector angles in their cyclic order. If a consecutive block of k\ge1 sectors has all its angles equal to \alpha, and each of the immediately adjacent sectors has angle strictly greater than \alpha, count the k+1 distinct creases bounding the sectors of the block. If M of these are mountains and V are valleys, then |M-V|\le1. In particular, a single sector strictly smaller than both its neighbors must be bounded by one mountain and one valley crease.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The geometry and counting condition use elementary angles and explicitly described paper folds; no advanced topology or algorithmic proof is needed to understand it.

Proof 1: Closing a fold stack and applying Maekawa (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorem 4.2, necessity proof pp. 6–7 and Figure 3.).

Use only necessity in Theorem 4.2. With k sectors in the statement, Hull's parameter is k-1. The larger neighboring sectors allow isolating the equal-angle stack in the cross-section without including another crease. Closing its two ends as in Figure 3 and applying Maekawa gives M-V=0 for odd k and M-V=+/-1 for even k. Both imply |M-V|<=1. No claim about the source's more broadly worded sufficiency is needed.
