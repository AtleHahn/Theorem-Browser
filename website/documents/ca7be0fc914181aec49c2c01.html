Statement

Let M\subseteq B(H) be a von Neumann algebra with cyclic separating unit vector v. On \mathfrak A=Mv use the inner product inherited from H and define

(xv)(yv)=xyv,\qquad(xv)^\sharp=x^*v.

Then \mathfrak A is a unital left Hilbert algebra with identity v and Hilbert completion H.

A left Hilbert algebra is an associative complex algebra \mathfrak A with involution \sharp and inner product such that left multiplication is bounded, (xy,z)=(y,x^\sharp z), the involution is closable, and the span of products is dense. Its Hilbert completion is taken in this inner product.

Level justification

These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Proof 1: Transport multiplication and involution to Mv — outline

Separation means that xv=0 implies x=0, and cyclicity means that Mv is dense in H. Thus the operations below are well defined and the Hilbert completion is H. Use also the closability theorem for the Tomita operator S_0(xv)=x^*v on Mv.

If M is a von Neumann algebra acting on a Hilbert space H with a cyclic separating unit vector v, then put {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {A}}} = Mv and define (xv)(yv) = xyv and (xv)♯ = x*v. The vector v is the identity of {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {A}}}, so {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {A}}} is a unital left Hilbert algebra.

Left multiplication by xv is the restriction of the bounded operator x, and (xy,z)=(y,x^\sharp z) is the Hilbert-space adjoint identity. Since v is the identity, every element is a product.
