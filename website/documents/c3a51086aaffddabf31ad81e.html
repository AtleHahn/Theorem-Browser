Statement

Let S\subseteq\mathbb R^n be closed with nonempty interior. Suppose that at every boundary point b there is a hyperplane through b such that S lies in one of its closed half-spaces. Then S equals the intersection of all its supporting closed half-spaces and is convex.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Euclidean topology; linear functionals; convex sets. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Separate an exterior point using its first boundary crossing

Let T be the intersection of all supporting closed half-spaces of S. Clearly S\subseteq T. For y\notin S, choose x\in\operatorname{int}S. Let t be the largest parameter in [0,1] for which [x,x+t(y-x)]\subseteq S. Closedness and the choice of endpoints give 0<t<1, and b=x+t(y-x) is a boundary point.

Take a supporting hyperplane at b, oriented by a nonzero linear functional f so that its supporting half-space is \{a:f(a)\geq f(b)\}. It contains T. Since x is interior to S, f(x)>f(b). Linearity yields

\frac{f(y)-f(b)}{1-t}=\frac{f(b)-f(x)}{t}<0.

Thus f(y)<f(b) and y\notin T. This proves S=T. An intersection of closed half-spaces is convex.
