Statement

Let f be injective and holomorphic on D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}, with f(0)=0. Write q(z)=zf'(z)/f(z), extended by q(0)=1, and let L be its analytic logarithm normalized by L(0)=0. The continuous argument \arg q(z)=\Im L(z) satisfies |\arg q(z)|\le\log((1+|z|)/(1-|z|)).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex differentiation and complex exponentials extend calculus. A normalized analytic logarithm is a function L with exp(L)=q and the supplied value at zero; its imaginary part defines the continuous argument. These direct function identities suffice for the explicit bound without Riemann surfaces.

Proof 1: Argument bound for the logarithmic derivative of a univalent function

Take imaginary parts of the supplied bound |L(z)|\le\log((1+|z|)/(1-|z|)). Since |\Im L(z)|\le|L(z)|, this is the claimed argument estimate.
