Statement

Let (f_n) be a uniformly bounded, uniformly equicontinuous sequence of continuous functions [a,b]\to\mathbb R^d, where d\ge1 is finite and bounds use the Euclidean norm. Then it has a uniformly convergent subsequence.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves finite-dimensional Euclidean spaces; uniform convergence; equicontinuity. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Successive extraction in each coordinate

Scalar dependency

For real-valued continuous functions on a compact interval, uniform boundedness and uniform equicontinuity imply that every sequence has a uniformly convergent subsequence.

Coordinate extraction

The Arzelà–Ascoli theorem holds, more generally, if the functions fn take values in d-dimensional Euclidean space Rd, and the proof is very simple: just apply the R-valued version of the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem d times to extract a subsequence that converges uniformly in the first coordinate, then a sub-subsequence that converges uniformly in the first two coordinates, and so on.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Each coordinate inherits the common bound and equicontinuity from the Euclidean norm. Apply the scalar theorem successively in the finitely many coordinates; the final subsequence converges uniformly in every coordinate, hence in Euclidean norm by ||v||<=sqrt(d) max_i |v_i|. This is undergraduate analysis and finite-dimensional linear algebra.
