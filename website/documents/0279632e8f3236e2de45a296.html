Statement

Let I=[a,b]\subset\mathbb R and let (f_n) be continuous real-valued functions on I. Assume |f_n(x)|\le M for one finite M and all n,x, and that for every \varepsilon>0 there is \delta>0 such that |x-y|<\delta implies |f_n(x)-f_n(y)|<\varepsilon for all n. Then (f_n) has a subsequence converging uniformly on I to a continuous function.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves real continuous functions on compact intervals; uniform convergence; equicontinuity; Uniform boundedness; Subsequences. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Diagonal selection on rational points and a finite cover

If a=b, Bolzano–Weierstrass applied to the bounded sequence (f_n(a)) gives the conclusion. Assume henceforth that a<b.

If one function occurs infinitely often in the given sequence, its repetitions form a uniformly convergent constant subsequence. Otherwise pass to a subsequence of distinct functions, and let \mathbf F be its infinite family of functions, as assumed in the source argument.

Fix an enumeration {xi}i ∈N of rational numbers in I. Since F is uniformly bounded, the set of points {f(x1)}f∈F is bounded, and hence by the Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem, there is a sequence {fn1} of distinct functions in F such that {fn1(x1)} converges. Repeating the same argument for the sequence of points {fn1(x2)} , there is a subsequence {fn2} of {fn1} such that {fn2(x2)} converges.

By induction this process can be continued forever, and so there is a chain of subsequences

{\displaystyle \left\{f_{n_{1}}\right\}\supseteq \left\{f_{n_{2}}\right\}\supseteq \cdots }

such that, for each k = 1, 2, 3, ..., the subsequence {fnk} converges at x1, ..., xk. Now form the diagonal subsequence {f} whose mth term fm is the mth term in the mth subsequence {fnm} . By construction, fm converges at every rational point of I.

Therefore, given any ε > 0 and rational xk in I, there is an integer N = N(ε, xk) such that

{\displaystyle |f_{n}(x_{k})-f_{m}(x_{k})|<{\tfrac {\varepsilon }{3}},\qquad n,m\geq N.}

Since the family F is equicontinuous, for this fixed ε and for every x in I, there is an open interval Ux containing x such that

{\displaystyle |f(s)-f(t)|<{\tfrac {\varepsilon }{3}}}

for all f ∈ F and all s, t in I such that s, t ∈ Ux.

The collection of intervals Ux, x ∈ I, forms an open cover of I. Since I is closed and bounded, by the Heine–Borel theorem I is compact, implying that this covering admits a finite subcover U1, ..., UJ. There exists an integer K such that each open interval Uj, 1 ≤ j ≤ J, contains a rational xk with 1 ≤ k ≤ K. Finally, for any t ∈ I, there are j and k so that t and xk belong to the same interval Uj. For this choice of k,

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\left|f_{n}(t)-f_{m}(t)\right|&\leq \left|f_{n}(t)-f_{n}(x_{k})\right|+|f_{n}(x_{k})-f_{m}(x_{k})|+|f_{m}(x_{k})-f_{m}(t)|\\&<{\tfrac {\varepsilon }{3}}+{\tfrac {\varepsilon }{3}}+{\tfrac {\varepsilon }{3}}\end{aligned}}}

for all n, m > N = max{N(ε, x1), ..., N(ε, xK)}. Consequently, the sequence {fn} is uniformly Cauchy, and therefore converges to a continuous function, as claimed. This completes the proof.

Level justification

Diagonal selection gives convergence at every rational point. Equicontinuity and a finite cover then upgrade this to the displayed uniform Cauchy estimate, and completeness gives a continuous uniform limit. These are standard undergraduate real-analysis techniques; the initial constant-subsequence and singleton-interval cases are handled explicitly.

Routine omissions

Source constructs a sequence of distinct family elements. Passing to an indexed subsequence of the original sequence, or a constant subsequence when one function repeats infinitely often, is routine. The singleton-interval case reduces to bounded real sequences. Choosing distinct functions in sequence order in the remaining case is a routine subsequence check; no compactness argument is added.
