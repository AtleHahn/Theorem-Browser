Statement

Let G be a finite group, H\le G, and \mathcal R(H) the free abelian group generated by its irreducible complex characters (the group of virtual characters). For every f\in\mathcal R(H), the induced virtual character \operatorname{Ind}_H^G f vanishes at every g\in G not conjugate to an element of H.

Proof 1: The coset summands have no fixed component

It is enough to prove the claim for the character {\displaystyle \phi } of a representation {\displaystyle \theta \colon H\to \mathrm {GL} (W)}, since any {\displaystyle f\in {\mathcal {R}}(H)} is a difference of two such characters. So, let {\displaystyle \rho \colon G\to \mathrm {GL} (V)} be the representation of {\displaystyle G} induced from the representation {\displaystyle \theta } of {\displaystyle H}, and let {\displaystyle (r_{i})} be a set of representatives of the cosets of {\displaystyle H} in {\displaystyle G}, which are the points of {\displaystyle G/H}. By definition of induced representation, {\displaystyle V} is the direct sum of its subspaces {\displaystyle \rho (r_{i})W}, and the linear transformation {\displaystyle \rho (g)} permutes these subspaces, since

{\displaystyle \rho (g)\circ \rho (r_{i})W=\rho (gr_{i})W=\rho (r_{i'})W}

where {\displaystyle gr_{i}=r_{i'}h} for some {\displaystyle h\in H}. To show that {\displaystyle {\text{Ind}}(\phi )(g)={\text{tr}}_{V}(\rho (g))} vanishes, we now choose a basis for {\displaystyle V} that is a union of the bases of the subspaces {\displaystyle \rho (r_{i})W}. In this basis for {\displaystyle V}, the diagonal matrix entry of {\displaystyle \rho (g)} vanishes for each basis vector in {\displaystyle \rho (r_{i})W} with {\displaystyle r_{i}\neq r_{i'}}. But {\displaystyle r_{i}=r_{i'}} would imply {\displaystyle r_{i}^{-1}gr_{i}=h\in H}, which is ruled out by our assumption that {\displaystyle g} is not conjugate to any element of {\displaystyle H}. Thus, all the diagonal matrix entries of {\displaystyle \rho (g)} vanish, so {\displaystyle {\text{tr}}_{V}(\rho _{g})=0} as desired, proving the lemma. ■
