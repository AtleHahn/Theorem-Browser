Statement

Let A be a complex C^*-algebra, not necessarily unital: a complex Banach algebra with conjugate-linear involution a\mapsto a^* satisfying (ab)^*=b^*a^* and \|a^*a\|=\|a\|^2. Write A' for its continuous complex-linear dual and A''=(A')' for its Banach bidual, with their dual norms. Let j:A\to A'' be the evaluation embedding j(a)(\ell)=\ell(a).

Define the universal representation as follows. For each state \rho of A (a positive linear functional of norm one, where positivity means \rho(a^*a)\geq0 for all a), put

N_\rho=\{a\in A:\rho(a^*a)=0\}.

Let H_\rho be the Hilbert-space completion of A/N_\rho with inner product \langle[a],[b]\rangle=\rho(b^*a), and let \pi_\rho(a)[b]=[ab], extended to H_\rho. On the Hilbert direct sum H_u=\bigoplus_\rho H_\rho, set \pi_u=\bigoplus_\rho\pi_\rho.

Let M be the weak-operator closure of \pi_u(A) in B(H_u), the algebra of bounded operators on H_u. Weak-operator convergence means convergence of \langle T\xi,\eta\rangle for every \xi,\eta\in H_u.

Then the bidual A'' admits a multiplication and involution extending those of A through j, making it a W^*-algebra with its given dual norm, and there is a surjective isometric *-isomorphism

\Phi:A''\longrightarrow M,\qquad\Phi(j(a))=\pi_u(a)\quad(a\in A).

Here a W^*-algebra is a C^*-algebra that is a dual Banach space; a *-isomorphism is a complex-linear multiplicative bijection preserving involution. Equivalently, this identifies the Banach bidual, with its extending operator-algebra structure, with the universal enveloping von Neumann algebra M. The empty Hilbert direct sum for A=\{0\} is understood as \{0\}.
