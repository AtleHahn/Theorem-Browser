Proof 1: Holomorphic gluing across a circle with common distributional boundary values — outline

The holomorphic functions f, g in the regions {\displaystyle r<|z|<1} and {\displaystyle 1<|z|<R} have Laurent expansions

{\displaystyle f(z)=\sum _{-\infty }^{\infty }a_{n}z^{n},\,\,\,\,g(z)=\sum _{-\infty }^{\infty }b_{n}z^{n}}

absolutely convergent in the same regions and their assumed distributional boundary values are given by the formal Fourier series

{\displaystyle f(\theta )=\sum _{-\infty }^{\infty }a_{n}e^{in\theta },\,\,\,\,g(\theta )=\sum _{-\infty }^{\infty }b_{n}e^{in\theta }.}

Their distributional boundary values are equal if and only if {\displaystyle a_{n}=b_{n}} for all n. It is then elementary that the common Laurent series converges absolutely in the whole region {\displaystyle r<|z|<R}.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The argument combines holomorphic Laurent expansions with distributional boundary values and Fourier uniqueness for distributions. These linked complex-analysis and distribution-theory prerequisites are substantial.
