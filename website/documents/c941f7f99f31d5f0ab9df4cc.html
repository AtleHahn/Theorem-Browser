Statement

There are no positive integers a,b,c,d such that (a,d,b) and (b,d,c) are right triangles with hypotenuses b,c, respectively.

Proof 1: No integer right triangles share a leg and hypotenuse as two legs

By the Pythagorean theorem, the two triangles would satisfy

\begin{aligned} a^2+d^2&=b^2,\\ b^2+d^2&=c^2. \end{aligned}

That is, the three squares a^2,b^2,c^2 would form an arithmetic progression whose gap, or congruum, was also a square d^2. Use the no-square-congruum form of Fermat's right triangle theorem: no positive square can be the gap between three integer squares in arithmetic progression. Thus there can be no two integer right triangles that share two sides in this way.
