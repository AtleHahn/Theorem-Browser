Statement

Let X be symmetric about its mean \mu with finite variance \sigma^2>0. For k>0, \Pr(X\le\mu-k\sigma)\le\frac1{2k^2},\qquad\Pr(X\ge\mu+k\sigma)\le\frac1{2k^2}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Distributional symmetry, mean and variance add linked scalar probability definitions to undergraduate integral averages.

Proof 1: Halve the variance tail bound

By symmetry the two events X-\mu\ge k\sigma and X-\mu\le-k\sigma have equal probabilities. They are disjoint and together form \{|X-\mu|\ge k\sigma\}, so each probability is half the two-sided one. Chebyshev’s inequality bounds that total by 1/k^2, giving the result.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use symmetry to split the two-sided deviation probability into equal disjoint tails, then apply Chebyshev. Undergraduate elementary probability and variance suffice.
