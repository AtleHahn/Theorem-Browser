Statement

For every positive integer n there is a number \varepsilon_n>0 with the following property. If a smooth compact n-dimensional manifold M admits a Riemannian metric g of diameter at most 1 such that |K_g(\sigma)|<\varepsilon_n for every tangent two-plane \sigma, then for every \varepsilon>0 it admits a Riemannian metric g_\varepsilon of diameter at most 1 with |K_{g_\varepsilon}(\sigma)|<\varepsilon for every tangent two-plane. Here K_g denotes sectional curvature; manifolds are understood without boundary.

Level justification

Smooth manifolds, tangent planes and sectional curvature require substantial differential geometry beyond the assumed undergraduate topology and calculus. The quantifiers over metrics add a geometric rather than merely numerical condition.

Proof 1: Infranil reduction and metric rescaling (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Rong Theorem 1, pp.1–4, with supporting Theorem 3 proved in the remainder; Farrell–Hsiang Lemma 6.5, pp.668–669.).

Supporting passages: Farrell–Hsiang, Lemma 6.5.

Use Rong Theorem 1 and its proof: the bounded nilmanifold cover is followed by an equivariant center-of-mass construction yielding the infranil quotient. For the converse use Farrell–Hsiang Lemma 6.5: choose holonomy-invariant summands of the lower central series and scaled invariant inner products; curvature tends to zero and diameters stay bounded. Normalize by a common diameter upper bound. For n>=2 choose the threshold from Rong’s theorem: the given metric identifies M as an infranilmanifold, and the converse construction then gives every requested smaller curvature bound. Dimension one is the circle with a rescaled metric. A finite Riemannian diameter entails connectedness.
