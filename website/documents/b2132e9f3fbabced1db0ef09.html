Statement

Let n\ge1 be an integer and let u,v\in L^1(\mathbb R^n) be complex-valued, with Lebesgue measure. Write \|w\|_1=\int_{\mathbb R^n}|w(x)|\,dx and r(x)=\int_{\mathbb R^n}u(t)v(x-t)\,dt wherever this integral exists. Then \iint|u(t)v(x-t)|\,dx\,dt=\|u\|_1\|v\|_1, the convolution exists almost everywhere, and r\in L^1(\mathbb R^n).

Proof 1: Translation invariance and Fubini

By Tonelli’s theorem and translation invariance:

{\displaystyle \iint |u(\tau )v(x-\tau )|\,dx\,d\tau =\int \left(|u(\tau )|\int |v(x-\tau )|\,dx\right)\,d\tau =\int |u(\tau )|\,\|v\|_{1}\,d\tau =\|u\|_{1}\|v\|_{1}.}

Hence by Fubini's theorem we have that {\displaystyle r\in L^{1}(\mathbb {R} ^{n})} and the defining convolution integral exists almost everywhere.
