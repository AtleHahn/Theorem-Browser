Statement

Let a\le b be real and let \delta:[a,b]\to(0,\infty) be any function. A tagged partition of [a,s] consists of finitely many endpoints and tags a=x_0\le t_1\le x_1\le\cdots\le t_n\le x_n=s; it is \delta-fine if each [x_{j-1},x_j] is contained in (t_j-\delta(t_j),t_j+\delta(t_j)). Every such gauge admits a \delta-fine tagged partition of [a,b].

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Arbitrary gauges, tagged partitions and the quantified fineness condition belong to undergraduate real analysis; the supplied definitions are not ordinary high-school interval arithmetic. A bright US college student should be able to understand the statement after about 30 minutes of focused background research; this rating concerns understanding the claim, not proving it.

Proof 1: Open induction on reachable prefixes — outline

If a=b, take the zero-length partition with tag a. Assume a<b.

An open subset {\displaystyle S}, in the relative topology, of a closed real interval {\displaystyle [a,b]} is said to be inductive if it satisfies that {\displaystyle [a,r)\subset S} implies {\displaystyle [a,r]\subset S}. The open induction principle states that any inductive open subset {\displaystyle S} of {\displaystyle [a,b]} must be the entire set.

Let {\displaystyle S} be the set of points {\displaystyle r\in[a,b]} such that there exists a {\displaystyle \delta }-fine tagged partition on {\displaystyle [a,s]} for some {\displaystyle s\geq r}. The set {\displaystyle S} is open, since it is downwards closed and any point in it is included in the open ray {\displaystyle [a,b]\cap [a,t_{n}+\delta (t_{n}))\subset S} for any associated partition.

Furthermore, it is inductive. For any {\displaystyle r}, suppose {\displaystyle [a,r)\subset S}. By that assumption (and using that either {\displaystyle r>a} or {\displaystyle r\in [a,a+\delta (a))\subset S} to handle the base case) we have a partition of length {\displaystyle n} with {\displaystyle x_{n}>\mathrm {max} (a,r-{\tfrac {1}{2}}\delta (r))}. Then either {\displaystyle x_{n}>b-(t_{n}+\delta (t_{n})-x_{n})} or {\displaystyle x_{n}<b}. In the first case {\displaystyle b<t_{n}+\delta (t_{n})}, so we can just replace {\displaystyle x_{n}} with {\displaystyle b} and get a partition of {\displaystyle [a,b]} that includes {\displaystyle r}.

If {\displaystyle x_{n}<b}, we may form a partition of length {\displaystyle n+1} that shows r\in S. To show this, we split into the cases {\displaystyle r>x_{n}} or {\displaystyle r<x_{n}+\delta (x_{n})}. In the first case, we set {\displaystyle t_{n+1}=r}, in the second we set {\displaystyle t_{n+1}=x_{n}}. In both cases, we can set {\displaystyle x_{n+1}=\mathrm {min} (b,t_{n+1}+{\tfrac {1}{2}}\delta (t_{n+1}))>x_{n}} and obtain a valid partition. In the second case, the open-ray inclusion already proved for the last tag gives r\in S, even if the chosen endpoint is less than r. So {\displaystyle [a,r]\subset S} in all cases, and {\displaystyle S} is inductive.

By open induction, {\displaystyle S=[a,b]}.

Membership of b gives a fine partition of [a,b].
