Statement

Let G be a group, V a nonzero complex vector space, and U:G\to\operatorname{GL}(V) a map to invertible complex-linear operators satisfying U(g)U(h)=\omega(g,h)U(gh) with \omega(g,h)\in\mathbb C^\times. Let \eta:G\to U(1), where U(1)=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|=1\}, and define \widehat U(g)=\eta(g)U(g). Then \widehat U(g)\widehat U(h)=\widehat\omega(g,h)\widehat U(gh) with \widehat\omega(g,h)=\omega(g,h)\eta(g)\eta(h)\eta(gh)^{-1}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Group multiplication and invertible linear maps are familiar algebra. The multiplier relation and phase change are supplied as equations, so the resulting identity requires no cohomology or projective-representation theory.

Proof 1: Substitution of the rephased operators — outline

Substitute \widehat U(g)=\eta(g)U(g) into the defining relation \widehat U(g)\widehat U(h)=\widehat\omega(g,h)\widehat U(gh). The resulting identity is

\widehat\omega(g,h)=\omega(g,h)\eta(g)\eta(h)\eta(gh)^{-1}.
