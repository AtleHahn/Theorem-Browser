Statement

For a prime p and nonzero integer u, let \nu_p(u) be the largest k\ge0 with p^k\mid u. Let x,y\in\mathbb Z, n\ge1, p\nmid xy, p\mid x-y, p\nmid n, and x^n\ne y^n. Then \nu_p(x^n-y^n)=\nu_p(x-y).

Level
High school only
Level justification

The valuation is explicitly defined using elementary divisibility; the result is high-school number theory.

Proof 1: Factor the difference and reduce the quotient modulo p

Because p\mid x-y\iff x\equiv y\pmod p,

x^{n-1}+x^{n-2}y+x^{n-3}y^2+\dots+y^{n-1} \equiv nx^{n-1}\not\equiv0\pmod p.

The fact that

x^n-y^n=(x-y)(x^{n-1}+x^{n-2}y+x^{n-3}y^2+\dots+y^{n-1})

completes the proof, by additivity of the valuation. For n=1, the parenthesized sum is 1.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Factor the power difference and show the quotient is nonzero modulo p, then add prime exponents. Elementary algebra and modular arithmetic suffice.
