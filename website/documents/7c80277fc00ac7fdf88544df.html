Statement

Let H be a real Hilbert space, let m\geq1, let U\subseteq H be nonempty, and let f:U\to\mathbb R^m have finite Lipschitz constant

L=\sup_{u,v\in U,\ u\ne v}\frac{\|f(u)-f(v)\|}{\|u-v\|},

with L=0 when U has only one point. Norms and inner products on \mathbb R^m are Euclidean. On H\times\mathbb R^m define

g(x,y)=\inf_{u\in U}\left(\langle f(u),y\rangle+\frac L2\|x-u\|^2\right)+\frac L2\|x\|^2+L\|y\|^2.

Let h=\operatorname{conv}(g) be the lower convex envelope of g, namely the greatest lower semicontinuous convex function not exceeding g. Then

F(x)=\nabla_y h(x,0)\qquad(x\in H)

is well-defined and is an extension of f satisfying

\|F(x)-F(x')\|\leq L\|x-x'\|\qquad(x,x'\in H).

The notation \nabla_y h(x,0) means the gradient at y=0 of the function y\mapsto h(x,y). A Hilbert space is a complete inner-product space with its induced norm. Convex means h(tz+(1-t)w)\leq th(z)+(1-t)h(w) for 0\leq t\leq1; lower semicontinuous means that all sets \{z:h(z)\leq c\} are closed. Complex Hilbert domains can be viewed as real Hilbert spaces with the same norm.
