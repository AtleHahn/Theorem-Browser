Statement

For a group G, write \operatorname{cd}_{\mathbb Z}G=\sup\{j\ge0:H^j(G,M)\ne0\text{ for some }\mathbb Z[G]\text{-module }M\}. If X is a connected aspherical n-dimensional CW complex with \pi_1(X)=G, then \operatorname{cd}_{\mathbb Z}G\le n.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Aspherical CW complexes; Fundamental groups; Group cohomology. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Use the universal-cover cellular resolution — outline

The stated cohomological-dimension bound is a consequence of cellular homology, and the fact that every free module is projective.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The argument requires aspherical CW complexes, universal covers and their cellular chain complexes as modules over a group ring, together with projective resolutions and group cohomology. This is a substantial algebraic-topology and homological-algebra framework; the final comparison of resolution length with n does not make it undergraduate.

Dependencies
The augmented cellular chains of the universal cover of a connected aspherical n-dimensional CW complex form a free Z[G]-resolution of Z concentrated in degrees 0 through n
Kind:

Theorem

Treatment:

Implicitly assumed

Statement used:

For a connected aspherical n-dimensional CW complex X with fundamental group G, the augmented cellular chains of its universal cover form a free Z[G]-resolution of the trivial module Z, with no chain groups in degrees above n.

Application in proof:

Obtain a resolution of length at most n.

Application level:

Beyond undergraduate level

Level justification:

Understanding this input requires covering spaces, cellular chains and the group action on lifted cells.

Cohomological dimension is the minimum projective-resolution length of the trivial module
Kind:

Theorem

Treatment:

Implicitly assumed

Statement used:

If the trivial Z[G]-module Z has a projective resolution of length at most n, then H^j(G,M)=0 for every Z[G]-module M and every j>n; equivalently cd_Z G<=n.

Application in proof:

Read off the cohomological-dimension bound from the cellular resolution.

Application level:

Beyond undergraduate level

Level justification:

This is the resolution interpretation of group cohomology, requiring homological algebra.
