Statement

Let X be a connected compact Riemann surface of genus g. A divisor is a finite integer sum of points, its degree is the sum of its coefficients, and K is the divisor of a nonzero meromorphic one-form. For a divisor D, let L(D) consist of zero and the meromorphic functions h with (h)+D\ge0, and put \ell(D)=\dim_{\mathbb C}L(D). For every divisor D, \ell(D)\ge\deg(D)-g+1.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The bound concerns divisor sections and the genus of a compact Riemann surface.

Proof 1: Use nonnegativity of the correction term

The Riemann–Roch theorem for a compact Riemann surface of genus {\displaystyle g} with canonical divisor {\displaystyle K} states

{\displaystyle \ell (D)-\ell (K-D)=\deg(D)-g+1}.

Because it is the dimension of a vector space, the correction term {\displaystyle \ell (K-D)} is always non-negative, so that

{\displaystyle \ell (D)\geq \deg(D)-g+1}.
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply Riemann–Roch and discard the nonnegative dimension term. Compact Riemann surfaces, divisors and meromorphic one-forms remain substantive prerequisites despite the elementary inequality.
