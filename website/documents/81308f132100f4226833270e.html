Statement

Let G be finite and K a field in which |G| is invertible. Every finite-dimensional K-linear representation of G is a finite direct sum of irreducible representations, where irreducible means nonzero with no nonzero proper invariant subspace.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Group theory and linear algebra suffice after defining a linear representation as an action by invertible linear maps. Invariant subspaces and direct complements are familiar, and irreducibility is explicitly the absence of a proper nonzero invariant subspace. No character or module classification theory is needed.

Proof 1: Dimension induction using invariant complements — outline

Maschke’s theorem is commonly formulated as a corollary to the following result: if a representation V of a finite group G over a field with characteristic not dividing |G| has a subrepresentation W, then it has another subrepresentation U such that V=W\oplus U.

Then the corollary is that every finite-dimensional representation under these hypotheses is a direct sum of irreducible representations. The source states the corollary without writing out the dimension induction.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Split a proper invariant subspace using the stated complement theorem and induct on dimension. Undergraduate linear algebra and finite group representations as linear actions suffice; the representation vocabulary is a small extension.
