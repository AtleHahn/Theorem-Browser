The rationals are dense in the real line yet have Lebesgue measure zero. This separates topological ubiquity from probabilistic or geometric size, showing that 'almost everywhere' depends on the chosen notion of largeness.

Statement

The set \mathbb Q of rational real numbers has Lebesgue measure zero.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Real analysis supplies the rational subspace metric, countability and interval covers. Measure zero here means covers by intervals of arbitrarily small total length, a limited null-set definition without general integration.

Proof 1: Countable-null-set specialization

The rational numbers form a countable set. Every countable subset of the real line is Lebesgue measurable with measure zero. Applying this measure-theoretic fact to \mathbb Q proves the claim.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Combine countability of Q with the supplied countable-null-set theorem. For this application, null means coverable by intervals of arbitrarily small total length, a small extension of undergraduate real analysis; general measurable integration is not needed.
