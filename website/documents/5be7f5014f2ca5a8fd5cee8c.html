Statement

Let a real random variable have a moment-generating function M(t)=\mathbb E e^{tX} finite on an open interval around zero, and put K(t)=\log M(t). Write m_n=\mathbb E X^n=M^{(n)}(0) and \kappa_j=K^{(j)}(0). Then m_n=B_n(\kappa_1,\ldots,\kappa_n) for n\ge1. For 1\le k\le n, define B_{n,k}(z_1,\ldots,z_{n-k+1})=\sum \frac{n!}{m_1!\cdots m_n!}\prod_{j=1}^{n-k+1}(z_j/j!)^{m_j}, summed over nonnegative integers with \sum m_j=k and \sum j m_j=n. Put B_{0,0}=1 and B_{n,0}=0 for n>0; B_n=\sum_{k=0}^nB_{n,k}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The generating functions, cumulants and Bell polynomials are explicitly defined through scalar expectations, derivatives and finite sums. Expectation as integral average is the only limited addition to undergraduate calculus.

Proof 1: Evaluate the exponential-composite formula at zero — outline

If f(x)=e^x, then all of the derivatives of f are the same and are a factor common to every term in Faà di Bruno's formula:

\frac{d^n}{dx^n}e^{g(x)} =e^{g(x)}B_n\bigl(g'(x),g''(x),\ldots,g^{(n)}(x)\bigr),

where B_n is the nth complete exponential Bell polynomial. This higher-derivative identity is used as a prerequisite.

In case g(x) is a cumulant-generating function, then f(g(x)) is a moment-generating function, and the polynomial in various derivatives of g is the polynomial that expresses the moments as functions of the cumulants. Here g=K, f\circ g=M, and the moments and cumulants are the derivatives at zero specified in the statement.
