Statement

Let a,b,c be the side lengths of a nondegenerate Euclidean triangle, R its circumradius, and s=(a+b+c)/2. Then 2R=\frac{abc}{2\sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}}=\frac{2abc}{\sqrt{(a^2+b^2+c^2)^2-2(a^4+b^4+c^4)}}.

Level
High school only

Proof 1: Heron substitution and polynomial expansion — outline

Use the area–circumradius identity 2R=abc/(2T) and Heron’s formula T=\sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}, where T is the triangle area. Substitution gives the first expression. The second is the source’s equivalent expanded form, using 16s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)=(a^2+b^2+c^2)^2-2(a^4+b^4+c^4).
