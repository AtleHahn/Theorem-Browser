Statement

Let G be a finite simple group of sectional 2-rank at least 5, and let P be a Sylow 2-subgroup. Then P has an abelian normal subgroup A of rank at least 3 with C_P(A)=A.

Simple means nontrivial with no proper nontrivial normal subgroup. Sectional 2-rank is the largest integer r for which there exist H\leq G and N\trianglelefteq H with H/N\cong(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^r. A Sylow 2-subgroup has order equal to the largest power of 2 dividing |G|. The rank of the finite abelian 2-group A is its minimum number of generators, equivalently \dim_{\mathbb F_2}(A/2A) in additive notation. The centralizer is C_P(A)=\{p\in P:pa=ap\text{ for all }a\in A\}; the equality says that A is self-centralizing in P.

Proof 1: MacWilliams and a maximal abelian subgroup (external)

A supporting source can be found here (Theorem 2.3(b) and the following MacWilliams discussion). The deduction below supplies the argument for the stored statement.

The following deduction from the cited MacWilliams theorem is supplied here. MacWilliams implies that a Sylow2 subgroup P of sectional rank at least5 has an abelian normal subgroup B of rank at least3. The sectional rank of G equals that of P: lift the Sylow2 subgroup of a subgroup H onto its elementary abelian2 quotient, then conjugate into P. Enlarge B to a maximal abelian normal subgroup A of P. Its rank is at least that of B, by the structure theorem for finite abelian groups. Put C=C_P(A), normal in P. If C>A, the nontrivial normal subgroup C/A of the2-group P/A meets its center in a nontrivial subgroup; choose an element xA of order2 there. Then <A,x> is abelian because x centralizes A, and normal in P because xA is central in P/A, contradicting maximality. Thus C_P(A)=A. This argument does not require simplicity.
