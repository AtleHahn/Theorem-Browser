Statement

Let n\ge1 be an integer and let a_1,\ldots,a_n and b_1,\ldots,b_n be real sequences that are both nonincreasing or both nondecreasing. Then \frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n a_jb_j\ge\left(\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^na_j\right)\left(\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^nb_j\right).

Level
High school only
Level justification

All hypotheses and averages are elementary real arithmetic on finite sequences.

Proof 1: Compare signs in a double sum

Consider the sum

{\displaystyle S=\sum _{j=1}^{n}\sum _{k=1}^{n}(a_{j}-a_{k})(b_{j}-b_{k}).}

The two sequences are both non-increasing or both non-decreasing, therefore aj − ak and bj − bk have the same sign for any j, k. Hence S ≥ 0.

Opening the brackets, we deduce:

{\displaystyle 0\leq 2n\sum _{j=1}^{n}a_{j}b_{j}-2\sum _{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\,\sum _{j=1}^{n}b_{j},}

hence

{\displaystyle {\frac {1}{n}}\sum _{j=1}^{n}a_{j}b_{j}\geq \left({\frac {1}{n}}\sum _{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\right)\!\!\left({\frac {1}{n}}\sum _{j=1}^{n}b_{j}\right)\!.}
Level
High school only
Level justification

Differences from similarly ordered sequences have the same sign. Sum their nonnegative products, expand and divide by 2n². Only finite sums and elementary inequalities are used.

Proof 2: Sum the cyclic rearrangement inequalities

Reindex the sequences as a_0,\ldots,a_{n-1} and b_0,\ldots,b_{n-1}.

An alternative proof is simply obtained with the rearrangement inequality, writing that

{\displaystyle \sum _{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}\sum _{j=0}^{n-1}b_{j}=\sum _{i=0}^{n-1}\sum _{j=0}^{n-1}a_{i}b_{j}=\sum _{i=0}^{n-1}\sum _{k=0}^{n-1}a_{i}b_{i+k~{\text{mod}}~n}=\sum _{k=0}^{n-1}\sum _{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}b_{i+k~{\text{mod}}~n}\leq \sum _{k=0}^{n-1}\sum _{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}b_{i}=n\sum _{i}a_{i}b_{i}.}

Dividing by n^2 gives the stated inequality.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Every cyclic shift pairs the ordered sequences no better than matching them in order. Summing all n rearrangement bounds counts each product a_i b_j exactly once. This is elementary finite permutation and inequality reasoning.
