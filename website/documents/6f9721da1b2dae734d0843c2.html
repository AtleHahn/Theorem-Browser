Statement

Let a>0 and let f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb C be a continuous representative of an L^2(\mathbb R) function whose L^2 Fourier transform F is supported in [-a,a], with inverse convention f(x)=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_{-a}^a F(\omega)e^{ix\omega}\,d\omega. Then f\in C^\infty(\mathbb R) and f(x)\to0 as |x|\to\infty.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The hypothesis requires the measure-theoretic L² Fourier transform; the conclusion is smoothness and decay of its continuous representative.

Proof 1: Use the compact Fourier support and Riemann–Lebesgue lemma — outline

The bandlimited continuous functions f in the statement are smooth functions on \mathbb R that tend to zero at infinity, essentially by the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma. Their compactly supported inverse Fourier representation is

f(x)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-a}^aF(\omega)e^{ix\omega}\,d\omega.
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof needs the L2 Fourier transform, almost-everywhere representatives, the L1 Riemann–Lebesgue lemma and differentiation under a Lebesgue integral. Compact support supplies all derivative bounds, but understanding these linked measure-theoretic and harmonic-analysis facts exceeds the short undergraduate extension.
