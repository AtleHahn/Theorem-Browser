Statement

Let h:X\to[0,\infty) and T:X\to\mathbb R^s be measurable on a fixed observation space X with reference measure \mu. For \eta\in\mathbb R^s let Z(\eta)=\int h(x)e^{\eta^TT(x)}\,d\mu(x), and wherever 0<Z(\eta)<\infty put A(\eta)=\log Z(\eta) and p_\eta(x)=h(x)e^{\eta^TT(x)-A(\eta)}. At an interior point of the natural parameter domain (where Z is finite on a neighborhood), \operatorname{Cov}_\eta(T_i(X),T_j(X))=\partial_i\partial_jA(\eta) for 1\le i,j\le s.

The covariance assertion is about a general measure-theoretic random vector.

Proof 1: Take mixed second derivatives of the cumulant function

The moment-generating function of T(X) is

\begin{aligned} M_T(u)&=\mathbb E_\eta\left[\exp\left(u^{\mathsf T}T(X)\right)\right]\\ &=\int_X h(x)\exp\left[(\eta+u)^{\mathsf T}T(x)-A(\eta)\right]\,d\mu(x)\\ &=e^{A(\eta+u)-A(\eta)}. \end{aligned}

Thus

K_T(u)=\log M_T(u)=A(\eta+u)-A(\eta)

is the cumulant generating function for T(X).

In particular, using the property that mixed second derivatives of the cumulant generating function at zero are covariances,

\operatorname{Cov}_\eta(T_i(X),T_j(X)) =\frac{\partial^2A(\eta)}{\partial\eta_i\,\partial\eta_j}.
