Statement

Let n be a positive integer. Let P(x)=a_0x^n+a_1x^{n-1}+\cdots+a_n have complex coefficients, a_0\ne0, and roots r_1,\ldots,r_n counted with multiplicity. Define E_k(r_1,\ldots,r_n)=\sum_{1\leq i_1<\cdots<i_k\leq n}r_{i_1}\cdots r_{i_k}. Then E_k=(-1)^ka_k/a_0 for 1\leq k\leq n.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement explicitly relates the coefficients of a finite complex polynomial to finite sums of products of its roots; only elementary polynomial algebra is needed. This rating concerns understanding the statement, independently of the difficulty of its proof.

Proof 1: Expand the linear factors and compare coefficients

The fundamental theorem of algebra and the factor theorem give the complete linear factorization below, with roots counted with multiplicity.

Vieta's formulas can be proved by considering the equality

a_0x^n+a_1x^{n-1}+\cdots+a_n =a_0(x-r_1)(x-r_2)\cdots(x-r_n),

expanding the products in the right-hand side, and equating the coefficients of each power of x between the two members of the equation.

Formally, if one expands (x-r_1)(x-r_2)\cdots(x-r_n) and regroups the terms by their degree in x, one gets

\sum_{k=0}^n(-1)^{n-k}x^k \left(\sum_{\substack{(\forall i)\ b_i\in\{0,1\}\\b_1+\cdots+b_n=n-k}} r_1^{b_1}\cdots r_n^{b_n}\right),

where the inner sum is exactly the (n-k)th elementary symmetric function (with E_0=1). Thus, comparison of the coefficient of x^{n-k} gives a_k=(-1)^ka_0E_k.

As an example, consider the quadratic

f(x)=a_0x^2+a_1x+a_2 =a_0(x-r_1)(x-r_2) =a_0(x^2-x(r_1+r_2)+r_1r_2).

Comparing identical powers of x, we find a_0=a_0, a_1=-a_0(r_1+r_2) and a_2=a_0(r_1r_2), with which we can identify r_1+r_2=-a_1/a_0 and r_1r_2=a_2/a_0, which are Vieta's formulas for n=2.

The roots r_1,r_2,r_3 of the cubic polynomial P(x)=ax^3+bx^2+cx+d satisfy

r_1+r_2+r_3=-\frac ba,\qquad r_1r_2+r_1r_3+r_2r_3=\frac ca,\qquad r_1r_2r_3=-\frac da.
