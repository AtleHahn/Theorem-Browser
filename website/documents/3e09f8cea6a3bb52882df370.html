Statement

For A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix} and real t, e^{tA}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos t&\sin t\\-\sin t&\cos t\end{pmatrix}, where e^{tA}=\sum_{k\ge0}t^kA^k/k!.

Proof 1: The exponential of the planar rotation generator

For the fixed real parameter t, put f(x)=e^{tx}.

Use the two-eigenvalue case of the Sylvester interpolation principle: for a complex two-by-two matrix with distinct eigenvalues, the exponential or sine matrix function f(A) equals c_0I+c_1A when c_0+c_1\lambda=f(\lambda) at both eigenvalues. Matrix exponential and sine are defined by their convergent power series.

The characteristic polynomial is p(x) = x2 + 1, and the eigenvalues are λ = ±i.

Evaluating the function at the eigenvalues gives us the linear equations eit = c0 + i c1 and e−it = c0 − ic1; the solution of which gives, c0 = (eit + e−it)/2 = cos t and c1 = (eit − e−it)/(2i) = sin t. Thus, for this case,

{\displaystyle e^{At}=(\cos t)I_{2}+(\sin t)A={\begin{pmatrix}\cos t&\sin t\\-\sin t&\cos t\end{pmatrix}}.}
