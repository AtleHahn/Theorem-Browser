Statement

Let x,y be distinct nonadjacent vertices of a finite undirected graph G. The minimum number of vertices other than x,y whose removal destroys every xy-path equals the maximum number of xy-paths sharing no vertices except x,y.

Proof 1: Neighbor-set reduction to set-to-set Menger — outline

Use the set-to-set version of Menger's theorem: for vertex sets A,B in a finite graph, the minimum size of an AB-separator is equal to the maximum size of an AB-connector. An AB-path has its first vertex in A, its last in B, and no internal vertex in A\cup B; a single vertex in A\cap B is allowed. A separator meets every such path, and a connector of size k is a union of k vertex-disjoint AB-paths.

For x,y\in G, apply this theorem to G-\{x,y\} with A=N(x), B=N(y), the neighboring vertices of x,y. Then a set of vertices disconnecting x and y is the same thing as an AB-separator, and removing the end vertices in a set of independent xy-paths, and trimming to AB-paths if necessary, gives an AB-connector. Conversely, adjoin x and y to the paths of a connector.
