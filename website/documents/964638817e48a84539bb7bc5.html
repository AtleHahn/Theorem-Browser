Statement

Let n\ge1 be an integer, let \lambda^n be Lebesgue measure on \mathbb R^n, and let f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R be locally integrable. Write B_r(x) for the open Euclidean ball of radius r>0 centered at x. Then for \lambda^n-almost every x, \lim_{r\downarrow0}\frac{1}{\lambda^n(B_r(x))}\int_{B_r(x)}f(y)\,d\lambda^n(y)=f(x).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The assertion is a measure-theoretic local averaging limit beyond ordinary calculus.

Proof 1: Specialize the locally finite Borel-measure theorem

Use the differentiation theorem for locally finite Borel measures on \mathbb R^n: for every such measure \mu and every locally \mu-integrable real function f, its ball averages converge to f(x) at \mu-almost every x. Lebesgue measure is locally finite and is a Borel measure, so the Lebesgue result is the stated special case.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Specialize the locally finite Borel-measure differentiation theorem to Lebesgue measure. General measure integration and almost-everywhere ball differentiation are graduate prerequisites.
