Statement

Let X=\omega_1, the first uncountable ordinal, with the countable/co-countable sigma-algebra and probability measure assigning zero to countable sets and one to co-countable sets. Put E=\{(x,y)\in X^2:x<y\}. Let f equal 1 on E and -1 on its complement. Then |f|=1 has product integral one, but the iterated integrals of f are -1 when x is integrated first and 1 in the reverse order. In particular f is not product-measurable.

Proof 1: Integrability of the absolute value does not replace measurability

The horizontal sections of E are countable and its vertical sections have countable complement. Thus the corresponding section integrals of f are -1 and 1. Meanwhile |f| is identically one, so its integral for the product probability measure is one. Fubini’s theorem therefore cannot apply: f is not measurable.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Use initial segments of the first uncountable ordinal with the countable/co-countable probability measure, then contradict product-measurable Fubini. Both ordinal set theory and product-measure theory are substantial prerequisites.
