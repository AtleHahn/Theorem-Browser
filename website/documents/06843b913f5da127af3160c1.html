Statement

Let N\ge1 and let positive integers m_1,\ldots,m_N be pairwise coprime. Set M=\prod_i m_i and M_i=M/m_i. For arbitrary integers x_i, choose integers h_i with M_i h_i\equiv1\pmod{m_i}. Then X=\sum_i x_iM_i h_i satisfies X\equiv x_i\pmod{m_i} for every i, and all simultaneous solutions are congruent to X modulo M. The coefficients h_i can be computed by the Euclidean algorithm.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The construction uses only explicitly given integer coefficients and congruences.

Proof 1: Compute modular inverses and form the reconstruction sum — outline

The goal is to determine x from its N remainders xi. The solution is to combine the multiple equations into a single linear Diophantine equation with a much larger modulus M that is the product of all the individual moduli mi, and define Mi as

{\displaystyle M_{i}={\frac {M}{m_{i}}}.}

Thus, each Mi is the product of all the moduli except mi. The solution depends on finding N new numbers hi such that

{\displaystyle M_{i}h_{i}\equiv 1{\pmod {m_{i}}}\,.}

With these numbers hi, any integer x can be reconstructed from its remainders xi by the equation

{\displaystyle x\equiv (x_{1}M_{1}h_{1}+x_{2}M_{2}h_{2}+\cdots +x_{N}M_{N}h_{N}){\pmod {M}}\,.}

Since these numbers hi are the multiplicative inverses of the Mi, they may be found using Euclid's algorithm by solving M_i h_i+m_i y_i=1 with the extended Euclidean algorithm.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Bézout supplies the inverses. Modulo m_i, every summand except the ith vanishes and that one equals x_i; differences of solutions are divisible by every coprime modulus and hence their product. This is elementary integer congruence arithmetic.
