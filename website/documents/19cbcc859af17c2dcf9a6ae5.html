Statement

Let a,b,c>0 be the side lengths of a nondegenerate Euclidean triangle, opposite angles \alpha,\beta,\gamma\in(0,\pi). Then a/\sin\alpha=b/\sin\beta=c/\sin\gamma.

Proof 1: Compute one altitude in two ways

The altitude to the line containing side a is both b\sin\gamma and c\sin\beta. Hence b\sin\gamma=c\sin\beta, so b/\sin\beta=c/\sin\gamma. Choosing either other side as the base gives the remaining equalities. An exterior altitude uses the supplementary acute angle, whose sine is the same.

Level justification

Compute a common altitude from two sides and divide by positive sines. Elementary right-triangle trigonometry suffices, including supplementary angles for exterior altitudes.

Proof 2: Divide two squared-side area identities

Use the stated identities

\frac{a^2}{2\Delta}=\frac{\sin\alpha}{\sin\beta\sin\gamma},\qquad \frac{b^2}{2\Delta}=\frac{\sin\beta}{\sin\alpha\sin\gamma},

where \Delta>0 is the triangle area. Dividing yields a^2/b^2=\sin^2\alpha/\sin^2\beta. Positivity gives a/b=\sin\alpha/\sin\beta. Cyclic relabeling supplies the full ratio equality.
