Statement

Let f be an injective complex analytic function on the open unit disk \{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}, with Taylor expansion f(z)=z+a_2z^2+a_3z^3+\cdots at zero. Then for every real 0\le\lambda<1, |a_3-\lambda a_2^2|\le1+2\exp\left(-\frac{2\lambda}{1-\lambda}\right). Complex analytic means complex differentiable at every point of the disk; injective means distinct arguments have distinct images.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex differentiation and convergent power-series coefficients are a small extension of undergraduate calculus/analysis for understanding this explicit bound; no conformal-mapping machinery is required.

Proof 1: Loewner coefficients and a variational bound (external)

A supporting source can be found here (Lemma 20 (embedding in a Loewner chain), equations (15)–(17), and Theorem 24 proof; explicit variational bridge in this record.). The deduction below supplies the argument for the stored statement.

Use Tao Lemma 20 and Theorem 24 proof: for every normalized univalent function there is a Loewner chain with Herglotz coefficients c1(t),c2(t), a2=-integral c1 e^-t, a3-a2^2=-integral c2 e^-2t. Herglotz representation gives |Re c1|<=2 and Re c2>= (Re c1)^2-2 by Jensen applied to cos(theta). Rotate the input so a3-lambdaa2^2 is nonnegative real. Put s=1-lambda and u(x)=Re c1(-log x)/2, so |u|<=1. Taking real parts gives |a3-lambdaa2^2|<=1+4[s(integral_0^1 u)^2-integral_0^1 xu^2]. Here is the optimization, supplied here. Flip u to arrange A=integral u>=0. For each A in (0,1) choose unique c in (0,1) with A=c(1+log(1/c)). Among functions with this integral and |u|<=1, v(x)=min(1,c/x) minimizes B=integral xu^2: expand B(u)-B(v) and subtract 2c*integral(u-v)=0; the remaining linear integrand (xv-c)(u-v) is nonnegative (zero for x>=c, both factors nonpositive for x<c). Thus B>=c^2(1/2+log(1/c)). Differentiating sA(c)^2-B(c) gives 2A-prime(c)[sA(c)-c]; its maximum is attained at log(1/c)=lambda/(1-lambda), with value c^2/2. Endpoint A=0,1 follows by continuity; lambda=0 gives c=1. The desired bound follows.
