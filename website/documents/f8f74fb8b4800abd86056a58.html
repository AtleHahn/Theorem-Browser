Statement

Let G be a finite group and \rho:G\to\operatorname{GL}(V) an irreducible complex representation of dimension d\ge1. For independent variables (x_g)_{g\in G}, the polynomial \det(\sum_{g\in G}x_g\rho(g)) is irreducible in \mathbb C[(x_g)_{g\in G}].

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A representation is a matrix-valued group homomorphism and irreducibility excludes proper invariant subspaces. These two related definitions extend undergraduate groups/linear algebra; determinants and multivariate polynomial factorization are ordinary algebra.

Proof 1: Density and the generic determinant lemma (outline)

By the Jacobson density theorem, for any matrix M\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}(V), there exists a particular choice of complex numbers for each x_g, g\in G, such that:

M=\sum_{g\in G}x_g\rho(g).

This shows that \sum_{g\in G}x_g\rho(g), when viewed as a matrix with polynomial entries, must have linearly independent entries. Thus, by letting each of these entries be an independent variable y_{ij}, it follows by the generic determinant lemma—the determinant of a matrix of independent variables is irreducible—that \det(\sum_{g\in G}x_g\rho(g)) is an irreducible polynomial. Here the independent linear forms are completed to polynomial coordinates if necessary; adjoining the unused coordinates preserves irreducibility.

Level justification

Apply the explicit matrix-span density consequence, complete the independent entry forms to linear coordinates and invoke generic determinant irreducibility. Undergraduate linear algebra and polynomial rings suffice with finite-group representations as matrix actions; the general module-theoretic density theorem is not required.
