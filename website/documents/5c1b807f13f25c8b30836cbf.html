Statement

Let X_0,X_1,\ldots be independent integrable real random variables and \mathcal F_n=\sigma(X_0,\ldots,X_n). If \mathbb E[X_k]=0 for every k, the Doob decomposition is A_n=-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}X_k and M_n=\sum_{k=0}^nX_k, where A_0=0. Here a Doob decomposition means a martingale M plus an integrable predictable process A starting at zero; predictability means A_n is measurable with respect to the preceding sigma algebra.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The mean-zero hypothesis and stochastic decomposition require probability theory, even though the displayed sums are finite.

Proof 1: Set the independent means to zero and shift the index

By the Doob decomposition formulas for independent observations, the Doob decomposition is given by

{\displaystyle A_{n}=\sum _{k=1}^{n}{\bigl (}\mathbb {E} [X_{k}]-X_{k-1}{\bigr )},\quad n\in \mathbb {N} _{0},}

and

{\displaystyle M_{n}=X_{0}+\sum _{k=1}^{n}{\bigl (}X_{k}-\mathbb {E} [X_{k}]{\bigr )},\quad n\in \mathbb {N} _{0}.}

If the random variables of the original sequence X have mean zero, this simplifies to

{\displaystyle A_{n}=-\sum _{k=0}^{n-1}X_{k}}    and    {\displaystyle M_{n}=\sum _{k=0}^{n}X_{k},\quad n\in \mathbb {N} _{0},}

hence both processes are (possibly time-inhomogeneous) random walks.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Setting the means to zero is elementary, but the conclusion is a Doob decomposition relative to a filtration and requires martingales, conditional expectation and predictability. Those interrelated probability concepts exceed the small-extension allowance.
