Statement

Let m\ge0 and f\in C^m(\mathbb R^n). Put f_\alpha=D^\alpha f for each multi-index \alpha with |\alpha|\le m. Then

f_\alpha(x)=\sum_{|\beta|\le m-|\alpha|}\frac{f_{\alpha+\beta}(y)}{\beta!}(x-y)^\beta+R_\alpha(x,y),

where R_\alpha(x,y)=o(|x-y|^{m-|\alpha|}) uniformly as x,y\to a, for every a\in\mathbb R^n. Multi-indices have nonnegative integer components; |\alpha| is their sum, \alpha! the product of their factorials and D^\alpha the corresponding partial derivative.

Proof 1: Taylor expansion of derivative data — outline

Given a real-valued Cm function f(x) on Rn, Taylor's theorem asserts that for each a, x, y ∈ Rn, there is a function Rα(x,y) approaching 0 uniformly as x,y → a such that

{\displaystyle f({\mathbf {x} })=\sum _{|\alpha |\leq m}{\frac {D^{\alpha }f({\mathbf {y} })}{\alpha !}}\cdot ({\mathbf {x} }-{\mathbf {y} })^{\alpha }+\sum _{|\alpha |=m}R_{\alpha }({\mathbf {x} },{\mathbf {y} }){\frac {({\mathbf {x} }-{\mathbf {y} })^{\alpha }}{\alpha !}}}

where the sum is over multi-indices α.

Let fα = Dαf for each multi-index α. Applying Taylor's theorem to each derivative (with its own remainder) yields

{\displaystyle f_{\alpha }({\mathbf {x} })=\sum _{|\beta |\leq m-|\alpha |}{\frac {f_{\alpha +\beta }({\mathbf {y} })}{\beta !}}({\mathbf {x} }-{\mathbf {y} })^{\beta }+R_{\alpha }({\mathbf {x} },{\mathbf {y} })}

where Rα is o(|x − y|m−|α|) uniformly as x,y → a.

The source leaves the derivative remainder estimate implicit.
