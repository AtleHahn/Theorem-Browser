Statement

Let S^1=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|=1\} under multiplication. The exponential homomorphism f:(\mathbb R,+)\to S^1, f(x)=e^{2\pi ix}, induces an isomorphism \mathbb R/\mathbb Z\cong S^1.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory abstract algebra supplies groups, ring operations, quotients and homomorphisms. The additional operations or central-element conditions are explicitly given; for the circle group, complex exponentials and continuity are familiar from calculus and topology. No further structural theory is needed to understand the claim.

Proof 1: Apply the first isomorphism theorem to the exponential map

Let {\displaystyle S^{1}} denote the circle group, consisting of all complex numbers with absolute value (or modulus) of {\displaystyle 1}, with the group operation being multiplication. Then the function {\displaystyle f:\mathbb {R} \to S^{1}} sending {\displaystyle x\mapsto e^{2\pi ix}=\cos(2\pi x)+i\sin(2\pi x)} is a surjective homomorphism with the integers being the kernel. The first isomorphism theorem then implies that {\displaystyle \mathbb {R} /\mathbb {Z} \cong S^{1}}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Compute the kernel and image of the trigonometric exponential homomorphism and apply the group isomorphism theorem. Quotient groups are ordinary introductory abstract algebra.
