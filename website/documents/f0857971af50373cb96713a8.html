Statement

Let G(k) be the least s such that every sufficiently large positive integer is a sum of at most s positive integer kth powers. Then G(2)=4.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The definition concerns representations of every integer beyond some threshold and is elementary.

Proof 1: Residue obstruction and the four-square upper bound

Since squares are congruent to 0, 1, or 4 modulo 8, no integer congruent to 7 modulo 8 can be represented as a sum of three squares, implying that G(2)\geq4.

Let g(k) denote the least number of kth powers needed for all positive integers. Since G(k)\leq g(k) for all k, and Lagrange's four-square theorem establishes g(2)=4, this shows that G(2)=4.
