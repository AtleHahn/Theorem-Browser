Statement

For an infinite set X and positive integers n,c, every coloring of the n-element subsets of X with c colors has an infinite subset M\subseteq X whose n-element subsets all receive one color.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Graphs, finite subset colorings and infinite sequences are elementary discrete mathematics. Matchings are disjoint edges and a ray is an infinite nonrepeating path; the conditions are explicitly given. No graph-minor or set-theoretic independence machinery is needed.

Proof 1: Build nested homogeneous tails

The proof is by induction on n, the size of the subsets. For n=1, the statement is equivalent to saying that if you split an infinite set into a finite number of sets, then one of them is infinite. This is evident. Assuming the theorem is true for n\le r, we prove it for n=r+1.

Given a c-colouring of the (r+1)-element subsets of X, let a_0 be an element of X and let Y=X\setminus\{a_0\}. We then induce a c-colouring of the r-element subsets of Y, by just adding a_0 to each r-element subset (to get an (r+1)-element subset of X). By the induction hypothesis, there exists an infinite subset Y_1 of Y such that every r-element subset of Y_1 is coloured the same colour in the induced colouring. Thus there is an element a_0 and an infinite subset Y_1 such that all the (r+1)-element subsets of X consisting of a_0 and r elements of Y_1 have the same colour.

By the same argument, there is an element a_1 in Y_1 and an infinite subset Y_2 of Y_1\setminus\{a_1\} with the same properties. Inductively, we obtain a sequence of distinct elements \{a_0,a_1,a_2,\ldots\} such that the colour of each (r+1)-element subset

\{a_{i(1)},a_{i(2)},\ldots,a_{i(r+1)}\}, \qquad i(1)<i(2)<\cdots<i(r+1),

depends only on the value of i(1). Further, there are infinitely many indices such that this colour will be the same. Take the a_i with these indices to get the desired monochromatic set.

Induct on subset size, recursively choose nested homogeneous tails and then retain infinitely many indices with the same color. Undergraduate sets and induction suffice with dependent choice in its sequential-selection form.
