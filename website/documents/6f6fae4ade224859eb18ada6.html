Proof 1: Take logarithms and apply Stolz–Cesàro — outline

Let (x_n) be a sequence of positive real numbers converging to \ell and define

a_n:=\log(x_1\cdots x_n),\qquad b_n:=n.

We compute

\begin{aligned} \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_{n+1}-a_n}{b_{n+1}-b_n} &=\lim_{n\to\infty}\log\left(\frac{x_1\cdots x_{n+1}}{x_1\cdots x_n}\right)\\ &=\lim_{n\to\infty}\log(x_{n+1}) =\lim_{n\to\infty}\log(x_n)=\log(\ell), \end{aligned}

where we used the fact that the logarithm is continuous. At the endpoints we understand \log(0)=-\infty and \log(+\infty)=+\infty, using the corresponding limits of the logarithm. Thus, by the Stolz–Cesàro theorem,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(x_1\cdots x_n)}n =\lim_{n\to\infty}\log\left((x_1\cdots x_n)^{1/n}\right)=\log(\ell).

By continuity of the exponential, including its limits at -\infty and +\infty, we can conclude that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{x_1\cdots x_n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}x_n.
