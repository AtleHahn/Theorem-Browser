Statement

For every \varepsilon>0 there is a constant c(\varepsilon)>0 such that every real normed space X of integer dimension N\ge1 contains a subspace E of dimension k\ge c(\varepsilon)\log N and a Euclidean norm |\cdot| on E satisfying |x|\le\|x\|\le(1+\varepsilon)|x| for every x\in E.

Proof 1: Invert the exponential dimension threshold — outline

Use Milman’s exponential threshold bound for Dvoretzky’s theorem: the dimension threshold for an almost-Euclidean k-dimensional subspace can be taken to satisfy N(k,\varepsilon)\le\exp(C(\varepsilon)k), with C(\varepsilon)>0 depending only on \varepsilon. Thus the subspace dimension can be bounded below by a positive constant depending only on \varepsilon times \log N.

Level justification

Apply the explicit finite-dimensional norm-comparison threshold and invert its exponential bound, adjusting integer rounding and small dimensions through the constant. Norms, subspaces and logarithms are undergraduate linear algebra and analysis; the deep theorem proof is not needed.
