Statement

Let {I_t:t∈T} be a nonempty family of nonempty closed bounded real intervals, totally ordered by inclusion. Then ⋂_{t∈T} I_t is nonempty.

Proof 1: Separate complementary rays and use connectedness

Write I_t=[a_t,b_t]. Consider the complement of each interval, written as (-\infty,a_t)\cup(b_t,\infty). By De Morgan's laws, the complement of the intersection is a union of two disjoint open sets,

U=\bigcup_{t\in T}(-\infty,a_t),\qquad V=\bigcup_{t\in T}(b_t,\infty).

The sets are nonempty, and nesting makes them disjoint. By the connectedness of the real line there must be something between them. This shows that the intersection of (even an uncountable number of) nested, closed, and bounded intervals is nonempty.

Level justification

The proof takes complements using De Morgan’s laws and observes that the two unions of open rays are disjoint because every pair of intervals is nested. Each union is nonempty, and connectedness of R prevents them from covering R. These are basic point-set topology and elementary set operations, including arbitrary unions; no advanced order theory is required.
