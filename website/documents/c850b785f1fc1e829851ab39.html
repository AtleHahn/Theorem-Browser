On \mathbb R^n with pairing (x,\xi)\mapsto e^{2\pi ix\cdot\xi}, Lebesgue measure is its own Fourier-dual measure.

Proof 1: Rescale the frequency variable (outline)

For the classical pairing e^{ix\cdot\xi}, Fourier inversion pairs Lebesgue measure with (2\pi)^{-n} times Lebesgue measure.

If we identify {\displaystyle \mathbb {R} ^{n}} with its dual group, by using the pairing

{\displaystyle (\mathbf {v} ,\mathbf {w} )\mapsto e^{2\pi i\mathbf {v} \cdot \mathbf {w} },}

then Lebesgue measure on {\displaystyle \mathbb {R} ^{n}} is equal to its own dual measure.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Rescaling the frequency is elementary, but the assertion identifies Fourier-dual Haar measures using the Euclidean dual group. Retain beyond undergraduate provisionally for that harmonic-analysis setting.
