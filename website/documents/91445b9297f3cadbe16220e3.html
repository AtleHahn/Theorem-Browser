For independent random vectors with fixed individual entropies, appropriately matched Gaussians minimize the entropy of the sum. This relates aggregation to quantitative uncertainty and gives Gaussian behavior an extremal role; differential entropy here should not be identified with thermodynamic entropy without additional modeling assumptions.

Statement

Let n be a positive integer. For a random vector Z in \mathbb R^n with density f, let h(Z)=-\int f\log f and N(Z)=(2\pi e)^{-1}\exp(2h(Z)/n). Let X,Y be independent random vectors with densities in L^p(\mathbb R^n) for some p>1. Suppose independent nondegenerate Gaussian vectors X',Y' with proportional covariance matrices are chosen so that h(X')=h(X) and h(Y')=h(Y). Then the entropy power inequality N(X+Y)\ge N(X)+N(Y) is equivalently h(X+Y)\ge h(X'+Y'). In particular the latter inequality holds, with a possible +\infty left-hand side.

Proof 1: Match Gaussian entropies and rewrite entropy power — outline

Choose the independent Gaussian vectors with proportional covariance matrices and matching entropies. The entropy power inequality can then be rewritten in the equivalent form h(X+Y)\ge h(X'+Y').

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The equivalence is a scalar exponential rewrite, but the hypotheses involve general Lp densities, differential entropy, possible infinite integrals and the equality case of a multidimensional entropy-power theorem. Retain beyond undergraduate provisionally for the combined measure-theoretic information-theory prerequisites.
