Statement

For n\ge2 let A_n=\{\lceil(n+1)/2\rceil,\ldots,n\} and H_n=\sum_{i=1}^n1/i. Then \frac1{H_n}\sum_{i\in A_n}1/i=O(1/\log n) as n\to\infty.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves harmonic sums; asymptotic bounds; real logarithm. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Large denominators — outline

All elements of A_n are large, lying in the upper half of \{1,\ldots,n\}. Therefore this subset has logarithmic density only O(1/\log n). For this asymptotic normalization, the harmonic sum H_n is equivalent to \log n.

Level justification
