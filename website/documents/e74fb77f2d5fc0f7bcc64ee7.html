Statement

For a closed connected oriented surface S, write \operatorname{Mod}(S) for orientation-preserving homeomorphisms modulo isotopy, and \operatorname{Mod}^{\pm}(S) when both orientations are allowed. Let S have genus g, let \rho:\operatorname{Mod}(S)\to\operatorname{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb Z) be its action on first integral homology, and let n\ge3 be an integer. Reduce matrix entries modulo n to obtain \Phi_n:\operatorname{Mod}(S)\to\operatorname{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z). Then \ker\Phi_n is a finite-index torsion-free subgroup. Here torsion-free means having no nonidentity finite-order element.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Mapping class groups, isotopy classes, homology actions and symplectic congruence subgroups require substantial geometric topology. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Apply Minkowski and Torelli torsion-freeness

An interesting class of finite-index subgroups is given by the kernels of the morphisms:

\Phi_n:\operatorname{Mod}(S)\to\operatorname{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb Z)\to\operatorname{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z).

The kernel of \Phi_n is usually called a congruence subgroup of \operatorname{Mod}(S). It is a torsion-free group for all n\geq3 (this follows easily from a classical result of Minkowski on linear groups and the fact that the Torelli group is torsion-free).

Here the Torelli group is \ker\rho, and the Minkowski result used is that the kernel of reduction \operatorname{GL}_m(\mathbb Z)\to\operatorname{GL}_m(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z) is torsion-free for n\geq3. The source supplies the deduction at this level of detail.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Map torsion into the matrix congruence kernel, then into the Torelli group, and use finiteness of the modular image. The group argument is elementary but surface isotopy, homology representations and Torelli groups require substantial topology.
