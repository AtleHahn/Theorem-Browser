Statement

Let g:J\to\mathbb R be continuous on an open interval J, and u,v:I\to J differentiable on an open real interval I. Then \frac d{dx}\int_{u(x)}^{v(x)}g(t)\,dt=g(v(x))v'(x)-g(u(x))u'(x).

Proof 1: Compose a primitive with the two endpoints

This follows from the chain rule and the first fundamental theorem of calculus. Define

G(x)=\int_{u(x)}^{v(x)}g(t)\,dt,

and, choosing a number c in the domain J of g,

\Gamma(y)=\int_c^y g(t)\,dt.

Then G(x) can be written as a composition:

G(x)=(\Gamma\circ v)(x)-(\Gamma\circ u)(x).

The chain rule then implies that

G'(x)=\Gamma'(v(x))v'(x)-\Gamma'(u(x))u'(x).

By the first fundamental theorem of calculus, \Gamma'(y)=g(y). Therefore, substituting this result above, we get the desired equation:

G'(x)=g(v(x))v'(x)-g(u(x))u'(x).
