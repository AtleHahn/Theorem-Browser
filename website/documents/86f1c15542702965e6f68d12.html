Statement

Let G\subset\mathbb C be a simply connected domain, a\in G, and D=\{z:|z|<1\}. Suppose the family \mathcal F of injective holomorphic maps f:G\to D satisfying f(a)=0, f^{\prime}(a)>0 is nonempty. Then \sup_{f\in\mathcal F}f^{\prime}(a) is attained, and every attaining map sends G onto D.

Proof 1: A nonempty normalized univalent family has a surjective extremal map (outline)

The family \mathcal F is normal by Montel's theorem. Let 0<M\leq\infty be the supremum of f'(a) for f\in\mathcal F. Pick f_n\in\mathcal F with f_n'(a) tending to M. By Montel's theorem, passing to a subsequence if necessary, f_n tends to a holomorphic function f uniformly on compacta. By Hurwitz's theorem, f is either univalent or constant. Convergence of the derivatives gives f'(a)=M; thus M is finite and positive, and f(a)=0. The open mapping theorem places the nonconstant limit's image in D, so f\in\mathcal F.

It remains to check that any maximizing conformal mapping f takes G onto D. If not, take c\ne0 in D\setminus f(G) and let H be a holomorphic square root of

\frac{f(z)-c}{1-\overline c f(z)}

on G, using simple connectivity and the fact that this function is nowhere zero. The function H is univalent and maps G into D. Let

F(z)=\frac{e^{i\theta}(H(z)-H(a))}{1-\overline{H(a)}H(z)},

where H'(a)/|H'(a)|=e^{-i\theta}. Then F\in\mathcal F and a routine computation shows that

F'(a)=\frac{e^{i\theta}H'(a)}{1-|H(a)|^2} =f'(a)\frac{\sqrt{|c|}+\sqrt{|c|^{-1}}}{2} >f'(a)=M.

This contradicts the maximality of M, so that f must take all values in D.
