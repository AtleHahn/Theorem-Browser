Statement

For an integer n\ge1, the even Bernoulli number has the explicit formula B_{2n}=\sum_{j=0}^{2n}\frac1{j+1}\sum_{m=0}^j(-1)^m\binom jm m^{2n}. In lowest terms with positive denominator, the denominator of B_{2n} equals D=\prod_{p\text{ prime},\ p-1\mid2n}p. In particular it is square-free and divisible by 6.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The explicitly defined rational number, reduced denominator, product of primes, and square-free condition require finite arithmetic and divisibility.

Proof 1: Denominator of a sum of prime reciprocals (outline)

Use the von Staudt–Clausen theorem:

if n is a positive integer and we add 1/p to the Bernoulli number B2n for every prime p such that p − 1 divides 2n, then we obtain an integer; that is,

{\displaystyle B_{2n}+\sum _{(p-1)|2n}{\frac {1}{p}}\in \mathbb {Z} .}

This fact immediately allows us to characterize the denominators of the non-zero Bernoulli numbers B2n as the product of all primes p such that p − 1 divides 2n; consequently, the denominators are square-free and divisible by 6.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The Bernoulli number is explicitly defined by a finite rational sum, and the proof uses only the supplied integer-plus-reciprocals assertion. Multiply by the product D of the finitely many relevant primes: modulo any such p, all terms except D/p vanish, so that prime cannot cancel from the denominator. Primes 2 and 3 qualify because 1 and 2 divide 2n. This is elementary divisibility and fraction arithmetic, without analytic Bernoulli-number theory.
