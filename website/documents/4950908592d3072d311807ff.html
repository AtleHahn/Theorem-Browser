Statement

Let K be a field. Write \mathcal P(K)=\bigcup_{n\ge1}K((T^{1/n})), where K((T^{1/n})) consists of formal series \sum_{k\ge k_0}c_kT^{k/n} with k_0\in\mathbb Z and c_k\in K. Assume K has characteristic zero. For 0\ne P(Y)=\sum_{a_i\ne0} a_iY^i\in\mathcal P(K)[Y], fix v_0\in\mathbb Q, put \mu=\min_i(v(a_i)+iv_0) and \chi(X)=\sum_{v(a_i)+iv_0=\mu}c_iX^i, where c_i is the initial coefficient. Suppose \gamma\ne0 is a root of \chi of multiplicity m. Set y_0=\gamma T^{v_0}. For any nonzero z\in\mathcal P(K) with v(z)>v_0 and integer j\ge0, v(P^{(j)}(y_0)z^j)\ge\mu+j(v(z)-v_0), with equality exactly when \chi^{(j)}(\gamma)\ne0. In particular, every term with j>m has valuation greater than the term with j=m. Here v is the least exponent of a nonzero series, v(0)=\infty, and all minimum/index conditions run over nonzero coefficients.

Proof 1: Differentiated initial coefficients — outline

Differentiation with respect to Y does not change the valuation in T of any surviving coefficient, since K has characteristic zero. Thus

v\left(P^{(j)}(y_0)z^j\right) \geq\min_i\bigl(v(a_i)+iv_0\bigr)+j\bigl(v(z)-v_0\bigr),

and equality occurs if and only if \chi^{(j)}(\gamma)\ne0. Here the coefficient at the candidate lowest exponent in P^{(j)}(y_0) is \chi^{(j)}(\gamma).

If m is the multiplicity of \gamma as a root of \chi, it results that the inequality is an equality for j=m. The terms such that j>m can be forgotten as far as one is concerned with the minimum valuation, as v(z)>v_0 and j>m imply

v\left(P^{(j)}(y_0)z^j\right) \geq\min_i\bigl(v(a_i)+iv_0\bigr)+j\bigl(v(z)-v_0\bigr) >v\left(P^{(m)}(y_0)z^m\right).

The scalar factors 1/j! in Taylor's formula have valuation zero, so the same comparison applies to the Taylor terms.
