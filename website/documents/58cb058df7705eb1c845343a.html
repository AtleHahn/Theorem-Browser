Statement

There are no sixteen real bilinear forms z_j(x,y) in x,y\in\mathbb R^{16} satisfying (\sum_{i=1}^{16}x_i^2)(\sum_{i=1}^{16}y_i^2)=\sum_{j=1}^{16}z_j(x,y)^2 identically. Here bilinear means of the form z_j(x,y)=\sum_{k,l=1}^{16}a_{jkl}x_ky_l with fixed real coefficients.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Bilinearity is explicitly a finite polynomial formula with real coefficients. Nonexistence of coefficients satisfying an identity requires no abstract multilinear theory.

Proof 1: Exclude sixteen using Hurwitz’s theorem

Hurwitz’s theorem says an identity (\sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2)(\sum_{i=1}^n y_i^2)=\sum_{i=1}^n z_i(x,y)^2 with bilinear z_i is possible only for n\in\{1,2,4,8\}. Since 16 is not in that set, no such identity exists.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Use the explicitly stated list of possible sizes and observe that sixteen is absent. Bilinear forms are defined as finite sums of coefficient products, so only school polynomial algebra and membership in a finite list are needed; the Hurwitz proof is not used.
