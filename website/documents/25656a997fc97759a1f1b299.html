Statement

For positive integers m,n, \int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\cos(mx)\cos(nx)\,dx=\pi\delta_{mn}, \int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\sin(mx)\sin(nx)\,dx=\pi\delta_{mn} and \int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\cos(mx)\sin(nx)\,dx=0, where \delta_{mn}=1 if m=n and 0 otherwise.

Proof 1: Integrate the product-to-sum identities

The product-to-sum identities give

\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\cos(mx)\cos(nx)\,dx=\frac12\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}[\cos((n-m)x)+\cos((n+m)x)]\,dx=\pi\delta_{mn},\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\sin(mx)\sin(nx)\,dx=\frac12\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}[\cos((n-m)x)-\cos((n+m)x)]\,dx=\pi\delta_{mn}.

Likewise,

\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\cos(mx)\sin(nx)\,dx=\frac12\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}[\sin((n+m)x)+\sin((n-m)x)]\,dx=0.
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Integrate the product-to-sum identities over a full period, separating the zero-frequency case. Undergraduate elementary integral calculus suffices.
