Statement

Let D be the open unit disk and f:D\to U a conformal bijection onto a bounded planar domain. If f has a continuous extension F:\overline D\to\mathbb C, then F(\overline D)=\overline U and F(\partial D)=\partial U.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex differentiability and the explicitly defined Fourier sums extend calculus directly. For the Fourier assertions, null sets can be understood through arbitrarily small interval covers, and residual sets through countable intersections of dense open sets. These limited additions to real analysis and topology suffice without general measure integration.

Proof 1: A continuous conformal extension maps boundary onto boundary — outline

Since f(D)=U, by compactness the continuous extension F carries the closure of D onto the closure of U and hence \partial D onto \partial U.

For the boundary assertion, a limit point F(\zeta) with \zeta\in\partial D must lie on \partial U, because f is a homeomorphism between D and U and thus a sequence converging in U has to be the image under f of a sequence converging in D. Applying this to a sequence in D tending to \zeta rules out F(\zeta)\in U. Surjectivity on closures and f(D)=U then give surjectivity on boundaries.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof is topological after using that the conformal bijection is a homeomorphism, but the statement concerns a general conformal map and its extension. Retain beyond undergraduate provisionally because the conformal framework is outside the baseline, even though no deeper analytic theorem is used here.
