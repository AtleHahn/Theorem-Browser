Statement

For a simple bipartite graph with parts U,V of sizes m,n, write z(m,n;s,t) for the largest edge count when no s vertices of U and t vertices of V form a complete bipartite subgraph K_{s,t}. Let 2\leq t\leq n and let A_1,\ldots,A_m\subseteq[n] all have size k. Suppose their families of t-element subsets are pairwise disjoint (a clique partition of their union). The bipartite incidence graph between the m subsets and the n points has km edges and contains no K_{2,t}. Thus z(m,n;2,t)\geq km.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The extremal number is defined as a largest edge count and the disjointness condition is explicitly about t-element subsets of finite sets. The incidence graph simply connects each indexed set to its members. Understanding the claimed count and forbidden K_(2,t) uses elementary finite sets and counting, not hypergraph theory.

Proof 1: Incidence graph of the clique partition

Let H=\bigcup_{i=1}^m\binom{A_i}{t}, the family partitioned into the t-subsets of the indexed sets A_i.

Supposing {\displaystyle A_{1},\dots ,A_{m}\subset [n]} is a partition of {\displaystyle H} into {\displaystyle m} cliques of size {\displaystyle k}, we can let {\displaystyle G} be the {\displaystyle m\times n} bipartite graph with {\displaystyle V_{1}=\{1,\dots ,m\}} and {\displaystyle V_{2}=[n]}, such that {\displaystyle i\sim v} in {\displaystyle G} if and only if {\displaystyle v\in A_{i}}. Since the {\displaystyle A_{i}} form a clique partition, {\displaystyle G} cannot contain a copy of {\displaystyle K_{2,t}}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Count k incidence edges for each of m sets. A K_(2,t) would give a t-subset common to two sets, contradicting disjointness of their t-subset families. Only finite counting is used.
