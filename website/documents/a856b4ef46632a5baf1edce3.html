Statement

Let \pi be an odd Gaussian prime and \alpha not divisible by \pi. There is a unique u\in\{1,i,-1,-i\} such that \alpha^{(N\pi-1)/4}\equiv u\pmod\pi; this defines the quartic character [\alpha/\pi].

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Gaussian integers are concrete complex numbers with integer coordinates. Ring ideals, quotient cardinality, prime elements and congruences are introductory algebra; the norm and primary/quartic conditions are explicit finite arithmetic definitions. No field-extension norm or ideal-class theory is required.

Proof 1: Take a fourth root in the residue field — outline

Here N\pi is the Gaussian norm and odd means not divisible by 1+i. Gauss proves the analogue of Fermat's theorem: if α is not divisible by an odd prime π, then

{\displaystyle \alpha ^{N\pi -1}\equiv 1{\pmod {\pi }}}

Since Nπ ≡ 1 (mod 4), {\displaystyle \alpha ^{\frac {N\pi -1}{4}}} makes sense, and {\displaystyle \alpha ^{\frac {N\pi -1}{4}}\equiv i^{k}{\pmod {\pi }}} for a unique unit ik.

This unit is called the quartic or biquadratic residue character of α (mod π) and is denoted by

{\displaystyle \left[{\frac {\alpha }{\pi }}\right]=i^{k}\equiv \alpha ^{\frac {N\pi -1}{4}}{\pmod {\pi }}.}
