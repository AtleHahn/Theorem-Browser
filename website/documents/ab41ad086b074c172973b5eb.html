Statement

Let X_1,\ldots,X_n be independent zero-one variables with success probability p\in(0,1), and let \bar X=n^{-1}\sum_iX_i. For 0<\varepsilon<1-p,

\Pr(\bar X\ge p+\varepsilon)\le e^{-2n\varepsilon^2}.

For 0<\varepsilon<p, \Pr(\bar X\le p-\varepsilon)\le e^{-2n\varepsilon^2}.

High school only
Level justification

The statement only uses finite independent trials and explicit real bounds.

Proof 1: Relax the relative-entropy bound — outline

Use the Chernoff–Hoeffding bound \Pr(\bar X\ge p+\varepsilon)\le e^{-nD(p+\varepsilon\Vert p)}, where D(q\Vert p)=q\log(q/p)+(1-q)\log((1-q)/(1-p)). A simpler bound follows by relaxing the Chernoff–Hoeffding theorem using D(p + ε || p) ≥ 2ε2, which follows from the convexity of D(p + ε || p) and the fact that

{\displaystyle {\frac {d^{2}}{d\varepsilon ^{2}}}D(p+\varepsilon \parallel p)={\frac {1}{(p+\varepsilon )(1-p-\varepsilon )}}\geq 4={\frac {d^{2}}{d\varepsilon ^{2}}}(2\varepsilon ^{2}).}

Both sides of the comparison have value and first derivative zero at \varepsilon=0. Therefore

\Pr(\bar X\ge p+\varepsilon)\le e^{-2n\varepsilon^2}.

To complete the proof for the symmetric case, we simply define the random variable Y_i=1-X_i, apply the same proof, and plug it into our bound.
