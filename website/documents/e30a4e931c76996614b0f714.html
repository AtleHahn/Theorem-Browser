Statement

Let A be the integral closure of \mathbb Z in \mathbb C, the ring of all algebraic integers. For every prime ideal \mathfrak p of A and every integer n>0, one has \mathfrak p^n=\mathfrak p.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory ring theory supplies ideals and primality. The ring A consists exactly of complex roots of monic integer polynomials; the ideal power is the ideal of finite sums of n-fold products. These two concrete definitions extend familiar polynomial and ideal arithmetic and can be learned within about 30 minutes. No number-field arithmetic, ramification or classification of integral closures is needed to understand the universal ideal equality.

Proof 1: Taking roots of algebraic integers

Take {\displaystyle A} to be the ring of algebraic integers (i.e., the integral closure of {\displaystyle \mathbb {Z} } in {\displaystyle \mathbb {C} }). If {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {p}}} is a prime ideal of A, then we have: {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {p}}^{n}={\mathfrak {p}}} for every integer {\displaystyle n>0}. Indeed, if {\displaystyle y\in {\mathfrak {p}}}, then {\displaystyle y=\alpha ^{n}} for some complex number {\displaystyle \alpha }. Now, {\displaystyle \alpha } is integral over {\displaystyle \mathbb {Z} }; thus in {\displaystyle A} and then in {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {p}}}, proving the claim.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory ring theory supplies prime ideals and ideal products. The added notion of an algebraic integer is just a complex root of a monic integer polynomial; transitivity of integrality is invoked, not proved. If y=alpha^n, transitivity puts alpha in A, primality puts alpha in p, and the product of n copies puts y in p^n. The opposite ideal inclusion is immediate. This is a small extension of polynomial and ideal algebra, without algebraic number field structure theory.

Treatment:

Statement used:

If S is integral over R and an element alpha is integral over S, then alpha is integral over R.

Application in proof:

Use R=Z, S=Z[y] and alpha^n=y to show the chosen complex root belongs to A.

Application level:

Undergraduate level

Level justification:

To apply transitivity, the student needs only the definition of integral as satisfying a monic polynomial; T^n-y supplies the second relation.

Review notes:

Alternatively, substitution of T^n into a monic integer polynomial satisfied by y gives a monic integer polynomial for alpha directly.
