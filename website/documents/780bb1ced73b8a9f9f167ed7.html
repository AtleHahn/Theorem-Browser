Statement

For a complex inner product linear in the first argument and antilinear in the second, \langle x,y\rangle=\tfrac14(\|x+y\|^2-\|x-y\|^2+i\|x+iy\|^2-i\|x-iy\|^2).

Proof 1: Conjugate the formula for the opposite convention

The complex part of the inner product depends on whether it is antilinear in the first or the second argument. Write \langle x\mid y\rangle for the convention antilinear in the first argument and \langle x,y\rangle for the convention antilinear in the second.

The polarization identity for the inner product \langle x\mid y\rangle, which is antilinear in the first argument, is

\langle x\mid y\rangle =\frac14\left(\|x+y\|^2-\|x-y\|^2-i\|x+iy\|^2+i\|x-iy\|^2\right).

The polarization identity for the inner product \langle x,y\rangle, which is antilinear in the second argument, follows from that of \langle x\mid y\rangle by the relationship

\langle x,y\rangle=\langle y\mid x\rangle =\overline{\langle x\mid y\rangle} \qquad\text{for all }x,y\in H.

So for any x,y\in H,

\langle x,y\rangle =\frac14\left(\|x+y\|^2-\|x-y\|^2+i\|x+iy\|^2-i\|x-iy\|^2\right).

Here H denotes the given complex inner-product space; the first-convention identity is used as a prerequisite.
