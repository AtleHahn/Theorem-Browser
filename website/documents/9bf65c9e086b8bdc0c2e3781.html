Statement

Let n\geq1 be an integer and let d_1\geq d_2\geq\cdots\geq d_n\geq0 be integers. There exists a finite simple undirected graph on vertices v_1,\ldots,v_n with \deg(v_i)=d_i for every i if and only if \sum_{i=1}^n d_i is even and

\sum_{i=1}^k d_i\leq k(k-1)+\sum_{i=k+1}^n\min(d_i,k) \qquad\text{for every integer }1\leq k\leq n.

A simple graph has no loops and at most one edge between each pair of distinct vertices; the degree of a vertex is the number of edges incident to it. An empty sum is zero.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The locally defined graph objects, finite sums, minimum and parity conditions require only elementary counting and inequalities. Understanding the equivalence does not require graph-realization algorithms, network flows or majorization theory.

Proof 1: Successive subrealizations and local edge exchanges (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorem1 and proof, pp.843–844).

In the edge-exchange proof, the symbol rendered as “=” between vertices denotes nonadjacency, not equality. Case (0) uses two distinct vertices. The all-zero list is realized by the edgeless graph; when n=1, the inequality forces this case. Every nonterminal exchange increases the degree list lexicographically within 0\le\deg(v_i)\le d_i, so the construction terminates.
