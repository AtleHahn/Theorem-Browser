Statement

A finite simple undirected graph consists of finitely many vertices and edges joining distinct unordered pairs, with no repeated edge. Neighbors are vertices joined by an edge; a vertex’s degree is its number of neighbors. Connected means that every two vertices are joined by a path. A proper coloring assigns colors to vertices so that neighbors have different colors. Let G have maximum degree at most \Delta. Suppose it has a spanning tree rooted at v with two distinct nonadjacent neighbors u,w of v that are leaves of the tree. Then G has a proper coloring with at most \Delta colors. A spanning tree is a connected acyclic collection of edges containing all vertices. With a specified root v, the parent of a nonroot vertex is the next vertex on its unique tree path to v; a nonroot leaf has no children.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Vertices, coloring, rooted spanning trees and leaves are defined as finite adjacency and path conditions.

Proof 1: Equal leaf colors and bottom-up tree ordering

Give u,w the same color, which is permitted because they are nonadjacent. Color the remaining vertices from the bottom of the rooted tree upwards, ending at v. Every remaining nonroot vertex has its parent still uncolored, so at most \Delta-1 colored neighbors forbid colors. At the root, the two neighbors u,w share a color, so again its neighbors use at most \Delta-1 distinct colors. A free color is available at every stage.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Assign the same color to the two nonadjacent leaves, then color toward the root. An uncolored parent or the repeated leaf color leaves a free color at every stage. Elementary finite counting suffices.
