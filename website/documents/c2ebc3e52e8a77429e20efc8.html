Statement

Fix positive real numbers \nu_0,\rho and an integer n\geq1. Reduce the frequencies \nu_0\rho^k (k=0,\ldots,n-1) modulo multiplication by integer powers of 2 to distinct representatives 1\leq r_1<\cdots<r_m<2. The cyclic neighboring frequency ratios

r_2/r_1,\ldots,r_m/r_{m-1},2r_1/r_m

have at most three distinct values (when m=1, the only ratio is 2).

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement is entirely about a finite list of positive real numbers, normalization by powers of two and their neighboring ratios.

Proof 1: Logarithmic mapping to circle rotations

Map a frequency \nu to angle 2\pi\log_2\nu modulo 2\pi, identifying octave-equivalent frequencies. Successive multiplication by \rho gives a fixed angular increment 2\pi\log_2\rho. Thus the external three-gap theorem gives at most three angular gaps. A neighboring ratio corresponds to 2 raised to the circular logarithmic gap, so the number of distinct ratios is likewise at most three.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Convert multiplication to addition of logarithms on a circle and apply the supplied finite-gap bound. School logarithms, exponent laws and circular gaps suffice; no dynamical-systems proof is used.
