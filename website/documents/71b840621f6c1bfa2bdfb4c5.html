Statement

Let L>0. Let C:[0,L]\to\mathbb R^2 and C^*:[0,L]\to\mathbb R^3 be regular C^2 curves parametrized by arc length. Assume that the plane curve C, together with the straight chord joining its endpoints, forms a convex closed curve. Write

\kappa(s)=\|C''(s)\|,\qquad \kappa^*(s)=\|(C^*)''(s)\|

for their curvatures. If \kappa^*(s)\le\kappa(s) for every s\in[0,L], then

\|C^*(L)-C^*(0)\|\ge\|C(L)-C(0)\|.

Thus the space curve has at least as large an endpoint distance as the corresponding convex plane arc of greater curvature.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

For an undergraduate familiar with vector calculus, arc length and the displayed curvature formula are a small extension; the conclusion is a comparison of Euclidean distances.
