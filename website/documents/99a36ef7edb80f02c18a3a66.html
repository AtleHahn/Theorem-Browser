Statement

Let n\geq1 and A,B\in M_n(\mathbb C). Write A^* for the conjugate transpose and \operatorname{tr}A for the sum of diagonal entries. Then there exists a unitary matrix U (so U^*U=UU^*=I_n) with B=U^*AU if and only if

\operatorname{tr}W(A,A^*)=\operatorname{tr}W(B,B^*)

for every word W in two noncommuting variables. A word is a finite ordered product of copies of the two variables; evaluation substitutes the two matrices in that order, and the empty word evaluates to I_n.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Standard undergraduate complex linear algebra suffices; words are explicitly finite products.

Proof 1: Semisimple characters and polar decomposition (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Section3.1, Theorem3.1, p.9; Theorem2.5 and Lemma2.6, pp.7–8).

Use a singleton family of matrices over \mathbb C. The word monoid includes the empty word and exchanges the two letters while reversing their order under its involution. If the source gives VA=BV with V unitary, take U=V^* to obtain B=U^*AU. Conversely, conjugation carries every adjoint word to its counterpart and preserves its trace.

In the character step, invariant orthogonal complements give irreducible summands. Scalar intertwiners and the projection onto an orbit of basis tuples show that the generated algebra can act independently on its inequivalent summands; equal traces then force equal multiplicities.
