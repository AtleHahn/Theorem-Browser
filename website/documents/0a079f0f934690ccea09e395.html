Statement

Let m=2^{32} and let (r_i)_{i\geq1} be any sequence of integers such that

0<r_i<m,\qquad r_{i+1}\equiv65539\,r_i\pmod m.

Set u_i=r_i/m. There exist at most fifteen affine planes in \mathbb R^3 whose union contains every consecutive triple

(u_i,u_{i+1},u_{i+2}),\qquad i\geq1.

An affine plane here is a set \{(x,y,z):ax+by+cz=d\} for real constants with (a,b,c)\ne(0,0,0).

Level
High school only
Level justification

The modular recurrence, normalization and planes are explicitly defined; understanding the concentration conclusion requires only integer arithmetic and elementary coordinate geometry. No probability theory or proof method is needed.

Review notes

  • A concrete and historically influential example of severe geometric correlation in a multiplicative pseudorandom generator; the fifteen-plane bound is much stronger than the general dimension-three bound.
