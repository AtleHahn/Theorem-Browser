Statement

Every complex character of a finite group G is a rational linear combination of characters induced from cyclic subgroups of G.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Characters and induction of representations of finite groups require representation theory, beyond ordinary real/complex linear algebra.

Proof 1: All cyclic subgroups cover the group

Use the finite-cokernel form of Artin's induction criterion: if G is the union of conjugates of the subgroups in X, then the cokernel of

\operatorname{Ind}:\bigoplus_{H\in X}\mathcal R(H)\longrightarrow\mathcal R(G)

is finite. Here \mathcal R(H) is the group of virtual complex characters, whose elements are differences of characters. Finite cokernel means that an integer multiple of each character belongs to the image, giving a rational linear combination of induced characters.

This implies Artin's original statement, by choosing X to be the set of all cyclic subgroups of G.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The final coverage observation is elementary group theory, but the invoked criterion concerns virtual characters, induction maps and representation rings. Understanding these objects and the finite-cokernel assertion requires a substantial representation-theory framework beyond introductory groups and linear algebra. The arithmetic conversion from an integer multiple to a rational combination does not remove that prerequisite burden.

Proof 1 — All cyclic subgroups cover the group

Statement used:

If the conjugates of subgroups H in X cover a finite group G, the cokernel of the additive induction map from the direct sum of R(H) to R(G) is finite.

Application in proof:

Take X to be all cyclic subgroups; every g belongs to its generated cyclic subgroup. A positive multiple of each character then lies in the induction image.

Replace virtual characters in an induction preimage by differences of actual characters before dividing by the positive integer multiple.
