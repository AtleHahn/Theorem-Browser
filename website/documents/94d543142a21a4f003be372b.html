Statement

Let R be a nonzero ring with identity and view R^2 as a left R-module. Then R is local, meaning that every x\in R has either x or 1-x a unit, if and only if every nonzero proper direct summand M\subset R^2 satisfies R^2=(0\times R)\oplus M or R^2=(R\times0)\oplus M.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The equivalence concerns arbitrary noncommutative rings and module splittings.

Proof 1: Azumaya implication and the split difference map

For the forward direction use the source-stated consequence of Azumaya’s theorem: a nonzero proper summand in the direct sum of two regular modules over a local ring has one of these coordinate complements. Conversely, suppose the coordinate-complement property holds, and fix x\in R. Put y=x-1, define \sigma:R^2\to R by \sigma(a,b)=a-b, and define \eta:R\to R^2 by \eta(a)=(ax,ay). Since x-y=1, \sigma\eta is the identity. Thus M=\eta(R) is a nonzero proper direct summand, complementary to the nonzero diagonal kernel of \sigma. If R^2=(0\times R)\oplus M, the first-coordinate projection restricts to an isomorphism M\to R, so the left-module endomorphism a\mapsto ax of R is invertible. Hence x is a unit. If instead R^2=(R\times0)\oplus M, the same argument gives that y is a unit, so -y=1-x is a unit. This is the local-ring condition.
