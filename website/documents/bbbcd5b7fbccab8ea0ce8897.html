Statement

A simple plane polygon is a closed chain of finitely many line segments with no intersections except common endpoints of successive sides. An ear at three consecutive vertices u,v,w is the triangle uvw whose opposite side uw is an internal diagonal (its open segment lies in the polygon interior). A triangulation divides the polygon into triangles using noncrossing internal diagonals. Every simple polygon with more than three vertices has at least two ears.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The local definitions describe finite configurations of line segments and triangles in the Euclidean plane; no tree theory is needed to understand the assertion.

Proof 1: Decompose at convex corners, then use dual leaves — outline

A finite tree is a connected finite graph without cycles; a leaf is a vertex with exactly one neighbor.

Construct a triangulation

Let {\displaystyle v} be a vertex that is preceded by vertex {\displaystyle u} and followed by vertex {\displaystyle w}.

We say that {\displaystyle v} is a convex corner if there is a point {\displaystyle A} on the edge {\displaystyle uv} and a point {\displaystyle B} on the edge {\displaystyle vw} such that the open segment {\displaystyle AB} lies within the polygon. If the points {\displaystyle A,B} were the vertices {\displaystyle u,w} then we would have an ear - but in general there are corners that are not ears.

Every simple polygon has at least three convex corners . If one of these vertices, v, is not an ear, then it can be connected by a diagonal to another polygon vertex x, distinct from u,v,w, in the closed triangle uvw formed by v and its two neighbors; x can be chosen to be the vertex within this triangle that is farthest from line uw. This diagonal decomposes the polygon into two smaller polygons, and repeated decomposition by ears and diagonals eventually produces a triangulation of the whole polygon, from which ears can be found as leaves of the dual tree.

The geometric visibility check for this extremal choice of x is implicit in the source.

Obtain two ears

Once the polygon is triangulated, the weak dual of the triangulation (a graph with one vertex per triangle and one edge per pair of adjacent triangles) will be a tree and each leaf of the tree will form an ear. Since every tree with more than one vertex has at least two leaves, every triangulated polygon with more than one triangle has at least two ears.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Split the polygon at convex corners to triangulate it, then interpret leaves of the dual tree as ears. Elementary planar geometry, finite decomposition and the stated tree properties suffice.

The source specifies the extremal-vertex diagonal construction and the decreasing decomposition, but leaves its geometric visibility check implicit.
