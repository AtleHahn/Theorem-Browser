Statement

Let S be a smooth regular surface in Euclidean three-space, let p\in S, and choose a unit normal N to S at p. If two smooth curves \gamma_1,\gamma_2 in S pass through p and have the same tangent line there, then they have the same normal curvature there. More precisely, parametrizing each curve by arc length with \gamma_j(0)=p,

\langle\gamma_1''(0),N\rangle=\langle\gamma_2''(0),N\rangle.

The scalar product with the surface normal is the normal curvature; reversing the tangent orientation does not change it. A regular surface is locally parametrized by a smooth map with two linearly independent first derivatives.

Level justification

Surface parametrizations, derivatives and normal-vector projection are a small extension of undergraduate multivariable calculus and linear algebra.
