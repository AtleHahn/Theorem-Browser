Statement

A Banach space X is a complete normed vector space over \mathbb R or \mathbb C. It is uniformly convex if for every 0<\epsilon\le2 there exists \delta>0 such that \|x\|,\|y\|\le1 and \|x-y\|\ge\epsilon imply \|(x+y)/2\|\le1-\delta. Its continuous dual X^* consists of bounded linear functionals with the operator norm, and X^{**}=(X^*)^*. Reflexivity means surjectivity of the canonical map J:X\to X^{**}, J(x)(f)=f(x). There exists a reflexive Banach space that is not isomorphic to any uniformly convex Banach space. Here an isomorphism is a bijective bounded linear map with bounded inverse. In particular, no equivalent norm on this space is uniformly convex; equivalent norms are those bounded above and below by positive constant multiples of each other.

Proof 1: Reflexive direct sum and binary averages (external)

A historical or contextual reference can be found here (historical attribution; the direct proof is given below). The complete argument below is supplied here and is not attributed to that reference.

The following direct argument is supplied here. Let X=(direct sum over m>=1 of l1^{2^m})_2, over R (or C). Its dual is the l2 sum of the finite-dimensional duals l_infinity^{2^m}, and its bidual is the original l2 sum, by coordinatewise duality and Cauchy–Schwarz; hence it is a reflexive Banach space. Suppose an equivalent uniformly convex norm |.| satisfies c||x||<=|x|<=C||x|| with 0<c<=C. Within the mth summand form the complete binary tree of averages of the 2^m unit coordinate vectors: each node is the average over a dyadic block, and each parent is the midpoint of its children. Every node has original l1 norm one, and siblings have disjoint supports and distance two. Put delta=delta_UC(2c/C)>0 (decrease it below 1 if necessary). Leaves have new norm at most C. If children have norm at most R<=C, their distance is at least 2c, so after division by R uniform convexity yields parent norm at most R(1-delta), since 2c/R>=2c/C. Continue until the estimate C(1-delta)^m<c, contradicting that the root has new norm at least c. If a radius R<c occurs earlier, the contradiction is already present. Thus no equivalent uniformly convex norm exists. An isomorphism to a uniformly convex Banach space would pull back such a norm.
