Testing a polygon correspondence by rigid motions

Statement

Let two plane polygons have their vertices paired in a specified boundary order, with distinct adjacent vertices. To test whether a rigid motion sends each vertex of the first to its specified partner, translate one paired vertex into place and rotate about it to match one paired side. If that side cannot be matched, the correspondence fails. Otherwise it succeeds exactly when either the resulting polygon already matches all paired vertices or reflection in the matched side does so.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement describes concrete translations, rotations and a possible line reflection of finitely many polygon vertices.

Proof 1: Translate, rotate and, if needed, reflect — outline

Congruence for the specified correspondence can be established graphically as follows:

  • First, match and label the corresponding vertices of the two figures.
  • Second, draw a vector from one of the vertices of one of the figures to the corresponding vertex of the other figure. Translate the first figure by this vector so that these two vertices match.
  • Third, rotate the translated figure about the matched vertex until one pair of corresponding sides matches.
  • Fourth, if the figures do not already match at all paired vertices, reflect the rotated figure about the line of this matched side to try to match the figures.

If at any time the step cannot be completed, the specified correspondence does not give congruence.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Match a point and side by translation and rotation, leaving only the two rigid motions fixing that side pointwise. School Euclidean transformations suffice.

The source gives the graphical recipe without proving exhaustiveness. After a side is fixed, the elementary two-point stabilizer fact accounts for the two final possibilities.
