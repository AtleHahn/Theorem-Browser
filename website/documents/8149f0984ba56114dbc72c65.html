Statement

Let M_1,\ldots,M_t be independent random complex d_1\times d_2 matrices with \mathbb E M_i=0 and \|M_i\|\le\gamma almost surely, where d_1,d_2,t are positive integers and \gamma>0. Here \|\cdot\| is the operator norm. For \varepsilon>0 and 0<\delta<1, if

t\ge\frac{8\gamma^2}{3\varepsilon^2}\log\frac{d_1+d_2}{\delta},

then \Pr(\|t^{-1}\sum_iM_i\|>\varepsilon)\le\delta.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Finite random matrices amount to finitely many scalar entries; expectation is entrywise, independence is between matrices, and almost surely means probability one. These linked probability definitions extend undergraduate operator norms and linear algebra.

Proof 1: Solve the quoted exponential bound for the sample count — outline

Use the matrix concentration bound

\Pr\left(\left\|t^{-1}\sum_iM_i\right\|>\varepsilon\right)\le(d_1+d_2)\exp\left(-\frac{3\varepsilon^2t}{8\gamma^2}\right).

Choosing the number of samples at the displayed logarithmic scale makes this bound at most \delta.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Solve the supplied exponential probability bound for t. Undergraduate linear algebra and probability suffice to understand random finite matrices and their operator norm; matrix concentration theory is not used beyond the explicit inequality.
