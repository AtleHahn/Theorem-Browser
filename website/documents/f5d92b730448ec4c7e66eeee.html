Statement

Let L(\phi^A,\partial_\mu\phi^A,x) be a smooth first-order Lagrangian density on an n-dimensional coordinate domain and let \phi be a smooth solution of its Euler–Lagrange equations. Write p_A^\mu=\partial L/\partial(\partial_\mu\phi^A); repeated indices are summed. Assume the Euler–Lagrange equations \partial_\mu p_A^\mu=\partial L/\partial\phi^A. Let Q be a smooth field-variation derivation which fixes the coordinates and commutes with coordinate derivatives. Suppose Q[L]=\partial_\mu f^\mu on the solution, for smooth f^\mu. Then J^\mu=p_A^\mu Q[\phi^A]-f^\mu satisfies \partial_\mu J^\mu=0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The Euler–Lagrange equations are written explicitly on a coordinate domain. A variation is a commuting parameter derivation, a limited extension of undergraduate multivariable differentiation; no geometric field theory is needed.

Proof 1: Boundary variation on arbitrary regions

For all compact regions N with smooth boundary in the coordinate domain, the symmetry hypothesis and the divergence theorem give

Q\left[\int_N L\,d^nx\right]=\int_{\partial N} f^\mu\,ds_\mu,

where ds_\mu is the oriented boundary surface element.

Now, for any such N, integration by parts and the Euler–Lagrange equations give, on the solution,

\begin{aligned} Q\left[\int_N L\,d^nx\right] &=\int_N\left[\frac{\partial L}{\partial\phi^A} -\partial_\mu\frac{\partial L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi^A)}\right] Q[\phi^A]\,d^nx +\int_{\partial N}\frac{\partial L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi^A)} Q[\phi^A]\,ds_\mu\\ &=\int_{\partial N}f^\mu\,ds_\mu. \end{aligned}

The interior term vanishes by the Euler–Lagrange equations. Since this is true for any N, the divergence theorem and localization give

\partial_\mu\left[\frac{\partial L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi^A)} Q[\phi^A]-f^\mu\right]=0.

But this is the continuity equation for the current J^\mu defined by

J^\mu:=\frac{\partial L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi^A)}Q[\phi^A]-f^\mu,

which is called the Noether current associated with the symmetry.
