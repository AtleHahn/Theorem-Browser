As integers n\to\infty, \sum_{p\le n}(\log p)/p=\log n+O(1), with p prime.

Proof 1: Factorial estimate with a convergent higher-power tail — outline

The main step is

\begin{aligned} O(n)+n\log n=\log n! &=\sum_{p^j\leq n}\lfloor n/p^j\rfloor\log p\\ &=\sum_{p^j\leq n}\left(\frac{n}{p^j}+O(1)\right)\log p =n\sum_{p^j\leq n}\frac{\log p}{p^j}+O(n), \end{aligned}

where the last equality needs \sum_{p^j\leq n}\log p=O(n), which follows from \sum_{p\in(n,2n]}\log p\leq\log\binom{2n}{n}=O(n).

Thus, we have proved that

\sum_{p^j\leq n}\frac{\log p}{p^j}=\log n+O(1).

Since the sum over prime powers with j\geq2 converges, this implies

\sum_{p\leq n}\frac{\log p}{p}=\log n+O(1).
