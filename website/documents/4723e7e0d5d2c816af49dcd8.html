Statement

Let X be a real topological vector space, X^* its continuous dual, and f:X\to\mathbb R\cup\{+\infty\} a proper function (not identically +\infty). Define f^*(p)=\sup_{y\in X}(p(y)-f(y)) and f^{**}(x)=\sup_{p\in X^*}(p(x)-f^*(p)). Then f^{**}(x)\le f(x) for all x\in X.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The assertion concerns dual suprema on a general topological vector space.

Proof 1: Take the dual supremum of the Fenchel bound — outline

Write \langle p,x\rangle=p(x) for the dual pairing. The proof follows from the definition of convex conjugate: {\displaystyle f^{*}(p)=\sup _{\tilde {x}}\left\{\langle p,{\tilde {x}}\rangle -f({\tilde {x}})\right\}\geq \langle p,x\rangle -f(x).}

The Fenchel inequality just obtained implies f^{**}\le f.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The supremum comparison is elementary, but the actual formulation uses an arbitrary topological vector space, its continuous dual and the dual pairing, followed by extended-real conjugation twice. Understanding that complete functional-analytic setup exceeds a confident short extension of the baseline, consistent with the general topological-vector-space calibration. The proof does not require f to be convex or lower semicontinuous.
