Statement

Let a,b,c,d\in\mathbb R, a\ne0, set p=(3ac-b^2)/(3a^2), q=(2b^3-9abc+27a^2d)/(27a^3) and assume 4p^3+27q^2<0. Put R=2\sqrt{-p/3}, h=-b/(3a) and \theta=\frac13\arccos((3q/(2p))\sqrt{-3/p}). The three points (h+R\cos(\theta-2\pi k/3),R\sin(\theta-2\pi k/3)), k=0,1,2, form an equilateral triangle whose x-coordinates are the roots of ax^3+bx^2+cx+d. Its center has x-coordinate h.

High school only
Level justification

The construction consists of explicitly defined real circle coordinates and their projections.

Proof 1: Interpret the cosine roots as circle projections

Viète's trigonometric expression of the roots in the three-real-roots case lends itself to a geometric interpretation in terms of a circle. When the cubic is written in depressed form t3 + pt + q = 0, the solution can be expressed as

{\displaystyle t_{k}=2{\sqrt {-{\frac {p}{3}}}}\cos \left({\frac {1}{3}}\arccos \left({\frac {3q}{2p}}{\sqrt {\frac {-3}{p}}}\right)-k{\frac {2\pi }{3}}\right)\quad {\text{for}}\quad k=0,1,2\,.}

Here {\displaystyle \arccos \left({\frac {3q}{2p}}{\sqrt {\frac {-3}{p}}}\right)} is an angle in the unit circle; taking ⁠1/3⁠ of that angle corresponds to taking a cube root of a complex number; adding −k⁠2π/3⁠ for k = 1, 2 finds the other cube roots; and multiplying the cosines of these resulting angles by {\displaystyle 2{\sqrt {-{\frac {p}{3}}}}} corrects for scale.

For the non-depressed case ax^3+bx^2+cx+d=0, the depressed case is obtained by defining t such that x = t − ⁠b/3a⁠ so t = x + ⁠b/3a⁠. Graphically this corresponds to simply shifting the graph horizontally when changing between the variables t and x, without changing the angle relationships. This shift moves the point of inflection and the centre of the circle onto the y-axis. Consequently, the roots of the equation in t sum to zero. The three angles differ by 2\pi/3, so the circle points form an equilateral triangle; returning to x shifts its center to h=-b/(3a).
