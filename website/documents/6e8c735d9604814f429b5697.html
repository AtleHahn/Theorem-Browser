Statement

Let C be a field complete for a discrete valuation, and let A,B be finite extensions inside a common algebraic closure, with ramification indices a=e(A/C) and b=e(B/C). If B/C is tamely ramified and b divides a, then the compositum AB is unramified over A. A discrete valuation is an additive map v:C^*\to\mathbb Z with v(x+y)\ge\min(v(x),v(y)) when x+y\ne0; use a surjective normalization. The valuation ring is \{0\}\cup\{v\ge0\} and the residue field is its quotient by \{0\}\cup\{v>0\}. For a finite extension D/C, the normalized valuations satisfy v_D|_{C^*}=e(D/C)v_C. Tame means separable with separable residue extension, ramification index prime to the residue characteristic when positive, and degree equal to ramification index times residue degree. Unramified means separable with separable residue extension and degree equal to residue degree (hence ramification index one). The compositum is the smallest field containing A and B.
