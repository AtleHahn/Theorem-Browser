Statement

Let n\ge1 be an integer. For a Hermitian matrix X=\sum_j\lambda_jP_j, scalar functions act spectrally: h(X)=\sum_j h(\lambda_j)P_j. Here P_j are the orthogonal eigenprojections and \operatorname{Tr} is the matrix trace. Let R,F be Hermitian n\times n matrices with \operatorname{Tr}e^R=1 and put g=\operatorname{Tr}(Fe^R). Then \operatorname{Tr}(e^F e^R)\ge\operatorname{Tr}e^{F+R}\ge e^g.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Hermitian matrices; matrix exponential; trace; real exponential inequalities. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Exponential substitution in Klein’s inequality — outline

Use Golden–Thompson, \operatorname{Tr}e^{F+R}\le\operatorname{Tr}(e^F e^R), for the first inequality. For the second apply Klein’s inequality \operatorname{Tr}(h(A)-h(B)-(A-B)h'(B))\ge0 to h(x)=e^x, A=R+F and B=R+gI. Since e^B=e^g e^R and A-B=F-gI, its linear term has trace e^g(\operatorname{Tr}(Fe^R)-g\operatorname{Tr}e^R)=0, while \operatorname{Tr}e^B=e^g. The conclusion is \operatorname{Tr}e^{R+F}\ge e^g.
