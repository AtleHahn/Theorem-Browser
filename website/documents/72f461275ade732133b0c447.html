Statement

Let k be a nonzero commutative ring with identity and p,q,r\in k. In k\langle x,y,z\rangle, order words first by length and then lexicographically with x<y<z. Let I=(yx-pxy,zx-qxz,zy-ryz) be the two-sided ideal. Reductions replace the respective leading subwords yx,zx,zy by pxy,qxz,ryz. These relations form a noncommutative Gröbner basis: every element has a unique linear combination of reduced words after reductions, and the images of x^ay^bz^c for integers a,b,c\ge0 form a k-basis of k\langle x,y,z\rangle/I.

Proof 1: Resolve the single overlap

Diamond lemma (external prerequisite)

For monic word reductions decreasing such an order, if every overlap or inclusion ambiguity is resolvable, the reduced words form a basis of the quotient algebra. Equivalently, every element has a unique reduced expression. An overlap ambiguity consists of two overlapping occurrences of leading words, and an inclusion ambiguity of one occurrence inside another. It is resolvable when reducing either occurrence can be followed by reductions to the same expression.

The only overlap

There are no inclusion ambiguities and the only overlap is zyx. Reducing yx first gives

{\displaystyle zyx=pzxy=pqxzy=pqrxyz,}

Reducing zy first gives

{\displaystyle zyx=ryzx=rqyxz=rqpxyz.}

The coefficients commute in k, so the results agree. The diamond lemma gives the Gröbner-basis conclusion. Words avoiding yx,zx,zy are precisely the ordered monomials x^ay^bz^c.

Level justification

Resolve the only overlap of monic word reductions and invoke the diamond lemma to obtain a basis of the quotient over an arbitrary coefficient ring. Free associative algebras, quotient modules and noncommutative reduction bases together exceed a confidently small extension of introductory rings; retain beyond undergraduate provisionally despite the explicit word computation.
