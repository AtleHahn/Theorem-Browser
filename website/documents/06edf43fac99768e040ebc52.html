Statement

Let ABC be a nondegenerate Euclidean triangle, with opposite side lengths a,b,c, respective interior angles \alpha,\beta,\gamma, semiperimeter s=(a+b+c)/2, and inradius r>0. Then \cot(\alpha/2)\cot(\beta/2)\cot(\gamma/2)=\cot(\alpha/2)+\cot(\beta/2)+\cot(\gamma/2).

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement relates products and sums of cotangents of triangle half-angles, using elementary trigonometric notation.

Proof 1: Apply cotangent addition at half the triangle angle sum

The cotangent addition formula is \cot(u+v+w)=\frac{\cot u+\cot v+\cot w-\cot u\cot v\cot w}{1-\cot u\cot v-\cot v\cot w-\cot w\cot u}. Apply it at u=\alpha/2, v=\beta/2, w=\gamma/2. Since their sum is \pi/2, the left side is zero and its numerator is zero. Thus \cot(\alpha/2)\cot(\beta/2)\cot(\gamma/2)=\cot(\alpha/2)+\cot(\beta/2)+\cot(\gamma/2).

Level
High school only
Level justification

Substitute the three half-angles into the stated addition formula and use their sum pi/2 to make its numerator zero. School trigonometry suffices.
