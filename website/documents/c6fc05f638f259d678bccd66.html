Statement

Let R be a GCD domain with fraction field F. If a monic polynomial f\in R[x] has a root a/b\in F, then a/b\in R.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory ring theory supplies polynomial division, gcds, units, irreducibility and characteristic. A fraction field uses ordinary fractions over a domain; content is the gcd of coefficients. These closely related definitions suffice without advanced commutative algebra.

Proof 1: A fraction-field root of a monic polynomial lies in the GCD domain — proof

To see the statement, let {\displaystyle a/b} be a root of {\displaystyle f} in {\displaystyle F} and assume {\displaystyle a,b} are relatively prime. In {\displaystyle F[x]} we can write {\displaystyle f=(x-a/b)g} with {\displaystyle cg\in R[x]} for some nonzero {\displaystyle c\in R}. Then

{\displaystyle cbf=(bx-a)cg}

is a factorization in {\displaystyle R[x]}. But {\displaystyle bx-a} is primitive (its coefficients have gcd a unit) and thus {\displaystyle cb} divides the coefficients of {\displaystyle cg} by Gauss’s lemma (multiplicativity of coefficient gcds in a GCD domain), and so

{\displaystyle f=(bx-a)h}

with {\displaystyle h} in {\displaystyle R[x]}. Since {\displaystyle f} is monic, this is possible only when {\displaystyle b} is a unit.

Level justification

Clear denominators in a polynomial factorization, apply the supplied coefficient-gcd product rule and compare leading coefficients. Introductory ring theory suffices; a GCD domain adds the familiar gcd condition to an integral domain.
