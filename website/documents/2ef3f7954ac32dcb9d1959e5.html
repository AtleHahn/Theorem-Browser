Statement

Let f:X\to Y be a function. Regard power sets as categories ordered by inclusion. Direct image S\mapsto f[S] is left adjoint to inverse image T\mapsto f^{-1}[T]; equivalently f[S]\subseteq T if and only if S\subseteq f^{-1}[T]. Write f^*T=f^{-1}[T] and \exists_f S=f[S].

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement explicitly gives the inclusion equivalence underlying this adjunction. Understanding its full categorical wording additionally needs the inclusion-ordered power set as a category with at most one arrow per object pair, and adjunction as the corresponding Hom equivalence. This restricted definition fits brief preparation from undergraduate sets and functions; general category-theoretic results are unnecessary.

Proof 1: Source argument

For {\displaystyle S\subseteq X}, let us figure out the left adjoint, which is defined via

{\displaystyle {\operatorname {Hom} }(\exists _{f}S,T)\cong {\operatorname {Hom} }(S,f^{*}T),}

which here just means

{\displaystyle \exists _{f}S\subseteq T\leftrightarrow S\subseteq f^{-1}[T].}

Consider {\displaystyle f[S]\subseteq T}. We see {\displaystyle S\subseteq f^{-1}[f[S]]\subseteq f^{-1}[T]}. Conversely, If for an {\displaystyle x\in S} we also have {\displaystyle x\in f^{-1}[T]}, then clearly {\displaystyle f(x)\in T}. So {\displaystyle S\subseteq f^{-1}[T]} implies {\displaystyle f[S]\subseteq T}. We conclude that left adjoint to the inverse image functor {\displaystyle f^{*}} is given by the direct image. Here is a characterization of this result, which matches more the logical interpretation: The image of {\displaystyle S} under {\displaystyle \exists _{f}} is the full set of {\displaystyle y}'s, such that {\displaystyle f^{-1}[\{y\}]\cap S} is non-empty. This works because it neglects exactly those {\displaystyle y\in Y} which are in the complement of {\displaystyle f[S]}. So

{\displaystyle \exists _{f}S=\{y\in Y\mid \exists (x\in f^{-1}[\{y\}]).\,x\in S\;\}=f[S].}
