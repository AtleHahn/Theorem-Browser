Statement

Let G be finite and let K be a field of characteristic zero. Then K[G] is isomorphic as a K-algebra to a finite product \prod_i M_{n_i}(D_i) of full matrix algebras over division K-algebras D_i, one factor for each isomorphism class of irreducible K-representations of G.

Proof 1: Maschke followed by Wedderburn–Artin

Since K has characteristic zero, its characteristic does not divide |G|. Maschke's theorem therefore says that K[G] is semisimple. It is finite-dimensional over K, with basis the elements of G.

The importance of Maschke’s semisimplicity theorem stems from the well developed theory of semisimple rings, in particular, their classification as given by the Wedderburn–Artin theorem. When {\displaystyle K} is the field of complex numbers, this shows that the algebra {\displaystyle K[G]} is a product of several copies of complex matrix algebras, one for each irreducible representation. If the field {\displaystyle K} has characteristic zero, but is not algebraically closed, for example if {\displaystyle K} is the field of real or rational numbers, then a somewhat more complicated statement holds: the group algebra {\displaystyle K[G]} is a product of matrix algebras over division rings over {\displaystyle K}. The summands correspond to irreducible representations of {\displaystyle G} over {\displaystyle K}.
