Statement

For a nondegenerate disk-bounding geodesic triangle on the unit round sphere, with interior angles \theta_1,\theta_2,\theta_3 and area A, \theta_1+\theta_2+\theta_3=\pi+A>\pi.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement compares the area of a triangle bounded by great-circle arcs on a unit sphere with its three angles. Its quantities and equality are accessible through elementary spatial geometry. This rating concerns understanding the statement, independently of the difficulty of its proof.

Proof 1: Angle excess of a triangle on the unit sphere — proof

The geodesic-triangle angle formula says that the sum of interior angles of a geodesic triangle is equal to \pi plus the total curvature enclosed by the triangle:

\theta_1+\theta_2+\theta_3=\pi+\int_T K\,dA.

On the standard sphere, where the curvature is everywhere 1, we see that the angle sum of geodesic triangles is always bigger than \pi; the curvature integral is the area A of the triangle.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the geodesic-triangle curvature integral formula and the unit sphere curvature value. The proof invokes intrinsic Gaussian curvature and surface integration, requiring differential geometry despite the final area substitution.
