Statement

Let S be a real symmetric n\times n matrix and choose an orthogonal eigendecomposition S=QEQ^{\mathsf T}, where E=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n). Put D=\operatorname{diag}(\operatorname{sgn}\lambda_i), W=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{|\lambda_i|}), and R=QW. Then S=RDR^{\mathsf T}. The factor R may be singular when some eigenvalue is zero.

Proof 1: Diagonal sign factorization

Any symmetric real matrix S has an eigendecomposition of the form QEQ^{\mathsf T}, where E is a diagonal matrix containing the eigenvalues of S, and Q is an orthogonal square matrix containing the eigenvectors. The matrix E can be written E=WDW^{\mathsf T}, where D is diagonal with entries 0,+1,-1, and W is diagonal with each W_{i,i}=\sqrt{|E_{i,i}|}. The matrix R=QW transforms D to S, since

S=QEQ^{\mathsf T}=QW\,D\,W^{\mathsf T}Q^{\mathsf T} =RDR^{\mathsf T}.

Level justification

Orthogonally diagonalize and split each eigenvalue into its sign times the square of its absolute square root. Undergraduate matrix spectral theory suffices.
