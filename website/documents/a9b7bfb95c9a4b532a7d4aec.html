First-order axioms admitting unboundedly large finite models or any infinite model also admit models of arbitrarily large size. This exposes limits on how completely first-order descriptions can determine the size of an infinite mathematical universe.

Statement

Let T be a first-order theory having either arbitrarily large finite models or an infinite model. For every cardinal \kappa, T has a model of cardinality at least \kappa.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement quantifies over models of a first-order theory and arbitrary infinite cardinal bounds; no language expansion is required to understand it.

Proof 1: Add a cardinal’s worth of distinct constants

Let T be the initial theory and let \kappa be any cardinal number. Add to the language of T one constant symbol for every element of \kappa. Then add to T a collection of sentences that say that the objects denoted by any two distinct constant symbols from the new collection are distinct. Since every finite subset of this new theory is satisfiable by a sufficiently large finite model of T, or by any infinite model, the entire extended theory is satisfiable by the compactness theorem. But any model of the extended theory has cardinality at least \kappa. Its reduct to the original language is a model of T.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Retain Beyond undergraduate provisionally. In addition to first-order structures, satisfaction, expanded languages and reducts, this version indexes the new constants by an arbitrary cardinal and compares model sizes. The finite-satisfiability argument is short, but the cumulative logic and cardinal framework is not confidently a thirty-minute extension of the stated baseline. The proof of compactness itself is not required.
