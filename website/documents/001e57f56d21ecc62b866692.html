Statement

Let E be a real vector space, F\subseteq E a vector subspace and K\subseteq E a convex cone, closed under addition and nonnegative scalar multiplication. Suppose E=K+F. If a linear functional \phi:F\to\mathbb R is nonnegative on F\cap K, then it has a linear extension \psi:E\to\mathbb R nonnegative on K.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra supplies subspaces and linear functionals. The cone and positivity conditions are explicitly defined by addition, scalar multiplication and inequalities, a direct extension without topological duality.

Proof 1: Choose an admissible value in one new direction — outline

By transfinite induction or Zorn's lemma it is sufficient to consider the case dim {\displaystyle E/F=1}.

Choose any {\displaystyle y\in E\setminus F}. Set

{\displaystyle a=\sup\{\,\phi (x)\mid x\in F,\ y-x\in K\,\},\ b=\inf\{\,\phi (x)\mid x\in F,x-y\in K\,\}.}

We will prove below that {\displaystyle -\infty <a\leq b<\infty}. For now, choose any {\displaystyle c} satisfying {\displaystyle a\leq c\leq b}, and set {\displaystyle \psi (y)=c}, {\displaystyle \psi |_{F}=\phi }, and then extend {\displaystyle \psi } to all of {\displaystyle E} by linearity. We need to show that {\displaystyle \psi } is {\displaystyle K}-positive. Suppose {\displaystyle z\in K}. If z\in F, then \psi(z)=\phi(z)\ge0. Otherwise, either {\displaystyle z=p(x+y)} or {\displaystyle z=p(x-y)} for some {\displaystyle p>0} and {\displaystyle x\in F}. In the first remaining case {\displaystyle x+y=y-(-x)\in K}, and so

{\displaystyle \psi (y)=c\geq a\geq \phi (-x)=\psi (-x)}

by definition. Thus

{\displaystyle \psi (z)=p\psi (x+y)=p(\psi (x)+\psi (y))\geq 0.}

In the second case, {\displaystyle x-y\in K}, and so similarly

{\displaystyle \psi (y)=c\leq b\leq \phi (x)=\psi (x)}

by definition and so

{\displaystyle \psi (z)=p\psi (x-y)=p(\psi (x)-\psi (y))\geq 0.}

In all cases, {\displaystyle \psi (z)\geq0}, and so {\displaystyle \psi } is {\displaystyle K}-positive.

We now prove that {\displaystyle -\infty <a\leq b<\infty}. Notice by assumption there exists at least one {\displaystyle x\in F} for which {\displaystyle y-x\in K}, and so {\displaystyle -\infty <a}. Applying E=K+F to -y likewise supplies x'\in F with x'-y\in K, so the upper constraint set is nonempty. To prove the inequality, it suffices to show that whenever {\displaystyle x\in F} and {\displaystyle y-x\in K}, and {\displaystyle x'\in F} and {\displaystyle x'-y\in K}, then {\displaystyle \phi (x)\leq \phi (x')}. Indeed,

{\displaystyle x'-x=(x'-y)+(y-x)\in K}

since {\displaystyle K} is a convex cone, and so

{\displaystyle 0\leq \phi (x'-x)=\phi (x')-\phi (x)}

since {\displaystyle \phi } is {\displaystyle K}-positive.

Both constraint sets are therefore nonempty, and every lower constraint is at most every upper constraint. Consequently a and b are finite with a\le b.

For the Zorn reduction, unions along chains remain positive extensions, and E=K+F persists when F is enlarged.
