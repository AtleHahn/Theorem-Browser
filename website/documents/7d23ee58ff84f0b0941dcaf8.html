Statement

Assume the ultrafilter lemma: every proper filter on a set extends to an ultrafilter. For any family (X_i)_{i\in I} of compact Hausdorff spaces, the product \prod_iX_i with the product topology is compact Hausdorff.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Basic topology and sets supply compactness, families and intersections. A filter/ultrafilter is a family satisfying the listed closure/maximality conditions, with its extension principle assumed. Here these set-family definitions require no ultraproduct or model-theoretic semantics.

Proof 1: Unique coordinate limits remove the second choice — outline

Assuming the ultrafilter lemma, a space is compact if and only if each ultrafilter on the space converges. If the product is empty, it is compact. Otherwise, with this criterion in hand, the proof becomes easy: the (filter generated by the) image of an ultrafilter on the product space under any projection map is an ultrafilter on the factor space, which therefore converges, to at least one x_i. One then shows that the original ultrafilter converges to x=(x_i).

Since each factor X_i is Hausdorff, each limit point x_i is unique and so choosing these limit points requires no further axiom of choice. The product of Hausdorff spaces is Hausdorff. The finite-coordinate convergence and separation checks are left implicit in the source.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Project ultrafilters to factors, use compactness and uniqueness of filter limits, then recover product convergence. Proper and maximal filters, pushforward filters and convergence form a cumulative extension not confidently within the allowance.
