Statement

Assume \mathsf{NP}\subseteq\mathsf{P/poly}. Then \mathsf{PH}=S_2^P. Here S_2^P consists of languages admitting a polynomial-time predicate R(z,u,v) with polynomial bounds such that z\in L implies \exists u\forall v\,R(z,u,v), and z\notin L implies \exists v\forall u\,\neg R(z,u,v). Here \mathsf{P/poly} is the class of languages with polynomial-size Boolean circuit families; \Sigma_2^P and \Pi_2^P use respectively existential–universal and universal–existential polynomially bounded quantifiers over polynomial-time predicates, and \mathsf{PH} is the polynomial hierarchy.

Proof 1: A witness circuit or a single universal counterexample

For a \Pi_2^P language with predicate \forall x\exists y\,\phi(x,y,z), the witness-circuit construction gives a polynomial-size D such that a true instance satisfies \phi(x,D(x,z),z) for every x. If the instance is false, one x admits no valid witness and defeats every circuit D. These are exactly the two symmetric-alternation conditions with u=D, v=x. Thus \Pi_2^P\subseteq S_2^P. The ordinary Karp–Lipton collapse gives \mathsf{PH}=\Pi_2^P=\Sigma_2^P, and S_2^P\subseteq\Sigma_2^P by its definition. Hence the classes coincide.
