Statement

Let d\ge2 be an integer. For j=1,\ldots,d, let \pi_j:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^{d-1} delete coordinate j, and let g_j:\mathbb R^{d-1}\to[0,\infty) be measurable with \int g_j^{d-1}<\infty. Then

\int_{\mathbb R^d}\prod_{j=1}^d g_j(\pi_j(x))\,dx\le\prod_{j=1}^d\left(\int_{\mathbb R^{d-1}}g_j(y)^{d-1}\,dy\right)^{1/(d-1)}.

Equivalently, for nonnegative integrable functions f_j on \mathbb R^{d-1},

\int_{\mathbb R^d}\prod_{j=1}^d f_j(\pi_j(x))^{1/(d-1)}\,dx\le\prod_{j=1}^d\left(\int_{\mathbb R^{d-1}}f_j(y)\,dy\right)^{1/(d-1)}.

All integrals use Lebesgue measure; the equivalent forms correspond to f_j=g_j^{d-1}.

General measurable functions and Lebesgue integrability in varying dimensions require measure-theoretic background beyond elementary multiple integrals; cumulative prerequisites exceed the conservative allowance.
