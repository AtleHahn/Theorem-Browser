Statement

Let n\geq1 and let A_1,\ldots,A_n be nonempty finite subsets of \mathbb Z^n. Put \Delta_i=\operatorname{conv}(A_i). For each i, choose a Laurent polynomial

f_i(x)=\sum_{\alpha\in A_i}c_{i,\alpha}x^\alpha,\qquad c_{i,\alpha}\in\mathbb C,\qquad x^\alpha=\prod_{j=1}^n x_j^{\alpha_j}.

For generic choices of the coefficients, the number of common solutions of f_1(x)=\cdots=f_n(x)=0 in (\mathbb C\setminus\{0\})^n is

n!V(\Delta_1,\ldots,\Delta_n).

Here generic means outside a proper algebraic subset of the joint coefficient space. The mixed volume is normalized so that n!V(\Delta_1,\ldots,\Delta_n) is the coefficient of t_1\cdots t_n in \operatorname{vol}_n(t_1\Delta_1+\cdots+t_n\Delta_n), for t_i\geq0; the sum is the Minkowski sum of the scaled polytopes and \operatorname{vol}_n is ordinary Euclidean volume.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Polynomial algebra and convex hulls are accessible, but joint genericity in coefficient varieties and mixed-volume polynomiality together form a borderline conceptual extension. Without assuming algebraic-geometry or convex-geometry electives, the complete claim is conservatively beyond undergraduate.

A link to the proof can be found here (Chapter VII, Sections 3–5: Theorem VII.15, Corollary VII.18, Proposition VII.19; Section 5.2 and Claim VII.28 (PDF p.137).).

The source uses mixed volume equal to the coefficient of t_1\cdots t_n, hence to the displayed n!V. Over \mathbb C, generic coefficients also make the isolated roots simple, so the multiplicity count is the number of distinct solutions. The zero-mixed-volume case gives no solutions for generic coefficients.
