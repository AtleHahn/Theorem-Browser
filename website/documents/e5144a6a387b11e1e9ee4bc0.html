Statement

Let n\ge1, let X_1,\ldots,X_n be independent Bernoulli random variables with success probabilities p_i\in[0,1], and put Z=\sum_i X_i and \bar p=n^{-1}\sum_i p_i. Then \operatorname{Var}(Z)\le n\bar p(1-\bar p).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The variables are finite Bernoulli trials or their binomial sums. Independence, expectation and variance are accessible through finite weighted sums, while the displayed comparison is algebraic. No infinite probability-space construction is needed to understand the statement.

Proof 1: Subtract the variance of the success probabilities

If the individual success probabilities are known, then the variance is known to take the form

{\displaystyle \operatorname {Var} (Z)=n{\bar {p}}(1-{\bar {p}})-ns^{2},}

where {\displaystyle {\bar {p}}} is the average probability and {\displaystyle s^{2}={\frac {1}{n}}\sum _{i=1}^{n}(p_{i}-{\bar {p}})^{2}}. This expression also implies that the variance is always less than or equal to that of the binomial distribution with {\displaystyle p={\bar {p}}}, because the standard expression for the variance is decreased by ns^2, a nonnegative number.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The final inequality follows immediately by discarding the nonnegative sum of squared deviations in the supplied identity. Understanding the quantities uses variance of a random sum and independent Bernoulli variables, standard introductory undergraduate probability; no asymptotic or measure-theoretic theory is needed. The deficit s^2 is explicitly an ordinary finite average of squared probability deviations.
