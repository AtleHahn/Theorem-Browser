Statement

Let G be a group with identity e acting on a set X from the left, so ex=x and g(hx)=(gh)x. The maps \alpha_g(x)=gx are bijections with inverses \alpha_{g^{-1}}, and g\mapsto\alpha_g is a homomorphism to \operatorname{Sym}(X). Conversely each such homomorphism defines an action by evaluation.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory group theory supplies subgroups, quotients, actions, products and homomorphisms. Perfect means generated by commutators, torsion means finite element orders, and the chain conditions are explicit sequences of subgroups. The relevant one of these closely related definitions is a small extension, without representation theory.

Proof 1: Actions and permutation homomorphisms

The identity and compatibility relations read

{\displaystyle \alpha _{e}(x)=x}

and

{\displaystyle \alpha _{g}(\alpha _{h}(x))=(\alpha _{g}\circ \alpha _{h})(x)=\alpha _{gh}(x)}

The second axiom states that the function composition is compatible with the group multiplication; they form a commutative diagram. This axiom can be shortened even further, and written as {\displaystyle \alpha _{g}\circ \alpha _{h}=\alpha _{gh}}.

From these two axioms, it follows that for any fixed {\displaystyle g} in {\displaystyle G}, the function from {\displaystyle X} to itself which maps {\displaystyle x} to {\displaystyle g\cdot x} is a bijection, with inverse bijection the corresponding map for {\displaystyle g^{-1}}. Therefore, one may equivalently define a group action of {\displaystyle G} on {\displaystyle X} as a group homomorphism from {\displaystyle G} into the symmetric group {\displaystyle \operatorname {Sym} (X)} of all bijections from {\displaystyle X} to itself.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Read the action axioms as composition identities and use the inverse group element to obtain inverse permutations; evaluation gives the converse. Undergraduate groups and functions suffice.
