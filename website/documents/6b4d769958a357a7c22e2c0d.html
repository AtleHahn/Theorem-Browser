Statement

Let (K_x)_{x\in X} be nonempty compact Hausdorff spaces, put U=\bigcup_xK_x, and let R\subseteq U\times U with R\cap(K_x\times K_y) closed for every x,y. Suppose that for every finite S\subseteq X there exists a choice function c\in\prod_xK_x satisfying (c(x),c(y))\in R for all x,y\in S. Then such a choice function exists with that consistency condition for all x,y\in X.

Proof 1: Closed consistency sets in a compact product

Let P=\prod_{x\in X}K_x, which is nonempty and compact (also Hausdorff) by Tychonoff's theorem. Nonemptiness also follows from the choice function assumed for S=\varnothing. Consider the family P^S parametrized by finite subsets S\subset X and given by

P^S=\text{the set of all }c\in P\text{ that are }R\text{-consistent on }S.

Here R-consistent means that (c(x),c(y))\in R for every x,y\in S. Each P^S is the intersection of

p_{x,y}^{-1}\bigl(R\cap(K_x\times K_y)\bigr)

over x,y\in S, where p_{x,y}(c)=(p_x(c),p_y(c)) and p_x(c)=c(x). Thus, it is closed and nonempty by assumption. These closed sets have the finite intersection property: P^{S_1\cup\cdots\cup S_n} is a nonempty subset of P^{S_1}\cap\cdots\cap P^{S_n}. By compactness, the intersection \bigcap_S P^S is nonempty and any element of it (which we can pick without the axiom of choice) is a required choice function.

Level justification

In the compact product, each finite consistency condition is a closed set. Finite unions of index sets establish the finite intersection property, giving a globally consistent point. This is standard product topology and compactness.
