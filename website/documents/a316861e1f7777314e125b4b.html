Statement

Let n,t\ge1 be integers and S_1,\ldots,S_m\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}, with each element in at most t sets. There is a vector x\in[-1,1]^n with at least one coordinate equal to 1 or -1 and with \sum_{i\in S_j}x_i=0 for every j such that |S_j|>t.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All objects are finite and explicitly defined; real interval coordinates are elementary.

Proof 1: Count constraints and normalize a null vector

Consider only sets with {\displaystyle \vert S_{j}\vert >t}. Since each element occurs in at most t of the sets, there are less than {\displaystyle n} such sets. Now, enforce linear constraints {\displaystyle \sum _{i\in S_{j}}x_{i}=0} for them. Since it is a non-trivial linear subspace of {\displaystyle \mathbf {R} ^{n}} with fewer constraints than variables, there is a non-zero solution. Normalize this solution so that its largest absolute coordinate is 1, and at least one of the values is either {\displaystyle +1,-1}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Count element-set incidences: there are at most nt, while each retained set contributes more than t, so fewer than n constraints remain. Undergraduate rank-nullity gives a nonzero solution, and division by its largest absolute coordinate puts it in the cube with one saturated coordinate. This is finite counting and linear algebra, without discrepancy-theory prerequisites.
