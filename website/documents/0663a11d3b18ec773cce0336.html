Statement

For the symmetric group \Sigma_n on n letters, with integral coefficients, H_1(\Sigma_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z for n\ge2, while H_1(\Sigma_1;\mathbb Z)=0.

Proof 1: Abelianization via permutation sign

The first homology of a group is the largest commutative quotient of that group. For the permutation groups \Sigma_n, n\ge2, the largest commutative quotient is given by the sign of a permutation, taking values in \{-1,1\}. This shows that H_1(\Sigma_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z, the cyclic group of order 2, for all n\ge2. (For n=1, \Sigma_1 is the trivial group, so H_1(\Sigma_1;\mathbb Z)=0.)

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Identify first group homology with abelianization and then use the permutation sign quotient. The quotient calculation is introductory algebra, but understanding group homology is an additional theory not supplied by its degree-one identification alone.
