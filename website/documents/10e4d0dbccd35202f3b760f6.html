Statement

The order of a nonabelian finite simple group is divisible by at least three distinct primes.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Finite groups, normal subgroups and simplicity are introductory algebra. Perfect means equal to the subgroup generated by commutators, one short group-theoretic definition. No classification or character theory is needed to understand these claims.

Proof 1: Apply Burnside’s solvability theorem — outline

Burnside's theorem states that if G is a finite group of order p^aq^b, where p and q are prime numbers and a and b are nonnegative integers, then G is solvable. Hence each nonabelian finite simple group has order divisible by at least three distinct primes.

Here the standard fact that a nonabelian simple group is not solvable is used without proof; the source states the consequence immediately.

Level justification
