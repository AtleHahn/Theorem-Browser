Statement

Let D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\} and let h:D\to D be holomorphic and injective with h(0)=0. For p>0, put \|f\|_{H^p}^p=\sup_{0<r<1}(2\pi)^{-1}\int_0^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^p\,d\theta and let H^p(D) consist of holomorphic functions for which this is finite. For 1\le p<\infty and f\in H^p(D), \|f\circ h\|_{H^p}\le\|f\|_{H^p}.

Proof 1: Apply H² subordination to a power of the outer factor

Use Hardy inner–outer factorization f=f_i f_o, where |f_i|\le1, \|f_o\|_{H^p}=\|f\|_{H^p}, and the zero-free outer factor has a holomorphic power g=f_o^{p/2}\in H^2 satisfying |g|^2=|f_o|^p. (The zero function is immediate.) Then \|f\circ h\|_{H^p}\le\|f_o\circ h\|_{H^p}=\|g\circ h\|_{H^2}^{2/p}\le\|g\|_{H^2}^{2/p}=\|f\|_{H^p}. The middle inequality is the H2 subordination theorem.

Level justification

Factor into inner and outer parts and pass to a holomorphic outer power in H2 before applying subordination. Hardy factorization and holomorphic power norm identities require graduate complex analysis.
