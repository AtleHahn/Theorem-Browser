A proposition is encoded by equality of two subsets of a doubleton

Statement

Work in constructive set theory with pairing, extensionality and separation for the formulas used below, and with the distinct finite ordinals 0=\varnothing, 1=\{0\} and 2=\{0,1\}. For a proposition P allowed in separation, put

U=\{x\in2:x=0\lor P\},\qquad V=\{x\in2:x=1\lor P\}.

Then P holds if and only if U=V.

Proof 1: Use extensionality and the distinguishing element — outline

If P holds, the definitions and extensionality give U=V=2. Conversely, if U=V, then 0\in U implies 0\in V, whose defining predicate gives 0=1\lor P. Since 0\ne1, disjunctive syllogism yields P.

Level justification

Under P both defined subsets equal the doubleton. Conversely equality puts zero in V, and zero unequal to one forces P. Only the stated membership predicates and elementary constructive implications are used; no general choice or decidability theorem is needed.
