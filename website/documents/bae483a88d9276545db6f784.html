Statement

Let k be an algebraically closed field, and let C_1,C_2\subset\mathbb P^2(k) be plane cubic curves whose intersection consists of exactly nine distinct points P_1,\ldots,P_9. Every plane cubic curve passing through any eight of these points also passes through the remaining point.

Here \mathbb P^2(k) consists of nonzero triples (x,y,z)\in k^3 modulo multiplication by a nonzero scalar. A plane cubic is the zero locus of a nonzero homogeneous polynomial of degree three; reducible cubics are allowed. The intersection assumption excludes a common curve component.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

With introductory algebra and linear algebra, homogeneous coordinates and zero loci are a small explicitly defined extension. No schemes, multiplicity theory or intersection theory is needed to understand the nine-distinct-point statement.

Proof availability

Source argument and limitations

The source's route is to show that the vector space of cubic equations vanishing at eight points has dimension two. If the equations of C_1 and C_2 span that space, every such equation is their linear combination and therefore vanishes also at P_9.

The dimension-two fact is stated rather than proved. The article discusses collinear and co-conic configurations using Bézout's theorem, but does not give a complete treatment of the conditions needed for its more general “intrinsic” formulation. The paragraph above records the supplied deduction and its unproved dimension premise; it is not a self-contained proof of the full statement. The nine-distinct-intersection formulation is the source's explicit original theorem.
