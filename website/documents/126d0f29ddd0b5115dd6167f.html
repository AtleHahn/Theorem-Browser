Statement

For every integer m\ge3 there exists a set of 2m noncollinear points in the Euclidean plane determining exactly m ordinary lines. An ordinary line is a line containing exactly two points of the set.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The claim describes a finite Euclidean configuration and counts lines containing exactly two points.

Mathematical Field
Convex and discrete geometry
Required topics
Finite Euclidean point setsOrdinary linesEven integer cardinality
Review notes
none

Proof 1: Regular polygon with its direction points (outline)

The construction, due to Károly Böröczky, consists of the vertices of a regular m-gon in the real projective plane and another m points (thus, n=2m) on the line at infinity corresponding to each of the directions determined by pairs of vertices. Although there are m(m-1)/2 pairs of these points, they determine only m distinct directions. This arrangement has only m ordinary lines, the lines that connect a vertex v with the point at infinity collinear with the two neighbors of v. As with any finite configuration in the real projective plane, a projective change of coordinates can make all points finite, without changing the number of ordinary lines.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The construction uses the projective plane only as homogeneous coordinates with one line at infinity, followed by a change of affine chart. This is a manageable extension of undergraduate linear algebra and Euclidean geometry. For a regular polygon, a chord direction is determined by the sum of its endpoint indices modulo m; from each vertex exactly one direction has no other vertex on that line, giving m ordinary lines. No algebraic geometry is needed.
