Statement

Let X be a compact Kähler manifold. For an integral cohomology class \alpha\in H^2(X,\mathbb Z), write \alpha_{\mathbb C} for its image in H^2(X,\mathbb C). Under the Hodge decomposition H^2(X,\mathbb C)=H^{0,2}(X)\oplus H^{1,1}(X)\oplus H^{2,0}(X), the image of the first Chern class map from holomorphic line bundles consists exactly of the classes \alpha with \alpha_{\mathbb C}\in H^{1,1}(X).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Hodge decomposition, integral cohomology and Chern classes of holomorphic line bundles require substantial Kähler geometry. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Exponential sheaf sequence

Use the standard fact that first Chern classes of holomorphic line bundles have complexifications of type (1,1).

Because X is a complex manifold, it admits an exponential sheaf sequence

{\displaystyle 0\to {\underline {\mathbf {Z} }}{\stackrel {2\pi i}{\longrightarrow }}{\mathcal {O}}_{X}{\stackrel {\operatorname {exp} }{\longrightarrow }}{\mathcal {O}}_{X}^{\times }\to 0.}

Taking sheaf cohomology of this exact sequence gives maps exact at the middle term

{\displaystyle H^{1}(X,{\mathcal {O}}_{X}^{\times }){\stackrel {c_{1}}{\to }}H^{2}(X,\mathbf {Z} ){\stackrel {i_{*}}{\to }}H^{2}(X,{\mathcal {O}}_{X}).}

The group Pic X of line bundles on X is isomorphic to {\displaystyle H^{1}(X,{\mathcal {O}}_{X}^{\times })}. The first Chern class map is c1 by definition, so it suffices to show that {\displaystyle i_{*}} is zero on the integral classes \alpha for which \alpha_{\mathbb C}\in H^{1,1}(X).

Because X is Kähler, Hodge theory implies that {\displaystyle H^{2}(X,{\mathcal {O}}_{X})\cong H^{0,2}(X)}. However, {\displaystyle i_{*}} factors through the map from H2(X, Z) to H2(X, C), and on H2(X, C), {\displaystyle i_{*}} is 2\pi i times the projection onto H0,2(X). It follows that it is zero on the integral classes \alpha for which \alpha_{\mathbb C}\in H^{1,1}(X), and consequently that every such integral class is the first Chern class of a holomorphic line bundle.
