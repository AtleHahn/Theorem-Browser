Statement

Let A,B be distinct points on a nondegenerate parabola. Let C be the intersection of the line through A parallel to the axis with the tangent at B. The area of triangle ABC is three times the area between the parabola and chord AB.

Proof 1: Balance chord sections on a reflected median — outline

Let D be the midpoint of AC. Construct a line segment JB through D, with J on the opposite side of D from B, where the distance from J to D is equal to the distance from B to D. We will think of the segment JB as a "lever" with D as its fulcrum. As Archimedes had previously shown, the center of mass of the triangle is at the point I on the "lever" where DI :DB = 1:3. Therefore, it suffices to show that if the whole weight of the interior of the triangle rests at I, and the whole weight of the section of the parabola at J, the lever is in equilibrium.

Consider an infinitely small interior cross-section of the triangle given by the segment HE, where the point H lies on BC, the point E lies on AB, and HE is parallel to the axis of symmetry of the parabola. Call the intersection of HE and the parabola F and the intersection of HE and the lever G. If the weight of all such segments HE rest at the points G where they intersect the lever, then they exert the same torque on the lever as does the whole weight of the triangle resting at I. Thus, we wish to show that if the weight of the cross-section HE rests at G and the weight of the cross-section EF of the section of the parabola rests at J, then the lever is in equilibrium. In other words, it suffices to show that EF :GD = EH :JD. But that is a routine consequence of the equation of the parabola. Q.E.D.

Level

Level justification

The slice-length comparison and centroid ratio use elementary coordinate geometry, but passing from infinitesimal slices to total weights and torques is integration of first moments. This is undergraduate calculus, even when expressed in the historical language of a lever. For y=x^2 with A=(0,0), B=(1,1), the triangle slice has length 1-t and the parabolic slice t(1-t); their lever distances are in ratio t, verifying the local balance.

2. Triangle centroid lies one-third of the way from the side midpoint to the opposite vertex
Kind:

Theorem

Treatment:

Statement used:

The centroid of a uniform triangle lies on each median, one third of the median length from the side midpoint toward the opposite vertex.

Application in proof:

Use DI=DB/3 and JD=DB to turn torque equality into the factor-three area relation.
