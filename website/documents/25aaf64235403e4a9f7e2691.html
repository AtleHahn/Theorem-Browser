Statement

Let \mathfrak g be a finite-dimensional Lie algebra over a field of characteristic zero. A Lie algebra is a vector space with an alternating bilinear bracket satisfying [x,[y,z]]+[y,[z,x]]+[z,[x,y]]=0 for all x,y,z. Brackets of subspaces denote the linear span of all brackets of their elements. An ideal I satisfies [\mathfrak g,I]\subseteq I. It is solvable if its derived series I^{(0)}=I, I^{(j+1)}=[I^{(j)},I^{(j)}] reaches zero. Write \mathfrak r for the radical, the largest solvable ideal. A Lie algebra is semisimple if its radical is zero. Let \mathfrak s_1,\mathfrak s_2 be semisimple subalgebras with \mathfrak g=\mathfrak r\oplus\mathfrak s_i as vector spaces. There exists z in the nilradical of \mathfrak g such that

\exp(\operatorname{ad}z)(\mathfrak s_1)=\mathfrak s_2.

The nilradical is the largest nilpotent ideal; an ideal N is nilpotent if the series N_1=N, N_{j+1}=[N,N_j] reaches zero. Here \operatorname{ad}z(x)=[z,x], and its exponential is the finite sum \sum_{k\ge0}(\operatorname{ad}z)^k/k!, since \operatorname{ad}z is nilpotent. This exponential is an inner automorphism of \mathfrak g.
