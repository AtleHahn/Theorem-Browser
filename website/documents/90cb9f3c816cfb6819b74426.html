Statement

Let H be a complex Hilbert space of complex-valued functions on a set X, with pointwise operations and inner product linear in the first argument. If every point evaluation is bounded, then for each x\in X there is a unique K_x\in H satisfying f(x)=\langle f,K_x\rangle for every f\in H.

Level justification

Hilbert and Banach spaces add completeness for the specified norm. The displayed forms, evaluations and inequalities use these direct extensions without weak topologies, biduals or spectral theory.

Proof 1: Apply complex Hilbert-space Riesz representation

The evaluation functional over the Hilbert space of functions H is a linear functional that evaluates each function at a point x,

L_x:f\longmapsto f(x),\qquad f\in H.

By hypothesis, L_x is a bounded operator on H: there exists some M_x>0 such that

|L_x(f)|=|f(x)|\leq M_x\|f\|_H\qquad\text{for all }f\in H.

The Riesz representation theorem implies that for all x in X there exists a unique element K_x of H with the reproducing property,

f(x)=L_x(f)=\langle f,K_x\rangle_H \qquad\text{for all }f\in H.
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof applies the precisely stated Hilbert-space Riesz theorem to the bounded evaluation functional. Linear algebra, norm bounds and Hilbert completeness suffice to understand this application, a small extension of undergraduate analysis. The proof of Riesz representation is not a prerequisite; no dual topology or spectral theory occurs.
