Statement

Let \Gamma be the gamma function: the meromorphic continuation to \mathbb C of

\Gamma(z)=\int_0^\infty t^{z-1}e^{-t}\,dt\qquad(\operatorname{Re}z>0),

where t^{z-1}=\exp((z-1)\log t) for t>0. For every integer n\ge0, there is no nonzero polynomial P\in\mathbb C[X,Y_0,\ldots,Y_n] such that

P\bigl(z,\Gamma(z),\Gamma'(z),\ldots,\Gamma^{(n)}(z)\bigr)=0 \quad\text{for every }z\in\mathbb C\setminus\{0,-1,-2,\ldots\}.

Thus the gamma function satisfies no nontrivial algebraic differential equation with rational-function coefficients.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The polynomial identity is elementary in form, but defining the full complex gamma function requires meromorphic continuation and complex differentiation, beyond the assumed introductory real-analysis background and a confidently small reading extension.

Proof 1: Difference-Galois criterion and a rational pole obstruction (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Section7.1, Proposition7.3, PDF p.14; Proposition6.1 and Corollaries6.7–6.9).

In Corollaries7.1–7.2 the coefficients \lambda_j must be not all zero, as in Corollary6.7. Here is the pole check in detail. A nonzero combination \sum_j\lambda_j(-1)^j j!/z^{j+1} has exactly one pole, at zero. If a rational g has no finite poles, g(z+1)-g(z) is polynomial. Otherwise, in any nonempty finite orbit of poles modulo integer translation, its two extreme poles yield distinct uncancelled poles of g(z+1)-g(z). Thus this difference cannot equal that combination. In the coefficient descent of Corollary7.1, include equations s\lambda_j=1 and tB_l=1 for chosen nonzero coefficients of the combination and of the denominator; this prevents a zero combination or zero denominator after descent. Finally, clearing rational denominators identifies differential transcendence with the polynomial formulation above.
