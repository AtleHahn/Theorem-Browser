Statement

Let G be a finite group, g\in G\setminus\{1\}, and q a prime. There is a nontrivial irreducible complex character \chi with \chi(g)\ne0 and q\nmid\chi(1).

Level

Level justification

Complex characters and irreducible representations are beyond calculus and linear algebra.

Proof 1: Column orthogonality and rational nonintegrality

Let (\chi_i)_{1\le i\le h} be the family of irreducible characters of G over \mathbb C; here \chi_1 denotes the trivial character. Because g is not in the same conjugacy class as 1, the orthogonality relation for the columns of the group's character table gives

0=\sum_{i=1}^h\chi_i(1)\chi_i(g) =1+\sum_{i=2}^h\chi_i(1)\chi_i(g).

Now the \chi_i(g) are algebraic integers, because they are sums of roots of unity. If all the nontrivial irreducible characters which do not vanish at g take a value divisible by q at 1, we deduce that

-\frac1q =\sum_{i\ge2,\ \chi_i(g)\ne0}\frac{\chi_i(1)}q\chi_i(g)

is an algebraic integer, since it is a sum of integer multiples of algebraic integers. This is absurd, because a rational algebraic integer must be an integer. This proves the statement.
