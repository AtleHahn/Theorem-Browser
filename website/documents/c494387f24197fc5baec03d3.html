Statement

For every finite graph H there is an integer k>0 such that every finite graph having no minor isomorphic to H can be assembled by the following operations. A minor is obtained from a subgraph by contracting edges.

Start with a finite list of graphs embedded in compact connected surfaces on which H cannot be embedded. A surface here is a compact connected topological two-dimensional manifold without boundary. An embedding draws edges as arcs meeting only at their common endpoints. To each embedded graph add at most k vortices, each of depth at most k, as defined below. Add at most k further vertices, called apices, and arbitrary edges each incident to at least one apex. Finally, join the resulting graphs by successive k-clique-sums. Empty embedded parts are permitted; in particular, when H is planar the pieces consist only of the bounded sets of apices.

Here a k-clique-sum selects cliques of the same size m\leq k in two graphs, identifies corresponding vertices, and optionally deletes edges within the identified clique. A clique is a set of pairwise adjacent vertices.

A vortex of depth at most k on a face F is the following construction. A face is an open disk complementary to the embedded graph. List its boundary vertices in cyclic order, and choose a finite list \Lambda of circular intervals of that list (consecutive vertices, allowing wraparound). For every L\in\Lambda add a new vertex v_L and any selection of edges from v_L to vertices of L. One may also add edges v_Lv_M when L\cap M\ne\varnothing. Each boundary vertex must belong to at most k intervals of \Lambda.
