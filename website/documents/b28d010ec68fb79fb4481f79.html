Statement

For a variety X/\mathbb F_q, write N_m=|X(\mathbb F_{q^m})| and Z(X,T)=\exp(\sum_{m\geq1}N_m T^m/m) as a formal power series. For X=\mathbb P^n with n\geq1, N_m=\sum_{j=0}^n q^{jm} and Z(X,T)=\prod_{j=0}^n(1-q^jT)^{-1}.

Proof 1: Affine pieces and logarithmic summation outline

The simplest example (other than a point) is to take X to be the projective line. The number of points of X over a field with q^m elements is just N_m=q^m+1 (where the “+1” comes from the “point at infinity”). The zeta function is just

\frac{1}{(1-T)(1-qT)}.

It is not much harder to do n-dimensional projective space. The number of points of X over a field with q^m elements is just

N_m=1+q^m+q^{2m}+\cdots+q^{nm}.

The zeta function is just

\frac{1}{(1-T)(1-qT)\cdots(1-q^nT)}.

The number of points on the projective line and projective space are so easy to calculate because they can be written as disjoint unions of a finite number of copies of affine spaces. The logarithmic summation in the definition of Z(X,T) uses -\log(1-u)=\sum_{m\geq1}u^m/m; the source leaves this calculation implicit.
