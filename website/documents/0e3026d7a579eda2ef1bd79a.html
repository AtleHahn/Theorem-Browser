Statement

Let f\in L^1(\mathbb R^2;\mathbb C) and define p(x)=\int_{\mathbb R}f(x,y)\,dy for almost every x. Use the Fourier convention

\widehat f(k_x,k_y)=\int_{\mathbb R^2}f(x,y)e^{-2\pi i(xk_x+yk_y)}\,dx\,dy.

Then p\in L^1(\mathbb R) and, for every real k, \widehat p(k)=\widehat f(k,0). The same projection/slice statement holds after a common rotation of the spatial and frequency coordinates.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Ordinary integrable-function Fourier transforms and almost-everywhere projections use measure-theoretic integration beyond the calculus/linear-algebra rubric.

Proof 1: Integrate the zero-frequency slice

Without loss of generality, we can take the projection line to be the x-axis. The rotated function is the Fourier pair of the rotated Fourier transform, for which the theorem again holds.

If f(x,y) is a two-dimensional function, then the projection of f(x,y) onto the x axis is p(x), where

p(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x,y)\,dy.

By Fubini, this exists almost everywhere and \int|p(x)|\,dx\leq\int|f(x,y)|\,dx\,dy, so p\in L^1(\mathbb R).

The Fourier transform of f(x,y) is

F(k_x,k_y)=\widehat f(k_x,k_y) =\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x,y)e^{-2\pi i(xk_x+yk_y)}\,dx\,dy.

The slice is then s(k_x):

\begin{aligned} s(k_x)=F(k_x,0) &=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x,y)e^{-2\pi ixk_x}\,dx\,dy\\ &=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left[\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x,y)\,dy\right]e^{-2\pi ixk_x}\,dx\\ &=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}p(x)e^{-2\pi ixk_x}\,dx, \end{aligned}

which is just the Fourier transform of p(x). The interchange of integrals is justified by Fubini and the assumed absolute integrability of f.

Level justification
