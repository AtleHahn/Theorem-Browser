Statement

Let f:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C be continuous and holomorphic on each of \{\operatorname{Im}z>0\} and \{\operatorname{Im}z<0\}. Then f is holomorphic on all of \mathbb C.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex differentiability is a direct extension of calculus. Periods, continuity and the specified planar domains are familiar; no singularities, contours or Riemann surfaces are needed to understand these assertions.

Proof 1: Holomorphic gluing across the real axis — outline

This result can be proved from Morera's theorem. Indeed, a continuous function is holomorphic provided its integral round any contour vanishes; a contour which crosses the real axis can be broken up into contours in the upper and lower half-planes and the integral round these vanishes by hypothesis.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof uses contour decomposition, Cauchy vanishing and Morera to establish holomorphy across a boundary. Together these complex-integration concepts exceed a small extension of ordinary real calculus.
