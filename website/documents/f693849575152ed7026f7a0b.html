Statement

For a convex polygon with distinct vertices on one circle, the sum of the inradii of the triangles in a triangulation by diagonals is independent of the triangulation.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Finite triangulations, circles and triangle inradii use elementary plane geometry.

Proof 1: Diagonal-flip reduction

Use the quadrilateral inradius-sum identity: no matter how one triangulates a cyclic quadrilateral, the sum of inradii of triangles is constant.

Given this quadrilateral case and connectivity of convex-polygon triangulations by diagonal flips, the general case of the cyclic polygon theorem is an immediate corollary. The quadrilateral rule can be applied to quadrilateral components of a general partition of a cyclic polygon, and repeated application of the rule, which "flips" one diagonal, will generate all the possible partitions from any given partition, with each "flip" preserving the sum of the inradii.
