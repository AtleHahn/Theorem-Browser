Statement

Let R be a commutative ring with identity and f,g\in R[x_1,\ldots,x_n]. Write c(f) for the ideal generated by the coefficients of f, and \sqrt I for the radical of an ideal I. Then c(fg)\subseteq c(f)c(g)\subseteq\sqrt{c(fg)}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory polynomial and ring theory supplies degrees, divisibility, ideals and finite-field arithmetic. The radical consists of elements with a power in the ideal, and Krull dimension counts lengths of prime-ideal chains. These limited ideal-based definitions suffice without schemes, localization or field-extension arithmetic.

Proof 1: Content of a product and its radical — proof

Clearly, {\displaystyle c (fg)\subseteq c (f)c (g)}. If {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {p}}} is a prime ideal containing {\displaystyle c (fg)}, then {\displaystyle fg\equiv 0} modulo {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {p}}}. Since {\displaystyle (R/{\mathfrak {p}})[x_{1},\dots ,x_{n}]} is a polynomial ring over an integral domain and thus is an integral domain, this implies either {\displaystyle f\equiv 0} or {\displaystyle g\equiv 0} modulo {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {p}}}. Hence, either {\displaystyle c (f)} or {\displaystyle c (g)} is contained in {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {p}}}. Since {\displaystyle {\sqrt {c (fg)}}} is the intersection of all prime ideals that contain {\displaystyle c (fg)} and the choice of {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {p}}} was arbitrary, {\displaystyle c (f)c (g)\subseteq {\sqrt {c (fg)}}}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Reduce coefficients modulo an arbitrary containing prime ideal, use the domain property and intersect the primes. Undergraduate commutative rings, quotient domains and ideals suffice with radical-as-prime-intersection as a small related extension; no localization or module theory enters.
