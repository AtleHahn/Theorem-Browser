Statement

A uniform planar flow of real velocity U>0 in the positive x direction, modified by a circular cylinder of radius a>0 centered at zero, has complex potential w(z)=U(z+a^2/z) for |z|>a.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A complex potential combines a velocity potential with a stream function, whose derivatives describe planar flow. This linked interpretation extends undergraduate vector calculus and complex differentiation; the cylinder rule is supplied.

Proof 1: Insert the uniform potential into the circle theorem

Use the circle theorem: an initial complex potential f, with singularities outside |z|\le a, is modified to f(z)+\overline{f(a^2/\bar z)} when the circular cylinder is inserted.

Consider a uniform irrotational flow {\displaystyle f(z)=Uz} with velocity {\displaystyle U} flowing in the positive {\displaystyle x} direction and place an infinitely long cylinder of radius {\displaystyle a} in the flow with the center of the cylinder at the origin. Then {\displaystyle f\left({\frac {a^{2}}{\bar {z}}}\right)={\frac {Ua^{2}}{\bar {z}}},\ \Rightarrow \ {\overline {f\left({\frac {a^{2}}{\bar {z}}}\right)}}={\frac {Ua^{2}}{z}}}, hence using circle theorem,

{\displaystyle w(z)=U\left(z+{\frac {a^{2}}{z}}\right)}

represents the complex potential of uniform flow over a cylinder.

Level justification

Insert the explicit linear potential into the stated circle transformation and conjugate the resulting rational expression. Introductory complex analysis and the concrete potential convention from multivariable calculus suffice as a small extension; no fluid PDE theory is used.
