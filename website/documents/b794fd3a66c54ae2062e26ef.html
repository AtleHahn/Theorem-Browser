Statement

If f is twice differentiable in a neighborhood of a real point x and f^{\prime\prime} is continuous there, then \lim_{h\to0}(f(x+h)+f(x-h)-2f(x))/h^2=f^{\prime\prime}(x).

Level justification

Proof 1: Differentiate twice with respect to the increment

One can use l'Hôpital's rule:

\begin{aligned} \lim_{h\to0}\frac{f(x+h)+f(x-h)-2f(x)}{h^2} &=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{f'(x+h)-f'(x-h)}{2h}\\[4pt] &=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{f''(x+h)+f''(x-h)}{2}\\[4pt] &=f''(x). \end{aligned}

Level justification

Apply L Hopital twice with respect to h and use continuity of the second derivative. Undergraduate differential calculus suffices.
