Statement

Let π_{Z[i]}(X) be the number of nonzero prime ideals of Z[i] with norm at most X, where the norm of an ideal a is the cardinality of Z[i]/a. Then π_{Z[i]}(X)∼X/log X as X→∞.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Gaussian integers are concrete complex numbers with integer coordinates. Ring ideals, quotient cardinality, prime elements and congruences are introductory algebra; the norm and primary/quartic conditions are explicit finite arithmetic definitions. No field-extension norm or ideal-class theory is required.

Proof 1: Count split and inert rational primes

For the Gaussian integers, for any prime number p of the form 4n + 1, p factors as a product of two Gaussian primes of norm p. Primes of the form 4n + 3 remain prime, giving a Gaussian prime of norm p2. These give prime ideals of the same respective norms. Apart from the bounded contribution above 2, we should therefore estimate

{\displaystyle 2r(X)+r^{\prime }({\sqrt {X}})}

where r counts primes in the arithmetic progression 4n + 1, and r′ in the arithmetic progression 4n + 3. By the quantitative form of Dirichlet's theorem on primes, each of r(Y) and r′(Y) is asymptotically

{\displaystyle {\frac {Y}{2\log Y}}.}

Therefore, the 2r(X) term dominates, and is asymptotically

{\displaystyle {\frac {X}{\log X}}.}

Level justification
