Statement

Let N_t, 0\leq t\leq T, be a centered real Gaussian process with continuous covariance R, and let S\in L^2([0,T]) be deterministic. Let (\lambda_i,\phi_i) be positive covariance eigenpairs with real orthonormal eigenfunctions. The coefficient integrals are understood in mean square. Put N_i=\int_0^T N_t\phi_i(t)\,dt, S_i=\int_0^T S(t)\phi_i(t)\,dt and X_i=\int_0^T X_t\phi_i(t)\,dt. If X=N under H and X=N+S under K, then X_i=N_i under H and X_i=N_i+S_i under K, where the N_i are independent N(0,\lambda_i) variables.

Proof 1: Project the noise and deterministic signal separately

We can use K–L expansion to decorrelate the noise process and get independent Gaussian observation 'samples'. For a complete orthonormal family of positive covariance eigenfunctions, the K–L expansion of N(t) in mean square is

N(t)=\sum_i N_i\phi_i(t),\qquad 0<t<T,

where N_i=\int_0^T N(t)\phi_i(t)\,dt and the orthonormal eigenfunctions are generated by the kernel R_N(t,s)=R(t,s), i.e., solutions to

\int_0^T R_N(t,s)\phi_i(s)\,ds=\lambda_i\phi_i(t),\qquad\operatorname{var}[N_i]=\lambda_i.

The same coefficient conclusion holds for any orthonormal subfamily. By the Gaussian KL coefficient theorem, N_i are independent Gaussian random variables with mean zero and variance \lambda_i. With S_i=\int_0^T S(t)\phi_i(t)\,dt, linearity gives

X_i=\int_0^T X(t)\phi_i(t)\,dt=N_i

under H and N_i+S_i under K.
