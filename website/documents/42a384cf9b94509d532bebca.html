Statement

Let (X_t)_{t\in[a,b]} be a centered real process on a probability space (\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P), with a<b, X_t\in L^2(\Omega) and continuous covariance K(s,t)=\mathbb E(X_sX_t). Define (Tf)(t)=\int_a^b K(s,t)f(s)\,ds on real L^2([a,b]). Let (\lambda_k,e_k) denote its positive eigenvalues and real orthonormal eigenfunctions, repeated by multiplicity, and put Z_k=\int_a^b X_t e_k(t)\,dt, with the integral understood in L^2(\Omega). Finite lists are allowed. If the process is Gaussian (every finite vector of its values is jointly Gaussian), the coefficients Z_k are jointly Gaussian and mutually independent, with Z_k\sim N(0,\lambda_k).

Proof 1: Gaussian closure and zero cross-covariances

The coefficient integrals are mean-square limits of linear combinations of process values. The KL coefficient covariance identity gives \mathbb E Z_i=0 and \mathbb E(Z_iZ_j)=\lambda_j\delta_{ij}.

Since the limit in the mean of jointly Gaussian random variables is jointly Gaussian, and jointly Gaussian random (centered) variables are independent if and only if they are orthogonal, we can also conclude:

The variables Z_i therefore have a joint Gaussian distribution and are stochastically independent if the original process \{X_t\}_t is Gaussian, with Z_i\sim N(0,\lambda_i).
