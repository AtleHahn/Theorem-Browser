Statement

Define \chi(X)=\sum_{j\ge0}(-1)^j b_j(X), where b_j(X)=\dim_{\mathbb Q}H_j(X;\mathbb Q); these dimensions are finite and vanish for all sufficiently large j. If X and Y are homotopy equivalent and have these finite-type homology groups, then χ(X)=χ(Y).

Beyond undergraduate level

Proof 1: Transfer the Betti numbers through homotopy equivalence

Homology is a topological invariant, and moreover a homotopy invariant: Two topological spaces that are homotopy equivalent have isomorphic homology groups. It follows that the Euler characteristic is also a homotopy invariant.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The argument transfers dimensions through homotopy-induced homology isomorphisms. Understanding the homology invariant itself requires algebraic topology even though the final sum is immediate.
