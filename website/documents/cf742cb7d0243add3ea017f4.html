Statement

Let (\alpha_k)_{k\ge1} be complex numbers and define (\beta_k)_{k\ge0} by the formal power-series identity

\sum_{k=0}^\infty\beta_kz^k=\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^\infty\alpha_kz^k\right).

Thus \beta_0=1, and each coefficient is defined algebraically by the exponential power series. Then

\sum_{k=0}^\infty|\beta_k|^2\le\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^\infty k|\alpha_k|^2\right).

Nonnegative sums are interpreted in [0,\infty], with \exp(+\infty)=+\infty.

Level justification

Undergraduate power-series algebra and convergence suffice; formal coefficients require only finitely many operations at each degree.
