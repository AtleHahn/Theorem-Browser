Every C*-algebra A has an isometric -representation \pi:A\to B(H) on a Hilbert space H. Its image is a C-subalgebra of B(H), so A is isometrically *-isomorphic to a concrete operator algebra.

Proof 1: Detect nonzero elements in the pure-state direct sum

The Gelfand–Naimark representation π is the Hilbert space analogue of the direct sum of representations πf of A where f ranges over the set of pure states of A and πf is the irreducible representation associated to f by the GNS construction. Thus the Gelfand–Naimark representation acts on the Hilbert direct sum of the Hilbert spaces Hf by

\pi(x)(\bigoplus_f\eta_f)=\bigoplus_f\pi_f(x)\eta_f,\qquad (\eta_f)_f\in\bigoplus_f H_f.

π(x) is a bounded linear operator since it is the direct sum of a family of operators, each one having norm ≤ ||x||.

It suffices to show the map π is injective, since for *-morphisms of C*-algebras injective implies isometric. Let x be a non-zero element of A. By the Krein extension theorem for positive linear functionals, there is a state f on A such that f(z) ≥ 0 for all non-negative z in A and f(−x* x) < 0. The Krein–Milman consequence

\sup_{q\in\operatorname{State}(A)}q(x^*x)=\sup_{q\in\operatorname{PureState}(A)}q(x^*x)

then supplies a pure state with positive value on x^*x; replace f by this pure state. Consider the GNS representation πf with cyclic vector ξ. Since

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\|\pi _{f}(x)\xi \|^{2}&=\langle \pi _{f}(x)\xi \mid \pi _{f}(x)\xi \rangle =\langle \xi \mid \pi _{f}(x^{*})\pi _{f}(x)\xi \rangle \\[6pt]&=\langle \xi \mid \pi _{f}(x^{*}x)\xi \rangle =f(x^{*}x)>0,\end{aligned}}}

it follows that πf (x) ≠ 0, so π (x) ≠ 0, so π is injective.

By isometry and completeness, the image is closed; the -representation property makes it a C-subalgebra.
