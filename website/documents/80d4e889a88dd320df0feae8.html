Statement

Let r:U\subseteq\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R^3 be C^2 on an open set U, with linearly independent r_u,r_v. Set E=r_u\cdot r_u,F=r_u\cdot r_v,G=r_v\cdot r_v, and let \mathbf n be a unit normal. Define \Gamma^k_{ij} as the tangential coefficients of the second derivatives in the basis r_u,r_v,\mathbf n. These six coefficients are uniquely determined by E,F,G and their first partial derivatives through the three pairs of linear equations displayed in the proof.

Proof 1: Differentiate dot products and solve three linear systems

Consider the second derivatives of a parametrisation r=r(u,v). These can be expressed in terms of the basis r_u,r_v,\mathbf n and Christoffel symbols \Gamma^k_{ij}:

\begin{aligned} r_{uu}&=\Gamma^1_{11}r_u+\Gamma^2_{11}r_v+L\mathbf n,\\ r_{uv}&=\Gamma^1_{12}r_u+\Gamma^2_{12}r_v+M\mathbf n,\\ r_{vv}&=\Gamma^1_{22}r_u+\Gamma^2_{22}r_v+N\mathbf n. \end{aligned}

Taking the dot product of each equation with \mathbf n shows that L=r_{uu}\cdot\mathbf n, M=r_{uv}\cdot\mathbf n, and N=r_{vv}\cdot\mathbf n are the coefficients of the second fundamental form.

Let E,F,G be the coefficients of the first fundamental form:

E=r_u\cdot r_u,\qquad F=r_u\cdot r_v,\qquad G=r_v\cdot r_v.

Their derivatives with respect to u,v are

\begin{aligned} E_u&=2r_{uu}\cdot r_u,\\ E_v&=2r_{uv}\cdot r_u,\\ F_u&=r_{uu}\cdot r_v+r_{uv}\cdot r_u,\\ F_v&=r_{uv}\cdot r_v+r_{vv}\cdot r_u,\\ G_u&=2r_{uv}\cdot r_v,\\ G_v&=2r_{vv}\cdot r_v. \end{aligned}

Now take dot products of the second derivatives of the surface with r_u and r_v to obtain expressions for the Christoffel symbols:

\begin{aligned} r_{uu}\cdot r_u&=\Gamma^1_{11}E+\Gamma^2_{11}F&=\tfrac12E_u,\\ r_{uu}\cdot r_v&=\Gamma^1_{11}F+\Gamma^2_{11}G&=F_u-\tfrac12E_v,\\ r_{uv}\cdot r_u&=\Gamma^1_{12}E+\Gamma^2_{12}F&=\tfrac12E_v,\\ r_{uv}\cdot r_v&=\Gamma^1_{12}F+\Gamma^2_{12}G&=\tfrac12G_u,\\ r_{vv}\cdot r_u&=\Gamma^1_{22}E+\Gamma^2_{22}F&=F_v-\tfrac12G_u,\\ r_{vv}\cdot r_v&=\Gamma^1_{22}F+\Gamma^2_{22}G&=\tfrac12G_v. \end{aligned}

Each pair of equations can be written as a matrix equation, for the first two:

\begin{pmatrix}E&F\\F&G\end{pmatrix} \binom{\Gamma^1_{11}}{\Gamma^2_{11}} =\binom{\tfrac12E_u}{F_u-\tfrac12E_v}.

For a regular surface the matrix is invertible with determinant EG-F^2>0, showing that the Christoffel symbols can be expressed in terms of the coefficients of the first fundamental form and their derivatives.
