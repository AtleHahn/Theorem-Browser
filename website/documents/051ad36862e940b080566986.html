Establishes an intrinsic tradeoff between localization in position and frequency for stable, complete Gabor representations at critical density. It shows that representational limitations can follow from mathematical structure rather than imperfect instruments; the conclusion depends on the stated basis and density assumptions.

Statement

Let g\in L^2(\mathbb R;\mathbb C), and let a,b>0 satisfy ab=1. For m,n\in\mathbb Z put g_{m,n}(x)=e^{2\pi i m b x}g(x-na). Suppose these functions form a Riesz basis of L^2(\mathbb R): their linear span is dense and there exist constants 0<A\le B<\infty such that for every finitely supported scalar family (c_{m,n}),

A\sum_{m,n}|c_{m,n}|^2\le\left\|\sum_{m,n}c_{m,n}g_{m,n}\right\|_2^2\le B\sum_{m,n}|c_{m,n}|^2.

Then at least one of \int_{\mathbb R}x^2|g(x)|^2\,dx and \int_{\mathbb R}\xi^2|\widehat g(\xi)|^2\,d\xi is infinite. Here \widehat g is the L^2 Fourier transform, with convention \widehat g(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb R}g(x)e^{-2\pi i x\xi}\,dx on L^1\cap L^2, extended by L^2 limits. In particular this applies to an orthonormal basis.
