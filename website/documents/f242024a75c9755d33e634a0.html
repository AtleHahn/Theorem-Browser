Statement

Let f:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb C be bounded and Lebesgue measurable, with \lim_{t\to\infty}f(t)=\alpha\in\mathbb C. For every real s>0 its Laplace transform F(s)=\int_0^\infty e^{-st}f(t)\,dt exists absolutely, and \lim_{s\downarrow0}sF(s)=\alpha.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Measurability and integration for arbitrary bounded measurable functions require measure theory, despite the elementary estimate.

Proof 1: Split the integral into a bounded interval and a tail — outline

Suppose for convenience that {\displaystyle |f(t)|\leq 1} on {\displaystyle (0,\infty ),} and let {\displaystyle \alpha =\lim _{t\to \infty }f(t)}. Let {\displaystyle \epsilon >0,} and choose {\displaystyle A>0} so that {\displaystyle |f(t)-\alpha |<\epsilon } for all {\displaystyle t>A.} Since

{\displaystyle s\int _{0}^{\infty }e^{-st}\,\mathrm {d} t=1,}

for every {\displaystyle s>0} we have

{\displaystyle sF(s)-\alpha =s\int _{0}^{\infty }(f(t)-\alpha )e^{-st}\,\mathrm {d} t;}

hence

{\displaystyle |sF(s)-\alpha |\leq s\int _{0}^{A}|f(t)-\alpha |e^{-st}\,\mathrm {d} t+s\int _{A}^{\infty }|f(t)-\alpha |e^{-st}\,\mathrm {d} t\leq 2s\int _{0}^{A}e^{-st}\,\mathrm {d} t+\epsilon s\int _{A}^{\infty }e^{-st}\,\mathrm {d} t\equiv I+II.}

Now for every {\displaystyle s>0} we have

{\displaystyle II<\epsilon s\int _{0}^{\infty }e^{-st}\,\mathrm {d} t=\epsilon .}

On the other hand, since {\displaystyle A<\infty } is fixed it is clear that {\displaystyle \lim _{s\to 0}I=0}, and so {\displaystyle |sF(s)-\alpha |<2\epsilon } if {\displaystyle s>0} is small enough. Since \epsilon>0 is arbitrary, the claimed limit follows. Rescaling f gives the result for any finite bound.

Level justification

The epsilon estimate and exponential integrals are undergraduate analysis, but the theorem and proof concern arbitrary bounded Lebesgue-measurable functions. Establishing integrability and interpreting the comparison for that class presupposes Lebesgue integration and measurability, beyond the explicitly assumed introductory real-analysis baseline. A continuous-function special case would require less preparation; the actual formulation is retained at Beyond undergraduate.

Bound f(t)exp(-st) by a constant times exp(-st), split the error integral at A, and bound the finite-interval and tail contributions separately.
