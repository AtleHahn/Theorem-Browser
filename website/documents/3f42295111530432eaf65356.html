Statement

Let f:I\to J be a bijection between open real intervals and g=f^{-1}. If f is differentiable at x\in I and g at y=f(x), then f^{\prime}(x)g^{\prime}(y)=1; in particular f^{\prime}(x)\ne0 and g^{\prime}(y)=1/f^{\prime}(x).

Proof 1: Differentiate the inverse identity

Let {\displaystyle f} be an invertible (bijective) function, let {\displaystyle x} be in the domain of {\displaystyle f}, and let {\displaystyle y=f(x).} Let {\displaystyle g=f^{-1}.} So, {\displaystyle f(g(y))=y.} Under the assumed differentiability of both functions at the corresponding points, differentiating this equation with respect to ⁠{\displaystyle y}⁠, and using the chain rule, one gets

{\displaystyle f'(g(y))\cdot g'(y)=1.}

That is,

{\displaystyle g'(y)={\frac {1}{f'(g(y))}}}

or

{\displaystyle \left[f^{-1}\right]^{\prime }(y)={\frac {1}{f^{\prime }(f^{-1}(y))}}.}

In Leibniz's notation, the same relation is obtained by differentiating f^{-1}(y)=x in terms of x and applying the chain rule:

\frac{dx}{dy}\,\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{dx}{dx}=1.

Proof 2: Reflect the two tangent lines — outline

Geometrically, a function and inverse function have graphs that are reflections in the line y=x. This reflection operation turns the gradient of any nonhorizontal, nonvertical line into its reciprocal. The tangent lines at the corresponding points have finite slopes f'(x) and g'(y) by hypothesis; reflection therefore gives

f'(x)g'(y)=1.
