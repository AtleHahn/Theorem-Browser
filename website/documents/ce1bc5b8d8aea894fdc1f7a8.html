Statement

Let G be a finite simple graph with at least two vertices. A forest is a graph with no cycle. Let a(G) be the least nonnegative integer t for which the edge set of G partitions into t forests (empty forests are allowed). For a vertex subset U, let e(U) denote the number of edges of the induced graph G[U]. Then

a(G)=\left\lceil\max_{U\subseteq V(G),\ |U|\ge2}\frac{e(U)}{|U|-1}\right\rceil.
Level
High school only
Level justification

Forests and edge partitions are defined on a finite graph; the maximum of finitely many rational counts and its ceiling are elementary.

Proof 1: Minimize the integer forest-count bounds

Use the Nash–Williams forest partition theorem: G can be partitioned into t edge-disjoint forests if and only if, for every nonempty U\subseteq V(G), the induced subgraph G[U] has at most t(|U|-1) edges. Singletons impose no condition.

In other words, for every induced subgraph S=G[U] with at least two vertices, we have

t\geq\left\lceil\frac{E(S)}{V(S)-1}\right\rceil,

where E(S) and V(S) denote its numbers of edges and vertices. For the least possible t, this is tight in that there is a subgraph S that saturates the inequality (or else we can choose a smaller t). This leads to the formula

a(G)=\left\lceil\max_{\substack{U\subseteq V(G)\\|U|\geq2}} \frac{e(U)}{|U|-1}\right\rceil.

Level justification

Rewrite the supplied finite-graph forest criterion as integer lower bounds and take their maximum. With the graph terms defined, only finite counting, inequalities and ceilings are needed; the forest-partition theorem is used without its proof.
