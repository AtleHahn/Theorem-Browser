Statement

For a locally compact Hausdorff abelian group G, write \widehat G for its continuous homomorphisms to \mathbb T=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|=1\}, with pointwise multiplication and the compact-open topology. For Haar measure \mu and \chi\in\widehat G, the map \Lambda_\chi:L^1(G)\to\mathbb C defined by \Lambda_\chi(f)=\int_G f(x)\overline{\chi(x)}\,d\mu(x) is linear, bounded by |\Lambda_\chi(f)|\le\|f\|_1, and multiplicative for convolution.

Proof 1: Evaluate the Fourier convolution identity

Write \widehat f(\chi)=\int_G f(x)\overline{\chi(x)}\,d\mu(x), and use additive notation for G, so that (f*g)(x)=\int_G f(x-y)g(y)\,d\mu(y).

Here \mathcal F(f)=\widehat f, and C_0(\widehat G) denotes the continuous functions vanishing at infinity, with the supremum norm.

The Fourier transform takes convolution to multiplication, i.e. it is a homomorphism of abelian Banach algebras {\displaystyle L^{1}(G)\to C_{0}\left({\widehat {G}}\right)} (of norm ≤ 1):

{\displaystyle {\mathcal {F}}(f*g)(\chi )={\mathcal {F}}(f)(\chi )\cdot {\mathcal {F}}(g)(\chi ).}

In particular, to every group character on {\displaystyle G} corresponds a unique multiplicative linear functional on the group algebra defined by

{\displaystyle f\mapsto {\widehat {f}}(\chi ).}

Here \Lambda_\chi(f)=\widehat f(\chi); the norm bound gives |\Lambda_\chi(f)|\leq\|f\|_1.
