Statement

Let B be standard Brownian motion with B_0=0. Let \mu and \sigma be deterministic real functions with \int_0^t|\mu_s|\,ds<\infty and \int_0^t\sigma_s^2\,ds<\infty for every finite t. For X_t=\int_0^t\mu_s\,ds+\int_0^t\sigma_s\,dB_s, where the second integral is the Itô integral, \mathbb E[X_t]=\int_0^t\mu_s\,ds.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the stochastic integral and expectation requires probability and stochastic calculus.

Proof 1: Zero-mean stochastic integral and deterministic drift

We use the zero-mean property of the Itô integral with a deterministic square-integrable integrand.

First, notice that every dB_t individually has mean 0, so the expected value of X_t is simply the integral of the drift function:

\mathbb E[X_t]=\int_0^t\mu_s\,ds.
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof applies a zero-mean theorem for an Ito integral driven by Brownian motion. Even deterministic integrands require stochastic integration and its L2 construction, beyond the calculus/probability baseline.
