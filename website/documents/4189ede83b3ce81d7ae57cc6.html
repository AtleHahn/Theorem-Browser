Statement

There exists a nonconvex polyhedron with positive angular defect at every vertex, where angular defect is 360^\circ minus the sum of incident face angles.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Angular defect is defined by subtracting face-angle sums from a full turn, within elementary Euclidean geometry.

Mathematical Field
High School Mathematics
Required topics
polyhedraplane angles
Review notes
none

Proof 1: Reflecting the square pyramid

A counterexample is provided by a cube where one face is replaced by a square pyramid: this elongated square pyramid is convex and the defects at each vertex are each positive. Now consider the same cube where the square pyramid goes into the cube: this is concave, but the defects remain the same, and so are all positive.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Reflect the attached pyramid inward, preserving each face angle while destroying convexity. School spatial geometry and reflection suffice.
