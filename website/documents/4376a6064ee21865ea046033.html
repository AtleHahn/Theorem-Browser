Statement

For an integer p\ge2, the formal solution x(z)\in z\mathbb Q[[z]] of x(z)^p-x(z)+z=0 is x(z)=\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{\binom{pk}{k}}{(p-1)k+1}z^{(p-1)k+1}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Calculus and power series supply composition, coefficients, differentiation and local inversion. Formal series retain the same coefficient operations without analytic convergence; Lambert W is specified by its inverse equation. These direct definitions suffice for the displayed coefficient identities.

Proof 1: Lagrange coefficient extraction outline

Use formal Lagrange inversion for f(x)=x-x^p=x(1-x^{p-1}). The coefficient formula gives [z^n]x(z)=\frac1n[t^{n-1}](1-t^{p-1})^{-n}. Expanding the negative integer power yields nonzero coefficients only when n-1=(p-1)k; the corresponding binomial coefficient is \binom{n+k-1}{k}=\binom{pk}{k}. This gives the displayed series.
