Statement

Let \mathfrak g be a finite-dimensional real Lie algebra and M a compact smooth manifold without boundary. Every smooth infinitesimal action of \mathfrak g on M integrates to a smooth action of a finite-dimensional Lie group G whose Lie algebra is identified with \mathfrak g.

Explicitly, the infinitesimal action assigns to each \alpha\in\mathfrak g a smooth vector field a(\alpha), linearly and compatibly with the Lie brackets using the convention for infinitesimal fields of the chosen action. There is a smooth group action \Phi:G\times M\to M whose infinitesimal fields are exactly the prescribed ones:

a(\alpha)_x=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\Phi(\exp(t\alpha),x)\qquad(\alpha\in\mathfrak g,\ x\in M).

For a left action as displayed, compatibility means a([\alpha,\beta])=-[a(\alpha),a(\beta)] with the usual vector-field bracket; equivalently one may use right actions and bracket-preserving infinitesimal actions.

The conclusion also holds for a compact smooth manifold with boundary, provided every vector field a(\alpha) is tangent to its boundary.

Proof 1: Complete flows on the product with a Lie group (external)

The following editorial argument completes the stated scope. A supporting source is Orbit equivalence of linear systems on manifolds and semigroup actions on homogeneous spaces (Section 4, Theorem 2 and Lemmas 3–4, Proposition 5, Theorem 6, printed pp.1179–1182; use D=g.).

Cossich–Hungaro–Rocio–Santana, https://www.scielo.cl/pdf/proy/v41n5/0716-0917-proy-41-05-1173.pdf, Section 4, Theorem 2 and Lemmas 3–4 through Proposition 5/Theorem 6, pp.1179–1182, use the distribution on G times M with horizontal component the invariant fields on simply connected G. Take D=g, so all the vector fields used in the proof are complete; no claim concerning merely a generating subset is needed. Compactness makes every smooth tangent vector field complete. For a boundary, tangency preserves both the boundary and interior, by uniqueness in boundary charts, and compactness gives complete flows on both. The same flow-coordinate construction yields integral leaves projecting as local diffeomorphisms/coverings onto G; leaves through boundary points lie in G times boundary(M). Simple connectivity trivializes these coverings and the flow formulas vary smoothly also in boundary charts. Their composition law supplies the action. The horizontal G-component ensures constant distribution rank even when the action has a kernel. Right-invariant fields on G give the left-action anti-homomorphism convention in the statement.
