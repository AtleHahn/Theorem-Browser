Statement

Let h be a proper k-coloring of a finite simple graph G. Give K_k any tournament orientation T, meaning exactly one of i\to j or j\to i for each pair of distinct colors. Direct each edge of G according to the direction between its colors in T. This is an orientation of G and h is a directed-graph homomorphism to T. If T directs colors from smaller to larger, the resulting orientation is acyclic.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The tournament directions are explicitly defined on the finite color labels, and the proof defines a graph homomorphism as an edge-preserving vertex map. Proper coloring, directing a finite edge and checking for a cycle require elementary finite graph reasoning. No college graph theory is needed to understand the full construction.

Proof 1: Orient edges by their endpoint colors — outline

A homomorphism is a map from the vertices of one graph to the vertices of another that always maps edges to edges. Thus, a k-coloring of an undirected graph G may be described by a homomorphism from G to the complete graph K_k. If the complete graph is given an orientation, it becomes a tournament, and the orientation can be lifted back across the homomorphism to give an orientation of G.

In particular, the graph may be acyclically oriented by numbering colors and by directing each edge from its lower-numbered endpoint to the higher-numbered endpoint. With this orientation, the numbers are strictly increasing along each directed path, so each path can include at most one vertex of each color.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Different endpoint colors select a unique tournament direction. With increasing color numbers, a directed cycle is impossible because the numbers would increase strictly back to their start. Only elementary finite graphs and ordering are needed.
