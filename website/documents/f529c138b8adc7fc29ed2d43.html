Statement

Let \theta>0 and let X have density p_\theta(x)=\theta e^{-x}/(1+e^{-x})^{\theta+1} on \mathbb R. Put T(x)=\log(1+e^{-x}). \operatorname{Var}(T(X))=1/\theta^2.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Explicit real density; Variance of a log statistic. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Differentiate the skew-logistic log-partition function

The density can be rewritten as

{\displaystyle {\frac {e^{-x}}{1+e^{-x}}}\exp[-\theta \log \left(1+e^{-x}\right)+\log(\theta )]}

Notice this is an exponential family with natural parameter

{\displaystyle \eta =-\theta ,}

sufficient statistic

{\displaystyle T=\log \left(1+e^{-x}\right),}

and log-partition function

{\displaystyle A(\eta )=-\log(\theta )=-\log(-\eta )}

Using the exponential-family variance identity \operatorname{Var}(T)=A''(\eta)

{\displaystyle \operatorname {var} \left[\log \left(1+e^{-X}\right)\right]={\frac {\partial ^{2}A(\eta )}{\partial \eta ^{2}}}={\frac {\partial }{\partial \eta }}\left[{\frac {1}{-\eta }}\right]={\frac {1}{{\left(-\eta \right)}^{2}}}={\frac {1}{\theta ^{2}}}.}
