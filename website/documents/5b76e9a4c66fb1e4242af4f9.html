Statement

Let k be an algebraically closed field of characteristic p>0. On the p-dimensional space V=k[x]/(x^p), let I be the identity, M multiplication by x, and D formal differentiation. The solvable Lie algebra of operators spanned by I,M,D has no common nonzero eigenvector in V, so the unrestricted Lie triangularization theorem fails in characteristic p.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Polynomial quotients and characteristic p are undergraduate algebra; operator commutators and the derived series defining solvability are a small related extension of linear algebra.

Proof 1: Verify the truncated-polynomial witness by a commutator — outline

Multiplication by x preserves the ideal (x^p). Differentiation also preserves it, since D(x^ph)=p x^{p-1}h+x^pDh=x^pDh in characteristic p. Thus both operators are well-defined on the quotient. The product rule gives [D,M]=I, while I commutes with both operators, so the spanned Lie algebra is solvable. If a nonzero v were a common eigenvector, say Dv=av and Mv=bv, then [D,M]v=(ab-ba)v=0. But [D,M]v=Iv=v\ne0, a contradiction. A common triangular basis would supply a common eigenvector, so it is impossible.
