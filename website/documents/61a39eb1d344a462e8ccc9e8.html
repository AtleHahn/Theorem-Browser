Statement

In the cyclic group C_4, its subgroup H of order two has no complement; equivalently the extension by C_4/H\cong C_2 does not split.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory algebra supplies groups, cosets, quotients, homomorphisms and linear independence. Complement or composition-series conditions and monoid vocabulary are small algebraic extensions; finite-tree automorphisms are adjacency-preserving permutations. No additional structural theory is required.

Proof 1: Unique involution — outline

Write C_2=H for the order-two subgroup. Then if {\displaystyle C_{4}} were a semidirect product of {\displaystyle C_{2}} and {\displaystyle C_{4}/C_{2}\cong C_{2}} then {\displaystyle C_{4}} would have to contain two elements of order 2, but it only contains one.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A complement would supply a second order-two subgroup, but the cyclic group of order four has only one involution. Complements and splitting require only a small extension of basic finite group theory.

Proof 2: Only the direct-product action is available — outline

Write C_2=H for the order-two subgroup. Another way to explain this impossibility of splitting {\displaystyle C_{4}} (i.e. expressing it as a semidirect product) is to observe that the automorphisms of {\displaystyle C_{2}} are the trivial group, so the only possible [semi]direct product of {\displaystyle C_{2}} with itself is a direct product (which gives rise to the Klein four-group, a group that is non-isomorphic with {\displaystyle C_{4}}).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The order-two group has only the identity automorphism, so any semidirect product with another order-two group is the direct product, which has the wrong element orders. The elementary semidirect-product definition is a small extension of introductory group theory.
