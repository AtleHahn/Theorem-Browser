Statement

Let g be an element of a group G, and L_g(x)=gx. Its permutation orbit through x is the right coset \langle g\rangle x=\{g^kx:k\in\mathbb Z\}. If g has finite order d, every cycle has length d; if g has infinite order, each orbit is an infinite cycle indexed bijectively by \mathbb Z.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Sets, relations, group multiplication, cosets and permutations are undergraduate foundations. Equivariance means respecting the stated action; regularity and orbit descriptions are explicit, requiring no representation theory.

Proof 1: Apply the fixed-point obstruction to powers — outline

Nonidentity left translations have no fixed point: hx=x implies h=e by cancellation. The same applies to powers of g below its order. Consequently the cycle length of L_g equals the order of g, and its elements are exactly g^kx, the right coset of the subgroup generated by g.

Level justification

Apply cancellation to powers of g and identify their orbit with a cyclic right coset. Introductory group theory and integer powers suffice, including the infinite-order case.
