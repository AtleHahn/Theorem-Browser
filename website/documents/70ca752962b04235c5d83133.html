Statement

Let m\ge1 and x\in\mathbb R^n. Let all coefficients A_{\alpha,k}(t,x) be real analytic near (0,0), and let \phi_0,\ldots,\phi_{m-1} be real analytic near x=0. Among classical C^m solutions near (0,0), there is a unique local solution (up to restriction to a smaller neighbourhood) of

\partial_t^m u=\sum_{k\le m-1,\,|\alpha|+k\le m}A_{\alpha,k}(t,x)\partial_x^\alpha\partial_t^k u,\qquad \partial_t^k u(0,x)=\phi_k(x)\ (0\le k<m).

Here \alpha is a multi-index of nonnegative integers and |\alpha| is its sum.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Multivariable calculus supplies partial derivatives and initial data, and real analytic means locally represented by a convergent power series. The multi-index convention is supplied. Existence and uniqueness concern classical derivatives, without weak solutions, Sobolev spaces or distributions.

Proof 1: Analyticity followed by Cauchy–Kowalevski uniqueness

By the analytic-data consequence of Holmgren’s theorem stated in the source, a solution of this linear Cauchy problem is real analytic near the initial point. Cauchy–Kowalevski supplies a real-analytic solution and uniqueness among real-analytic solutions. Consequently every classical solution agrees locally with that analytic solution.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Combine the stated Holmgren consequence with analytic existence and uniqueness. Higher-order PDE Cauchy problems and analytic regularity require substantial advanced PDE background.
