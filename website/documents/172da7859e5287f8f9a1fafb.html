Statement

Let X be finite, G a finite permutation group acting on X, and Y a set of m colors. The action on colorings \phi:X\to Y is (g\phi)(x)=\phi(g^{-1}x). If c(g) is the number of cycles of g on X, the number of coloring orbits is |G|^{-1}\sum_{g\in G}m^{c(g)}. Use 0^0=1 for the empty coloring.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Finite group actions, cycles and orbit counting are introductory algebra/combinatorics. The weighted generating functions and cycle-index substitution are explicitly defined by coefficient counts, a direct formal-series extension.

Proof 1: Unweighted Pólya enumeration — proof

The simplified form of the Pólya enumeration theorem follows from Burnside's lemma, which says that the number of orbits of colorings is the average of the number of elements of Y^X fixed by the permutation g of G over all permutations g.

The element g fixes an element \phi\in Y^X if and only if the function \phi is constant on every cycle q of g. There are m choices of color for each cycle, giving m^{c(g)} fixed colorings. Hence the number of orbits is

\left|Y^X/G\right|=\frac1{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}m^{c(g)}.

Level justification

Apply Burnside to the finite group action and count one independent color choice per cycle. Undergraduate introductory group actions and combinatorics suffice.
