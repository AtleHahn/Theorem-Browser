Statement

Let (X,\mathcal M,\mu) be a sigma-finite measure space and K\in L^2(\mu\otimes\mu) a real symmetric kernel. The associated operator on real L^2(\mu) is specified by

\langle T_K\varphi,\psi\rangle=\int_{X\times X}K(y,x)\varphi(y)\psi(x)\,d(\mu\otimes\mu).

Then it has an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors. Those for nonzero eigenvalues form an at most countable set, even if L^2(\mu) is not separable.

Proof 1: Kernel symmetry and the compact spectral theorem — outline

By the square-integrable-kernel theorem, T_K is a compact operator (actually it is even a Hilbert–Schmidt operator). If the kernel K is symmetric, by the spectral theorem for compact self-adjoint operators, T_K has an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors. Those eigenvectors that correspond to nonzero eigenvalues form an at most countable set (regardless of separability).
