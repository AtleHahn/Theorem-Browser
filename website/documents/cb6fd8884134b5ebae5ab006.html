Statement

For an integer n\ge1, use deterministic Turing machines with n operational states, a separate Halt state, one starting state, a two-way unbounded tape over \{0,1\}, and a transition for each operational state and scanned symbol. Each transition writes a bit, moves one cell left or right, and enters a state (possibly Halt). The machine starts on an all-zero tape. Among machines that halt, \Sigma(n) is the largest number of ones left on the tape, and S(n) the largest number of transitions, including the halting transition. Changing the move direction of a halting transition, reversing all move directions, or permuting operational states while fixing Start preserves the halting score of a halting machine. These operations therefore take score-maximizing machines to score-maximizing machines.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All machine actions are defined, and the three transformations are a change of the last move, left-right reflection, and renaming state labels. The assertion concerns preserving the integer count of final ones. These local operations and finite maxima require elementary symmetry and counting, without a computational theory prerequisite.

Proof 1: Final moves, reflection and state labels — outline

Changing a halting move changes no tape symbol after that final write. Reversing all move directions reflects the entire blank-tape computation and leaves its number of ones unchanged. Relabeling the operational states other than Start produces the same computation with new state names.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Each transformation is checked directly on the specified tape rules: the final move cannot alter written symbols, reflection preserves counts, and relabeling preserves transitions. These are elementary symmetry and counting arguments.
