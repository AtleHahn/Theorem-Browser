Statement

Let f:S^1\to S^1 be a smooth orientation-preserving diffeomorphism, with f(e^{i\theta})=e^{ig(\theta)} and g(\theta+2\pi)=g(\theta)+2\pi. There are univalent holomorphic maps F_1 of D=\{|z|<1\} and F_2 of D^*=\{|z|>1\}\cup\{\infty\} onto complementary Jordan domains in the Riemann sphere, extending continuously to boundary homeomorphisms f_1,f_2:S^1\to\Gamma onto their common Jordan curve, with f=f_1^{-1}\circ f_2.

Proof 1: Smooth extension and the Beltrami equation — outline

If f is an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism of the circle, the Alexander extension gives a way of extending f to a diffeomorphism of the unit disk D:

{\displaystyle \displaystyle {F(r,\theta )=r\exp[i\psi (r)g(\theta )+i(1-\psi (r))\theta ],}}

with F(0)=0, where ψ is a smooth function with values in [0,1], equal to 0 near 0 and 1 near 1, and

{\displaystyle \displaystyle {f(e^{i\theta })=e^{ig(\theta )},}}

with g(θ + 2π) = g(θ) + 2π.

The extension F can be continued to any larger disk |z| < R with R > 1. Accordingly in the unit disc

{\displaystyle \displaystyle {\|\mu \|_{\infty }<1,\,\,\,\mu (z)=F_{\overline {z}}/F_{z}.}}

Now extend μ to a Beltrami coefficient on the whole of C by setting it equal to 0 for |z| ≥ 1. By the measurable Riemann mapping theorem, let G be the corresponding quasiconformal homeomorphism of the sphere, normalized to fix infinity, solving the Beltrami equation:

{\displaystyle \displaystyle {G_{\overline {z}}=\mu G_{z}.}}

Let F1(z) = G {\displaystyle \circ } F−1(z) for |z| ≤ 1 and F2(z) = G (z) for |z| ≥ 1. By the Beltrami composition rule (equal coefficients give a conformal transition, and coefficient zero gives conformality), F1 and F2 are univalent holomorphic maps of |z| < 1 and |z| > 1 onto the inside and outside of a Jordan curve. They extend continuously to homeomorphisms fi of the unit circle onto the Jordan curve on the boundary. By construction they satisfy the conformal welding condition:

{\displaystyle \displaystyle {f=f_{1}^{-1}\circ f_{2}.}}
