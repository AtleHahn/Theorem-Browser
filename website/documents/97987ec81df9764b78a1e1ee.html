Uniform convergence with strict Fatou inequality

Statement

On \mathbb R with Lebesgue measure, f_n=n^{-1}1_{[0,n]} converges uniformly to zero but \int_{\mathbb R}f_n\,dx=1 for all n. Thus uniform convergence alone does not force equality in Fatou’s inequality on this infinite measure space.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Lebesgue integration of these interval indicators is ordinary length times height; Fatou compares integral of liminf with liminf of integrals. These limited measure definitions extend undergraduate uniform convergence.

Proof 1: Spread constant area over longer intervals — outline

Let {\displaystyle S} denote the set of all real numbers. Define

{\displaystyle f_{n}(x)={\begin{cases}{\frac {1}{n}}&{\text{for }}x\in [0,n],\\0&{\text{otherwise.}}\end{cases}}}

The sequence (f_n)_{n\ge1} converges uniformly on S to the zero function (with zero integral), but every f_n has integral one.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The full statement includes the meaning of Fatou’s nonnegative-integral inequality, not just rectangle areas. This is a limited measure-integral extension of undergraduate real analysis. Here the sup norm is 1/n, each interval integral is one and the zero limit has integral zero, giving strict inequality.
