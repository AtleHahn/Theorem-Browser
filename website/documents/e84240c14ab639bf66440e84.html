Statement

For a closed orientable surface of genus g\ge1, the maximum chromatic number among finite maps on that surface is \left\lfloor\frac{7+\sqrt{1+48g}}2\right\rfloor. Here maps have connected regions and adjacency across a boundary segment; the chromatic number is the least number of region colors avoiding equal colors on adjacent regions.

Closed orientable surfaces and handle genus add related surface definitions to basic topology. Maps and coloring are finite, and the formula uses a square root and the floor function.

Proof 1: Substitute the surface Euler characteristic

For closed surfaces with positive genus, the maximum number p of colors needed depends on the Euler characteristic \chi of the surface. For orientable surfaces the Heawood formula is

p=\left\lfloor\frac{7+\sqrt{49-24\chi}}2\right\rfloor,

where the outermost brackets denote the floor function.

For an orientable surface, \chi=2-2g, so this implies that p can be given in terms of the genus g of the surface:

p=\left\lfloor\frac{7+\sqrt{1+48g}}2\right\rfloor.
