Statement

Let d\ge0 be an integer. If \sigma is a real polynomial of degree at most d, then the scalar functions g(x)=\sum_{j=1}^k c_j\sigma(a_jx+b_j) on [0,1], with arbitrary finite k and real parameters, are not dense in C([0,1]) for the uniform norm.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves real polynomials and affine substitution; uniform approximation on a real interval; continuous functions and uniform norm. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Degree preservation and a closed polynomial subspace

Let F_\sigma be the family of functions g in the statement. If the function \sigma is a polynomial of degree at most d, then F_\sigma is contained in the closed subspace of all polynomials of degree at most d in C([0,1]). (This subspace is closed because it is finite-dimensional.) So its closure is also contained in it, which is not all of C([0,1]): for example, x^{d+1} lies outside it.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Affine substitution and finite sums preserve the degree bound, and the resulting finite-dimensional polynomial subspace is closed in the uniform norm. Undergraduate normed linear algebra and uniform convergence suffice.
