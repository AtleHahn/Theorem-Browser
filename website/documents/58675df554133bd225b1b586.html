Statement

Let G have vertex set \mathbb R^2, with an edge precisely between pairs at Euclidean distance 1. Every graph endomorphism of G is a Euclidean isometry and a graph automorphism.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A graph endomorphism is a vertex map preserving edges. Here edges mean distance one, so the full claim is directly a condition on maps of the Euclidean plane and their preservation of distances; no advanced graph theory is needed.

Proof 1: Translate graph edges into the preserved distance

Use the Beckman–Quarles theorem: a map from \mathbb R^2 to itself preserving every unit distance preserves every Euclidean distance.

One way of rephrasing the Beckman–Quarles theorem involves graph homomorphisms, mappings between undirected graphs that take vertices to vertices and edges to edges. For the unit distance graph whose vertices are all of the points in the plane, with an edge between any two points at unit distance, a homomorphism from this graph to itself is the same thing as a unit-distance-preserving transformation of the plane. Thus, the Beckman–Quarles theorem states that the only homomorphisms from this graph to itself are the obvious ones coming from isometries of the plane. For this graph, all homomorphisms are symmetries of the graph.
