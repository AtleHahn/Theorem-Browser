Statement

Let \lambda be an indeterminate, E=\mathbb Q(\lambda), and let \sigma be the rational automorphism determined by \sigma(\lambda)=1/\lambda. The elements fixed by \sigma form \mathbb Q(\lambda+1/\lambda).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory field theory supplies rational functions and automorphisms. The automorphism is a concrete substitution, and the fixed set means the rational functions unchanged by it. The generated field on the right uses the same rational operations, without general Galois theory.

Proof 1: Specialize the orbit-polynomial coefficient prescription — outline

Use the source-stated orbit-coefficient theorem: for a finite subgroup H of the rational automorphism group of \mathbb Q(\lambda), the coefficients of \prod_{h\in H}(T-h(\lambda)) generate its fixed field over \mathbb Q. Apply the source orbit-polynomial coefficient prescription to H=\{1,\sigma\}. Its orbit polynomial is (T-\lambda)(T-\sigma(\lambda))=T^2-(\lambda+1/\lambda)T+1. Its only nonconstant coefficient generates \mathbb Q(\lambda+1/\lambda), which the prescription identifies with the fixed field.
