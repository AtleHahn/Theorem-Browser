Statement

Fix 0<T<\infty. On [0,T], let W^P be a Brownian motion on a filtered probability space satisfying the usual conditions. Suppose a positive asset price satisfies dS_t/S_t=\mu_t\,dt+\sigma_t\,dW_t^P with \sigma_t>0. Let r_t be the given rate and \lambda_t=(\mu_t-r_t)/\sigma_t. Assume the displayed stochastic integrals are defined and Z_t=\exp(-\int_0^t\lambda_s\,dW_s^P-\tfrac12\int_0^t\lambda_s^2\,ds) is a true martingale. For dQ/dP=Z_T, the process W_t^Q=W_t^P+\int_0^t\lambda_s\,ds is Q-Brownian and dS_t/S_t=r_t\,dt+\sigma_t\,dW_t^Q.

Proof 1: Substitute the changed Brownian increment

Girsanov's theorem for stochastic-integral exponential densities, applied to -\lambda, implies that

W_t^Q=W_t^P+\int_0^t\lambda_s\,ds

is a Brownian motion under Q. Substitution into the asset-price equation gives

\frac{dS_t}{S_t} =(\mu_t-\sigma_t\lambda_t)\,dt+\sigma_t\,dW_t^Q =r_t\,dt+\sigma_t\,dW_t^Q.

Thus, the change of measure changes the asset's drift from \mu_t to the risk-free rate r_t, while leaving the diffusion coefficient \sigma_t, or instantaneous volatility, unchanged.

Level justification

Use the stochastic-exponential change of measure to identify the new Brownian motion, then substitute increments in the stochastic differential equation. Filtered probability, martingales and Ito integration require graduate probability.
