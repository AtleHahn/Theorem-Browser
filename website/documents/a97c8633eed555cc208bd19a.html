Statement

Let A be a bounded self-adjoint operator on a complex Hilbert space with nonzero absolutely continuous spectral subspace. No bounded self-adjoint operator B with B-A trace class is unitarily equivalent to a diagonal operator.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Absolutely continuous spectral subspaces and trace-class perturbations require substantial operator spectral theory. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Kato–Rosenblum obstruction

Use the Kato–Rosenblum theorem: bounded self-adjoint operators differing by a trace-class operator have unitarily equivalent absolutely continuous parts. Consequently the nonzero absolutely continuous part of A persists for B, whereas a diagonal operator has pure point spectral measures. Thus B cannot be unitarily equivalent to a diagonal operator.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Use preservation of the absolutely continuous spectral part under trace-class perturbation and contrast diagonal pure-point spectra. Infinite-dimensional spectral measures and operator ideals require functional analysis.
