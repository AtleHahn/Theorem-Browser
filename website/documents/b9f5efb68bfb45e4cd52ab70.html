Statement

For real x,y, \cos x\cos y=\tfrac12(\cos(x+y)+\cos(x-y)).

Level

Level justification

The statement is a trigonometric identity for real angles.

Proof 1: Multiply and regroup the exponential cosine expressions

Here i^2=-1, and Euler's formula gives \cos t=(e^{it}+e^{-it})/2.

Complex exponentials can simplify trigonometry, because they are mathematically easier to manipulate than their sine and cosine components. One technique is simply to convert sines and cosines into equivalent expressions in terms of exponentials sometimes called complex sinusoids. After the manipulations, the simplified result is still real-valued. For example:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\cos x\cos y&={\frac {e^{ix}+e^{-ix}}{2}}\cdot {\frac {e^{iy}+e^{-iy}}{2}}\\&={\frac {1}{2}}\cdot {\frac {e^{i(x+y)}+e^{i(x-y)}+e^{i(-x+y)}+e^{i(-x-y)}}{2}}\\&={\frac {1}{2}}{\bigg (}{\frac {e^{i(x+y)}+e^{-i(x+y)}}{2}}+{\frac {e^{i(x-y)}+e^{-i(x-y)}}{2}}{\bigg )}\\&={\frac {1}{2}}\left(\cos(x+y)+\cos(x-y)\right).\end{aligned}}}
