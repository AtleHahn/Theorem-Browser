Statement

Let k be a nonzero commutative ring with identity. Write k\langle X\rangle for the free associative k-algebra whose basis consists of finite words in letters X, including the empty word 1. Fix a total order on words preserved by adjoining words on either side, such that every word has only finitely many predecessors. Let I be the two-sided ideal generated by monic relations w_\sigma-f_\sigma, where every word in f_\sigma is smaller than w_\sigma. An elementary reduction replaces a term cuw_\sigma v by cuf_\sigma v and fixes all other words. A word is reduced if it contains no w_\sigma as a contiguous subword. Every element can be transformed by a finite sequence of reductions into a linear combination of reduced words, congruent to the original element modulo I. Consequently, the images of the reduced words span k\langle X\rangle/I as a k-module.

Proof 1: Finite descent and congruence — outline

A word w is reduced with respect to the relations w_\sigma-f_\sigma if it does not contain any of the leading words w_\sigma. Otherwise, w=uw_\sigma v for some words u,v and some \sigma. Then there is a reduction r_{u\sigma v}:k\langle X\rangle\to k\langle X\rangle, which is a k-linear endomorphism of k\langle X\rangle that fixes all words apart from w=uw_\sigma v and sends this to uf_\sigma v. By the choice of ordering there are only finitely many words less than any given word, hence a finite composition of reductions will send any h\in k\langle X\rangle to a linear combination of reduced words.

Any element shares an equivalence class modulo I with its reduced form. Thus the canonical images of the reduced words in k\langle X\rangle/I form a k-spanning set.

Level justification

Descend through a compatible word order and preserve congruence modulo the two-sided ideal. The finite descent is elementary, but free associative algebras, quotient modules and noncommutative ideal reductions together exceed a confidently small extension.
