Statement

On the Borel sets of [0,1], let \mu be Lebesgue length and \nu(A)=|A\cap\{0.1,0.2,\ldots,0.9\}|. Then \nu is not absolutely continuous with respect to \mu, and has no density with respect to \mu.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement uses measure-integral densities and null-set equality, beyond the calculus/linear-algebra baseline.

Proof 1: Test the zero-length atoms

Each of the listed singleton points has length zero but positive \nu measure. This violates absolute continuity and prevents a measure-integral density representation.

Level justification

The singleton obstruction is immediate, but the full claim concerns Borel measures, absolute continuity and measure-integral density representations. Retain beyond undergraduate provisionally because understanding that framework requires more than learning the singleton calculation.
