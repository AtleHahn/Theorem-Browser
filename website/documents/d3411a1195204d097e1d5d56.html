Statement

Let K be a field, n\ge1 and d\ge0 integers. The vector space of homogeneous degree-d polynomials over K in n+1 variables has dimension \binom{n+d}{d}. Its projectivization, whose points are nonzero forms up to multiplication by a nonzero scalar, has projective dimension \binom{n+d}{d}-1. Here projective dimension is the dimension of the underlying vector space minus one.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra supplies polynomial coefficient spaces. Projectivization is explicitly the set of nonzero vectors modulo nonzero scalar, with dimension one less. This one quotient-coordinate definition suffices for the parameter-space claim.

Proof 1: Count monomial coefficients and quotient by scalar — outline

The number of monomials of degree d in n+1 variables (n-dimensional projective space has n+1 homogeneous coordinates) is the multiset coefficient

\left(\!\!{n+1\choose d}\!\!\right)={n+d\choose d},

from which 1 is subtracted because of projectivization: multiplying a polynomial by a nonzero constant does not change its zeros.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Count the degree-d monomial basis by stars and bars and subtract one using the explicit projective-dimension convention. Undergraduate vector spaces and polynomial algebra suffice.
