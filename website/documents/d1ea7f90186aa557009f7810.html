Statement

In the hyperbolic plane, let \ell_1 and \ell_2 be distinct complete geodesic lines which are ultraparallel: they are disjoint and have no common ideal endpoint on the boundary at infinity. Then there is exactly one complete geodesic line meeting both \ell_1 and \ell_2 at right angles.

Proof 1: Concentric-circle normalization (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Common perpendicular theorem; linked orthogonal-circle TheoremsO2–O3).

In the concentric normalization, the boundary circle becomes a line through the common center. The unique perpendicular line through that center is orthogonal to both concentric circles. Each of its intersections with either circle has exactly one point in the transformed disk half-plane, so it gives an actual common perpendicular, not merely orthogonal extensions. This supplies existence as well as the uniqueness asserted in Theorem O3.
