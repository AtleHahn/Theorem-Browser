Statement

For every integer n\geq2, there are n nonatomic probability measures on a circle such that a union of arcs having common value 1/n requires at least n-1 arcs.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Nonatomic circle measures assign no mass to single points; finite arc unions and common mass are a limited extension of interval integration and probability.

Proof 1: Separate support neighborhoods by too many length gaps — outline

  • Normalize the circle to have length 1. Choose {\displaystyle (n-1)(n+1)} equally-spaced points along the circle; call them {\displaystyle P_{1},\ldots ,P_{(n-1)(n+1)}}.
  • Define {\displaystyle n-1} measures in the following way. Measure {\displaystyle i} is distributed without atoms in small disjoint neighbourhoods of the following {\displaystyle (n+1)} points: {\displaystyle P_{i},P_{i+(n-1)},\ldots ,P_{i+n(n-1)}}. So, near each point {\displaystyle P_{i+k(n-1)}}, there is a fraction {\displaystyle 1/(n+1)} of the measure {\displaystyle u_{i}}.
  • Define the {\displaystyle n}-th measure as proportional to the length measure.
  • Every subset whose consensus value is {\displaystyle 1/n}, must meet at least two support neighborhoods for each of the first {\displaystyle n-1} measures (since the value near each single point is {\displaystyle 1/(n+1)} which is slightly less than the required {\displaystyle 1/n}). Hence, it must meet at least {\displaystyle 2(n-1)} support neighborhoods.
  • On the other hand, every subset whose consensus value is {\displaystyle 1/n}, must have total length {\displaystyle 1/n} (because of the {\displaystyle n}-th measure). The spacing between the points is {\displaystyle 1/{\big (}(n+1)(n-1){\big )}}; with the neighborhoods sufficiently small, bridging n gaps would require more than length 1/n, hence the subset can bridge at most {\displaystyle n-1} gaps.
  • The consensus subset must meet {\displaystyle 2(n-1)} support neighborhoods but bridge at most {\displaystyle n-1} gaps; hence it must contain at least {\displaystyle n-1} intervals.

Level justification

Distribute finitely many mass packets around the circle and compare the number of visited supports with the number of affordable gaps. Nonatomic measures here can be ordinary constant densities on short arcs; this is a small extension of interval probabilities, without abstract measure integration.
