Statement

Let f be a nonzero complex polynomial with no zero on a positively oriented simple closed contour C. Let z_1,\ldots,z_p list its zeros inside C with multiplicity, and let k be a nonnegative integer. Then

{\displaystyle {\frac {1}{2\pi i}}\oint _{C}z^{k}{\frac {f'(z)}{f(z)}}\,dz=z_{1}^{k}+z_{2}^{k}+\cdots +z_{p}^{k},}
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Polynomial roots, multiplicities and derivatives are familiar algebra/calculus. The contour integral is an ordinary parametrized complex integral around the specified curve; understanding the identity requires this direct extension, not the residue theorem used to prove it.

Proof 1: Monomial specialization of the generalized argument principle

Dependency

For a meromorphic function f and analytic g on a region containing a positively oriented simple contour C and its interior, with no zero or pole of f on C, the generalized argument principle gives \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_C g(z)f'(z)/f(z)\,dz=\sum_a g(a)-\sum_b g(b), counting zeros and poles with their multiplicities and orders.

Specialization

Apply this formula with g(z)=z^k. A polynomial has no poles, and its zeros inside C are z_1,\ldots,z_p counted with multiplicity. Thus

{\displaystyle {\frac {1}{2\pi i}}\oint _{C}z^{k}{\frac {f'(z)}{f(z)}}\,dz=z_{1}^{k}+z_{2}^{k}+\cdots +z_{p}^{k},}
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Substitute the monomial into the fully stated weighted argument principle and omit poles because the function is a polynomial. Undergraduate complex analysis suffices.
