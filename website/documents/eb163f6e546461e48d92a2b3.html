Statement

Let ABCD be a convex tangential quadrilateral, meaning it has an incircle tangent to all four sides, with at most one pair of parallel opposite sides. Let E,F be the midpoints of AC,BD, and P the incircle center. Then P lies on the line EF.

High school only

Proof 1: Equal opposite area sums and Anne’s theorem

Write a=AB, b=BC, c=CD and d=DA. Newton's theorem can easily be derived from Anne's theorem considering that in tangential quadrilaterals the combined lengths of opposite sides are equal (Pitot theorem: a+c=b+d). According to Anne's theorem, showing that the combined areas of opposite triangles PAD and PBC and the combined areas of triangles PAB and PCD are equal is sufficient to ensure that the interior point P lies on EF in this non-parallelogram quadrilateral. Let r be the radius of the incircle, then r is also the altitude of all four triangles. Writing A(\triangle) for a triangle's area,

\begin{aligned} A(\triangle PAB)+A(\triangle PCD) &=\frac12ra+\frac12rc=\frac12r(a+c)\\ &=\frac12r(b+d)=\frac12rb+\frac12rd\\ &=A(\triangle PBC)+A(\triangle PAD). \end{aligned}
