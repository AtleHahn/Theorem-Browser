Statement

Let \mathbb Z_2 be the ring of 2-adic integers and define

G(x_1,x_2,x_3)=\sum_{i=1}^3x_i^4-\sum_{i<j}x_i^2x_j^2-x_1x_2x_3(x_1+x_2+x_3).

For six triples \mathbf x,\mathbf y,\mathbf z,\mathbf u,\mathbf v,\mathbf w\in\mathbb Z_2^3, the quartic form

G(\mathbf x)+G(\mathbf y)+G(\mathbf z)+4G(\mathbf u)+4G(\mathbf v)+4G(\mathbf w)

is zero only when all 18 coordinates are zero. Thus it is a quartic form in 18>4^2 variables with no nontrivial 2-adic integer zero.

Level justification

Proof 1: Congruence obstruction (outline)

The form G is congruent to 1 modulo 4 if some coordinate is odd, and to 0 modulo 16 otherwise. It follows from these congruences that the displayed quartic form has no nontrivial zero over the 2-adic integers.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The congruence descent requires interpreting arbitrary 2-adic integer coordinates, finite minimum valuation and division to a primitive vector. Although the modular obstruction is simple, the p-adic framework is not confidently a small addition to ordinary undergraduate algebra.
