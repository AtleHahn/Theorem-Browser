Statement

For \mathbb K=\mathbb R or \mathbb C, let c_0(\mathbb K) be the space of scalar sequences tending to zero, with norm \|x\|_\infty=\sup_i|x_i|, and let e_i denote the sequence with entry 1 at i and 0 elsewhere. For every bilinear form B:c_0(\mathbb K)\times c_0(\mathbb K)\to\mathbb K,

\left(\sum_{i,j\ge1}|B(e_i,e_j)|^{4/3}\right)^{3/4}\le\sqrt2\,\|B\|,\qquad \|B\|=\sup_{\|x\|_\infty,\|y\|_\infty\le1}|B(x,y)|.

Nonnegative sums and the norm may be +\infty; the assertion is informative for bounded forms. The exponent is optimal: for any 0<p<4/3 there is no finite constant valid for all bounded forms with the left side replaced by (\sum_{i,j}|B(e_i,e_j)|^p)^{1/p}. For real scalars, \sqrt2 is also the smallest possible constant.
