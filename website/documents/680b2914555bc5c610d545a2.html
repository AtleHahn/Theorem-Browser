Statement

Let A,B,C be distinct points in a real inner product space, set O=(A+C)/2 and R=\|A-C\|/2. Then \langle A-B,C-B\rangle=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\|B-O\|=R.

Proof 1: One dot-product identity proves both directions

Translate O to the origin and use the same letters for the translated vectors. Then C=-A and R=\|A\|. The dot-product calculation gives \langle A-B,B-C\rangle=\langle A-B,B+A\rangle=\|A\|^2-\|B\|^2. Changing the sign of the second factor does not affect whether the expression vanishes. Hence orthogonality holds exactly when the two nonnegative norms agree, which is the stated sphere condition. Both directions follow from this single identity.
