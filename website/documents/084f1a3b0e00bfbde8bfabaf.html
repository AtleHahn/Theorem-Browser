Statement

Let a<b and f:[a,b]\to\mathbb R be continuous. Some c\in(a,b) satisfies \int_a^b f(x)\,dx=f(c)(b-a).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Continuous real functions; Definite integrals; Intermediate values. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Trap the integral average between extrema

Let m and M be the minimum and maximum values of f on [a,b], respectively. Then

m\leq \frac{1}{b-a}\int_a^b f(x)\,dx\leq M.

By the intermediate value theorem, since f maps [a,b] onto [m,M], there exists a c with the required property.

To ensure that c can be chosen in (a,b), if f is constant, any interior point works. Otherwise each strict deviation from the minimum or maximum persists, by continuity, on a subinterval of positive length. Thus the two inequalities are strict, and the intermediate value theorem between points attaining the extrema gives an interior point c.
