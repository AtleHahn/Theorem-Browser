An exponential-power product density has independent coordinates

Statement

Let n\ge1 be an integer and \alpha>1. Suppose a real random vector (X_1,\ldots,X_n) has joint Lebesgue density f(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=C\exp(-\sum_{j=1}^n|x_j|^\alpha), where C>0 normalizes its integral to one. Then X_1,\ldots,X_n are independent.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The joint density is an explicit continuous nonnegative function on real coordinate space. Probability is its integral and independence means products of coordinate event probabilities. These definitions extend undergraduate multivariable integration; Lebesgue terminology here does not require learning arbitrary conditional measures.

Proof 1: Factor the exponential density — outline

The density factorizes as f(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=C\prod_{j=1}^n\exp(-|x_j|^\alpha). By the product-density criterion for independence, the coordinates are independent.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The joint density is an explicit continuous function on real coordinate space. Factor the exponential and apply the supplied product-density criterion, with normalization absorbing the one-variable integrals. Ordinary multivariate density probability suffices despite the Lebesgue terminology.
