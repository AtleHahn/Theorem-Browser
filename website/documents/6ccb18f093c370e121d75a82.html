Statement

Work in classical first-order logic over nonempty domains, with a standard sound proof calculus, such as Hilbert–Ackermann. A formula is refutable if its negation is provable. Satisfiability of an open formula allows an assignment to its free variables. The following global properties are equivalent: every valid formula is provable; every formula is refutable or satisfiable.

Proof 1: Negation and double-negation elimination

If every valid formula is provable, and \varphi is not satisfiable in any structure, then \neg\varphi is valid in all structures and therefore provable, thus \varphi is refutable and every formula is refutable or satisfiable.

If on the other hand every formula is refutable or satisfiable and \varphi is valid in all structures, then \neg\varphi is not satisfiable in any structure and therefore refutable; then \neg\neg\varphi is provable and then so is \varphi, by classical double-negation elimination. Thus every valid formula is provable.
