Statement

Let X be a reflexive real or complex Banach space, T>0, and \varphi:[0,T]\to X absolutely continuous in norm. Then its norm derivative \varphi'(t)=\lim_{h\to0}(\varphi(t+h)-\varphi(t))/h exists for Lebesgue-almost every t\in(0,T), is Bochner integrable, and

\varphi(t)=\varphi(0)+\int_0^t\varphi'(s)\,ds\quad(0\le t\le T).

A Banach space is complete in its norm; reflexive means that the canonical map into its double continuous dual is onto. Absolute continuity means that for every \varepsilon>0 there is \delta>0 such that any finite pairwise disjoint intervals (a_j,b_j)\subseteq[0,T] of total length less than \delta satisfy \sum_j\|\varphi(b_j)-\varphi(a_j)\|<\varepsilon. Bochner integrability means approximation by measurable simple X-valued functions in the norm \int_0^T\|g(s)\|\,ds; the integral is the norm limit of their integrals.
