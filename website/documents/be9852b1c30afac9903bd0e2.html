Statement

Work over a field of characteristic zero. The surface singularity x^2+y^3z+z^3=0 has, after blowing up the origin, a chart with equation x^2+y^2z+yz^3=0. Both equations have multiplicity two and contain monomials of degrees two, three and four.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Affine algebraic surfaces; Point blowups; Local multiplicity; Monomial degrees. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Inspect the supplied blowup equation — outline

The isolated singularity of x^2+y^3z+z^3=0 at the origin gives an example. Blowing it up gives, in a chart, the singularity x^2+y^2z+yz^3=0. It is not immediately obvious that this new singularity is better, as both singularities have multiplicity 2 and are given by the sum of monomials of degrees 2, 3, and 4.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the supplied strict-transform chart and compare lowest monomial degrees. The full claim involves surface singularities and a point blowup, whose geometric chart interpretation requires advanced algebraic geometry despite elementary degree counting.
