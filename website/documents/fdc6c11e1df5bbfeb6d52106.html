Statement

Let k=\mathbb F_q be a finite field, \overline k its algebraic closure, and X\subseteq\mathbb A_k^m an affine variety defined over k. On its geometric points define F_q(x_1,\ldots,x_m)=(x_1^q,\ldots,x_m^q). Then \operatorname{Fix}(F_q)=X(k).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory abstract algebra supplies fields and polynomial equations. For this formulation the additional notions are an algebraic closure as an ambient field containing algebraic roots, an affine variety as a simultaneous polynomial solution set, and rational points as solutions whose coordinates lie in the base field. The Frobenius map is explicitly coordinatewise exponentiation. These concrete definitions suffice to interpret every hypothesis and the fixed-point equality within about 30 minutes; no field norm, extension-degree computation, scheme or geometric structure theory is needed.

Proof 1: Coordinate fixed equations and the finite-field fixed elements

Use the finite-field characterization k=\{a\in\overline{k}:a^q=a\}. The Frobenius endomorphism F_q maps a point with coordinates {\displaystyle x_{1},\ldots ,x_{m}} to the point with coordinates {\displaystyle x_{1}^{q},\ldots ,x_{m}^{q}}. Thus the fixed points of {\displaystyle F_{q}} are exactly the points of {\displaystyle X} with coordinates in {\displaystyle k}; the set of such points is denoted by {\displaystyle X(k)}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof is entirely coordinatewise: a point is fixed precisely when each coordinate satisfies a^q=a, and the cited field characterization says exactly that these coordinates lie in k. An affine variety is used only as a polynomial solution set and X(k) as its solutions with k-valued coordinates. From introductory field algebra, the definitions of an algebraic closure, polynomial solution sets and rational points are a small extension; no schemes, cohomology or general algebraic geometry are needed.

Statement used:

Inside an algebraic closure of the field with q elements, the solutions of a^q=a are exactly the elements of that finite field.

Application in proof:

Apply separately to every coordinate of a geometric point of X. The fixed-coordinate condition is equivalent to all coordinates belonging to k, which defines X(k).
