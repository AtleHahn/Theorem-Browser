Statement

Let a<b, R,C>0 and N\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}. Suppose f_+ and f_- are holomorphic respectively on (a,b)+i(0,R) and (a,b)+i(-R,0) and satisfy |f_\pm(x+iy)|<C|y|^{-N}. For every \varphi\in C_c^\infty((a,b)), the limits \langle T_\pm,\varphi\rangle=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\int_a^b f_\pm(x\pm i\varepsilon)\varphi(x)\,dx exist and define distributions on (a,b).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The boundary-limit statement requires distribution theory and complex analysis.

Proof 1: Polynomial growth gives distributional boundary values — outline

Under the polynomial-growth hypothesis, f_\pm is the (N+1)st complex derivative of a holomorphic function which extends continuously to the boundary. This continuous primitive gives the distributional boundary limit of f_\pm.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Use a continuous-boundary holomorphic primitive and transfer derivatives onto compactly supported test functions. Distributional boundary values and continuity of distributional differentiation require graduate analysis.
