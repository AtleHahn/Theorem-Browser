Statement

Let X be a compact complex manifold without boundary and V a holomorphic vector bundle on X. Then \chi(X,V)=\int_X\operatorname{ch}(V)\operatorname{Td}(TX), where \chi(X,V)=\sum_p(-1)^p\dim H^p(X,V) is the holomorphic Euler characteristic (coherent cohomology), ch is the Chern character, and Td is the Todd class of the complex tangent bundle.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves compact complex manifolds; holomorphic vector bundles; Chern character and Todd class; Coherent cohomology and holomorphic Euler characteristic; Evaluation of characteristic classes on the fundamental class. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Dolbeault index and characteristic roots

Index formula (dependency)

Use the Atiyah–Singer formula for an elliptic operator on a real 2n-dimensional compact manifold: \operatorname{ind}D=(-1)^n\int_X[(\operatorname{ch}E-\operatorname{ch}F)/e(TX)]\operatorname{Td}(TX\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C). Characteristic-root quotients in the ensuing calculation are formally canceled.

Dolbeault operator

Take X to be a complex manifold of (complex) dimension n with a holomorphic vector bundle V. We let the vector bundles E and F be the sums of the bundles of differential forms with coefficients in V of type (0, i) with i even or odd, and we let the differential operator D be the sum

{\displaystyle {\overline {\partial }}+{\overline {\partial }}^{*}}

restricted to E.

This derivation of the Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem is more natural if we use the index theorem for elliptic complexes rather than elliptic operators. We can take the complex to be

{\displaystyle 0\rightarrow V\rightarrow V\otimes \Lambda ^{0,1}T^{*}(X)\rightarrow V\otimes \Lambda ^{0,2}T^{*}(X)\rightarrow \dotsm }

with the differential given by {\displaystyle {\overline {\partial }}}. Then the *i'*th cohomology group is just the coherent cohomology group Hi(X, V), so the analytical index of this complex is the holomorphic Euler characteristic of V:

{\displaystyle \operatorname {index} (D)=\sum _{p}(-1)^{p}\dim H^{p}(X,V)=\chi (X,V)}

Since we are dealing with complex bundles, the computation of the topological index is simpler. Using Chern roots x_i of the holomorphic tangent bundle, the Euler class is given by {\textstyle e(TX)=\prod _{i}^{n}x_{i}(TX)} and

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\operatorname {ch} \left(\sum _{j}^{n}(-1)^{j}V\otimes \Lambda ^{j}{\overline {T^{*}X}}\right)&=\operatorname {ch} (V)\prod _{j}^{n}\left(1-e^{x_{j}}\right)(TX)\\\operatorname {Td} (TX\otimes \mathbb {C} )=\operatorname {Td} (TX)\operatorname {Td} \left({\overline {TX}}\right)&=\prod _{i}^{n}{\frac {x_{i}}{1-e^{-x_{i}}}}\prod _{j}^{n}{\frac {-x_{j}}{1-e^{x_{j}}}}(TX)\end{aligned}}}

Applying the index theorem, we obtain the Hirzebruch-Riemann-Roch theorem:

{\displaystyle \chi (X,V)=\int _{X}\operatorname {ch} (V)\operatorname {Td} (TX)}
