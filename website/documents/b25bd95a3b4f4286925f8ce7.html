Statement

Let (\Omega,\Sigma) be a measurable space and let \mu,\nu be sigma-finite signed measures on it. There are unique sigma-finite signed measures \nu_0,\nu_1 such that

\nu=\nu_0+\nu_1,\qquad\nu_0\ll\mu,\qquad\nu_1\perp\mu.

A signed measure is countably additive, takes values in the extended real numbers, and does not take both +\infty and -\infty. Write |\eta| for its total variation: on a measurable set it is the supremum of sums of |\eta(E_j)| over finite measurable partitions. Sigma-finite means that \Omega is a countable union of measurable sets of finite total variation. The signed-measure notation \nu_0\ll\mu means |\nu_0|\ll|\mu|: every |\mu|-null set is |\nu_0|-null. The notation \nu_1\perp\mu means that there is N\in\Sigma with |\mu|(N)=0 and |\nu_1|(\Omega\setminus N)=0.
