Polynomial space permits computations that no nondeterministic logarithmic-space procedure can perform. This gives an unconditional hierarchy of computational capacities, showing that feasibility depends on available memory as well as on whether a problem is computable at all.

Statement

With \mathrm{NL}=\mathrm{NSPACE}(\log n) and \mathrm{PSPACE}=\bigcup_{k\ge1}\mathrm{DSPACE}(n^k), one has \mathrm{NL}\subsetneq\mathrm{PSPACE}.

Proof 1: Separate squared-logarithmic from linear deterministic space

Savitch’s theorem gives \mathrm{NL}\subseteq\mathrm{DSPACE}(\log^2n). The deterministic space hierarchy theorem gives \mathrm{DSPACE}(\log^2n)\subsetneq\mathrm{DSPACE}(n). Since \mathrm{DSPACE}(n)\subseteq\mathrm{PSPACE}, the desired inclusion is strict.
