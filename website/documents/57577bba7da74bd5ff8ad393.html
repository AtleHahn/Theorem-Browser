Statement

The algebraic closure \overline{\mathbb Q} cannot be written NM with both N and M proper subfields of \overline{\mathbb Q} that are Galois over \mathbb Q.

Proof 1: Apply the Haran–Jarden condition to Q — outline

The source invokes the Haran–Jarden condition: the compositum of two nonnested Galois extensions of a Hilbertian field is Hilbertian. The field \mathbb Q is Hilbertian by Hilbert’s irreducibility theorem. If two proper Galois subfields had compositum \overline{\mathbb Q}, neither could contain the other, since then their compositum would be the larger proper subfield. Haran–Jarden would make \overline{\mathbb Q} Hilbertian, contradicting the standard fact that an algebraically closed field is not Hilbertian.
