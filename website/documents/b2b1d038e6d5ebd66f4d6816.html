Statement

If \mathsf{NP}\subseteq\mathsf{P/poly}, then \mathsf{AM}=\mathsf{MA}. In the two-message polynomial-time verifier formulations, AM permits a witness after public random bits, whereas MA commits the witness before those bits. Completeness is at least 2/3 and soundness at most 1/3.

Proof 1: Send the witness circuit before the verifier’s random bits

Suppose that L is in \mathsf{AM}, with a polynomial-time predicate \phi(x,y,z), where x consists of uniformly random polynomially many bits and y is a polynomial-length witness. Thus

z\in L\implies\Pr_x[\exists y\,\phi(x,y,z)]\geq\frac23,z\notin L\implies\Pr_x[\exists y\,\phi(x,y,z)]\leq\frac13.

By Cook–Levin and SAT self-reducibility, the assumption \mathsf{NP}\subseteq\mathsf{P/poly} supplies a polynomial-size circuit D_n that outputs a satisfying witness if it exists. Rewrite \exists y\,\phi(x,y,z) using this circuit:

z\in L\implies\Pr_x[\phi(x,D_n(x,z),z)]\geq\frac23,z\notin L\implies\Pr_x[\phi(x,D_n(x,z),z)]\leq\frac13.

Since D_n can be guessed,

z\in L\implies\exists D\ \Pr_x[\phi(x,D(x,z),z)]\geq\frac23,z\notin L\implies\forall D\ \Pr_x[\phi(x,D(x,z),z)]\leq\frac13,

where the circuits have the polynomial size bound just obtained. This proves L is in the smaller class \mathsf{MA}: Merlin sends D before Arthur chooses x. The standard reverse containment \mathsf{MA}\subseteq\mathsf{AM} gives equality.
