Statement

Let H be a real normed vector space whose norm satisfies \|x+y\|^2+\|x-y\|^2=2\|x\|^2+2\|y\|^2. Then there is a unique real inner product inducing this norm, namely B(x,y)=\tfrac14(\|x+y\|^2-\|x-y\|^2).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Inner products; Norms; Linear algebra. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Construct the polarization form and verify its axioms — outline

By expanding \|x+y\|^2 and \|x-y\|^2 using an inducing inner product, if the norm is described by an inner product (as we hope), then it must satisfy

{\displaystyle \langle x,\ y\rangle ={\frac {1}{4}}\left(\|x+y\|^{2}-\|x-y\|^{2}\right)\quad {\text{ for all }}x,y\in H,}

which, with \langle x,y\rangle=B(x,y), may serve as a definition of the unique candidate {\displaystyle \langle \cdot ,\cdot \rangle } for the role of a suitable inner product. Thus, the uniqueness is guaranteed.

It remains to prove that this formula indeed defines an inner product and that this inner product induces the norm {\displaystyle \|\cdot \|.} Explicitly, the following will be shown:

  1. {\displaystyle \langle x,x\rangle =\|x\|^{2},\quad x\in H}
  2. {\displaystyle \langle x,y\rangle =\langle y,x\rangle ,\quad x,y\in H}
  3. {\displaystyle \langle x+z,y\rangle =\langle x,y\rangle +\langle z,y\rangle \quad {\text{ for all }}x,y,z\in H,}
  4. {\displaystyle \langle \alpha x,y\rangle =\alpha \langle x,y\rangle \quad {\text{ for all }}x,y\in H{\text{ and all }}\alpha \in \mathbb {R} }

(This axiomatization omits positivity, which is implied by (1) and the fact that {\displaystyle \|\cdot \|} is a norm.)

For properties (1) and (2), substitute: {\textstyle \langle x,x\rangle ={\frac {1}{4}}\left(\|x+x\|^{2}-\|x-x\|^{2}\right)=\|x\|^{2},} and {\displaystyle \|x-y\|^{2}=\|y-x\|^{2}.}

For property (3), it is convenient to work in reverse. It remains to show that

{\displaystyle \|x+z+y\|^{2}-\|x+z-y\|^{2}{\overset {?}{=}}\|x+y\|^{2}-\|x-y\|^{2}+\|z+y\|^{2}-\|z-y\|^{2}}

or equivalently,

{\displaystyle 2\left(\|x+z+y\|^{2}+\|x-y\|^{2}\right)-2\left(\|x+z-y\|^{2}+\|x+y\|^{2}\right){\overset {?}{=}}2\|z+y\|^{2}-2\|z-y\|^{2}.}

Now apply the parallelogram identity:

{\displaystyle 2\|x+z+y\|^{2}+2\|x-y\|^{2}=\|2x+z\|^{2}+\|2y+z\|^{2}}{\displaystyle 2\|x+z-y\|^{2}+2\|x+y\|^{2}=\|2x+z\|^{2}+\|z-2y\|^{2}}

Thus it remains to verify:

{\displaystyle {\cancel {\|2x+z\|^{2}}}+\|2y+z\|^{2}-({\cancel {\|2x+z\|^{2}}}+\|z-2y\|^{2}){\overset {?}{{}={}}}2\|z+y\|^{2}-2\|z-y\|^{2}}{\displaystyle \|2y+z\|^{2}-\|z-2y\|^{2}{\overset {?}{=}}2\|z+y\|^{2}-2\|z-y\|^{2}}

But the latter claim can be verified by subtracting the following two further applications of the parallelogram identity:

{\displaystyle \|2y+z\|^{2}+\|z\|^{2}=2\|z+y\|^{2}+2\|y\|^{2}}{\displaystyle \|z-2y\|^{2}+\|z\|^{2}=2\|z-y\|^{2}+2\|y\|^{2}}

Thus (3) holds.

It can be verified by induction that (3) implies (4), as long as {\displaystyle \alpha \in \mathbb {Z} .} But "(4) when {\displaystyle \alpha \in \mathbb {Z} }" implies "(4) when {\displaystyle \alpha \in \mathbb {Q} }". And any positive-definite, real-valued, {\displaystyle \mathbb {Q} }-bilinear form satisfies the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, so that {\displaystyle \langle \cdot ,\cdot \rangle } is continuous. Thus {\displaystyle \langle \cdot ,\cdot \rangle } must be {\displaystyle \mathbb {R} }-linear as well.
