Statement

Let X be a connected compact Riemann surface of genus g. A divisor is a finite integer sum of points, its degree is the sum of its coefficients, and K is the divisor of a nonzero meromorphic one-form. For a divisor D, let L(D) consist of zero and the meromorphic functions h with (h)+D\ge0, and put \ell(D)=\dim_{\mathbb C}L(D). If \deg(D)\ge2g-1, then \ell(D)=\deg(D)-g+1.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The degree threshold and section dimension belong to complex-analytic divisor theory.

Proof 1: Eliminate the negative-degree correction

Use Riemann–Roch in the form \ell(D)-\ell(K-D)=\deg D-g+1, together with the fact that a negative-degree divisor has no nonzero sections.

On a general Riemann surface of genus {\displaystyle g}, {\displaystyle K} has degree {\displaystyle 2g-2}, independently of the meromorphic form chosen to represent the divisor. This follows from putting {\displaystyle D=K} in Riemann–Roch, using \ell(0)=1 and \ell(K)=g. In particular, as long as {\displaystyle D} has degree at least {\displaystyle 2g-1}, the correction term is 0, so that

{\displaystyle \ell (D)=\deg(D)-g+1}.

Level justification

Apply Riemann–Roch twice to identify canonical degree and eliminate the negative-degree term. Compact Riemann surfaces, meromorphic forms and divisors are substantial graduate prerequisites.
