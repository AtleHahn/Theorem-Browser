Statement

On a planar open set, let a smooth positive metric have the form ds^2=\lambda|dz+\mu\,d\bar z|^2 with \lambda>0 and |\mu|<1. Suppose a smooth w=u+iv has nonzero real Jacobian and satisfies w_{\bar z}=\mu w_z, where w_z=(w_x-iw_y)/2 and w_{\bar z}=(w_x+iw_y)/2. Then (u,v) are local coordinates and ds^2=\rho(du^2+dv^2),\qquad\rho=\lambda/|w_z|^2>0.

Proof 1: Substituting the Beltrami equation into the metric

In isothermal coordinates (u, v) the metric should take the form

{\displaystyle ds^{2}=\rho (du^{2}+dv^{2})}

with ρ > 0 smooth. The complex coordinate w = u + i v satisfies

{\displaystyle \rho \,|dw|^{2}=\rho |w_{z}|^{2}\left|dz+{w_{\overline {z}} \over w_{z}}\,d{\overline {z}}\right|^{2},}

so that the coordinates (u, v) will be isothermal locally provided the Beltrami equation

{\displaystyle {\partial w \over \partial {\overline {z}}}=\mu {\partial w \over \partial z}}

has a locally diffeomorphic solution, i.e. a solution with non-vanishing Jacobian.

Under the stated nonzero-Jacobian assumption, w_z\ne0. Taking \rho=\lambda/|w_z|^2 in the displayed identity gives the required metric. The inverse function theorem supplies the local coordinates.

Level justification

Everything is expressed in real coordinates on a plane domain. Expand dw=w_z dz+w_bar z dbar z, substitute the given equation, and divide the metric coefficient by |w_z|^2. The nonzero Jacobian gives local invertibility by the ordinary inverse function theorem. Multivariable calculus, complex-number notation and quadratic forms suffice; no general surface theory or existence theorem for the Beltrami equation is invoked.
