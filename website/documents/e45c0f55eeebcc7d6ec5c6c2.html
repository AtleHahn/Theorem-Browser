Statement

Let \Sigma_n be the permutation group on n letters, included in \Sigma_{n+1} by fixing the last letter, and let \Sigma_\infty=\bigcup_{n\ge1}\Sigma_n. Write H_k(G) for integral group homology. For k\ge0 and n\ge\max(1,2k), the inclusion induces an isomorphism H_k(\Sigma_n)\cong H_k(\Sigma_\infty).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Group homology and its stabilization under inclusions require substantial homological-algebra background. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Pass Nakaoka stability to the union (outline)

The infinite symmetric group \Sigma_\infty is the union of the finite symmetric groups \Sigma_n. Nakaoka’s theorem implies that its group homology is the stable homology of \Sigma_n: for n\ge2k, H_k(\Sigma_\infty)\cong H_k(\Sigma_n).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Pass homological stability through the increasing union. Group homology, induced homomorphisms and direct limits require homological algebra.
