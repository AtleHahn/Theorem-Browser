Uniform tail bound for an absolutely summable trigonometric series

Statement

Let P>0 and let (c_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z} be complex coefficients with \sum_n|c_n|<\infty. Put s(x)=\sum_nc_ne^{2\pi inx/P} and s_N(x)=\sum_{|n|\le N}c_ne^{2\pi inx/P}. Then \sup_{x\in\mathbb R}|s(x)-s_N(x)|\le\sum_{|n|>N}|c_n|\to0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Complex exponential series; Absolute convergence; Uniform convergence. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Control the function tail by the numerical absolute tail — outline

In the absolutely summable case, the inequality:

{\displaystyle \sup _{x}{\bigl |}s(x)-s_{N}(x){\bigr |}\leq \sum _{|n|>N}{\bigl |}c_n{\bigr |}}

proves uniform convergence.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The modulus of each complex exponential is one. Apply the triangle inequality to finite tails and pass to the absolutely convergent sum, obtaining a bound independent of x. The numerical tail tends to zero, which is exactly uniform convergence. This uses introductory series analysis and complex arithmetic, not spectral or Fourier-transform theory.
