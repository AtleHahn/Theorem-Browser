Statement

Let T be the free term algebra on a variable set V. Every map \sigma:V\to T extends uniquely to a term-algebra homomorphism \widehat\sigma:T\to T, whose value on a term is its substitution instance.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Terms are finite expressions in operation symbols; free algebra and homomorphism add a limited universal-extension definition to undergraduate algebra.

Mathematical Field
Universal algebraMathematical logic

Proof 1: Apply the universal property with target the term algebra

The set T of terms is the free term algebra over the set V of variables, hence, by its universal property, for each substitution mapping \sigma:V\to T there is a unique homomorphism \widehat\sigma:T\to T that agrees with \sigma on V\subseteq T. The application of \sigma to a term t is then viewed as applying the function \widehat\sigma to the argument t.

Level justification

Apply the explicitly stated free-algebra extension property with target T. From elementary algebraic operations and homomorphisms, finite term expressions and their operation-preserving extension are a small related extension; no general category theory is used.
