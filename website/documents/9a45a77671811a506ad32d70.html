Statement

Let a\in\mathbb R\setminus\mathbb Q and \mathbb T^2=\{(z,w)\in\mathbb C^2:|z|=|w|=1\}. The map

f:\mathbb R\longrightarrow\mathbb T^2,\qquad f(t)=(e^{2\pi it},e^{2\pi iat})

is an analytic injective immersion whose inverse onto its image with the relative topology is not continuous. In particular it is not an embedding.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Immersions, manifold topology and embeddings are beyond calculus and basic linear algebra.

Proof 1: Compare the parameter and relative topologies (outline)

Put H=f(\mathbb R). Use the irrational-winding fact that H is dense in \mathbb T^2 and that in the relative topology a small open subset of H is composed of infinitely many almost parallel line segments on the surface of the torus. In the topology transported from the parameter t\in\mathbb R, the small open sets are single line segments on the surface of the torus.

While the map f:\mathbb R\to H is an analytic bijection, its inverse is not continuous. That is, if U\subset\mathbb R is a small open interval -\varepsilon<t<\varepsilon, there is no open neighborhood V\subset H of the identity in the relative topology with f^{-1}(V)\subset U, due to the appearance of the sets V. With the topology transported from \mathbb R, the injection H\to\mathbb T^2 is an analytic injective immersion, but not a homeomorphism onto its image, hence not an embedding.

Level justification

Use returning local torus segments to distinguish the relative topology from the parameter topology, within the claimed analytic immersion. Immersions and analytic manifold structures together with recurrence form a substantial prerequisite burden; retain beyond undergraduate conservatively.
