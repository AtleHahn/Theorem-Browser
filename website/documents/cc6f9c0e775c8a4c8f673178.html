Statement

Let U\subseteq\mathbb R^n be open and let f:U\to\mathbb R be real analytic, meaning locally equal to a convergent real power series. Suppose its zero set Z=\{x\in U:f(x)=0\} is nonempty.

For every compact set K\subseteq U, there are constants \alpha>0 and C>0 such that

\operatorname{dist}(x,Z)^\alpha\leq C|f(x)|\qquad(x\in K),

where \operatorname{dist}(x,Z)=\inf_{z\in Z}\|x-z\| and \|\cdot\| is the Euclidean norm.

The gradient form asserts that for every p\in U, there are an open neighborhood W\subseteq U of p, a constant c>0, and 0<\theta<1 such that

|f(x)-f(p)|^\theta\leq c\|\nabla f(x)\|\qquad(x\in W).

Here \nabla f is the vector of first partial derivatives.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Undergraduate analysis and multivariable calculus suffice. Real analyticity requires only the small extension from familiar Taylor series to convergent multivariable series; distance, compactness and the two quantified inequalities use baseline analysis.
