Statement

Let U\subseteq\mathbb R^n be open and let f:U\to\mathbb R be real analytic, meaning locally equal to a convergent real power series. Suppose its zero set Z=\{x\in U:f(x)=0\} is nonempty.

For every compact set K\subseteq U, there are constants \alpha>0 and C>0 such that

\operatorname{dist}(x,Z)^\alpha\leq C|f(x)|\qquad(x\in K),

where \operatorname{dist}(x,Z)=\inf_{z\in Z}\|x-z\| and \|\cdot\| is the Euclidean norm.

The gradient form asserts that for every p\in U, there are an open neighborhood W\subseteq U of p, a constant c>0, and 0<\theta<1 such that

|f(x)-f(p)|^\theta\leq c\|\nabla f(x)\|\qquad(x\in W).

Here \nabla f is the vector of first partial derivatives.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Undergraduate analysis and multivariable calculus suffice. Real analyticity requires only the small extension from familiar Taylor series to convergent multivariable series; distance, compactness and the two quantified inequalities use baseline analysis.

Proof 1: Resolution and finite-fiber gradient estimates (external)

The following editorial argument completes the stated scope. A supporting source is Resolution of singularities and geometric proofs of the Łojasiewicz inequalities (Theorem 1 proof §4.4 pp.17–18; Corollary 4 proof §5.1 pp.20–21, v13 (2020).).

The following argument replaces the single-chart/open-map passage in Feehan §4.4. For g=f-f(p), cover the compact fiber of the proper resolution over p by finitely many precompact normal-crossing coordinate charts. Apply the monomial gradient estimates in each, bound Dpi, choose the largest of their exponents (still less than 1), and shrink so |g|<=1. Properness gives a neighborhood W of p whose entire inverse image lies in this union: the image of its closed complement is closed and misses p. The chain rule therefore yields a uniform gradient bound downstairs. If g is locally zero the assertion is trivial; if its gradient is nonzero at p shrink and choose any exponent in (0,1). For distance use Corollary 4(2) applied to f squared, with the critical-zero-set condition justified in Remark 1.4. Cover K intersect Z finitely and take the largest exponent; on the compact remainder |f| has positive minimum and distance to the nonempty Z is bounded.
