If nonnegative integers m,n are sums of two integer squares, then so is mn.

Proof 1: Diophantus’s product identity

Write m=a^2+b^2 and n=c^2+d^2 with integers a,b,c,d. The Diophantus identity gives

mn=(ac+bd)^2+(ad-bc)^2,

which is a sum of two integer squares.

Proof 2: Multiplicativity of the complex modulus

A Gaussian integer is a complex number a+ib such that a and b are integers. The norm N(a+ib)=a^2+b^2 of a Gaussian integer is an integer equal to the square of the absolute value of the Gaussian integer. The norm of a product of Gaussian integers is the product of their norms. This is the Diophantus identity, which results immediately from the similar property of the absolute value.

Thus, if m=N(a+ib) and n=N(c+id), their product is the norm of the Gaussian integer (a+ib)(c+id), hence a sum of two integer squares.
