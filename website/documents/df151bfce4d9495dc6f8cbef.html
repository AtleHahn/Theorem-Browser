Statement

Let f,g\in L^1(\mathbb R) and use \widehat f(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb R}f(x)e^{-ix\xi}\,dx. If \widehat f(\xi_0)=0 but \widehat g(\xi_0)\ne0 at some real \xi_0, then g is not in the L1 closure of the linear span of translates f_a(x)=f(x+a).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement concerns Fourier transforms of Lebesgue-integrable functions and approximation in L1.

Proof 1: Persistent Fourier zeros — outline

If the Fourier transform of a function {\displaystyle f} vanishes on a certain set {\displaystyle Z}, the Fourier transform of any linear combination of translations of {\displaystyle f} also vanishes on {\displaystyle Z}. Therefore, the linear combinations of translations of {\displaystyle f} cannot approximate a function whose Fourier transform does not vanish on {\displaystyle Z}. The background used to pass to the L1 closure is continuity of Fourier evaluation at each frequency in the L1 norm.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Translate Fourier zeros through finite linear combinations and use continuity in the L1 norm to pass to closure. The proof uses Lebesgue function spaces and their norm-closure structure.
