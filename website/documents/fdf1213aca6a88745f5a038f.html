All first-order field truths of the reals hold in some countable field model. Thus even the full first-order theory fails to capture the real continuum's uncountable size, exposing a gap between an intended structure and what its language can distinguish.

Statement

The theory of (\mathbb R,+,\times,0,1), consisting of all first-order sentences true in that structure, has a countable model.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level

Proof 1: Apply the model-cardinality theorem

Use Löwenheim–Skolem: an infinite structure in signature \sigma has an elementarily equivalent structure of every infinite cardinality \kappa\ge|\sigma|, smaller ones as elementary substructures and larger ones as elementary extensions.

The field signature is finite. Apply the downward theorem to the infinite real field with \kappa=\aleph_0. The elementary substructure so obtained is countable and satisfies every sentence true in the real field.
