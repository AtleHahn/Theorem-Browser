Statement

A Euclidean Hurwitz algebra is a nonzero finite-dimensional real unital algebra A (not assumed associative), with a positive-definite quadratic form q satisfying q(ab)=q(a)q(b). Write (a,b)=\frac12(q(a+b)-q(a)-q(b)), \|a\|^2=q(a), a^*=-a+2(a,1)1, L(a)b=ab, R(a)b=ba, and \operatorname{Re}a=(a,1)1. Define [a,b,c]=a(bc)-(ab)c. Then this trilinear map changes sign under every transposition of its arguments; it vanishes whenever one argument is a real multiple of 1.

Level justification

The algebra axioms and all involution/multiplication formulas are explicitly specified. Understanding these finite algebraic conditions is a small extension without classification theory.

Proof 1: Polarization of alternativity

The Hurwitz alternativity identities imply [a,a,b]=[b,a,a]=0. Polarizing these identities shows antisymmetry in adjacent pairs, hence in all three arguments. If any argument is real, bilinearity of multiplication and the unit identity give zero.

Polarize the two supplied alternativity identities to obtain antisymmetry, then use the unit for real arguments. Undergraduate multilinear and algebraic identities suffice; the nonassociative multiplication and associator are explicitly defined.
