Statement

Every real polynomial of positive odd degree has at least one real root.

Level

Proof 1: Count conjugate pairs with multiplicity

Since nonreal complex roots come in conjugate pairs, there are an even number of them. But a polynomial of odd degree has an odd number of roots by the fundamental theorem of algebra. Therefore some of them must be real.

Roots are counted with multiplicity here. This requires some care in the presence of multiple roots, but a complex root and its conjugate have the same multiplicity, so the parity argument remains valid.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Count the supplied complex roots with multiplicity and pair every nonreal root with its conjugate. School polynomial algebra, complex conjugation and parity suffice for these exact assertions; no proof of the fundamental theorem is required.

Proof 2: Pass to an irreducible factor of odd degree

A real polynomial of odd degree has an irreducible factor of odd degree. Such a factor has no multiple roots. By the fundamental theorem of algebra it has an odd number of roots, and its nonreal roots occur in conjugate pairs. Hence it has a real root, which is also a root of the original polynomial.

Level

Level justification

Factor over R, choose an odd-degree irreducible factor and use its lack of repeated roots before the conjugate-pair count. Undergraduate polynomial ring algebra suffices; this actual proof uses irreducibility and characteristic-zero separability beyond the direct parity argument.
