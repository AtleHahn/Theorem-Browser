Statement

If (a_k)_{k\ge1} is real and \sum_{k=1}^\infty|a_k|<\infty, then \sum_{k=1}^\infty a_k converges.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves real series; monotone convergence. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Nonnegative partial sums

Let {\textstyle \sum \left|a_{k}\right|,a_{k}\in \mathbb {R} } be convergent. Since {\displaystyle 0\leq a_{k}+\left|a_{k}\right|\leq 2\left|a_{k}\right|,} we have

{\displaystyle 0\leq \sum _{k=1}^{n}(a_{k}+\left|a_{k}\right|)\leq \sum _{k=1}^{n}2\left|a_{k}\right|.}

Since {\textstyle \sum 2\left|a_{k}\right|} is convergent, {\textstyle s_{n}=\sum _{k=1}^{n}\left(a_{k}+\left|a_{k}\right|\right)} is a bounded monotonic sequence of partial sums, and {\textstyle \sum \left(a_{k}+\left|a_{k}\right|\right)} must also converge. Noting that {\textstyle \sum a_{k}=\sum \left(a_{k}+\left|a_{k}\right|\right)-\sum \left|a_{k}\right|} is the difference of convergent series, we conclude that it too is a convergent series, as desired.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The partial sums of a_k+|a_k| increase and are bounded by twice the given absolute sum. The monotone sequence theorem yields their convergence; subtracting the convergent absolute series gives the desired sum. The proof lies wholly within introductory real analysis.

1. Monotone convergence of bounded real sequences
Kind:

Theorem

Treatment:

Cited without proof

Statement used:

Every monotone increasing real sequence that is bounded above converges to a finite real limit.

Application in proof:

Apply to sum_{k=1}^n(a_k+|a_k|), whose terms are nonnegative and whose partial sums are bounded.

Application level:

Undergraduate level

Level justification:

This is the standard monotone sequence theorem for real numbers, not the measure-theoretic monotone convergence theorem.
