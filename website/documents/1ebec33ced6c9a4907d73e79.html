Statement

For complex a_0\ne0,a_1,a_2,m and x\ne0, the equation a_0x^4+a_1x^3+a_2x^2+a_1mx+a_0m^2=0 is equivalent, under z=x+m/x, to a_0z^2+a_1z+a_2-2ma_0=0.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement concerns explicit complex polynomial roots, conjugate pairs, factorization or an algebraic substitution; only elementary polynomial and complex-number algebra is needed. This rating concerns understanding the statement, independently of the difficulty of its proof.

Proof 1: Divide by the square and combine reciprocal terms

For x\ne0, the steps are:

  1. Divide by x2.
  2. Use variable change z = x + m/x.
  3. So, z2 = x2 + (m/x)2 + 2m.

This leads to:

{\displaystyle a_{0}(x^{2}+m^{2}/x^{2})+a_{1}(x+m/x)+a_{2}=0},{\displaystyle a_{0}(z^{2}-2m)+a_{1}(z)+a_{2}=0},

{\displaystyle a_{0}z^{2}+a_{1}z+(a_{2}-2ma_{0})=0} (a quadratic in z = x + m/x)

Level justification
