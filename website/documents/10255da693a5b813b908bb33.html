Statement

For a finite sequence of real numbers let V count changes of sign after deleting all zero terms. For a nonzero real polynomial f of degree n, put W_t(f)=V(f(t),f^{\prime}(t),\ldots,f^{(n)}(t)) and let v_t(f) count sign changes in the coefficients of f(x+t), read in increasing powers. Roots are counted with multiplicity. For every real t, v_t(f)=W_t(f).

Understanding the statement involves polynomial derivatives; Taylor coefficients; finite sign variations. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. Proof 1: Translated coefficients and derivatives have equal sign variation outline

For the polynomial p=f, use the polynomial Taylor expansion

{\displaystyle p(x)=\sum _{i=0}^{\deg p}{\frac {p^{(i)}(h)}{i!}}(x-h)^{i}}

of the polynomial p at h, which implies that the coefficient of xi in p(x + h) is the quotient of {\displaystyle p^{(i)}(h)} by i!, a positive number. Thus the derivative-value sequence and translated coefficient sequence have the same number of sign variations.
