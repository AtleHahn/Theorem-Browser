Statement

For the real system \dot x=-x+x^2y, \dot y=xy^2, the equilibrium (0,0) is Lyapunov stable: for every \epsilon>0 sufficiently small initial data remain within \epsilon of the origin for all t\ge0. Moreover, for every sufficiently small initial condition the solution exists for all t\ge0 and satisfies x(t)\to0, y(t)\to c for some real constant c depending on that solution.

Proof 1: Check the Lyapunov–Malkin hypotheses

Use the Lyapunov–Malkin theorem for \dot x=Ax+X(x,y), \dot y=Y(x,y): when the eigenvalues of A have negative real parts and the smooth higher-order nonlinear terms vanish for x=0, the origin is stable, and nearby solutions have x(t)\to0, y(t)\to c. Here A=-1, X(x,y)=x^2y and Y(x,y)=xy^2. The nonlinearities are smooth cubic terms and X(0,y)=Y(0,y)=0 for every y. Thus the theorem applies and gives the stated stability and limits.
