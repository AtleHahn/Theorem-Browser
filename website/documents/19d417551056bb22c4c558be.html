Statement

In V=C_2\times C_2, each subgroup H of order two has two distinct complements, and these complements are not conjugate in V.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory group theory supplies the explicit four-element group, its subgroups, direct products and conjugation. A complement is a subgroup with trivial intersection whose product is the whole group, a single elementary definition.

Proof 1: Two complements and trivial conjugation — outline

The Klein four-group has three subgroups of order two. Fix one as H; either of the other two intersects H trivially and their product is V, so each complements H. Since V is abelian, conjugation fixes every subgroup, and the two distinct complements cannot be conjugate.

Level justification

Enumerate the three order-two subgroups, check complement conditions and use commutativity to make conjugation trivial. Standard undergraduate finite group theory suffices.
