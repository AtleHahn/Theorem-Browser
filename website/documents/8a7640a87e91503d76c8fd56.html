Statement

Let H be a real or complex Hilbert space, with inner product \langle x,y\rangle linear in x. Write [y,x]=\langle x,y\rangle, so brackets are linear in their second argument. Let \varphi\in H^* and x\in H satisfy \varphi(x)\ne0. If x_K is the orthogonal projection of x onto \ker\varphi, then f_\varphi=\|\varphi\|^2(x-x_K)/\varphi(x). Here H^* denotes the continuous linear scalar functionals on H.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra supplies inner products, kernels, orthogonality and scalar-valued linear maps. Hilbert completeness and the functional norm as a unit-ball supremum are direct analysis extensions. A Riesz vector is explicitly a vector representing the functional by inner product, so no dual topology or spectral theory is needed.

Proof 1: Solve the kernel projection formula

The projection formula is x_K=x-\langle x,f_\varphi\rangle f_\varphi/\|f_\varphi\|^2. Substitute \langle x,f_\varphi\rangle=\varphi(x) and \|f_\varphi\|^2=\|\varphi\|^2, then solve for f_\varphi.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Combine the Hilbert dual representation with orthogonal kernel projection and the norm identification. These linked infinite-dimensional dual concepts are not developed locally; conservatively retain beyond undergraduate provisionally despite the scalar rearrangement.
