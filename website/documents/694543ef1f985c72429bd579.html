Statement

Let H be a semisimple linear algebraic group over an algebraically closed field and B\subseteq H a Borel subgroup. Every nonzero finite-dimensional rational representation of B has an invariant line.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves semisimple algebraic groups; Borel subgroups; rational representations; invariant lines. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Apply Lie–Kolchin to a Borel subgroup

A Borel subgroup is connected and solvable. Thus its representation satisfies the hypotheses of the Lie–Kolchin invariant-line theorem, which supplies the line.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The deduction applies Lie–Kolchin directly, but understanding rational representations of connected solvable algebraic groups and Borel subgroups requires substantial algebraic-group theory. These objects are not a small extension of ordinary matrix linear algebra.
