Statement

If x_1,x_2,x_3\in\mathbb C are linearly independent over \mathbb Q and y_1,y_2\in\mathbb C are linearly independent over \mathbb Q, then at least one of e^{x_iy_j}, 1\le i\le3, 1\le j\le2, is transcendental, meaning not algebraic over \mathbb Q.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Rational linear independence, complex exponentials and polynomial roots are undergraduate algebra/calculus. Transcendental is explicitly not algebraic over Q, requiring no number-field theory for comprehension.

Proof 1: Set all algebraic shifts to zero

Use the sharp six exponentials theorem (an external prerequisite):

Let x1, x2, and x3 be complex numbers that are linearly independent over the rational numbers, and let y1 and y2 be a pair of complex numbers that are linearly independent over the rational numbers, and suppose that βij are six algebraic numbers for 1 ≤ i ≤ 3 and 1 ≤ j ≤ 2 such that the following six numbers are algebraic:

{\displaystyle e^{x_{1}y_{1}-\beta _{11}},e^{x_{1}y_{2}-\beta _{12}},e^{x_{2}y_{1}-\beta _{21}},e^{x_{2}y_{2}-\beta _{22}},e^{x_{3}y_{1}-\beta _{31}},e^{x_{3}y_{2}-\beta _{32}}.}

Then xi yj = βij for 1 ≤ i ≤ 3 and 1 ≤ j ≤ 2. The six exponentials theorem then follows by setting βij = 0 for every i and j.

Rational linear independence makes each x_i and y_j nonzero, so the conclusion x_i y_j=0 would be impossible.

Set all six algebraic shifts to zero in the explicitly stated theorem and use rational independence to exclude zero factors. Undergraduate complex exponentials and linear algebra suffice, with algebraicity defined by polynomial equations; no transcendence-theory proof is invoked.
