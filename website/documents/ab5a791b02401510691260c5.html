Statement

On the unit sphere of \mathbb C^3, there is no function p taking only the values 0 and 1 such that p(u_1)+p(u_2)+p(u_3)=1 for every orthonormal basis (u_1,u_2,u_3). Such a function would assign deterministic, context-independent probabilities to the outcomes of every spin-1 projective measurement.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex linear algebra supplies orthonormal bases of C3. The mathematical assertion is explicitly the nonexistence of a zero-one function with the given basis-sum property; the final measurement sentence is an interpretation, not an additional quantum-theory hypothesis.

Proof 1: Gleason representation and connectedness — outline

Use Gleason's theorem in the three-dimensional complex case: a probability assignment normalized on every orthonormal basis has the form p(u)=\langle\rho u,u\rangle for a density matrix \rho (positive semidefinite with trace 1).

By this theorem the assignment must take the form of a mapping {\displaystyle u\to \langle \rho u,u\rangle } for some density operator {\displaystyle \rho }. This mapping is continuous on the unit sphere of the Hilbert space, and since this unit sphere is connected, no continuous probability measure on it can be deterministic.

Level justification

Use the supplied three-dimensional Gleason formula to obtain a continuous quadratic form on the connected sphere; a zero-one-valued continuous function is constant, contradicting basis normalization. Undergraduate linear algebra and point-set topology suffice; a density matrix is explicitly defined.
