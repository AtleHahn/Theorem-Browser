Statement

Let (\Omega,\mathcal F,\mu) be a measure space, with \mu allowed to take the value +\infty. For measurable sets A_1,A_2,\ldots, \mu(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}A_i)\le\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mu(A_i).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Sets, inverse images and countable operations are familiar from analysis and topology. A sigma-algebra is a family closed under complements and countable unions, and measurability is the corresponding inverse-image condition. Here no general integration, null-set quotient or stochastic process is required; the limited set-family definitions suffice.

Proof 1: Disjointify by first occurrence

Let the measurable sets A_1,A_2,\ldots be given. The countable additivity of the measure {\displaystyle \mu } states that if {\displaystyle B_{1},B_{2},B_{3},\dots } are pairwise disjoint measurable sets, then

{\displaystyle \mu \left(\bigcup _{i}B_{i}\right)=\sum _{i}\mu (B_{i}).}

Set

{\displaystyle B_{i}:=A_{i}-\bigcup _{j=1}^{i-1}A_{j}.}

Then {\displaystyle B_{1},B_{2},B_{3},\dots } are pairwise disjoint. We claim that:

{\displaystyle \bigcup _{i=1}^{\infty }A_{i}=\bigcup _{i=1}^{\infty }B_{i}.}

One inclusion is clear. Indeed, since {\displaystyle B_{i}\subset A_{i}} for all i, thus {\displaystyle \bigcup _{i=1}^{\infty }B_{i}\subset \bigcup _{i=1}^{\infty }A_{i}}.

For the other inclusion, let {\displaystyle x\in \bigcup _{i=1}^{\infty }A_{i}} be given. Write {\displaystyle k} for the minimum positive integer such that {\displaystyle x\in A_{k}}. Then {\displaystyle x\in A_{k}-\bigcup _{j=1}^{k-1}A_{j}=B_{k}}. Thus {\displaystyle x\in \bigcup _{i=1}^{\infty }B_{i}}. Therefore {\displaystyle \bigcup _{i=1}^{\infty }A_{i}\subset \bigcup _{i=1}^{\infty }B_{i}}.

Therefore

{\displaystyle \mu \left(\bigcup _{i}A_{i}\right)=\mu \left(\bigcup _{i}B_{i}\right)=\sum _{i}\mu (B_{i})\leq \sum _{i}\mu (A_{i}),}

where the last inequality holds because {\displaystyle B_{i}\subset A_{i}} implies that {\displaystyle \mu (B_{i})\leq \mu (A_{i}),} for all i.
