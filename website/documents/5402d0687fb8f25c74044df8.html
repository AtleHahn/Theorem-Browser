Statement

Let D be a nonempty bounded connected open subset of a complex Banach space X. For z\in D,v\in X, set \alpha(z,v)=\sup_g|g^{\prime}(z)v|, where g:D\to\{\zeta\in\mathbb C:|\zeta|<1\} is holomorphic. Define \ell(\gamma)=\int_0^1\alpha(\gamma(t),\dot\gamma(t))\,dt for piecewise differentiable curves in D, and d(x,y)=\inf_{\gamma(0)=x,\gamma(1)=y}\ell(\gamma). If R>\operatorname{diam}D, then \alpha(z,v)\ge\|v\|/R and d(x,y)\ge\|x-y\|/R; in particular d is a metric.

Proof 1: Use affine holomorphic test functions — outline

The displayed path distance is finite and continuous on D\times D for the norm topology.

If the diameter of D is less than R then, by taking suitable holomorphic functions g of the form

{\displaystyle \displaystyle {g(z)=a(z)+b}}

with a in X*, the continuous complex linear dual of X, and b in C, it follows that

{\displaystyle \displaystyle {\alpha (z,v)\geq \|v\|/R,}}

and hence that

{\displaystyle \displaystyle {d(x,y)\geq \|x-y\|/R.}}

In particular d defines a metric on D.

Level justification

The proof concerns holomorphic functions on an arbitrary complex Banach space, its continuous dual and an infinitesimal path metric. These linked infinite-dimensional analytic concepts remain substantial even though the final lower bound follows from a norming functional.
