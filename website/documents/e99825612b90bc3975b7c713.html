Statement

Let (a_k) be real and suppose \sum_{k=0}^{n}a_k\to+\infty. Then

{\displaystyle \lim _{x\to 1^{-}}\liminf _{n\to \infty }\sum _{k=0}^{n}a_{k}x^{k}=+\infty }

Proof 1: Lower bounds after summation by parts

To prove the case for {\displaystyle \sum _{k=0}^{\infty }a_{k}=+\infty }, let {\displaystyle x\in (0,1)} and {\displaystyle s_{n}=\sum _{k=0}^{n}a_{k}}. Since {\displaystyle (s_{k})_{k}} diverges to {\displaystyle +\infty }, we can find, for any {\displaystyle M\in \mathbb {R} _{+}}, a positive integer {\displaystyle N} such that {\displaystyle s_{k}>3M} for all {\displaystyle k\geq N}. We write, for {\displaystyle n>N}:

{\displaystyle \sum _{k=0}^{n}a_{k}x^{k}=\left[{(1-x)\sum _{k=0}^{N-1}s_{k}x^{k}}\right]+\left\{{(1-x)\left[{\sum _{k=N}^{n-1}s_{k}x^{k}}\right]+s_{n}x^{n}}\right\}}

Since the first term on the right hand side vanishes as {\displaystyle x\to 1}, we can find an {\displaystyle x_{1}\in (0,1)} such that it exceeds {\displaystyle -M} whenever {\displaystyle x_{1}<x<1}. The second term may be estimated by:

{\displaystyle (1-x)\left[{\sum _{k=N}^{n-1}s_{k}x^{k}}\right]+s_{n}x^{n}>3M\left\{{(1-x)\left[{\sum _{k=N}^{n-1}x^{k}}\right]+x^{n}}\right\}=3Mx^{N}.}

Hence, if we let {\displaystyle x_{2}=(2/3)^{1/N}}, then for {\displaystyle x_{2}<x<1} this exceeds {\displaystyle 2M}. Combining, we get, for any {\displaystyle n>N} and {\displaystyle \max(x_{1},x_{2})<x<1}:

{\displaystyle \sum _{k=0}^{n}a_{k}x^{k}>M.}

This establishes:

{\displaystyle \lim _{x\to 1^{-}}\liminf _{n\to \infty }\sum _{k=0}^{n}a_{k}x^{k}=+\infty }

In the absence of additional assumptions, the series {\displaystyle \sum _{k=0}^{\infty }a_{k}x^{k}} might not converge when {\displaystyle |x|<1}, hence the use of the limit inferior.
