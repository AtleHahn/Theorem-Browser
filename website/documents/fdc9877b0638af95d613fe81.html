Statement

Let X_1,X_2 be nonempty Polish spaces, \mu a Borel probability on X_1\times X_2, \pi_1(x_1,x_2)=x_1 and \nu=(\pi_1)_*\mu. There is a Borel probability kernel x_1\mapsto k_{x_1} on X_2, unique for \nu-almost every x_1, such that for every nonnegative Borel f:X_1\times X_2\to[0,\infty],

\int f\,d\mu=\int_{X_1}\int_{X_2}f(x_1,x_2)\,dk_{x_1}(x_2)\,d\nu(x_1).

For Borel A\subseteq X_1,B\subseteq X_2, \mu(A\times B)=\int_A k_{x_1}(B)\,d\nu(x_1).

Proof 1: Identify the projection fibers — outline

The disintegration theorem supplies for a Borel probability \eta on a Polish space Y, a Polish space X, and a Borel map p:Y\to X a Borel probability kernel (\eta_x) supported on p^{-1}(x) for p_*\eta-almost every x, uniquely determined almost everywhere, with \int f\,d\eta=\int\int f\,d\eta_x\,d(p_*\eta) for all nonnegative Borel f.

When Y is written as a Cartesian product Y=X_1\times X_2 and \pi_i:Y\to X_i is the natural projection, then each fibre \pi_1^{-1}(x_1) can be canonically identified with X_2 and there exists a Borel family of probability measures \{k_{x_1}\}_{x_1\in X_1} on X_2 (which is (\pi_1)_*\mu-almost everywhere uniquely determined). Regarding each measure as a measure on its fibre, this gives

\mu=\int_{X_1}(\delta_{x_1}\otimes k_{x_1})\,d(\pi_1)_*\mu(x_1),

where \delta_{x_1} is the point mass at x_1. In particular,

\int_{X_1\times X_2}f(x_1,x_2)\,\mu(dx_1,dx_2) =\int_{X_1}\left(\int_{X_2}f(x_1,x_2)\,k_{x_1}(dx_2)\right)\,d\nu(x_1)

and

\mu(A\times B)=\int_A k_{x_1}(B)\,d\nu(x_1).
