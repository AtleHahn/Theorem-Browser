Simpler proof assessment

Likely alternative prerequisite level: Undergraduate level; lowest recorded proof level: Beyond undergraduate level. For a continuous circle homomorphism, choose its argument near 1 with value zero at 1. For sufficiently small real s,t its argument at exp(i(s+t)) is the sum of the arguments at exp(is),exp(it); continuity removes the possible 2pi ambiguity. Rational subdivision then gives a linear argument ct, extends it to all real t, and periodicity forces c to be an integer. Powers z^c give every map, with pointwise multiplication adding and composition multiplying the integers. This uses undergraduate continuity and elementary groups, not Pontryagin duality.

Statement

For the circle group \mathbb T, the ring of continuous group endomorphisms (pointwise group operation as addition, composition as multiplication) is isomorphic to \mathbb Z.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory abstract algebra supplies groups, ring operations, quotients and homomorphisms. The additional operations or central-element conditions are explicitly given; for the circle group, complex exponentials and continuity are familiar from calculus and topology. No further structural theory is needed to understand the claim.

Proof 1: Transfer the integer endomorphism ring through duality

The ring {\displaystyle {\text{End}}(G)} of endomorphisms in the category of locally compact abelian groups and continuous homomorphisms is changed by duality into its opposite ring (change the multiplication to the other order). For example, if {\displaystyle G} is an infinite cyclic discrete group, {\displaystyle {\widehat {G}}} is a circle group: the former has {\displaystyle {\text{End}}(G)=\mathbb {Z} } so this is true also of the latter.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The argument transfers an endomorphism ring through Pontryagin duality for locally compact abelian groups. Continuous characters, the dual topology, and contravariant duality constitute substantial additional background, although the integer endomorphism calculation itself is elementary algebra.
