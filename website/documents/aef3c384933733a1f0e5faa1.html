Statement

Consider two nondegenerate Euclidean triangles with two corresponding side lengths a,b>0. Let c,\widehat c be their third sides and \gamma,\widehat\gamma\in(0,\pi) the respective included angles between the sides a,b. Then \widehat\gamma>\gamma if and only if \widehat c>c.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Understanding the statement requires only Euclidean triangle side lengths, included angles and comparisons of lengths and angles.

Proof 1: Compare the law-of-cosines expressions

The theorem is an immediate corollary of the law of cosines. For two triangles with sides {\displaystyle \{a,b,c\}} and {\displaystyle \{a,b,{\hat {c}}\}} with angles {\displaystyle \gamma } and {\displaystyle {\hat {\gamma }}} opposite the respective sides {\displaystyle c} and {\displaystyle {\hat {c}}}, the law of cosines states:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}c^{2}&=a^{2}+b^{2}-2ab\cos \gamma ,\\{\hat {c}}^{2}&=a^{2}+b^{2}-2ab\cos {\hat {\gamma }}.\end{aligned}}}

The cosine function is monotonically decreasing for angles between {\displaystyle 0} and {\displaystyle \pi } radians, so {\displaystyle {\hat {\gamma }}>\gamma } implies {\displaystyle {\hat {c}}>c} (and the converse as well).

High school only
Level justification

Compare the two cosine-rule equations using strict decrease of cosine on the triangle-angle range. High-school trigonometry and inequalities suffice.
