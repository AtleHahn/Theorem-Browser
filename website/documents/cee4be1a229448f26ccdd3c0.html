Statement

If p is a prime number, then (1+z)^p\equiv1+z^p\pmod p as an integer polynomial identity: every coefficient of (1+z)^p-1-z^p is divisible by p.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The source’s modulo-prime polynomial identity is explicitly defined coefficientwise, requiring only integer divisibility and finite polynomial algebra.

Proof 1: Frobenius applied to one plus the variable

In (\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)[z], the Frobenius endomorphism preserves addition. Applying it to 1+z yields (1+z)^p=1^p+z^p=1+z^p. Equality in this coefficient ring is precisely the claimed coefficientwise congruence.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the Frobenius ring endomorphism to 1+z in the polynomial ring over the prime field. Undergraduate rings and polynomial coefficients suffice; characteristic-p Frobenius is one explicit additional identity.
