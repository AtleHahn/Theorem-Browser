Statement

Every probability distribution P on \mathbb R can be written P=P_1*P_2, where P_1 has no indecomposable convolution factor and P_2 is either a point mass or a convolution of finitely or countably many indecomposable distributions. Convolution Q*R is the law of the sum of independent random variables with laws Q,R. A distribution is indecomposable if it is not a point mass and in every representation as a convolution one of the factors is a point mass. A convolution factor of P_1 means a distribution Q for which P_1=Q*R for some probability distribution R. A countable convolution means a weak limit of finite partial convolutions, where weak convergence means convergence of integrals of bounded continuous real functions. Such a factorization need not be unique.
