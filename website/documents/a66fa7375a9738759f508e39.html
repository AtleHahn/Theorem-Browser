Proof 1: Compare fifth powers modulo twenty-five — outline

Suppose that x^5+y^5+z^5=0 with 5\nmid xyz. By Fermat's little theorem,

x5 ≡ x (mod 5)

y5 ≡ y (mod 5)

z5 ≡ z (mod 5)

and therefore

x + y + z ≡ 0 (mod 5)

This equation forces two of the three numbers x, y, and z to be equivalent modulo 5, which can be seen as follows: Since they are indivisible by 5, x, y and z cannot equal 0 modulo 5, and must equal one of four possibilities: 1, −1, 2, or −2. If they were all different, two would be opposites and their sum modulo 5 would be zero (implying contrary to the assumption of this case that the other one would be 0 modulo 5).

Without loss of generality, x and y can be designated as the two equivalent numbers modulo 5. By the binomial expansion, congruence modulo 5 implies congruence of fifth powers modulo 25. That equivalence implies that

x5 ≡ y5 (mod 25) (note change in modulus)

−z5 ≡ x5 + y5 ≡ 2x5 (mod 25)

However, the equation x ≡ y (mod 5) also implies that

−z ≡ x + y ≡ 2x (mod 5)

−z5 ≡ 25x5 ≡ 32x5 (mod 25)

Combining the two results and cancelling (since 5\nmid x) x5 yields a contradiction

2 ≡ 32 ≡ 7 (mod 25)

Thus the first case for exponent 5 has been proven.
