Statement

For every integer n\geq1 and every neighborhood U of 0 in \mathbb R^n, there exists \varepsilon>0 such that the additive lattice \Gamma=\varepsilon\mathbb Z^n is generated by \Gamma\cap U. Here generated means every lattice vector is an integer linear combination of points of that intersection.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Euclidean neighborhoods; scaled integer lattices; additive generation. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Scale the coordinate generators into the neighborhood

Use the source example \Gamma=\varepsilon\mathbb Z^n for sufficiently small \varepsilon>0. Its coordinate generators \varepsilon e_1,\ldots,\varepsilon e_n then lie in U, where e_j are the standard coordinate vectors. These vectors generate \varepsilon\mathbb Z^n by integer combinations, so \Gamma is generated by \Gamma\cap U.
