Likely alternative prerequisite level: Undergraduate level; lowest recorded proof level: Beyond undergraduate level. In the hyperplane sum_i u_i=0 of R^(d+2), its unit sphere has dimension d. Normalize positive and negative coordinates separately to barycentric points u+ and u- of the simplex; their supports are disjoint and nonempty. The continuous map f(u+)-f(u-) is odd, so Borsuk-Ulam (with its Tucker combinatorial proof) gives a zero. This proves the full disjoint-face conclusion; d=0 is immediate. Coordinate geometry, continuity and the concrete antipodal theorem replace deleted joins and equivariant index theory.

Statement

Every continuous map from a (d+1)-simplex to \mathbb R^d maps two disjoint nonempty faces to sets with nonempty intersection. A simplex is the convex hull of affinely independent vertices and a face is the convex hull of a subset of those vertices.

Proof 1: Apply the deleted-join index theorem

Let K be the simplex \Delta^{d+1}, and let K_\Delta^{*2} be the deleted join of K with itself: its simplices consist of a face in one copy and a disjoint face in the other copy. The involution exchanges the two copies.

The geometric realization of K_\Delta^{*2} is equivariantly homeomorphic to the sphere S^{d+1} with its antipodal involution; therefore, the \mathbb Z_2-index of K_\Delta^{*2} equals d+1.

The topological Radon theorem follows from the following more general theorem: for any simplicial complex K, if the \mathbb Z_2-index of K_\Delta^{*2} is larger than d, then for every continuous mapping from |K| to \mathbb R^d, the images of two disjoint faces of K intersect.

Here the \mathbb Z_2-index is the least n admitting an equivariant map to the antipodal sphere S^n. The sphere identification, its index, and the general deleted-join theorem are external prerequisites.
