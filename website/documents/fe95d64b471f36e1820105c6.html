Statement

Let G be a compact group. Matrix coefficients of its finite-dimensional continuous unitary representations form a unital subalgebra of C(G) invariant under complex conjugation. A coefficient is a function g\mapsto L(\pi(g)) with L linear on \operatorname{End}(V).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

From group theory, topology and complex linear algebra, a compact group is a compact topological space whose group operations are continuous. A finite-dimensional continuous unitary representation is a continuous homomorphism to unitary matrices; its coefficients and C(G) are continuous complex functions. The displayed coefficient definition and pointwise algebra operations are a coherent extension within about 30 minutes, without irreducible representation theory.

Proof 1: Matrix coefficients form a self-conjugate algebra — outline

The matrix coefficients form a unital algebra invariant under complex conjugation because the product of two matrix coefficients is a matrix coefficient of the tensor product representation, and the complex conjugate is a matrix coefficient of the dual representation. Here the representations are unitary, so the dual matrices are the complex conjugate matrices; direct sums and the trivial representation supply linear combinations and constants.

Level justification

Represent the operations by block direct sums, Kronecker products and conjugate unitary matrices. A finite-dimensional representation is a continuous matrix homomorphism; these concrete operations and coefficient functions are a small extension of linear algebra and group theory.
