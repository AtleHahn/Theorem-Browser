Statement

Let (X,\mathcal M,\mu) have finite positive measure, and let \Phi\subseteq L^1(\mu) be nonempty. Hunt’s condition \inf_{g\in L^1(\mu),\ g\ge0}\sup_{f\in\Phi}\int_{\{|f|>g\}}|f|\,d\mu=0 is equivalent to the conjunction of \sup_{f\in\Phi}\|f\|_1<\infty and uniform absolute continuity: for every \varepsilon>0 there is \delta>0 such that \mu(E)<\delta implies \sup_{f\in\Phi}\int_E|f|\,d\mu<\varepsilon.

Proof 1: Specializing the weight to one

Use the following weighted characterization: on a sigma-finite measure space, for any strictly positive almost everywhere integrable h, Hunt’s condition is equivalent to L^1 boundedness and the following property: for every \varepsilon>0 there is \delta>0 such that \int_Eh\,d\mu<\delta implies \sup_{f\in\Phi}\int_E|f|\,d\mu<\varepsilon. On a finite measure space choose h\equiv1, which is positive and integrable, and note that \int_Eh\,d\mu=\mu(E). Substitution gives exactly the stated equivalence.

Level justification

Set the weight to one in the general Hunt characterization. The full quantified L1-family conditions and weighted uniform absolute continuity require Lebesgue integration despite the simple substitution.
