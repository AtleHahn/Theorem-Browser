Statement

A continuous surjective linear map T:E\to F between real or complex Banach spaces is open.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Banach means complete normed vector space, a direct combination of linear algebra and metric completeness. Bounded linear maps and open maps have familiar norm/topological definitions; the claim adds no dual-space or spectral constructions.

Proof 1: Baire category and successive corrections

We first prove the two local facts used in the argument. A linear map f:E\to F is called nearly open if, for each neighborhood U of zero, the closure \overline{f(U)} contains a neighborhood of zero.

First suppose that the image of f is nonmeager in F. Shrinking U, we can assume U is an open ball centered at zero. We have f(E)=f(\bigcup_{n\ge1}nU)=\bigcup_{n\ge1}f(nU). Thus, some \overline{f(nU)} contains an interior point y; that is, for some radius r>0,

B(y,r)\subset\overline{f(nU)}.

Then for any v in F with \|v\|<r, by linearity, convexity and (-1)U\subset U,

v=v-y+y\in\overline{f(-nU)}+\overline{f(nU)} \subset\overline{f(2nU)},

which proves near openness by dividing by 2n.

The completeness on the domain then allows us to upgrade nearly open to open. More precisely, if f is continuous and

B_F(0,\delta)\subset\overline{f(B_E(0,1))}

for some \delta>0, and if E is complete, then

B_F(0,\delta)\subset f(B_E(0,1)).

Here B(x,r) is an open ball with radius r and center x.

Let y be in B_F(0,\delta) and c_n>0 some sequence. We have: \overline{B_F(0,\delta)}\subset\overline{f(B_E(0,1))}. Thus, for each \epsilon>0 and nonzero z in F, we can find an x with \|x\|<\delta^{-1}\|z\| and z in B(f(x),\epsilon). The zero target has the preimage zero. Thus, taking z=y\ne0, we find an x_1 such that

\|y-f(x_1)\|<c_1,\qquad \|x_1\|<\delta^{-1}\|y\|.

Applying the same argument with z=y-f(x_1), we then find an x_2 such that

\|y-f(x_1)-f(x_2)\|<c_2,\qquad \|x_2\|<\delta^{-1}c_1,

where we observed \|x_2\|<\delta^{-1}\|z\|<\delta^{-1}c_1. If a residual is zero, stop and take all remaining terms zero. Then so on. Thus, if c:=\sum c_n<\infty, we have found a sequence x_n such that x=\sum_{1}^{\infty}x_n converges by completeness, and f(x)=y by continuity. Also,

\|x\|\le\sum_{1}^{\infty}\|x_n\| \le\delta^{-1}\|y\|+\delta^{-1}c.

Since \delta^{-1}\|y\|<1, by making c small enough, we can achieve \|x\|<1. Scaling and translating this ball inclusion proves that f is open.

Finally, apply these facts with f=T. By Baire's category theorem, the complete space F=T(E) is nonmeager in itself, so the first fact applies. Then the conclusion of the theorem follows from the second fact.
