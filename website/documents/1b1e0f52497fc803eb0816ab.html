Statement

Let V be finite-dimensional over a field of characteristic zero and let \mathfrak g\subseteq\mathfrak{gl}(V) be a solvable Lie subalgebra. Then \operatorname{tr}(XY)=0 for all X\in\mathfrak g and Y\in[\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g].

Proof 1: Trace orthogonality for a solvable matrix Lie algebra — source deduction

After extending the base field to its algebraic closure, Lie’s theorem gives a basis in which \mathfrak g consists of upper triangular matrices. Commutators have diagonal consisting of zeros, so [\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g] consists of strictly upper triangular matrices. The implication is then seen easily: \operatorname{tr}(XY)=0 for X\in\mathfrak g and Y\in[\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g].

Here extension of scalars does not change the trace. The source gives this direction as an immediate consequence of triangularization.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Extend scalars and triangularize a solvable matrix Lie algebra; commutators are strictly triangular. The trace calculation is elementary but understanding Lie solvability and this representation theorem requires advanced algebra.
