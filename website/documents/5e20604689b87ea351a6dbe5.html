Statement

Over intuitionistic propositional logic, the schema ((P\to Q)\to P)\to P is equivalent to excluded middle P\lor\neg P. Here \neg P abbreviates P\to\bot.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Equivalence over intuitionistic deduction requires its constructive inference rules, which are not fully specified locally. These add a limited formal-logic extension rather than a general proof-theory course.

Proof 1: Consequentia mirabilis and double negation

Assume Peirce’s schema and specialize Q=\bot to get consequentia mirabilis (\neg P\to P)\to P. Given \neg\neg P, an assumption \neg P yields \bot and by explosion P; hence \neg P\to P and consequentia mirabilis gives P. Thus double-negation elimination holds. Apply it to the intuitionistically valid \neg\neg(P\lor\neg P) to obtain excluded middle. Conversely, excluded middle and explosion give P\lor(P\to Q). Under (P\to Q)\to P, either disjunct gives P, proving Peirce’s schema.

Level justification

Use finite intuitionistic natural deduction to derive double-negation elimination from the Peirce instance and prove the converse by cases. Undergraduate logic suffices with the restriction on excluded middle as a small related extension.
