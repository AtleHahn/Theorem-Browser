Statement

Let K be a nonempty finite geometric simplicial complex. Its mesh is the largest diameter of one of its simplices. Let \operatorname{Bd}^jK be the result of j barycentric subdivisions. Then \operatorname{mesh}(\operatorname{Bd}^jK)\to0 as j\to\infty; equivalently, for every \varepsilon>0, all sufficiently iterated subdivisions have every simplex of diameter less than \varepsilon.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Finite geometric simplices are convex hulls of independent vertices; complexes glue them along faces. Barycentric subdivision forms simplices from chains of faces and their averages, a short geometric extension of undergraduate linear algebra/topology.

Proof 1: Iterate the simplex diameter contraction — outline

Use the barycentric mesh estimate: a p-simplex subdivides into simplices of diameter at most p/(p+1) times its own diameter. Since this factor is less than 1, iterating the subdivision makes the maximal diameter arbitrarily small.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the supplied simplex diameter factor and the maximum finite dimension to obtain a uniform geometric contraction; zero-dimensional simplices already have zero diameter. Undergraduate Euclidean convex geometry and sequences suffice with barycentric subdivision as a finite simplex construction.
