Statement

Let X be a complex manifold with Hermitian metric, and let \omega be its associated real two-form. For an integer k\ge1 and a compact oriented smooth embedded real 2k-dimensional submanifold M,

\operatorname{vol}(M)\ge\frac1{k!}\int_M\omega^k.
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Hermitian metrics, differential forms and integration on real submanifolds of a complex manifold require substantial differential geometry. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Integrating the local linear inequality

Local linear inequality

Let V be a finite-dimensional real vector space with positive-definite inner product g, a symplectic form \omega, and a complex structure J (J^2=-I), related by \omega(u,v)=g(Ju,v). For an integer k\ge1, if v_1,\ldots,v_{2k} are orthonormal and 1\le 2k\le\dim V, then

\omega^k(v_1,\ldots,v_{2k})\le k!,

where \omega^k is the k-fold exterior product.

Two vectors

In the special case k = 1, the Wirtinger inequality is a special case of the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality:

{\displaystyle \omega (v_{1},v_{2})=g(J(v_{1}),v_{2})\leq \|J(v_{1})\|_{g}\|v_{2}\|_{g}=1.}

Exterior powers

Let v1, ..., v2k be fixed, and let T denote their span. Then there is an orthonormal basis e1, ..., e2k of T with dual basis w1, ..., w2k such that

{\displaystyle \iota ^{\ast }\omega =\sum _{j=1}^{k}\omega (e_{2j-1},e_{2j})w_{2j-1}\wedge w_{2j},}

where ι denotes the inclusion map from T into V. This implies

{\displaystyle \underbrace {\iota ^{\ast }\omega \wedge \cdots \wedge \iota ^{\ast }\omega } _{k{\text{ times}}}=k!\prod _{i=1}^{k}\omega (e_{2i-1},e_{2i})w_{1}\wedge \cdots \wedge w_{2k},}

which in turn implies

{\displaystyle (\underbrace {\omega \wedge \cdots \wedge \omega } _{k{\text{ times}}})(e_{1},\ldots ,e_{2k})=k!\prod _{i=1}^{k}\omega (e_{2i-1},e_{2i})\leq k!,}

The two-vector Cauchy–Schwarz estimate also gives |\omega(e_{2i-1},e_{2i})|\le1. Thus the displayed exterior-power value has absolute value at most k!, so the bound is unaffected by a change of orientation.

Finally, the dependence of the quantity

{\displaystyle (\underbrace {\omega \wedge \cdots \wedge \omega } _{k{\text{ times}}})(v_{1},\ldots ,v_{2k})}

on v1, ..., v2k is only on the quantity v1 ∧ ⋅⋅⋅ ∧ v2k, and from the orthonormality condition on v1, ..., v2k, this wedge product is well-determined up to a sign. This relates the above work with e1, ..., e2k to the desired statement in terms of v1, ..., v2k.

Submanifold consequence

Given a complex manifold with hermitian metric, the Wirtinger theorem immediately implies that for any 2k-dimensional embedded submanifold M, there is

{\displaystyle \operatorname {vol} (M)\geq {\frac {1}{k!}}\int _{M}\omega ^{k},}
