Statement

Let C=\mathsf{BP}\mathbin{\cdot}\oplus\mathsf P, using the bounded-error probability operator and parity-polynomial-time class. Then \mathsf{PH}\subseteq C. Here \mathsf{PH} is the polynomial hierarchy, obtained from \mathsf P by finitely many polynomially bounded existential/universal quantifier stages.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

These complexity-class operations and hierarchy definitions exceed the calculus/linear-algebra baseline.

Proof 1: Hierarchy induction from closure properties (outline)

Use the closure property

\Sigma^{\mathsf P}\mathbin{\cdot}\mathsf{BP}\mathbin{\cdot}\oplus\mathsf P \subseteq\mathsf{BP}\mathbin{\cdot}\oplus\mathsf P,

where \Sigma^{\mathsf P}\mathbin{\cdot} denotes applying a polynomially bounded existential quantifier. Its proof uses a variation of the Valiant–Vazirani theorem and is not supplied in the source.

Because \mathsf{BP}\mathbin{\cdot}\oplus\mathsf P contains \mathsf P and is closed under complement, it follows by induction that

\mathsf{PH}\subseteq\mathsf{BP}\mathbin{\cdot}\oplus\mathsf P.

These containment and complement-closure facts are also used without proof; the source leaves the hierarchy induction implicit.

Level justification

Induct using the stated class closures, but the full framework combines bounded-error class operators, parity computation and alternating polynomial quantifiers. These linked complexity notions are not defined sufficiently locally for a confidently small undergraduate extension; retain beyond undergraduate provisionally.
