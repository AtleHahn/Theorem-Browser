Statement

Let f be C1 on an open set D⊂R and a\in D with f^{\prime}(a)\ne0. There are open intervals I containing a and J=f(I) such that f maps I bijectively onto J and the inverse g is C1, with g^{\prime}(y)=1/f^{\prime}(g(y)).

Proof 1: One-variable inverse function theorem

We may without loss of generality assume that {\displaystyle f'(a)>0}. Given that {\displaystyle D} is an open set and {\displaystyle f'} is continuous at {\displaystyle a}, there exists {\displaystyle r>0} such that {\displaystyle (a-r,a+r)\subseteq D} and

{\displaystyle |f'(x)-f'(a)|<{\dfrac {f'(a)}{2}}\qquad {\text{for all }}|x-a|<r.}

In particular,

{\displaystyle f'(x)>{\dfrac {f'(a)}{2}}>0\qquad {\text{for all }}|x-a|<r.}

This shows that {\displaystyle f} is strictly increasing for all {\displaystyle |x-a|<r}. Let {\displaystyle \delta >0} be such that {\displaystyle \delta <r}. Then {\displaystyle [a-\delta ,a+\delta ]\subseteq (a-r,a+r)}. By the intermediate value theorem, we find that {\displaystyle f} maps the interval {\displaystyle [a-\delta ,a+\delta ]} bijectively onto {\displaystyle [f(a-\delta ),f(a+\delta )]}. Denote by {\displaystyle I=(a-\delta ,a+\delta )} and {\displaystyle J=(f(a-\delta ),f(a+\delta ))}. Then {\displaystyle f:I\to J} is a bijection and the inverse {\displaystyle f^{-1}:J\to I} exists. In particular, the result follows from the fact that if {\displaystyle f:I\to \mathbb {R} } is a strictly monotonic and continuous function that is differentiable at x_0\in I with f'(x_0)\neq0, then {\displaystyle f^{-1}:f(I)\to \mathbb {R} } is differentiable with {\displaystyle (f^{-1})'(y_{0})={\dfrac {1}{f'(x_0)}}}, where {\displaystyle y_{0}=f(x_0)} (a standard result in analysis). Applying this result at each point of I gives g'(y)=1/f'(g(y)). The inverse of a continuous strictly monotone function on an interval is continuous, so this formula and continuity of f' show that g' is continuous. This completes the proof.
