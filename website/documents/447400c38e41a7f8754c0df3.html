Statement

As positive integers n\to\infty, n!=\sqrt{2\pi n}(n/e)^n(1+1/(12n)+O(n^{-2})).

Level

Level justification

A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Gaussian change of variables — outline

For x>0, define \Gamma(x)=\int_0^\infty e^{-t}t^{x-1}\,dt. Two changes of variables give:

{\displaystyle \int _{\mathbb {R} }e^{x\left(1+t-e^{t}\right)}\,dt\ {\overset {t\mapsto \ln t}{=}}\ e^{x}\int _{0}^{\infty }t^{x-1}e^{-xt}\,dt\ {\overset {t\mapsto {\frac {t}{x}}}{=}}\ x^{-x}e^{x}\int _{0}^{\infty }e^{-t}t^{x-1}\,dt=x^{-x}e^{x}\Gamma (x).}

Now the function {\displaystyle t\mapsto 1+t-e^{t}} is unimodal, with maximum value zero. Locally around zero, it looks like {\textstyle -{\frac {t^{2}}{2}}}, which is why we are able to perform Laplace's method. In order to extend Laplace's method to higher orders, we perform another change of variables, with \tau having the sign of t, by {\textstyle 1+t-e^{t}=-{\frac {\tau ^{2}}{2}}}. This equation cannot be solved in closed form, but it can be solved by serial expansion, which gives us

{\displaystyle t=\tau -{\frac {\tau ^{2}}{6}}+{\frac {\tau ^{3}}{36}}+a_{4}\tau ^{4}+O\left(\tau ^{5}\right).}

Now plug back into the integral. Using the local Jacobian expansion in the higher-order Laplace method, with localization and tail control supplied by that method, gives

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}x^{-x}e^{x}\Gamma (x)&=\int _{\mathbb {R} }e^{-{\frac {x\tau ^{2}}{2}}}\left(1-{\frac {\tau }{3}}+{\frac {\tau ^{2}}{12}}+4a_{4}\tau ^{3}\right)\,d\tau+O(x^{-5/2}) \\&={\sqrt {2\pi }}\left(x^{-{\frac {1}{2}}}+{\frac {x^{-{\frac {3}{2}}}}{12}}\right)+O\left(x^{-{\frac {5}{2}}}\right).\end{aligned}}}

Notice how it is not actually necessary to find {\displaystyle a_{4}}, since it is cancelled out by the integral. Taking x=n and using \Gamma(n+1)=n\Gamma(n)=n! gives

n!=\sqrt{2\pi n}\left(\frac ne\right)^n\left(1+\frac1{12n}+O(n^{-2})\right).
