Statement

Let Q(x_1,\ldots,x_d), d\ge1, be a positive definite quadratic form with integer coefficients: a homogeneous polynomial of degree two satisfying Q(x)>0 for every nonzero real vector x. Say that Q represents an integer m if Q(x)=m for some x\in\mathbb Z^d. Then Q represents every positive integer if and only if it represents each member of

\{1,2,3,5,6,7,10,13,14,15,17,19,21,22,23,26,29,30,31,34,35,37,42,58,93,110,145,203,290\}.

Moreover, for each integer s in this set there is such a positive definite integral quadratic form that represents every positive integer except s. In particular, representing every integer from 1 through 290 suffices.

Proof 1: Escalator lattices and theta series (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorems 1–2, p.1; Sections 2–5 escalator and theta-series analysis; Section 6, proofs of Theorems 1–2, pp.13–14.).

Theorems 1 and 2 give the displayed list and a form missing exactly each critical integer. Section 6 reduces universality to the escalator classification in Sections 2–5; its indispensability construction adds four copies of [t+1] and one copy of [2t+1] to a lattice with truant t. The proof includes finite computational enumeration, theta-series coefficient estimates and local-density calculations.
