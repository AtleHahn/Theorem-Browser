Statement

Let X be a real vector space, M\subseteq X a vector subspace, f:M\to\mathbb R linear, and p:X\to\mathbb R convex, meaning p(tu+(1-t)v)\le tp(u)+(1-t)p(v) for 0\le t\le1. Assume f\le p on M. For every x\notin M, the interval with endpoints a=\sup_{m\in M,r>0}(f(m)-p(m-rx))/r and c=\inf_{n\in M,s>0}(p(n+sx)-f(n))/s is nonempty and finite, and every b\in[a,c] gives a dominated linear extension F_b(m+tx)=f(m)+tb to M\oplus\mathbb Rx.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra supplies a one-dimensional extension of a subspace. Analysis supplies infima and suprema, while convexity/sublinearity is explicitly defined by inequalities. These direct conditions suffice without topological vector-space or dual theory.

Proof 1: One-dimensional convex dominated extension

Given any m,n\in M and any positive real r,s>0, the positive real numbers t:=\frac{s}{r+s} and \frac{r}{r+s}=1-t sum to 1 so that the convexity of p on X guarantees

\begin{aligned} p\left(\frac{s}{r+s}m+\frac{r}{r+s}n\right) &=p\left(\frac{s}{r+s}(m-rx)+\frac{r}{r+s}(n+sx)\right)\\ &\le\frac{s}{r+s}p(m-rx)+\frac{r}{r+s}p(n+sx) \end{aligned}

and hence

\begin{aligned} sf(m)+rf(n) &=(r+s)f\left(\frac{s}{r+s}m+\frac{r}{r+s}n\right) &&\text{by linearity of }f\\ &\le(r+s)p\left(\frac{s}{r+s}m+\frac{r}{r+s}n\right) &&f\le p\text{ on }M\\ &\le sp(m-rx)+rp(n+sx), \end{aligned}

thus proving that -sp(m-rx)+sf(m)\le rp(n+sx)-rf(n), which after multiplying both sides by \frac1{rs} becomes

\frac1r[-p(m-rx)+f(m)]\le\frac1s[p(n+sx)-f(n)].

This implies that the values defined by

a=\sup_{\substack{m\in M\\r>0}}\frac1r[-p(m-rx)+f(m)] \qquad\text{and}\qquad c=\inf_{\substack{n\in M\\s>0}}\frac1s[p(n+sx)-f(n)]

are real numbers that satisfy a\le c. For any real b\in\mathbb R define the linear extension F_b:M\oplus\mathbb Rx\to\mathbb R by F_b(m+rx)=f(m)+rb. It can be verified that if a\le b\le c then F_b\le p, where rb\le p(m+rx)-f(m) follows from b\le c when r>0 (respectively, follows from a\le b when r<0). For r=0 it is the assumed inequality f(m)\le p(m).

Level justification

Use convexity to compare all lower and upper candidate slopes, take real suprema/infima and check each scalar sign in the extension. Undergraduate linear algebra and real analysis suffice; no topology or dual-space representation is needed.
