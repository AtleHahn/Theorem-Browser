Statement

Let A,B be events with 0<P(A)<1 and P(B\mid A^c)>0. For events E,F with P(F)>0, conditional probability means P(E\mid F)=P(E\cap F)/P(F). Write O(A)=P(A)/P(A^c) and O(A\mid B)=P(A\mid B)/P(A^c\mid B). Then

O(A\mid B)=O(A)\frac{P(B\mid A)}{P(B\mid A^c)}.
Level
High school only
Level justification

The finite-event notation is explained by the ratio definition; the statement uses elementary real-number arithmetic.

Proof 1: Divide the two posterior identities

Probabilities are sometimes specified in terms of odds. For any proposition {\displaystyle A}, the ratio of the probability that {\displaystyle A} is true to the probability that {\displaystyle A} is false is called the odds on {\displaystyle A}. Bayes' theorem can be written using odds as follows. First, apply Bayes' theorem to the probability that {\displaystyle A} is true given some other proposition {\displaystyle B}:

{\displaystyle P(A|B)=P(A){\frac {P(B|A)}{P(B)}}.}

Likewise, Bayes' theorem holds for the probability that {\displaystyle A} is false:

{\displaystyle P(A^c|B)=P(A^c){\frac {P(B|A^c)}{P(B)}}.}

Dividing these two equations, the probability {\displaystyle P(B)} drops out:

{\displaystyle {\frac {P(A|B)}{P(A^c|B)}}={\frac {P(A)}{P(A^c)}}{\frac {P(B|A)}{P(B|A^c)}}.}

Consequently, the odds of {\displaystyle A} given {\displaystyle B} are the odds of {\displaystyle A} multiplied by the ratio {\displaystyle P(B|A)/P(B|A^c)}, a quantity called the Bayes factor or likelihood ratio. In symbols:

{\displaystyle O(A|B)=O(A){\frac {P(B|A)}{P(B|A^c)}}.}

This is often summarized by saying that the posterior odds are the prior odds times the likelihood.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Apply Bayes to A and its complement and divide the resulting fractions, cancelling P(B). The assumptions imply P(B intersect A complement)>0, so both P(B) and the posterior denominator are positive. The argument is elementary probability and fraction algebra.
