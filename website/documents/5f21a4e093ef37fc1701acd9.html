Statement

Let R be a principal ideal domain and let a,b\in R. If d is a greatest common divisor of a,b (defined up to multiplication by a unit), then ax+by=d for some x,y\in R.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory ring theory and linear algebra supply principal ideals or matrix blocks and inverses. The statements are explicit ring identities or kernel-basis assertions; extending scalar entries to a field or unital ring introduces no additional theory needed for comprehension.

Proof 1: The ideal generated by two elements — outline

The ideal Ra+Rb is principal and equals Rd. Therefore d\in Ra+Rb, so d=ax+by for some x,y\in R.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory ring theory supplies ideals and divisibility. The extra PID condition says every ideal is generated by one element. A generator c of Ra+Rb divides a and b, while every common divisor divides c; a chosen gcd d differs by a unit and hence generates the same ideal. Membership then is precisely a linear combination. These are closely related elementary ring definitions, with no deeper module theory required.

Treatment:

Statement used:

In a principal ideal domain, any generator of Ra+Rb is a gcd of a and b; all gcd choices generate the same principal ideal.

Application in proof:

Identify Ra+Rb with Rd and infer that d is a combination of a and b.

Application level:

Undergraduate level

Level justification:

The assertion follows directly by comparing ideal membership and divisibility in an integral domain.

Review notes:

When a=b=0, the ideal and gcd are zero and the identity is immediate. For nonzero gcds, different choices differ by a unit.
