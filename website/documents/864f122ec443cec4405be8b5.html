Statement

Let \Omega be a set and D\subseteq\mathcal P(\Omega). Call D a Dynkin system when it contains \Omega, is closed under B\setminus A for A,B\in D with A\subseteq B, and contains the union of each increasing sequence of its members. If D is also closed under finite intersections, then D is a sigma-algebra: it contains \Omega and is closed under complements and arbitrary countable unions.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The Dynkin-system axioms are explicit closure conditions on a family of subsets. Sigma-algebra means closure under complements and countable unions; ordinary set operations and sequences suffice, with no measure or integration theory.

Proof 1: Pass from finite intersections to finite and countable unions — outline

Complements follow from the Dynkin difference axiom by taking the larger set to be \Omega. The difference and increasing-union conditions, together with finite-intersection closure, imply finite-union closure. Finite-union closure then implies countable-union closure by applying increasing-union closure to the finite partial unions.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Derive complement and finite-union closure, then express each countable union as the increasing union of its finite partial unions. The two set-family definitions are supplied and require only elementary countable set operations.
