Statement

Any n\ge3 distinct noncollinear Euclidean planar points determine at least n distinct connecting lines. The bound is attained by n-1 collinear points together with one point off that line.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement counts connecting lines through pairs of finitely many planar points.

Proof 1: Remove an endpoint of an ordinary line

The theorem is clearly true for three non-collinear points. We proceed by induction.

Assume n > 3 and the lower bound in the statement is true for n − 1. Let P be a set of n points not all collinear. The Sylvester–Gallai theorem states that there is a line containing exactly two points of P. Such two point lines are called ordinary lines. Let a and b be the two points of P on an ordinary line.

If the removal of point a produces a set of collinear points then P generates a near pencil of n lines (the n - 1 ordinary lines through a plus the one line containing the other n - 1 points).

Otherwise, the removal of a produces a set, P' , of n − 1 points that are not all collinear. By the induction hypothesis, P' determines at least n − 1 lines. The ordinary line determined by a and b is not among these, so P determines at least n lines.

The near-pencil count also shows that the bound is attained.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Use an ordinary line, delete one endpoint, and either count a near pencil or apply induction and restore that new line. This is finite Euclidean incidence counting.
