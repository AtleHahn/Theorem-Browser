Medium

Almost surely, the observed frequency of independent Bernoulli trials converges to their common probability. This supplies a precise connection between probability and long-run frequency, while relying on independence and a common distribution rather than deriving those assumptions from observation.

Statement

Let I_1,I_2,\ldots be independent Bernoulli random variables with common success probability p\in[0,1], and let N_n=\sum_{j=1}^n I_j. Then N_n/n\to p almost surely.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Bernoulli variables are success/failure bits with fixed success probability, and N_n/n is their observed success fraction. From elementary probability and undergraduate sequence limits, the added almost-sure condition says that the set of infinite trial outcomes for which the fractions converge has probability one. This concrete coin-trial formulation and independent-product probabilities fit a small preparation within about 30 minutes; general stochastic-process or measure-integration theory is unnecessary.

Proof 1: Bernoulli indicators and the strong law

It follows from the strong law of large numbers that the empirical probability of success in a series of Bernoulli trials will converge almost surely to the theoretical probability. For a Bernoulli random variable, the expected value is the theoretical probability of success, and the average of n such variables (assuming they are independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.)) is precisely the relative frequency. Here this average is N_n/n, and the expected value is p.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof applies the stated strong law to bounded iid Bernoulli variables, whose mean is p. Almost-sure convergence means that the set of sample outcomes on which the numerical averages converge has probability one. This is a small extension of undergraduate probability; no proof of the strong law or advanced stochastic process theory is used.
