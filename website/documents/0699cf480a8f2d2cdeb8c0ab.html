Statement

Let G be a finite Frobenius group: it has a nontrivial proper subgroup H such that H\cap gHg^{-1}=\{1\} for every g\in G\setminus H. Then G is not simple.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory group theory supplies subgroups, quotients, actions, products and homomorphisms. Perfect means generated by commutators, torsion means finite element orders, and the chain conditions are explicit sequences of subgroups. The relevant one of these closely related definitions is a small extension, without representation theory.

Proof 1: Use the nontrivial proper Frobenius kernel

By Frobenius’s theorem, a Frobenius group has a Frobenius kernel. This is a nontrivial proper normal subgroup, so the group is not simple.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The Frobenius condition is defined by subgroup intersections, and the supplied kernel theorem produces precisely the normal subgroup excluded by simplicity. Introductory finite group theory suffices without the theorem proof.
