Statement

For real random variables X,Y with finite second moments, define \operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=\mathbb E[(X-\mathbb EX)(Y-\mathbb EY)] and \operatorname{Var}(X)=\mathbb E[(X-\mathbb EX)^2]. Then |\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)|^2\le\operatorname{Var}(X)\operatorname{Var}(Y). If \operatorname{Var}(Y)>0, this gives \operatorname{Var}(X)\ge\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)^2/\operatorname{Var}(Y).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The definitions require general probability, even though the final inequality is scalar.

Proof 1: Center both random variables and apply the second-moment bound

After defining an inner product on real square-integrable random variables, identified when equal almost surely, using the expectation of their product,

{\displaystyle \langle X,Y\rangle :=\operatorname {E} (XY),}

the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality becomes

{\displaystyle {\bigl |}\operatorname {E} (XY){\bigr |}^{2}\leq \operatorname {E} (X^{2})\operatorname {E} (Y^{2}).}

To prove the covariance inequality using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, let {\displaystyle \mu =\operatorname {E} (X)} and {\displaystyle \nu =\operatorname {E} (Y),} then

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}{\bigl |}\operatorname {Cov} (X,Y){\bigr |}^{2}&={\bigl |}\operatorname {E} ((X-\mu )(Y-\nu )){\bigr |}^{2}\\&={\bigl |}\langle X-\mu ,Y-\nu \rangle {\bigr |}^{2}\\&\leq \langle X-\mu ,X-\mu \rangle \langle Y-\nu ,Y-\nu \rangle \\&=\operatorname {E} \left((X-\mu )^{2}\right)\operatorname {E} \left((Y-\nu )^{2}\right)\\&=\operatorname {Var} (X)\operatorname {Var} (Y),\end{aligned}}}

where {\displaystyle \operatorname {Var} } denotes variance and {\displaystyle \operatorname {Cov} } denotes covariance.

When \operatorname{Var}(Y)>0, dividing by it gives the stated quotient inequality.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The calculation is a direct Cauchy-Schwarz application, but this supplied proof constructs the inner-product space of arbitrary square-integrable random variables modulo almost-sure equality. That measure-theoretic quotient framework exceeds the baseline; retain beyond undergraduate provisionally rather than replacing it by a discrete argument.
