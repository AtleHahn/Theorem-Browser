Statement

Let (B_t)_{t\ge0} be standard Brownian motion starting at zero. For fixed t\ge0, define its local time at zero by the L^2 limit

L_t=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\frac1{2\varepsilon}\int_0^t\mathbf1_{\{|B_s|<\varepsilon\}}\,ds.

Then |B_t|=\int_0^t\operatorname{sgn}(B_s)\,dB_s+L_t, where \operatorname{sgn}(0)=0.

Beyond undergraduate level

Proof 1: Piecewise quadratic approximation (outline)

Approximate |x| by f_\varepsilon(x)=x^2/(2\varepsilon)+\varepsilon/2 on [-\varepsilon,\varepsilon], leaving it equal to |x| outside that interval. Apply Itô's formula to this approximation, and let \varepsilon\downarrow0. The second-derivative term is the occupation expression defining L_t, while the first-derivative integral tends to the integral of \operatorname{sgn}(B_s) and the function difference f_\varepsilon(B_t)-f_\varepsilon(0) tends to |B_t|.

The version of Itô's formula used here permits a continuously differentiable function with locally absolutely continuous derivative; the approximation is piecewise twice differentiable at \pm\varepsilon.

Level justification

Apply generalized Ito to the piecewise quadratic approximation and pass to stochastic-integral and occupation limits. Brownian stochastic integration, local time and L2 probability limits require advanced stochastic analysis.
