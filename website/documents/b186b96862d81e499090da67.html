Statement

Let K be an algebraically closed field, n\ge1 an integer, and A,B\in K^{n\times n}. If v\in K^n\setminus\{0\} and Av=\lambda Bv for \lambda\in K, then \det(A-\lambda B)=0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Matrices, determinants, kernels, linear independence and bases are standard undergraduate linear algebra. Over an algebraically closed field, the extra condition simply guarantees polynomial roots; infinite dimensions mean cardinalities of bases. The specific matrix or dimension claim adds no specialist construction.

Proof 1: Apply the nonzero-kernel determinant criterion

The generalized eigenvector equation is Av=\lambda Bv with v\ne0. Its possible values of \lambda therefore obey \det(A-\lambda B)=0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Rearrange to (A-lambda B)v=0 and use the nonzero-kernel criterion for a zero determinant. Standard undergraduate linear algebra over a field suffices.
