Statement

For a smooth Riemannian metric on S^2 invariant under x\mapsto-x, some p\in S^2 satisfies d(p,-p)^2\le(\pi/4)\operatorname{Area}(S^2).

Proof 1: Pass to the antipodal quotient — outline

Pu's inequality states that every Riemannian surface M homeomorphic to the real projective plane satisfies

\operatorname{Area}(M)\geq\frac2\pi\operatorname{Systole}(M)^2,

where the systole is the length of the shortest loop in M that cannot be contracted to a point in M.

The source gives the following reformulation: every metric on the sphere S^2 invariant under the antipodal map admits a pair of opposite points p,q\in S^2 at Riemannian distance d=d(p,q) satisfying

d^2\leq\frac{\pi}{4}\operatorname{Area}(S^2).

The quotient conversion is not expanded in the source's reformulation paragraph.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Applying Pu’s inequality requires a Riemannian quotient metric, the noncontractible-loop systole of the projective plane, and length-preserving lifts through its double cover. The quotient has half the sphere area, and a systolic loop lifts to an antipodal path, giving the stated constant. This combined Riemannian and covering-space framework is substantially beyond the undergraduate allowance.

Antipodal quotient metric and area under the double cover
Kind:

Theorem

Treatment:

Implicitly assumed

Statement used:

An antipodally invariant Riemannian metric on S^2 descends to a metric on RP^2 for which the quotient map is a two-sheeted local isometry; Area(RP^2)=Area(S^2)/2.

Application in proof:

Replace quotient area in Pu’s inequality by half the sphere area, producing the factor pi/4.

Application level:

Beyond undergraduate level

Level justification:

This uses descent of metrics under a free isometric group action and area under a finite Riemannian covering.
