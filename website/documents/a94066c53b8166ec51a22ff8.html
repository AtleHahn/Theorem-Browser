Statement

Let a sequent proof system be called consistent when its empty sequent \vdash is not derivable. Suppose every derivable sequent has a cut-free proof and the empty sequent has no cut-free proof. Then the system is consistent.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Consistency is defined in the statement. Understanding a sequent proof system and cut-free derivation additionally requires formal proof trees and a designated cut rule, a brief introduction to syntactic deduction over undergraduate discrete reasoning. The assertion assumes conversion to cut-free proofs; no proof of cut elimination or semantic completeness is required.

Proof 1: Apply cut elimination to a supposed empty-sequent proof

If the system has a cut elimination theorem, then if it has a proof of the empty sequent, it should also have a proof of the empty sequent without cuts. By hypothesis there are no such proofs. Thus the system is consistent.
