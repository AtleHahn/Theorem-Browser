Statement

Let D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\} and let H_2 be its holomorphic square-integrable functions with area measure dS. Every f\in H_2 satisfies

f(z)=\frac1\pi\iint_D\frac{f(\zeta)}{(1-\overline\zeta z)^2}\,dS(\zeta),\qquad z\in D.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Holomorphic functions and their area-integral representations require complex analysis beyond the calculus and linear-algebra baseline.

Proof 1: Substitution of a holomorphic function

Use Wirtinger's projection theorem as an explicit external dependency: for F\in L^2(D,dS), its orthogonal projection onto H_2 is

PF(z)=\frac1\pi\iint_D\frac{F(\zeta)}{(1-\overline\zeta z)^2}\,dS(\zeta).

Replacing F(\zeta) by f(\zeta), for f\in H_2, gives the representation formula.

Substitute a Bergman-space function into the L2 projection kernel. Holomorphic square-integrable Hilbert spaces and their integral projections require functional complex analysis.
