Statement

Let I be [a,b], [a,b) or [a,\infty), with a<b in the finite-endpoint cases. Let \beta\ge0 be measurable with \int_a^t\beta(s)\,ds<\infty for every t. Let u,\alpha be measurable real functions with \int_a^t|u(s)|\beta(s)\,ds<\infty and u(t)\le\alpha(t)+\int_a^t\beta(s)u(s)\,ds. Suppose either \alpha\ge0 or \int_a^t|\alpha(s)|\beta(s)\,ds<\infty for each t. Then u(t)\le\alpha(t)+\int_a^t\alpha(s)\beta(s)\exp(\int_s^t\beta(r)\,dr)\,ds.

Proof 1: Substitute a density measure

Set \mu(ds)=\beta(s)\,ds. This measure is atomless and finite on compact initial intervals. The hypotheses are exactly the measure version’s weighted integrability assumptions, and \mu((s,t))=\int_s^t\beta(r)\,dr. Substituting in that version gives the formula.

Define the atomless measure with density beta, check the weighted-integrability hypotheses and apply measure Gronwall. Construction of density measures and general measurable integration require advanced analysis.
