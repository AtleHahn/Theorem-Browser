Proof 1: Apply Carnot to the three midpoints

Use Carnot’s perpendicular theorem as an external prerequisite. For a nondegenerate Euclidean triangle ABC and points P_a,P_b,P_c on the supporting lines BC,CA,AB, respectively, the perpendiculars to these lines at those points concur if and only if

|AP_c|^2+|BP_a|^2+|CP_b|^2=|BP_c|^2+|CP_a|^2+|AP_b|^2.

Take P_c,P_a,P_b to be the midpoints of AB,BC,CA, respectively. Then |AP_c|=|BP_c|, |BP_a|=|CP_a| and |CP_b|=|AP_b|. The square-distance identity holds term by term. Carnot’s converse therefore implies that the three perpendicular bisectors are concurrent.
