Statement

Let f:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C be a smooth function that is zero outside a compact set. Define \partial_{\bar z}f=\tfrac12(\partial_x f+i\partial_y f) for z=x+iy. For every \zeta\in\mathbb C,

f(\zeta)=-\frac1\pi\iint_{\mathbb R^2}\frac{\partial_{\bar z}f(z)}{z-\zeta}\,dx\,dy,

where the integrable singularity at z=\zeta is interpreted by the improper area integral.

Proof 1: The compact support removes the boundary term — outline

Use the disk Cauchy–Pompeiu formula as an external prerequisite:

f(\zeta)=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{\partial D}\frac{f(z)}{z-\zeta}\,dz-\frac1\pi\iint_D\frac{\partial_{\bar z}f(z)}{z-\zeta}\,dx\,dy.

Choose a disk containing \zeta and the compact support of f in its interior. The boundary term vanishes because f=0 there, and the area term can be extended to the whole plane because the derivative is zero outside the support. This gives the claimed formula.

Level justification

The disk identity is explicitly supplied for smooth complex-valued functions, with barpartial defined by real partial derivatives. Choose a large disk, remove its zero boundary integral, and extend the area integral by zero. Real multivariable calculus with complex arithmetic and an improper integral suffices; holomorphic theory is not required for this application.
