Statement

Let R be an integral domain, n\ge1, and P\in R[x_1,\ldots,x_n] a nonzero polynomial of total degree d\ge0. For a finite nonempty S\subseteq R and independent uniform r_1,\ldots,r_n\in S, \Pr[P(r_1,\ldots,r_n)=0]\le d/|S|. Equivalently P has at most d|S|^{n-1} zeros in S^n.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Polynomial degree and integral-domain arithmetic are introductory algebra. Uniform independent sampling from a finite set is equivalent to the explicitly given finite zero count, so no general probability foundations are needed.

Proof 1: Induction on variables and the leading coefficient

The proof is by mathematical induction on n. For n = 1, P can have at most d roots. This gives us the base case. Now, assume that the statement holds for all polynomials in n − 1 variables. We can then consider P to be a polynomial in x1 by writing it as

{\displaystyle P(x_{1},\dots ,x_{n})=\sum _{i=0}^{d}x_{1}^{i}P_{i}(x_{2},\dots ,x_{n}).}

Since P is not identically 0, there is some i such that {\displaystyle P_{i}} is not identically 0. Take the largest such i. Then {\displaystyle \deg P_{i}\leq d-i}, since the degree of {\displaystyle x_{1}^{i}P_{i}} is at most d.

Now we randomly pick {\displaystyle r_{2},\dots ,r_{n}} from S. By the induction hypothesis, {\displaystyle \Pr[P_{i}(r_{2},\ldots ,r_{n})=0]\leq {\frac {d-i}{|S|}}.}

For each fixed choice of r_2,\ldots,r_n with P_i(r_2,\ldots,r_n)\ne0, the polynomial P(x_1,r_2,\ldots,r_n) has degree i (and thus is not identically zero), so at most i of the |S| choices for r_1 make it zero.

If we denote the event {\displaystyle P(r_{1},r_{2},\ldots ,r_{n})=0} by A, the event {\displaystyle P_{i}(r_{2},\ldots ,r_{n})=0} by B, and the complement of B by {\displaystyle B^{c}}, we have

\begin{aligned} \Pr[A]&=\Pr[A\cap B]+\Pr[A\cap B^c]\\ &\leq\Pr[B]+\Pr[B^c]\frac{i}{|S|}\\ &\leq\frac{d-i}{|S|}+\frac{i}{|S|} =\frac d{|S|}. \end{aligned}

Counting the equally likely tuples in S^n gives the equivalent zero-set bound.
