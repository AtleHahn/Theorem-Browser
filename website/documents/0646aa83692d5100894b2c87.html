Statement

For a closed connected oriented surface S, write \operatorname{Mod}(S) for orientation-preserving homeomorphisms modulo isotopy, and \operatorname{Mod}^{\pm}(S) when both orientations are allowed. Let S have genus g. Its action on H_1(S;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{2g} gives a homomorphism

\rho:\operatorname{Mod}(S)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb Z),

where a symplectic basis identifies the algebraic intersection form with J=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_g\\-I_g&0\end{pmatrix} and \operatorname{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb Z)=\{A\in\operatorname{GL}_{2g}(\mathbb Z):A^TJA=J\}.

Proof 1: Functorial homology preserves the intersection form

As singular homology is functorial and homotopy-invariant, the mapping class group \operatorname{Mod}(S) acts by automorphisms on the first homology group H_1(S;\mathbb Z). This is a free abelian group of rank 2g if S is closed of genus g. This action thus gives a linear representation

\operatorname{Mod}(S)\to\operatorname{GL}_{2g}(\mathbb Z).

Its image lies in the integer points \operatorname{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb Z) of the symplectic group. This comes from the fact that the intersection number of closed curves induces a symplectic form on the first homology, which is preserved by the action of the orientation-preserving mapping class group.
