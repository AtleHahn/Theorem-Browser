Statement

Let \pi_{4,a}(x) count primes below x congruent to a modulo 4. Then \pi_{4,1}(x)/\pi_{4,3}(x)\to1 as x\to\infty.

Proof 1: Arithmetic-progression prime theorem specialization (outline)

The source states the arithmetic-progression prime theorem: for coprime a,d, \pi_{d,a}(x)\sim\operatorname{Li}(x)/\varphi(d), where \operatorname{Li}(x)=\int_2^xdt/\log t and \varphi is Euler’s totient. Specialize to d=4, a=1,3. Since \varphi(4)=2, both counts are asymptotic to \operatorname{Li}(x)/2, so their quotient tends to 1.

Level justification

Specialize the explicitly stated progression prime theorem to residues one and three modulo four and divide the asymptotic equivalents. Undergraduate calculus limits and the defined logarithmic integral suffice; the proof of the prime theorem is not required.
