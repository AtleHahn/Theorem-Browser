Statement

Let L=\{a^ib^jc^k:i,j,k\ge0,\ i=0\text{ or }j=k\}. Then some integer p\ge1 has the property that every word w of length at least p in the language factors as w=xyz, |y|\ge1, |xy|\le p, with xy^nz in the language for every integer n\ge0. One may take p=1. Here words are finite strings of letters, juxtaposition means concatenation, |w| is the number of letters, and y^n means n repetitions of y, with y^0 the empty word.

Proof 1: Pump the first letter in two cases

Take p=1, and write \varepsilon for the empty word.

All nonempty words in the language satisfy the condition of the pumping lemma:

  • For all words in {\displaystyle L} with at least one {\displaystyle a}, we must also have {\displaystyle j=k} and may therefore choose {\displaystyle x=\varepsilon ,y=a} to obtain a decomposition that satisfies the pumping lemma.
  • For a word {\displaystyle w\in L} that does not contain an {\displaystyle a}, the condition {\displaystyle j=k} does not need to hold since {\displaystyle i=0}, so choose x=\varepsilon and y to be its first letter in a decomposition {\displaystyle w=xyz}, where {\displaystyle |y|=1}.

In both cases |xy|=1.

Level justification

The proof requires no pumping-lemma theorem: it directly checks the quantified pumping property. The reader follows finite words, concatenation, an empty word and the cases i>0 and i=0, including deletion when n=0. The nested quantification over words, decompositions and all repetitions makes undergraduate discrete mathematics a safer rating than high school only.
