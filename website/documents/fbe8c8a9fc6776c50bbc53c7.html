Proof 1: Write the six chords as sines

Choose four successive arcs of a circle of unit diameter with half-arc angles \theta_1,\theta_2,\theta_3,\theta_4. In the case of a circle of unit diameter the sides S_1,S_2,S_3,S_4 of the resulting cyclic quadrilateral ABCD are numerically equal to the sines of the angles \theta_1,\theta_2,\theta_3,\theta_4 which they subtend, by the law of sines. Similarly the diagonals are equal to the sine of the sum of whichever pair of angles they subtend.

We may then write Ptolemy's theorem, AB\cdot CD+BC\cdot DA=AC\cdot BD, in the following trigonometric form:

\sin\theta_1\sin\theta_3+\sin\theta_2\sin\theta_4 =\sin(\theta_1+\theta_2)\sin(\theta_1+\theta_4).
Level
High school only
Level justification

Construct the cyclic quadrilateral from the positive half-arcs, express its chords as sines and substitute in Ptolemy. School circle geometry and trigonometry suffice.
