Simpler proof assessment

Likely alternative prerequisite level: Undergraduate level; lowest recorded proof level: Beyond undergraduate level. Use the explicitly stated Rouche zero-count comparison on a small circle around an isolated zero of the nonzero limit; uniform convergence preserves its count, contradicting zero-freeness. Holomorphy of a locally uniform limit and isolated zeros are standard power-series consequences, not winding-number calculations. Apply the zero-free conclusion to f_n(z)-f_n(a) on a disk avoiding a, then the identity theorem for injectivity. The additional local holomorphic facts form a small package atop the statement and undergraduate analysis, without derivative contour integrals.

Statement

Let G\subset\mathbb C be a domain and f_n\to f uniformly on compact subsets. If every f_n is holomorphic and nowhere zero, then f is identically zero or nowhere zero. If every f_n is holomorphic and injective, then f is constant or injective.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Real analysis and calculus supply limits, derivatives, series and integration. Complex differentiability, primitives and logarithms are direct extensions, and circle integrals are parametrized ordinary integrals. The full claims use no Riemann surfaces, residues or abstract differential forms.

Proof 1: Limits of zero-free and univalent holomorphic functions (outline)

If the limit function is not identically zero, then its zeros have to be isolated. Zeros with multiplicities can be counted by the winding number {\displaystyle {\frac {1}{2\pi i}}\int _{C}\left(g(z)\right)^{-1}g'(z)\mathrm {d} z} for a holomorphic function {\displaystyle g}. Choose C as the boundary of a small zero-isolating disk, with no zeros on C. Locally uniform convergence of holomorphic functions also gives locally uniform convergence of their derivatives. Hence the integer winding numbers are continuous under these limits, so that if each function in the sequence has no zeros nor can the limit. For the second statement suppose that a\ne b and {\displaystyle f(a)=f(b)} and set {\displaystyle g_{n}(z)=f_{n}(z)-f_{n}(a)}. These are nowhere-vanishing on a disk about b not containing a, but {\displaystyle g(z)=f(z)-f(a)} vanishes at {\displaystyle b}, so {\displaystyle g} must vanish identically on that disk, and therefore on G by the identity theorem.

Use argument-principle zero counts and locally uniform derivative convergence, then apply the first conclusion to differences. Zero multiplicity, winding integrals and compact-uniform holomorphic convergence together exceed the small-extension allowance; retain conservatively.
