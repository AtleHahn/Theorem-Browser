Statement

Let X=\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}, S=\mathbb C, and S^{\prime}=\{0\}, with inclusion maps f:X\hookrightarrow S and g:S^{\prime}\hookrightarrow S. Then X^{\prime}=X\times_S S^{\prime}=\varnothing; let f^{\prime},g^{\prime} be the resulting maps. For a local system \mathcal F of abelian groups on X, the target f^{\prime}_*g^{\prime*}\mathcal F is zero, while g^*f_*\mathcal F is the monodromy-invariant subgroup of a stalk \mathcal F_x. Thus when this invariant subgroup is nonzero, the base-change map is not an isomorphism.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves local systems of abelian groups; monodromy invariants; sheaf stalks; punctured complex plane. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Monodromy invariants at the missing point

On the one hand {\displaystyle f'_{*}g'^{*}{\mathcal {F}}} is always zero, but if {\displaystyle {\mathcal {F}}} is a local system on {\displaystyle \mathbb {C} \setminus \{0\}} corresponding to a representation of the fundamental group {\displaystyle \pi _{1}(X)} (which is isomorphic to Z), then {\displaystyle g^{*}f_{*}{\mathcal {F}}} can be computed as the invariants of the monodromy action of {\displaystyle \pi _{1}(X,x)} on the stalk {\displaystyle {\mathcal {F}}_{x}} (for any {\displaystyle x\neq 0}), which need not vanish.

Level justification

Compare the empty-fiber direct image with the monodromy-invariant stalk of an open direct image. Local systems, sheaf operations, stalks and monodromy require graduate sheaf topology.
