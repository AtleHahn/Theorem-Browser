Statement

For a real random variable X, put M(t)=\mathbb E e^{tX} and C(a)=\inf_{t\in\mathbb R}M(t)e^{-ta}. Then C is log-concave, allowing zero values: for a,b\in\mathbb R and 0<\lambda<1,

C(\lambda a+(1-\lambda)b)\ge C(a)^\lambda C(b)^{1-\lambda}.
Level

Level justification

From undergraduate analysis the additional probability notion is expectation of a positive function as a possibly infinite weighted integral. This small addition and the given real-function inequality fit about 30 minutes, without a course on large deviations.

Proof 1: Use the convex conjugate

The negative logarithm of the two-sided Chernoff bound is known as the rate function (or Cramér transform) {\displaystyle I=-\log C}. It is equivalent to the Legendre–Fenchel transform or convex conjugate of the cumulant generating function {\displaystyle K=\log M}, defined as:

{\displaystyle I(a)=\sup _{t}at-K(t)}

The moment generating function is log-convex, so by a property of the convex conjugate, the Chernoff bound must be log-concave.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understand the complete statement by reading M(t) as the possibly infinite probability-weighted integral of exp(tX), a small extension of undergraduate integration. The displayed I(a)=sup_t(at-K(t)) is a supremum of affine functions and hence convex. Negating gives concavity of log C, with zero values handled by extended limits. No proof of a large-deviation theorem or general duality theory is used.
