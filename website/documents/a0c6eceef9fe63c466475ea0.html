Statement

For every integer n>1, there exists a full-rank lattice \Lambda\subseteq\mathbb R^n whose packing by equal Euclidean balls centered at lattice points has density at least

\frac{\zeta(n)}{2^{n-1}},\qquad \zeta(n)=\sum_{j=1}^\infty j^{-n}.

A full-rank lattice is A\mathbb Z^n for an invertible real matrix A, with covolume |\det A|. For the largest admissible ball radius r=\tfrac12\min_{0\ne v\in\Lambda}\|v\|_2, density is the volume of the radius-r ball divided by the covolume; the interiors of these translated balls are disjoint.

Proof 1: Mahler’s convex-body lattice bound (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (pp.611–621, culminating in the theorem on p.621.).

Let K be the unit ball and \Delta(K) the infimum of determinants of K-admissible lattices. Mahler proves \operatorname{vol}(K)/\Delta(K)\ge2\zeta(n)+a with a>0, so \Delta(K)<\operatorname{vol}(K)/(2\zeta(n)). By the definition of infimum, an admissible lattice has determinant below the latter quantity. Radius-1/2 balls then have density greater than \zeta(n)/2^{n-1}, which implies the stated non-strict bound.

Level justification

The statement uses lattice determinants, ball volumes and a convergent series. In Mahler’s dimensional recurrence for balls, every central section is again a ball, and the required section concavity follows by differentiating its explicit volume. The initial planar bound follows from the triangular lattice. Critical-lattice existence and boundary vectors follow from a fundamental-cell volume argument, bounded bases and finite-dimensional compactness. The remaining recurrence uses integration, inequalities and induction.
