Statement

Let Z\ge0 be a random variable with 0<\mathbb E[Z^2]<\infty, and put \mu=\mathbb E[Z] and \sigma^2=\operatorname{Var}(Z). For 0\le\theta\le1 with \sigma^2+(1-\theta)^2\mu^2>0, \mathbb P(Z>\theta\mu)\ge(1-\theta)^2\mu^2/(\sigma^2+(1-\theta)^2\mu^2).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Mean, variance and the tail event are scalar integral-moment notions, a limited extension of undergraduate integration.

Proof 1: Center the variable and apply Cauchy–Schwarz

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

{\displaystyle \operatorname {E} [Z-\theta \operatorname {E} [Z]]\leq \operatorname {E} [(Z-\theta \operatorname {E} [Z])\mathbf {1} _{\{Z>\theta \operatorname {E} [Z]\}}]\leq \operatorname {E} [(Z-\theta \operatorname {E} [Z])^{2}]^{1/2}\operatorname {P} (Z>\theta \operatorname {E} [Z])^{1/2}}

which, after rearranging, implies that

{\displaystyle \operatorname {P} (Z>\theta \operatorname {E} [Z])\geq {\frac {(1-\theta )^{2}\operatorname {E} [Z]^{2}}{\operatorname {E} [(Z-\theta \operatorname {E} [Z])^{2}]}}={\frac {(1-\theta )^{2}\operatorname {E} [Z]^{2}}{\operatorname {Var} Z+(1-\theta )^{2}\operatorname {E} [Z]^{2}}}.}
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Discard the nonpositive part of the centered variable, apply probabilistic Cauchy-Schwarz with an indicator, and expand its second moment into variance plus squared mean. Undergraduate probability and algebra suffice.
