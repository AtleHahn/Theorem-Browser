Statement

Let U\subseteq\mathbb C be nonempty, open and connected, and let f:U\to\mathbb C be nonconstant and holomorphic. Then f is open: f(V) is open in \mathbb C for every open subset V of U.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex differentiation is a direct calculus extension. Biholomorphism means a bijective holomorphic map with holomorphic inverse, and the square root is explicitly a holomorphic function satisfying h squared equals q. The remaining image, derivative and injectivity conditions are familiar analysis.

Proof 1: Stable zero count on a small disk

Let V be an arbitrary open subset of U. We have to show that every point in f(V) is an interior point of f(V), i.e. that every point in f(V) has a neighborhood (open disk) which is also in f(V).

Consider an arbitrary w_0 in f(V). Then there exists a point z_0 in V such that w_0=f(z_0). Since V is open, we can find d>0 such that the closed disk B around z_0 with radius d is fully contained in V. Consider the function g(z)=f(z)-w_0. Note that z_0 is a root of the function.

We know that g(z) is non-constant and holomorphic on the connected domain U. The roots of g are isolated by the identity theorem, so by further decreasing the radius of the disk B, we can ensure that z_0 is the only root of g(z) in B (although this single root may have multiplicity greater than 1).

The boundary of B is a circle and hence a compact set, on which |g(z)| is a positive continuous function, so the extreme value theorem guarantees the existence of a positive minimum e, that is, e is the minimum of |g(z)| for z on the boundary of B and e>0.

Denote by D the open disk around w_0 with radius e. By Rouché's theorem, the function g(z)=f(z)-w_0 will have the same number of roots (counted with multiplicity) in B as h(z):=f(z)-w_1 for any w_1 in D. This is because h(z)=g(z)+(w_0-w_1), and for z on the boundary of B, |g(z)|\ge e>|w_0-w_1|. Thus, for every w_1 in D, there exists at least one z_1 in B such that f(z_1)=w_1. This means that the disk D is contained in f(B).

The image of the ball B, f(B), is a subset of the image of V, f(V). Thus w_0 is an interior point of f(V). Since w_0 was arbitrary in f(V) we know that f(V) is open. Since V was arbitrary, the function f is open.
