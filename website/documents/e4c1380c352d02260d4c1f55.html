Statement

Let X be a Hausdorff locally convex topological vector space over \mathbb R or \mathbb C, and let K\subset X be nonempty, compact and convex. A map T:K\to K is affine if T(tx+(1-t)y)=tT(x)+(1-t)T(y) for 0\le t\le1. Let \mathcal S be any family of continuous affine self-maps of K that commute pairwise. Their common fixed-point set K^{\mathcal S}=\{y\in K:T(y)=y\text{ for every }T\in\mathcal S\} is nonempty, compact and convex.

Proof 1: Orbit-average lemma and invariant finite intersections

Let {\displaystyle T} be a continuous affine self-mapping of {\displaystyle K}.

For {\displaystyle x} in {\displaystyle K} define a net {\displaystyle \{x(N)\}_{N\in \mathbb {N} }} in {\displaystyle K} by

{\displaystyle x(N)={1 \over N+1}\sum _{n=0}^{N}T^{n}(x).}

Since {\displaystyle K} is compact, there is a convergent subnet in {\displaystyle K}:

{\displaystyle x(N_{i})\rightarrow y.\,}

To prove that {\displaystyle y} is a fixed point, it suffices to show that {\displaystyle f(Ty)=f(y)} for every {\displaystyle f} in the continuous dual of {\displaystyle X}. (The dual separates points by the Hahn-Banach theorem; this is where the assumption of local convexity is used.)

Since {\displaystyle K} is compact, {\displaystyle |f|} is bounded on {\displaystyle K} by a positive constant {\displaystyle M}. On the other hand

{\displaystyle |f(Tx(N))-f(x(N))|={1 \over N+1}|f(T^{N+1}x)-f(x)|\leq {2M \over N+1}.}

Taking {\displaystyle N=N_{i}} and passing to the limit as {\displaystyle i} goes to infinity (so that the subnet indices tend to infinity), it follows that

{\displaystyle f(Ty)=f(y).\,}

Hence

{\displaystyle Ty=y.\,}

For the commuting family, proceed as follows.

The set of fixed points of a single affine mapping {\displaystyle T} is a non-empty compact convex set {\displaystyle K^{T}} by the result for a single mapping. The other mappings in the family {\displaystyle \mathcal S} commute with {\displaystyle T} so leave {\displaystyle K^{T}} invariant. Applying the result for a single mapping successively, it follows that any finite subset of {\displaystyle \mathcal S} has a non-empty fixed point set given as the intersection of the compact convex sets {\displaystyle K^{T}} as {\displaystyle T} ranges over the subset. From the compactness of {\displaystyle K} it follows that the set

{\displaystyle K^{\mathcal S}=\{y\in K\mid Ty=y,\,T\in\mathcal S\}=\bigcap _{T\in\mathcal S}K^{T}\,}

is non-empty (and compact and convex).

Fixed-point sets are closed by continuity and Hausdorffness, and convex by affineness. Commutativity gives T(Rz)=R(Tz)=Rz for z\in K^T. For an empty family, the intersection is K.
