Statement

For every real M>0, there is a smooth embedded closed curve K\subset\mathbb R^3 that is ambient isotopic to a round circle and satisfies \int_K\kappa(s)\,ds>M. Here s is arc length, \kappa(s)=\|\gamma^{\prime\prime}(s)\| for an arc-length parametrization \gamma, and ambient isotopy is a continuous deformation of the ambient space through homeomorphisms starting from the identity.

Level justification

Knot isotopy and curvature together exceed the conservative undergraduate allowance, despite a simple existential conclusion.

Proof 1: Oscillating radial circles (external)

A supporting source can be found here (pp.248–252, total curvature and tangent turning). The deduction below supplies the argument for the stored statement.

The following construction is supplied here: for each integer n>=3 let gamma_n(theta)=((2+sin(n theta))cos(theta),(2+sin(n theta))sin(theta),0). Its radial coordinate is positive, so it is a smooth regular embedding. The ambient isotopy in cylindrical coordinates H_s(r,theta,z)=(r(1+(s/2)sin(n theta)),theta,z), with the axis fixed, consists of homeomorphisms and takes the radius-two circle to gamma_n at s=1. Continuity of H_s and its inverse at the axis follows from factors between 1/2 and 3/2. Put r=2+sin(n theta); a continuous tangent angle is alpha(theta)=theta+arg(r prime+i r), with the second argument in (0,pi). At theta_j=j*pi/n it alternates between a=arctan(2/n) and pi-a. Consequently the total variation of alpha, equal to integral kappa ds, is at least 2n(pi-2arctan(2/n)-pi/n), which tends to infinity. Choose n large enough.
