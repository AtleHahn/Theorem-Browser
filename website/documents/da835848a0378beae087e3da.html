Statement

Let A_n be events on a probability space and define S_{m,n}=\sum_{i=m}^n\mathbf1_{A_i} for 1\le m\le n, with S_{1,0}=0. Assume \mathbb E S_{1,n}\to\infty and

c=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{(\mathbb E S_{1,n})^2}{\mathbb E[S_{1,n}^2]}>0.

Then

\mathbb P\left(\bigcap_{m\ge1}\bigcup_{i\ge m}A_i\right)\ge c.

The ratios are taken for sufficiently large n, when their denominators are positive.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The random variables here are finite sums of event indicators: their expectations and second moments are finite sums of event and pairwise-intersection probabilities. Countable event limits and a numerical limsup are a limited extension of undergraduate analysis.

Proof 1: Second moments and removal of finitely many events

We have that

\mathbb P\left(\bigcup_{i=m}^n A_i\right)=\mathbb P(S_{m,n}>0).

Now, notice that by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, for any square-integrable random variable X\geq0:

(\mathbb EX)^2=(\mathbb E[X\mathbf1_{\{X>0\}}])^2 \leq\mathbb E[X^2]\mathbb P(X>0),

therefore, for sufficiently large n,

\mathbb P(S_{m,n}>0)\geq \frac{(\mathbb ES_{m,n})^2}{\mathbb E[S_{m,n}^2]}.

We then have, since 0\leq S_{m,n}\leq S_{1,n},

\frac{(\mathbb ES_{m,n})^2}{\mathbb E[S_{m,n}^2]} \geq\frac{(\mathbb E[S_{1,n}-S_{1,m-1}])^2}{\mathbb E[S_{1,n}^2]}.

Given m, since \lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb ES_{1,n}=\infty, we can find n large enough so that

\left|\frac{\mathbb ES_{1,n}-\mathbb ES_{1,m-1}}{\mathbb ES_{1,n}}-1\right|<\epsilon,

for any given \epsilon>0. Thus, taking the upper limit in n for this fixed m, and using continuity of probability from below, gives

\mathbb P\left(\bigcup_{i\geq m}A_i\right)\geq c.

Therefore,

\lim_{m\to\infty}\sup_{n\geq m}\mathbb P\left(\bigcup_{i=m}^nA_i\right) \geq\lim_{m\to\infty}\sup_{n\geq m} \frac{(\mathbb ES_{1,n})^2}{\mathbb E[S_{1,n}^2]}=c>0.

But the left side is precisely the probability that the A_n occur infinitely often, by continuity of probability from above, since

\{A_k\text{ i.o.}\}=\{\omega\in\Omega:\forall m,\exists n\geq m\text{ such that }\omega\in A_n\}.

We are done, since we have shown that \mathbb P(A_k\text{ i.o.})\geq c.
