Statement

Let k be a commutative ring with identity, and let k\langle X\rangle be the free associative k-algebra on a set X: its elements are finite k-linear combinations of words in X, including the empty word 1, and multiplication concatenates words. Fix a total order < on words such that adjoining the same words on the left and right preserves strict inequalities and each word has only finitely many predecessors.

Let I be the two-sided ideal generated by relations g_\sigma=w_\sigma-f_\sigma, where w_\sigma is a word and every word occurring in f_\sigma is smaller than w_\sigma. A reduction r_{u\sigma v} is the k-linear map replacing the word uw_\sigma v by uf_\sigma v and fixing all other words. A word is reduced if it contains no w_\sigma as a contiguous subword.

An overlap ambiguity is a word w=tvu with nonempty t,v,u and w_\sigma=tv, w_\tau=vu; it is resolvable if r_{1\sigma u}(w) and r_{t\tau1}(w) can be reduced to the same expression by finite sequences of reductions. An inclusion ambiguity is a word w=w_\sigma=tw_\tau u, with t,u possibly empty; it is resolvable if r_{1\sigma1}(w) and r_{t\tau u}(w) can be so reduced.

The following conditions are equivalent:

  1. Every overlap and inclusion ambiguity is resolvable.
  2. Every element of k\langle X\rangle has a unique reduced form: any two finite sequences of reductions ending in linear combinations of reduced words give the same result.
  3. The images of the reduced words form a k-module basis of k\langle X\rangle/I; equivalently, every coset has a unique finite k-linear expression in these images.
