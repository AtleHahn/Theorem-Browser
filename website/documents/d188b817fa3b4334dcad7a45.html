Statement

For a,b,c,x,y,z\in\mathbb R^3,

\det\begin{pmatrix}a\cdot x&a\cdot y&a\cdot z\\b\cdot x&b\cdot y&b\cdot z\\c\cdot x&c\cdot y&c\cdot z\end{pmatrix}=[a\cdot(b\times c)][x\cdot(y\times z)].
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Three-dimensional vectors; Dot and cross products; Determinants. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Convert the full minors into scalar triple products

Use the Cauchy–Binet identity

\det(AB)=\sum_{\substack{S\subseteq[n]\\|S|=m}} \det(A_{[m],S})\det(B_{S,[m]}),

where A is m\times n, B is n\times m, and the selected column and row indices are increasing. Taking m=n=3, A with rows a,b,c, and B with columns x,y,z, there is only the full three-element subset. Thus

\begin{aligned} &\begin{vmatrix} a\cdot x&a\cdot y&a\cdot z\\ b\cdot x&b\cdot y&b\cdot z\\ c\cdot x&c\cdot y&c\cdot z \end{vmatrix}\\ &=\begin{vmatrix}a_1&a_2&a_3\\b_1&b_2&b_3\\c_1&c_2&c_3\end{vmatrix} \begin{vmatrix}x_1&y_1&z_1\\x_2&y_2&z_2\\x_3&y_3&z_3\end{vmatrix}\\ &=[a\cdot(b\times c)][x\cdot(y\times z)]. \end{aligned}

Level justification

Use Cauchy-Binet for square three-dimensional matrices, then identify each determinant as a scalar triple product. Standard vector and matrix algebra suffice.
