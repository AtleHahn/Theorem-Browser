Statement

Let G be a finite simple undirected graph with n vertices and e edges. A plane drawing places vertices at distinct points and draws edges as arcs between their endpoints, with no vertex in an edge interior; crossings are intersections of edge interiors. Drawings may be taken with finitely many transverse crossings and no triple crossings. Let \operatorname{cr}(G) be the minimum number of crossings over these drawings. Then \operatorname{cr}(G)\ge e-3n; for n\ge3 the same argument gives \operatorname{cr}(G)\ge e-3n+6.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The drawing conventions and crossing count are locally explained finite graph and elementary plane geometry.

Proof 1: Delete one edge at each crossing

We first give a preliminary estimate: for any finite simple graph G with n vertices and e edges, we have

{\displaystyle \operatorname {cr} (G)\geq e-3n.}

To prove this, consider a diagram of G which has exactly cr(G) crossings. Each of these crossings can be removed by removing an edge from G. Thus we can find a graph with at least e − cr(G) edges and n vertices with no crossings, and is thus a planar graph. But from Euler's formula we must then have e − cr(G) ≤ 3n, and the claim follows. (In fact we have e − cr(G) ≤ 3n − 6 for n ≥ 3).

Level
High school only
Level justification

Remove at most one edge per crossing and apply the supplied planar edge bound to the remaining simple graph. Rearranging the inequality gives both versions.
