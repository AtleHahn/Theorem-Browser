Statement

A dependency graph for events A_1,\ldots,A_n means that each A_i is independent of the sigma-algebra generated by all A_j with j\ne i that are not its neighbours. Write \Gamma(i) for its neighbours. Suppose this graph has maximum degree at most the integer d\ge0, each \Pr(A_i)\le p, and ep(d+1)\le1, where e=\exp(1). Then \Pr(\bigcap_iA_i^c)>0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The events form a finite family. The generated sigma-algebra here is only the finite collection of their Boolean combinations, and independence means the usual product rule on those combinations. The dependency graph and explicit finite inequalities add a small extension of elementary finite probability.

Proof 1: Assign equal asymmetric parameters

Use the asymmetric local lemma: if a dependency graph admits x_i\in[0,1) with \Pr(A_i)\le x_i\prod_{j\in\Gamma(i)}(1-x_j), then \Pr(\bigcap_iA_i^c)\ge\prod_i(1-x_i)>0.

For d\ge1, the symmetric version follows immediately from the asymmetric version by setting

x_i=\frac1{d+1}\qquad(1\le i\le n)

to get the sufficient condition

p\le\frac1{d+1}\cdot\frac1e

since

\frac1e\le\left(1-\frac1{d+1}\right)^d.

For d=0 the events are jointly independent; their complementary intersection has probability \prod_i(1-\Pr(A_i))>0, because p\le1/e<1.

Level justification

Choose equal parameters in the explicit finite-event asymmetric lemma and use the scalar exponential bound. Undergraduate discrete probability suffices; the d=0 case is a finite independence product.
