Statement

Let A=(a_{rs}) be an n\times n Hermitian matrix, j\ne k two indices, and 0\le t\le1. There is a unitarily conjugate matrix B with the same eigenvalues, with b_{jj}=ta_{jj}+(1-t)a_{kk}, b_{kk}=(1-t)a_{jj}+ta_{kk}, and b_{ll}=a_{ll} for all l\notin\{j,k\}.

Proof 1: Choose a phase and conjugate by a unitary block — outline

Let {\displaystyle \xi } be a complex number of modulus {\displaystyle 1} such that {\displaystyle {\overline {\xi a_{jk}}}=-\xi a_{jk}} and {\displaystyle U} be a unitary matrix with {\displaystyle \xi {\sqrt {t}},{\sqrt {t}}} in the {\displaystyle j,j} and {\displaystyle k,k} entries, respectively, {\displaystyle -{\sqrt {1-t}},\xi {\sqrt {1-t}}} at the {\displaystyle j,k} and {\displaystyle k,j} entries, respectively, {\displaystyle 1} at all diagonal entries other than {\displaystyle j,j} and {\displaystyle k,k,} and {\displaystyle 0} at all other entries. Then {\displaystyle UAU^{-1}} has {\displaystyle ta_{jj}+(1-t)a_{kk}} at the {\displaystyle j,j} entry, {\displaystyle (1-t)a_{jj}+ta_{kk}} at the {\displaystyle k,k} entry, and {\displaystyle a_{ll}} at the {\displaystyle l,l} entry where {\displaystyle l\neq j,k.}

Unitary conjugation preserves the eigenvalues.
