Statement

Let n>2, let \Omega\subset\mathbb R^n be open, and let a\in\mathbb R^n\setminus\Omega, b\in\mathbb R^n, \alpha\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}, A\in SO(n) and \varepsilon\in\{0,2\}. Here SO(n) denotes the real orthogonal matrices with determinant one. The smooth map f(x)=b+\frac{\alpha A(x-a)}{|x-a|^\varepsilon} is conformal on \Omega: its derivative at every point is a nonzero real scalar times an orthogonal matrix.

Proof 1: Factor the derivative into a scalar and an orthogonal matrix — outline

Write u=(x-a)/|x-a|. The displayed derivative is Df(x)=\frac{\alpha}{|x-a|^\varepsilon}A(I-\varepsilon uu^{\mathsf T}). The factor in parentheses is I when \varepsilon=0 and the Householder reflection I-2uu^{\mathsf T} when \varepsilon=2, hence is orthogonal. Thus its product with the rotation A is orthogonal. Since x\ne a and \alpha\ne0, the scalar prefactor is nonzero, giving conformality at every point.
