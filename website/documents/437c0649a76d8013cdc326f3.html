Statement

For nonnegative quotas q_1,\ldots,q_n summing to an integer h>0, first give each agent \lfloor q_i\rfloor seats and give the remaining seats to agents in decreasing order of quota, allowing arbitrary choices between ties. This rule is weakly exact: when every quota is an integer, the allocation is the quota vector. But with h=6 and q^{(k)}=(4+1/k,2-1/k) for integers k\geq1, it returns (5,1) for every k, although q^{(k)}\to(4,2) and the rule returns (4,2) at that limit.

Proof 1: Compute floors along the prescribed quota sequence

Consider the following rule: (a) give each agent its quota rounded down, \lfloor q_i\rfloor; (b) give the remaining seats to the largest parties in decreasing order of quota. This rule is weakly exact, but does not have the limiting exactness property.

For example, suppose h=6 and consider the sequence of quota vectors (4+1/k,2-1/k). The rule yields the allocation (5,1) for all k, even though the limit when k\to\infty is the integer vector (4,2). At that integer vector the rule returns (4,2). The source supplies the example without expanding the floor calculations.
