Statement

A topological space X admits a metric inducing its topology if and only if it is regular, is T_0, and has a basis \mathcal B=\bigcup_{n\ge1}\mathcal B_n with each \mathcal B_n discrete. Here T_0 means that for distinct points some open set contains one but not the other; regular means that a point and a closed set not containing it have disjoint open neighborhoods. A basis is a family of open sets whose unions give every open set. A family of subsets is discrete if every point has an open neighborhood meeting at most one member of the family.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Basic point-set topology supplies the concepts; the sigma-discrete condition is explicitly a countable union of locally separated families, a modest extension.
