Statement

Let K be a number field, that is, a finite extension of \mathbb Q, and let \mathcal O_K be its ring of integers, consisting of the elements of K that satisfy a monic polynomial with coefficients in \mathbb Z. For a nonzero ideal \mathfrak a\subseteq\mathcal O_K, define its norm by

N\mathfrak a=|\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak a|.

Let \pi_K(X) count the nonzero prime ideals \mathfrak p of \mathcal O_K with N\mathfrak p\leq X. A prime ideal is a proper ideal such that ab\in\mathfrak p implies a\in\mathfrak p or b\in\mathfrak p.

Then

\pi_K(X)\sim\frac{X}{\log X}\qquad(X\to\infty),

meaning that \pi_K(X)/(X/\log X) tends to 1; \log denotes the natural logarithm.

Level justification

Number fields, their rings of integers, prime ideals and finite quotient norms must be understood together; their cumulative algebraic-number-theory burden exceeds a short extension of introductory algebra.

Lemma 9 transfers this to Möbius cancellation, and Lemma 1 gives the prime-ideal asymptotic. The convergent sum \sum_{\mathfrak a}N\mathfrak a^{-2} still makes that tail tend to zero.
