Statement

Let L be a finite distributive lattice and let \mu:L\to[0,\infty) satisfy

\mu(x\wedge y)\mu(x\vee y)\geq\mu(x)\mu(y)\qquad(x,y\in L).

Then for every pair of increasing functions f,g:L\to\mathbb R,

\left(\sum_{x\in L}f(x)g(x)\mu(x)\right)\left(\sum_{x\in L}\mu(x)\right) \geq\left(\sum_{x\in L}f(x)\mu(x)\right)\left(\sum_{x\in L}g(x)\mu(x)\right).

The inequality also holds if both functions are decreasing, and reverses if one is increasing and the other decreasing.

A lattice is a partially ordered set in which each pair has a greatest lower bound x\wedge y and a least upper bound x\vee y. Distributivity means each of these two operations distributes over the other. Increasing means x\leq y implies f(x)\leq f(y); decreasing means the reverse inequality. No positivity of f,g, strict positivity of \mu, or normalization of its total mass is required.

Proof availability

Source proof sketch and limitations

The source invokes Holley's inequality with weights \mu_1=g\mu and \mu_2=\mu. When these weights meet Holley's stated positivity assumptions, stochastic domination gives \langle fg\rangle_\mu/\langle g\rangle_\mu\geq\langle f\rangle_\mu, yielding the claimed correlation inequality.

As written, that argument does not cover the whole statement: g may have either sign and \mu may vanish. The article supplies neither the required reductions nor a zero-weight limiting argument preserving the lattice condition. It also sketches a monotone Markov-chain approach for product configurations, without the full construction for an arbitrary finite distributive lattice. These limitations concern the supplied argument; the statement above retains the full source theorem.
