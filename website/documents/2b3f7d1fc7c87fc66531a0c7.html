Statement

Let K\subset\mathbb C be compact, and let f be holomorphic on an open neighborhood of K. Let A\subset\widehat{\mathbb C}\setminus K meet each connected component of the complement in the Riemann sphere. Then rational functions with poles only in A approximate f uniformly on K.

Proof 1: Approximate a Cauchy integral and transport poles — outline

There is a closed piecewise-linear boundary cycle \Gamma in the holomorphic neighborhood, surrounding K and disjoint from it; a boundary cycle allows for holes in K. By Cauchy's integral formula

f(w)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_\Gamma\frac{f(z)\,dz}{z-w}

for w in K. Riemann approximating sums can be used to approximate the contour integral uniformly over K. Each term in the sum is a scalar multiple of (z-w)^{-1} for some point z on the contour. This gives a uniform approximation by a rational function with poles on \Gamma.

To modify this to an approximation with poles at specified points in each component of the complement of K, it is enough to check this for terms of the form (z-w)^{-1}. If z_0 is a chosen finite point in the same component as z, take a path from z to z_0.

If two points a,b are sufficiently close on the path, we may use the formula

\frac{1}{w-a}=\frac{1}{w-b}\sum_{n=0}^\infty\left(\frac{a-b}{w-b}\right)^n

(verified by geometric series), valid on the circle-complement |a-b|<|w-b|; note that the chosen path has a positive distance to K by compactness. Choose the steps so that |a-b|<\inf_{w\in K}|w-b|. That series can be truncated to give a rational function with poles only at the second point uniformly close to the original function on K. Proceeding by finitely many steps along the path from z to z_0, the original function (z-w)^{-1} can be successively modified to give a rational function with poles only at z_0.

If the chosen point is infinity, then by the above procedure the rational function (z-w)^{-1} can first be approximated by a rational function g with poles at R>0 where R is so large that K lies in |w|<R. The Taylor series expansion of g about 0 can then be truncated to give a polynomial approximation on K.

Applying this to the finitely many terms of a Riemann sum gives the required approximation. The source sketches the uniform Riemann-sum estimate, treatment of higher-order poles and accumulated approximation errors without spelling them out.
