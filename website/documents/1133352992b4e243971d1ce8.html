Statement

Let p be prime and h,k positive even integers neither divisible by p−1. Let B_r denote the r-th Bernoulli number. If h≡k modulo p−1, then B_h/h\equiv B_k/k\pmod p. Rational congruences are interpreted in the p-integral rationals \mathbb Z_{(p)}=\{u/v:u,v\in\mathbb Z,\ p\nmid v\}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Calculus and number theory supply power-series coefficients and congruences. Bernoulli numbers have a single generating-series definition; the statement explicitly extends congruences to the displayed rational numbers. No p-adic field or extension arithmetic is required.

Proof 1: Set a=0 in the general congruence

Use the general Kummer congruence: for a≥0 and h≡k modulo φ(p^{a+1}), $ (1-p^{h-1})B_h/h\equiv(1-p^{k-1})B_k/k\pmod{p^{a+1}}$. Here φ is Euler’s totient. The source instructs taking a=0. Then φ(p)=p−1 and the modulus is p. Since h,k≥2, both factors 1−p^{h−1} and 1−p^{k−1} are congruent to 1 modulo p. Using the p-integrality of B_h/h and B_k/k in this setting, reduction modulo p gives the claimed simpler congruence.

Level justification

Set the modulus parameter to zero and reduce the two factors to one using p-integrality. Undergraduate number theory suffices with Bernoulli numbers as a rational sequence and rational congruence defined by denominators prime to p; no p-adic completion is used.
