Statement

Every self-adjoint operator on a finite-dimensional real or complex inner-product space has an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves finite-dimensional real or complex inner-product spaces; self-adjoint operators; orthonormal eigenbases. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Maximize the norm, then pass to an orthogonal complement — outline

Let {\displaystyle A} be a self-adjoint operator on a finite-dimensional inner product space and {\displaystyle \mathbf {u} } be a non-zero vector which maximizes {\displaystyle {\frac {\|A\mathbf {u} \|}{\|\mathbf {u} \|}}}. The existence of {\displaystyle \mathbf {u} } is guaranteed by the Heine-Borel theorem. If {\displaystyle A\mathbf {u} =\mathbf {0} } then {\displaystyle \mathbf {u} } is an eigenvector of {\displaystyle A}. Otherwise the maximizing property of {\displaystyle \mathbf {u} } implies that

{\displaystyle {\frac {\|A\mathbf {u} \|}{\|\mathbf {u} \|}}\geq {\frac {\|A(A\mathbf {u} )\|}{\|A\mathbf {u} \|}}.}

In the other direction, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality implies that

{\displaystyle \|A\mathbf {u} \|^{2}=\langle A\mathbf {u} ,A\mathbf {u} \rangle =\langle \mathbf {u} ,A^{2}\mathbf {u} \rangle \leq \|\mathbf {u} \|\|A^{2}\mathbf {u} \|,}

hence equality holds. By the characterization of equality, {\displaystyle \mathbf {u} } and {\displaystyle A^{2}\mathbf {u} } are linearly dependent, therefore {\displaystyle \mathbf {u} } is an eigenvector of {\displaystyle A^{2}}. From here it is straightforward to deduce that {\displaystyle A} has an eigenvector, then the spectral theorem follows by taking the orthogonal complement and arguing by induction on the dimension of the inner product space.

The zero-dimensional case is immediate. The source leaves the eigenvector deduction and the orthogonal-complement induction as an outline.
