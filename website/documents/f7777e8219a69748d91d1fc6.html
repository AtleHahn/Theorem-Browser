Statement

Let X be a real random variable on a probability space with finite second moment, mean \mu=\mathbb E X, and variance \sigma^2=\mathbb E[(X-\mu)^2]. Let \lambda>0. Then \Pr(X-\mu\ge\lambda)\le\frac{\sigma^2}{\lambda^2}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The mean and variance are given by explicit expectation formulas. Interpreting expectation as a probability-weighted integral and the event as a real threshold adds closely related definitions to undergraduate analysis within about 30 minutes. No infinite sampling, limiting law or measure construction is needed for the statement.

Proof 1: Containment in the two-sided deviation event

Use Chebyshev’s inequality \Pr(|X-\mu|\ge\lambda)\le\sigma^2/\lambda^2. The upper-tail event is contained in the two-sided event, so \Pr(X-\mu\ge\lambda)\le\Pr(|X-\mu|\ge\lambda)\le\frac{\sigma^2}{\lambda^2}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The upper-tail event is contained in the two-sided deviation event. Combine probability monotonicity with the supplied variance bound. Ordinary undergraduate mean and variance notation is the only background beyond elementary set inclusion.
