Statement

For 0<\alpha<\pi, I(\alpha):=\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\log(1+\cos\alpha\cos x)}{\cos x}\,dx=\frac{\pi^2}8-\frac{\alpha^2}2. At x=\pi/2, interpret the integrand by its continuous limit \cos\alpha.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Real logarithms; Definite integrals; Trigonometric functions. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Differentiate and integrate a half-angle rational function

The endpoint extension follows from \log(1+u)/u\to1. On each compact parameter interval in (0,\pi), the integrand and its parameter derivative extend continuously.

Differentiating under the integral with respect to {\displaystyle \alpha }, we have

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}{\frac {d}{d\alpha }}I(\alpha )&=\int _{0}^{\pi /2}{\frac {\partial }{\partial \alpha }}\left({\frac {\ln(1+\cos \alpha \cos x)}{\cos x}}\right)\,dx\\[6pt]&=-\int _{0}^{\pi /2}{\frac {\sin \alpha }{1+\cos \alpha \cos x}}\,dx\\&=-\int _{0}^{\pi /2}{\frac {\sin \alpha }{\left(\cos ^{2}{\frac {x}{2}}+\sin ^{2}{\frac {x}{2}}\right)+\cos \alpha \left(\cos ^{2}{\frac {x}{2}}-\sin ^{2}{\frac {x}{2}}\right)}}\,dx\\[6pt]&=-{\frac {\sin \alpha }{1-\cos \alpha }}\int _{0}^{\pi /2}{\frac {1}{\cos ^{2}{\frac {x}{2}}}}{\frac {1}{{\frac {1+\cos \alpha }{1-\cos \alpha }}+\tan ^{2}{\frac {x}{2}}}}\,dx\\[6pt]&=-{\frac {2\sin \alpha }{1-\cos \alpha }}\int _{0}^{\pi /2}{\frac {{\frac {1}{2}}\sec ^{2}{\frac {x}{2}}}{{\frac {2\cos ^{2}{\frac {\alpha }{2}}}{2\sin ^{2}{\frac {\alpha }{2}}}}+\tan ^{2}{\frac {x}{2}}}}\,dx\\[6pt]&=-{\frac {2\left(2\sin {\frac {\alpha }{2}}\cos {\frac {\alpha }{2}}\right)}{2\sin ^{2}{\frac {\alpha }{2}}}}\int _{0}^{\pi /2}{\frac {1}{\cot ^{2}{\frac {\alpha }{2}}+\tan ^{2}{\frac {x}{2}}}}\,d\left(\tan {\frac {x}{2}}\right)\\[6pt]&=-2\cot {\frac {\alpha }{2}}\int _{0}^{\pi /2}{\frac {1}{\cot ^{2}{\frac {\alpha }{2}}+\tan ^{2}{\frac {x}{2}}}}\,d\left(\tan {\frac {x}{2}}\right)\\[6pt]&=-2\arctan \left(\tan {\frac {\alpha }{2}}\tan {\frac {x}{2}}\right){\bigg |}_{0}^{\pi /2}\\[6pt]&=-\alpha .\end{aligned}}}

Therefore:

{\displaystyle I(\alpha )=C-{\frac {\alpha ^{2}}{2}}.}

But {\textstyle I{\left({\frac {\pi }{2}}\right)}=0} by definition so {\textstyle C={\frac {\pi ^{2}}{8}}} and

{\displaystyle I(\alpha )={\frac {\pi ^{2}}{8}}-{\frac {\alpha ^{2}}{2}}.}
