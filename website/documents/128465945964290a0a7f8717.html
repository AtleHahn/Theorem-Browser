Statement

Let \xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n be independent real random variables, n\ge1, and let \alpha_j,\beta_j be nonzero real constants such that \beta_i/\alpha_i+\beta_j/\alpha_j\ne0 whenever i\ne j. Put L_1=\sum_j\alpha_j\xi_j and L_2=\sum_j\beta_j\xi_j. If the conditional law of L_2 given L_1 is symmetric about zero almost surely, meaning that (L_1,L_2) and (L_1,-L_2) have the same joint distribution, then every \xi_j is Gaussian. Gaussian includes a point mass or a distribution with density (2\pi\sigma^2)^{-1/2}\exp(-(x-m)^2/(2\sigma^2)), where m\in\mathbb R and \sigma^2>0.

Proof 1: Finite differences of characteristic functions (external)

A supporting source can be found here (Section 2.5, Theorems 2.5.2–2.5.3). The deduction below supplies the argument for the stored statement.

The following deduction is supplied here, using the cited characterization theorems: rescale eta_j=alpha_j xi_j, and group indices with equal c_j=beta_j/alpha_j, writing their independent sums as Z_1,...,Z_m with distinct nonzero ratios d_l. No d_l is the negative of another. Symmetry gives product phi_l(u+d_l v)=product phi_l(u-d_l v). Choose continuous logarithms psi_l near0 with psi_l(0)=0; the logarithmic equality is exact on a connected small neighborhood. The 2m linear forms u±d_l v have pairwise different kernels. To isolate psi_i(u+d_i v), apply successively differences along vectors in the kernels of each other form. Every other term disappears, and the surviving (2m-1)-fold difference of psi_i is zero for arbitrary small increments, since each corresponding directional coefficient is nonzero. A continuous function with these vanishing local differences is a polynomial locally, of degree at most2m-2 (equivalently take these directional derivatives in distributions). Local Marcinkiewicz implies every Z_l is Gaussian, including constants. Cramer decomposition then makes every eta_j, and hence xi_j, Gaussian. For m=1 the same argument says its log characteristic function is constant near0, so Z_1=0 almost surely and Cramer still applies. The continuous finite-difference polynomial lemma, local Marcinkiewicz theorem and Cramér theorem are the named dependencies.

Level justification

The joint-law statement requires probability and linear forms. Beyond the local finite-difference calculation, the Gaussian characterization uses analytic continuation of characteristic functions, exponential-moment criteria, an entire logarithm and a Liouville growth argument in the proofs of Cramér and Marcinkiewicz. Learning this combination of probability foundations and complex analysis exceeds the short preparation allowance from introductory real analysis and algebra.
