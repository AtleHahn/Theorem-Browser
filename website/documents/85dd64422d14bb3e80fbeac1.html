Statement

Let (X_t)_{t\in\mathbb N_0} be a real integrable martingale adapted to a filtration (\mathcal F_t) on a probability space, and let \tau:\Omega\to\mathbb N_0\cup\{\infty\} be a stopping time for that filtration. Suppose at least one of these conditions holds:

(a) \tau\le c almost surely for a fixed integer c.

(b) \mathbb E\tau<\infty and a constant c satisfies \mathbb E(|X_{t+1}-X_t|\mid\mathcal F_t)\le c almost surely on \{\tau>t\} for every t.

(c) |X_{t\wedge\tau}|\le c almost surely for every t, for a fixed constant c.

Then X_\tau is well defined and integrable, with X_\infty interpreted on \{\tau=\infty\} as the almost-sure limit of the stopped process. \mathbb E X_\tau=\mathbb E X_0.

Proof 1: Stopped process and integrable domination

Let X^\tau denote the stopped process, X_t^\tau=X_{t\wedge\tau}, which is also a martingale by the stopped-process theorem. The framework of the proof relies on analyzing this process under the provided conditions:

  • Under condition (a) or (b), \tau<\infty almost surely, so X_\tau is well defined and X_t^\tau\to X_\tau almost surely.
  • Under condition (c) the stopped process X^\tau is bounded, hence by Doob's convergence theorem for martingales it converges almost surely pointwise to a random variable which we call X_\tau.

If condition (c) holds, then the stopped process X^\tau is bounded by the constant random variable M:=c. Otherwise, writing the stopped process as

X_t^\tau=X_0+\sum_{s=0}^{\min(t,\tau)-1}(X_{s+1}-X_s), \qquad t\in\mathbb N_0,

with an empty sum interpreted as zero, gives |X_t^\tau|\le M for all t\in\mathbb N_0, where

M:=|X_0|+\sum_{s=0}^{\tau-1}|X_{s+1}-X_s| =|X_0|+\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}|X_{s+1}-X_s|\mathbf1_{\{\tau>s\}}.

By the monotone convergence theorem,

\mathbb E[M]=\mathbb E[|X_0|] +\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\mathbb E\bigl[|X_{s+1}-X_s|\mathbf1_{\{\tau>s\}}\bigr].

If condition (a) holds, then this series only has a finite number of non-zero terms, each integrable, hence M is integrable.

If condition (b) holds, then we continue by inserting a conditional expectation and using that the event \{\tau>s\} is known at time s (note that \tau is assumed to be a stopping time with respect to the filtration). This yields:

\begin{aligned} \mathbb E[M] &=\mathbb E[|X_0|] +\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\mathbb E\left[ \underbrace{\mathbb E[|X_{s+1}-X_s|\mid\mathcal F_s] \,\mathbf1_{\{\tau>s\}}}_{\le c\,\mathbf1_{\{\tau>s\}}\text{ a.s. by (b)}} \right]\\ &\le\mathbb E[|X_0|]+c\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\mathbb P(\tau>s)\\ &=\mathbb E[|X_0|]+c\,\mathbb E[\tau]<\infty, \end{aligned}

where a representation of the expected value of non-negative integer-valued random variables is used for the last equality.

Therefore, under any one of the three conditions in the theorem, the stopped process is dominated by an integrable random variable M. Since the stopped process X^\tau converges almost surely to X_\tau, the dominated convergence theorem implies:

\mathbb E[X_\tau]=\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb E[X_t^\tau].

By the martingale property of the stopped process,

\mathbb E[X_t^\tau] = \mathbb E[X_0], \qquad t\in\mathbb N_0,

hence

\mathbb E[X_\tau] = \mathbb E[X_0].
