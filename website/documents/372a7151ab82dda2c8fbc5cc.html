Statement

Let X be a set of N\ge1 distinct sample points. There is a map \Phi:X\to\mathbb R^{N-1} such that every binary labeling of X is strictly separable after mapping: for any \varepsilon:X\to\{-1,1\} some affine functional \ell(v)=w\cdot v+c satisfies \varepsilon(x)\ell(\Phi(x))>0 for all x\in X.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Finite point sets; Real affine spaces; Affine linear separation. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Map samples to simplex vertices — outline

A deterministic mapping may be used: suppose there are N points. Lift them onto the vertices of the simplex in the N-1 dimensional real space. Since every partition of the samples into two sets is separable by an affine linear separator, the property follows.

Level justification

Map the points to an affine simplex and invoke the supplied labeling-separation fact. Arbitrary-dimensional affine independence and affine functionals are undergraduate linear algebra; no learning-theoretic machinery is used.
