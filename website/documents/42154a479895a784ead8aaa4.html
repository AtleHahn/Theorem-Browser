Statement

Let A,B,C be three mutually tangent spheres in Euclidean three-space, whose three tangency points are distinct. There is a closed chain of six generalized spheres, each tangent to A,B,C and its two cyclic neighbors. Here a generalized sphere may be a plane, with parallel planes regarded as tangent at infinity. Rotating the annular configuration in the construction produces a one-parameter family of such chains.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Generalized spheres and parallel tangency at infinity are defined conventions. The remaining configuration is elementary sphere tangency, rotations and a six-member chain.

Proof 1: Reduction to an annular hexlet — outline

The general problem of finding a hexlet for three given mutually tangent spheres A, B and C can be reduced to the annular case using inversion. This geometrical operation always transforms spheres into spheres or into planes, which may be regarded as spheres of infinite radius. A sphere is transformed into a plane if and only if the sphere passes through the center of inversion. An advantage of inversion is that it preserves tangency; if two spheres are tangent before the transformation, they remain so after. Thus, if the inversion transformation is chosen judiciously, the problem can be reduced to a simpler case, such as the annular Soddy's hexlet. Inversion is reversible; repeating an inversion in the same point returns the transformed objects to their original size and position.

Inversion in the point of tangency between spheres A, B transforms them into parallel planes, which may be denoted as a and b. Since sphere C is tangent to both A and B and does not pass through the center of inversion, C is transformed into another sphere c that is tangent to both planes; hence, c is sandwiched between the two planes a and b. If r is the radius of c, the planes are separated by 2r. The annular hexlet consists of six spheres of radius r packed like ball bearings around c and likewise sandwiched between the planes. Six spheres s1–s6 may be packed around c and likewise sandwiched between the bounding planes a and b. Re-inversion restores the three original spheres, and transforms s1–s6 into a hexlet for the original problem (allowing a plane when an annular sphere passes through the inversion center). In general, these hexlet spheres S1–S6 have different radii.

An infinite variety of hexlets may be generated by rotating the six balls s1–s6 in their plane by an arbitrary angle before re-inverting them.
