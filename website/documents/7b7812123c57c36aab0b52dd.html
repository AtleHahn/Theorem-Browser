Statement

Let V be a finite-dimensional real normed vector space, and let f:[a,b]\to V be continuous and differentiable on (a,b), where a<b. Then \|f(b)-f(a)\|\le(b-a)\sup_{a<c<b}\|f'(c)\|. (An infinite supremum makes the inequality immediate.)

Proof 1: Scalarization by the dual norm (outline)

The mean value theorem for real-valued functions implies that if g is continuous on [a,b] and differentiable on (a,b), then there is a point c in (a,b) such that

\frac{g(b)-g(a)}{b-a}=g'(c).

Since \inf g'\leq g'(c)\leq\sup g', the corresponding inequality is a useful substitute for equality. Using the pairing of the dual of V with its dual norm yields

\left\|f(b)-f(a)\right\|\leq(b-a)\sup_{c\in(a,b)}\left\|f'(c)\right\|.

Here the norm-duality identity \|v\|=\sup_{\|\ell\|_*\leq1}|\ell(v)|, where \|\ell\|_*=\sup_{\|v\|\leq1}|\ell(v)|, is used. The source gives this dual-pairing argument as an outline.
