Statement

Let A be a commutative Noetherian ring that is either a local ring or an integral domain, and let I be a proper ideal of A. Then \bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty} I^n=0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory ring theory supplies ideals, prime/maximal ideals, products and inclusion. Noetherian means every ideal is finitely generated. The further conditions are short ideal-based definitions: height counts chains of primes, local means a unique maximal ideal, and primary uses powers in the defining product condition. The relevant definitions are a small extension of familiar ideals, without localization or scheme theory.

Proof 1: Artin–Rees and annihilation

Use the Artin–Rees lemma: for a finitely generated module M over a commutative Noetherian ring and a submodule L, there is k\ge1 such that I^nM\cap L=I^{n-k}(I^kM\cap L) for all n\ge k. Put N=\bigcap_{n\ge1}I^n. Applying that lemma with M=A gives an integer k such that, for n\ge k,

{\displaystyle I^{n}\cap N=I^{n-k}(I^{k}\cap N).}

Taking {\displaystyle n=k+1}, this means {\displaystyle I^{k+1}\cap N=I(I^{k}\cap N)} or {\displaystyle N=IN}. Thus, if A is local, {\displaystyle N=0} by Nakayama's lemma. If A is an integral domain, then one uses the determinant trick (that is, a variant of the Cayley–Hamilton theorem that yields Nakayama's lemma):

Theorem—Let u be an endomorphism of an A-module N generated by n elements and I an ideal of A such that {\displaystyle u(N)\subset IN}. Then there is a relation:

{\displaystyle u^{n}+a_{1}u^{n-1}+\cdots +a_{n-1}u+a_{n}=0,\,a_{i}\in I^{i}.}

In the setup here, take u to be the identity operator on N; that will yield a nonzero element x in A such that {\displaystyle xN=0}, which implies {\displaystyle N=0}, as {\displaystyle x} is a nonzerodivisor.
