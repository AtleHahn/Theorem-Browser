Statement

Let (X,Σ,μ) be a measure space and s=∑{i=1}^m c_i 1{E_i} a nonnegative simple measurable function with E_i∈Σ and finite c_i≥0. Then ν_s(A)=∫_A s dμ defines a measure on Σ.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A simple-function integral is a finite sum of coefficients times set measures. Sigma-algebras and countable additivity are direct set-family definitions; this limited finite-sum setting does not require constructing integration for arbitrary measurable functions.

Proof 1: Write the density integral as a finite sum of restricted measures

By the simple-function integral definition, ν_s(A)=∑_{i=1}^m c_i μ(A∩E_i). Each A↦μ(A∩E_i) is a measure; multiplying by a nonnegative constant and taking a finite sum preserves countable additivity and zero on the empty set. Thus ν_s is a measure. This is the independently valid local observation within the source lower-bound argument.
