Statement

The polynomial-time hierarchy is contained in deterministic polynomial time with a PP oracle, and consequently with a counting oracle:

\mathsf{PH}\subseteq\mathsf P^{\mathsf{PP}}\subseteq\mathsf P^{\#\mathsf P}.

Here \mathsf P consists of decision problems solvable by a deterministic Turing machine in polynomial time. Put \Sigma_0^P=\Pi_0^P=\mathsf P, \Sigma_{k+1}^P=\mathsf{NP}^{\Sigma_k^P}, and \Pi_k^P=\operatorname{co}\Sigma_k^P; then \mathsf{PH}=\bigcup_{k\geq0}\Sigma_k^P. The class \mathsf{NP} uses nondeterministic polynomial-time machines.

A language belongs to \mathsf{PP} if membership is decided by whether strictly more than half the computation paths of a polynomial-time nondeterministic machine accept. A function belongs to \#\mathsf P if it counts accepting paths of such a machine. The superscript denotes oracle access, allowing adaptive queries with answers supplied as one oracle step; counting answers are returned in binary. Equivalently, every problem at every finite level of the polynomial hierarchy admits a deterministic polynomial-time Turing reduction to an exact counting problem.

Proof availability

Source proof outline and limitations

The source describes two steps. A variant of the Valiant–Vazirani argument gives closure under an existential polynomially bounded quantifier for the bounded-error parity class. Together with closure under complement and containment of \mathsf P, induction over hierarchy levels gives containment of \mathsf{PH} in that class. The second step asserts that this bounded-error parity computation can be simulated in \mathsf P^{\#\mathsf P}.

The crucial closure and counting-simulation arguments are not supplied. The final displayed chain also inserts a \mathsf P\mathbin{\cdot}\oplus\mathsf P class without justification. The outline does not separately prove the \mathsf P^{\mathsf{PP}} formulation stated at the start of the article. This is a roadmap with identified missing steps, not a complete proof of either formulation.
