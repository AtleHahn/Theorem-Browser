Statement

Let p be an odd prime, write p-1=2^s d with integers s>0 and odd d>0, and let \gcd(a,p)=1. Then either a^d\equiv1\pmod p or a^{2^r d}\equiv-1\pmod p for some 0\le r<s.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The stated objects and conditions use elementary integer arithmetic.

Proof 1: Prime-modulus repeated-squaring property — outline

This result may be deduced from Fermat's little theorem, a^{p-1}\equiv1\pmod p for p\nmid a, by the fact that, if p is an odd prime, then the integers modulo p form a finite field, in which 1 modulo p has exactly two square roots, 1 and -1 modulo p.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Start with the Fermat congruence and trace the first occurrence of one backward through squaring. Only the concrete fact that a square equal to one modulo an odd prime has base plus or minus one is needed; no abstract field construction is used.
