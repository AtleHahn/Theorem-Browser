Statement

Let d\geq1 and let f:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb C be continuous with f(0)=1. Then f is positive definite if and only if there is a unique Borel probability measure \mu on \mathbb R^d such that

f(t)=\int_{\mathbb R^d}e^{-2\pi i\xi\cdot t}\,d\mu(\xi).

Here positive definiteness means \sum_{p,q}f(\alpha_p-\alpha_q)c_p\overline{c_q}\geq0 for every finite choice of \alpha_p\in\mathbb R^d and c_p\in\mathbb C.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Even with the finite quadratic-form definition of positive definiteness explicit, the representation concerns general probability measures and measure-theoretic Fourier integrals.

Proof 1: Specialize the dual-group representation

Use Bochner's theorem for locally compact abelian groups: normalized continuous positive-definite functions are exactly the Fourier transforms of unique regular Borel probability measures on the dual group. Apply the theorem to the additive group \mathbb R^d. Its dual is again \mathbb R^d, with character corresponding to \xi given by t\mapsto e^{-2\pi i\xi\cdot t}. The general representation is therefore precisely the stated integral.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof specializes harmonic analysis on locally compact abelian groups using dual groups, continuous characters and regular Borel measures. These are substantial additional frameworks beyond undergraduate Fourier integrals, even though the Euclidean character formula is explicit.
