Statement

Let n\ge k\ge t\ge1 be integers and write [n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}. A family \mathcal F of k-element subsets of [n] is called t-intersecting if |A\cap B|\ge t for all A,B\in\mathcal F. For each integer r\ge0 with t+2r\le n, define

\mathcal F_{n,k,t,r}=\{A\subseteq[n]:|A|=k,\ |A\cap\{1,\ldots,t+2r\}|\ge t+r\}.

Then every t-intersecting family \mathcal F satisfies |\mathcal F|\le\max_{r\ge0,\ t+2r\le n}|\mathcal F_{n,k,t,r}|. This bound is attained by a family \mathcal F_{n,k,t,r} of maximum size.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All objects are finite sets of integers. The locally defined condition counts common elements, and the comparison families are specified by two elementary counting conditions. Nested finite families require care but no college theory; proof difficulty is irrelevant.
