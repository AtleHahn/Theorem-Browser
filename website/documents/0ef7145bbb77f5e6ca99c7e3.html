Statement

Let n\ge1 and let K\subseteq\mathbb R^n be a convex body (a compact convex set with nonempty interior) satisfying K=-K. Let f:\mathbb R^n\to[0,\infty) be Lebesgue measurable and integrable, with f(x)=f(-x) for every x. Suppose \{x:f(x)\ge t\} is convex for every t\ge0. Then for every y\in\mathbb R^n and every c\in[0,1],

\int_K f(x+cy)\,dx\ \ge\ \int_K f(x+y)\,dx.
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Convexity and symmetry are elementary, but the complete claim concerns arbitrary measurable multivariate densities and Lebesgue integrals. Measure theory is not assumed in introductory real analysis; confidently understanding these together exceeds the conservative reading allowance.
