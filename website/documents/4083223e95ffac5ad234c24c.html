Statement

Work in constructive set theory with pairing, extensionality and separation for the formulas used below, and with the distinct finite ordinals 0=\varnothing, 1=\{0\} and 2=\{0,1\}. For a proposition P allowed in separation, put

U=\{x\in2:x=0\lor P\},\qquad V=\{x\in2:x=1\lor P\},\qquad X=\{U,V\}.

A choice function on X is a function f:X\to2 satisfying f(U)\in U and f(V)\in V. Assume either full separation and the axiom of choice, or the law of excluded middle. Then X has cardinality one or two, and hence is finite, where finite means bijective with a finite ordinal.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement asserts finiteness under alternative constructive logical axioms, not a decidable finite list supplied independently of those axioms.

Proof 1: Apply excluded middle to the two possible domains — outline

Use excluded middle for P; in the full-choice case it follows from Diaconescu's theorem. If P holds, then U=V=2 and X=\{2\} has cardinality one. If \neg P holds, then U=\{0\} and V=\{1\} are distinct, so X has cardinality two. In either case it is finite.

Level justification

The direct P/not-P cases are finite, but the full-choice branch invokes a theorem in constructive set theory relating separation and choice to excluded middle. Retain beyond undergraduate provisionally because the distinction between constructive axioms and classical logic is essential here.
