Statement

If f is holomorphic on 0<|z-c|<R, then for 0<\varepsilon<R, \operatorname{Res}(f,c)=\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_{|z-c|=\varepsilon} f(z)\,dz, with counterclockwise orientation.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Holomorphic means complex differentiable; contour integration is parametrized integration, and residue is the minus-first Laurent coefficient. These linked local definitions extend undergraduate calculus/series for a single punctured disk.

Proof 1: Apply the residue theorem to one puncture

According to the residue theorem, we have:

{\displaystyle \operatorname {Res} (f,c)={1 \over 2\pi i}\oint _{\gamma }f(z)\,dz}

where {\displaystyle \gamma } traces out a circle around {\displaystyle c} in a counterclockwise manner and does not pass through or contain other singularities within it. We may choose the path {\displaystyle \gamma } to be a circle of radius {\displaystyle \varepsilon } around {\displaystyle c}. Since {\displaystyle \varepsilon } can be as small as we desire it can be made to contain only the singularity of {\displaystyle c} due to nature of isolated singularities.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the residue theorem to the counterclockwise circle surrounding the only puncture. Undergraduate complex analysis and contour integrals suffice.
