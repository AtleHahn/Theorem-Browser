Statement

Let R be a right Noetherian ring. Every nil left or right ideal is nilpotent. Here nil means every element is nilpotent, and an ideal I is nilpotent when I^m=0 for some m\ge1. The lower nilradical N_*(R) (the intersection of prime ideals) equals the upper nilradical (the largest nil two-sided ideal); it is nilpotent and contains every nilpotent left or right ideal.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory ring theory supplies ideals and their products. Noetherian, nil and nilpotent are finite-generation and power conditions; the radicals here are explicitly described by intersections or largest ideals. These related ideal-based definitions are a small extension consistent with the Hilbert basis calibration.

Proof 1: Maximal nilpotent ideal and semiprime quotient

Use the source’s annihilator lemma: if R satisfies the ascending chain condition on right annihilators \{r:ar=0\}, every nil one-sided ideal lies in the lower nilradical N_*(R). Right Noetherianity supplies this condition. It therefore suffices to prove that N_*(R) is nilpotent.

The ascending chain condition on right ideals gives a maximal nilpotent two-sided ideal N. By maximality, R/N has no nonzero nilpotent ideals and is semiprime. Hence N_*(R)\subseteq N. Conversely the lower nilradical contains every nilpotent ideal, so N\subseteq N_*(R). Thus N=N_*(R) is nilpotent. The annihilator lemma now places every nil one-sided ideal inside N. In particular all nilpotent one-sided ideals lie there, and the upper nilradical, the largest nil two-sided ideal, equals N as well.
