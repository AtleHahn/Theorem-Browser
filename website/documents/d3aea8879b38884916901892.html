Statement

For every \epsilon>0, \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-1-\epsilon} converges and is at most (1+\epsilon)/\epsilon.

Proof 1: Convergence and an upper bound for p-series — proof

The series

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1{n^{1+\epsilon}}

converges for every \epsilon>0 by the integral test, because by the power rule

\int_1^M\frac1{x^{1+\epsilon}}\,dx =\left.-\frac1{\epsilon x^\epsilon}\right|_1^M =\frac1\epsilon\left(1-\frac1{M^\epsilon}\right) \le\frac1\epsilon<\infty \qquad\text{for all }M\ge1.

From the integral-test bound \sum_{n=1}^\infty f(n)\le f(1)+\int_1^\infty f(x)\,dx for a nonnegative decreasing function, we get the upper estimate

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1{n^{1+\epsilon}}\le\frac{1+\epsilon}{\epsilon}.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Integrate the decreasing power function and apply the integral-test upper bound. Introductory undergraduate calculus suffices.
