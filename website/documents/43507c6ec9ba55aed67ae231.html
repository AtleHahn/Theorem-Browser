Statement

Let a,m be integers with m>1. If X,Y are positive integers satisfying

X\leq m<XY,

then there exist integers x,y such that

ay\equiv x\pmod m,\qquad |x|<X,\qquad 0<y<Y.

The congruence means that m divides ay-x. No coprimality of a,m or of y,m is required.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All domains, quantifiers and bounds are elementary integer arithmetic; congruence is explicitly defined by divisibility.

Proof 1: Pigeonhole principle for residue classes (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorem 2.33 and its proof, p. 43).

The source uses (n,b,r^*,t^*)=(m,a,X,Y) and obtains r\equiv at\pmod m with |r|<X and 0<|t|<Y. Take (x,y)=(r,t) when t>0 and (x,y)=(-r,-t) otherwise.

Source algorithm sketch and limitations

The source describes extended-Euclidean sequences satisfying t_i m+y_i a=x_i with decreasing nonnegative remainders x_i, and proposes stopping at the first x_i<X, adjusting signs to obtain a positive denominator. This supplies the congruence and suggests how to find a small numerator.

The article does not specify the initialization/sign cases or prove the required strict bound 0<y<Y. It mentions Thue's pigeonhole proof but does not include it. This section records the incomplete algorithmic argument; it does not replace it with a newly invented pigeonhole or continued-fraction proof.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The proof counts XY integer pairs in m residue classes and subtracts a colliding pair. The pigeonhole principle follows by counting at most one pair per class, and excluding a zero denominator uses only divisibility and absolute values. Including these justifications requires only high-school arithmetic.
