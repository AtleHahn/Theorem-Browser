Statement

Let \Lambda\subset\mathbb R^d be a full-rank lattice of fundamental volume L>0. Suppose X\subset\mathbb R^d contains a bounded measurable set Y of volume greater than L and Y-Y\subseteq X, where Y-Y=\{u-v:u,v\in Y\}. Then X contains a nonzero lattice point.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The criterion uses measurable volume and arbitrary-dimensional lattices.

Proof 1: Apply Blichfeldt to a set of differences

Use the Blichfeldt pairwise-difference theorem: a bounded measurable set of volume greater than L has two distinct points whose difference belongs to \Lambda. Apply it to Y. For the resulting distinct p,q\in Y, the difference p-q is nonzero and belongs both to \Lambda and to Y-Y\subseteq X.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The subtraction step is elementary, but the stated prerequisite applies to arbitrary bounded measurable subsets of Euclidean space, using Lebesgue volume and full-rank lattice covolume. Interpreting the full measurable-set hypothesis together with the lattice-volume theorem exceeds a confident small extension of the ordinary undergraduate baseline.
