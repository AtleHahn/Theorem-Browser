Statement

The loop \gamma(t)=e^{it}, 0\le t\le2\pi, is not homotopic to a constant loop within \mathbb C\setminus\{0\}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Basic topology supplies continuous maps into the punctured plane. A homotopy of loops is a continuous family of loops, a single geometric extension; the explicit circle loop makes the noncontraction claim concrete without homotopy groups.

Proof 1: A nonzero reciprocal integral obstructs contraction — outline

The homotopy form of Cauchy’s theorem is an external prerequisite: a holomorphic function on an open subset of the complex plane has integral zero around every smooth closed curve that is null-homotopic in that subset. The function 1/z is holomorphic on \mathbb C\setminus\{0\}. Its integral on this loop is nonzero:

For \gamma(t)=e^{it}, one has \gamma'(t)=ie^{it}. Hence

\int_\gamma\frac{dz}{z}=\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{1}{e^{it}}ie^{it}\,dt=\int_0^{2\pi}i\,dt=2\pi i.

Thus the loop cannot be null-homotopic in the punctured plane, since the homotopy theorem would force that integral to vanish.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Compute the explicit reciprocal contour integral and apply the supplied null-homotopy version of Cauchy. Introductory complex integration plus continuous loop contraction as a small topological addition suffice; no fundamental-group machinery is used.
