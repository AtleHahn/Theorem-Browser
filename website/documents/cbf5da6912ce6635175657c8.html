Statement

Let X,Y,Z be subgroups of a group G. Use [x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy, x^y=y^{-1}xy, and let [H,K] be the subgroup generated by all [h,k] with h\in H,k\in K. Write [H,K,L]=[[H,K],L]. If [X,Y,Z]=\{1\} and [Y,Z,X]=\{1\}, then [Z,X,Y]=\{1\}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory field/group theory and linear algebra supply all objects. Commutator, graph automorphism and 2-rank are explicit group or finite-coordinate definitions; the displayed identities and subgroup assertions require no specialist structural theory.

Proof 1: Hall–Witt identity and the centralizer subgroup

Use the Hall–Witt identity: if {\displaystyle x,y,z\in G}, then

{\displaystyle [x,y^{-1},z]^{y}\cdot [y,z^{-1},x]^{z}\cdot [z,x^{-1},y]^{x}=1.}

Here [x,y,z]=[[x,y],z] and C_G(Y)=\{g\in G:gy=yg\text{ for every }y\in Y\} is the centralizer subgroup.

Let {\displaystyle x\in X}, {\displaystyle y\in Y}, and {\displaystyle z\in Z}. Then {\displaystyle [x,y^{-1},z]=1=[y,z^{-1},x]}, and by the Hall–Witt identity above, it follows that {\displaystyle [z,x^{-1},y]^{x}=1} and so {\displaystyle [z,x^{-1},y]=1}. Therefore, {\displaystyle [z,x^{-1}]\in \mathbf {C} _{G}(Y)} for all {\displaystyle z\in Z} and {\displaystyle x\in X}. Since these elements generate {\displaystyle [Z,X]}, we conclude that {\displaystyle [Z,X]\subseteq \mathbf {C} _{G}(Y)} and hence {\displaystyle [Z,X,Y]=1}.

Insert the given vanishing commutators into the explicit Hall-Witt identity and use the centralizer subgroup to pass from generators to the whole commutator subgroup. Introductory group theory suffices.
