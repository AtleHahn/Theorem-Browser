Statement

Let F be a field. For elements a_1,b_1,c_1,a_2,b_2,c_2 of F with D=a_1b_2-b_1a_2\ne0, the equations a_1x+b_1y=c_1 and a_2x+b_2y=c_2 have unique solution x=(c_1b_2-b_1c_2)/D, y=(a_1c_2-c_1a_2)/D.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Matrices, determinants, kernels, linear independence and bases are standard undergraduate linear algebra. Over an algebraically closed field, the extra condition simply guarantees polynomial roots; infinite dimensions mean cardinalities of bases. The specific matrix or dimension claim adds no specialist construction.

Proof 1: Expand the two Cramer determinants

Consider the linear system

{\displaystyle \left\{{\begin{matrix}a_{1}x+b_{1}y&={c_{1}}\\a_{2}x+b_{2}y&={c_{2}}\end{matrix}}\right.}

which in matrix format is

{\displaystyle {\begin{bmatrix}a_{1}&b_{1}\\a_{2}&b_{2}\end{bmatrix}}{\begin{bmatrix}x\\y\end{bmatrix}}={\begin{bmatrix}{c_{1}}\\{c_{2}}\end{bmatrix}}.}

Assume a1b2 − b1a2 is nonzero. Then, with the help of determinants, x and y can be found with Cramer's rule as

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}x&={\frac {\begin{vmatrix}{{c_{1}}}&b_{1}\\{{c_{2}}}&b_{2}\end{vmatrix}}{\begin{vmatrix}a_{1}&b_{1}\\a_{2}&b_{2}\end{vmatrix}}}={{c_{1}}b_{2}-b_{1}{c_{2}} \over a_{1}b_{2}-b_{1}a_{2}},\quad y={\frac {\begin{vmatrix}a_{1}&{{c_{1}}}\\a_{2}&{{c_{2}}}\end{vmatrix}}{\begin{vmatrix}a_{1}&b_{1}\\a_{2}&b_{2}\end{vmatrix}}}={a_{1}{c_{2}}-{c_{1}}a_{2} \over a_{1}b_{2}-b_{1}a_{2}}\end{aligned}}.}
