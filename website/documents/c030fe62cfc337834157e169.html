Statement

Let n\ge2 and let S\subset\mathbb R^n be a bounded open set with C^1 boundary. Write \operatorname{vol}(S) for its n-dimensional volume, \operatorname{per}(S) for its boundary surface area, and \omega_n=\operatorname{vol}(B_1) for the volume of the unit ball. Then \operatorname{per}(S)\ge n\operatorname{vol}(S)^{(n-1)/n}\omega_n^{1/n}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Multivariable calculus supplies smooth boundaries, surface area and volume in Euclidean space. The general-dimensional inequality uses those familiar quantities and needs no abstract manifold or geometric-measure theory to understand.

Proof 1: Brunn–Minkowski expansion and the outer-volume derivative

The proof of the inequality follows directly from Brunn–Minkowski inequality between a set {\displaystyle S} and a ball with radius {\displaystyle \epsilon }, i.e. {\displaystyle B_{\epsilon }=\epsilon B_{1}}. Indeed, {\displaystyle \operatorname {vol} (S+B_{\epsilon })\geq (\operatorname {vol} (S)^{1/n}+\operatorname {vol} (B_{\epsilon })^{1/n})^{n}\geq \operatorname {vol} (S)+n\operatorname {vol} (S)^{(n-1)/n}\epsilon \operatorname {vol} (B_{1})^{1/n}.} The isoperimetric inequality follows by subtracting {\textstyle \operatorname {vol} (S)}, dividing by {\displaystyle \epsilon }, and taking the limit as {\displaystyle \epsilon \to 0.}

Here S+B_\epsilon=\{s+b:s\in S,b\in B_\epsilon\}, and for the stated C^1 boundary the outer-volume derivative is

\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}\frac{\operatorname{vol}(S+B_\epsilon)-\operatorname{vol}(S)}{\epsilon}=\operatorname{per}(S).

Level justification

Apply the supplied volume inequality to a smooth bounded Euclidean region and a ball, expand the scalar power, and use the explicitly supplied outer-volume derivative. Multivariable volume, smooth surface area and one-sided derivatives suffice in this restricted setting; no proof for arbitrary measurable boundaries is needed.
