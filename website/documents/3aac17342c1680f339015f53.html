Simpler proof assessment

Likely alternative prerequisite level: Undergraduate level; lowest recorded proof level: Beyond undergraduate level. For fixed k, write n=qk+r, 0<=r<k. Subadditivity gives a_n<=q a_k plus one of finitely many remainders (handle r=0 without a_0). Divide by n to get limsup a_n/n<=a_k/k. Take the infimum over k and use the opposite pointwise lower bound. If that infimum is -infinity, the same bounds give convergence to -infinity. This is elementary real-sequence analysis, not ergodic theory.

Statement

If (a_n)_{n\ge1} is a real sequence with a_{n+m}\le a_n+a_m, then a_n/n converges to a value in [-\infty,\infty).

Level

Level justification

A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Constant-function specialization of Kingman

Apply Kingman’s theorem to the constant functions g_n(x)=a_n on a probability space with the identity transformation. The subadditivity hypothesis is exactly the sequence inequality, and the theorem gives convergence of a_n/n.
