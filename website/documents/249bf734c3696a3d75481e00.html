Statement

On a measure space (S,\Sigma,\mu), write \|h\|_t=(\int_S|h|^t\,d\mu)^{1/t} for 0<t<\infty, and \|h\|_\infty=\operatorname{ess\,sup}|h|. Norms may be infinite; products with a zero factor are defined to be zero, including 0\cdot\infty. For n\ge2, p_k\in(0,\infty], weights \theta_k\in(0,1) summing to1 and 1/p=\sum_k\theta_k/p_k, \|f\|_p\le\prod_k\|f\|_{p_k}^{\theta_k}. In the two-exponent case this is Littlewood’s inequality. All functions appearing here are measurable real- or complex-valued functions on S.

Proof 1: Interpolation of Lp norms

Use the weighted product interpolation inequality

\left\||f_1|^{\theta_1}\cdots|f_n|^{\theta_n}\right\|_p \le\|f_1\|_{p_1}^{\theta_1}\cdots\|f_n\|_{p_n}^{\theta_n}.

In particular, taking {\displaystyle f_{1}=\cdots =f_{n}=:f} gives

{\displaystyle \|f\|_{p}\leqslant \prod _{k=1}^{n}\|f\|_{p_{k}}^{\theta _{k}}.}

Specifying further θ1 = θ and θ2 = 1-θ, in the case {\displaystyle n=2,} we obtain the interpolation result

Littlewood's inequality—For {\displaystyle \theta \in (0,1)} and {\displaystyle {\frac {1}{p_{\theta }}}={\frac {\theta }{p_{1}}}+{\frac {1-\theta }{p_{0}}}},

{\displaystyle \|f\|_{p_{\theta }}\leqslant \|f\|_{p_{1}}^{\theta }\cdot \|f\|_{p_{0}}^{1-\theta },}
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Set all functions equal in the weighted product inequality. Although the substitution is immediate, the application concerns arbitrary measurable functions, Lp norms and essential-supremum endpoints; conservatively retain that measure-theoretic level.
