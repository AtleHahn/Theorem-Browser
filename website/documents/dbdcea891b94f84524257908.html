Statement

Let (E_n)_{n\ge1} be events in a probability space (\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P) with \sum_{n\ge1}\mathbb P(E_n)<\infty. Then \mathbb P(\limsup_nE_n)=0. Here \limsup_nE_n=\bigcap_{N\ge1}\bigcup_{n\ge N}E_n, the event of infinitely many occurrences.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A probability space is a set with a sigma-algebra of events and a normalized countably additive nonnegative set function. These linked event-level definitions extend undergraduate countable sets and nonnegative series. The limsup event is explicitly defined; there are no random-variable integrals, independence or distributional limits to learn.

Proof 1: Decreasing tail unions and subadditivity

Let (E_n) be a sequence of events in the given probability space.

The sequence of events \{\bigcup_{n=N}^\infty E_n\}_{N=1}^\infty is non-increasing:

\bigcup_{n=1}^\infty E_n\supseteq \bigcup_{n=2}^\infty E_n\supseteq\cdots\supseteq \bigcup_{n=N}^\infty E_n\supseteq \bigcup_{n=N+1}^\infty E_n\supseteq\cdots\supseteq \limsup_{n\to\infty}E_n.

By continuity from above,

\mathbb P\left(\limsup_{n\to\infty}E_n\right) =\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb P\left(\bigcup_{n=N}^\infty E_n\right).

By subadditivity,

\mathbb P\left(\bigcup_{n=N}^\infty E_n\right) \leq\sum_{n=N}^\infty\mathbb P(E_n).

By the original assumption, \sum_{n=1}^\infty\mathbb P(E_n)<\infty. As the series \sum_{n=1}^\infty\mathbb P(E_n) converges,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{n=N}^\infty\mathbb P(E_n)=0,

as required.

Level justification

Use countable union bounds and continuity of probability on decreasing events, then tails of a convergent numerical series. These event-level rules and infinitely-many-occurrences interpretation fit undergraduate probability and analysis; no Lebesgue integral or function-space theory is needed.
