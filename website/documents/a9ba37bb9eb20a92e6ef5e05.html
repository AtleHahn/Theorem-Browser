Statement

Let P(x)=x^3-15x-20 and let r_1,r_2,r_3 be its roots. Its splitting field \mathbb Q(r_1,r_2,r_3) is contained in \mathbb R, while the numbers 10\pm5i appearing under cube roots in Cardano’s formula do not belong to that field.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory polynomial and field algebra supplies characteristic and roots. An algebraic closure supplies all roots, and a splitting field is the field generated by the listed roots. These narrowly used definitions, together with explicit radical formulas, are a small extension; no norm or Galois-group theory is required.

Proof 1: Three real roots and nonreal radicands

Consider the polynomial {\displaystyle P(x)=x^{3}-15x-20}. According to Cardano's formula, one of its roots (all of them, actually) can be expressed as the sum of a cube root of {\displaystyle 10+5i} with a cube root of {\displaystyle 10-5i}. On the other hand, since {\displaystyle P(-3)<0}, {\displaystyle P(-2)>0}, {\displaystyle P(-1)<0}, and {\displaystyle P(5)>0}, the roots {\displaystyle r_{1}}, {\displaystyle r_{2}}, and {\displaystyle r_{3}} of {\displaystyle P(x)} are all real and therefore the field {\displaystyle \mathbf {Q} (r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})} is a subfield of {\displaystyle \mathbf {R} }. But then the numbers {\displaystyle 10\pm 5i} cannot belong to {\displaystyle \mathbf {Q} (r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Sign changes on three disjoint real intervals give three real roots. A field generated over Q by real numbers stays in R, whereas the explicit radicands are nonreal. Only introductory field theory and real continuity are needed.
