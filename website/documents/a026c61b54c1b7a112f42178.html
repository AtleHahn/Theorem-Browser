Proof 1: Use two epsilon-half bounds

Let x_n\to x in a metric space with metric d. Given an arbitrary \varepsilon>0, convergence supplies N such that for any n,m\geq N,

d(x_n,x)<\frac{\varepsilon}{2},\qquad d(x_m,x)<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Then by the triangle inequality,

d(x_n,x_m)\leq d(x_n,x)+d(x_m,x) <\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}=\varepsilon,

so that the sequence \{x_n\} is a Cauchy sequence, by definition.
