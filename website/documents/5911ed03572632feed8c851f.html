Statement

Let \Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^2 be open. There is a constant C>0, independent of u, such that every u\in H^2(\Omega)\cap H_0^1(\Omega) satisfies

\|u\|_{L^\infty(\Omega)}\le C\|u\|_{L^2(\Omega)}^{1/2}\|u\|_{H^2(\Omega)}^{1/2}.

Here H^j(\Omega) consists of the functions whose weak derivatives D^\alpha u of orders |\alpha|\le j lie in L^2(\Omega), with norm (\sum_{|\alpha|\le j}\int_\Omega|D^\alpha u|^2)^{1/2}. A weak derivative satisfies the integration-by-parts identity against every smooth compactly supported test function. H_0^1(\Omega) is the closure in H^1 of smooth compactly supported functions. The L^2 norm is (\int_\Omega |u|^2)^{1/2} and the L^\infty norm is the essential supremum of |u|, with Lebesgue measure throughout.
