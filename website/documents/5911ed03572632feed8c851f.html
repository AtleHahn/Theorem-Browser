Statement

Let \Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^2 be open. There is a constant C>0, independent of u, such that every u\in H^2(\Omega)\cap H_0^1(\Omega) satisfies

\|u\|_{L^\infty(\Omega)}\le C\|u\|_{L^2(\Omega)}^{1/2}\|u\|_{H^2(\Omega)}^{1/2}.

Here H^j(\Omega) consists of the functions whose weak derivatives D^\alpha u of orders |\alpha|\le j lie in L^2(\Omega), with norm (\sum_{|\alpha|\le j}\int_\Omega|D^\alpha u|^2)^{1/2}. A weak derivative satisfies the integration-by-parts identity against every smooth compactly supported test function. H_0^1(\Omega) is the closure in H^1 of smooth compactly supported functions. The L^2 norm is (\int_\Omega |u|^2)^{1/2} and the L^\infty norm is the essential supremum of |u|, with Lebesgue measure throughout.

Proof 1: Gaussian domination and semigroup interpolation (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Proposition 2.2.4, printed p.19; Theorem 3.1.4, p.32; §4.2, Theorem 4.2.1 and Lemmas 4.2.2–4.2.3 through Theorem 4.2.4, pp.46–48 (PDF pp.55–57 zero-based).).

For the Dirichlet heat semigroup T(t), the Gaussian bound in dimension two gives \|T(t)f\|_\infty\le c t^{-1/2}\|f\|_2. Proposition 2.2.4 gives u=T(t)u-\int_0^tT(s)\Delta u\,ds, hence \|u\|_\infty\le c t^{-1/2}\|u\|_2+2c t^{1/2}\|\Delta u\|_2. The integral estimate follows first on [\varepsilon,t] and then by its L^2 limit and an almost-everywhere convergent subsequence. Optimizing t and using \|\Delta u\|_2\le\sqrt2\|u\|_{H^2} gives the claim. If \Delta u=0, let t\to\infty. This deduction applies on arbitrary open sets.
