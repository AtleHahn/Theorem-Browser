Statement

Let \Delta be an n-dimensional simplex with vertices A_1,\ldots,A_{n+1} and a finite triangulation meeting face to face, where n\geq1. Label its triangulation vertices in \{1,\ldots,n+1\}, label A_i by i, and allow on each face only labels of that face's original vertices. Then at least one triangulation n-simplex has all n+1 labels.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Finite-dimensional simplices; Face-to-face triangulations; Sperner labeling. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Substitute the dimension in the polytopal lower bound

Use the polytopal Sperner theorem: a labeled triangulation of a convex d-polytope with N vertices, obeying the face-label condition, contains at least N-d fully labeled d-simplices. For a simplex, N=d+1, so this bound is 1.

Level justification

Set the number of original vertices equal to dimension plus one in the supplied polytopal bound. Undergraduate affine linear algebra suffices for finite-dimensional simplices, faces and triangulations; no topological proof of Sperner is required.
