Statement

Let \Omega\subset\mathbb R^n be a bounded open connected domain satisfying the cone condition. Let 0\le j<m be integers, 1\le q,r\le\infty, 1\le p<\infty, and j/m\le\theta\le1 satisfy

\frac1p=\frac jn+\theta\left(\frac1r-\frac mn\right)+\frac{1-\theta}{q}.

If r>1 and m-j-n/r is a nonnegative integer, also require \theta<1. Then every real u\in L^q(\Omega)\cap W^{m,r}(\Omega) satisfies

\|D^ju\|_p\le C\|u\|_{W^{m,r}}^\theta\|u\|_q^{1-\theta},

where C depends on the fixed domain and parameters, not on u. The Sobolev norm includes all weak derivatives through order m, and 1/\infty=0.

Proof 1: Absorb the lower-order norm using finite measure

Use the bounded-domain Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality, with the parameters and exception in the statement:

\|D^ju\|_{L^p(\Omega)} \leq C\|D^mu\|_{L^r(\Omega)}^\theta\|u\|_{L^q(\Omega)}^{1-\theta} +C\|u\|_{L^\sigma(\Omega)}.

Under the stronger assumption u\in L^q(\Omega)\cap W^{m,r}(\Omega), it is possible to recast this inequality in such a way that the penalization term is "absorbed" in the first term at right hand side. Indeed, one can choose \sigma=\min(r,q) and get

\begin{aligned} \|D^ju\|_{L^p(\Omega)} &\leq C\|D^mu\|_{L^r(\Omega)}^\theta\|u\|_{L^q(\Omega)}^{1-\theta} +C\|u\|_{L^{\min(r,q)}(\Omega)}^\theta\|u\|_{L^{\min(r,q)}(\Omega)}^{1-\theta}\\ &\leq C\|u\|_{W^{m,r}(\Omega)}^\theta\|u\|_{L^q(\Omega)}^{1-\theta}. \end{aligned}

Here we use the embeddings L^r(\Omega),L^q(\Omega)\subseteq L^{\min(r,q)}(\Omega) on a finite-measure domain, enlarging C if necessary. The zero function is immediate, and at the weight endpoints the absent factor is omitted.

This formulation has the advantage of recovering the structure of the theorem in the full Euclidean space, with the only caution that the Sobolev seminorm is replaced by the full W^{m,r}-norm.

Finally, observe that the whole-space exceptional case requiring additional finite-exponent integrability when q=\infty is no longer relevant in bounded domains, since for finite measure sets we have that L^\infty(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^\rho(\Omega) for any finite \rho\geq1.
