Statement

Let (\Omega,\mathcal F,(\mathcal F_t)_{t\ge0},\mathbb P) be a probability space with a complete right-continuous filtration. Let X be an adapted right-continuous submartingale with X_0=0, and assume the family \{X_T:T\text{ is a stopping time taking finitely many finite values}\} is uniformly integrable. Then

X_t=M_t+A_t\quad(t\ge0),

where M is a uniformly integrable martingale and A is an adapted predictable right-continuous increasing process with A_0=0. The pair is unique up to indistinguishability: any two such pairs agree at all times outside one null event. A filtration is an increasing family of sigma-algebras; right-continuity means \mathcal F_t=\bigcap_{s>t}\mathcal F_s, and completeness means that null events and their subsets belong to \mathcal F_0. Adapted means X_t is \mathcal F_t-measurable. The submartingale condition is integrability of each X_t and \mathbb E[X_t\mid\mathcal F_s]\ge X_s for s\le t; for a martingale use equality. A stopping time has \{T\le t\}\in\mathcal F_t for every t. A family \mathcal Y is uniformly integrable if \lim_{K\to\infty}\sup_{Y\in\mathcal Y}\mathbb E[|Y|\mathbf1_{|Y|>K}]=0; this is imposed on (M_t)_{t\ge0} as well. Predictable means measurable for the sigma-algebra on \Omega\times[0,\infty) generated by left-continuous adapted processes (with the initial-time sigma-algebra included). Increasing refers to the sample paths.
