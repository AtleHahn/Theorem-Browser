Statement

Let p_1,p_2,q_1,q_2 be real-valued functions on an interval, and let differentiable real-valued u,v on that interval satisfy (p_1u')'+q_1u=0 and (p_2v')'+q_2v=0, with p_1u',p_2v' differentiable. At every point where v\ne0,

{\displaystyle \left({\frac {u}{v}}(p_{1}u'v-p_{2}uv')\right)'=\left(q_{2}-q_{1}\right)u^{2}+\left(p_{1}-p_{2}\right)u'^{2}+p_{2}\left(u'-v'{\frac {u}{v}}\right)^{2}.}
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves ordinary differential equations; differentiation. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Differentiate and complete a square

Suppress the common argument x and differentiate where v\ne0:

\begin{aligned} \left(\frac uv(p_1u'v-p_2uv')\right)' &=\left(up_1u'-p_2v'u^2\frac1v\right)'\\ &=u'p_1u'+u(p_1u')'-(p_2v')'u^2\frac1v -p_2v'2uu'\frac1v+p_2v'u^2\frac{v'}{v^2}\\ &=p_1u'^2-2p_2\frac{uu'v'}v+p_2\frac{u^2v'^2}{v^2} +u(p_1u')'-(p_2v')'\frac{u^2}v\\ &=p_1u'^2-p_2u'^2+p_2u'^2-2p_2u'\frac{uv'}v +p_2\left(\frac{uv'}v\right)^2-u(q_1u)+(q_2v)\frac{u^2}v\\ &=(p_1-p_2)u'^2+p_2\left(u'-v'\frac uv\right)^2+(q_2-q_1)u^2. \end{aligned}
