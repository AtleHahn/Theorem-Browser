Statement

Let (A_i)_{i\in I} be a finite indexed family of finite sets, allowing repetitions. There is an injective map f:I\to\bigcup_i A_i with f(i)\in A_i for every i if and only if |\bigcup_{i\in J}A_i|\ge|J| for every J\subseteq I.

Proof 1: Hall criterion for a finite family of finite sets

A problem defined by a finite indexed family of finite sets (A_i)_{i\in I} with union X can be translated into a bipartite graph G=(I,X,E) where each edge connects an index i to an element of A_i. The two vertex classes are taken as disjoint copies. An I-perfect matching in this graph defines a system of unique representatives for the family.

In the other direction, from any finite bipartite graph G=(U,V,E) one can define a finite indexed family of sets, the family of neighborhoods of the vertices in U, such that any system of unique representatives for this family corresponds to a U-perfect matching in G. In this way, the combinatorial formulation for finite families of finite sets and the graph-theoretic formulation for finite graphs are equivalent.

The graph-theoretic Hall theorem says that an I-perfect matching exists if and only if |N_G(J)|\geq|J| for every J\subseteq I. Here N_G(J)=\bigcup_{i\in J}A_i, giving the stated criterion.

Build the finite incidence bipartite graph and translate distinct representatives into disjoint matching edges. The supplied Hall criterion then is exactly the union-size condition. Elementary finite sets and graph definitions suffice.
