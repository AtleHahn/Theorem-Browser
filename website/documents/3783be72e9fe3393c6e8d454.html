Statement

Independently draw with replacement from n\geq1 coupon types, each with probability 1/n, and let T be the number of draws until every type has appeared. Here n is an integer. As n\to\infty, \mathbb ET=n\log n+\gamma n+1/2+O(1/n), where \gamma is the Euler–Mascheroni constant and log is natural.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The sampling experiment is explicit and discrete. Expectation and variance are weighted sums over possible integer waiting times; analysis supplies infinite sums and asymptotic notation, and algebra supplies permutation cycles where used. These concrete additions do not require general stochastic-process theory.

Proof 1: Multiply the harmonic-number expansion

We use \operatorname E(T)=nH_n, where H_n=\sum_{j=1}^n1/j. Using the asymptotics of the harmonic numbers,

H_n=\log n+\gamma+\frac1{2n}+O(n^{-2}),

we obtain:

\operatorname E(T)=n\cdot H_n=n\log n+\gamma n+\frac12+O(1/n),

where \gamma\approx0.5772156649 is the Euler–Mascheroni constant.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Multiply the supplied harmonic-number expansion by n and rescale its remainder. Only discrete expectation, logarithms and elementary big-O notation are needed.
