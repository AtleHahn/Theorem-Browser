Statement

The image of every nonconstant entire function f:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C is dense in \mathbb C.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex differentiability extends familiar calculus; entire means differentiable throughout the plane. Density, power-series convergence and local continuation across a boundary are direct analytic/topological notions, with the continuation condition explicitly described. No additional complex-geometric structures are needed.

Proof 1: Invert the distance from a missing disk

If the image of {\displaystyle f} is not dense, then there is a complex number {\displaystyle w} and a real number {\displaystyle r>0} such that the open disk centered at {\displaystyle w} with radius {\displaystyle r} has no element of the image of {\displaystyle f}. Define

{\displaystyle g(z)={\frac {1}{f(z)-w}}.}

Then {\displaystyle g} is a bounded entire function, since for all {\displaystyle z},

{\displaystyle |g(z)|={\frac {1}{|f(z)-w|}}\leq{\frac {1}{r}}.}

So, by Liouville’s theorem, {\displaystyle g} is constant, and therefore {\displaystyle f} is constant.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A missing disk gives a bounded reciprocal g=1/(f-w). Complex differentiation rules show it is entire, and the supplied Liouville conclusion makes it constant. Basic calculus, complex arithmetic and metric density suffice after the small extension from real to complex differentiability.
