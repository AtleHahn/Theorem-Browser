Statement

For positive integers a,b,w, let T(a,b) count Euclidean remainder divisions, including the final zero-producing division. Then T(wa,wb)=T(a,b). In particular, if g=\gcd(a,b), then T(a,b)=T(a/g,b/g).

Level
High school only
Level justification

The counted operations and the positive integer scaling are explicit.

Proof 1: Scale every remainder equation

The number of steps to calculate the GCD of two natural numbers, a and b, may be denoted by T(a, b). If g is the GCD of a and b, then a = mg and b = ng for two coprime numbers m and n. Then

T(a, b) = T(m, n)

as may be seen by dividing all the steps in the Euclidean algorithm by g. By the same argument, the number of steps remains the same if a and b are multiplied by a common factor w: T(a, b) = T(wa, wb).

Level
High school only
Level justification

Multiply or divide every remainder equation and inequality by the same positive integer factor. The quotients and zero-remainder step stay the same.
