Statement

A genus-g handlebody is a compact three-manifold homeomorphic to a regular neighborhood of a bouquet of g circles; a Heegaard splitting is a decomposition into two such handlebodies along their common boundary. Let T^3=(S^1)^3, choose x_0\in S^1, and put \Gamma=(S^1\times\{x_0\}\times\{x_0\})\cup(\{x_0\}\times S^1\times\{x_0\})\cup(\{x_0\}\times\{x_0\}\times S^1). For a small closed regular neighborhood V of \Gamma, V and the closure of T^3\setminus V are genus-three handlebodies; thus \partial V gives a Heegaard splitting.

Proof 1: Coordinate-loop construction of a three-torus splitting — outline

Recall that the three-torus T^3 is the Cartesian product of three copies of S^1 (circles). Let x_0 be a point of S^1 and consider the graph

\Gamma=(S^1\times\{x_0\}\times\{x_0\}) \cup(\{x_0\}\times S^1\times\{x_0\}) \cup(\{x_0\}\times\{x_0\}\times S^1).

It is an easy exercise to show that V, a small closed regular neighborhood of \Gamma, is a handlebody as is \overline{T^3\setminus V}. Thus the boundary of V in T^3 is a Heegaard splitting and this is the standard splitting of T^3.

The source leaves the handlebody checks as an exercise. The three coordinate loops give genus three.
