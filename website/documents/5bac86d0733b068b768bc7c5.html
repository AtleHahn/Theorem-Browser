Statement

Every Hausdorff second-countable smooth manifold M of dimension m>1 admits a one-to-one smooth immersion into \mathbb R^{2m} and a smooth immersion into \mathbb R^{2m-1}. An immersion is a smooth map whose differential on each tangent space is injective; it need not be globally one-to-one. Manifolds here have no boundary.

Proof 1: Whitney cancellation and compact exhaustion (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Immersion paper: §12 Theorem 6 and four-case proof, pp. 270 ff.; Case III explicitly handles open manifolds, using Appendix Lemma 20. Embedding paper: §8 Theorem 5, pp. 236–237; cancellation theorem and its construction in §§8–12.).

Supporting sources: Whitney, The Self-Intersections of a Smooth n-Manifold, Theorem 5.

Apply Whitney's immersion Theorem 6 to a constant initial map. Its four-case proof includes open manifolds, using compact exhaustion to move remaining singularities beyond earlier compact sets. The injective immersion follows from the stronger embedding Theorem 5 in the companion paper. For a disconnected second-countable manifold there are at most countably many components; apply the constructions componentwise and place their embeddings in disjoint Euclidean balls. The infinitely differentiable option in the sources gives smooth maps.
