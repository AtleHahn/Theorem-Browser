Statement

Let P be a simple polygon in the Euclidean plane, meaning that its boundary is a closed polygonal curve without self-intersections. A pocket is the simple polygon bounded by a consecutive chain of edges of P and one edge of its convex hull that is not itself an edge of P. The convex hull is the smallest convex set containing P.

A pocket flip replaces that boundary chain by its reflection in the line containing the corresponding convex-hull edge, leaving the remaining boundary unchanged. Starting from any nonconvex simple polygon, repeatedly choose any pocket of the current polygon and flip it. Every such process reaches a convex polygon after finitely many flips, regardless of the choices of pockets.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All notions are elementary plane geometry; pocket and convex hull are defined in the statement. The assertion concerns finite termination without any advanced analytic prerequisites.
