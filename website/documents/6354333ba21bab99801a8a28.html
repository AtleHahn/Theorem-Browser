Statement

Let a<b and let f:I\to\mathbb R be continuously differentiable on an open interval I containing [a,b]. Then

|f(b)-f(a)|\le(b-a)\sup_{a<c<b}|f'(c)|.
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Real continuously differentiable functions; Endpoint differences; Supremum derivative bound. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Bound the integral of the derivative

By the fundamental theorem of calculus for {\displaystyle C^{1}} functions {\displaystyle f} on an open interval {\displaystyle I} with {\displaystyle [a,b]\subset I}

{\displaystyle \int _{a}^{b}f^{\prime }(x)\,dx=f(b)-f(a).}

Hence

{\displaystyle \left|f(b)-f(a)\right|\leq (b-a)\,\sup _{c\in (a,b)}\left|f^{\prime }(c)\right|.}
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Integrate the derivative and bound the absolute integral by length times the supremum. Undergraduate single-variable calculus and suprema suffice.
