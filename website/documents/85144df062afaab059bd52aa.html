Statement

Let V be a finite-dimensional vector space over a field K, and let G\leq\operatorname{GL}(V) be finite. Assume \operatorname{char}K=0 or that its positive characteristic does not divide |G|. Let S=K[V]=\operatorname{Sym}(V^*) be the polynomial algebra on the dual space, with action (g\cdot f)(v)=f(g^{-1}v), and let A=S^G be its invariant subalgebra. The following conditions are equivalent:

  1. G is generated by pseudoreflections, meaning nonidentity elements s\in G for which \ker(s-I) has codimension one in V.
  2. A is a polynomial algebra over K.
  3. A is a regular ring.
  4. S is a free module over A.
  5. S is a projective module over A.

Here regular means Noetherian with every localization at a prime ideal a regular local ring; a Noetherian local ring is regular when its maximal ideal can be generated by as many elements as its Krull dimension. A free module has a basis, and a projective module is a direct summand of a free module.

Level justification

The full five-way equivalence requires invariant rings, local regularity/Krull dimension and projective modules; these interdependent commutative-algebra notions substantially exceed the undergraduate baseline even with short definitions.

Proof 1: Nonmodular reflections and graded algebra (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorem 2, p.5; Sections 2–3 proof and classification input; regular/projective extensions given below.).

Apply Broer Theorem 2 and proof in Sections 2–3 for irreducible actions, with Reynolds averaging supplying the direct-summand property in nonmodular characteristic. Work over an algebraic closure first. By Maschke split V into irreducible summands; every pseudoreflection acts nontrivially on exactly one summand. A group generated by them is the direct product of the groups on those summands, so its invariant ring is the tensor product of their polynomial invariant rings. Trivial summands contribute polynomial variables. Descent back to K uses invariants commuting with scalar extension and faithfully flat descent of regularity. The reverse polynomial-to-reflection implication is the Serre result cited by Broer at the start of Theorem 2. For the remaining equivalences: a connected graded finite-type K-domain A regular at its homogeneous maximal ideal is a polynomial ring, by choosing homogeneous minimal generators (their number is dim A) and comparing dimensions of the resulting polynomial surjection. Polynomial A implies S free: its homogeneous generators are a system of parameters in the Cohen–Macaulay polynomial ring S and a regular sequence, giving a homogeneous free basis by graded Nakayama/Hilbert series. Free implies projective. Since S is finite over A, projective implies finite faithfully flat (integral extension has surjective spectrum); regularity descends from S. Handle dimension zero separately: all conditions are immediate.
