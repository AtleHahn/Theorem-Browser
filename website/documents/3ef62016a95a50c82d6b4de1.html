Statement

Let 0<n_0<n_1<\cdots be integers with n_k/k\to\infty. Let a_k\in\mathbb C and suppose f(z)=\sum_{k\ge0}a_kz^{n_k} has radius of convergence 1. Then the unit circle is a natural boundary: f has no analytic continuation across any of its points.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex differentiability extends familiar calculus; entire means differentiable throughout the plane. Density, power-series convergence and local continuation across a boundary are direct analytic/topological notions, with the continuation condition explicitly described. No additional complex-geometric structures are needed.

Proof 1: Set the gap density to zero in Pólya’s theorem — outline

Put D=\limsup_{k\to\infty}k/n_k=0. Pólya’s gap theorem states that every arc of the circle of convergence of length greater than 2\pi D contains a singularity of f. Thus every positive-length arc of the unit circle contains a singularity, so singularities are dense on it. This gives the natural-boundary conclusion.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof invokes analytic continuation, singularities on a convergence circle and natural boundaries. Those linked complex-analysis notions exceed ordinary series analysis even though the density substitution is simple.
