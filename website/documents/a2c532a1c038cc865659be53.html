Statement

The three medians of a nondegenerate triangle are concurrent.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement asks whether the three lines joining vertices to opposite midpoints pass through one point.

Proof 1: Specialize the side ratios — outline

Routh's theorem states that if in triangle ABC points D, E, and F lie in the interiors of segments BC, CA, and AB, then writing CD/BD=x, AE/CE=y, and BF/AF=z, the area of the triangle formed by the pairwise intersections of the cevians AD, BE, and CF is

S_{ABC}\cdot\frac{(xyz-1)^2}{(xy+y+1)(yz+z+1)(zx+x+1)},

where S_{ABC} is the area of the triangle ABC.

The x=y=z=1 case implies that the three medians are concurrent.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Set all three side ratios to one in the supplied area formula, obtaining a degenerate cevian triangle. Elementary Euclidean geometry and algebra suffice.
