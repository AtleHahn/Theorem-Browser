Statement

Let G=(V,E) be a finite simple undirected d-regular graph with n=|V|\ge1 and adjacency matrix A (entry A_{xy}=1 when xy\in E, and 0 otherwise). Let \lambda\ge0 bound the absolute values of the eigenvalues of A on the subspace perpendicular to the all-ones vector \mathbf1. Let J be the all-ones matrix and M=A-(d/n)J. If d,\mu_2,\ldots,\mu_n are the eigenvalues of A in an orthonormal basis beginning with \mathbf1/\sqrt n, then those of M are 0,\mu_2,\ldots,\mu_n. For every T\subseteq V, with indicator vector 1_T, \|M1_T\|\le\lambda\sqrt{|T|}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Finite simple regular graphs; Real symmetric adjacency matrices; Eigenvalues and orthogonality; All-ones and indicator matrices; Euclidean norm. Proof 1: Diagonalize the centered adjacency matrix

Let d=\mu_1,\mu_2,\ldots,\mu_n be the eigenvalues of A (these eigenvalues are real because A is symmetric). We know that \mu_1=d with corresponding eigenvector v_1=\mathbf1/\sqrt n, the normalization of the all-1's vector. Because A is symmetric, we can pick eigenvectors v_2,\ldots,v_n of A corresponding to eigenvalues \mu_2,\ldots,\mu_n so that \{v_1,\ldots,v_n\} forms an orthonormal basis of \mathbb R^n.

Let J be the n\times n matrix of all 1's. Note that v_1 is an eigenvector of J with eigenvalue n and each other v_i, being perpendicular to v_1=\mathbf1/\sqrt n, is an eigenvector of J with eigenvalue 0. Let M=A-(d/n)J. Because A and J share eigenvectors, the eigenvalues of M are 0,\mu_2,\ldots,\mu_n.

For a vertex subset T\subseteq V, let 1_T be the column vector with vth coordinate equal to 1 if v\in T and 0 otherwise. Because M is self-adjoint, we can write

\begin{aligned} \|M1_T\|^2 &=\langle M1_T,M1_T\rangle =\langle1_T,M^21_T\rangle\\ &=\left\langle1_T,\sum_{i=1}^n M^2\langle1_T,v_i\rangle v_i\right\rangle\\ &=\sum_{i=2}^n\mu_i^2\langle1_T,v_i\rangle^2 \leq\lambda^2\|1_T\|^2. \end{aligned}

This implies that \|M1_T\|\leq\lambda\|1_T\|=\lambda\sqrt{|T|}.

Diagonalize the real symmetric adjacency matrix with the constant eigenvector first, subtract its rank-one contribution and expand an indicator in that orthonormal basis. This is standard finite-dimensional spectral linear algebra.
