Statement

Let R be a commutative ring with identity and let f,g\in R[x_1,\ldots,x_n]. With c(h) the ideal generated by the coefficients of h, c(fg)=R if and only if c(f)=c(g)=R.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory ring/group theory and linear algebra supply polynomial coefficients, ideals, determinants, bases and matrix products. Elementary transvections are explicitly given elementary row-operation matrices, and division ring means every nonzero element has an inverse. These direct definitions suffice without specialist theory.

Proof 1: A product has unit content exactly when both factors do — proof

Use the content inclusions

c(fg)\subseteq c(f)c(g)\subseteq\sqrt{c(fg)},

where \sqrt I denotes the radical of the ideal I. The equivalence follows using the fact that \sqrt I=R implies I=R.

The source leaves the two directions of this deduction implicit.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the supplied ideal inclusions; a unit radical implies one belongs to the ideal, and a product ideal equal to R forces each factor ideal equal to R. Introductory ring ideals with the small radical definition suffice.
