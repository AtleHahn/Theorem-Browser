Statement

If a square matrix A is similar to a block upper triangular matrix with square diagonal blocks B_1,\ldots,B_h, then \rho(A)=\max_i\rho(B_i).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves finite-dimensional matrices; eigenvalues and spectral radius. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Source matrix argument

The spectrum of a block upper triangular matrix is the union of the spectra of its diagonal blocks. Similarity preserves the spectrum. Taking the largest eigenvalue modulus therefore gives \max_i\rho(B_i).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Block triangular matrices have the union of their diagonal-block eigenvalues, and similarity preserves eigenvalues. Taking the maximum modulus is exactly the definition of spectral radius. Standard undergraduate eigenvalue theory suffices.
