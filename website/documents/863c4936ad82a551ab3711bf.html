Statement

For two triangles ABC and abc in a projective space, central perspectivity means that the joins Aa,Bb,Cc meet at a point; axial perspectivity means that the intersection points AB\cap ab, AC\cap ac, BC\cap bc lie on a line. Suppose the two noncollinear vertex triples span distinct planes, the joins Aa,Bb,Cc are well-defined distinct lines concurrent at one point, and corresponding side lines are distinct. Then the three corresponding-side intersections exist and are collinear.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Central and axial perspectivity are defined. The additional projective incidence background consists of points, lines and planes with coplanar lines meeting and distinct planes sharing at most a line, a short extension of undergraduate linear geometry. Abstract projective-space classification is unnecessary.

Proof 1: Intersect the two triangle planes — outline

The points A, B, a and b are coplanar (lie in the same plane) because of the assumed concurrency of Aa and Bb. Therefore, the lines AB and ab belong to the same plane and must intersect. Further, if the two triangles lie on different planes, then the point AB ∩ ab belongs to both planes. By a symmetric argument, the points AC ∩ ac and BC ∩ bc also exist and belong to the planes of both triangles. Since these two planes intersect in more than one point, their intersection is a line that contains all three points.

This proves Desargues's theorem if the two triangles are not contained in the same plane.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Concurrency makes each pair of corresponding sides coplanar, so their intersections exist and lie in both triangle planes. Projective incidence and the line intersection of distinct planes are a small extension of elementary linear geometry.
