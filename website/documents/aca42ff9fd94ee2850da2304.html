Statement

Let (X,\mu) and (Y,\nu) be measure spaces, and let K:X\times Y\to\mathbb R be measurable for the product sigma-algebra. Let 1\le p,q,r<\infty satisfy

\frac1q=\frac1p+\frac1r-1.

Suppose C\ge0 and

\int_Y|K(x,y)|^r\,d\nu(y)\le C^r\quad(x\in X),\qquad \int_X|K(x,y)|^r\,d\mu(x)\le C^r\quad(y\in Y).

For a real measurable f\in L^p(Y,\nu), the integral operator Tf(x)=\int_Y K(x,y)f(y)\,d\nu(y) satisfies

\int_X|Tf(x)|^q\,d\mu(x)\le C^q\left(\int_Y|f(y)|^p\,d\nu(y)\right)^{q/p},

or equivalently \|Tf\|_{L^q(X)}\le C\|f\|_{L^p(Y)}. The operator is understood up to equality almost everywhere.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

General measure spaces, product measurability and Lp operator bounds require measure-theoretic analysis beyond the assumed baseline; ordinary integration alone does not cover the full formulation.
