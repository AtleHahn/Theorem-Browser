Statement

Let X_1,\ldots,X_n be independent real random variables, n\ge1, and S=\sum_iX_i. Put M_i(t)=\mathbb E e^{tX_i}, allowing +\infty. For a\in\mathbb R,

\Pr(S\ge a)\le\inf_{t>0}e^{-ta}\prod_iM_i(t),\qquad\Pr(S\le a)\le\inf_{t<0}e^{-ta}\prod_iM_i(t).
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

From undergraduate analysis, expectation is a probability-weighted integral, and independence of finitely many real quantities means joint events factor into products. The exponential moments and tail bounds are explicitly defined; infima are familiar. These related finite-probability additions fit about 30 minutes, without infinite-sequence convergence or measure construction.

Proof 1: Factor the exponential moment of the sum

Using the exponential Markov bounds for the two tails:

When S is the sum of n independent random variables X_1,\ldots,X_n, the moment generating function of S is the product of the individual moment generating functions, giving that:

{\displaystyle \Pr(S\geq a)\leq \inf _{t>0}{\frac {\operatorname {E} \left[\prod _{i}e^{t\cdot X_{i}}\right]}{e^{t\cdot a}}}=\inf _{t>0}e^{-t\cdot a}\prod _{i}\operatorname {E} \left[e^{t\cdot X_{i}}\right].}

and:

{\displaystyle \Pr(S\leq a)\leq \inf _{t<0}e^{-ta}\prod _{i}\operatorname {E} \left[e^{tX_{i}}\right]}
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Exponentiating a finite sum gives a product, independence factors its expectation, and the supplied exponential Markov bounds give both signs. This is ordinary undergraduate probability and real infimum notation.
