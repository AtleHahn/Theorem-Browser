Proof 1: Apply Pythagoras to the coordinate triangle — outline

The distance formula in Cartesian coordinates is derived from the Pythagorean theorem. If (x1, y1) and (x2, y2) are points in the plane, then the distance between them, also called the Euclidean distance, is given by

{\displaystyle {\sqrt {(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}+(y_{1}-y_{2})^{2}}}.}
Level
High school only
Level justification

Use the coordinate differences as the legs of a right triangle and take the nonnegative square root of their squared sum.
