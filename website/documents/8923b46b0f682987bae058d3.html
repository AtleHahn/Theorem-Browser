Statement

For n≥1 and variables x_1,\ldots,x_n, let p_k=\sum_jx_j^k for k≥1, let e_0=1, and let e_k=\sum_{j_1<\cdots<j_k}x_{j_1}\cdots x_{j_k}, with e_k=0 for k>n. For k≥1 let M be k×k with M_{ij}=p_{i-j+1} for j≤i, M_{i,i+1}=i, and other entries zero. Then e_k=det(M)/k!.

Proof 1: Symmetric-polynomial determinant: e from p — outline

Consider the first k Newton identities

j e_j=\sum_{i=1}^j(-1)^{i-1}e_{j-i}p_i,\qquad 1\le j\le k,

as linear equations in which the power sums are known and the elementary symmetric functions are unknown, and apply Cramer's rule to find the solution for the final unknown. Solving for e_k gives

e_k=\frac1{k!} \begin{vmatrix} p_1&1&0&\cdots\\ p_2&p_1&2&0&\cdots\\ \vdots&&\ddots&\ddots\\ p_{k-1}&p_{k-2}&\cdots&p_1&k-1\\ p_k&p_{k-1}&\cdots&p_2&p_1 \end{vmatrix}.

The source prescribes this Cramer computation and supplies the final determinant, leaving its intermediate details implicit.
