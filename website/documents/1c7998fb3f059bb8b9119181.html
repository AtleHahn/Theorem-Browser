Statement

Let X_1,\ldots,X_n be independent Bernoulli variables with common success probability p\in[0,1], and let \overline X=n^{-1}\sum_iX_i. For \varepsilon>0 and 0<\alpha<1, if n\ge\log(2/\alpha)/(2\varepsilon^2) then \mathbb P(|\overline X-p|\le\varepsilon)\ge1-\alpha.

Proof 1: Apply the two-sided bound and solve for sample size

We consider a coin that shows heads with probability p and tails with probability 1 − p. We toss the coin n times, generating n samples {\displaystyle X_{1},\ldots ,X_{n}} (which are i.i.d Bernoulli random variables). The expected number of times the coin comes up heads is pn. Write H(n) for the number of heads in n coin tosses.

For ε > 0, Hoeffding's inequality bounds the upper-tail probability by a term that is exponentially small in ε2n:

{\displaystyle \operatorname {P} (H(n)-pn>\varepsilon n)\leq \exp \left(-2\varepsilon ^{2}n\right).}

Since this bound holds on both sides of the mean, Hoeffding's inequality implies that the number of heads that we see is concentrated around its mean, with exponentially small tail.

{\displaystyle \operatorname {P} \left(|H(n)-pn|>\varepsilon n\right)\leq 2\exp \left(-2\varepsilon ^{2}n\right).}

Thinking of \overline X=H(n)/n as the “observed” mean, this gives

\mathbb P(\overline X\notin[p-\varepsilon,p+\varepsilon])\le2e^{-2\varepsilon^2n}.

To make this bound at most \alpha, it suffices that

n\ge\frac{\log(2/\alpha)}{2\varepsilon^2}.

Then \mathbb P(|\overline X-p|\le\varepsilon)\ge1-\alpha.
