Finiteness is a Markov property

Statement

A property P of finitely presentable groups is called a Markov property if it is preserved by isomorphisms, some finitely presentable group A_+ has P, and some finitely presentable group A_- cannot embed as a subgroup of any finitely presentable group with P.

The property of being finite is a Markov property.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Markov property is explicitly defined. Finite presentations add finite generators and relations to undergraduate groups, and embedding means an injective homomorphism. No Markov undecidability theorem or algorithmic group theory is needed to understand this particular property.

Proof 1: Trivial and infinite cyclic witnesses

We can take {\displaystyle A_{+}} to be the trivial group and we can take {\displaystyle A_{-}} to be the infinite cyclic group {\displaystyle \mathbb {Z} }.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the trivial group as the positive witness and the infinite cyclic group as the nonembedding witness. Finite presentation adds generators and relations to introductory groups; the Markov-property definition is supplied and no undecidability theorem is used.
