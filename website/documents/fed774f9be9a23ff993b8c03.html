Statement

Let \mathcal A be an algebra of subsets of a set \Omega, and let \mu:\mathcal A\to[0,\infty) be finitely additive with \mu(\varnothing)=0. For A_1,\ldots,A_n\in\mathcal A, n\ge1, set S_j=\sum_{1\le i_1<\cdots<i_j\le n}\mu(A_{i_1}\cap\cdots\cap A_{i_j}). For 1\le K\le n, \sum_{j=1}^K(-1)^{j-1}S_j is an upper bound for \mu(\bigcup_{i=1}^nA_i) when K is odd, and a lower bound when K is even. For K=n, equality holds.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Finite additivity is an explicit rule for weights of disjoint sets, and an algebra of sets is closed under elementary finite set operations. The assertion is a finite inclusion-exclusion inequality using only those rules and finite sums, without sigma-algebras or integration theory.

Proof 1: Partition into membership atoms and telescope — outline

Use \binom Lj=0 for j>L, and \binom L0=1. First take odd K.

Let {\displaystyle E=\bigcap _{i=1}^{n}B_{i}}, where {\displaystyle B_{i}\in \{A_{i},A_{i}^{c}\}} for each {\displaystyle i=1,\dots ,n}. These such {\displaystyle E} partition the set \Omega, and for each {\displaystyle E} and every {\displaystyle i}, {\displaystyle E} is either contained in {\displaystyle A_{i}} or disjoint from it.

If {\displaystyle E=\bigcap _{i=1}^{n}A_{i}^{c}}, then {\displaystyle E} contributes 0 to both sides of the inequality.

Otherwise, assume {\displaystyle E} is contained in exactly {\displaystyle L} of the {\displaystyle A_{i}}. Then {\displaystyle E} contributes exactly {\displaystyle \mu (E)} to the right side of the inequality, while it contributes

{\displaystyle \sum _{j=1}^{K}(-1)^{j-1}{L \choose j}\mu (E)}

to the left side of the inequality. However, by Pascal's rule, this is equal to

{\displaystyle \sum _{j=1}^{K}(-1)^{j-1}{\Big (}{L-1 \choose j-1}+{L-1 \choose j}{\Big )}\mu (E)}

which telescopes to

{\displaystyle {\Big (}1+{L-1 \choose K}{\Big )}\mu (E)\geq \mu (E)}

Thus, the inequality holds for all events {\displaystyle E}, and so by summing over {\displaystyle E}, we obtain the desired inequality:

{\displaystyle \sum _{j=1}^{K}(-1)^{j-1}S_{j}\geq \mu {\Big (}\bigcup _{i=1}^{n}A_{i}{\Big )}}

The proof for even {\displaystyle K} is nearly identical.

The argument uses only finite additivity and nonnegativity, so applies to the stated finite content. At K=n it gives the inclusion–exclusion equality.
