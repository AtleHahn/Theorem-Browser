Statement

Let K be an algebraic number field (a finite extension of \mathbb Q), let k,l be positive integers, and let r_1,\ldots,r_k be positive odd integers. There is an integer \psi(r_1,\ldots,r_k,l,K) such that, for every integer n\geq\psi(r_1,\ldots,r_k,l,K) and every collection of homogeneous polynomials f_1,\ldots,f_k\in K[x_1,\ldots,x_n] with \deg f_i=r_i, there is an l-dimensional K-linear subspace V\subseteq K^n on which all the polynomials vanish:

f_1(x)=\cdots=f_k(x)=0\qquad(x\in V).

Here homogeneous of degree r_i means that every monomial of f_i has total degree r_i.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The formula is short, but understanding arbitrary number fields as coefficient and scalar fields together with homogeneous forms and uniform dimension thresholds is a borderline extension of introductory algebra. Field-extension fluency cannot be assumed; conservatively beyond undergraduate.
