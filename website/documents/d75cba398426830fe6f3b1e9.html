Statement

Let K be an algebraic number field (a finite extension of \mathbb Q), let k,l be positive integers, and let r_1,\ldots,r_k be positive odd integers. There is an integer \psi(r_1,\ldots,r_k,l,K) such that, for every integer n\geq\psi(r_1,\ldots,r_k,l,K) and every collection of homogeneous polynomials f_1,\ldots,f_k\in K[x_1,\ldots,x_n] with \deg f_i=r_i, there is an l-dimensional K-linear subspace V\subseteq K^n on which all the polynomials vanish:

f_1(x)=\cdots=f_k(x)=0\qquad(x\in V).

Here homogeneous of degree r_i means that every monomial of f_i has total degree r_i.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The formula is short, but understanding arbitrary number fields as coefficient and scalar fields together with homogeneous forms and uniform dimension thresholds is a borderline extension of introductory algebra. Field-extension fluency cannot be assumed; conservatively beyond undergraduate.

Proof 1: Diagonalization and restriction of scalars (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorem 1 (linear spaces of projective dimension m), definitions pp.1 and 4; proof sections 2–5. Use equal-degree theorem plus the explicit homogenizing bridge, avoiding the abbreviated wording in Theorem 5.1’s proof.).

Let d=[K:Q] and express each f_i in a fixed Q-basis of K, obtaining at most dk rational homogeneous forms in dn rational variables, of the same odd degrees. Apply the rational linear-space theorem with vector-space dimension dl (projective dimension dl-1). To use its equal-degree form, choose an odd D>=7 at least all the degrees and multiply each rational component of degree r by (sum y_j^2)^((D-r)/2); this preserves its rational zero set. The resulting rational subspace W is contained in the common zero set. For a Q-basis w_1,...,w_t of W, each polynomial f_i(sum T_j w_j) vanishes at every rational tuple T and therefore is identically zero over K. Thus it vanishes on the K-linear span of W. This span has K-dimension at least dim_Q(W)/d>=l. Select an l-dimensional K-subspace. The bound depends only on the listed degrees, k,l,K. This explicitly supplies the step that a bare appeal to restriction of scalars would omit.
