Statement

If a vector subspace M of a real or complex topological vector space X is nonmeager in X, then M is dense in X.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement concerns density and category of subspaces of arbitrary topological vector spaces; equicontinuity is not involved.

Proof 1: A proper closed subspace is nowhere dense

Every proper vector subspace of a topological vector space X has an empty interior in X. So in particular, every proper vector subspace that is closed is nowhere dense in X and thus of first category (meager) in X (and the same is also true of all its subsets). Consequently, any vector subspace of X that is of second category (nonmeager) in X must be dense in X (since otherwise, its closure would be a closed proper vector subspace of X and thus of first category).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Use closure and interior properties for subspaces in an arbitrary topological vector space. General vector topologies combined with category notions are not confidently a small extension of ordinary undergraduate metric topology; retain beyond undergraduate for this full setting.
