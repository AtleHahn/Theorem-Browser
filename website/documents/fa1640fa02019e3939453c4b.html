Statement

The following are the strong and weak Whitney embedding theorems. Smooth manifolds are assumed Hausdorff and second-countable.

Strong embedding theorem. Every smooth real manifold M of dimension m>0 admits a smooth embedding into \mathbb R^{2m}. Compactness is not required.

Weak embedding theorem. Let M and N be smooth manifolds without boundary of dimensions n and q, with q>2n. Every continuous map f:M\to N can be approximated by smooth embeddings. More precisely, choose a distance d_N induced by a Riemannian metric on N. For every nonempty compact subset K\subseteq M and every \varepsilon>0, there is a smooth embedding g:M\to N with

\sup_{x\in K}d_N(g(x),f(x))<\varepsilon.

A smooth embedding is a smooth injective map whose derivative is injective at each point and which is a homeomorphism onto its image with the subspace topology. Approximation here is in the compact-open C^0 topology: uniform on each prescribed compact subset. On compact M one may take K=M. The strong assertion uses the usual manifold convention without boundary; it also holds for manifolds with boundary, as allowed in the source's proof discussion.

Proof availability

Source proof sketches and limitations

For the weak theorem, the article proposes embedding a compact manifold using finitely many charts and reducing dimension by suitable projections. It does not provide the full approximation argument for arbitrary maps or the noncompact case.

For the strong theorem, the source starts from an immersion into \mathbb R^{2m} with transverse double points. It describes creating a compensating double point and cancelling opposite-sign pairs by a Whitney-disk move. The article explicitly places a dimension restriction on its disk argument; it does not finish the low-dimensional cases, the required disjointness/framing checks, or the global noncompact construction. Its explicit local immersion formulas illustrate one step and are not a proof of the whole theorem.

Both source strategies are retained here as sketches with substantive omissions. Neither the weak theorem nor the strong theorem is presented as completely proved by these paragraphs.
