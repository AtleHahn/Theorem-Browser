Simpler proof assessment

Likely alternative prerequisite level: Undergraduate level; lowest recorded proof level: Beyond undergraduate level. The checked Tucker-lemma route labels a sufficiently fine antipodally symmetric triangulation by the signed largest coordinate of the proposed odd map. A complementary edge forces two images separated by a fixed positive distance, contradicting uniform continuity. The finite combinatorial labeling lemma avoids projective cohomology. It works in every dimension, in particular n>=3, with Euclidean triangulations, finite graph counting and compactness. These add a small concrete package to undergraduate linear algebra and topology.

Statement

Write S^j=\{x\in\mathbb R^{j+1}:\|x\|_2=1\} and call a map odd if g(-x)=-g(x). For every integer n\ge3, there is no continuous odd map h:S^n\to S^{n-1}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Euclidean spheres; Continuous odd maps. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Projective-space cohomology obstruction (outline)

Assume that h:S^n\to S^{n-1} is an odd continuous function with n\ge3. By passing to orbits under the antipodal action, we then get an induced continuous function

h':\mathbb{RP}^n\to\mathbb{RP}^{n-1}

between real projective spaces, and it induces an isomorphism on fundamental groups because h is odd. Here both fundamental groups are \mathbb Z/2, and the path-lifting criterion for the sphere double covers gives the asserted nontrivial induced map.

By the degree-one Hurewicz theorem and the universal coefficient theorem for cohomology, the induced homomorphism on cohomology with \mathbb Z/2 coefficients,

H^1(\mathbb{RP}^n;\mathbb Z/2)\leftarrow H^1(\mathbb{RP}^{n-1};\mathbb Z/2),

is an isomorphism. Let a be the generator of H^1(\mathbb{RP}^n;\mathbb Z/2)\cong\mathbb Z/2 and b be that of H^1(\mathbb{RP}^{n-1};\mathbb Z/2)\cong\mathbb Z/2. Then the isomorphism sends b to a. Therefore, using the standard cohomology rings of real projective spaces, the induced cohomology ring homomorphism

(\mathbb Z/2)[a]/(a^{n+1}) =H^*(\mathbb{RP}^n;\mathbb Z/2) \quad\longleftarrow\quad H^*(\mathbb{RP}^{n-1};\mathbb Z/2) =(\mathbb Z/2)[b]/(b^n)

sends b^n=0 to a^n\ne0, a contradiction.
