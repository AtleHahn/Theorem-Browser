Statement

For a=(a_1,\ldots,a_8) and b=(b_1,\ldots,b_8) in \mathbb R^8, define a\times b=(c_1,\ldots,c_8) by

\begin{aligned}c_1&=a_{1}b_{1}-a_{2}b_{2}-a_{3}b_{3}-a_{4}b_{4}-a_{5}b_{5}-a_{6}b_{6}-a_{7}b_{7}-a_{8}b_{8}\\c_2&=a_{1}b_{2}+a_{2}b_{1}+a_{3}b_{4}-a_{4}b_{3}+a_{5}b_{6}-a_{6}b_{5}-a_{7}b_{8}+a_{8}b_{7}\\c_3&=a_{1}b_{3}-a_{2}b_{4}+a_{3}b_{1}+a_{4}b_{2}+a_{5}b_{7}+a_{6}b_{8}-a_{7}b_{5}-a_{8}b_{6}\\c_4&=a_{1}b_{4}+a_{2}b_{3}-a_{3}b_{2}+a_{4}b_{1}+a_{5}b_{8}-a_{6}b_{7}+a_{7}b_{6}-a_{8}b_{5}\\c_5&=a_{1}b_{5}-a_{2}b_{6}-a_{3}b_{7}-a_{4}b_{8}+a_{5}b_{1}+a_{6}b_{2}+a_{7}b_{3}+a_{8}b_{4}\\c_6&=a_{1}b_{6}+a_{2}b_{5}-a_{3}b_{8}+a_{4}b_{7}-a_{5}b_{2}+a_{6}b_{1}-a_{7}b_{4}+a_{8}b_{3}\\c_7&=a_{1}b_{7}+a_{2}b_{8}+a_{3}b_{5}-a_{4}b_{6}-a_{5}b_{3}+a_{6}b_{4}+a_{7}b_{1}-a_{8}b_{2}\\c_8&=a_{1}b_{8}-a_{2}b_{7}+a_{3}b_{6}+a_{4}b_{5}-a_{5}b_{4}-a_{6}b_{3}+a_{7}b_{2}+a_{8}b_{1}\end{aligned}

With \|v\|=(\sum_{j=1}^8v_j^2)^{1/2}, one has \|a\times b\|=\|a\|\|b\|.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Each vector is simply a list of eight real numbers. Every product coordinate and the norm are defined by finite arithmetic and square roots, so the statement requires elementary algebra despite its eight-dimensional notation.

Proof 1: Interpret the eight-square identity as a product norm

The eight-square identity can be written in the form of a product of two inner products of 8-dimensional vectors, yielding again an inner product of 8-dimensional vectors: (a·a)(b·b) = (a×b)·(a×b).

Taking nonnegative square roots gives \|a\times b\|=\|a\|\|b\|.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The product and norm are given by explicit finite coordinate formulas. Take nonnegative square roots of the supplied eight-square identity. No octonion or abstract normed-algebra theory is used.
