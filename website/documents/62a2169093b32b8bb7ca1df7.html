Statement

Let G=(V,E) be a finite simple undirected graph. A matching is a subset of edges with no shared endpoints. A vertex is unmatched if no edge of the matching meets it. A path has distinct vertices; its length is its number of edges. An alternating path or cycle has successive edges alternately in and outside the matching (including the closing adjacency for a cycle). If M is a maximum matching and C is an alternating cycle or an even-length alternating path starting at an unmatched vertex, then M\triangle E(C) is also a maximum matching.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Matching and alternating paths or cycles are explicit finite adjacency conditions; maximum means largest edge count.

Proof 1: Interchange alternating edges — outline

Let M be a maximum matching and consider an alternating chain such that the edges in the path alternates between being and not being in M. If the alternating chain is a cycle or a path of even length starting on an unmatched vertex, then a new maximum matching M′ can be found by interchanging the edges found in M and not in M. For example, if the alternating chain is (m1, n1, m2, n2, ...), where mi ∈ M and ni ∉ M, interchanging them would make all ni part of the new matching and make all mi not part of the matching.

This interchange is the symmetric difference M\triangle E(C) in the statement. The source leaves the matching and cardinality checks implicit.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Swap selected and unselected edges along the indicated alternating component. Each affected vertex remains incident to at most one selected edge and the equal edge counts preserve maximal cardinality. Finite counting suffices.
