Statement

Every finite-dimensional central simple algebra R over a field k is isomorphic to M_n(D) for a finite-dimensional division algebra D with center k.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

From undergraduate rings and matrices, add simple as having no proper nonzero two-sided ideals, central as having center k, and a division algebra as a ring in which every nonzero element is invertible. Compatible finite-dimensional k-vector-space structure is familiar. These related definitions fit about 30 minutes and make the matrix-algebra isomorphism claim concrete, without classification theory.

Proof 1: Preservation of the center (outline)

The Wedderburn–Artin theorem reduces the problem of classifying finite-dimensional central simple algebras over a field {\displaystyle k} to the problem of classifying finite-dimensional central division algebras over {\displaystyle k}: that is, division algebras over {\displaystyle k} whose center is {\displaystyle k}. It implies that any finite-dimensional central simple algebra over {\displaystyle k} is isomorphic to a matrix algebra {\displaystyle \textstyle M_{n}(D)} where {\displaystyle D} is a finite-dimensional central division algebra over {\displaystyle k}.

Here the center identity Z(M_n(D))=Z(D)I_n identifies centrality of the matrix algebra with centrality of D.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The supplied structure theorem gives a matrix algebra over a division algebra; comparing centers then identifies its scalar center with k. For a student with introductory rings and linear algebra, the extra definitions of simple, central and division algebra are a small related extension. The proof does not develop semisimple module classification.
