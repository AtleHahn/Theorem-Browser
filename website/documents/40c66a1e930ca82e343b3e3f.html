Shows that first-order descriptions of ordered fields cannot capture the Archimedean restriction excluding infinitely large elements. The real field and a suitable non-Archimedean ultrapower satisfy the same first-order sentences, illustrating how a formal language can leave distinct conceptions of numerical magnitude indistinguishable.

Statement

In the first-order language of ordered fields, there is no sentence true in exactly the Archimedean ordered fields. Here Archimedean means that every field element is smaller than some positive integer multiple of 1.

Proof 1: An ultrapower violates Archimedeanness — outline

An ultrafilter \mathcal U is a proper filter such that for each subset A of its index set exactly one of A and its complement belongs to \mathcal U. The ultrapower \mathbb R^{\mathbb N_{>0}}/\mathcal U identifies sequences agreeing on a set in \mathcal U, and [s]<[t] means \{i:s_i<t_i\}\in\mathcal U. Real numbers are represented by constant sequences. The hyperreal numbers are the ultraproduct of one copy of the real numbers for every positive integer, with regard to an ultrafilter over the positive integers containing all cofinite sets. Their order is the extension of the order of the real numbers. For example, the sequence \omega given by \omega_i=i defines an equivalence class representing a hyperreal number that is greater than any real number.

Consider, however, the Archimedean property of the reals, which states that there is no real number x such that x>1,\;x>1+1,\;x>1+1+1,\ldots for every inequality in the infinite list. By Łoś's theorem, an ultrapower of \mathbb R satisfies the same first-order sentences as \mathbb R. The Archimedean property is false for the hyperreals, as shown by the construction of the hyperreal number \omega above. It therefore cannot be stated in the first-order language of ordered fields.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof combines ultrafilters, equivalence classes of sequences, the induced field order and preservation of first-order sentences under an ultrapower. The sequence i is easily larger than every constant modulo the cofinite filter, but understanding why that refutes first-order definability requires the full ultraproduct and model-theoretic framework. These interdependent prerequisites exceed the conservative undergraduate allowance.

2. Existence of an ultrafilter extending the cofinite filter
Kind:

Theorem

Treatment:

Implicitly assumed

Statement used:

The cofinite filter on the positive integers extends to a proper ultrafilter, which is necessarily nonprincipal.

Application in proof:

Choose the ultrafilter for which every tail {i:i>r} is large, so the class of i exceeds every real constant.

Application level:

Beyond undergraduate level

Level justification:

This is an ultrafilter-existence result applied within the ultrapower construction; its proof by a choice principle is not required.
