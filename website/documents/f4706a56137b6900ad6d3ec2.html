Explanation

Elementary number theory may suffice, but needs verification. Taking A={1,...,n} reduces the question to proving that the multiplication table has o(n^2) distinct products, while |A+A|=2n-1. That is a promising simpler construction than the stored smooth-number example, but the needed product-count estimate is itself nontrivial and was not proved here.

Statement

For a finite numerical set A, write A+A=\{a+b:a,b\in A\} and A\cdot A=\{ab:a,b\in A\}. There is no constant K>0 such that every nonempty finite integer set A satisfies \max(|A+A|,|A\cdot A|)\geq K|A|^2.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The conclusion quantifies over finite integer sets and a real constant using a quadratic inequality.

Proof 1: A fixed quadratic sum-product lower bound cannot hold — outline

Use the stated Erdős–Szemerédi smooth-set examples, of unbounded cardinality, for which, with a fixed c>0, \max(|A+A|,|A\cdot A|)\leq |A|^2\exp\!\left(-\frac{c\log|A|}{\log\log|A|}\right). This shows that the error in the quadratic exponent cannot be omitted.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the supplied examples of unbounded size and let their logarithmic correction tend to zero. Undergraduate real limits and finite-set cardinalities suffice; the construction of the smooth sets is not required.
