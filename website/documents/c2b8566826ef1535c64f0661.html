Statement

Let R be a commutative ring with identity, I an ideal, and h\in R[X] have leading coefficient \alpha whose image in R/I is a unit. Let f,g be monic with \deg f+\deg g=\deg h, h\equiv\alpha fg\pmod I, and af+bg\equiv1\pmod I for some a,b\in R[X]. For each integer n\ge1 there are monic f_n,g_n of the same degrees as f,g, respectively, with f_n\equiv f, g_n\equiv g\pmod I and h\equiv\alpha f_ng_n\pmod {I^n}. These lifts are unique modulo I^n.

Level

Level justification

Introductory ring theory supplies ideals, quotient congruences, powers and polynomial degrees. All lifting conditions are explicit finite algebraic identities modulo ideal powers; no adic completion or topology is needed to understand these formulations.

Proof 1: Linear corrections and uniqueness by monic division

We lift a factorization h\equiv\alpha fg\pmod{I^k} one power at a time. Here f,g are monic of the prescribed degrees, and af+bg\equiv1\pmod I. Congruence modulo an ideal means that every coefficient of the difference belongs to that ideal.

Proof: By hypothesis, {\displaystyle \alpha } is invertible modulo I. This means that there exists {\displaystyle \beta \in R} and {\displaystyle \gamma \in I} such that {\displaystyle \alpha \beta =1-\gamma .}

Let {\displaystyle \delta _{h}\in I^{k}R[X],} of degree less than {\displaystyle \deg h,} such that

{\displaystyle \delta _{h}\equiv h-\alpha fg{\pmod {I^{k+1}}}.}

(One may choose {\displaystyle \delta _{h}=h-\alpha fg,} but other choices may lead to simpler computations. For example, if {\displaystyle R=\mathbb {Z} } and {\displaystyle I=p\mathbb {Z} ,} it is possible and better to choose {\displaystyle \delta _{h}=p^{k}\delta '_{h}} where the coefficients of {\displaystyle \delta '_{h}} are integers in the interval {\displaystyle [0,p-1].})

As g is monic, the Euclidean division of {\displaystyle a\delta _{h}} by g is defined, and provides q and c such that {\displaystyle a\delta _{h}=qg+c,} and {\displaystyle \deg c<\deg g.} Moreover, both q and c are in {\displaystyle I^{k}R[X].} Similarly, let {\displaystyle b\delta _{h}=q'f+d,} with {\displaystyle \deg d<\deg f,} and {\displaystyle q',d\in I^{k}R[X].}

One has {\displaystyle q+q'\in I^{k+1}R[X].} Indeed, one has

{\displaystyle fc+gd=af\delta _{h}+bg\delta _{h}-fg(q+q')\equiv \delta _{h}-fg(q+q'){\pmod {I^{k+1}}}.}

As {\displaystyle fg} is monic, the degree modulo {\displaystyle I^{k+1}} of {\displaystyle fg(q+q')} can be less than {\displaystyle \deg fg} only if {\displaystyle q+q'\in I^{k+1}R[X].}

Thus, considering congruences modulo {\displaystyle I^{k+1},} one has

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\alpha (f+\beta d)&(g+\beta c)-h\\&\equiv \alpha fg-h+\alpha \beta (f(a\delta _{h}-qg)+g(b\delta _{h}-q'f))\\&\equiv \delta _{h}(-1+\alpha \beta (af+bg))-\alpha \beta fg(q+q')\\&\equiv 0{\pmod {I^{k+1}}}.\end{aligned}}}

So, the existence assertion is verified with

{\displaystyle \delta _{f}=\beta d,\qquad \delta _{g}=\beta c.}

The degree bounds preserve monicity, and the corrections belong to I^kR[X]. They also preserve a(f+\delta_f)+b(g+\delta_g)\equiv1\pmod I, allowing iteration.

Uniqueness.

For uniqueness, retain R,I,h and \alpha from the statement. Let

{\displaystyle h\equiv \alpha fg{\pmod {I}}}

be a factorization into coprime polynomials (in the above sense), such {\displaystyle \deg f+\deg g=\deg h.} The application of linear lifting for {\displaystyle k=1,2,\ldots ,n-1\ldots ,} shows the existence of {\displaystyle \delta _{f}} and {\displaystyle \delta _{g}} such that {\displaystyle \deg \delta _{f}<\deg f,} {\displaystyle \deg \delta _{g}<\deg g,} and

{\displaystyle h\equiv \alpha (f+\delta _{f})(g+\delta _{g}){\pmod {I^{n}}}.}

The polynomials {\displaystyle \delta _{f}} and {\displaystyle \delta _{g}} are uniquely defined modulo {\displaystyle I^{n}.} This means that, if another pair {\displaystyle (\delta '_{f},\delta '_{g})} satisfies the same conditions, then one has

{\displaystyle \delta '_{f}\equiv \delta _{f}{\pmod {I^{n}}}\qquad {\text{and}}\qquad \delta '_{g}\equiv \delta _{g}{\pmod {I^{n}}}.}

The corrections lie in IR[X]. Since a congruence modulo {\displaystyle I^{n}} implies the same congruence modulo {\displaystyle I^{n-1},} one can proceed by induction and suppose that the uniqueness has been proved for n − 1, the case n = 1 being the prescribed reductions. That is, one can suppose that

{\displaystyle \delta _{f}-\delta '_{f}\in I^{n-1}R[X]\qquad {\text{and}}\qquad \delta _{g}-\delta '_{g}\in I^{n-1}R[X].}

By hypothesis, one has

{\displaystyle h\equiv \alpha (f+\delta _{f})(g+\delta _{g})\equiv \alpha (f+\delta '_{f})(g+\delta '_{g}){\pmod {I^{n}}},}

and thus

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\alpha (f+\delta _{f})(g+\delta _{g})&-\alpha (f+\delta '_{f})(g+\delta '_{g})\\&=\alpha (f(\delta _{g}-\delta '_{g})+g(\delta _{f}-\delta '_{f}))+\alpha (\delta _{f}(\delta _{g}-\delta '_{g})+\delta '_{g}(\delta _{f}-\delta '_{f}))\in I^{n}R[X].\end{aligned}}}

By induction hypothesis, the second term of the latter sum belongs to {\displaystyle I^{n},} and the same is thus true for the first term. As {\displaystyle \alpha } is invertible modulo I, there exist {\displaystyle \beta \in R} and {\displaystyle \gamma \in I} such that {\displaystyle \alpha \beta =1+\gamma .} Thus

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}f(\delta _{g}-\delta '_{g})&+g(\delta _{f}-\delta '_{f})\\&=\alpha \beta (f(\delta _{g}-\delta '_{g})+g(\delta _{f}-\delta '_{f}))-\gamma (f(\delta _{g}-\delta '_{g})+g(\delta _{f}-\delta '_{f}))\in I^{n}R[X],\end{aligned}}}

using the induction hypothesis again.

The coprimality modulo I implies the existence of {\displaystyle a,b\in R[X]} such that {\textstyle 1\equiv af+bg{\pmod {I}}.} Using the induction hypothesis once more, one gets

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\delta _{g}-\delta '_{g}&\equiv (af+bg)(\delta _{g}-\delta '_{g})\\&\equiv g(b(\delta _{g}-\delta '_{g})-a(\delta _{f}-\delta '_{f})){\pmod {I^{n}}}.\end{aligned}}}

Thus one has a polynomial of degree less than {\displaystyle \deg g} that is congruent modulo {\displaystyle I^{n}} to the product of the monic polynomial g and another polynomial w. This is possible only if {\displaystyle w\in I^{n}R[X],} and implies {\displaystyle \delta _{g}-\delta '_{g}\in I^{n}R[X].} Similarly, {\displaystyle \delta _{f}-\delta '_{f}} is also in {\displaystyle I^{n}R[X],} and this proves the uniqueness.
