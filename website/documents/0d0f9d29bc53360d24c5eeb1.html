Statement

Let p be a positive prime and let a,b be integers. If p\mid ab, then p\mid a or p\mid b.

Proof 1: Apply the coprime identity to a prime — outline

If p\mid a, the conclusion holds. Otherwise a and p are coprime. Put L=ab.

If the greatest common divisor of a and p is 1, then integers s and t can be found such that

1 = sa + tp

by Bézout's identity. Multiplying both sides by b gives the relation:

b = sab + tpb = sL + tpb

Since p divides both terms on the right-hand side, it must also divide the left-hand side, b.

Proof 2: Use least integer ratios as in Elements — outline

After the routine zero and sign reductions, take a,b>0. In the terminology below, “measures” means “divides,” and “prime to one another” means “coprime.” We use the following propositions of Euclid’s Elements, Book VII.

Proposition 19

If four numbers be proportional, the number produced from the first and fourth is equal to the number produced from the second and third; and, if the number produced from the first and fourth be equal to that produced from the second and third, the four numbers are proportional.

Proposition 20

The least numbers of those that have the same ratio with them measures those that have the same ratio the same number of times—the greater the greater and the less the less.

Proposition 21

Numbers prime to one another are the least of those that have the same ratio with them.

Proposition 29

Any prime number is prime to any number it does not measure.

Proof of Proposition 30 (Euclid’s lemma)

Writing c=p, if c, a prime number, measure ab, c measures either a or b. Suppose c does not measure a. Therefore c, a are prime to one another. ［VII. 29］ Suppose ab=mc. Therefore c : a = b : m. ［VII. 19］ Hence ［VII. 20, 21］ b=nc, where n is some integer. Therefore c measures b. Similarly, if c does not measure b, c measures a. Therefore c measures one or other of the two numbers a, b. Q.E.D.
