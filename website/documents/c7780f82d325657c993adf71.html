Statement

Let f(x)=x+\sin x on I=[3\pi/4,5\pi/4]. Then f maps I to itself with Lipschitz constant 1-1/\sqrt2<1, has unique fixed point \pi there, and every iteration x_{n+1}=f(x_n) with x_0\in I converges to \pi. In particular one may take x_0=3.

Proof 1: Derivative bound and contraction theorem

We use the contraction theorem for a closed real interval: a self-map with Lipschitz constant q<1 has a unique fixed point and every sequence of successive iterates converges to it.

Consider the function {\displaystyle f(x)=x+\sin(x)}. It can be verified that {\displaystyle \pi } is a fixed point of {\displaystyle f}, and that {\displaystyle f} maps the interval {\displaystyle \left[{\frac {3\pi }{4}},{\frac {5\pi }{4}}\right]} to itself. Moreover, {\displaystyle f^{\prime }(x)=1+\cos(x)}, and it can be verified that

{\displaystyle 0\leqslant 1+\cos(x)\leqslant 1-{\frac {1}{{\sqrt {2\,}}\,}}\approx 0.292893<1}

on this interval. Therefore, by an application of the mean value theorem, {\displaystyle f} has a Lipschitz constant less than {\displaystyle 1} (namely {\displaystyle 1-1/{\sqrt {2}}}). Applying the Banach fixed-point theorem shows that the fixed point {\displaystyle \pi } is the unique fixed point on the interval, allowing for fixed-point iteration to be used.

For example, the value {\displaystyle 3} may be chosen to start the fixed-point iteration, as {\displaystyle {\frac {3\pi }{4}}\leqslant 3\leqslant {\frac {5\pi }{4}}}.
