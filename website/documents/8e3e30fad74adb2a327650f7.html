Statement

Let a,b be perpendicular unit vectors in \mathbb R^m (m\ge2 integer), G=ba^T-ab^T, and P=-G^2, the orthogonal projection onto their plane. Rotation by angle \beta in the oriented (a,b) plane, fixing its orthogonal complement, is R=I-P+(I\cos\beta+G\sin\beta)P=e^{\beta G}.

Proof 1: Use plane coordinates and complementary projections — outline

One starts with the plane defined by the pair of perpendicular unit vectors a and b. In this plane choose a unit vector x with perpendicular unit vector y. Solve for y in terms of x and substitute into an expression for a rotation in the plane:

\begin{aligned} x&=a\cos\alpha+b\sin\alpha\\ y&=-a\sin\alpha+b\cos\alpha\\[8pt] \cos\alpha&=a^Tx\\ \sin\alpha&=b^Tx\\[8pt] y&=-ab^Tx+ba^Tx=(ba^T-ab^T)x\\[8pt] x'&=x\cos\beta+y\sin\beta\\ &=\bigl(I\cos\beta+(ba^T-ab^T)\sin\beta\bigr)x. \end{aligned}

Thus, on the plane,

\begin{aligned} R&=I\cos\beta+(ba^T-ab^T)\sin\beta\\ &=I\cos\beta+G\sin\beta,\\ G&=ba^T-ab^T. \end{aligned}

To include vectors outside the plane in the rotation one needs to modify this expression for R by including two projection operators that partition the space. This modified rotation matrix can be rewritten as an exponential function:

\begin{aligned} P&=-G^2,\\ R&=I-P+(I\cos\beta+G\sin\beta)P=e^{G\beta}. \end{aligned}

Here the exponential is defined by its convergent power series; the source leaves the projection and power-series checks implicit.
