Statement

Let M be a smooth Riemannian manifold with its Levi–Civita connection, and p\in M. Let B_\epsilon(0)\subset T_pM be a ball on which the exponential map is defined and smooth, with \exp_p(tv)=\gamma_{p,v}(t) for the geodesic having initial velocity v. Identify the tangent spaces of the vector space T_pM with T_pM itself. There is a neighborhood of zero on which \exp_p is a diffeomorphism onto a neighborhood of p.

Proof 1: Apply the inverse theorem to the identity differential

Let \alpha:I\to T_pM be a curve differentiable in T_pM, with I an interval about 0, such that \alpha(0)=0 and \alpha'(0)=v. Since T_pM\cong\mathbb R^n, it is clear that we can choose \alpha(t)=vt. In this case, by the definition of the differential of the exponential at 0 applied to v, we obtain:

\begin{aligned} (d\exp_p)_0(v) &=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\bigl(\exp_p\circ\alpha(t)\bigr)\right|_{t=0}\\ &=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\bigl(\exp_p(vt)\bigr)\right|_{t=0} =\left.\frac{d}{dt}\gamma_{p,v}(t)\right|_{t=0}\\ &=\gamma_{p,v}'(0)=v. \end{aligned}

So (with the identification T_0T_pM\cong T_pM) the differential of \exp_p at zero is the identity.

By the inverse function theorem (the local inverse consequence of the implicit function theorem), \exp_p is a diffeomorphism on a neighborhood of 0\in T_pM onto a neighborhood of p.
