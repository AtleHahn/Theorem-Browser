Statement

For real x\ne0 and real y, the nonzero vectors (x,y) and (-x,-y) have the same quotient of second to first coordinate, but their polar angles differ by \pi modulo 2\pi. Thus \arctan(y/x) alone cannot distinguish their arguments.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement concerns ordinary plane angles and a real quotient.

Proof 1: Compare opposite vectors with the same tangent

For any real x\neq0 and real y, the angles of the vectors (x,y) and (-x,-y) differ by \pi radians, but have the identical value of \tan\varphi=y/x. Thus writing \varphi=\tan^{-1}(y/x) needs adjustment when x<0.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Opposite nonzero vectors point a half-turn apart, while negating both coordinates leaves their quotient unchanged. Therefore a function of that quotient alone cannot distinguish the two polar angles. This is elementary plane geometry and trigonometry.
