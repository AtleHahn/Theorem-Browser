The same differential equation and initial value admit distinct trajectories when uniqueness regularity fails. A mathematical law in differential-equation form therefore does not alone establish determinism.

Statement

Fix a<0, with real y^{2/3}=(\sqrt[3]{y})^2. The equation y'=ay^{2/3}, y(0)=0, has at least two distinct continuously differentiable solutions on \mathbb R.

Proof 1: A cubic trajectory joined to the zero solution — outline

Consider the homogeneous nonlinear equation dy/dt=ay^{2/3}, which has at least these two solutions corresponding to the initial condition y(0)=0: y(t)=0 and

y(t)= \begin{cases} \left(\dfrac{at}{3}\right)^3,&t<0,\\ 0,&t\ge0. \end{cases}

So the previous state of the system is not uniquely determined by its state at or after t=0.

The source leaves the derivative and matching-at-zero checks implicit.
