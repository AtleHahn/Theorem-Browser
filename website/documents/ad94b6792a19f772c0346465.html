Statement

For f(t)=\sin t on [0,\infty), its Laplace transform is F(s)=1/(s^2+1) for real s>0. Although \lim_{s\downarrow0}sF(s)=0, the limit \lim_{t\to\infty}f(t) does not exist.

Proof 1: Sine transform and oscillation — outline

The source uses the standard sine transform to compute \lim_{s\downarrow0}sF(s)=\lim_{s\downarrow0}\frac{s}{s^2+1}=0. The sine function does not approach a limit at infinity, so this supplies the counterexample.
