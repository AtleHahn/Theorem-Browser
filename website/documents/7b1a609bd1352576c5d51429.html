Statement

Let a,b,c\in\mathbb Z with (a,b)\ne(0,0), and put g=\gcd(a,b)>0. The equation ax+by=c has an integer solution if and only if g\mid c. If sa+tb=g, one solution is (x_1,y_1)=(sc/g,tc/g); all solutions are x=x_1-bu/g, y=y_1+au/g for u\in\mathbb Z.

Proof 1: Scale one Bézout solution and compare two solutions — outline

Let g be the greatest common divisor of a and b. Both terms in ax + by are divisible by g; therefore, c must also be divisible by g, or the equation has no solutions. If g\mid c, use Bézout's identity

sa + tb = g,

where s and t can be found by the extended Euclidean algorithm. This provides one solution to the Diophantine equation, x1 = s (c/g) and y1 = t (c/g).

In general, a linear Diophantine equation has no solutions, or an infinite number of solutions. To find the latter, consider two solutions, (x1, y1) and (x2, y2), where

ax1 + by1 = c = ax2 + by2

or equivalently

a(x1 − x2) = b(y2 − y1).

Since a/g and b/g are coprime, the difference pair is an integer multiple of (-b/g,a/g); the same formula covers a zero coefficient when the other is nonzero. Thus, the solutions may be expressed as

x = x1 − bu/g

y = y1 + au/g.

By allowing u to vary over all possible integers, an infinite family of solutions can be generated from a single solution (x1, y1).
