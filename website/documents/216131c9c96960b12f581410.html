Statement

Let g:U\to V and f:V\to\mathbb R^k, where U\subset\mathbb R^n and V\subset\mathbb R^m are open, g is differentiable at a\in U, and f is differentiable at g(a). Then J_{f\circ g}(a)=J_f(g(a))J_g(a).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves total differentiability; real finite-dimensional maps; Jacobian matrices. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Represent composed linear derivatives by matrices

Use the total-derivative chain rule D(f\circ g)(a)=Df(g(a))\circ Dg(a).

Because the total derivative is a linear transformation, the functions appearing in the formula can be rewritten as matrices. The matrix corresponding to a total derivative is called a Jacobian matrix, and the composite of two derivatives corresponds to the product of their Jacobian matrices. From this perspective the chain rule therefore says:

{\displaystyle J_{f\circ g}(\mathbf {a} )=J_{f}(g(\mathbf {a} ))J_{g}(\mathbf {a} ),}

or for short,

{\displaystyle J_{f\circ g}=(J_{f}\circ g)J_{g}.}

That is, the Jacobian of a composite function is the product of the Jacobians of the composed functions (evaluated at the appropriate points).
