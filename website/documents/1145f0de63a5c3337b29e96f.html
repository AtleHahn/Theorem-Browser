Statement

Let X_1,\ldots,X_n be iid real random variables with \mathbb EX_1=0, \mathbb EX_1^2=\sigma^2 and \mathbb EX_1^4=\tau<\infty. For S_n=\sum_{j=1}^nX_j, \mathbb ES_n^4=n\tau+3n(n-1)\sigma^4.

Level

Level justification

The additional notions are finite iid sampling and expectations of powers; these linked moment definitions extend undergraduate integration and finite polynomial expansion without probability limit theory.

Proof 1: Count the nonzero terms of the fourth-power expansion

We compute

{\displaystyle {\mathbb {E} }[S_{n}^{4}]={\mathbb {E} }\left[\left(\sum _{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\right)^{4}\right]={\mathbb {E} }\left[\sum _{1\leq i,j,k,l\leq n}X_{i}X_{j}X_{k}X_{l}\right].}

We first claim that every term of the form {\displaystyle X_{i}^{3}X_{j},X_{i}^{2}X_{j}X_{k},X_{i}X_{j}X_{k}X_{l}} where all subscripts are distinct, must have zero expectation. This is because {\displaystyle {\mathbb {E} }[X_{i}^{3}X_{j}]={\mathbb {E} }[X_{i}^{3}]{\mathbb {E} }[X_{j}]} by independence, and the last term is zero—and similarly for the other terms. Therefore the only terms in the sum with nonzero expectation are {\displaystyle {\mathbb {E} }[X_{i}^{4}]} and {\displaystyle {\mathbb {E} }[X_{i}^{2}X_{j}^{2}]}. Since the {\displaystyle X_{i}} are identically distributed, all terms of each of these two forms have the same expectation, and moreover {\displaystyle {\mathbb {E} }[X_{i}^{2}X_{j}^{2}]=({\mathbb {E} }[X_{i}^{2}])^{2}}.

There are {\displaystyle n} terms of the form {\displaystyle {\mathbb {E} }[X_{i}^{4}]} and {\displaystyle 3n(n-1)} terms of the form {\displaystyle ({\mathbb {E} }[X_{i}^{2}])^{2}}, and so

{\displaystyle {\mathbb {E} }[S_{n}^{4}]=n\tau +3n(n-1)\sigma ^{4}.}
