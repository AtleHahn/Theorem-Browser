Statement

For finite complex matrices A\in\mathbb C^{m\times n} and B\in\mathbb C^{n\times m} and every nonzero real \lambda, \det(AB-\lambda I_m)=(-\lambda)^{m-n}\det(BA-\lambda I_n). Consequently AB and BA have the same nonzero complex eigenvalues.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves finite complex matrices; characteristic determinants; complex eigenvalues. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Determinant identity and nonzero scalar factor (outline)

Use the Weinstein–Aronszajn identity, namely \det(I_m+CD)=\det(I_n+DC) for rectangular finite matrices C,D. The source applies this identity to obtain

{\displaystyle \det(AB-\lambda I_{m})=(-\lambda )^{m-n}\det(BA-\lambda I_{n}).}

The scaling substitution into the identity is left implicit in the source. The characteristic expressions are polynomials after clearing any negative power of lambda. Their equality for every nonzero real lambda implies polynomial equality and hence equality for nonzero complex lambda. Since the scalar factor is nonzero there, the characteristic determinants have the same nonzero complex roots, giving the stated eigenvalue consequence.
