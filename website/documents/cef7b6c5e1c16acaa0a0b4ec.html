Statement

If r_1,\ldots,r_n\ge0, n\ge1, and \sum_i r_i^2\le C, then \sum_i r_i\le\sqrt{nC}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement involves only finite sums of nonnegative real numbers, squares, square roots and an inequality between them.

Proof 1: A squared-radius bound controls the sum of radii — source argument

Apply Jensen’s inequality to the convex function x\mapsto x^2: (n^{-1}\sum_i r_i)^2\le n^{-1}\sum_i r_i^2\le C/n. Taking square roots and multiplying by n gives the bound.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Apply the explicit finite square-mean inequality and take nonnegative square roots. School real inequalities suffice; general convex-function theory is unnecessary.
