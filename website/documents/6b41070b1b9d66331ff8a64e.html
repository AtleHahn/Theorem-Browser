Statement

Let ABC be a nondegenerate Euclidean triangle, with opposite side lengths a,b,c, respective interior angles \alpha,\beta,\gamma, semiperimeter s=(a+b+c)/2, and inradius r>0. Then \frac{\cot(\alpha/2)}{s-a}=\frac{\cot(\beta/2)}{s-b}=\frac{\cot(\gamma/2)}{s-c}=\frac1r. Furthermore, r=\sqrt{\frac{(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}{s}}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Both the half-angle cotangent ratios and the positive square-root formula use only triangle side lengths, interior angles, the incircle and elementary trigonometry. High-school geometry and algebra suffice.

Proof 1: Incircle and half-angle calculation

The points of tangency of the incircle with the sides of the triangle break the perimeter into 6 segments, in 3 pairs. In each pair the segments are of equal length. For example, the 2 segments adjacent to vertex A are equal. If we pick one segment from each pair, their sum will be the semiperimeter s. Write x,y,z for their lengths from A,B,C, respectively. The two segments on side a add up to a=y+z, so the segment from A must be of length x=s-a. The other segments similarly have lengths s-a, s-b, or s-c.

The radius to a point of tangency is perpendicular to the side, and the line joining the incenter to a vertex bisects that vertex angle. Using the definition of the cotangent function in the resulting right triangle, we have

\cot\frac{\alpha}{2}=\frac{s-a}{r},

and similarly for the other two angles, proving the first assertion.

For the inradius formula, we start from the general addition formula:

\cot(u+v+w)= \frac{\cot u+\cot v+\cot w-\cot u\cot v\cot w} {1-\cot u\cot v-\cot v\cot w-\cot w\cot u}.

Applying it to

\cot\left(\frac12\alpha+\frac12\beta+\frac12\gamma\right) =\cot\frac{\pi}{2}=0,

we obtain

\cot\frac{\alpha}{2}\cot\frac{\beta}{2}\cot\frac{\gamma}{2} =\cot\frac{\alpha}{2}+\cot\frac{\beta}{2}+\cot\frac{\gamma}{2}.

(This is also the triple cotangent identity.)

Substituting the values obtained in the first part, we get

\begin{aligned} \frac{s-a}{r}\frac{s-b}{r}\frac{s-c}{r} &=\frac{s-a}{r}+\frac{s-b}{r}+\frac{s-c}{r}\\ &=\frac{3s-2s}{r}\\ &=\frac{s}{r}. \end{aligned}

Multiplying through by r^3/s gives the value of r^2, and taking the positive square root proves the assertion.
