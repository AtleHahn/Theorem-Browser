Statement

Let V be a real Hilbert space with norm \|\cdot\|, and let a:V\times V\to\mathbb R be bilinear with |a(v,w)|\le\gamma\|v\|\|w\| and a(v,v)\ge\alpha\|v\|^2 for all v,w\in V, where \gamma,\alpha>0. Let L:V\to\mathbb R be bounded and linear, and let V_h\subseteq V be finite-dimensional. Let u\in V and u_h\in V_h satisfy a(u,v)=L(v) for all v\in V and a(u_h,v)=L(v) for all v\in V_h. Then a(u-u_h,w)=0 for every w\in V_h.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra supplies linear functionals and bilinear forms; real analysis supplies norm bounds and completeness. The interpolation or variational equations are explicit. These direct normed/Hilbert-space extensions require no dual-space topology or PDE background.

Proof 1: Subtract the two variational equations

For every w\in V_h, the two variational equations give a(u,w)=L(w)=a(u_h,w), hence a(u-u_h,w)=0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Subtract the two supplied equations and use bilinearity. The Hilbert-space setting only adds completeness of an inner-product norm; all continuity and coercivity bounds are explicit, and no variational existence theory is used.
