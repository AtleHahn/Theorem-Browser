Likely alternative prerequisite level: Undergraduate level; lowest recorded proof level: Beyond undergraduate level. Periodize the Schwartz transform and compute its Fourier coefficients by termwise integration over one period followed by unfolding to the real line. Fourier inversion identifies them as T s(nT), with the sign convention in the statement. Rapid decay of both transforms gives absolute/uniform convergence, so Fourier uniqueness yields pointwise equality. Ordinary real Fourier integrals, series and estimates replace tempered distributions and Dirac combs; the alternative remains undergraduate.

Statement

Let s be Schwartz on \mathbb R, S(f)=\int_{\mathbb R}s(x)e^{-2\pi ixf}\,dx, and T>0. Then \sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}S(f+k/T)=T\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}s(nT)e^{-2\pi inTf}. Both series converge absolutely for every real f.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Calculus supplies improper Fourier integrals and analysis supplies convergent series. Schwartz means smooth with each derivative decaying faster than every inverse power; this is a direct quantified growth condition. The transform is explicitly given, with no distribution or L2 completion involved.

Proof 1: Fourier transform a sampled Dirac comb

Use the distributional comb identity

\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{i2\pi(k/T)x} =T\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\delta(x-nT)

and the Fourier modulation rule. Then

\begin{aligned} \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}S(f-k/T) &=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\mathcal F\{s(x)e^{i2\pi(k/T)x}\}(f)\\ &=\mathcal F\left\{s(x)\underbrace{\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{i2\pi(k/T)x}}_{T\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\delta(x-nT)}\right\}(f)\\ &=\mathcal F\left\{\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}T s(nT)\delta(x-nT)\right\}(f)\\ &=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}T s(nT)\mathcal F\{\delta(x-nT)\}(f)\\ &=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}T s(nT)e^{-i2\pi nTf}. \end{aligned}

Equivalently, writing * for convolution, the same calculation begins

\begin{aligned} \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}S(f-k/T) &=S(f)*\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\delta(f-k/T)\\ &=S(f)*\mathcal F\left\{T\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\delta(x-nT)\right\}(f)\\ &=\mathcal F\left\{s(x)T\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\delta(x-nT)\right\}(f)\\ &=\mathcal F\left\{\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}T s(nT)\delta(x-nT)\right\}(f), \end{aligned}

and finishes as above.

Reindex k to replace the minus sign by a plus sign. For Schwartz s, both sides are convergent smooth periodic functions, so their distributional equality gives pointwise equality.
