The numbers of positive and negative directions of a real quadratic form survive every invertible coordinate change. In geometric models this explains why Euclidean and Lorentzian metric signatures express a structural distinction rather than a mere choice of coordinates.

Statement

Let n\geq1. For real invertible symmetric n\times n matrices A and B, let q_A(x)=x^{\mathsf T}Ax and q_B(x)=x^{\mathsf T}Bx. Let n_+(A) and n_-(A) denote the numbers of positive and negative eigenvalues of A, counted with multiplicity, and define n_+(B),n_-(B) similarly. Then there exists an invertible real matrix P such that

P^{\mathsf T}AP=B

if and only if

(n_+(A),n_-(A))=(n_+(B),n_-(B)).

Thus the signature, the pair of positive and negative indices, is a complete invariant of nonsingular real quadratic forms under invertible linear changes of variables.
