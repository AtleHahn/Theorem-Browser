Statement

Let F be a field, A\in M_n(F) with n\ge1, p(t)=\det(tI_n-A), and v\ne0 an eigenvector with eigenvalue \lambda\in F. Then p(A)v=p(\lambda)v=0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Undergraduate linear algebra supplies inner products, determinants, characteristic polynomials and traces. Introductory ring theory supplies the general scalar setting where needed, and the displayed formulas define all remaining notation. No specialist extension is required for understanding.

Proof 1: Evaluate each power on the eigenvector

Write p(t)=t^n+c_{n-1}t^{n-1}+\cdots+c_0.

If a vector v of size n is an eigenvector of A with eigenvalue λ, in other words if A⋅v = λv, then

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}p(A)\cdot v&=A^{n}\cdot v+c_{n-1}A^{n-1}\cdot v+\cdots +c_{1}A\cdot v+c_{0}I_{n}\cdot v\\[6pt]&=\lambda ^{n}v+c_{n-1}\lambda ^{n-1}v+\cdots +c_{1}\lambda v+c_{0}v=p(\lambda )v,\end{aligned}}}

which is the zero vector since p(λ) = 0 (the eigenvalues of A are precisely the roots of p(t)).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Repeatedly use Av=lambda v to evaluate each matrix power on v, then use the characteristic-root criterion. This is standard undergraduate eigenvector algebra.
