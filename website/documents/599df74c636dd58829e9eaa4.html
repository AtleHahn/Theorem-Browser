Statement

Let S be an associative unital ring, a a central element of S, and S[t] the polynomial ring in a central indeterminate. The map \operatorname{ev}_a(\sum_i s_it^i)=\sum_i s_i a^i is a unital ring homomorphism.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory abstract algebra supplies groups, ring operations, quotients and homomorphisms. The additional operations or central-element conditions are explicitly given; for the circle group, complex exponentials and continuity are familiar from calculus and topology. No further structural theory is needed to understand the claim.

Proof 1: Polynomial evaluation at a central element is a homomorphism — outline

A product s_it^iu_jt^j=(s_iu_j)t^{i+j} is defined assuming that t commutes with u_j.

The right-evaluation map becomes a ring homomorphism if a is in the center of the ring of coefficients, so that it commutes with all the coefficients of the polynomials (the argument proving this is straightforward, exactly because commuting t with coefficients is now justified after evaluation).

Level justification

Preservation of addition and identity is immediate. For multiplication, centrality allows s_i a^i u_j a^j=s_i u_j a^(i+j), and finite distributivity completes the check. This is introductory ring theory with the central-indeterminate convention stated explicitly.
