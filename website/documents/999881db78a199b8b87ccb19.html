Statement

Let D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\} and let h:D\to D be holomorphic and injective with h(0)=0. For 1\le p<\infty, let A^p(D) be the holomorphic functions with \|f\|_{A^p}^p=\pi^{-1}\iint_D|f(z)|^p\,dx\,dy<\infty. Then \|f\circ h\|_{A^p}\le\|f\|_{A^p}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Holomorphic means complex differentiable on the disk, a limited extension of real differentiability. The Bergman space and norm are explicitly defined by a nonnegative area integral, requiring no general function-space theory.

Proof 1: Integrate the circular-mean inequality radially — outline

Use the stated Littlewood circular-mean inequality: for 0<r<1, \int_0^{2\pi}|f(h(re^{i\theta}))|^p\,d\theta\le\int_0^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^p\,d\theta. Integrate the inequality with radial weight r/\pi for 0<r<1. Polar integration identifies the two sides with \|f\circ h\|_{A^p}^p and \|f\|_{A^p}^p, respectively. Taking the positive pth root proves the assertion.
