Statement

Let K be a field finitely generated over its prime subfield (\mathbb Q in characteristic zero or \mathbb F_p in characteristic p). Let X be a smooth geometrically connected scheme of finite type over K, and choose a geometric base point \bar x; geometrically connected means that X remains connected after extension to an algebraic closure of K. The structure map induces

\pi_1^{\mathrm{\acute et}}(X,\bar x)^{\mathrm{ab}}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{sep}}/K)^{\mathrm{ab}}.

Here K^{\mathrm{sep}} is a separable closure of K. Let N be its kernel. Here the étale fundamental group is the profinite automorphism group of the fiber functor on finite étale covers, and abelianization is quotient by the closure of the commutator subgroup. If \operatorname{char}K=0, then N is finite. If \operatorname{char}K=p>0, then the maximal prime-to-p quotient of N is finite; this is the inverse limit of its finite continuous quotients whose orders are coprime to p.

Proof 1: Generalized Jacobians and finite-field specialization (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (§II Theorem 1 and proof, PDF pp.11–17; Theorems 1(bis), 1(ter) in the reduction.).

Apply Theorem 1 with S=\operatorname{Spec}K. The proof allows passage to a finite extension to obtain a rational point and an elementary-fibration tower; it does not assume X(K) is nonempty. In characteristic p, retain precisely the prime-to-p quotient. In characteristic zero the two distinct residue characteristics in Theorem 1(bis) control every prime component.
