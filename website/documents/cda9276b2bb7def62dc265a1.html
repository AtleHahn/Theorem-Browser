Statement

Let B=(B_t)_{t\ge0} be a real standard Brownian motion on a filtered probability space, and let (\mathcal G_t) be its usual augmented natural filtration (the right-continuous completion of the information generated by B up to time t). Put \mathcal G_\infty=\sigma(\bigcup_{t\ge0}\mathcal G_t). If X is a real \mathcal G_\infty-measurable random variable with \mathbb E[X^2]<\infty, there exists a \mathcal G_t-predictable process C with \mathbb E\int_0^\infty C_s^2\,ds<\infty such that

X=\mathbb E[X]+\int_0^\infty C_s\,dB_s\quad\text{almost surely}.

The integral is the square-integrable Itô stochastic integral, with the infinite-horizon integral interpreted as the L^2 limit of the finite-horizon integrals. For every t\ge0, the same process satisfies

\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal G_t]=\mathbb E[X]+\int_0^t C_s\,dB_s\quad\text{almost surely}.

Predictable means measurable for the sigma-algebra generated by adapted left-continuous processes. Brownian motion has continuous paths, starts at zero, and has independent Gaussian increments of mean zero and variance equal to the time increment.
