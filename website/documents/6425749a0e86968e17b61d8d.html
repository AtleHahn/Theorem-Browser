Statement

Let N\ge1 be an integer. For nonempty subsets Q_1,\ldots,Q_N of a real vector space, \operatorname{conv}(\sum_nQ_n)=\sum_n\operatorname{conv}Q_n, where \operatorname{conv}Q is the set of finite convex combinations of points of Q.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra and real analysis supply convex combinations, suprema and infima. The finite-support variance is a weighted sum of squared distances, and all other set operations are explicitly defined. These are limited extensions of familiar Euclidean convexity.

Proof 1: Induct from the binary hull identity — outline

Minkowski addition behaves well with respect to taking convex hulls. Specifically, for all subsets A,B of a real vector space, the convex hull of their Minkowski sum is the Minkowski sum of their convex hulls. That is,

\operatorname{conv}(A+B)=(\operatorname{conv}A)+(\operatorname{conv}B).

And by induction it follows that

\operatorname{conv}\sum_{n=1}^NQ_n=\sum_{n=1}^N\operatorname{conv}Q_n

for any positive integer N and non-empty subsets Q_n, 1\leq n\leq N. Here A+B=\{a+b:a\in A,\ b\in B\}, and the finite sum is defined recursively. The two-set identity is used as a prerequisite.
