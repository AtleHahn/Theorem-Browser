Statement

Let f(t,x) be smooth and complex-valued near (0,0)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^n. Suppose \partial_t^j f(0,0)=0 for 0\le j<k and \partial_t^k f(0,0)\ne0, where k\ge1. Assuming the smooth division theorem for this f, there are smooth functions c(t,x) and a_0(x),\ldots,a_{k-1}(x) near the origin, with c(0,0)\ne0, such that f(t,x)=c(t,x)\left(t^k+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}a_j(x)t^j\right). The assumed division theorem says each smooth g can be written g=qf+\sum_{j<k}t^jr_j(x) with smooth q,r_j.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves smooth functions of real variables; complex-valued functions; partial derivatives; polynomial factorization. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Divide t to the kth power and invert the quotient — outline

The preparation form is the special case of the “division with remainder” form where g is t^k.

The source gives this implication without the quotient-inversion calculation; the smooth division theorem is assumed here.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the locally stated division identity to t^k, differentiate k times at the origin to see the quotient is nonzero, and invert it smoothly nearby. Undergraduate multivariable calculus suffices to fill in these steps; no preparation-theorem machinery is needed.
