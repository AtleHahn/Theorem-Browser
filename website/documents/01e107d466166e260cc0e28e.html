Statement

Let H_n denote the nth physicists’ Hermite polynomial. For every integer n\ge1 and real x, H_n(x)^2>H_{n-1}(x)H_{n+1}(x).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Undergraduate algebra and analysis supply the objects. Hermite polynomials have a single defining recurrence, Jordan products and graph-end conditions are explicitly specified, and well-quasi-order is defined by a sequence comparison. These concrete additions do not require their advanced proof theories.

Proof 1: Positive difference from the Hermite sum-of-squares identity — outline

Use the source-stated Hermite identity and the defining normalization H_0(x)=1. The source gives the positivity reduction H_n(x)^2-H_{n-1}(x)H_{n+1}(x)=(n-1)!\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{2^{n-i}}{i!}H_i(x)^2>0. This yields the strict inequality.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the supplied sum of squares and its positive constant-polynomial term. The inequality argument is elementary; identifying the physicists’ Hermite family is a small extension of undergraduate polynomial calculus, with no orthogonal-function theory required.
