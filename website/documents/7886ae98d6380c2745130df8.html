Direct construction of a separator hierarchy takes n log n time

Statement

Suppose an algorithm, on each current planar subgraph with m vertices, spends O(m) steps finding a vertex partition A,S,B with no edges between A and B and |A|,|B|\le2m/3. Repeatedly apply it to the induced subgraphs on A and B, stopping at bounded-size subgraphs. This top-down separator hierarchy on an n-vertex input takes O(n\log n) steps. The notation O(f(n)) bounds a quantity by a constant multiple of f(n) for all sufficiently large n.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The graph partition and asymptotic bound are explicitly defined. Geometric shrinking and finite work counts require elementary logarithms.

Proof 1: Direct construction of a separator hierarchy takes n log n time (outline)

Each recursive child contains at most a fixed fraction, here 2/3, of its parent’s vertices, so the hierarchy has O(\log n) levels. At a fixed level the remaining vertex sets are disjoint and their sizes sum to at most n. Linear-time separator work therefore totals O(n) at each level and O(n\log n) overall.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Count logarithmically many geometric-shrinkage levels and at most n vertices per level. Big-O is defined explicitly, so school logarithms and finite counting suffice for this operation-count argument.
