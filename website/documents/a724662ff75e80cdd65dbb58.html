Statement

Let n\ge1 be an integer, and let m\le a_i\le M be real numbers and p_i\ge0 with \sum_{i=1}^np_i=1. Put \mu=\sum_i p_i a_i and \sigma^2=\sum_i p_i a_i^2-\mu^2. Then \sigma^2\le(M-\mu)(\mu-m).

Level
High school only
Level justification

All probability expressions are explicitly finite weighted sums; only real algebra and nonnegativity are used.

Proof 1: Expand the nonnegative endpoint product

In this proof A takes the listed values a_i with weights p_i, and \mathbb E[h(A)]=\sum_i p_i h(a_i).

Since {\displaystyle m\leq A\leq M},

{\displaystyle 0\leq \mathbb {E} [(M-A)(A-m)]=-\mathbb {E} [A^{2}]-mM+(m+M)\mu }.

Thus,

{\displaystyle \sigma ^{2}=\mathbb {E} [A^{2}]-\mu ^{2}\leq -mM+(m+M)\mu -\mu ^{2}=(M-\mu )(\mu -m)}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Each endpoint product (M-a_i)(a_i-m) is nonnegative. Take its finite weighted average, expand it, and subtract mu^2 to obtain the factored upper bound. Expectation is explicitly defined as a finite sum, so elementary algebra and nonnegative weights suffice.
