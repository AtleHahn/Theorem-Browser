Statement

Let n\ge1 and let \gamma satisfy \gamma\ge1/2 if n=1, \gamma>0 if n=2, or \gamma\ge0 if n\ge3. Let L_{\gamma,n} be a constant valid in the Lieb–Thirring bound \operatorname{tr}(-\Delta+V)_-^\gamma\le L_{\gamma,n}\int_{\mathbb R^n}V_-^{\gamma+n/2}. Here the trace is the sum of the \gamma-powers of the magnitudes of negative eigenvalues, with the zeroth power counting negative eigenvalues, and V_- =\max(-V,0). Then L_{\gamma,n}\ge L_{\gamma,n}^{\rm cl}=(4\pi)^{-n/2}\frac{\Gamma(\gamma+1)}{\Gamma(\gamma+1+n/2)}.

Proof 1: Scale the potential and pass to the Weyl limit

Fix an admissible nonzero negative smooth compactly supported potential V and write I=\int V_-^{\gamma+n/2}, so 0<I<\infty. Apply the bound to \beta V and divide by \beta^{\gamma+n/2}: \beta^{-(\gamma+n/2)}\operatorname{tr}(-\Delta+\beta V)_-^\gamma\le L_{\gamma,n}I. The semiclassical approximation becomes exact in the limit of large coupling, that is, for potentials \beta V the Weyl asymptotics

\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\frac{1}{\beta^{\gamma+n/2}} \operatorname{tr}(-\Delta+\beta V)_-^\gamma =L_{\gamma,n}^{\rm cl}\int_{\mathbb R^n}V(x)_-^{\gamma+n/2}\,d^n x

hold. We use this asymptotic formula as a prerequisite. Passing to the limit and cancelling the positive number I gives L_{\gamma,n}^{\rm cl}\le L_{\gamma,n}.
