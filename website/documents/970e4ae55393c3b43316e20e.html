Statement

Let a<b and f,g,h:[a,b]\to\mathbb R be continuous and differentiable inside. Set D(x)=\det\begin{pmatrix}f(x)&g(x)&h(x)\\f(a)&g(a)&h(a)\\f(b)&g(b)&h(b)\end{pmatrix}. Some c\in(a,b) satisfies D'(c)=\det\begin{pmatrix}f'(c)&g'(c)&h'(c)\\f(a)&g(a)&h(a)\\f(b)&g(b)&h(b)\end{pmatrix}=0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Three-by-three determinants; Continuous differentiable real functions. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Differentiate a determinant with equal endpoint rows

Notice that

{\displaystyle D'(x)={\begin{vmatrix}f'(x)&g'(x)&h'(x)\\f(a)&g(a)&h(a)\\f(b)&g(b)&h(b)\end{vmatrix}}}

The proof of the generalization is quite simple: each of {\displaystyle D(a)} and {\displaystyle D(b)} are determinants with two identical rows, hence {\displaystyle D(a)=D(b)=0}. Then Rolle's theorem implies that there exists {\displaystyle c\in (a,b)} such that {\displaystyle D'(c)=0}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Multilinearity makes D differentiable by differentiating its first row. Both endpoint determinants have repeated rows, so Rolle gives the desired zero derivative. Standard calculus and linear algebra suffice.
