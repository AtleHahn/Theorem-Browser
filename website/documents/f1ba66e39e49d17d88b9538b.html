Statement

There exists C>0 such that every nonzero u\in H^2(\mathbb R^2) satisfies

\|u\|_\infty\le C\|u\|_{H^1}\left(1+\sqrt{\log\left(1+\frac{\|\Delta u\|_2}{\|u\|_{H^1}}\right)}\right),\qquad \Delta u=\partial_{11}u+\partial_{22}u.

Here H^k denotes the real-valued Sobolev space with square-integrable weak derivatives through order k, with its usual norm; L^\infty uses the essential supremum.

Proof 1: Whole-plane Hessian identity and norm substitution — outline

Noticing that, for any u\in H^2(\mathbb R^2), there holds

\int_{\mathbb R^2}\bigl((\partial_{11}u)^2+2(\partial_{12}u)^2+(\partial_{22}u)^2\bigr) =\int_{\mathbb R^2}(\Delta u)^2.

Consequently \|u\|_{H^2}\le C(\|u\|_{H^1}+\|\Delta u\|_2). Apply the Brezis–Gallouët inequality in the plane, as an external prerequisite,

\|u\|_\infty\le C\|u\|_{H^1}\left(1+\sqrt{\log\left(1+\frac{\|u\|_{H^2}}{\|u\|_{H^1}}\right)}\right).

Substitute the preceding norm estimate and absorb fixed constants into C. One thus deduces the asserted Laplacian form from the Brezis–Gallouët inequality.
