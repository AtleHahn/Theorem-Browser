Statement

Let ABC be a nondegenerate Euclidean triangle with sides a,b,c, semiperimeter s, area \Delta, circumradius R and inradius r. Its three vertex circles have radii s-a,s-b,s-c. The signed curvatures of the two circles tangent to these three are \frac{4R+r\pm2s}{\Delta}. An enclosing circle is assigned negative reciprocal radius; other finite circles positive reciprocal radius, and a line zero.

Proof 1: Explicit substitution — outline

Apply the Descartes fourth-curvature formula

k_4=k_1+k_2+k_3\pm2\sqrt{k_1k_2+k_2k_3+k_3k_1}

to the three vertex-circle curvatures k_1=1/(s-a),k_2=1/(s-b),k_3=1/(s-c). In terms of the triangle’s area, inradius and circumradius, the two expressions are (4R+r+2s)/\Delta and (4R+r-2s)/\Delta.

The standard triangle identities used in this conversion are Heron’s formula \Delta^2=s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c), \Delta=rs, and abc=4R\Delta.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Insert reciprocal vertex-circle radii into the explicit Descartes formula and simplify with the three stated triangle area identities. Signed curvature is defined as reciprocal radius, so school algebra and circle geometry suffice.
