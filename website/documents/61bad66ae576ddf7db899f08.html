Statement

Let X be a locally compact Hausdorff space with a Borel measure \mu finite on compact sets. Assume outer regularity,

\mu(E)=\inf\{\mu(U):E\subseteq U,\ U\text{ open}\}

for Borel E, and inner regularity by compact sets for every open Borel set and every Borel set of finite measure:

\mu(E)=\sup\{\mu(K):K\subseteq E,\ K\text{ compact}\}.

For every real measurable function f with \int_X|f|\,d\mu<\infty and every \varepsilon>0, there exist functions u:X\to[-\infty,\infty) and v:X\to(-\infty,\infty] such that u\le f\le v, u is upper semicontinuous and bounded above, v is lower semicontinuous and bounded below, and

\int_X(v-u)\,d\mu<\varepsilon.

Upper semicontinuity means \{u<t\} is open for each real t; lower semicontinuity means \{v>t\} is open. The measure may be completed when interpreting measurable f.
