Statement

Let A=(a_{ij})\in\mathbb C^{n\times n}, n\ge1, and put R_i=\sum_{j\ne i}|a_{ij}| and D_i=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z-a_{ii}|\le R_i\}. Every eigenvalue of A lies in \bigcup_iD_i.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Complex matrices; Eigenvalues and eigenvectors; Absolute values; Gershgorin disks. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Choose the largest eigenvector coordinate

Let \lambda be an eigenvalue of A with corresponding eigenvector x=(x_j). Find i such that the element of x with the largest absolute value is x_i. Since x\ne0, we have x_i\ne0. Since Ax=\lambda x, in particular we take the ith component of that equation to get:

\sum_j a_{ij}x_j=\lambda x_i.

Taking a_{ii}x_i to the other side:

\sum_{j\ne i}a_{ij}x_j=(\lambda-a_{ii})x_i.

Therefore, applying the triangle inequality and recalling that \frac{|x_j|}{|x_i|}\le1 based on how we picked i,

\begin{aligned} |\lambda-a_{ii}| &=\left|\sum_{j\ne i}\frac{a_{ij}x_j}{x_i}\right| \le\sum_{j\ne i}\left|\frac{a_{ij}x_j}{x_i}\right|\\ &=\sum_{j\ne i}|a_{ij}|\frac{|x_j|}{|x_i|} \le\sum_{j\ne i}|a_{ij}|=R_i. \end{aligned}
