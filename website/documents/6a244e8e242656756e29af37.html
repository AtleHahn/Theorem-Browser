Statement

Let n\ge1 and let f_1,\ldots,f_n:[-1,1]^n\to\mathbb R be continuous functions. For each i\in\{1,\ldots,n\} assume f_i(x)\le0 at every point of the face x_i=-1 and f_i(x)\ge0 at every point of the opposite face x_i=1. Then there exists x\in[-1,1]^n such that f_i(x)=0 for every i.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Finite-dimensional continuity and inequalities suffice; fixed-point or degree theory is not needed to understand the statement.

Proof 1: Convex projection and Brouwer fixed points (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorem7 and Corollary6, Section5; Brouwer dependency in Section3).

Set every R_i=1 in Corollary6. Projection onto the cube is coordinatewise clipping to [-1,1]. At a boundary point, every outward normal is a nonnegative combination of the outward normals of its incident faces, so the weak sign hypotheses imply the required scalar-product inequality even at corners; the f_k in the two-face example is f_j.

In the supporting Theorem2, choose the positive separation constant for both boundary homotopies \lambda x-T(\lambda x) and x-\lambda T(x) simultaneously, and choose the bump support inside both B(0,\delta/2) and the interior of B_R. Polynomial approximation can be obtained by extending T to a containing cube by radial projection and taking multivariate Bernstein polynomials. The compact-support Stokes step is integration by parts in Euclidean coordinates.
