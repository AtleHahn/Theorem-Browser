Proof 1: Substitute n=2 in the cosine sum

We use the binomial multiple-angle formula, for real x and nonnegative integers n,

\cos nx=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk(\cos x)^k(\sin x)^{n-k} \cos\frac{(n-k)\pi}{2}.

The final trigonometric function equals one or minus one or zero, thus removing half the entries in the sum. Here is the concrete instance for n=2, using \sin^2x=1-\cos^2x:

\cos 2x=(\cos x)^2+((\cos x)^2-1)=2(\cos x)^2-1.
