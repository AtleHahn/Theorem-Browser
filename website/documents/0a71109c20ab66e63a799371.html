Statement

Let G=(V,E) be a finite simple graph. Its incidence order has elements V\sqcup E, with v<e exactly when v is an endpoint of e; distinct vertices and distinct edges are incomparable. A pair of total orders realizes this incidence order if a comparison holds in both exactly when it belongs to the incidence order. If a pair of total orders realizes the incidence order, then some linear ordering of the vertices has every edge joining consecutive vertices; in particular, G is a subgraph of a path. Conversely, for any vertex ordering in which all edges join consecutive vertices, the ordering and its reverse extend to a pair realizing the incidence order by inserting each edge immediately after its later endpoint.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The incidence order and realization by two total orders are explicit finite comparisons. Consecutive vertices and inserting edges in lists require elementary combinatorics.

Proof 1: Reverse vertex orders and insert edges — outline

Necessary ordering

When an incidence poset is realized by two total orders, these orders (when restricted to the graph's vertices) are the reverse of each other. Any other two orders would have an intersection that includes an order relation between two vertices, which is not allowed for incidence posets.

Construction

For these two orders on the vertices, an edge between consecutive vertices can be included in the ordering by placing it immediately following the later of the two edge endpoints, but no other edges can be included. Thus the graph is a path or a subgraph of a path, and the stated insertion gives the required pair of orders.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Reverse the two vertex orders to avoid vertex comparisons and insert each allowed edge after its later endpoint. Elementary finite orders and graph incidence suffice.
