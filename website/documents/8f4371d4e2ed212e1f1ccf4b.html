Statement

A graphon is a symmetric measurable function W:[0,1]^2\to[0,1]. For a finite simple graph F with vertices 1,\ldots,q, define

t(F,W)=\int_{[0,1]^q}\prod_{ij\in E(F)}W(x_i,x_j)\,dx_1\cdots dx_q.

For a real integrable measurable W on [0,1]^2, put \|W\|_\square=\sup_{S,T}|\int_{S\times T}W|, with measurable S,T\subseteq[0,1]. For any graphons W,U, |t(K_3,W)-t(K_3,U)|\leq3\|W-U\|_\square.

Proof 1: Expand three factors and fix the third variable

Cut-norm lemma

For a real integrable measurable W on [0,1]^2, put \|W\|_\square=\sup_{S,T}|\int_{S\times T}W|, with measurable S,T\subseteq[0,1].

The cut norm also equals the supremum over measurable tests 0\leq u,v\leq1 of |\int W(x,y)u(x)v(y)\,dx\,dy|.

Indicator functions u=\mathbf1_S, v=\mathbf1_T show that the supremum over sets is no larger than the supremum over measurable [0,1]-valued functions. In the reverse direction, the functional is bilinear in u,v, and its extremal values over these unit intervals are attained for functions taking values zero or one. Such functions are indicators, yielding the reverse inequality.

Triangle calculation

In the case F=K_3, we observe that

\begin{aligned} t(K_3,W)-t(K_3,U) &=\int_{[0,1]^3}\bigl(W(x,y)W(x,z)W(y,z)-U(x,y)U(x,z)U(y,z)\bigr)\,dx\,dy\,dz\\ &=\int_{[0,1]^3}(W-U)(x,y)W(x,z)W(y,z)\,dx\,dy\,dz\\ &\quad+\int_{[0,1]^3}U(x,y)(W-U)(x,z)W(y,z)\,dx\,dy\,dz\\ &\quad+\int_{[0,1]^3}U(x,y)U(x,z)(W-U)(y,z)\,dx\,dy\,dz. \end{aligned}

By the cut-norm lemma, for almost every fixed z,

\left|\int_{[0,1]^2}(W-U)(x,y)W(x,z)W(y,z)\,dx\,dy\right| \le\|W-U\|_\square.

Therefore, when integrating over all z\in[0,1], we get

\left|\int_{[0,1]^3}(W-U)(x,y)W(x,z)W(y,z)\,dx\,dy\,dz\right| \le\|W-U\|_\square.

Using this bound on each of the three summands, we get that the absolute value of the whole sum is bounded by 3\|W-U\|_\square. Fubini's theorem supplies the almost-everywhere interpretation of the fixed-variable integrals.
