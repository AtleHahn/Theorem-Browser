Statement

Let f(t)=P(\sin t,\cos t)/Q(\sin t,\cos t) be a rational expression with real polynomial numerator and denominator. The following substitutions transform \int f(t)\,dt into the integral of a rational function of the new variable, on intervals where the expression and change of variable are defined:

  1. If f(-t)=-f(t), use u=\cos t.
  2. If f(\pi-t)=-f(t), use u=\sin t.
  3. If f(\pi+t)=f(t), use u=\tan t.
  4. If any two of these three symmetry identities hold, use u=\cos(2t).
  5. In all other cases, use u=\tan(t/2).

The symmetry conditions are identities wherever both sides are defined. In terms of the differential expression \omega=f(t)\,dt, the first three conditions say that \omega is invariant under t\mapsto-t, t\mapsto\pi-t, and t\mapsto\pi+t, respectively; the minus signs in the first two conditions account for the change in dt.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Calculus substitution and elementary trigonometric symmetry suffice. Differential-expression invariance is explicitly translated into signed function identities, so differential geometry is unnecessary.
