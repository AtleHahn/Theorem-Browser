Statement

Work in any abelian category, or in the category of groups. Consider two sequences

A\xrightarrow{f}B\xrightarrow{g}C\xrightarrow{h}D\xrightarrow{j}E, \qquad A'\xrightarrow{r}B'\xrightarrow{s}C'\xrightarrow{t}D'\xrightarrow{u}E',

with vertical morphisms l:A\to A', m:B\to B', n:C\to C', p:D\to D', and q:E\to E'. Suppose the rows are exact at their three intermediate objects and the four squares commute:

mf=rl,\qquad ng=sm,\qquad ph=tn,\qquad qj=up.

If m and p are isomorphisms, l is an epimorphism and q is a monomorphism, then n is an isomorphism.

Exactness means that the image of each incoming morphism equals the kernel of the outgoing morphism. A monomorphism is left-cancellable, an epimorphism right-cancellable, and an isomorphism has a two-sided inverse.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Arbitrary abelian-category structure and categorical exactness require several interdependent concepts beyond standard undergraduate algebra; locally defining cancellation does not remove that burden.

Proof 1: Categorical four lemmas and the multiplicative group chase (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Lemma12.5.20, with Lemmas12.5.19 and12.5.17).

The four-lemma proof uses the snake lemma directly, with no smallness restriction. For groups, use the multiplicative chase: if n(c)=1, write c=g(b), then m(b)=r(a\prime); lift a\prime=l(a) to obtain b=f(a) and c=1. For c\prime\in C\prime, lift t(c\prime) through p; injectivity of q and exactness then give c\in C with t(c\prime)=tn(c). Write c\prime n(c)^{-1}=s(b\prime) and lift b\prime=m(b); then c\prime=n(g(b)c).
