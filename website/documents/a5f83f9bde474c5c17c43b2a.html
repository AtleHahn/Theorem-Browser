Statement

Let \Omega\subset\mathbb R^n be a bounded Lipschitz domain. Let H_0^1(\Omega) denote the Sobolev space of real functions with square-integrable weak first derivatives and zero boundary trace. Put B[u,v]=\int_\Omega\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\,dx, and use the norm \|u\|_{H^1}^2=\|u\|_{L^2}^2+\|\nabla u\|_{L^2}^2. The bilinear form B is continuous on H_0^1(\Omega)\times H_0^1(\Omega): |B[u,v]|\le\|u\|_{H^1}\|v\|_{H^1}.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The underlying infinite-dimensional Sobolev space and weak derivatives are beyond calculus and finite-dimensional linear algebra.

Proof 1: Bound the gradient integral by Cauchy–Schwarz

Apply Cauchy–Schwarz to the gradient integral: |B[u,v]|\le\|\nabla u\|_{L^2}\|\nabla v\|_{L^2}\le\|u\|_{H^1}\|v\|_{H^1}. This is the source’s indicated continuity argument, with the gradient terms controlled by the defining Sobolev norm.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The inequality is short but applies to Sobolev functions with weak derivatives and zero trace. Understanding these objects and the L2 gradient pairing requires measure theory and Sobolev-space background.
