Statement

Let R be a commutative ring with identity and A\in R^{n\times n}, n\ge1. If d=\det A is a unit of R, then A is invertible and A^{-1}=d^{-1}\operatorname{adj}(A), where the adjugate is the transpose of the cofactor matrix.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory ring/group theory and linear algebra supply polynomial coefficients, ideals, determinants, bases and matrix products. Elementary transvections are explicitly given elementary row-operation matrices, and division ring means every nonzero element has an inverse. These direct definitions suffice without specialist theory.

Proof 1: Rescale the adjugate identities

Use the adjugate identities A\operatorname{adj}(A)=\operatorname{adj}(A)A=dI. Multiplying by the unit d^{-1} gives the inverse formula.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Multiply the two adjugate identities by the inverse unit determinant. Standard matrix algebra and introductory ring units suffice.
