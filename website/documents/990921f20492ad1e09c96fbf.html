Statement

Let A,B,C be a nondegenerate Euclidean triangle, with D,E,F on the side lines BC,CA,AB respectively and none at a vertex. Suppose the directed ratios satisfy \dfrac{AF}{FB}\dfrac{BD}{DC}\dfrac{CE}{EA}=1. Assume AD and BE meet in a finite point O lying on none of the side lines, and that CO meets AB in a finite point F^{\prime}. Then F=F^{\prime}, so AD,BE,CF concur at O.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All incidence hypotheses and ratio equations are elementary real plane geometry.

Proof 1: Compare the ratio with the third-line intersection

Local forward implication

Let A,B,C be noncollinear points in the Euclidean plane and let O lie on none of the three side lines. Suppose AO,BO,CO meet the opposite side lines at finite points D,E,F, respectively. Ratios of collinear segments are directed: AF/FB is positive when F lies between A,B and negative otherwise.

The forward identity \frac{AF}{FB}\frac{BD}{DC}\frac{CE}{EA}=1 can be proven using Menelaus's theorem. From the transversal BOE of triangle △ACF,

{\displaystyle {\frac {\overline {AB}}{\overline {BF}}}\cdot {\frac {\overline {FO}}{\overline {OC}}}\cdot {\frac {\overline {CE}}{\overline {EA}}}=-1}

and from the transversal AOD of triangle △BCF,

{\displaystyle {\frac {\overline {BA}}{\overline {AF}}}\cdot {\frac {\overline {FO}}{\overline {OC}}}\cdot {\frac {\overline {CD}}{\overline {DB}}}=-1.}

The forward identity follows by dividing these two equations.

Compare the two ratios

The converse follows as a corollary of the local forward implication. Let D, E, F be given on the lines BC, AC, AB so that the equation holds. By hypothesis, AD and BE meet at O and F' is the finite point where CO crosses AB. Then by the local forward implication, the equation also holds for D, E, F'. Comparing the two,

{\displaystyle {\frac {\overline {AF}}{\overline {FB}}}={\frac {\overline {AF'}}{\overline {F'B}}}}

But at most one point can cut a segment in a given ratio so F = F’.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The local forward result follows by dividing two directed Menelaus products. Comparing it at F and F prime gives the same directed division ratio, which determines a point uniquely on the line. All prerequisites are Euclidean triangle geometry and algebra.
