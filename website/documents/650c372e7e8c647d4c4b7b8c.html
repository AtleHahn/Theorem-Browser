Statement

Let n\geq1 and let L_1,\ldots,L_n be linearly independent linear forms in n variables with algebraic complex coefficients. Thus L_i(x)=\sum_{j=1}^n a_{ij}x_j, each a_{ij} is a root of a nonzero polynomial with rational coefficients, and \det(a_{ij})\ne0. For every real \varepsilon>0, all nonzero integer vectors x\in\mathbb Z^n satisfying

|L_1(x)\cdots L_n(x)|<\|x\|^{-\varepsilon}

lie in a union of finitely many proper \mathbb Q-linear subspaces of \mathbb Q^n. Here |\cdot| is complex modulus and \|x\|=(\sum_{j=1}^n x_j^2)^{1/2} is the Euclidean norm. The finite collection of subspaces may depend on the forms and on \varepsilon.

Level justification

Undergraduate linear algebra, elementary complex numbers and the explicit algebraic-number definition suffice. Projective heights and number-field valuations are not prerequisites for this formulation.
