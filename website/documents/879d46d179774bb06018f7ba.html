Statement

Let G be a smooth connected algebraic group over the finite field F_q, and let σ be its q-power Frobenius. The Lang morphism L:G→G, L(x)=x^{-1}σ(x), is surjective; equivalently every a∈G(\overline{\mathbf F_q}) has this form for some geometric point x.

Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Smooth connected algebraic groups; Finite fields; Frobenius morphisms; Geometric points. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Intersect dense open twisted-conjugacy images

Define:

{\displaystyle f_{a}:G\to G,\quad f_{a}(x)=x^{-1}a\sigma (x).}

Work over an algebraic closure of \mathbf F_q. Let h(x,y,z)=xyz. The differential of the Frobenius \sigma vanishes, so

(df_a)_e =dh_{(e,a,e)}\circ(-1,0,0).

Identifying the tangent space at a with the tangent space at the identity by right translation by a^{-1}, this is the negative identity. It follows that (df_a)_e is bijective.

Since {\displaystyle f_{a}(bx)=f_{f_{a}(b)}(x)}, we also see that {\displaystyle (df_{a})_{b}} is bijective for any b. Let X be the closure of the image of {\displaystyle f_{1}}. The smooth points of X form an open dense subset; thus, there is some b in G such that {\displaystyle f_{1}(b)} is a smooth point of X. The differential of f_1 factors through the tangent space to X at f_1(b) and is surjective onto the tangent space to G there. Since the tangent space to X at {\displaystyle f_{1}(b)} and the tangent space to G at b have the same dimension, it follows that X and G have the same dimension, since G is smooth. A connected smooth algebraic group is irreducible, so X=G. By constructibility of the image of a morphism, the image of {\displaystyle f_{1}} then contains an open dense subset U of G. Now, given an arbitrary element a in G, by the same reasoning, the image of {\displaystyle f_{a}} contains an open dense subset V of G. The intersection {\displaystyle U\cap V} is then nonempty. Choose

z=x^{-1}\sigma(x)=y^{-1}a\sigma(y)\in U\cap V.

Then

a=yx^{-1}\sigma(x)\sigma(y)^{-1} =(xy^{-1})^{-1}\sigma(xy^{-1}),

so a is in the image of f_1.
