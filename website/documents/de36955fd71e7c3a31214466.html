Statement

For a topological group G, a matrix coefficient means a function g\mapsto L(\pi(g)), where \pi:G\to GL(V) is a continuous representation on a finite-dimensional complex vector space and L is a linear functional on \operatorname{End}(V). For a compact Hausdorff G with normalized Haar measure, the matrix coefficients are dense in L^2(G).

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Proof 1: Matrix coefficients are dense in L2 of a compact group — outline

The Peter–Weyl uniform density theorem asserts that the set of matrix coefficients of G is dense in the space of continuous complex functions C(G) on G, equipped with the uniform norm. A corollary of this result is that the matrix coefficients of G are dense in L^2(G). Here one uses that continuous functions are dense in L^2(G) and that, for normalized Haar measure, the L^2 norm is bounded by the uniform norm.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Combine Peter-Weyl uniform approximation with density of continuous functions in Haar L2. Haar measure, L2 equivalence classes and compact-group representation theory together require advanced analysis.
