Statement

A graphon is a symmetric measurable function W:[0,1]^2\to[0,1]. For a finite simple graph F with vertices 1,\ldots,q, define

t(F,W)=\int_{[0,1]^q}\prod_{ij\in E(F)}W(x_i,x_j)\,dx_1\cdots dx_q.

For two graphons let \delta_\square(W,U)=\inf_\phi\|W-U^\phi\|_\square, where U^\phi(x,y)=U(\phi(x),\phi(y)) and \phi runs over measure-preserving bijections of [0,1]. For a real integrable measurable W on [0,1]^2, put \|W\|_\square=\sup_{S,T}|\int_{S\times T}W|, with measurable S,T\subseteq[0,1]. Then |t(F,W)-t(F,U)|\leq|E(F)|\delta_\square(W,U).

Proof 1: Telescope edge factors and minimize over relabelings — outline

It suffices to prove

{\displaystyle |t(F,W)-t(F,U)|\leq |E(F)|\|W-U\|_{\square }.}

Indeed, by considering the above, with the right hand side expression having a factor {\displaystyle \|W-U^{\phi }\|_{\Box }} instead of {\displaystyle \|W-U\|_{\Box }}, and using invariance of homomorphism densities under measure-preserving relabeling and taking the infimum over all measure-preserving bijections {\displaystyle \phi }, we obtain the desired result.

Cut-norm lemma

Step 1: Reformulation. We prove a reformulation of the cut norm, which is by definition the left hand side of the following equality. The supremum in the right hand side is taken among measurable functions {\displaystyle u} and {\displaystyle v}:

{\displaystyle \sup _{S,T\subseteq [0,1]}\left|\int _{S\times T}W\right|=\sup _{u,v:[0,1]\rightarrow [0,1]}\left|\int _{[0,1]^{2}}W(x,y)u(x)v(y)dxdy\right|.}

Here's the reason for the above to hold: By taking {\displaystyle u=\mathbb {1} _{S}} and {\displaystyle v=\mathbb {1} _{T}}, we note that the left hand side is less than or equal than the right hand side. The right hand side is less than or equal than the left hand side by bilinearity of the integrand in {\displaystyle u,v}, and by the fact that the extrema are attained for {\displaystyle u,v} taking values at {\displaystyle 0} or {\displaystyle 1}.

Edge-by-edge bound

Step 2: Proof for {\displaystyle F=K_{3}}. In the case that {\displaystyle F=K_{3}}, we observe that

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}t(K_{3},W)-t(K_{3},U)&=\int _{[0,1]^{3}}(W(x,y)W(x,z)W(y,z)-U(x,y)U(x,z)U(y,z))dxdydz\\&=\int _{[0,1]^{3}}(W-U)(x,y)W(x,z)W(y,z)dxdydz\\&\qquad +\int _{[0,1]^{3}}U(x,y)(W-U)(x,z)W(y,z)dxdydz\\&\qquad +\int _{[0,1]^{3}}U(x,y)U(x,z)(W-U)(y,z)dxdydz.\end{aligned}}}

By Step 1, we have that for almost every fixed {\displaystyle z} that

{\displaystyle \left|\int _{[0,1]^{2}}(W-U)(x,y)W(x,z)W(y,z)dxdy\right|\leq \|W-U\|_{\square }}

Therefore, when integrating over all {\displaystyle z\in [0,1]} we get that

{\displaystyle \left|\int _{[0,1]^{3}}(W-U)(x,y)W(x,z)W(y,z)dxdydz\right|\leq \|W-U\|_{\square }}

Using this bound on each of the three summands, we get that the whole sum is bounded by {\displaystyle 3\|W-U\|_{\square }}. Step 3: General case. For a general graph {\displaystyle F}, we need the following lemma to make everything more convenient:

Lemma.

The following expression holds:

{\displaystyle a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{n}-b_{1}b_{2}\cdots b_{n}=(a_{1}-b_{1})a_{2}\cdots a_{n}+b_{1}(a_{2}-b_{2})\cdots a_{n}+\cdots +b_{1}b_{2}\cdots b_{n-1}(a_{n}-b_{n}).}

The above lemma follows from a straightforward expansion of the right hand side. Then, by the triangle inequality of norm, we have the following

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\left|t(F,W)-t(F,U)\right|&=\left|\int \left(\prod _{u_{i}v_{i}\in E(F)}W(u_{i},v_{i})-\prod _{u_{i}v_{i}\in E(F)}U(u_{i},v_{i})\right)\prod _{v\in V}dv\right|\\&\leq \sum _{i=1}^{|E(F)|}\left|\int \left(\ \prod _{j=1}^{i-1}U(u_{j},v_{j})(W(u_{i},v_{i})-U(u_{i},v_{i}))\prod _{k=i+1}^{|E(F)|}W(u_{k},v_{k})\right)\prod _{v\in V}dv\right|.\end{aligned}}}

All integrals in the last display are over one variable in [0,1] for each vertex; u_i,v_i denote the variables at the endpoints of edge i. Since F is simple, after fixing the other variables the remaining factors separate into a [0,1]-valued function of each endpoint, times a factor in [0,1] independent of both. By Fubini these slice statements hold almost everywhere.

Here, each absolute value term in the sum is bounded by the cut norm {\displaystyle \|W-U\|_{\square }} if we fix all the variables except for {\displaystyle u_{i}} and {\displaystyle v_{i}} for each {\displaystyle i}-th term, altogether implying that {\displaystyle |t(F,W)-t(F,U)|\leq |E(F)|\ \delta _{\square }(W,U)}. This finishes the proof.
