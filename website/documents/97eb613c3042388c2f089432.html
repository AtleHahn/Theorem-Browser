Statement

Let R=k[x,y,z_1,\ldots,z_h] with k a field. Let P,Q be relatively prime homogeneous forms in x,y of positive degrees m,n, with coefficients in k[z_1,\ldots,z_h]. Set I=(P,Q) and let D\in k[z_1,\ldots,z_h] be their homogeneous resultant. The (x,y)-primary component of I is I:D^\infty=\{t\in R: D^e t\in I\text{ for some }e\ge0\}.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Primary decomposition, saturation and parameter-dependent homogeneous resultants are interacting commutative-algebra constructions. Understanding the specified primary component requires more than a short undergraduate refresher. Proof 1: Invert the resultant to isolate the coordinate component — outline

Since P and Q are relatively prime, I has no primary component of height one. As I is generated by two elements, this implies that it is a complete intersection (more precisely, it defines an algebraic set, which is a complete intersection), and thus all primary components have height two. Therefore, the associated primes of I are exactly the prime ideals of height two that contain I.

It follows that {\displaystyle \langle x,y\rangle } is an associated prime of I.

Let {\displaystyle D\in k[z_{1},\ldots ,z_{h}]} be the homogeneous resultant in x, y of P and Q. As the greatest common divisor of P and Q is a constant, the resultant D is not zero, and resultant theory implies that I contains all products of D by a monomial in x, y of degree m + n – 1. As {\displaystyle D\not \in \langle x,y\rangle ,} all these monomials belong to the primary component whose radical is {\displaystyle \langle x,y\rangle .} This primary component contains P and Q, and the behavior of primary decompositions under localization shows that this primary component is

{\displaystyle \{t\in R\mid \exists e\ge0,\ D^{e}t\in I\}=I:D^{\infty}.}

The proof combines associated primes, height and unmixed complete intersections with homogeneous resultants, primary decomposition and localization. Understanding how inverting the resultant removes the other primary components requires several linked commutative-algebra theories, not a small addition to introductory ring theory.

Homogeneous resultant is nonzero for coprime binary forms and annihilates all degree-(m+n−1) monomials modulo (P,Q)
Kind:

Theorem

Treatment:

Cited without proof

Statement used:

For relatively prime homogeneous binary forms P,Q of degrees m,n, their nonzero resultant D satisfies D times every degree-(m+n-1) monomial in x,y belongs to (P,Q).

Application in proof:

After inverting D, force the radical to be (x,y).

Application level:

Beyond undergraduate level

Level justification:

This ideal-theoretic use of the binary resultant over a polynomial coefficient ring goes well beyond applying a scalar polynomial root criterion.

Treatment:

Statement used:

Localizing a primary decomposition at powers of D removes components whose radicals contain D; contraction of the localized ideal equals I:D^infinity.

Application in proof:

Isolate the (x,y)-primary component since D avoids (x,y) and every surviving prime contains x,y.

Application level:

Beyond undergraduate level

Level justification:

The application presupposes primary ideals, decomposition, localization and contraction.

Review notes:

Every surviving prime contains x,y by the monomial containment. Unmixed height two then forces it to equal (x,y), explaining why the saturation selects just this component.
