Statement

Let P(z)=\sum_{j=0}^n a_jz^j, n\ge1, a_n\ne0, and b=(a_0/a_n,\ldots,a_{n-1}/a_n). For a finite complex vector v, write \|v\|_p=(\sum_j|v_j|^p)^{1/p} for 1\le p<\infty and \|v\|_\infty=\max_j|v_j|. For conjugate exponents 1\le p,q\le\infty, with 1/p+1/q=1, put R_p(v)=\|(1,\|v\|_p)\|_q. Thus R_1(v)=\max(1,\|v\|_1) and R_\infty(v)=1+\|v\|_\infty. Every root \zeta satisfies |\zeta|\le R_p(b) for every 1\le p\le\infty. In particular, at p=2 this is |\zeta|\le\sqrt{1+\sum_{j<n}|a_j/a_n|^2}.

Proof 1: Apply Hölder’s inequality and sum a geometric progression

Dividing all coefficients by a_n\ne0 reduces to the monic case, with coefficients b_j=a_j/a_n. Let ζ be a root of the polynomial

{\displaystyle z^{n}+b_{n-1}z^{n-1}+\cdots +b_{1}z+b_{0};}

in order to prove the inequality |ζ| ≤ Rp we can assume, of course, |ζ| > 1, since R_p(b)\ge1. Writing the equation as

{\displaystyle -\zeta ^{n}=b_{n-1}\zeta ^{n-1}+\cdots +b_{1}\zeta +b_{0},}

and using the Hölder's inequality we find

{\displaystyle |\zeta |^{n}\leq \|b\|_{p}\left\|\left(\zeta ^{n-1},\ldots ,\zeta ,1\right)\right\|_{q}.}

Now, if p = 1, this is

{\displaystyle |\zeta |^{n}\leq \|b\|_{1}\max \left\{|\zeta |^{n-1},\ldots ,|\zeta |,1\right\}=\|b\|_{1}|\zeta |^{n-1},}

thus

{\displaystyle |\zeta |\leq \max\{1,\|b\|_{1}\}.}

In the case 1 < p ≤ ∞, taking into account the summation formula for a geometric progression, we have

{\displaystyle |\zeta |^{n}\leq \|b\|_{p}\left(|\zeta |^{q(n-1)}+\cdots +|\zeta |^{q}+1\right)^{\frac {1}{q}}=\|b\|_{p}\left({\frac {|\zeta |^{qn}-1}{|\zeta |^{q}-1}}\right)^{\frac {1}{q}}\leq \|b\|_{p}\left({\frac {|\zeta |^{qn}}{|\zeta |^{q}-1}}\right)^{\frac {1}{q}},}

thus

{\displaystyle |\zeta |^{nq}\leq \|b\|_{p}^{q}{\frac {|\zeta |^{qn}}{|\zeta |^{q}-1}}}

and simplifying,

{\displaystyle |\zeta |^{q}\leq 1+\|b\|_{p}^{q}.}

Therefore

{\displaystyle |\zeta |\leq \left\|\left(1,\|b\|_{p}\right)\right\|_{q}=R_{p}(b)}

holds, for all 1 ≤ p ≤ ∞.
