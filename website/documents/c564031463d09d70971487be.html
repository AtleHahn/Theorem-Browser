Statement

For a real m\times n matrix A and 1\le p,q\le\infty, write \|A\|_{p\to q}=\max_{\|x\|_p=1}\|Ax\|_q, using the usual finite-dimensional \ell_p norms. Then \|A\|_{p\to q}\le\|A\|_{\infty\to1} for all such p,q.

Proof 1: The infinity-to-one norm dominates the other induced norms

For what value of p and q is \|A\|_{p\to q} maximized? Since A is linear, it suffices to consider p such that

\{x\in\mathbb R^n:\|x\|_p\le1\}

contains as many points as possible, and also q such that \|Ax\|_q is as large as possible. The infinity-norm unit ball contains every p-norm unit ball, and the 1-norm of each output is at least its q-norm. By these comparisons, one sees that

\|A\|_{\infty\to1}\ge\|A\|_{p\to q} \quad\text{for all }1\le p,q\le\infty.
