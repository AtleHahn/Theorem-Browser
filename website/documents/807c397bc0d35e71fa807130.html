Statement

Let a<b and let f,g:[a,b]\to\mathbb R be both nondecreasing or both nonincreasing. Then, with Riemann integration, \frac1{b-a}\int_a^b fg\,\ge\,\left(\frac1{b-a}\int_a^b f\right)\left(\frac1{b-a}\int_a^b g\right).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves monotone real functions on compact intervals; Riemann integrals; iterated integrals. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Integrate the nonnegative product of differences

Let {\displaystyle f} and {\displaystyle g} be monotonic functions of the same monotonicity on {\displaystyle [a,b]}. Then for any {\displaystyle x,y\in [a,b]} we have

{\displaystyle (f(x)-f(y))(g(x)-g(y))\geq 0.}

Integrate this inequality with respect to {\displaystyle x} and {\displaystyle y} over {\displaystyle [a,b]}:

{\displaystyle \int _{a}^{b}\int _{a}^{b}(f(x)-f(y))(g(x)-g(y))\,dx\,dy\geq 0.}

Expanding the integrand gives:

{\displaystyle \int _{a}^{b}\int _{a}^{b}\left[f(x)g(x)-f(x)g(y)-f(y)g(x)+f(y)g(y)\right]\,dx\,dy\geq 0.}

Separate the double integral into four parts:

{\displaystyle \int _{a}^{b}\int _{a}^{b}f(x)g(x)\,dx\,dy-\int _{a}^{b}\int _{a}^{b}f(x)g(y)\,dx\,dy-\int _{a}^{b}\int _{a}^{b}f(y)g(x)\,dx\,dy+\int _{a}^{b}\int _{a}^{b}f(y)g(y)\,dx\,dy\geq 0.}

Since the integration variable in each inner integral is independent, we have:

  • {\displaystyle \int _{a}^{b}\int _{a}^{b}f(x)g(x)\,dx\,dy=(b-a)\int _{a}^{b}f(x)g(x)\,dx,}
  • {\displaystyle \int _{a}^{b}\int _{a}^{b}f(y)g(y)\,dx\,dy=(b-a)\int _{a}^{b}f(y)g(y)\,dy=(b-a)\int _{a}^{b}f(x)g(x)\,dx,}
  • {\displaystyle \int _{a}^{b}\int _{a}^{b}f(x)g(y)\,dx\,dy=\left(\int _{a}^{b}f(x)\,dx\right)\left(\int _{a}^{b}g(y)\,dy\right),}
  • {\displaystyle \int _{a}^{b}\int _{a}^{b}f(y)g(x)\,dx\,dy=\left(\int _{a}^{b}f(y)\,dy\right)\left(\int _{a}^{b}g(x)\,dx\right)=\left(\int _{a}^{b}f(x)\,dx\right)\left(\int _{a}^{b}g(x)\,dx\right).}

Let

{\displaystyle I=\int _{a}^{b}f(x)g(x)\,dx,\quad F=\int _{a}^{b}f(x)\,dx,\quad G=\int _{a}^{b}g(x)\,dx.}

Substitute these into the inequality:

{\displaystyle (b-a)I-FG-FG+(b-a)I\geq 0.}

Simplify:

{\displaystyle 2(b-a)I-2FG\geq 0.}

Dividing by {\displaystyle 2(b-a)} (noting that {\displaystyle b-a>0}):

{\displaystyle I\geq {\frac {FG}{(b-a)}}.}

Divide both sides by {\displaystyle b-a} to obtain:

{\displaystyle {\frac {1}{b-a}}\int _{a}^{b}f(x)g(x)\,dx\geq \left({\frac {1}{b-a}}\int _{a}^{b}f(x)\,dx\right)\left({\frac {1}{b-a}}\int _{a}^{b}g(x)\,dx\right).}

This completes the proof.
