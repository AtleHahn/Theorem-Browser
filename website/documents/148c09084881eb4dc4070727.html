Statement

Let (X_j)_{j\ge1} be a stationary Markov chain and let g be real measurable with \mathbb E[g(X_1)^2]<\infty. Put \widehat\mu_n=n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^n g(X_j) and c_k=\operatorname{cov}(g(X_1),g(X_{1+k})) for k\ge0. If the series \sum_{k=1}^\infty c_k converges to a finite real value, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}n\operatorname{var}(\widehat\mu_n) =c_0+2\sum_{k=1}^\infty c_k.

Proof 1: Count covariance lags and apply Cesàro convergence — outline

Use Bienaymé’s covariance identity: the variance of a finite sum of square-integrable real random variables is the sum of their variances plus twice the sum of their pairwise covariances. Stationarity makes the covariance depend only on the lag. There are n-k pairs with lag k, hence

n\operatorname{var}(\widehat\mu_n) =c_0+2\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{n-k}{n}c_k.

The weighted covariance sum is a Cesàro mean. Explicitly, if s_j=\sum_{k=1}^j c_k and s_0=0, the weighted sum equals n^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}s_j. By the stated convergence assumption s_j\to s=\sum_{k=1}^\infty c_k. The Cesàro convergence theorem gives n^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}s_j\to s, proving the formula.
