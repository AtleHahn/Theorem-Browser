Statement

Let n\geq2 be an integer and let D>0. A full-rank lattice in \mathbb R^n is a subgroup \Lambda=B\mathbb Z^n for an invertible real n\times n matrix B; its covolume is |\det B|, the volume of a fundamental parallelepiped.

The space \mathcal L_{n,D} of lattices of covolume D can be identified with

\mathrm{SL}_n(\mathbb R)/\mathrm{SL}_n(\mathbb Z),\qquad g\,\mathrm{SL}_n(\mathbb Z)\longmapsto D^{1/n}g\mathbb Z^n,

with its quotient topology. Here \mathrm{SL}_n means the group of determinant-one matrices over the indicated ring. Equip this space with its invariant probability measure \mu_{n,D}, normalized to total mass one and invariant under \Lambda\mapsto h\Lambda for each h\in\mathrm{SL}_n(\mathbb R); use the completion of this measure when integrating.

For every bounded Jordan-measurable set S\subseteq\mathbb R^n (equivalently, a bounded set whose boundary has Lebesgue measure zero),

\int_{\mathcal L_{n,D}}\#\bigl((\Lambda\setminus\{0\})\cap S\bigr)\,d\mu_{n,D}(\Lambda) =\frac{\operatorname{vol}_n(S)}{D}.

Thus the mean number of nonzero lattice points in S is its volume divided by the prescribed covolume.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The lattice and volume notions extend linear algebra, but the averaging is over a noncompact homogeneous space with invariant probability measure. Understanding that quotient-measure construction in addition to lattice equivalence and measurable counting exceeds the conservative undergraduate reading allowance.
