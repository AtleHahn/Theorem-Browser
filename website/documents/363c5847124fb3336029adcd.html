Statement

Let (X,\mathcal A,\mu) be a measure space, let 1\leq p<\infty, and let f_n be real- or complex-valued measurable functions with \int_X|f_n|^p\,d\mu<\infty. Convergence in measure to f means

\mu(\{|f_n-f|>\delta\})\longrightarrow0\quad\text{for every }\delta>0.

Convergence in L^p means \int_X|f_n-f|^p\,d\mu\to0.

If \mu(X)<\infty and f is measurable, then f\in L^p and f_n\to f in L^p if and only if f_n\to f in measure and the powers |f_n|^p are uniformly integrable:

\lim_{M\to\infty}\sup_n\int_{\{|f_n|^p>M\}}|f_n|^p\,d\mu=0.

For arbitrary \mu(X) and f\in L^p, convergence f_n\to f in L^p holds if and only if all three conditions below hold:

  • f_n\to f in measure;
  • the power integrals are uniformly absolutely continuous: for every \varepsilon>0 there is \delta>0 such that \mu(A)<\delta implies \sup_n\int_A|f_n|^p\,d\mu<\varepsilon for all A\in\mathcal A;
  • for every \varepsilon>0 there is E\in\mathcal A with \mu(E)<\infty and \sup_n\int_{X\setminus E}|f_n|^p\,d\mu<\varepsilon.

Here L^p consists of measurable functions with finite integral of their pth absolute power, identified up to equality almost everywhere.
