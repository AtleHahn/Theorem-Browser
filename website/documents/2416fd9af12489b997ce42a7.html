Statement

Over ZF, the axiom of choice is equivalent to the assertion that every indexed family (S_i)_{i\in I} of nonempty sets has nonempty Cartesian product \prod_{i\in I}S_i. Here a choice function on a set X of nonempty sets is a function f with domain X satisfying f(A)\in A for every A\in X; a product element is a function s with domain I and s(i)\in S_i.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Undergraduate sets, functions, orders and infinite sequences suffice. Choice, well-ordering or maximal-chain principles are explicit set-selection/order assertions where invoked, not a demand to learn their independence proofs. The displayed quantifiers and constructions can be understood through familiar subsets and functions.

Proof 1: Identification of selectors with product elements

Each choice function on a family {\displaystyle X} of nonempty sets is an element of the Cartesian product of the sets in {\displaystyle X}, and vice versa. Therefore, the axiom of choice is equivalent to nonemptiness of the Cartesian product of every collection of nonempty sets.

This form implies a more general form where the Cartesian product is of a general indexed family of sets (which may contain duplicates), since one can always select the same element from duplicate factors.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Both formulations are explicitly defined as selector functions. A choice function on distinct factors supplies indexed choices even with duplicate factors, and an indexed product applied to the family indexed by its own sets gives the converse. Introductory set theory and functions suffice; no independence or model theory is used.
