Statement

Let F be a Ck map between open subsets of real Banach spaces, k≥1 or k=∞, with a C1 inverse G and invertible derivative everywhere. Then G is Ck.

Level

Level justification

Proof 1: Higher regularity of a Banach-space inverse

Use the inverse derivative formula DG(y)=(DF(G(y)))^{-1}. If an invertible function F is C^k with k>1, then so too is its inverse. This follows by induction using the fact that the map A\mapsto A^{-1} on bounded invertible operators is C^k for any k. For k=\infty, this applies to every finite order.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Induction is elementary once its inputs are understood, but the argument differentiates maps between arbitrary Banach spaces repeatedly and composes the Fréchet derivative with smooth inversion on a space of bounded operators. Operator-valued derivatives and higher Banach-space calculus are cumulative prerequisites beyond the short undergraduate extension.

Smoothness of bounded-operator inversion
Kind:

Theorem

Treatment:

Cited without proof

Statement used:

The inversion map A->A inverse on the open set of bounded invertible operators between Banach spaces is C^k for every finite k, hence smooth.

Application in proof:

If G is C^j and F is C^(j+1), make DG C^j and therefore G C^(j+1).

Application level:

Beyond undergraduate level

Level justification:

Understanding smoothness on operator spaces requires infinite-dimensional differential calculus.

Review notes:

For k=infinity, the induction establishes each finite order; no separate infinite-step differentiation is needed.
