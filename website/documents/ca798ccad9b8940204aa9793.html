Statement

Let p_1,\ldots,p_k be the first k primes, with k\ge1, and let A_k be the positive integers all of whose prime divisors belong to this list (including 1). Then \sum_{n\in A_k}1/n=\prod_{i=1}^k(1-1/p_i)^{-1}.

Proof 1: Expand the finite product of geometric series

Let P_k denote the set of the k first prime numbers, and let N_k=A_k be the set of the positive integers whose prime factors are all in P_k.

To show the equality, one expands each factor in the product as a geometric series, and distributes the product over the sum:

\begin{aligned} \prod_{p\in P_k}\frac{1}{1-\frac1p} &=\prod_{p\in P_k}\sum_{i\ge0}\frac{1}{p^i}\\ &=\left(\sum_{i\ge0}\frac{1}{p_1^i}\right) \left(\sum_{i\ge0}\frac{1}{p_2^i}\right)\cdots \left(\sum_{i\ge0}\frac{1}{p_k^i}\right)\\ &=\sum_{e_1,\ldots,e_k\ge0}\frac{1}{p_1^{e_1}\cdots p_k^{e_k}}\\ &=\sum_{n\in N_k}\frac1n. \end{aligned}

In the penultimate sum, every product of primes in P_k appears exactly once, so the last equality is true by the fundamental theorem of arithmetic.
