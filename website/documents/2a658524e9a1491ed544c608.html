Statement

Let D be a nonempty bounded connected open subset of a complex Banach space X. For z\in D,v\in X, set \alpha(z,v)=\sup_g|g^{\prime}(z)v|, where g:D\to\{\zeta\in\mathbb C:|\zeta|<1\} is holomorphic. Define \ell(\gamma)=\int_0^1\alpha(\gamma(t),\dot\gamma(t))\,dt for piecewise differentiable curves in D, and d(x,y)=\inf_{\gamma(0)=x,\gamma(1)=y}\ell(\gamma). If f:D\to D is holomorphic, then \alpha(f(z),f^{\prime}(z)v)\le\alpha(z,v) and d(f(x),f(y))\le d(x,y).

The statement concerns an infinitesimal holomorphic length and its path distance in a Banach domain.

Proof 1: Compose the disk-valued test functions

The chain rule (g\circ f)^{\prime}(z)=g^{\prime}(f(z))f^{\prime}(z) gives \alpha(f(z),f^{\prime}(z)v)\le\alpha(z,v). Applying this to curve lengths and taking the infimum gives d(f(x),f(y))\le d(x,y).

Level justification

Compose disk-valued test maps, apply the derivative chain rule and compare path lengths. Holomorphic Frechet maps on complex Banach spaces and infinitesimal path metrics are a substantial graduate framework.
