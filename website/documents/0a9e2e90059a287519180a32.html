Statement

Let p be an odd prime and suppose there is a prime q such that no two nonzero pth powers differ by 1 modulo q, and p is not a pth power modulo q. Then there are no positive integers x,y,z with x^p+y^p=z^p and p\nmid xyz.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All auxiliary-prime conditions are explicit elementary congruences.

Proof 1: Apply the p-squared divisibility conclusion

Sophie Germain’s divisibility theorem states that under these auxiliary-prime conditions, any positive integer solution of x^p+y^p=z^p has at least one of x,y,z divisible by p^2. That makes p divide xyz, excluding a solution in the first case.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Apply the supplied integer divisibility theorem: divisibility of one variable by p squared implies divisibility of xyz by p. The exact theorem uses only integer powers, primes and congruences; its deeper proof is not required.
