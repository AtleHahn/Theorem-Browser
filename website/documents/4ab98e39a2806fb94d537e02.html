A variable-merging substitution has no inverse

Statement

Here a formal term is an expression built from variable, constant and function symbols, compared as written without evaluating it. A listed substitution simultaneously replaces each occurrence of its listed variables by the assigned terms, fixes all other variables, and leaves constant and function symbols unchanged; replacements are not repeated inside the inserted terms in the same application. For distinct variables x,y,z, the substitution \{x\mapsto z,y\mapsto z\} cannot have an inverse substitution. An inverse substitution would undo the replacement on every term. In the proof, + is a formal binary function symbol.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Substitution and inversion are explicitly defined on written expressions.

Proof 1: Merged occurrences cannot be distinguished afterwards

The flat substitution \{x\mapsto z,y\mapsto z\} cannot have an inverse, since e.g.

(x+y)\{x\mapsto z,y\mapsto z\}=z+z,

and the latter term cannot be transformed back to x+y, as the information about the origin a z stems from is lost.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Both occurrences of z must undergo the same replacement, so they cannot recover two distinct original variables. Elementary symbolic substitution suffices.
