Statement

Let L be a language over a finite alphabet \Sigma, and put G_L(x)=\sum_{k\ge0}|L\cap\Sigma^k|x^k. If this formal power series is not algebraic over \mathbb Q(x), then no unambiguous context-free grammar generates L. Here unambiguous means that every generated word has a unique parse tree; algebraic means satisfying a nonzero polynomial equation in the series with coefficients in \mathbb Q[x].

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Formal series extend undergraduate polynomials by coefficientwise operations, and algebraicity and unambiguity are explicitly defined. Context-free production rules and parse trees add a limited finite rewriting model.

Proof 1: Apply the enumeration theorem contrapositively

Use the Chomsky–Schützenberger enumeration theorem: the word-count generating series of a language with an unambiguous context-free grammar is algebraic over \mathbb Q(x). Therefore a nonalgebraic word-count generating series rules out any such grammar.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Take the contrapositive of the supplied enumeration theorem. Undergraduate automata theory and generating series suffice; algebraicity is explicitly defined as a polynomial identity, so no general algebraic function-field theory is required.
