Statement

Let \pi(t) be the number of primes at most the positive integer t. For each n\geq1, the integer sequence x_0=n, x_{r+1}=n+\pi(x_r) eventually stabilizes at the nth positive nonprime, counting 1 as a nonprime.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The recurrence uses only counting primes, addition and the claim that an integer procedure eventually stops at a specified nonprime. These are elementary arithmetic concepts. This rating concerns understanding the statement, independently of the difficulty of its proof.

Proof 1: Substitute the prime-counting function into the complement iteration

Let F increasingly enumerate an infinite set of positive integers with infinite complement, and let F^\#(t) denote the number of values of x for which F(x)\leq t. The Lambek–Moser limit formula states that the nth non-member is the limit of

n,\quad n+F^\#(n),\quad n+F^\#\bigl(n+F^\#(n)\bigr),\quad\dots,

meaning that, after a certain point, all successive values in this sequence become equal to each other.

Taking F to enumerate the primes, F^\#=\pi. Thus, if \pi(n) is the prime-counting function (the number of primes less than or equal to n), then the nth nonprime (1 or a composite number) is given by the limit of the sequence

n,\quad n+\pi(n),\quad n+\pi\bigl(n+\pi(n)\bigr),\quad\dots.
Level
High school only
Level justification

Identify the counting function of the prime enumeration with pi and substitute into the explicit complementary-sequence rule. Elementary primes, integer counting and recursion suffice; the stated limit is eventual equality.
