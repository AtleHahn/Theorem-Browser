Statement

Let n be a nonnegative integer, \alpha>-1, and 0\le t<1. In standard generalized hypergeometric notation (with (a)_k denoting the rising factorial),

{\displaystyle {}_{3}F_{2}\left(-n,n+\alpha +2,{\tfrac {1}{2}}(\alpha +1);{\tfrac {1}{2}}(\alpha +3),\alpha +1;t\right)>0,\qquad 0\leq t<1,\quad \alpha >-1.}
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Calculus and algebra supply finite sums and factorial products. Generalized hypergeometric notation can be expanded by its defining coefficient formula; the negative-integer parameter makes this a terminating polynomial. Learning that notation is a limited extension, not learning special-function theory.

Proof 1: Ekhad decomposition and Clausen squares — outline

Clausen dependency

The square identity from the archived article “Clausen’s formula” is

{\displaystyle \;_{2}F_{1}\left[{\begin{matrix}a&b\\a+b+1/2\end{matrix}};x\right]^{2}=\;_{3}F_{2}\left[{\begin{matrix}2a&2b&a+b\\a+b+1/2&2a+2b\end{matrix}};x\right]}

Ekhad decomposition

Combine the following identity with the Clausen square identity:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}&{\frac {(\alpha +2)_{n}}{n!}}\times {}_{3}F_{2}\left(-n,n+\alpha +2,{\tfrac {1}{2}}(\alpha +1);{\tfrac {1}{2}}(\alpha +3),\alpha +1;t\right)\\=&\sum _{j}{\frac {\left({\tfrac {1}{2}}\right)_{j}\left({\tfrac {\alpha }{2}}+1\right)_{n-j}\left({\tfrac {\alpha }{2}}+{\tfrac {3}{2}}\right)_{n-2j}(\alpha +1)_{n-2j}}{j!\left({\tfrac {\alpha }{2}}+{\tfrac {3}{2}}\right)_{n-j}\left({\tfrac {\alpha }{2}}+{\tfrac {1}{2}}\right)_{n-2j}(n-2j)!}}\times {}_{3}F_{2}\left(-n+2j,n-2j+\alpha +1,{\tfrac {1}{2}}(\alpha +1);{\tfrac {1}{2}}(\alpha +2),\alpha +1;t\right)\end{aligned}}}

Here the finite sum is over integers 0\le j\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The supplied identities reduce the claim to coefficient signs and squares. Undergraduate power-series algebra suffices after learning rising-factorial and hypergeometric coefficient notation, a limited extension rather than special-function theory. With m=n-2j, Clausen uses a=-m/2, b=(m+alpha+1)/2; all denominators and decomposition coefficients are positive. Strict positivity follows from the last summand: Q_0=1 for even n, or Q_1=1-t for odd n. The printed outline leaves these checks implicit, but they require no further advanced result.

Statement used:

For n>=0, alpha>-1, let P_n(t)=3F2(-n,n+alpha+2,(alpha+1)/2;(alpha+3)/2,alpha+1;t) and Q_m(t)=3F2(-m,m+alpha+1,(alpha+1)/2;(alpha+2)/2,alpha+1;t). Then ((alpha+2)n/n!) P_n(t)=sum(j=0)^floor(n/2) c_(n,j) Q_(n-2j)(t), where c_(n,j)=(1/2)j (alpha/2+1)(n-j) (alpha/2+3/2)(n-2j) (alpha+1)(n-2j) / [j! (alpha/2+3/2)(n-j) (alpha/2+1/2)(n-2j) (n-2j)!].

Application in proof:

Express the target polynomial as a positively weighted finite sum to which Clausen applies.
