Statement

For a real continuous function f on a compact real interval [a,b] and every \varepsilon>0, there exists a real polynomial p with \sup_{x\in[a,b]}|f(x)-p(x)|<\varepsilon.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves continuous functions on a compact interval; uniform norm; polynomials. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Apply the real density theorem to polynomials

Use the real Stone–Weierstrass theorem: a subalgebra of C(X,\mathbb R) containing the constants is uniformly dense if it separates points, where X is compact Hausdorff.

This implies Weierstrass' original statement since the polynomials on [a, b] form a subalgebra of C[a, b] which contains the constants and separates points.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Check that polynomials form a unital point-separating algebra and apply the stated uniform density theorem. Real analysis and elementary point-set topology suffice.
