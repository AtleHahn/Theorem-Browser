Statement

Let m,n,r be nonnegative integers. An urn contains n red and m blue marbles. Choose an r-element subset uniformly, where 0\le r\le m+n, and let K be the number of red marbles chosen. Then \Pr(K=k)=\frac{\binom nk\binom m{r-k}}{\binom{m+n}r}\quad(0\le k\le r), with impossible choices having probability zero; these probabilities sum to one.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All samples are subsets of a finite set and their probabilities are finite counting ratios.

Proof 1: Counting and normalizing urn samples

There are \binom{m+n}{r} subsets of r marbles. The number containing k red marbles and r-k blue marbles is \binom nk\binom m{r-k}. Counting by the number of red marbles gives Vandermonde's identity:

\binom{m+n}{r}=\sum_{k=0}^r\binom nk\binom m{r-k}.

When both sides have been divided by the expression on the left, so that the sum is 1, then the terms of the sum may be interpreted as probabilities. The resulting probability distribution is the hypergeometric distribution. That is the probability distribution of the number of red marbles in r draws without replacement from an urn containing n red and m blue marbles.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Count favorable red-blue subsets and divide by the total, then partition all samples by their red count. Elementary finite probability and binomial counting suffice.
