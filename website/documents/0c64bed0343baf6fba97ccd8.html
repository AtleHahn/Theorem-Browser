Statement

For a,b>0, \int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\sin^2x}{(a\cos^2x+b\sin^2x)^2}\,dx=\pi/(4\sqrt{ab^3}).

Level

Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Real definite integrals; Trigonometric functions; Positive real parameters. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Differentiate the tangent-substitution integral in b

First we calculate:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}J&=\int _{0}^{\pi /2}{\frac {1}{a\cos ^{2}x+b\sin ^{2}x}}dx\\[6pt]&=\int _{0}^{\pi /2}{\frac {\frac {1}{\cos ^{2}x}}{a+b{\frac {\sin ^{2}x}{\cos ^{2}x}}}}dx\\[6pt]&=\int _{0}^{\pi /2}{\frac {\sec ^{2}x}{a+b\tan ^{2}x}}dx\\[6pt]&={\frac {1}{b}}\int _{0}^{\pi /2}{\frac {1}{\left({\sqrt {\frac {a}{b}}}\right)^{2}+\tan ^{2}x}}\,d(\tan x)\\[6pt]&=\left.{\frac {1}{\sqrt {ab}}}\arctan \left({\sqrt {\frac {b}{a}}}\tan x\right)\right|_{0}^{\pi /2}\\[6pt]&={\frac {\pi }{2{\sqrt {ab}}}}.\end{aligned}}}

On each compact parameter neighborhood with positive a,b, the integrand and its parameter derivatives are continuous, so differentiation under the integral sign is valid. The limits of integration are independent of b. Pursuing \partial J/\partial b yields

\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\sin^2x}{(a\cos^2x+b\sin^2x)^2}\,dx=\frac{\pi}{4\sqrt{ab^3}}.

The source leaves this parameter differentiation implicit.
