Statement

Assume the Smith conjecture for smooth involutions of S^3: a fixed-point circle of such an involution is unknotted. Then every nontrivial orientation-preserving finite even-order diffeomorphism f of S^3 with fixed points has an unknotted fixed-point circle.

Proof 1: Take the half-order iterate — outline

Smith's fixed-set theorem says that a nontrivial orientation-preserving diffeomorphism of finite order with fixed points must have a fixed point set equal to a circle. The source states that the Smith conjecture for diffeomorphisms of order 2 implies the case of any even order.

This implication is asserted parenthetically; the source does not supply the half-order-iterate argument or the comparison of the two fixed-point circles.

Level justification

The source asserts a reduction among periodic sphere diffeomorphisms and their fixed-point knots. Smooth three-manifold actions, embedded circles and unknotting require substantial topology; the missing iterate details do not lower that prerequisite burden.
