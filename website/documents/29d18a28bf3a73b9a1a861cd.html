Statement

The real system x+y+z=1, x+y+z=2, x+y+z=3 is inconsistent, although its coefficient determinant and each determinant obtained by replacing one coefficient column by the right-hand column are zero.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Real linear systems; Three-by-three determinants. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Repeated columns in an explicit inconsistent system — outline

A simple example where all determinants vanish (equal zero) but the system is still inconsistent is the 3×3 system x+y+z=1, x+y+z=2, x+y+z=3.

The source asserts the vanishing determinants and inconsistency without writing out their checks.
