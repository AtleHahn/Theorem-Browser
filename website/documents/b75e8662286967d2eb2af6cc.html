Statement

A nonconstant entire function of finite order maps \mathbb C onto either \mathbb C or \mathbb C\setminus\{w\} for one complex number w.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Entire means complex differentiable everywhere, and finite order is a growth condition given by a limsup of logarithms. Real analysis supplies zero counts, series, growth estimates and limits; no general entire-function factorization theory is needed to understand these claims.

Proof 1: A nonconstant finite-order entire function omits at most one value — source argument

If f does not assume the value z_0, then by Hadamard factorization,

f(z)-z_0=e^{Q(z)}

for a nonconstant polynomial Q. By the fundamental theorem of algebra, Q assumes all values, so f(z)-z_0 assumes all nonzero values. Thus z_0 is the only omitted value.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The supplied proof uses the zero-free case of Hadamard factorization together with entire-function growth order and preservation of order under constant shifts. Retain beyond undergraduate provisionally for these linked complex-analysis concepts; the later polynomial-surjectivity step is elementary.
