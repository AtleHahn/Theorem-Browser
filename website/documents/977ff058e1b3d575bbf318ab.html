Statement

Let \pi(x) count primes at most x. For every \varepsilon>0, there exists S such that for every x>S, (1-\varepsilon)x/\log x<\pi(x)<(1+\varepsilon)x/\log x.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Prime counting; Asymptotic limits; Logarithms. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Limit definition applied to the prime number theorem

The prime number theorem says \pi(x)/(x/\log x)\to1. Its limit definition gives 1-\varepsilon<\pi(x)/(x/\log x)<1+\varepsilon for sufficiently large x. Multiply by x/\log x>0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the epsilon definition of the supplied prime-number limit and multiply by a positive real function. First undergraduate calculus suffices; the analytic proof of the prime number theorem is not invoked.
