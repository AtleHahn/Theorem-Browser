Statement

Let G be a finitely generated group, and let b(n) count elements of word length at most n for a finite symmetric generating set. Define its growth degree as d=\inf\{s\ge0:b(n)=O(n^s)\}, with value \infty when there is no polynomial bound. Then d is a nonnegative integer or \infty.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A finite generating set and word length are direct group-theoretic concepts; the growth degree is explicitly defined through a counting function and big-O bounds. Undergraduate group theory and real asymptotics suffice without the proof theory of polynomial-growth groups.

Proof 1: Transfer the Bass–Guivarc’h integer degree (outline)

By Gromov’s theorem a group of polynomial growth has a finitely generated nilpotent subgroup H of finite index. Use the standard finite-index invariance of growth degree. If H=H_1\supseteq H_2\supseteq\cdots is its lower central series, the Bass–Guivarc’h formula gives

d(H)=\sum_{j\ge1}j\operatorname{rank}(H_j/H_{j+1}).

The series terminates, and every rank is a nonnegative integer. Thus the degree of H, and hence of G, is an integer.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply Gromov, pass to a finite-index nilpotent subgroup and interpret the Bass-Guivarch ranks of successive central quotients. Word growth, nilpotence, abelian ranks and finite-index invariance form several linked extensions; retain beyond undergraduate provisionally.
