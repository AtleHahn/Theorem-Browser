Statement

Write i^2=-1 and e^z=\sum_{m=0}^\infty z^m/m! for the complex exponential. The map t\mapsto e^{it} from \mathbb R onto \{z\in\mathbb C:|z|=1\} is continuous, satisfies e^{i(s+t)}=e^{is}e^{it}, and has e^{it}=1 exactly for t\in2\pi\mathbb Z.

Proof 1: Read the unit circle and period from Euler’s formula — outline

Euler’s formula writes e^{it}=\cos t+i\sin t, the usual unit-circle parametrization by the angle t. The exponential addition law gives the multiplication rule. The same trigonometric parametrization has value one at precisely the integer multiples of 2\pi.

Level justification

Apply Euler formula to identify the familiar circle parametrization, and use the exponential addition law. The series-defined complex exponential and continuity require ordinary calculus, not Lie groups or covering-space theory.
