Statement

Let I be [a,b], [a,b) or [a,\infty), with a<b in the finite-endpoint cases. Let \mu be a nonnegative atomless Borel measure finite on each [a,t]. If measurable u\ge0 satisfies \int_{[a,t)}u\,d\mu<\infty and u(t)\le\int_{[a,t)}u(s)\,\mu(ds) for every t, then u=0.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The full statement and argument require measure theory beyond undergraduate calculus and linear algebra.

Proof 1: Apply the measure inequality with zero data

Apply the measure Grönwall inequality with \alpha=0. It gives u(t)\le0 for every t; combine this with u\ge0.

Level

Level justification

Set alpha to zero in the measure inequality and combine the resulting upper bound with nonnegativity. Retain beyond undergraduate provisionally for the arbitrary atomless Borel-measure and measurable-function setting.
