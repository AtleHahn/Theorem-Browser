Statement

Let a<b be finite real numbers. If f:[a,b]\to\mathbb R has generalized bounded variation in the restricted sense, then its finite ordinary derivative f'(x) exists for almost every x\in(a,b). The restricted-variation condition means that [a,b]=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}E_n, where for each n there is a finite constant C_n such that every finite family of intervals [u_j,v_j]\subseteq[a,b] with pairwise disjoint interiors and endpoints u_j,v_j\in E_n satisfies

\sum_j\operatorname{osc}(f;[u_j,v_j])\le C_n.

Here \operatorname{osc}(f;I)=\sup\{|f(s)-f(t)|:s,t\in I\}. In particular the oscillation is taken over the entire interval, not merely its intersection with E_n. Almost every means outside a Lebesgue-null set: for every \varepsilon>0 that exceptional set can be covered by countably many open intervals of total length less than \varepsilon. The derivative is the usual two-sided limit \lim_{h\to0}(f(x+h)-f(x))/h.

Level justification

Undergraduate real analysis supplies derivatives, suprema and countable interval covers. The statement adds one explicitly defined oscillation-sum condition and the interval-cover meaning of null sets, a realistically small extension within about thirty minutes; no generalized integration theory is required.
