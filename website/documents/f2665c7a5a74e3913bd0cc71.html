Independent discrete die rolls produce Gaussian fluctuations after centering and scaling. This concrete central-limit result illustrates how a smooth aggregate law emerges from simple random components, without requiring any individual component to have a Gaussian distribution.

Statement

Let (D_i)_{i\ge1} be independent rolls of a fair six-sided die, taking values 1,\ldots,6 each with probability 1/6. Put \mu=\mathbb E D_1 and \sigma^2=\operatorname{Var}(D_1). Then (\sum_{i=1}^nD_i-n\mu)/(\sigma\sqrt n)\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The experiment is an explicit sequence of independent fair dice. Means and variances are finite weighted sums; convergence in distribution uses cumulative probabilities and almost-sure convergence means probability one. These limited probability additions make the concrete limit assertion accessible without general stochastic-process theory.

Proof 1: Apply the iid central limit theorem to fair dice — outline

The identical fair dice give identically distributed independent random variables with finite positive variance. Applying the classical central limit theorem to their sum gives the normal limit.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Check independence, identical distribution and finite positive variance for six equally likely outcomes, then apply the supplied central limit theorem. The normal law and convergence of distribution functions are standard undergraduate probability.
