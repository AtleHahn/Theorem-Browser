Statement

Let ABC and A' B' C' be nondegenerate Euclidean triangles. If \angle A=\angle A', \angle B=\angle B' and BC=B' C', then the triangles are congruent with these corresponding vertices.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement compares three specified lengths and angles of ordinary nondegenerate Euclidean triangles.

Proof 1: Angle sum reduces AAS to ASA

AAS is equivalent to an ASA condition, by the fact that if any two angles are given, so is the third angle, since their sum should be 180^\circ. Here BC=B'C' is the side included between the equal angles at B,C and B',C', so the angle-side-angle (ASA) criterion gives congruence.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The proof subtracts two given angles from 180 degrees to identify the third angle, then applies the usual angle-side-angle congruence criterion to the specified included side. Both the dependencies and the application belong to standard high-school Euclidean geometry.
