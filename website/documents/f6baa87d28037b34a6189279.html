Statement

Let d be a square-free integer different from 0 and 1, let K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt d)\subset\mathbb C, and define

D=\begin{cases}d,&d\equiv1\pmod4,\\4d,&d\not\equiv1\pmod4.\end{cases}

Then the smallest positive integer n for which

K\subseteq\mathbb Q(e^{2\pi i/n})

is |D|. Here square-free means that no square of a prime divides d; \mathbb Q(\sqrt d)=\{a+b\sqrt d:a,b\in\mathbb Q\}, and \mathbb Q(e^{2\pi i/n}) is the smallest subfield of \mathbb C containing \mathbb Q and e^{2\pi i/n}. The integer D is the discriminant of K, and the least n in the assertion is its cyclotomic conductor.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Although the discriminant is given explicitly, understanding minimal containment between quadratic and cyclotomic fields requires number-field background not confidently within the short undergraduate extension.
