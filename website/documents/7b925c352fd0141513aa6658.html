Statement

Let H be a real or complex Hilbert space with countable orthonormal basis (g_m), let f\in H, and let M\geq1 be an integer. Among projections of f onto spans of M selected basis vectors, the least squared error is obtained by selecting M largest values of |\langle f,g_m\rangle| (ties may be resolved arbitrarily). Its error is the sum of squares of the unselected coefficients; it is no greater than the error of any fixed choice of M indices.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Inner products and orthogonal projections are linear algebra; completeness and convergent series are analysis. An orthonormal basis in this setting means a complete orthonormal sequence. The minimum-error claim extends finite orthogonal approximation using the explicit squared-coefficient tail.

Proof 1: Minimize the sum of unselected coefficient squares

Linear approximations project the signal on M vectors a priori. The approximation can be made more precise by choosing the M orthogonal vectors depending on the signal properties. A signal {\displaystyle f\in \mathrm {H} } is approximated with M vectors selected adaptively in an orthonormal basis for {\displaystyle \mathrm {H} }

{\displaystyle \mathrm {B} =\left\{g_{m}\right\}_{m\in \mathbb {N} }}

Let {\displaystyle f_{M}} be the projection of f over M vectors whose indices are in IM:

{\displaystyle f_{M}=\sum _{m\in I_{M}}\left\langle f,g_{m}\right\rangle g_{m}}

The approximation error is the sum of the remaining coefficients

{\displaystyle \varepsilon [M]=\left\{\left\|f-f_{M}\right\|^{2}\right\}=\sum _{m\notin I_{M}}\left\{\left|\left\langle f,g_{m}\right\rangle \right|^{2}\right\}}

To minimize this error, the indices in IM must correspond to the M vectors having the largest inner product amplitude

{\displaystyle \left|\left\langle f,g_{m}\right\rangle \right|.}

These are the vectors that best correlate f. They can thus be interpreted as the main features of f. The resulting error is necessarily no greater than the error of a linear approximation which selects the M approximation vectors independently of f. Resolve ties arbitrarily, selecting exactly M indices. Let us sort

{\displaystyle \left\{\left|\left\langle f,g_{m}\right\rangle \right|\right\}_{m\in \mathbb {N} }}

in decreasing order (listing the nonzero coefficients, and padding with zero coefficients if only finitely many are nonzero)

{\displaystyle \left|\left\langle f,g_{m_{k}}\right\rangle \right|\geq \left|\left\langle f,g_{m_{k+1}}\right\rangle \right|.}

The best non-linear approximation is

{\displaystyle f_{M}=\sum _{k=1}^{M}\left\langle f,g_{m_{k}}\right\rangle g_{m_{k}}}

The non-linear error is

{\displaystyle \varepsilon [M]=\left\{\left\|f-f_{M}\right\|^{2}\right\}=\sum _{k=M+1}^{\infty }\left\{\left|\left\langle f,g_{m_{k}}\right\rangle \right|^{2}\right\}}
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply Parseval to express the projection error as a nonnegative series, then select its M largest removed terms. Undergraduate inner-product geometry and convergent series suffice with countable Parseval as an explicit extension; square summability makes selection of largest nonzero coefficients legitimate.
