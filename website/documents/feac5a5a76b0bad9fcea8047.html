Statement

Let k be a Euclidean circle with center O and let P be outside it. Let H be the midpoint of OP and let T,T' be the intersections of k with the circle of center H and radius OH. Then PT and PT' are tangents to k.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The construction uses only plane circles, midpoint placement and tangency.

Proof 1: Use right angles on the auxiliary diameter — outline

The auxiliary circle has diameter OP. Therefore its triangles OTP and OT' P are right at T and T' by Thales’s theorem. Thus PT\perp OT and PT'\perp OT'. A line through a point of a circle perpendicular to its radius is tangent there, giving the construction.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The auxiliary diameter gives right angles and hence radius-perpendicular tangents. School circle geometry suffices.
