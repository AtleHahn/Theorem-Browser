Statement

Let (X_n)_{n\geq1} be independent identically distributed random variables on a probability space (\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P), taking values in a measurable space (E,\mathcal A).

Let B be a measurable subset of E^{\mathbb N} for the product sigma algebra, and suppose its membership is unchanged by every permutation of \mathbb N fixing all but finitely many indices: for every such permutation \pi and every sequence (x_n),

(x_n)_{n\geq1}\in B\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad(x_{\pi(n)})_{n\geq1}\in B.

Then

\mathbb P\bigl((X_n)_{n\geq1}\in B\bigr)\in\{0,1\}.

Equivalently, every symmetric event determined by the sequence has probability zero or one.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The arbitrary measurable-space formulation and product-event sigma algebra require measure-theoretic probability, a cumulative extension beyond standard undergraduate analysis and elementary probability.
