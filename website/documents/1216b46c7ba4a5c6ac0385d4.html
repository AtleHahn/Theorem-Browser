Statement

Let g be holomorphic and injective on |z|>1, with Laurent expansion g(z)=z+\sum_{n\ge1}b_nz^{-n}. Then \sum_{n\ge1}n|b_n|^2\le1, with equality if and only if \mathbb C\setminus g(\{|z|>1\}) has planar Lebesgue measure zero.

Proof 1: Compute the omitted area by a boundary integral

In fact, if {\displaystyle r>1}, the complement of the image of the disk {\displaystyle |z|>r} is a bounded domain {\displaystyle X(r)}. Its area is given by

{\displaystyle \int _{X(r)}dx\,dy={1 \over 2i}\int _{\partial X(r)}{\overline {z}}\,dz={1 \over 2i}\int _{|z|=r}{\overline {g}}\,dg=\pi r^{2}-\pi \sum _{n=1}^{\infty }n|b_{n}|^{2}r^{-2n}.}

Since the area is positive, the result follows by letting {\displaystyle r} decrease to {\displaystyle 1}. The above proof shows equality holds if and only if the complement of the image of {\displaystyle g} has zero area, i.e. Lebesgue measure zero.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Compute the omitted area by contour integration of the Laurent series and pass to the boundary using continuity of planar measure. Univalent functions, Laurent integration and measurable omitted sets require advanced analysis.
