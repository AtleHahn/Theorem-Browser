Statement

Let f=1_{\mathbb Q} on [0,1] and let \varepsilon>0. For any enumeration (x_n)_{n\ge1} of \mathbb Q, put G_n=(x_n-\varepsilon/2^n,x_n+\varepsilon/2^n) and E=[0,1]\setminus\bigcup_{n\ge1}G_n. Then E is compact, contains no rational numbers, and its Lebesgue measure satisfies \mu(E)>1-2\varepsilon. The restriction f|_E is continuous.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Real analysis supplies rational enumerations, interval covers and compactness. The measure of the complement can be understood through countably additive interval length; only this restricted notion, not general Lebesgue integration, is needed for the displayed example.

Proof 1: Remove summably short rational neighborhoods

Let {\displaystyle \{x_{n};n=1,2,\dots \}} be any enumeration of {\displaystyle \mathbb {Q} }. Set

{\displaystyle G_{n}=(x_{n}-\varepsilon /2^{n},x_{n}+\varepsilon /2^{n})}

and

{\displaystyle E:=[0,1]\setminus \bigcup _{n=1}^{\infty }G_{n}}.

Then the sequence of open sets {\displaystyle G_{n}} "knocks out" all of the rationals, leaving behind a compact, closed set {\displaystyle E} which contains no rationals, and has a measure of more than {\displaystyle 1-2\varepsilon }.

Indeed, the interval lengths sum to \sum_{n\ge1}2\varepsilon/2^n=2\varepsilon. By countable subadditivity the removed measure is at most this sum, and strictly less on [0,1] because an interval about some rational greater than 1+\varepsilon misses [0,1]. The restriction to E is the constant function zero, so it is continuous.

Level justification

Enumerate the rationals and sum the lengths of the removed intervals. For this explicit closed subset of an interval, only interval length, countable subadditivity and subtraction from total length one are needed, a small related extension of real analysis; no general Lebesgue integration theory is involved.
