Duality interchanges faces and vertices of Platonic solids

Statement

A Platonic solid is a convex polyhedron in Euclidean three-space whose faces are congruent regular p-gons and at each vertex of which q faces meet. The concentric dual has type (q,p) and counts (V^*,E^*,F^*)=(F,E,V), where V,E,F count the original vertices, edges and faces.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The regular solids, their face-center dual construction and vertex/edge/face counts use elementary spatial geometry.

Proof 1: Construct the dual from face centers — outline

One can construct the dual polyhedron by taking the vertices of the dual to be the centers of the faces of the original figure. Connecting the centers of adjacent faces in the original forms the edges of the dual and thereby interchanges the number of faces and vertices while maintaining the number of edges.

If a polyhedron has Schläfli symbol \{p,q\}, then its dual has the symbol \{q,p\}: around an original vertex the q incident faces give a dual q-gon, and an original p-gon gives a dual vertex incident with p faces.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Place vertices at face centers and count the corresponding edges and vertex-surrounding faces. School spatial geometry and finite incidence counting suffice.
