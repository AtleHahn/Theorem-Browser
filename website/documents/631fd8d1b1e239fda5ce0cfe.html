Statement

Let p be an odd prime, let \zeta be a primitive p-th root of unity, and put K=\mathbb Q(\zeta). Write \Delta=\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q), with \sigma_a(\zeta)=\zeta^a for 1\leq a\leq p-1.

Let G be the p-primary part of the ideal class group of K: the subgroup of classes of p-power order in the group of nonzero fractional ideals modulo principal fractional ideals. Regard G as an additive \mathbb Z_p[\Delta]-module, with the natural action on ideal classes. Here \mathbb Z_p is the ring of p-adic integers. Let \omega(a)\in\mathbb Z_p^\times be the unique (p-1)-st root of unity congruent to a modulo p. Define

\epsilon_n=\frac1{p-1}\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}\omega(a)^n\sigma_a^{-1},\qquad G_n=\epsilon_nG\quad(1\leq n\leq p-1).

For every odd n in this range,

G_n\ne0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p\text{ divides the numerator of }B_{p-n}\text{ in lowest terms}.

The Bernoulli numbers are defined by the formal power series

\frac{t}{e^t-1}=\sum_{m\geq0}B_m\frac{t^m}{m!}.

Proof 1: Modular Galois representations (proof outline) (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorem 1, p. 273; reduction and proof outline, pp. 274–284). The linked exposition is a proof sketch with the indicated dependencies.

For odd n>1 put k=p-n, so 2<=k<=p-3 and 1-k is congruent to n modulo p-1. The idempotent acts on the omega^n eigenspace. A finite p-primary module is nonzero iff its mod-p quotient is nonzero, giving the comparison with Khare's formulation. For n=1, take theta=(1/p)sum_{a=1}^{p-1} a sigma_a^{-1}. The integral element p theta annihilates the class group by Stickelberger. Its scalar on G_1 is sum a omega(a)^{-1}, congruent to p-1 modulo p, hence a p-adic unit; therefore G_1=0. Von Staudt–Clausen gives v_p(B_{p-1})=-1, so p does not divide its numerator in lowest terms. This handles p=3 too.
