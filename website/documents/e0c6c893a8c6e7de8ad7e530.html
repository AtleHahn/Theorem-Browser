Statement

Let (X_t)_{t\in[a,b]} be a centered real process on a probability space (\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P), with a<b, X_t\in L^2(\Omega) and continuous covariance K(s,t)=\mathbb E(X_sX_t). Define (Tf)(t)=\int_a^b K(s,t)f(s)\,ds on real L^2([a,b]). Let (f_k)_{k\geq1} be a real orthonormal basis of L^2([a,b]), A_k=\int_a^b X_t f_k(t)\,dt in mean square, and \widehat X_N(t)=\sum_{k=1}^N A_kf_k(t). Then

\int_a^b\mathbb E|X_t-\widehat X_N(t)|^2\,dt=\sum_{k>N}\int_a^b\!\int_a^b K(s,t)f_k(s)f_k(t)\,ds\,dt.

Proof 1: Orthogonality removes the error cross terms

Given the orthonormal basis \{f_k\} of L^2([a,b]), we may decompose the process in integrated mean square as

X_t=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}A_kf_k(t), \qquad A_k=\int_a^b X_t f_k(t)\,dt,

and approximate X_t by the finite sum

\widehat X_N(t)=\sum_{k=1}^N A_kf_k(t).

Consider the error resulting from the truncation at the N-th term in this orthonormal expansion:

\varepsilon_N(t)=\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}A_kf_k(t).

To interpret the calculation in integrated mean square, first truncate this residual at M>N, writing \varepsilon_{N,M}(t)=\sum_{k=N+1}^M A_kf_k(t). Its mean-square error can be written as

\begin{aligned} \mathbb E|\varepsilon_{N,M}(t)|^2 &=\mathbb E\left[\sum_{i=N+1}^M\sum_{j=N+1}^M A_iA_jf_i(t)f_j(t)\right]\\ &=\sum_{i=N+1}^M\sum_{j=N+1}^M \mathbb E\left[\int_a^b\int_a^b X_uX_s f_i(u)f_j(s)\,ds\,du\right]f_i(t)f_j(t)\\ &=\sum_{i=N+1}^M\sum_{j=N+1}^M f_i(t)f_j(t) \int_a^b\int_a^b K(s,u)f_i(u)f_j(s)\,ds\,du. \end{aligned}

We then integrate this last equality over [a,b]. The orthonormality of the f_k yields

\int_a^b\mathbb E|\varepsilon_{N,M}(t)|^2\,dt =\sum_{k=N+1}^M\int_a^b\int_a^b K(s,u)f_k(u)f_k(s)\,ds\,du.

Letting M\to\infty in integrated mean square gives

\int_a^b\mathbb E|X_t-\widehat X_N(t)|^2\,dt =\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}\int_a^b\int_a^b K(s,t)f_k(t)f_k(s)\,ds\,dt.
