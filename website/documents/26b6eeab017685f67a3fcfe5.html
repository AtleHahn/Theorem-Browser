Statement

Let f:X\to Y be a finite-type morphism between Noetherian schemes. For x\in X, put y=f(x) and let \widehat{\mathcal O_{Y,y}} and \widehat{\mathcal O_{X,x}} be the completions at their maximal ideals. If the induced map \widehat{\mathcal O_{Y,y}}\to\widehat{\mathcal O_{X,x}} is an isomorphism for every x, then f is étale (flat and unramified).

Beyond undergraduate level

These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Proof 1: Contract completed maximal ideals and descend flatness

Assume that \widehat{\mathcal O_{Y,y}}\to\widehat{\mathcal O_{X,x}} is an isomorphism and we show f is étale. Write \mathcal O_y=\mathcal O_{Y,y} and \mathcal O_x=\mathcal O_{X,x}, with maximal ideals \mathfrak m_y and \mathfrak m_x. First, since \mathcal O_x\to\widehat{\mathcal O_x} is faithfully flat (in particular is a pure subring), we have:

\begin{aligned} \mathfrak m_y\mathcal O_x &=\mathfrak m_y\widehat{\mathcal O_x}\cap\mathcal O_x\\ &=(\mathfrak m_y\widehat{\mathcal O_y})\widehat{\mathcal O_x}\cap\mathcal O_x\\ &=\mathfrak m_x\widehat{\mathcal O_x}\cap\mathcal O_x\\ &=\mathfrak m_x. \end{aligned}

Hence, f is unramified by the local criterion: the maximal ideal is generated from the base and the residue extension is separable. Separability is trivial here because the isomorphism of completions identifies the residue fields.

Now, that \mathcal O_y\to\mathcal O_x is flat follows from (1) the assumption that the induced map on completion is an isomorphism, so \widehat{\mathcal O_x}\simeq\widehat{\mathcal O_y} is flat over \mathcal O_y, and (2) the fact that flatness descends through the faithfully flat extension \mathcal O_x\to\widehat{\mathcal O_x}. These conclusions hold at every x, so f is étale.
