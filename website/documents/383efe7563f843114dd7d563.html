Statement

For Euclidean-plane points with polar coordinates (r_1,\theta_1),(r_2,\theta_2), where r_j\ge0 and \theta_j\in\mathbb R, their distance s satisfies s^2=r_1^2+r_2^2-2r_1r_2\cos(\theta_1-\theta_2), or equivalently s=\sqrt{r_1^2+r_2^2-2r_1r_2\cos(\theta_1-\theta_2)}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement uses polar coordinates, Euclidean distance, cosine and square roots, all elementary coordinate geometry.

Proof 1: Expand the Cartesian distance after polar substitution

The formulas can be discovered by using Pythagoras's theorem with the equations relating the curvilinear coordinates to Cartesian coordinates. For example, the polar coordinates (r, θ) can be introduced as:

{\displaystyle x=r\cos \theta ,\quad y=r\sin \theta .}

Then two points with locations (r1, θ1) and (r2, θ2) are separated by a distance s:

{\displaystyle s^{2}=(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}+(y_{1}-y_{2})^{2}=(r_{1}\cos \theta _{1}-r_{2}\cos \theta _{2})^{2}+(r_{1}\sin \theta _{1}-r_{2}\sin \theta _{2})^{2}.}

Performing the squares and combining terms, the Pythagorean formula for distance in Cartesian coordinates produces the separation in polar coordinates as:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}s^{2}&=r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}-2r_{1}r_{2}\left(\cos \theta _{1}\cos \theta _{2}+\sin \theta _{1}\sin \theta _{2}\right)\\&=r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}-2r_{1}r_{2}\cos \left(\theta _{1}-\theta _{2}\right)\\&=r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}-2r_{1}r_{2}\cos \Delta \theta ,\end{aligned}}}

using the cosine-difference identity, with \Delta\theta=\theta_1-\theta_2.

Level justification

Substitute polar coordinates into the Cartesian squared distance, expand and use the cosine-difference identity. Elementary coordinate geometry and trigonometry suffice.

Proof 2: Apply the law of cosines to the radial segments — outline

It is also possible to compute the distance for points given by polar coordinates. If the polar coordinates of {\displaystyle p} are {\displaystyle (r,\theta )} and the polar coordinates of {\displaystyle q} are {\displaystyle (s,\psi )}, then their distance is given by the law of cosines:

{\displaystyle d(p,q)={\sqrt {r^{2}+s^{2}-2rs\cos(\theta -\psi )}}.}
