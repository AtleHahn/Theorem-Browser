Statement

Let \mathcal C be a category, \sharp:\mathcal C\times\mathcal C\to\mathcal C a bifunctor, and \Delta:\mathcal C\to\mathcal C\times\mathcal C the diagonal functor. Let \delta:\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal C}\Rightarrow\sharp\circ\Delta be a natural transformation and t,A,C objects. Suppose F:A\mathbin\sharp A\to C and \sigma:C\to C are morphisms, and there exists a_0:t\to A such that for every a:t\to A,

\sigma\circ F\circ\delta_A\circ a=F\circ(a_0\mathbin\sharp a)\circ\delta_t.

Then c=F\circ\delta_A\circ a_0:t\to C satisfies \sigma\circ c=c.

Proof 1: Verify the supplied diagonal witness — outline

Use the source's specified witness c=F\circ\delta_A\circ a_0. Taking a=a_0 in the hypothesis gives

\sigma\circ c=F\circ(a_0\mathbin\sharp a_0)\circ\delta_t.

Naturality of \delta at a_0:t\to A says (a_0\mathbin\sharp a_0)\circ\delta_t=\delta_A\circ a_0. Hence \sigma\circ c=F\circ\delta_A\circ a_0=c.
