Statement

Let s:\mathbb R\to\mathbb C be Schwartz, S(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb R}s(x)e^{-2\pi i\xi x}\,dx, and P>0. Then \sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}s(x+nP)=\frac1P\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}S(k/P)e^{2\pi ikx/P} for every real x, with absolute convergence.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Calculus supplies improper Fourier integrals and analysis supplies convergent series. Schwartz means smooth with each derivative decaying faster than every inverse power; this is a direct quantified growth condition. The transform is explicitly given, with no distribution or L2 completion involved.

Proof 1: Compute the periodization Fourier coefficients — outline

First, the periodization s_P(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}s(x+nP) converges in L^1 norm to an L^1([0,P]) function which is periodic on \mathbb R, and therefore integrable on any interval of length P. We must show that its Fourier series coefficients are \frac1P S(k/P).

Proceeding from the definition of the Fourier coefficients we have:

\begin{aligned} S[k]&:=\frac1P\int_0^P s_P(x)e^{-i2\pi(k/P)x}\,dx\\ &=\frac1P\int_0^P\left(\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}s(x+nP)\right)e^{-i2\pi(k/P)x}\,dx\\ &=\frac1P\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\int_0^P s(x+nP)e^{-i2\pi(k/P)x}\,dx, \end{aligned}

where the interchange of summation with integration is justified by dominated convergence. With a change of variables \tau=x+nP, this becomes

\begin{aligned} S[k]&=\frac1P\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} \int_{nP}^{(n+1)P}s(\tau)e^{-i2\pi(k/P)\tau} \underbrace{e^{i2\pi kn}}_{1}\,d\tau\\ &=\frac1P\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}s(\tau)e^{-i2\pi(k/P)\tau}\,d\tau =\frac1P S(k/P). \end{aligned}

Here S[k] denotes a Fourier coefficient of s_P, whereas S(\xi) denotes the Fourier transform of s. This identifies the possibly divergent Fourier series for general L^1 data.

Finally, if s_P has an absolutely convergent Fourier series, the resulting identity holds almost everywhere. This is the case, in particular, when s is a Schwartz function. Rapid decay gives a continuous periodization and an absolutely convergent Fourier series; Fourier uniqueness and continuity give

s_P(x)=\frac1P\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}S(k/P)e^{2\pi i(k/P)x}

at every x.
