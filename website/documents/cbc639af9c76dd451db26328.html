Statement

Let Q_d be the graph on \{0,1\}^d, with two vertices adjacent exactly when one bit differs. For S\subseteq\{0,1\}^d, let \Gamma(S) be the vertices having a neighbor in S. For integers d\ge1 and 0\le r<d, if |S|\ge\sum_{i=0}^r\binom di, then |S\cup\Gamma(S)|\ge\sum_{i=0}^{r+1}\binom di.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The graph and neighborhood are defined by bit strings, and the threshold is a finite binomial sum. Elementary subsets and counting suffice.

Proof 1: Harper minimization and the next Hamming layer — outline

Harper’s theorem minimizes the vertex boundary by Hamming balls. A full Hamming ball of radius r consists of bit strings of weight at most r, and its closed neighborhood is the ball of radius r+1. Applying Harper’s theorem at the radius-r cardinality gives the displayed radius-(r+1) lower bound; for larger S the closed neighborhood cannot decrease on passing from a subset of that cardinality to S.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Apply Harper at the full-ball size, count the next layer and use monotonicity for larger sets. Elementary finite binary strings and binomial counts suffice.
