Statement

A simple undirected graph has a vertex set V and edges that are unordered pairs of distinct vertices. A proper k-coloring is a map V\to\{1,\ldots,k\} assigning different values to adjacent vertices. Assume choice. If a graph has an orientation with at most k outgoing edges at each vertex, for an integer k\ge0, then it is (2k+1)-colorable.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The graph and coloring conditions are explicitly defined. Undergraduate sets and functions suffice for infinite vertex sets, and the axiom of choice is one set-selection principle. Understanding the degree bound and conclusion needs no advanced graph theory.

Proof 1: Apply finite coloring and graph compactness

Every finite subgraph inherits the outdegree bound and is (2k+1)-colorable by the finite recursive coloring theorem. De Bruijn–Erdős therefore gives a (2k+1)-coloring of the whole graph.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Each finite subgraph inherits the outdegree bound and thus the finite coloring theorem applies. The explicitly stated compactness theorem then gives a coloring of the arbitrary graph. Sets, finite graph coloring and a stated finite-to-infinite implication suffice; neither a proof from choice nor general logical compactness is required.
