Statement

Let X_t be the simple symmetric random walk with independent steps \pm1 of equal probability, starting at an integer 0\le a\le m, where m\ge1 is an integer. Let \tau be its first hit of \{0,m\}, and assume \mathbb E\tau<\infty. Then the probability of stopping at m is a/m.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The random walk is specified by independent fair steps and the stopping event is hitting either endpoint. Expectation is a weighted average of integer waiting times. These concrete elementary-probability definitions suffice for the exit-probability assertion.

Proof 1: Endpoint expectation equation

The walk is a martingale with increments of absolute value one. The assumed finite expected stopping time allows optional stopping, giving \mathbb E X_\tau=a. If p is the probability of the endpoint m, then a=pm+(1-p)0, so p=a/m.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

For this explicit coin walk, the conditional next position averages to the current position. Apply the supplied bounded-increment optional-stopping criterion and solve the two-endpoint expectation equation. Stopping times and the fair-game conditional-average definition are a small extension of undergraduate probability; no general conditional-expectation construction is needed.
