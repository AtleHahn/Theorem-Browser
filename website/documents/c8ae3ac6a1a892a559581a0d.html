Statement

For a subset A of a complete metric space (X,d), define \alpha(A)=\inf\{r\geq0:A\text{ is covered by finitely many subsets of diameter at most }r\}, with infimum of the empty admissible family equal to infinity. If A is compact then α(A)=0.

Level

Level justification

Proof 1: Use finite subcovers of small balls

If A is itself compact, then \alpha(A)=0, since every cover of A by open balls of arbitrarily small diameter will have a finite subcover. For the empty compact set the empty finite cover gives the same conclusion.

Level

Level justification

Cover the compact set by balls of arbitrarily small radius and take finite subcovers. The supplied diameter-infimum definition then gives zero. Basic metric compactness suffices.
