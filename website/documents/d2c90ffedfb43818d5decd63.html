Statement

Let (X,\omega) be a Kähler manifold. Every point of X has a neighborhood U and a smooth function f:U\to\mathbb C with \omega|_U=i\partial\bar\partial f.

Proof 1: Local Poincaré and Dolbeault potentials in bidegree (1,1)

A Kähler form \omega is a closed (1,1)-form. Suppose {\displaystyle \omega } is {\displaystyle d}-closed. Then by the Poincaré lemma there exists an open neighbourhood {\displaystyle U} of any point {\displaystyle p\in X} and a form {\displaystyle \gamma \in \Omega ^{1}(U)} such that {\displaystyle \omega =d\gamma }. Now writing {\displaystyle \gamma =\gamma '+\gamma ''} for {\displaystyle \gamma '\in \Omega ^{0,1}(U)} and {\displaystyle \gamma ''\in \Omega ^{1,0}(U)} and comparing the bidegrees in \omega=d\gamma=(\partial+\bar\partial)\gamma implies that {\displaystyle {\bar {\partial }}\gamma '=0} and {\displaystyle \partial \gamma ''=0} and that {\displaystyle \omega =\partial \gamma '+{\bar {\partial }}\gamma ''}. After possibly shrinking the size of the open neighbourhood {\displaystyle U}, the Dolbeault–Grothendieck lemma may be applied to {\displaystyle \gamma '} and {\displaystyle {\overline {\gamma ''}}} (the latter because {\displaystyle {\overline {\partial \gamma ''}}={\bar {\partial }}({\overline {\gamma ''}})=0}) to obtain local forms {\displaystyle \eta ',\eta ''\in C^\infty(U,\mathbb C)} such that {\displaystyle \gamma '={\bar {\partial }}\eta '} and {\displaystyle {\overline {\gamma ''}}={\bar {\partial }}\eta ''}. Noting then that {\displaystyle \gamma ''=\partial {\overline {\eta ''}}} this completes the proof as {\displaystyle \omega =\partial {\bar {\partial }}\eta '+{\bar {\partial }}\partial {\overline {\eta ''}}=i\partial {\bar {\partial }}f } where {\displaystyle f =-i\eta '+i{\overline {\eta ''}}}.
