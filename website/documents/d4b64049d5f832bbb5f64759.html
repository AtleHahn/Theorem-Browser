Statement

There is a bijection \mathbb R^2\to\mathbb C, (a,b)\mapsto a+bi; consequently |\mathbb C|=|\mathbb R|=\mathfrak c.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Undergraduate set theory supplies bijections and countability; complex numbers are pairs of reals. Transcendence is explicitly defined by failure to satisfy an integer polynomial. Understanding the cardinality comparison needs no transcendence proof techniques.

Proof 1: Real and imaginary parts

By Cantor's theorem on the cardinality of Euclidean space, |\mathbb R^2|=\mathfrak c. By definition, any c\in\mathbb C can be uniquely expressed as a+bi for some a,b\in\mathbb R. We therefore define the bijection

\begin{aligned} f:\mathbb R^2&\longrightarrow\mathbb C,\\ (a,b)&\longmapsto a+bi. \end{aligned}
