Proof 1: Substitute powers of q in the triple product — outline

Use the imported Jacobi triple-product identity: For complex x,y with |x|<1 and y≠0, \prod_{m=1}^{\infty}(1-x^{2m})(1+x^{2m-1}y^2)(1+x^{2m-1}y^{-2})=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}x^{n^2}y^{2n}. At x=0 the n=0 term is 1.

For q≠0 choose a square root s of q, and use x=qs and y²=−s in the Jacobi triple product. Its three product factors become 1-q^{3m}, 1-q^{3m-1} and 1-q^{3m-2}, collectively giving all positive powers. The sum becomes \sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}(-1)^nq^{(3n^2+n)/2}. Reindex n↦−n to obtain the displayed series. For q=0 both sides equal 1.
