Statement

Let G be a locally compact Hausdorff topological group. For any distinct elements g,h\in G, there exist a nonzero complex Hilbert space H and a strongly continuous irreducible unitary representation

\rho:G\longrightarrow U(H)

such that \rho(g)\ne\rho(h).

Here U(H) is the group of surjective linear operators preserving the inner product, and a representation is a group homomorphism. Strong continuity means that s\mapsto\rho(s)v is continuous for each v\in H. Irreducibility means that the only closed linear subspaces invariant under every \rho(s) are \{0\} and H. The Hilbert space is not required to be finite-dimensional.
