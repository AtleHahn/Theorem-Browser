Statement

For each fixed positive integer k, some language in S_2^P has no Boolean circuit family of size O(n^k). Here S_2^P consists of languages with a polynomial-time predicate R(z,u,v) and polynomial length bounds such that yes instances admit \exists u\forall v\,R(z,u,v) and no instances admit \exists v\forall u\,\neg R(z,u,v).

Proof 1: Use the stronger collapse in Kannan’s case split (outline)

Use the same source case split as for Kannan’s theorem. If SAT has no polynomial-size circuit family, it already has no size-O(n^k) circuit family and belongs to \mathsf{NP}\subseteq S_2^P. Otherwise, the stronger Karp–Lipton collapse gives \mathsf{PH}=S_2^P. The source-stated diagonalization result provides a \Sigma_4^P language outside \mathsf{SIZE}(n^k); after this collapse it belongs to S_2^P, proving the claim.
