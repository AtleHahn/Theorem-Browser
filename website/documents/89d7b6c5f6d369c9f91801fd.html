The Euclidean Pythagorean formula fails on the sphere

A spherical triangle can have three right angles and violate the Euclidean Pythagorean relation. This supplies a concrete reminder that familiar geometrical truths depend on the geometry assumed, rather than following from an unrestricted intuition of space.

Statement

A geodesic triangle bounding an octant of the unit sphere has all three angles right and all three sides \pi/2, and does not satisfy a^2+b^2=c^2.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The assertion concerns great-circle arcs, their angles and lengths or a half-turn on a sphere. These concrete spatial-geometric constructions and trigonometric expressions can be understood with school geometry. This rating concerns understanding the statement, independently of the difficulty of its proof.

Proof 1: Compute the three quarter-circle lengths

The sides are quarter arcs of unit great circles, hence each has length \pi/2, and their meeting angles are right. Thus a^2+b^2=2c^2>c^2.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Compute quarter-circle lengths and compare their squares. Great-circle arcs and perpendicular coordinate planes require only school solid geometry here.
