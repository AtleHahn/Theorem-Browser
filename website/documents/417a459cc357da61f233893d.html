Statement

Let H be a real RKHS on X with evaluation representers K_x and K(x,y)=\langle K_x,K_y\rangle. Then K(x,y)=K(y,x), and for all finite x_1,\ldots,x_n\in X and c_1,\ldots,c_n\in\mathbb R, \sum_{i,j}c_ic_jK(x_i,x_j)\ge0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

RKHS adds completeness of an inner-product function space and bounded point evaluation, with the representers supplied. These are related short definitions extending undergraduate linear algebra and metric analysis, not an assumed functional-analysis course.

Proof 1: Write the Gram sum as a squared norm

From the definition K(x,y)=\langle K_x,K_y\rangle_H it is easy to see that {\displaystyle K:X\times X\to \mathbb {R} } is both symmetric and positive semidefinite, i.e.

{\displaystyle \sum _{i,j=1}^{n}c_{i}c_{j}K(x_{i},x_{j})=\sum _{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\left\langle K_{x_{i}},\sum _{j=1}^{n}c_{j}K_{x_{j}}\right\rangle _{H}=\left\langle \sum _{i=1}^{n}c_{i}K_{x_{i}},\sum _{j=1}^{n}c_{j}K_{x_{j}}\right\rangle _{H}=\left\|\sum _{i=1}^{n}c_{i}K_{x_{i}}\right\|_{H}^{2}\geq 0}

for every {\displaystyle n\in \mathbb {N} ,x_{1},\dots ,x_{n}\in X,{\text{ and }}c_{1},\dots ,c_{n}\in \mathbb {R} .}

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The full RKHS assumption means a complete inner-product space of functions with continuous point evaluations represented by K_x; these linked definitions extend undergraduate inner products, completeness and linear maps. Given those representatives, symmetry and the finite Gram sum follow from a squared norm.
