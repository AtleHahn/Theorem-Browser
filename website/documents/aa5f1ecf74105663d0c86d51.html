Statement

Let K be a number field with ring of integers \mathcal O_K, let \mathfrak p be a nonzero prime ideal, and let \zeta\in\mathcal O_K be a primitive nth root of unity, where n\ge1 and n\notin\mathfrak p. Put q=|\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak p|. For each \alpha\in\mathcal O_K\setminus\mathfrak p, there is a unique nth root of unity \eta\in\{1,\zeta,\ldots,\zeta^{n-1}\} with \eta\equiv\alpha^{(q-1)/n}\pmod{\mathfrak p}.

Proof 1: Existence and uniqueness of the power residue symbol — outline

We use the norm congruence q\equiv1\pmod n and the fact that no two distinct nth roots of unity can be congruent modulo \mathfrak p.

There is an analogue of Fermat's theorem in \mathcal O_K. If \alpha\in\mathcal O_K and \alpha\notin\mathfrak p, then

\alpha^{q-1}\equiv1\pmod{\mathfrak p},

and since q\equiv1\pmod n,

\alpha^{(q-1)/n}\equiv\zeta^s\pmod{\mathfrak p}

is well-defined and congruent to a unique nth root of unity \zeta^s. As in the source, the polynomial-root bound that identifies all the nth roots in the residue field with these n distinct roots is implicit in this last step.
