Statement

For a nonzero real polynomial f, let V(f) count sign changes between successive nonzero coefficients in descending exponent order, and let Z(f) count positive real roots with multiplicity (a root has multiplicity k when (x-r)^k is its exact power factor). If V(f) is zero or one, then Z(f)=V(f).

Level
High school only
Level justification

The claim says that a coefficient list with zero or one variation determines the positive-root count exactly.

Proof 1: Exclude a positive even deficit

By Descartes’ rule, Z(f) is nonnegative and equals V(f) minus a nonnegative even integer. For V(f)\in\{0,1\} the only possibility is equality.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The nonnegative root count cannot be zero or one minus a positive even number, so the deficit vanishes.
