Statement

A relation R on a set X is reflexive if xRx for every x\in X, symmetric if xRy implies yRx, and transitive if xRy and yRz imply xRz. An equivalence relation has all three properties. The three axioms are independent: for each one there is a relation satisfying the other two and failing that axiom. Here examples may be taken on \mathbb N_0=\{0,1,2,\ldots\} and on \mathbb Z.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The three relation axioms and their independence are explicitly stated, and the witnesses use elementary integer arithmetic.

Proof 1: Independence of the equivalence-relation axioms — outline

For reflexivity and transitivity without symmetry, take \leq on \mathbb N_0.

For symmetry and transitivity without reflexivity, take aRb if and only if ab\ne0 on \mathbb N_0.

For reflexivity and symmetry without transitivity, take aRb on \mathbb Z if and only if a-b is divisible by at least one of 2 or 3.

These three examples establish independence.
