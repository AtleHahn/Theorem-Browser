Statement

For a measure space (\Omega,\mathcal F,\mu), L^1(\mu) is the space of integrable scalar functions modulo equality almost everywhere, with norm \|f\|_1=\int|f|\,d\mu, and L^\infty(\mu) uses the essential-supremum norm. For an operator defined on L^1, the condition on its L^\infty norm means that it maps L^1\cap L^\infty into L^\infty with the stated bound on that intersection. For every \varepsilon>0, there exist a measure space (\Omega,\mathcal F,\mu), a linear operator T:L^1(\mu)\to L^1(\mu), and f\in L^1(\mu) such that

\|Tg\|_1\le\|g\|_1\quad(g\in L^1),\qquad \|Tg\|_\infty\le(1+\varepsilon)\|g\|_\infty\quad(g\in L^1\cap L^\infty),

but the averages n^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}T^jf fail to converge to a finite limit on a set of positive measure.
