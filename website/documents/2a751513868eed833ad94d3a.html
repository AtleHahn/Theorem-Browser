Statement

Let G be a locally compact Hausdorff group. For every strongly continuous unitary representation \rho:G\to U(H) on a complex Hilbert space and h,k\in H, let c_{h,k}^{\rho}(g)=\langle h,\rho(g)k\rangle, taking the inner product linear in its second argument. Let \mathcal A be the set of all such coefficient functions, allowing all representations. For every compact K\subseteq G, the restrictions \mathcal A|_K are uniformly dense in C(K;\mathbb C).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Unitary representation theory of locally compact groups is beyond undergraduate calculus and linear algebra.

Proof 1: Apply Stone–Weierstrass to the coefficient algebra (outline)

The set of all matrix coefficients for all unitary representations is closed under scalar multiplication (because we can replace {\displaystyle k\to \lambda k}), addition (because of direct sum representations), multiplication (because of tensor representations) and complex conjugation (because of the complex conjugate representations).

The constant function 1 is a coefficient of the trivial one-dimensional representation.

The Gel'fand–Raikov theorem states that the points of G are separated by its irreducible unitary representations, i.e. for any two distinct group elements g,h\in G there exist a Hilbert space H and an irreducible unitary representation \rho:G\to U(H) such that \rho(g)\neq\rho(h). The matrix elements thus separate points. It then follows from the complex Stone–Weierstrass theorem that on every compact subset of the group, the matrix elements are uniformly dense in the space of continuous complex-valued functions.
