Statement

If independent random vectors X,Y\in\mathbb R^n have log-concave probability densities, then X+Y has a log-concave density.

Proof 1: Change to sum-and-difference coordinates and marginalize

We use the fact that marginalization preserves log-concavity of probability densities. A nonnegative function h is log-concave if h((1-\lambda)u+\lambda v)\ge h(u)^{1-\lambda}h(v)^\lambda for 0<\lambda<1.

To see the preservation of log-concavity by independent sums, suppose that X and Y are independent random variables with log-concave distribution. Since the product of two log-concave functions is log-concave, the joint distribution of (X,Y) is also log-concave. Log-concavity is preserved by affine changes of coordinates, so the distribution of (X + Y, X − Y) is log-concave as well. Since the distribution of X+Y is a marginal over the joint distribution of (X + Y, X − Y), we conclude that X + Y has a log-concave distribution.
