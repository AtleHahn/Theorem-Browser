Statement

Let V be finite-dimensional over a field K, and let 0\ne\mathfrak g\subseteq\operatorname{End}_K(V) be a Lie algebra consisting of nilpotent operators. Every maximal proper Lie subalgebra \mathfrak h of \mathfrak g is an ideal of codimension one.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra supplies nilpotent maps, flags and codimension. A Lie subalgebra here is a linear space closed under the commutator; a Lie ideal is closed under brackets with the ambient algebra. These explicit finite-dimensional algebraic conditions, and the supplied lower-central sequence, are a small extension without Lie groups or root systems.

Proof 1: An annihilated adjoint quotient class — outline

Let \mathfrak h be any maximal proper subalgebra of \mathfrak g. We claim it is an ideal of codimension one. For each {\displaystyle X\in {\mathfrak {h}}}, it is easy to check that (1) {\displaystyle \operatorname {ad} (X)} induces a linear endomorphism {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {g}}/{\mathfrak {h}}\to {\mathfrak {g}}/{\mathfrak {h}}} and (2) this induced map is nilpotent (in fact, {\displaystyle \operatorname {ad} (X)} is nilpotent as {\displaystyle X} is nilpotent; using the standard nilpotent-commutator fact). Thus, by the common-annihilator theorem (a Lie algebra of nilpotent operators on a nonzero finite-dimensional space has a nonzero common kernel) applied to the Lie subalgebra of {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {gl}}({\mathfrak {g}}/{\mathfrak {h}})} generated by {\displaystyle \operatorname {ad} ({\mathfrak {h}})}, there exists a nonzero vector v in {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {g}}/{\mathfrak {h}}} such that {\displaystyle \operatorname {ad} (X)(v)=0} for each {\displaystyle X\in {\mathfrak {h}}}. That is to say, if {\displaystyle v=[Y]} for some Y in {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {g}}} but not in {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {h}}}, then {\displaystyle [X,Y]=\operatorname {ad} (X)(Y)\in {\mathfrak {h}}} for every {\displaystyle X\in {\mathfrak {h}}}. But then the subspace {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {h}}'\subset {\mathfrak {g}}} spanned by {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {h}}} and Y is a Lie subalgebra in which {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {h}}} is an ideal of codimension one. Hence, by maximality, {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {h}}'={\mathfrak {g}}}. This proves the claim.
