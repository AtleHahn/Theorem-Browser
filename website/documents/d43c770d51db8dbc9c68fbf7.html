Statement

A finite-dimensional Lie algebra \mathfrak g over any field is nilpotent if and only if every \operatorname{ad}X:Y\mapsto[X,Y] is nilpotent. Here C^0\mathfrak g=\mathfrak g, C^{i+1}\mathfrak g=[\mathfrak g,C^i\mathfrak g], and nilpotent means C^k\mathfrak g=0 for some k.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement uses the lower central series and adjoint action of an abstract Lie algebra over a field.

Proof 1: Apply the flag theorem to the adjoint representation — outline

Indeed, if {\displaystyle \operatorname {ad} ({\mathfrak {g}})} consists of nilpotent operators, then by Engel’s simultaneous strict triangularization theorem applied to the algebra {\displaystyle \operatorname {ad} ({\mathfrak {g}})\subset {\mathfrak {gl}}({\mathfrak {g}})}, there exists a flag {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {g}}={\mathfrak {g}}_{0}\supset {\mathfrak {g}}_{1}\supset \cdots \supset {\mathfrak {g}}_{n}=0} such that {\displaystyle [{\mathfrak {g}},{\mathfrak {g}}_{i}]\subset {\mathfrak {g}}_{i+1}}. Since {\displaystyle C^{i}{\mathfrak {g}}\subset {\mathfrak {g}}_{i}}, this implies {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {g}}} is nilpotent. (The converse follows straightforwardly from the definition.)

Level justification

The proof relates the adjoint representation, the Lie lower central series and an invariant flag. Retain beyond undergraduate provisionally because their combination goes beyond a confidently small extension of undergraduate linear algebra.
