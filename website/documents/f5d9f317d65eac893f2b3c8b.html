Statement

For positive integers a>b, the Euclidean algorithm takes at most 5h remainder divisions, including the final zero remainder, where h is the number of decimal digits of b.

Proof 1: Combine the Fibonacci lower bound with logarithms — outline

Use the Fibonacci input bound: if the algorithm requires N steps for a>b>0, then b is greater than or equal to F_{N+1}, which in turn is greater than or equal to \varphi^{N-1}, where \varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2 is the golden ratio. Since b\ge\varphi^{N-1}, then N-1\le\log_\varphi b. Since \log_{10}\varphi>1/5, for N>1,

\frac{N-1}{5}<\log_{10}\varphi\,\log_\varphi b =\log_{10}b<h.

Thus N-1<5h, and integrality gives N\le5h. The case N=1 is immediate.

This retains the source's Fibonacci and logarithm argument, correcting its intermediate conclusion N\le5\log_{10}b, which does not follow.
