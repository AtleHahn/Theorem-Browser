Statement

For a positive real \alpha and integer n\geq1, place cuts at the distinct fractional parts \{\alpha\},\{2\alpha\},\ldots,\{n\alpha\} in [0,1], together with endpoints 0,1. The resulting nonempty subintervals have at most three distinct lengths.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement involves ordinary real numbers, integer parts and lengths of finitely many intervals.

Proof 1: Identify the endpoints of the interval

Identify the endpoints of the unit interval to form a circle of circumference 1. The cuts together with 0 are consecutive rotation points 0,\alpha,\ldots,n\alpha modulo 1, with rotation angle 2\pi\alpha. Apply the external three-gap theorem to these n+1 rotation points. Because 0 is itself marked, cutting the circle there yields precisely the interval partition, with the same gap lengths.

Level justification

Join the interval endpoints, mark zero and apply the supplied circle gap bound; cutting at the marked zero preserves gap lengths. Elementary fractional parts and circle geometry suffice.
