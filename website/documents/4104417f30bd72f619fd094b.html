Statement

A finite connected bridgeless undirected graph admits a strong orientation obtained by a depth-first search: orient tree edges away from the root and each nontree edge from descendant to ancestor. This construction runs in time linear in the number of vertices plus edges.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Finite graphs and paths are elementary discrete objects. Depth-first search follows an unexplored branch until it must backtrack; the edge directions are specified explicitly. Strong orientation means directed paths between every ordered pair, a small finite-graph extension.

Proof 1: Direct tree edges downward and back edges upward — outline

A strong orientation of a given connected bridgeless undirected graph may be found in linear time by performing a depth-first search of the graph, orienting all edges in the depth-first search tree away from the tree root, and orienting all the remaining edges (which must necessarily connect an ancestor and a descendant in the depth-first search tree) from the descendant to the ancestor.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the depth-first ancestor classification and orient tree and back edges as prescribed. Undergraduate graph algorithms suffice to understand the traversal and linear running time; the source gives the recipe without the full strong-connectivity verification.
