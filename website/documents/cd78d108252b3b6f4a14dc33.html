Statement

Fix real parameters \alpha and \beta with \beta\geq0. Then

\sum_{k=0}^n\frac{P_k^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)}{P_k^{(\beta,\alpha)}(1)}\geq0 \quad\text{for every integer }n\geq0\text{ and every }x\in[-1,1]

if and only if \alpha+\beta\geq-2.

The Jacobi polynomials here are defined by the finite expression

P_k^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)=\sum_{s=0}^k\binom{k+\alpha}{k-s}\binom{k+\beta}{s} \left(\frac{x-1}{2}\right)^s\left(\frac{x+1}{2}\right)^{k-s}.

For real u and integer j\geq0, \binom uj=u(u-1)\cdots(u-j+1)/j!, with \binom u0=1; zeroth powers are interpreted as 1. In particular P_k^{(\beta,\alpha)}(1)=\binom{k+\beta}{k}>0.

Level
High school only
Level justification

With the explicit finite-sum definition, the actual claim involves only real arithmetic, products, finite sums, integer indices and inequalities. No orthogonality or hypergeometric theory is required to understand this formulation.
