Statement

Let (C_k,\partial_k) be an exact chain complex of finitely generated free abelian groups. For each k, write B_k=\operatorname{im}\partial_{k+1}. Then C_k\cong B_k\oplus B_{k-1}. Under these splittings, the restriction of \partial_k to the second summand is an isomorphism onto the first summand of C_{k-1}, so suitable integral bases pair the nonzero differential generators with coefficients \pm1.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory group theory and linear algebra supply free abelian groups as integer lattices, homomorphisms, kernels, images and direct sums. Here a chain complex is a sequence of integer matrices and exactness says that each image equals the next kernel. Learning this notation and interpreting the displayed splitting as paired lattice bases is a small, concrete extension within about 30 minutes; understanding general homology or derived constructions is unnecessary.

Proof 1: An acyclic finite free chain complex splits into paired summands (outline)

The chain complex is exact. Thus

C_k\cong\operatorname{coker}\partial_{k+1}\oplus\operatorname{im}\partial_{k+1}.

Indeed, exactness identifies \operatorname{coker}\partial_{k+1} with \operatorname{im}\partial_k, which is a subgroup of the free abelian group C_{k-1} and hence is free; the quotient map therefore splits.

Then \partial_k, which is an integer matrix, restricts to an invertible morphism between the paired summands. It can thus be diagonalized via elementary integral row operations and must have only \pm1 on the diagonal because it is invertible. Thus the basis elements in these summands are paired, with coefficients \pm1.
