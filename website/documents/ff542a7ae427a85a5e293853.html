Statement

Let C be the boundary curve of a bounded convex region in the Euclidean plane, with constant width w>0: for every direction, the distance between its two parallel supporting lines is w. Then the perimeter of C is \pi w.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Constant width is explicitly described by the distance between parallel supporting lines. The conclusion relates ordinary plane perimeter and width and needs no calculus or advanced convex-geometry machinery to understand. This rating concerns understanding the statement, independently of the difficulty of its proof.

Mathematical Field
Convex and discrete geometry
Required topics

Review notes

Proof 1: Add the perimeters of a body and its half-turn

Let K be the convex region bounded by C. The Minkowski sum is A+B=\{a+b:a\in A,b\in B\}.

One proof of the theorem uses the properties of Minkowski sums. If K is a body of constant width w, then the Minkowski sum of K and its 180° rotation is a disk with radius w and perimeter 2πw. The Minkowski sum acts linearly on the perimeters of convex bodies, so the perimeter of K must be half the perimeter of this disk, which is πw as the theorem states.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The argument uses two supplied facts about planar convex bodies. A Minkowski sum is explicitly the set of vector sums, so undergraduate vector geometry and elementary set notation suffice to understand it. Perimeter additivity and the half-turn identity then give 2 perimeter(K)=2pi w. The extra convex-geometric vocabulary is a small concrete extension; no theory of mixed volumes or proof of the imported identities is needed.

Proof 2: Crofton comparison with a circle

Alternatively, the theorem follows immediately from the Crofton formula in integral geometry according to which the length of any curve equals, up to a fixed normalization, the measure of the set of lines that cross the curve, multiplied by their numbers of crossings. Any two convex curves that have the same constant width are crossed by sets of lines with the same measure, and therefore they have the same length.

For a circle, the width is the same as the diameter; a circle of width w has perimeter \pi w.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

For the needed planar Crofton formula, parameterize a line by its normal angle theta in [0,pi) and signed distance p. For each theta, the p values crossing a convex body form an interval of length w, and non-tangent crossings have multiplicity two. The supplied formula therefore gives length=(1/2) integral_0^pi 2w dtheta=pi w. This is a small extension of multivariable calculus and planar geometry; no general invariant-measure theory is required for this explicit two-parameter application.
