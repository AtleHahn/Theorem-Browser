Statement

Let f:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C, and let g(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}\binom zn\Delta^nf(0) be a Newton series converging at every z\in\mathbb C, where \Delta f(k)=f(k+1)-f(k) and iterates define \Delta^n, and \binom z0=1, \binom zn=z(z-1)\cdots(z-n+1)/n! for n\ge1. Suppose h=f-g is entire and, for some C>0, \tau\in\mathbb R and c<\pi, satisfies |h(z)|\le Ce^{\tau|z|} for all complex z and |h(iy)|\le Ce^{c|y|} for all real y. Then f=g.

Level justification

Proof 1: Integer interpolation followed by Carlson — outline

Integer interpolation

Consider the Newton series

g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom zn\Delta^nf(0),

where \binom zn is the binomial coefficient and \Delta^nf(0) is the nth forward difference, as defined in the statement. By construction, one has f(k)=g(k) for all nonnegative integers k, so that the difference h(k)=f(k)-g(k)=0. The source leaves the finite Newton interpolation verification implicit.

Apply Carlson’s theorem

This is one of the conditions of Carlson's theorem: an entire function with the two exponential bounds in the statement and zeros at every nonnegative integer is identically zero. Since h obeys the other conditions by hypothesis, h is identically zero, and f=g.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use finite Newton interpolation at nonnegative integers, then apply the explicitly stated entire-function uniqueness criterion. Undergraduate complex analysis, finite differences and power series suffice; the exact growth hypotheses are given, and no proof of Carlson theorem is required.
