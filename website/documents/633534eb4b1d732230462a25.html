Statement

Let r>0 and let \mathcal P be any collection of closed balls of radius r in \mathbb R^3 with pairwise disjoint interiors. Let E be their union. Define its upper asymptotic packing density by

\overline\delta(\mathcal P)=\limsup_{R\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{vol}(E\cap B(0,R))}{\operatorname{vol}(B(0,R))},

where B(0,R) is the Euclidean ball of radius R about the origin and \operatorname{vol} is three-dimensional volume. Then

\overline\delta(\mathcal P)\leq\frac{\pi}{3\sqrt2}.

The bound is attained by the face-centered cubic and hexagonal close packings: congruent balls are placed in triangular lattice layers, each successive layer resting in hollows of the previous layer, with layer positions repeating respectively in the patterns ABCABC\ldots and ABAB\ldots.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Volume and Euclidean geometry are elementary; the limsup definition of density adds standard undergraduate analysis. No computational proof or lattice classification is needed to understand the bound.

Proof 1: Finite packing bounds and the Flyspeck proof (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (§3 pp.3–5; §4.2 pp.5–6; computational components in §§5, 7, 9.).

Normalize the ball radius to 1. Every ball meeting B(0,R) has center in B(0,R+1), so the finite-container count bounds the occupied-volume ratio by \pi/(3\sqrt2)+O(1/R). For either close packing, the area per center in a triangular layer is 2\sqrt3 and layer spacing is \sqrt{8/3}; the volume per ball is 4\sqrt2, giving the same density.
