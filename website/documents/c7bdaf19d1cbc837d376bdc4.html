Statement

Let f be a nonzero entire function of finite order \rho, and let n(r) count its nonzero zeros of modulus less than r, with multiplicity. For every \varepsilon>0, n(r)=O(r^{\rho+\varepsilon}) as r\to\infty. If there are infinitely many zeros, their counting exponent \alpha=\limsup_{r\to\infty}\log n(r)/\log r satisfies \alpha\le\rho. Here the finite order \rho\ge0 means \rho=\limsup_{r\to\infty}\log\log\max(e,M_f(r))/\log r, where M_f(r)=\max_{|z|=r}|f(z)|.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Entire means complex differentiable everywhere, and finite order is a growth condition given by a limsup of logarithms. Real analysis supplies zero counts, series, growth estimates and limits; no general entire-function factorization theory is needed to understand these claims.

Proof 1: Zero counting is bounded by the order of an entire function (outline)

Let f be an entire function of finite order \rho. If f has a zero of order m at the origin, write

f(z)=z^m f_0(z),

where f_0 is entire and f_0(0)\ne0. Factoring off z^m and multiplying by a nonzero constant do not affect the order or the nonzero zeros, so it is enough to prove the assertion under the normalization f(0)=1.

Let n(r) denote the number of zeros of f in |z|<r, counted with multiplicity. By Jensen's formula, one obtains an estimate of the form

n(r)\log2\leq\log M(2r),

where

M(r)=\max_{|z|=r}|f(z)|.

Since f has order \rho, for every \varepsilon>0 and all sufficiently large r,

\log M(r)\leq r^{\rho+\varepsilon}.

It follows that

n(r)=O(r^{\rho+\varepsilon}).

When there are infinitely many zeros, taking logarithms and the limit superior gives \alpha\leq\rho+\varepsilon for every \varepsilon>0, hence \alpha\leq\rho.

Level justification

The proof removes a zero of given multiplicity, invokes Jensen formula and compares holomorphic growth order with zero counts. Entire-function order and Jensen zero analysis add a substantial complex-analysis framework.
