Statement

For every Lebesgue measurable E⊆R^n, \lim_{r\downarrow0}|E\cap B_r(x)|/|B_r(x)|=1_E(x) for almost every x∈R^n. Thus the limit is one almost everywhere in E and zero almost everywhere outside E.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement uses Lebesgue measure and almost-everywhere density, beyond elementary calculus.

Proof 1: Substitute the characteristic function

Apply the centered-ball Lebesgue differentiation theorem for locally integrable functions to the characteristic function 1_E. It is locally integrable because 0≤1_E≤1. Its integral over B_r(x) equals |E∩B_r(x)|. The differentiation conclusion is exactly the displayed density limit.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply differentiation to the indicator and identify its ball integral with intersection measure. General Lebesgue measurability, local integrability and almost-everywhere differentiation require measure theory.
