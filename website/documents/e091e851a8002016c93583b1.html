Statement

Let p\equiv1\pmod4 be prime and j^2\equiv-1\pmod p. For the lattice L=\mathbb Z(1,j)+\mathbb Z(0,p), the first vector (a,b) of a 3/4-LLL reduced basis satisfies a^2+b^2=p and is a shortest nonzero vector.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The planar lattice and congruence are explicit. LLL reduction adds the Gram–Schmidt size and reduction inequalities, a limited extension of undergraduate bases and norms.

Proof 1: Combine the LLL length bound with squared-norm divisibility

For the lattice L in the statement, \det(L)=p. Every lattice vector has the form (x_1,jx_1+px_2), and simple modular arithmetic shows that

x_1^2+(jx_1+px_2)^2\equiv0\pmod p.

Thus finding any nonzero vector with squared norm less than 2p in L gives a decomposition of p as a sum of two squares. Such vectors can be found efficiently, for instance using the LLL algorithm. In particular, if b_1,b_2 is a 3/4-LLL reduced basis, then, by the imported property

\|b_1\|\le\left(\frac1{\delta-.25}\right)^{(n-1)/4}\det(L)^{1/n},

with n=2 and \delta=3/4,

\|b_1\|^2\le\sqrt2p<2p.

Thus, by running the LLL lattice basis reduction algorithm with \delta=3/4, we obtain a decomposition of p as a sum of squares. Note that because every vector in L has norm squared a multiple of p, the vector returned by the LLL algorithm in this case is in fact a shortest vector.
