Statement

Let p be an odd prime, \zeta a primitive pth root of unity, and \Delta=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb Q(\zeta)/\mathbb Q), with \sigma_a(\zeta)=\zeta^a for 1\le a\le p-1. Let \omega:(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^\times\to\mathbb Z_p^\times be the Teichmüller character, taking values in the (p-1)st roots of unity and satisfying \omega(a)\equiv a\pmod p. Let G be the p-primary part of the ideal class group, a \mathbb Z_p[\Delta]-module. Define \epsilon_n=\frac1{p-1}\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}\omega(a)^n\sigma_a^{-1}\quad(1\le n\le p-1). Then \sum_n\epsilon_n=1 and \epsilon_i\epsilon_j=\delta_{ij}\epsilon_i, and G=\bigoplus_{n=1}^{p-1}\epsilon_nG.

Proof 1: Finite character sums and orthogonal-idempotent splitting — outline

We now define idempotent elements of the group ring for each n from 1 to p − 1, as

{\displaystyle \epsilon _{n}={\frac {1}{p-1}}\sum _{a=1}^{p-1}\omega (a)^{n}\sigma _{a}^{-1}.}

It is easy to see that {\displaystyle \sum \epsilon _{n}=1} and {\displaystyle \epsilon _{i}\epsilon _{j}=\delta _{ij}\epsilon _{i}} where {\displaystyle \delta _{ij}} is the Kronecker delta. This allows us to break up the p part of the ideal class group G of Q(ζ) by means of the idempotents; if G is the p-primary part of the ideal class group, then, letting Gn = εn(G), we have {\displaystyle G=\oplus G_{n}}.
