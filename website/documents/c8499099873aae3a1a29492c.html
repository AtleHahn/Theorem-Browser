Statement

Let n\ge0 be an integer and x,y,z\in\mathbb C. Suppose x+kz\ne0 and y+(n-k)z\ne0 for all integers 0\le k\le n, and x+y+nz\ne0. Then

\sum_{k=0}^n\frac{x}{x+kz}\binom{x+kz}{k}\frac{y}{y+(n-k)z}\binom{y+(n-k)z}{n-k}=\frac{x+y}{x+y+nz}\binom{x+y+nz}{n}.

For w\in\mathbb C and an integer r\ge0, \binom wr=w(w-1)\cdots(w-r+1)/r!, with \binom w0=1 and 0!=1.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Finite algebraic products and sums with explicitly defined coefficients are elementary.
