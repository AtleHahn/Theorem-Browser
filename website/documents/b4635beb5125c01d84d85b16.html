Commutator of variational symmetry derivations

Variational symmetries are closed under commutators, and the resulting symmetry again yields a conserved current on solutions. This connects the algebraic organization of transformations with conservation laws, strengthening the role of symmetry in physical explanation under the stated variational assumptions.

Statement

Let Q_1,Q_2 be field-variation derivations commuting with coordinate derivatives for a smooth first-order density L(\phi^A,\partial_\mu\phi^A,x) on a coordinate domain in \mathbb R^n, with finitely many field components \phi^A and summation over repeated indices. The derivations fix the coordinates. Suppose Q_i[L]=\partial_\mu f_i^\mu as identities. Then [Q_1,Q_2][L]=\partial_\mu f_{12}^\mu, where f_{12}^\mu=Q_1[f_2^\mu]-Q_2[f_1^\mu]. On smooth solutions of \partial_\mu L_{\partial_\mu\phi^A}=L_{\phi^A} the associated current is J_{12}^\mu=L_{\partial_\mu\phi^A}[Q_1,Q_2][\phi^A]-f_{12}^\mu, and has zero divergence.

Proof 1: Commutator calculation

Suppose we have two symmetry derivations Q_1 and Q_2. Then [Q_1,Q_2] is also a symmetry derivation. Let us see this explicitly. Let us say

Q_1[L]=\partial_\mu f_1^\mu

and

Q_2[L]=\partial_\mu f_2^\mu.

Then,

[Q_1,Q_2][L]=Q_1[Q_2[L]]-Q_2[Q_1[L]] =\partial_\mu f_{12}^\mu,

where f_{12}^\mu=Q_1[f_2^\mu]-Q_2[f_1^\mu]. Here the derivations commute with the coordinate derivatives, as assumed. So, by the Noether field-current identity, the associated current on Euler–Lagrange solutions is

J_{12}^\mu= \frac{\partial L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi^A)} \bigl(Q_1[Q_2[\phi^A]]-Q_2[Q_1[\phi^A]]\bigr)-f_{12}^\mu,

and has zero divergence.

Level justification

The commutator identity is formal, but applying the field-current identity requires field-variation derivations acting on differential densities and Euler-Lagrange systems. This combined variational framework is not confidently a small addition to multivariable calculus.
