Statement

For every sequence (a_n)_{n\ge1} of nonnegative real numbers, \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(a_1\cdots a_n)^{1/n}\le e\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n. Sums are interpreted in [0,\infty].

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves nonnegative real sequences; infinite series; geometric means; extended nonnegative sums. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Weighted AM–GM and telescoping — outline

From the inequality of arithmetic and geometric means applied to the numbers {\displaystyle 1\cdot a_{1},2\cdot a_{2},\dots ,n\cdot a_{n}}

{\displaystyle \mathrm {MG} (a_{1},\dots ,a_{n})=\mathrm {MG} (1a_{1},2a_{2},\dots ,na_{n})(n!)^{-1/n}\leq \mathrm {MA} (1a_{1},2a_{2},\dots ,na_{n})(n!)^{-1/n}}

where MG stands for geometric mean, and MA — for arithmetic mean. The Stirling-type inequality {\displaystyle n!\geq {\sqrt {2\pi n}}\,n^{n}\mathrm {e} ^{-n}} applied to {\displaystyle n+1} implies

{\displaystyle (n!)^{-1/n}\leq {\frac {\mathrm {e} }{n+1}}} for all {\displaystyle n\geq 1.}

Therefore,

{\displaystyle MG(a_{1},\dots ,a_{n})\leq {\frac {\mathrm {e} }{n(n+1)}}\,\sum _{1\leq k\leq n}ka_{k}\,,}

whence

{\displaystyle \sum _{n\geq 1}MG(a_{1},\dots ,a_{n})\leq \,\mathrm {e} \,\sum _{k\geq 1}{\bigg (}\sum _{n\geq k}{\frac {1}{n(n+1)}}{\bigg )}\,ka_{k}=\,\mathrm {e} \,\sum _{k\geq 1}\,a_{k}\,,}

proving the inequality.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The finite weighted AM–GM estimate is summed using nonnegative-series rearrangement and the telescoping identity sum_(n>=k)1/(n(n+1))=1/k. These are undergraduate real-analysis tools; allowing infinite sums adds no measure-theory prerequisites.

Proof 2: The large-exponent limit of Hardy’s inequality — outline

One can also prove Carleman's inequality by starting with Hardy's inequality

{\displaystyle \sum _{n=1}^{\infty }\left({\frac {a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots +a_{n}}{n}}\right)^{p}\leq \left({\frac {p}{p-1}}\right)^{p}\sum _{n=1}^{\infty }a_{n}^{p}}

for the non-negative numbers {\displaystyle a_{1}}, {\displaystyle a_{2}},… and {\displaystyle p>1}, replacing each {\displaystyle a_{n}} with {\displaystyle a_{n}^{1/p}}, and letting {\displaystyle p\to \infty }.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the supplied Hardy inequality after replacing a_k by a_k^(1/p). For each fixed finite initial sum the terms converge to geometric means and (p/(p-1))^p tends to e; then take the supremum of finite partial sums. This avoids assuming unrestricted limit interchange and needs only undergraduate sequence calculus.
