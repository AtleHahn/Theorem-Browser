Statement

Let d\ge1 be an integer and N a point process on \mathbb R^d, viewed as a random locally finite counting measure, not necessarily simple. Its intensity measure is \Lambda(B)=\mathbb E[N(B)] for Borel sets B. Sums over N count multiplicities. Let f:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R be measurable with \int|f|\,d\Lambda<\infty. Then \sum_{x\in N}f(x) is integrable and \mathbb E\!\left[\sum_{x\in N}f(x)\right]=\int_{\mathbb R^d}f\,d\Lambda.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The objects are random measures and measure-theoretic integrals, beyond the stipulated calculus and linear algebra.

Proof 1: Fubini interchange for the random counting measure (outline)

The equation

\mathbb E\left[\sum_{x\in N}f(x)\right]=\int_{\mathbb R^d}f(x)\,\Lambda(dx)

is essentially an application of Fubini's theorem and it holds for a wide class of point processes, simple or not. Here the point sum is integration against the random counting measure N, and \Lambda(B)=\mathbb E[N(B)] is its mean measure. The interchange and its absolute-integrability check are left implicit in the source.
