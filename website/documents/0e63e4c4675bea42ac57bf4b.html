Statement

For every pair of positive integers n,r there is a finite constant K(n,r) such that every real 2\pi-periodic C^r function f with zero Fourier coefficients at frequencies |j|<n satisfies \|f\|_\infty\le K(n,r)\|f^{(r)}\|_\infty. Here \|h\|_\infty=\max_{0\le x\le2\pi}|h(x)| and the Fourier coefficient at j\in\mathbb Z is (2\pi)^{-1}\int_0^{2\pi}f(x)e^{-ijx}\,dx. The least such constant is \sup\{\|f\|_\infty:\|f^{(r)}\|_\infty\le1,\ f\text{ as above}\}.

Proof 1: Zero mean and repeated integration (external)

A supporting source can be found here (Section 5.3, fundamental theorem of calculus). The deduction below supplies the argument for the stored statement.

The following direct calculus argument is supplied here. For a real continuous periodic zero-mean function h there is a zero c; for any x choose a lift of c within distance pi of x. If h is C1, the fundamental theorem gives |h(x)|<=pi||h prime||_infinity. The j=0 Fourier coefficient makes f zero mean. Each derivative f^(j), 1<=j<r, also has zero mean by periodicity of f^(j-1). Repeated application yields ||f||_infinity<=pi^r||f^(r)||_infinity, a valid K(n,r) for every n. Let S be the stated supremum; it is finite. A function with derivative norm zero is a periodic polynomial, hence constant and then zero. Normalize all other functions by their derivative norm to see that S itself is admissible and is no larger than any admissible constant. Thus S is the least constant.
