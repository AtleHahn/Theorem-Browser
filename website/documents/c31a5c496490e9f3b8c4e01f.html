Statement

Let A,B be Hermitian d\times d complex matrices, d\ge1, and let e^A denote the matrix exponential. Write X\preceq Y when Y-X is positive semidefinite. If e^A\preceq e^B, then A\preceq B.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Hermitian matrices; Matrix exponentials; Positive semidefinite order. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Turn the quadratic-form order into a contraction

Use the operator Golden–Thompson bound \|e^{X+Y}\|_{op}\le\|e^Xe^Y\|_{op} for Hermitian X,Y; vector norms below are Euclidean.

For any {\textstyle x}, we have {\textstyle \langle e^{A}x,x\rangle \leq \langle e^{B}x,x\rangle }, thus {\textstyle \|e^{A/2}x\|\leq \|e^{B/2}x\|}.

Substitute x=e^{-B/2}y. The operator e^{A/2}e^{-B/2} has norm at most one, as does its adjoint. Thus {\textstyle e^{-B/2}e^{A/2}} is a contraction map, thus {\textstyle \|e^{-B/2}e^{A/2}\|_{op}\leq 1}, thus {\textstyle \|e^{A/2-B/2}\|_{op}\leq 1}, thus all eigenvalues of {\textstyle (A/2-B/2)} are nonpositive, thus {\textstyle A\preceq B}.
