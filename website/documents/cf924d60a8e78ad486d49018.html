Statement

Let a,b,c be the positive side lengths of a nondegenerate Euclidean triangle, opposite interior angles \alpha,\beta,\gamma. Then \tan^2(\alpha/2)=\dfrac{(a+b-c)(a-b+c)}{(a+b+c)(-a+b+c)}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All objects and manipulations are elementary triangle trigonometry.

Proof 1: Multiply the tangent product and ratio identities

Use the two stated identities

\tan(\alpha/2)\tan(\beta/2)=\frac{a+b-c}{a+b+c},\quad \frac{\tan(\alpha/2)}{\tan(\beta/2)}=\frac{a-b+c}{-a+b+c}.

Multiplying them cancels \tan(\beta/2)>0 and yields the claimed square.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Multiply the two explicit identities and cancel the nonzero second tangent. School algebra and trigonometry suffice.
