Statement

Let F(1)=F(2)=F(4)=0, F(3)=1 and F(i)=F(i-2)+F(i-4) for integers i\ge5. Then F(i)=0 exactly when i=1 or i is positive and even.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The initial values and recurrence explicitly define an integer sequence; the conclusion specifies its zero positions using parity.

Proof 1: Parity subsequences — outline

The recurrence separates the even and odd positions. The even positions remain zero; the odd positions follow the Fibonacci recurrence, beginning 0,1,1,2,3,5,\ldots. Thus the sequence alternates zeros and Fibonacci numbers, and its zero positions are the singleton \{1\} together with all positive even integers.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Separate even and odd indices, retain zero in the even recurrence and positive Fibonacci values after the initial odd zero. High-school recurrence reasoning and induction suffice.
