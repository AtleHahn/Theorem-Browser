Statement

Let (\Omega,\mathcal B,\mu) be a probability space and T:\Omega\to\Omega a measurable, measure-preserving, ergodic transformation. Thus \mu(T^{-1}A)=\mu(A) for every measurable A, and any measurable set invariant under T^{-1} up to a null set has measure 0 or 1. Let P be a finite or countable measurable partition with finite entropy

H(P)=-\sum_{A\in P}\mu(A)\ln\mu(A)<\infty,

where 0\ln0=0.

For n\geq1, let P^{(n)}=\bigvee_{k=0}^{n-1}T^{-k}P be the common refinement of these partitions, and let P^{(n)}(x) be its atom containing x. The entropy rate

h_T(P)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{H(P^{(n)})}{n}

exists, and

-\frac1n\ln\mu(P^{(n)}(x))\longrightarrow h_T(P)

for \mu-almost every x and in L^1(\mu). Here L^1 convergence means that the integral of the absolute difference from h_T(P) tends to zero. Information functions can be assigned arbitrary values on the null union of zero-measure atoms.
