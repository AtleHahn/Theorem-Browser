Statement

For an integer n\ge3 put S_n(X)=1+X+\cdots+X^{n-1} and P_n(X)=\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(X^k-1). The following are equivalent:

  1. n is prime.
  2. P_n(X)\equiv n-S_n(X)\pmod{X^n-1} in \mathbb Z[X].
  3. P_n(X)\equiv n\pmod{S_n(X)} in \mathbb Z[X]. Here a polynomial congruence modulo Q(X) means that the difference is Q(X) times a polynomial with integer coefficients.

Review notes

  • For prime p, multiplication by each nonzero residue permutes the nontrivial pth roots. Their product of (1-root) is S_p(1)=p. Since p-1 is even the sign agrees with the target product.; The difference P_p-p+S_p also vanishes at 1, hence at every pth root. Monic division has integer quotient and a remainder of degree less than p; root counting makes the remainder zero.; The second congruence implies the third since S_n divides X^n-1.
