Statement

For an integer n\ge1, use deterministic Turing machines with n operational states, a separate Halt state, one starting state, a two-way unbounded tape over \{0,1\}, and a transition for each operational state and scanned symbol. Each transition writes a bit, moves one cell left or right, and enters a state (possibly Halt). The machine starts on an all-zero tape. Among machines that halt, \Sigma(n) is the largest number of ones left on the tape, and S(n) the largest number of transitions, including the halting transition. Then \Sigma(n)\le S(n) for every n\ge1.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The complete transition rules, score and running-time maxima are defined in the statement. The conclusion compares the largest number of surviving ones with the largest number of writes/moves. These are finite counts under explicit local rules, understandable without general machine theory.

Proof 1: One writing step for every surviving one

For each n, S(n)\ge\Sigma(n). Each shift may write a 0 or a 1 on the tape, while \Sigma counts a subset of the shifts that wrote a 1, namely the ones that had not been overwritten by the time the Turing machine halted.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Assign each surviving one its final writing transition. Different surviving cells need different transitions, so the score is at most the run length; taking maxima preserves the inequality.
