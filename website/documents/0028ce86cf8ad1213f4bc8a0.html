Statement

Let T be any index set. For each t\in T, let (\Omega_t,\mathcal F_t) be a measurable space with a Hausdorff topology \tau_t. For J\subseteq T put \Omega_J=\prod_{t\in J}\Omega_t, equipped with the product sigma-algebra \mathcal F_J=\bigotimes_{t\in J}\mathcal F_t. Give each finite product its product topology. Suppose that for every finite J\subseteq T a probability measure \mu_J on (\Omega_J,\mathcal F_J) is inner regular with respect to that topology: for every measurable B,

\mu_J(B)=\sup\{\mu_J(K):K\subseteq B,\ K\text{ compact and measurable}\}.

Assume compatibility under coordinate projections: for finite I\subseteq J\subseteq T,

\mu_I=(\pi_I^J)_*\mu_J,\qquad \pi_I^J(\omega)=\omega|_I.

Then there is a unique probability measure \mu on (\Omega_T,\mathcal F_T) such that

\mu_J=(\pi_J^T)_*\mu\quad\text{for every finite }J\subseteq T.

Here pushforward is defined by (\pi_*\nu)(B)=\nu(\pi^{-1}(B)). A Hausdorff topology separates any two distinct points by disjoint open neighborhoods. The product sigma-algebra is generated by inverse images of coordinate measurable sets; the empty product is a one-point space. The coordinate random variables X_t(\omega)=\omega_t realize the prescribed finite-dimensional distributions.
