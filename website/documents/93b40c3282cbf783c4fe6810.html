Statement

Let K be a field of characteristic different from 2. In the linear group G=\{I,-I\} acting on K^2, no element is a pseudoreflection (a nonidentity linear map whose fixed subspace has codimension one). In particular G is not generated by pseudoreflections.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra supplies fixed subspaces and codimension. Pseudoreflection is explicitly defined, and the group consists of only the two stated matrices, so no invariant theory is needed.

Proof 1: Compute the fixed-space dimension

The sole nonidentity element is s=-I, and \dim\ker(s-I)=0. It is therefore not a pseudoreflection, and G cannot be generated by pseudoreflections.

Level justification

Compute the kernel of minus twice the identity over a field of characteristic not two and compare its dimension with the defined reflection condition. Undergraduate fields and linear algebra suffice.
