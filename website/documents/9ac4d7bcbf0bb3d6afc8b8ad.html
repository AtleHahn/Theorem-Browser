Statement

All graphs here are finite, simple and undirected. A clique is a set of pairwise adjacent vertices. A cycle is induced if no edge joins two of its nonconsecutive vertices; a graph is chordal if every induced cycle has length three. Let a finite family of nonempty connected subgraphs of a finite path be given. Its intersection graph has one vertex for each subpath and edges exactly for pairs sharing a path vertex. This graph is chordal.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Subpaths, intersection graphs and chordality are explicitly finite edge and cycle conditions.

Proof 1: Intersection graphs of subpaths are chordal

A path is a special case of a tree, so its connected subgraphs are subtrees of a tree. By the source-stated Gavril theorem, intersection graphs of subtrees of a tree are chordal. Apply that theorem to the given subpaths.
