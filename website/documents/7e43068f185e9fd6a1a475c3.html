Statement

A simple plane polygon is a closed chain of finitely many line segments with no intersections except common endpoints of successive sides. Call a vertex a mouth if the segment joining its neighbors lies outside the polygon apart from its endpoints and does not cross the polygon or meet another vertex. Repeatedly removing mouths from a nonconvex simple polygon eventually gives the convex hull of its original vertices.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Proof 1: Enlarge the polygon while deleting vertices — outline

Every nonconvex simple polygon has at least one mouth. Repeatedly finding and removing a mouth from a nonconvex polygon will eventually turn it into the convex hull of the initial polygon.
