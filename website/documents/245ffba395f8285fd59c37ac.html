Chernoff bounds of iid averages are powers of the single-variable bound

Statement

Let X_1,\ldots,X_n be independent identically distributed real random variables, n\ge1, and let M(t)=\mathbb E e^{tX_1}. Put \bar X=n^{-1}\sum_iX_i. For either parameter set T=(0,\infty), T=(-\infty,0), or T=\mathbb R,

\inf_{t\in T}\mathbb E(e^{t\bar X})e^{-ta}=\left(\inf_{u\in T}M(u)e^{-ua}\right)^n.
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Finite independence and exponential expectations are a limited probability extension of undergraduate integration. The infima and averages are explicit; there is no stochastic limit or general conditional distribution.

Proof 1: Rescale the common exponential moment — outline

For identically distributed independent summands, the product moment formula gives \mathbb E e^{t\bar X}=M(t/n)^n. Thus the Chernoff bound for their average is the nth power of the single-variable bound, after rescaling the parameter by n.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Factor the exponential expectation and rescale t=nu; a positive nth power commutes with the nonnegative infimum. Undergraduate probability and real infima suffice.
