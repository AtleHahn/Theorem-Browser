Statement

Let \Omega\subset\mathbb C be a bounded Jordan domain. For an open set U\subset\mathbb C, write A^2(U) for its holomorphic functions with finite norm \|g\|_U^2=\int_U|g(z)|^2\,dA(z), where dA is planar area measure. The complex polynomials are dense in A^2(\Omega) in this norm.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Density of polynomials in a holomorphic L2 space involves complex analysis and an infinite-dimensional function-space norm.

Proof 1: Farrell–Markushevich theorem — outline

Let Ω be the bounded Jordan domain and let Ωn be bounded outer Jordan domains containing Ω and decreasing to Ω in the Carathéodory kernel sense, with Ωn containing the closure of Ωn + 1. By the Riemann mapping theorem there is a conformal mapping fn of Ωn onto Ω, normalised to fix a given point in Ω with positive derivative there. By the Carathéodory kernel theorem fn(z) converges uniformly on compacta in Ω to z. In fact Carathéodory's theorem implies that the inverse maps tend uniformly on compacta to z. By normality of the bounded family of holomorphic maps into Ω, given a subsequence of fn, it has a subsequence, convergent on compacta in Ω. Since the inverse functions converge to z, it follows that the subsequence converges to z on compacta. Hence fn converges to z on compacta in Ω.

As a consequence the derivative of fn tends to 1 uniformly on compacta.

Let g be a square integrable holomorphic function on Ω, i.e. an element of the Bergman space A2(Ω). Define gn on Ωn by gn(z) = g(fn(z))fn'(z). By change of variable (the real Jacobian is |f_n'|^2)

{\displaystyle \displaystyle {\|g_{n}\|_{\Omega _{n}}^{2}=\|g\|_{\Omega }^{2}.}}

Let hn be the restriction of gn to Ω. Then the norm of hn is at most that of gn. Thus these norms are uniformly bounded. Passing to a subsequence if necessary, it can therefore be assumed that hn has a weak limit in A2(Ω). On the other hand, hn tends uniformly on compacta to g. Since the evaluation maps are continuous linear functions on A2(Ω), g is the weak limit of hn. On the other hand, since g_n is holomorphic on a neighborhood of \overline\Omega and the complement of \overline\Omega is connected, by Runge's theorem, hn lies in the closed subspace K of A2(Ω) generated by complex polynomials. Hence g lies in the weak closure of K, which is K itself, since a norm-closed linear subspace of a Hilbert space is weakly closed.

Level justification

The proof combines conformal mapping and kernel convergence, normal families, Bergman Hilbert spaces, weak subsequences and Runge approximation. These substantial complex-analysis and functional-analysis theories cannot be learned as a small extension.
