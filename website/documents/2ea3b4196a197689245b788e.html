Statement

Let K be a field of characteristic different from 2 and 3. Let p,q\in K satisfy 4p^3+27q^2=0. If p=0, then q=0 and t^3+pt+q=t^3. If p\ne0, then t^3+pt+q=(t-3q/p)(t+3q/(2p))^2, with simple root 3q/p and double root -3q/(2p).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory algebra supplies polynomial factorization, multiplicity and field characteristic. An algebraic closure only supplies all polynomial roots here, and a primitive cube root is a nonidentity root of X cubed minus one. The displayed finite formulas need no Galois or extension-degree theory.

Proof 1: Expand the proposed linear factors — outline

The discriminant of the depressed cubic {\displaystyle t^{3}+pt+q} is zero if {\displaystyle 4p^{3}+27q^{2}=0.} If p is also zero, then p = q = 0 , and 0 is a triple root of the cubic. If {\displaystyle 4p^{3}+27q^{2}=0,} and p ≠ 0 , then the cubic has a simple root

{\displaystyle t_{1}={\frac {3q}{p}}}

and a double root

{\displaystyle t_{2}=t_{3}=-{\frac {3q}{2p}}.}

In other words,

{\displaystyle t^{3}+pt+q=\left(t-{\frac {3q}{p}}\right)\left(t+{\frac {3q}{2p}}\right)^{2}.}

This result can be proved by expanding the latter product or retrieved by solving the rather simple system of equations resulting from Vieta's formulas.
