Statement

Let S,P\subseteq\mathbb R be nonempty open intervals and U:S\times P\to\mathbb R be C^2. Suppose for each p\in P there is a unique maximizer s^*(p)\in S of U(\cdot,p), the map s^*:P\to S is differentiable, and U_{ss}(s^*(p),p)<0. Then \frac{ds^*}{dp}(p)=-\frac{U_{sp}(s^*(p),p)}{U_{ss}(s^*(p),p)}, so its sign equals that of U_{sp}(s^*(p),p). In particular a negative cross partial there gives ds^*/dp<0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves real functions of two variables; twice continuous differentiability; interior maximizers; differentiable maximizing branch. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Differentiate the first-order condition

Taking the first order condition, we know that at the optimum, {\displaystyle U_{s}(s^{\ast }(p),p)=0}. Differentiating the first order condition with respect to p using the assumed differentiability of s^* and the chain rule, we find that

{\displaystyle U_{ss}(s^{\ast }(p),p)(\partial s^{\ast }(p)/(\partial p))+U_{sp}(s^{\ast }(p),p)=0}

or that

{\displaystyle {\frac {\partial s^{\ast }(p)}{\partial p}}={\frac {-U_{sp}(s^{\ast }(p),p)}{U_{ss}(s^{\ast }(p),p)}}.}

Since {\displaystyle U_{ss}(s^{\ast }(p),p)<0},

{\displaystyle {\frac {\partial s^{\ast }(p)}{\partial p}}{\overset {\text{sign}}{=}}U_{sp}(s^{\ast }(p),p).}

In particular, if the cross partial is negative,

{\displaystyle U_{sp}(s^{\ast }(p),p)<0}

and hence

{\displaystyle {\frac {\partial s^{\ast }(p)}{\partial p}}<0}
