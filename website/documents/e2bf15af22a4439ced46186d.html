Statement

Let G\subset\mathbb C be a domain such that every nowhere-zero holomorphic function on G has a holomorphic square root. Every closed piecewise smooth curve in G has winding number zero about every w\notin G.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The extra notions are complex differentiability, a single holomorphic choice of square root, and winding number as the integer total change of angle divided by 2pi along a closed curve avoiding a point. These are concrete calculus/curve-topology extensions; no covering-space classification or full complex-analysis course is needed.

Proof 1: Holomorphic square roots force zero exterior winding numbers

Fix w\notin G. The assertion follows because if {\displaystyle \gamma } is a piecewise closed curve and {\displaystyle f_{n}} are successive square roots of {\displaystyle z-w} for {\displaystyle w} outside {\displaystyle G}, then the winding number of {\displaystyle \gamma } about {\displaystyle w} is {\displaystyle 2^{n}} times the winding number of {\displaystyle f_{n}\circ \gamma } about {\displaystyle 0}. Hence the winding number of {\displaystyle \gamma } about {\displaystyle w} must be divisible by {\displaystyle 2^{n}} for all {\displaystyle n}, so it must equal {\displaystyle 0}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Repeated roots make the winding integer divisible by every power of two. Undergraduate complex curves and the integer net-turn interpretation of winding suffice with the supplied power rule as a small geometric extension.
