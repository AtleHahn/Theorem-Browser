Statement

Assume the generalized Riemann hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions: for every positive integer m and Dirichlet character \chi modulo m, every zero of the meromorphic continuation of

L(s,\chi)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\chi(n)}{n^s}\quad(\operatorname{Re}s>1)

lying in 0<\operatorname{Re}s<1 has real part 1/2. A Dirichlet character here is a completely multiplicative function \chi:\mathbb Z\to\mathbb C, periodic modulo m, with \chi(n)=0 exactly when \gcd(n,m)>1.

For integers d\geq2 and 1\leq a\leq d-1 with \gcd(a,d)=1, let p(a,d) be the least prime of the form a+nd with an integer n\geq1. Write \varphi(d)=|\{1\leq j\leq d:\gcd(j,d)=1\}|, and let \log denote the natural logarithm. For every real \delta>0 there is an integer D\geq2 such that, for all d\geq D and all the above residues a,

p(a,d)\leq(1+\delta)\varphi(d)^2(\log d)^2.

Proof 1: GRH explicit formulas with one prime removed (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Sections 4.1–4.3, especially the weighted prime-power bound (4.13) and inequality (4.15), printed pp.13–16; additional omitted-prime perturbation given below.).

Use Sections 4.1–4.3, especially (4.15), with h=phi(d), q=d and X=(1+delta)(phi(d)log d)^2. If the progression contains no prime <=X other than the possible prime a, the weighted von Mangoldt sum differs from the no-prime upper bound only by at most (log a)log(X/a)=O_delta((log d)^2), uniformly for 1<=a<d. Accordingly the inequality leading to (4.15) gains at most h O_delta((log d)^2) on its right side. Its other terms give X<=sqrt(X)[(h-1)log d+O(h+log X)]+h O_delta((log d)^2). Divide by X. Since phi(d) tends to infinity (indeed phi(d)>=sqrt(d/2)), the right side is 1/sqrt(1+delta)+o(1), uniformly in a, a contradiction for large d. The prime-power estimates already cover all bases, including a. This additional perturbation removes the possible prime a and supplies the n>=1 conclusion.
