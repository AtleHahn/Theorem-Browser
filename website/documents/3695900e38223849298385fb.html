Statement

Let n\ge1 be an integer. Let A:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R^{n\times n} and b:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R^n have Lebesgue-integrable components on every bounded interval. For any (t_0,y_0)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^n, the problem

y'(t)=A(t)y(t)+b(t),\qquad y(t_0)=y_0

has a unique local solution in the extended sense: y is absolutely continuous and satisfies the differential equation almost everywhere.

Proof 1: Local solvability of a linear ODE with integrable coefficients — outline

Set f(t,y)=A(t)y+b(t). This right-hand side satisfies the Carathéodory conditions on a bounded rectangle around (t_0,y_0): continuity in y, measurability in t, and an integrable bound in t. Its linear dependence on y also gives the integrable Lipschitz condition there.

Apply the Carathéodory existence and uniqueness theorem: on such a rectangle, those three conditions together with an integrable function k(t) satisfying |f(t,u)-f(t,v)|\le k(t)|u-v| give a unique local absolutely continuous solution of the initial value problem, satisfying the equation almost everywhere.
