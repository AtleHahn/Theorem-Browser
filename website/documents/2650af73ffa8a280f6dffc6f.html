Statement

Let R be a commutative ring, n\ge1, and A_1,\ldots,A_{2n}\in M_n(R). For the symmetric group S_{2n} and permutation sign \operatorname{sgn}, \sum_{\sigma\in S_{2n}}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)A_{\sigma(1)}\cdots A_{\sigma(2n)}=0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Undergraduate linear algebra supplies determinants, cofactors, matrices and permutation signs; introductory algebra supplies commutative rings and polynomial coefficients. The displayed formula uses these directly, so moving the entries from a field to a commutative ring is a small extension, with no additional structural theory required.

Proof 1: Reduction to Swan’s Eulerian-path parity theorem (outline)

By linearity it is enough to prove the theorem when each matrix has only one nonzero entry, which is 1. In this case each matrix can be encoded as a directed edge of a graph with n vertices. So all matrices together give a graph on n vertices with 2n directed edges. The identity holds provided that for any two vertices A and B of the graph, the number of odd Eulerian paths from A to B is the same as the number of even ones. (Here a path is called odd or even depending on whether its edges taken in order give an odd or even permutation of the 2n edges.) Swan showed that this was the case provided the number of edges in the graph is at least 2n, thus proving the Amitsur–Levitzki theorem.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Multilinearity reduces the expression to matrix units over the commutative coefficient ring. Multiplying matrix units encodes composable directed edges, so the signed count of labeled Eulerian trails is each matrix entry. Matrix multiplication and permutation signs are undergraduate algebra; the extra graph notion is simply traversing every labeled edge once. Swan’s parity theorem is applied as a statement, and its checked formulation allows the loops and repeated edges needed here. No polynomial-identity algebra theory is required.

Proof 1 — Reduction to Swan’s Eulerian-path parity theorem (outline)

Statement used:

In a finite directed multigraph with V vertices and E>=2V labeled edges, allowing loops, the numbers of even and odd Eulerian trails between any fixed ordered pair of endpoints are equal.

Application in proof:

Encode matrix units as labeled directed edges; each matrix entry of the alternating sum is the difference of these two counts.
