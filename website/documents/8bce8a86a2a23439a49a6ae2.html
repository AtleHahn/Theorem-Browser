Statement

Let I=[t_0-\alpha,t_0+\alpha] with \alpha>0 and let B be a closed ball about y_0 in \mathbb R^n. Suppose f:I\times B\to\mathbb R^n is continuous and L-Lipschitz in its second variable uniformly in the first. Suppose its Picard operator

(\Gamma\varphi)(t)=y_0+\int_{t_0}^t f(s,\varphi(s))\,ds

maps C(I,B), the continuous maps I\to B, into itself. Write \|\varphi-\psi\|_\infty=\sup_{t\in I}\|\varphi(t)-\psi(t)\| for their uniform distance, and \Gamma^m for m-fold iteration of \Gamma.

For m\ge1, \varphi_1,\varphi_2\in C(I,B) and t\in I,

\|\Gamma^m\varphi_1(t)-\Gamma^m\varphi_2(t)\|\le\frac{L^m|t-t_0|^m}{m!}\|\varphi_1-\varphi_2\|_\infty.

Consequently, \|\Gamma^m\varphi_1-\Gamma^m\varphi_2\|_\infty\le (L\alpha)^m\|\varphi_1-\varphi_2\|_\infty/m!.

Proof 1: Iterated absolute-integral estimate

For the first iteration, the Lipschitz bound and the absolute integral estimate give L|t-t_0|\|\varphi_1-\varphi_2\|_\infty. Induct on m. The absolute-integral calculation covers either side of t_0:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\left\|\Gamma ^{m}\varphi _{1}(t)-\Gamma ^{m}\varphi _{2}(t)\right\|&=\left\|\Gamma \Gamma ^{m-1}\varphi _{1}(t)-\Gamma \Gamma ^{m-1}\varphi _{2}(t)\right\|\\&\leq \left|\int _{t_{0}}^{t}\left\|f\left(s,\Gamma ^{m-1}\varphi _{1}(s)\right)-f\left(s,\Gamma ^{m-1}\varphi _{2}(s)\right)\right\|ds\right|\\&\leq L\left|\int _{t_{0}}^{t}\left\|\Gamma ^{m-1}\varphi _{1}(s)-\Gamma ^{m-1}\varphi _{2}(s)\right\|ds\right|\\&\leq L\left|\int _{t_{0}}^{t}{\frac {L^{m-1}|s-t_{0}|^{m-1}}{(m-1)!}}\left\|\varphi _{1}-\varphi _{2}\right\|ds\right|\\&\leq {\frac {L^{m}|t-t_{0}|^{m}}{m!}}\left\|\varphi _{1}-\varphi _{2}\right\|.\end{aligned}}}

By taking a supremum over {\displaystyle t\in [t_{0}-\alpha ,t_{0}+\alpha ]} we see that {\displaystyle \left\|\Gamma ^{m}\varphi _{1}-\Gamma ^{m}\varphi _{2}\right\|\leq {\frac {L^{m}\alpha ^{m}}{m!}}\left\|\varphi _{1}-\varphi _{2}\right\|}. Here every norm without an argument t is the uniform norm.

Level justification

Induct on the number of iterations using the Lipschitz bound, absolute vector-integral estimate and integral of a power, then take the uniform supremum. Undergraduate real analysis and ODE estimates suffice.
