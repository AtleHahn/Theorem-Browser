Statement

Let R be a commutative unital ring containing \mathbb Q, let n\ge1 be an integer, and let A\in M_n(R). Put s_j=\operatorname{tr}(A^j) and write \det(tI_n-A)=\sum_{i=0}^n c_it^i, c_n=1. If A is invertible, then A^{-1}=(\det A)^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{n+k-1}A^{n-k-1}B_k(s_1,-1!s_2,\ldots,(-1)^{k-1}(k-1)!s_k)/k!, where B_k is the complete exponential Bell polynomial and B_0=1.

Level

Level justification

Linear algebra supplies inverse matrices, determinants and traces. The Bell polynomial adds only its finite coefficient-sum definition, a small notational extension of polynomial algebra sufficient for the displayed formula.

Proof 1: An inverse matrix from Bell trace coefficients

For a general {\displaystyle n\times n} invertible matrix A, A−1 can be written as an {\displaystyle (n-1)} order polynomial expression in A. The Cayley–Hamilton theorem amounts to the identity

{\displaystyle p(A)=A^{n}+c_{n-1}A^{n-1}+\cdots +c_{1}A+(-1)^{n}\det(A)I_{n}=0.}

Here p(t)=\det(tI_n-A). Put s_k=\operatorname{tr}(A^k). Use the characteristic-coefficient identity in terms of complete exponential Bell polynomials, with B_0=1.

In general, the formula for the coefficients ci is given in terms of complete exponential Bell polynomials as

{\displaystyle c_{n-k}={\frac {(-1)^{k}}{k!}}B_{k}(s_{1},-1!s_{2},2!s_{3},\ldots ,(-1)^{k-1}(k-1)!s_{k}).}

The Cayley–Hamilton identity can be written as

{\displaystyle -(-1)^{n}\det(A)I_{n}=A(A^{n-1}+c_{n-1}A^{n-2}+\cdots +c_{1}I_{n}),}

and, by multiplying both sides by A−1 and dividing by the unit \det A (note −(−1)n = (−1)n−1), one is led to an expression for the inverse of A as a trace identity,

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}A^{-1}&={\frac {(-1)^{n-1}}{\det A}}(A^{n-1}+c_{n-1}A^{n-2}+\cdots +c_{1}I_{n}),\\[5pt]&={\frac {1}{\det A}}\sum _{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{n+k-1}{\frac {A^{n-k-1}}{k!}}B_{k}(s_{1},-1!s_{2},2!s_{3},\ldots ,(-1)^{k-1}(k-1)!s_{k}).\end{aligned}}}

When n=1, each displayed characteristic-coefficient polynomial is interpreted as the finite sum \sum_{i=1}^n c_iA^{i-1}.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Rearrange Cayley–Hamilton to isolate the inverse and substitute the supplied Bell-polynomial coefficient formula. Undergraduate ring and matrix algebra suffice with Bell polynomials as an explicit polynomial family; no combinatorial derivation is needed.
