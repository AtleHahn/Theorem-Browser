Statement

Let C be a Jordan curve in the plane and U its bounded complementary domain. Points q\in C for which some straight segment from a point of U to q lies in U except at q are dense in C.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Basic topology supplies continuous injections, connected components, path connectedness and homeomorphisms. The sphere or Jordan curve is explicitly a familiar Euclidean subset or continuous circle image; the full assertion needs no homology or manifold machinery used by its proof.

Proof 1: Take a nearest-contact disk from inside — outline

Use the Jordan curve theorem, in particular that C is the boundary of its bounded domain U.

A given point on the curve is arbitrarily close to some point in the interior and there is a smallest closed disk about that point which intersects the curve only on its boundary; those boundary points are close to the original point on the curve and by construction are accessible.

A radius of this disk to a contact point is the required segment, with only its endpoint on C.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Choose an interior point near the boundary and a nearest boundary contact, then use the disk radius as an interior segment. Undergraduate compactness and planar topology suffice with the explicit Jordan boundary assertion.

Choosing the interior point close enough controls the distance of the contact point by twice that distance; the source suppresses this routine estimate. The disk stays in the interior component because it is connected and misses the curve.
