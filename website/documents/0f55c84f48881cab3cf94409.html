Large eliminated formulas force double-exponential output cost

Eliminating quantifiers over real closed fields can intrinsically require doubly exponential explicit output. This separates the existence of a decision or reformulation method from feasible use: the complexity can reside in every equivalent output, not merely in a poorly chosen algorithm.

Statement

For quantifier elimination over real closed fields that produces an explicit quantifier-free formula, there are inputs of length O(n) whose every equivalent quantifier-free output has length 2^{2^{\Omega(n)}}. Thus worst-case running time and output space (including the stored output) have a double-exponential lower bound.

Proof 1: Bound resources by the required output length

Davenport and Heintz supply formulas \Phi_n of length O(n), with n quantifiers and constant-degree input polynomials, for which every equivalent quantifier-free formula has length 2^{2^{\Omega(n)}}. Such an explicit output occupies that many symbols, and producing it requires writing them. This gives the output-space and time lower bounds, without asserting the same bound for auxiliary working space in a streaming-output model.

Level justification

Apply the stated lower bound to explicit quantifier-free output and compare symbol count with resources. Real closed fields, first-order equivalence and quantifier elimination are an undeveloped specialist framework beyond a confidently small addition to introductory algebra and logic, although the final output argument is elementary; retain beyond undergraduate provisionally.
