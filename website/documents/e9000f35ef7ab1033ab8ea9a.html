Statement

Let X be a real random variable on a probability space with finite second moment, mean \mu=\mathbb E X, and variance \sigma^2=\mathbb E[(X-\mu)^2]. Let \lambda>0. Then \Pr(X-\mu\le-\lambda)\le\frac{\sigma^2}{\sigma^2+\lambda^2}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

From undergraduate integration, expectation can be understood as a probability-weighted integral of a real quantity; the mean and variance formulas are given. The tail event is an ordinary real inequality. Adding these related probability-law and moment definitions fits about 30 minutes, without learning convergence of random variables or constructing measures.

Proof 1: Reflect the upper-tail inequality

Use Cantelli’s upper-tail inequality: for any real random variable Y of finite variance and \lambda>0, \Pr(Y-\mathbb EY\ge\lambda)\le\frac{\operatorname{Var}(Y)}{\operatorname{Var}(Y)+\lambda^2}. Apply this to Y=-X. Since \mathbb E(-X)=-\mu and \operatorname{Var}(-X)=\sigma^2, its upper-tail event is X-\mu\le-\lambda, giving the bound.

Level justification

Apply the given scalar tail inequality to -X and use elementary mean and variance transformation rules. This is standard undergraduate probability and does not require constructing a measure integral.
