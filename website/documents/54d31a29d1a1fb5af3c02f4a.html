Statement

A minor is obtained from a subgraph by contracting edges, up to isomorphism. A clique is a set of pairwise adjacent vertices. A k-clique-sum identifies cliques of equal size at most k in two graphs and may delete edges within the identified clique. Define treewidth \operatorname{tw}(G) as the least k\ge0 for which G is obtainable by successive k-clique-sums of graphs on at most k+1 vertices. For every finite planar graph H, there is an integer k>0 such that every finite graph having no H minor has treewidth less than k.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Graph decompositions/minors, uniform asymptotic bounds or approximation schemes require undergraduate graph theory and algorithms; the finite constructions can be learned without their deeper proof theory. A bright US college student should be able to understand the statement after about 30 minutes of focused background research; this rating concerns understanding the claim, not proving it.

Proof 1: Apply the empty surface-part specialization (outline)

Use the graph structure theorem and its planar case described in the source: because the forbidden planar graph embeds on every surface, the surface-and-vortex portions are empty, leaving pieces consisting of only a uniformly bounded number of apices. The graph is assembled by clique-sums of those bounded-size pieces. By the source characterization of treewidth through clique-sums of bounded-size graphs, this bounds treewidth uniformly in terms of H. Increase the bound by one to express it as a strict inequality.
