Simpler proof assessment

Likely alternative prerequisite level: Undergraduate level; lowest recorded proof level: Beyond undergraduate level. For nonzero g with eventual square values, g is eventually positive. Put h(x)=sqrt(g(x)) there and choose an integer k>deg(g)/2. Elementary derivative estimates give h^(k)(x)->0, hence Delta^k h(n)->0 by repeated mean-value estimates. These differences are integers for all sufficiently large n, so eventually vanish. Finite Newton interpolation makes h(n) eventually a rational polynomial P(n); infinitely many equalities imply g=P^2. Writing P=A/c with primitive integer A, Gauss's content lemma forces c=1. The zero polynomial is immediate. Real calculus and polynomial algebra replace Hilbert irreducibility.

Statement

If g\in\mathbb Z[X] and g(m) is an integer square for every sufficiently large positive integer m, then g=h^2 for some h\in\mathbb Z[X].

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement concerns integer coefficients, squares and integer inputs only.

Mathematical Field
High School Mathematics
Required topics

Review notes
none

Proof 1: Apply irreducibility to Y squared minus g — outline

This follows from Hilbert's irreducibility theorem with one parameter X, one variable Y, and the single polynomial

f_1(X,Y)=Y^2-g(X).

The integer-specialization form used here says that an irreducible polynomial over \mathbb Q(X) has irreducible specializations at arbitrarily large positive integers X.

The source supplies this specialization and the conclusion; the quadratic factorization and descent of the square root to \mathbb Z[X] are left implicit.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Invoke Hilbert irreducibility over Q(X), infer quadratic reducibility and descend a rational-function square root to an integer polynomial. The compressed proof leaves the rational-function and Gauss-lemma descent implicit; this cumulative algebraic burden is not confidently a small extension, so retain beyond undergraduate.
