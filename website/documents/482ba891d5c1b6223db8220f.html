Statement

Let G be a finite simple graph (no loops or multiple edges). An induced subgraph on a vertex subset retains exactly the edges between those vertices. A clique is a set of pairwise adjacent vertices; its maximum size is the clique number. The chromatic number is the minimum number of colors in a vertex coloring assigning different colors to adjacent vertices. Call G perfect if every induced subgraph has equal clique and chromatic numbers.

Then G is perfect if and only if it contains neither an induced odd cycle of length at least five nor an induced subgraph isomorphic to the complement of such a cycle. The complement of a graph joins precisely the distinct pairs that were not adjacent.
