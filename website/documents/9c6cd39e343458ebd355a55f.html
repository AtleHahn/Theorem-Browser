Statement

Consider ten independent fair coin tosses. Let A be the event that three consecutive tosses are heads and B the event of at least five heads. Let C be the event that some consecutive triple of heads has at least five further heads outside that triple. Then \Pr(C)\le\Pr(A)\Pr(B).

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement uses explicit events in a finite ten-toss sample space and their probabilities.

Proof 1: Apply BK to the triple and the five remaining heads — outline

For a toss configuration, a coordinate set certifies an event if fixing the tosses in that set forces the event regardless of all other tosses. The disjoint occurrence A\square B means disjoint certificates for A and B. In this example the source identifies it as three consecutive heads together with another five heads after discarding those three; hence it is C. Apply the binary BK inequality for finite product probability spaces: \Pr(A\square B)\le\Pr(A)\Pr(B). The ten fair tosses have the required product distribution, yielding the stated bound.
