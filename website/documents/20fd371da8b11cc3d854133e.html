Statement

A metric space (X,d) is complete if and only if every decreasing sequence (C_k)_{k\ge1} of nonempty closed subsets with \operatorname{diam}(C_k)\to0 has nonempty intersection. Here \operatorname{diam}(C)=\sup\{d(x,y):x,y\in C\}. For any such sequence in a complete space, its intersection consists of exactly one point.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Basic point-set topology and real analysis supply compactness, closed sets, continuity, metrics and completeness. The Cantor-set construction or cofinite topology, where present, is a direct set-based example learnable briefly. The complete assertion needs no algebraic or differential topology.

Proof 1: Cauchy selections and closures of sequence tails (outline)

Completeness implies the intersection property

Since the diameters tend to zero, any two points in the intersection of the {\displaystyle C_{k}} have distance zero, so the intersection is either empty or consists of a single point. So it is sufficient to show that it is not empty. Pick an element {\displaystyle x_{k}\in C_{k}} for each {\displaystyle k}. Since the diameter of {\displaystyle C_{k}} tends to zero and the {\displaystyle C_{k}} are nested, the {\displaystyle x_{k}} form a Cauchy sequence. Since the metric space is complete this Cauchy sequence converges to some point {\displaystyle x}. Since each {\displaystyle C_{k}} is closed, and {\displaystyle x} is a limit of the tail sequence in {\displaystyle C_{k}}, {\displaystyle x} must lie in {\displaystyle C_{k}}. This is true for every {\displaystyle k}, and therefore the intersection of the {\displaystyle C_{k}} must contain {\displaystyle x}.

The intersection property implies completeness

To prove this, let (x_k)_{k\ge1} be a Cauchy sequence in X, and let C_k be the closure of the tail (x_j)_{j\ge k} of this sequence. The source leaves the verification of the diameter and convergence properties implicit.

Level justification

The forward argument is a Cauchy-sequence selection followed by closedness. Conversely, tail closures are nested and their diameters tend to zero; an intersection point is within every tail diameter of all later terms, proving convergence. These are standard undergraduate metric-space arguments.
