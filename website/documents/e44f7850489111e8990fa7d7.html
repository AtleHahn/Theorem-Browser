Statement

Let \mathbb T=\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Z) and let (x_j) be any countable dense sequence in \mathbb T. In C(\mathbb T,\mathbb C) with the uniform norm, the set of functions whose symmetric Fourier partial sums are unbounded at every x_j is residual and dense. Here \widehat f(k)=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_0^{2\pi}f(t)e^{-ikt}\,dt and S_N(f)(x)=\sum_{k=-N}^N\widehat f(k)e^{ikx}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex differentiability and the explicitly defined Fourier sums extend calculus directly. For the Fourier assertions, null sets can be understood through arbitrarily small interval covers, and residual sets through countable intersections of dense open sets. These limited additions to real analysis and topology suffice without general measure integration.

Proof 1: Unbounded Fourier evaluation norms and condensation — outline

Let \mathbb T be the circle, and let C(\mathbb T) be the Banach space of continuous functions on \mathbb T, with the uniform norm. For f\in C(\mathbb T), the N-th symmetric partial sum is

S_N(f)(x)=\sum_{k=-N}^N\widehat f(k)e^{ikx} =\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}f(t)D_N(x-t)\,dt,

where D_N is the N-th Dirichlet kernel,

D_N(t)=\frac{\sin((N+\tfrac12)t)}{\sin(t/2)},

with removable values at multiples of 2\pi. Fix x\in\mathbb T and consider the convergence of \{S_N(f)(x)\}. The functional \varphi_{N,x}:C(\mathbb T)\to\mathbb C defined by

\varphi_{N,x}(f)=S_N(f)(x),\qquad f\in C(\mathbb T),

is bounded. The norm of \varphi_{N,x}, in the dual of C(\mathbb T), is the total-variation norm of the signed measure (2\pi)^{-1}D_N(x-t)\,dt, namely

\|\varphi_{N,x}\|=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}|D_N(x-t)|\,dt =\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}|D_N(s)|\,ds.

It can be verified that

\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}|D_N(t)|\,dt \geq\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} \frac{|\sin((N+\tfrac12)t)|}{t/2}\,dt\longrightarrow\infty.

So the collection (\varphi_{N,x}) is unbounded in C(\mathbb T)^*, the dual of C(\mathbb T).

Therefore, by the uniform boundedness principle, for any x\in\mathbb T, the set of continuous functions whose Fourier series diverges at x is dense in C(\mathbb T).

More can be concluded by applying the principle of condensation of singularities: for countably many families of bounded linear maps on a Banach space, each unbounded in operator norm, the vectors on which every family is unbounded form a residual dense set. Let (x_j) be the prescribed dense sequence in \mathbb T. Define \varphi_{N,x_j} in the same way as above. The principle of condensation of singularities then says that the set of continuous functions whose Fourier partial sums are unbounded at each x_j is residual and dense in C(\mathbb T).
