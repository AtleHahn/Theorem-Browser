Statement

Let P,Q be events in a probability space with 0<\Pr(P)<1 and \Pr(Q^c)>0. Conditional probabilities mean \Pr(E\mid F)=\Pr(E\cap F)/\Pr(F). If \Pr(Q\mid P)=1, then \Pr(P^c\mid Q^c)=1.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement involves finitely many events, their unions, intersections or complements and elementary probability identities; conditional probabilities, when used, are explicitly defined. This rating concerns understanding the statement, independently of the difficulty of its proof.

Proof 1: Substitute a zero conditional into Bayes’ formula

Bayes’ formula in the supplied form is \Pr(P^c\mid Q^c)=\frac{\Pr(Q^c\mid P^c)\Pr(P^c)}{\Pr(Q^c\mid P^c)\Pr(P^c)+\Pr(Q^c\mid P)\Pr(P)}. Since \Pr(Q\mid P)=1, its complementary conditional satisfies \Pr(Q^c\mid P)=0. The denominator is positive by the stated conditional-probability domain, so the displayed ratio equals one.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Replace the complementary conditional probability by zero in the supplied Bayes formula and use positivity of its denominator. Undergraduate elementary event probability suffices; no measure construction or general conditional expectation is involved.
