Statement

For a convex Euclidean polygon with N\ge3 sides and interior vertex angles \alpha_v in radians, \sum_v\alpha_v=\pi(N-2).

Level
High school only
Level justification

Only elementary Euclidean polygon geometry is needed for the statement.

Proof 1: Collect the polygon vertex, edge and interior contributions

For a two-dimensional polygon, the Gram–Euler relation expands into:

\sum_v\alpha_v-\sum_e\pi+2\pi=0,

where the first term A=\sum_v\alpha_v is the sum of the internal vertex angles, the second sum is over the edges, each of which has internal angle \pi, and the final term corresponds to the entire polygon, which has a full internal angle 2\pi. For a polygon with N sides, the theorem tells us that A-\pi N+2\pi=0, or equivalently, A=\pi(N-2).

Level
High school only
Level justification

Use only the explicitly expanded two-dimensional Gram-Euler relation: sum vertex angles minus pi per edge plus a full turn is zero. School angle arithmetic suffices; higher-dimensional solid-angle machinery is not used in this specialization.
