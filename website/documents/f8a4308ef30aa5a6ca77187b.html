The variance bound relates attainable precision to the information in observations while explicitly accounting for estimator bias. It makes a limit on inference quantitative under regularity assumptions, and shows why precision cannot be assessed independently of bias.

Statement

Let \theta range over an open real interval and let X have a density f(x;\theta) on a parameter-independent support S\subseteq\mathbb R. Suppose the score V=\partial_\theta\log f(X;\theta) exists almost surely and I(\theta)=E_\theta[V^2] is finite and positive. Let T=t(X) have finite variance and differentiable mean \psi(\theta)=E_\theta T=\theta+b(\theta). Assume differentiation and integration commute for both normalization and the estimator mean: \partial_\theta\int_S f(x;\theta)\,dx=\int_S\partial_\theta f(x;\theta)\,dx,\qquad \partial_\theta\int_S t(x)f(x;\theta)\,dx=\int_S t(x)\partial_\theta f(x;\theta)\,dx. Then \operatorname{Var}_\theta(T)\ge[1+b^{\prime}(\theta)]^2/I(\theta).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

This is a single real density model: expectations and variances are integrals of a function and its centered square. The statement explicitly supplies the score, information and differentiation-interchange assumptions; bias is the difference of the mean and parameter. These linked definitions fit a brief extension of undergraduate calculus and integration, without general measure-theoretic probability or asymptotic statistics.

Proof 1: Substitute the prescribed mean bias

Consider an estimator T with bias b(\theta)=E_\theta T-\theta, and let \psi(\theta)=b(\theta)+\theta. By the scalar Cramér–Rao inequality, any estimator whose expectation is \psi(\theta) has variance greater than or equal to [\psi'(\theta)]^2/I(\theta). Thus, any estimator T whose bias is given by a function b(\theta) satisfies

\operatorname{var}_\theta(T)\geq\frac{[1+b'(\theta)]^2}{I(\theta)}.
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understand the full density model using E_theta h(X)=integral h(x) f(x;theta) dx and variance as the centered second moment. The score and information formulas and both differentiation-interchange hypotheses are supplied. Apply the stated scalar bound with psi=theta+b and differentiate to get 1+b prime; proving Cramér–Rao or the interchange theorem is not required.
