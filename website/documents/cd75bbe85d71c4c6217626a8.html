Statement

For a positive integer n, let e(n) be the least integer N such that every compact connected smooth n-manifold (Hausdorff and second-countable) admits a smooth embedding into \mathbb R^N. If n=2^k for an integer k\ge0, then e(n)=2n.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement quantifies over compact smooth manifolds and their smooth embeddings in Euclidean spaces.

Proof 1: Whitney upper bound and projective-space obstruction

Whitney's strong embedding theorem states that e(n)\leq2n. For n=2^k we have e(n)=2n, as the 2^k-dimensional real projective space shows: real projective space of dimension n cannot be embedded into real (2n-1)-space if n is a power of two. The embedding theorem and this nonembedding obstruction are external prerequisites.

Level justification

Combine Whitney embedding with a projective-space obstruction. General smooth manifolds, smooth embeddings and projective-space nonembedding require differential topology.
