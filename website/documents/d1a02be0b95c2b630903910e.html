Statement

For an acute Euclidean triangle with side lengths a,b,c and area S, 27(b^2+c^2-a^2)^2(c^2+a^2-b^2)^2(a^2+b^2-c^2)^2\le(4S)^6.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement uses elementary triangle geometry and real algebra.

Proof 1: Triangle tangent identity and AM–GM

Let A,B,C be the angles opposite a,b,c. Dividing both sides of the inequality by {\displaystyle 64(abc)^{4}}, we obtain:

{\displaystyle 27{\frac {(b^{2}+c^{2}-a^{2})^{2}}{4b^{2}c^{2}}}{\frac {(c^{2}+a^{2}-b^{2})^{2}}{4a^{2}c^{2}}}{\frac {(a^{2}+b^{2}-c^{2})^{2}}{4a^{2}b^{2}}}\leq {\frac {4S^{2}}{b^{2}c^{2}}}{\frac {4S^{2}}{a^{2}c^{2}}}{\frac {4S^{2}}{a^{2}b^{2}}}}

Using the formula {\displaystyle S={\tfrac {1}{2}}bc\sin {A}} for the area of triangle, and applying the cosines law to the left side, we get:

{\displaystyle 27(\cos {A}\cos {B}\cos {C})^{2}\leq (\sin {A}\sin {B}\sin {C})^{2}}

And then using the identity {\displaystyle \tan {A}+\tan {B}+\tan {C}=\tan {A}\tan {B}\tan {C}} which holds for the acute triangle under consideration, we transform the inequality above into:

{\displaystyle 27(\tan {A}\tan {B}\tan {C})\leq (\tan {A}+\tan {B}+\tan {C})^{3}}

Since the angles of the triangle are acute, the tangent of each corner is positive, which means that the inequality above is correct by AM-GM inequality.

Level justification

Convert sides and area to sines and cosines, use the triangle tangent identity and apply finite AM-GM. School trigonometry and algebraic inequalities suffice.
