Statement

For every positive element a of a C*-algebra A and integer n\geq1, there is exactly one positive b\in A with b^n=a.

Level

Level justification

Positivity and roots in a general C*-algebra require substantial operator-algebra preparation. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Scalar roots and composition

For each {\displaystyle n\ge1 }, the root function {\displaystyle f_{n}\colon \mathbb {R} _{0}^{+}\to \mathbb {R} _{0}^{+},x\mapsto {\sqrt[{n}]{x}}} is a continuous function on {\displaystyle \sigma (a)\subseteq \mathbb {R} _{0}^{+}}. If {\displaystyle b\;\colon =f_{n}(a)} is defined using the continuous functional calculus, then {\displaystyle b^{n}=(f_{n}(a))^{n}=(f_{n}^{n})(a)=\operatorname {Id} _{\sigma (a)}(a)=a} follows from the properties of the calculus. From the spectral mapping theorem follows {\displaystyle \sigma (b)=\sigma (f_{n}(a))=f_{n}(\sigma (a))\subseteq [0,\infty )}, i.e. {\displaystyle b} is positive. If {\displaystyle c\in A} is another positive element with {\displaystyle c^{n}=a=b^{n}}, then {\displaystyle c=f_{n}(c^{n})=f_{n}(b^{n})=b} holds, as the root function on the nonnegative real numbers is an inverse function to the function {\displaystyle z\mapsto z^{n}}.

Since f_n(0)=0, the functional calculus in the unitization gives f_n(a)\in A also when A is nonunital. The uniqueness calculation uses the composition rule for the continuous functional calculus.
