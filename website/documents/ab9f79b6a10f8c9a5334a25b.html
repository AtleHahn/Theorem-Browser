Statement

Let M=\overline\Omega be a compact C^2 manifold with boundary, with a C^1 Riemannian metric. Let u\in C^1(M,\mathbb R) and let X be a C^1 vector field. Write N for the outward unit normal and dV,dS for the Riemannian volume and induced boundary measures. Then \int_M\langle\operatorname{grad}u,X\rangle\,dV=-\int_Mu\operatorname{div}X\,dV+\int_{\partial M}u\langle X,N\rangle\,dS. In the proof, (f,h)=\int_Mfh\,dV for scalars, and (Y,Z)=\int_M\langle Y,Z\rangle\,dV for vector fields.

Proof 1: Integration by parts on a compact Riemannian manifold with boundary — outline

In local coordinates write g=\det(g_{ij}) in the expressions \sqrt g, and sum repeated indices.

We use the Einstein summation convention. By using a partition of unity, we may assume that {\displaystyle u} and {\displaystyle X} have compact support in a coordinate patch {\displaystyle O\subset {\overline {\Omega }}}. First consider the case where the patch is disjoint from {\displaystyle \partial \Omega }. Then {\displaystyle O} is identified with an open subset of {\displaystyle \mathbb {R} ^{n}} and integration by parts produces no boundary terms:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}(\operatorname {grad} u,X)&=\int _{O}\langle \operatorname {grad} u,X\rangle {\sqrt {g}}\,dx\\&=\int _{O}\partial _{j}uX^{j}{\sqrt {g}}\,dx\\[5pt]&=-\int _{O}u\partial _{j}\left({\sqrt {g}}X^{j}\right)\,dx\\[5pt]&=-\int _{O}u{\frac {1}{\sqrt {g}}}\partial _{j}\left({\sqrt {g}}X^{j}\right){\sqrt {g}}\,dx\\[5pt]&=\left(u,-{\frac {1}{\sqrt {g}}}\partial _{j}\left({\sqrt {g}}X^{j}\right)\right)\\[5pt]&=(u,-\operatorname {div} X).\end{aligned}}}

In the last equality we used the Voss–Weyl coordinate formula for the divergence, although the preceding identity could be used to define {\displaystyle -\operatorname {div} } as the formal adjoint of {\displaystyle \operatorname {grad} }. Now suppose {\displaystyle O} intersects {\displaystyle \partial \Omega }. Then {\displaystyle O} is identified with an open set in {\displaystyle \mathbb {R} _{+}^{n}=\{x\in \mathbb {R} ^{n}:x_{n}\geq 0\}}. We zero extend {\displaystyle u} and {\displaystyle X} to {\displaystyle \mathbb {R} _{+}^{n}} and perform integration by parts to obtain

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}(\operatorname {grad} u,X)&=\int _{O}\langle \operatorname {grad} u,X\rangle {\sqrt {g}}\,dx\\&=\int _{\mathbb {R} _{+}^{n}}\partial _{j}uX^{j}{\sqrt {g}}\,dx\\&=(u,-\operatorname {div} X)-\int _{\mathbb {R} ^{n-1}}u(x',0)X^{n}(x',0){\sqrt {g(x',0)}}\,dx',\end{aligned}}}

where {\displaystyle dx'=dx_{1}\cdots dx_{n-1}}. By a variant of the straightening theorem for vector fields, we may choose {\displaystyle O} so that {\displaystyle {\frac {\partial }{\partial x_{n}}}} is the inward unit normal {\displaystyle -N} at {\displaystyle \partial \Omega }. In this case {\displaystyle {\sqrt {g(x',0)}}\,dx'={\sqrt {g_{\partial \Omega }(x')}}\,dx'=dS} is the volume element on {\displaystyle \partial \Omega } and the above formula reads

{\displaystyle (\operatorname {grad} u,X)=(u,-\operatorname {div} X)+\int _{\partial \Omega }u\langle X,N\rangle \,dS.}
