Statement

Let P,Q be complete lattices and let f:P\times Q\to P, g:P\times Q\to Q be monotone in each variable. Write \mu z.h(z) for the least fixed point of a map h. Then the least fixed point of (f,g):P\times Q\to P\times Q in the coordinatewise order is (x_0,y_0), where x_0=\mu x.f(x,\mu y.g(x,y)) and y_0=\mu y.g(x_0,y). Equivalently y_0=\mu y.g(\mu x.f(x,y),y).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Undergraduate order and supremum concepts extend directly to complete lattices. Monotonicity, coordinatewise order and least fixed points then describe explicitly nested choices; a small finite lattice provides an example. No domain-theoretic topology is required for this formulation.

Proof 1: Nested fixed points and pre-fixed-point comparison

Use Knaster–Tarski: a monotone self-map of a complete lattice has a least fixed point, lying below every pre-fixed point. Its least fixed point is monotone in monotone parameters.

Taking {\displaystyle y_{0}=\mu y.g(x_{0},y)}, it is clear that {\displaystyle y_{0}=g(x_{0},y_{0})} since {\displaystyle y_{0}} is the fixed point. Similarly letting {\displaystyle x_{0}=\mu x.f(x,\mu y.g(x,y))}, we have {\displaystyle x_{0}=f(x_{0},\mu y.g(x_{0},y))=f(x_{0},y_{0})}. Hence {\displaystyle (x_{0},y_{0})} is a fixed point of {\displaystyle (f,g)}.

Furthermore, if there is a pre-fixed point {\displaystyle (x_{1},y_{1})} with {\displaystyle (x_{1},y_{1})\geq (f(x_{1},y_{1}),g(x_{1},y_{1}))}, then {\displaystyle x_{1}\geq f(x_{1},\mu y.g(x_{1},y))\geq x_{0}} and {\displaystyle y_{1}\geq \mu y.g(x_{1},y)\geq \mu y.g(x_{0},y)=y_{0}}; hence {\displaystyle (x_{1},y_{1})\geq (x_{0},y_{0})} and we conclude {\displaystyle (x_{0},y_{0})} is the least fixed point.

Interchanging the roles of P,f and Q,g gives the symmetric expression for y_0.
