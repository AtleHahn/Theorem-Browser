Statement

Suppose an autocorrelation r and spectral density S satisfy the representation r(\tau)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{2\pi i\tau f}S(f)\,df, and the hypotheses for ordinary Fourier inversion hold. Then S(f)=\int_{\mathbb R}r(\tau)e^{-2\pi i f\tau}\,d\tau at every point where that inversion theorem applies.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

This is explicitly a conditional inversion claim: the integral representation is assumed, and equality is asserted only where ordinary Fourier inversion applies. No existence of a spectral density for every process, universal convergence, or fixed unstated inversion theorem is claimed. For example, integrable S and r with S continuous at the evaluation point give a standard ordinary-inversion setting; this is an illustration, not an extra hypothesis silently inserted into the text. The full meanings of autocorrelation and spectral density involve correlation/second-moment or signal-energy concepts as well as Fourier integrals, so the cumulative preparation remains a genuine threshold concern. The cumulative prerequisite learning cannot confidently fit approximately 30 minutes beyond the stipulated undergraduate baseline. Assess Beyond undergraduate under the conservative rule, without requiring proofs of imported results.

Proof 1: Application of Fourier inversion

Assuming that r and S are “sufficiently nice” such that the Fourier inversion theorem is valid, the given representation

r(\tau)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{2\pi i\tau f}S(f)\,df

says that r and S are a Fourier transform pair, and

S(f)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}r(\tau)e^{-2\pi if\tau}\,d\tau.
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The assumed representation uses the positive exponential sign, so ordinary inversion gives the displayed negative-sign integral for S at the points covered by the assumed inversion theorem. Applicability is a premise, not an omitted claim that must be proved for every autocorrelation. There is no missing normalization factor with the 2 pi convention used here. Understanding the full autocorrelation/spectral-density setting in addition to the transform-pair inference cannot confidently fit the preparation allowance; assess Beyond undergraduate conservatively. General spectral-measure existence and a proof of inversion are not required.
