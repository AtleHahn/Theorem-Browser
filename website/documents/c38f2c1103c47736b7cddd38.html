Statement

Let N be a homogeneous Poisson point process on \mathbb R^d, where d\ge1 is an integer, with constant intensity \lambda>0. Let f:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R be measurable and s\ge0, with \int\min(|f|,1)\,dx<\infty and \int|e^{-sf}-1|\,dx<\infty. Then S=\sum_{x\in N}f(x) is almost surely absolutely convergent and \mathbb E[e^{-sS}]=\exp\!\left(-\lambda\int_{\mathbb R^d}(1-e^{-sf(x)})\,dx\right).

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Homogeneous point processes and their expectations require probability and measure theory.

Proof 1: Poisson exponential formula with a constant intensity density

Write \Lambda(dx)=\lambda\,dx for the intensity measure. Use the stated Poisson exponential formula: if \int\min(|f|,1)\,d\Lambda<\infty, then S=\sum_{x\in N}f(x) is almost surely absolutely convergent, and \mathbb E[e^{\theta S}]=\exp\!\left(\int(e^{\theta f(x)}-1)\,\Lambda(dx)\right) whenever the displayed integral converges. Set \theta=-s. Under the assumed convergence, rewrite e^{-sf}-1=-(1-e^{-sf}) to obtain the Laplace formula. For a homogeneous process \Lambda(dx)=\lambda\,dx. Substitute this density and take the constant \lambda outside the integral.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Set theta=-s in the Poisson exponential formula and insert the constant intensity density. Point processes, intensity measures and almost-sure convergence of random sums require graduate probability.
