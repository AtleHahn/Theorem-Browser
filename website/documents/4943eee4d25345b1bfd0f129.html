Statement

Let K be algebraically closed. Every maximal ideal of K[X_1,\ldots,X_n] equals (X_1-a_1,\ldots,X_n-a_n) for some (a_1,\ldots,a_n)\in K^n.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory ring theory supplies polynomial ideals and maximality. Algebraically closed means every nonconstant one-variable polynomial has a root; the extension here simply contains the coefficient field. The claims concern simultaneous polynomial zeros, requiring no Galois arithmetic or schemes.

Proof 1: Apply Zariski’s lemma to the residue field

Zariski's lemma asserts that if a field is finitely generated as an associative algebra over a field K, then it is a finite field extension of K (that is, it is also finitely generated as a vector space). If K is an algebraically closed field and {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {m}}} is a maximal ideal of the ring of polynomials {\displaystyle K[X_{1},\ldots ,X_{n}]}, then Zariski's lemma implies that {\displaystyle K[X_{1},\ldots ,X_{n}]/{\mathfrak {m}}} is a finite field extension of K, and thus, by algebraic closure, must be K. From this, it follows that there is an {\displaystyle a=(a_{1},\dots ,a_{n})\in K^{n}} such that {\displaystyle X_{i}-a_{i}\in {\mathfrak {m}}} for {\displaystyle i=1,\dots ,n}. In other words,

{\displaystyle {\mathfrak {m}}\supseteq {\mathfrak {m}}_{a}=(X_{1}-a_{1},\ldots ,X_{n}-a_{n})}

for some {\displaystyle a=(a_{1},\dots ,a_{n})\in K^{n}}. But {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {m}}_{a}} is clearly maximal, so {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {m}}={\mathfrak {m}}_{a}}. This is the weak Nullstellensatz: every maximal ideal of {\displaystyle K[X_{1},\ldots ,X_{n}]} for algebraically closed K is of the form {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {m}}_{a}=(X_{1}-a_{1},\ldots ,X_{n}-a_{n})} for some {\displaystyle a=(a_{1},\dots ,a_{n})\in K^{n}}.

Level justification

The proof applies the explicitly stated Zariski lemma to a maximal-ideal quotient field generated as an algebra by the coordinate images, then uses algebraic closedness. Ideals, quotient fields and finite-dimensional extensions are undergraduate algebra; the distinction between finite algebra generation and field degree is a small additional prerequisite. The lemma proof is not required.
