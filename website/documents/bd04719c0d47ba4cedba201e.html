Statement

Let f be smooth on (a-r,a+r), where r>0, and suppose a single constant M bounds |f^{(j)}(x)| there for all integers j\ge1. Then its Taylor polynomials at a converge uniformly to f on that interval.

Level

Level justification

These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. Proof 1: Use a factorial uniform error majorant — outline

Let P_k be the Taylor polynomial of order k at a, and write R_k(x)=f(x)-P_k(x). The Lagrange form of the remainder gives

|R_k(x)|\leq M\frac{|x-a|^{k+1}}{(k+1)!}\leq M\frac{r^{k+1}}{(k+1)!}

for all x\in(a-r,a+r). The second inequality is called a uniform estimate, because it holds uniformly for all x on the interval (a-r,a+r).

Here the constants bounding the derivatives can be chosen bounded above for fixed r and all k. Since Mr^{k+1}/(k+1)!\to0, the Taylor series of f converges uniformly to f on (a-r,a+r).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The Lagrange remainder is bounded uniformly by M r^(k+1)/(k+1)!, which tends to zero for fixed r. Taylor’s theorem and the definition of uniform convergence are standard undergraduate analysis.
