Statement

Let m,n>0. The intersection above the x-axis of the parabola y=x^2/m with the circle y^2+x(x-n/m^2)=0 has x-coordinate equal to the positive root of x^3+m^2x=n. Conversely that positive root gives this intersection with y=x^2/m.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The two conics and their intersection are given by explicit real coordinate equations.

Proof 1: Compare the two expressions for the squared ordinate — outline

For solving the cubic equation x3 + m2x = n where n > 0, Omar Khayyám constructed the parabola y = x2/m, the circle that has as a diameter the line segment [0, n/m2] on the positive x-axis, and a vertical line through the point where the circle and the parabola intersect above the x-axis. The solution is given by the length of the horizontal line segment from the origin to the intersection of the vertical line and the x-axis.

Multiplying the equation by x/m2 and regrouping the terms gives

{\displaystyle {\frac {x^{4}}{m^{2}}}=x\left({\frac {n}{m^{2}}}-x\right).}

The left-hand side is the value of y2 on the parabola. The equation of the circle being y2 + x(x − ⁠n/m2⁠) = 0, the right hand side is the value of y2 on the circle.

Level justification

Equate the two squared ordinates and cancel the positive x, with polynomial continuity and monotonicity ensuring the unique positive solution. Undergraduate real analysis and elementary conic algebra suffice.
