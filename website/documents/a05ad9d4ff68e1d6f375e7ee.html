Statement

Let n\in\{2,3\} and let \Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^n be a Lipschitz domain. Write H^1(\Omega) for the space of square-integrable functions with square-integrable weak first derivatives, and H_0^1(\Omega) for the closure of C_c^\infty(\Omega) in the norm

\|u\|_{H^1(\Omega)}^2=\int_\Omega\bigl(|u|^2+|\nabla u|^2\bigr)\,dx.

Here C_c^\infty(\Omega) consists of smooth functions with compact support in \Omega. For 1\le p<\infty, put \|u\|_{L^p(\Omega)}=(\int_\Omega|u|^p\,dx)^{1/p}; the gradient norm uses its Euclidean length.

There is a constant C>0, depending only on \Omega, such that every real-valued u\in H_0^1(\Omega) belongs to L^4(\Omega) and satisfies

\|u\|_{L^4(\Omega)}\le \begin{cases} C\|u\|_{L^2(\Omega)}^{1/2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^2(\Omega)}^{1/2},&n=2,\\ C\|u\|_{L^2(\Omega)}^{1/4}\|\nabla u\|_{L^2(\Omega)}^{3/4},&n=3. \end{cases}
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full statement requires Lebesgue function spaces, weak differentiation, Sobolev norm closure and boundary regularity. These interdependent concepts exceed a small extension of standard undergraduate analysis within 30 minutes, even with the displayed norm definitions. The statement is therefore beyond undergraduate level; no proof method is used to set this level.

  • These named interpolation bounds have independent significance in PDE estimates and the study of Navier–Stokes equations; they are the original signature assertions, not the weaker routine Sobolev-norm substitutions.

Proof availability

The source states both gradient-norm inequalities. Its proposed Gagliardo–Nirenberg specialization uses the full Sobolev norm and does not supply the argument needed to obtain the gradient norm. No usable proof of the stated inequalities is included in the source article.
