Statement

Let X be a normal topological space, A\subseteq X closed, and f:A\to\mathbb R bounded and continuous with c=\sup_{a\in A}|f(a)|>0. There is a continuous function g:X\to\mathbb R such that |g(x)|\le c/3 for every x\in X and |f(a)-g(a)|\le2c/3 for every a\in A.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Normality, closed sets and continuous real functions belong to basic topology. The separation and approximation bounds are explicit, requiring no further geometric or functional-analytic structures.

Proof 1: Separate the upper and lower threshold sets — outline

Write c_0=c and define

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}c_{0}&=\sup\{|f(a)|:a\in A\}\\E_{0}&=\{a\in A:f(a)\geq c_{0}/3\}\\F_{0}&=\{a\in A:f(a)\leq -c_{0}/3\}.\end{aligned}}}

Observe that {\displaystyle E_{0}} and {\displaystyle F_{0}} are closed subsets of X, since A is closed, and, because c_0>0, are disjoint subsets of {\displaystyle A}. By taking a linear combination of a Urysohn separating function for these disjoint closed sets, there exists a continuous function {\displaystyle g_{0}:X\to \mathbb {R} } such that

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}g_{0}&={\frac {c_{0}}{3}}{\text{ on }}E_{0}\\g_{0}&=-{\frac {c_{0}}{3}}{\text{ on }}F_{0}\end{aligned}}}

and furthermore

{\displaystyle -{\frac {c_{0}}{3}}\leq g_{0}\leq {\frac {c_{0}}{3}}}

on {\displaystyle X}. In particular, it follows that

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}|g_{0}|&\leq {\frac {c_{0}}{3}}\\|f-g_{0}|&\leq {\frac {2c_{0}}{3}}\end{aligned}}}

on {\displaystyle A}. Take g=g_0.
