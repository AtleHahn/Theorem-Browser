For integers 1\le n<k, the based homotopy group \pi_n(S^k) is trivial: every based continuous map S^n\to S^k is based nullhomotopic.

Proof 1: Sphere homotopy groups vanish below the dimension — outline

Cellular approximation: a continuous map between CW complexes is homotopic to a cellular map, and the homotopy may be fixed on any subcomplex on which it is already cellular.

Give {\displaystyle S^{n}} and {\displaystyle S^{k}} their canonical CW-structure, with one 0-cell each, and with one n-cell for {\displaystyle S^{n}} and one k-cell for {\displaystyle S^{k}.} Any base-point preserving map {\displaystyle f\colon S^{n}\to S^{k}} is then homotopic to a map whose image lies in the n-skeleton of {\displaystyle S^{k},} which consists of the base point only. That is, any such map is nullhomotopic.

Level justification

Use the relative cellular approximation theorem on the two-cell CW structures of spheres. CW skeleta, based homotopy groups and relative homotopies are cumulative algebraic-topology prerequisites.
