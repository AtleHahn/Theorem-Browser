Statement

For a convex quadrilateral let p,q be its diagonal lengths and m,n the lengths of the segments joining opposite side midpoints. Then p^2+q^2=2(m^2+n^2).

Level
High school only
Level justification

Proof 1: Parallelogram law in the midpoint figure

The midpoint quadrilateral is a parallelogram whose adjacent side lengths are p/2,q/2: in each triangle a segment joining two side midpoints is parallel to and half as long as the third side. Its diagonals are the two bimedians, of lengths m,n. The elementary parallelogram law gives m^2+n^2=2((p/2)^2+(q/2)^2), proving the formula.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Use the midpoint theorem to form a parallelogram and apply its length identity to its two diagonals. School plane geometry and algebra suffice.

Proof 2: Add the two bimedian length formulas

In a convex quadrilateral with sides a, b, c and d, the length of the bimedian that connects the midpoints of the sides a and c is

{\displaystyle m={\tfrac {1}{2}}{\sqrt {-a^{2}+b^{2}-c^{2}+d^{2}+p^{2}+q^{2}}}}

where p and q are the length of the diagonals. The length of the bimedian that connects the midpoints of the sides b and d is

{\displaystyle n={\tfrac {1}{2}}{\sqrt {a^{2}-b^{2}+c^{2}-d^{2}+p^{2}+q^{2}}}.}

Hence

{\displaystyle \displaystyle p^{2}+q^{2}=2(m^{2}+n^{2}).}
