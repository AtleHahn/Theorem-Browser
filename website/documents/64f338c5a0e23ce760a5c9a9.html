Statement

Let d,N\ge1 be integers and let \alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_d\in\mathbb R. The body S=\{(x,y_1,\ldots,y_d)\in\mathbb R^{d+1}:|x|\le N+\tfrac12,\ |\alpha_i x-y_i|\le N^{-1/d}\ (1\le i\le d)\} contains a nonzero integer point.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The set is explicitly defined by a finite list of real inequalities. The conclusion asks for integer coordinates, not all zero, satisfying those inequalities; understanding it requires elementary arithmetic, inequalities and positive roots, not the geometric theorem used in its proof.

Proof 1: Apply Minkowski to the displayed body — outline

Minkowski's theorem states that a centrally symmetric convex body in \mathbb R^m of volume greater than 2^m contains a nonzero integer point.

For d=1, apply Minkowski's theorem to the set

{\displaystyle S=\left\{(x,y)\in \mathbb {R} ^{2}:-N-{\frac {1}{2}}\leq x\leq N+{\frac {1}{2}},\vert \alpha_1 x-y\vert \leq {\frac {1}{N}}\right\}.}

Since the volume of {\displaystyle S} is greater than {\displaystyle 4}, Minkowski's theorem establishes the existence of a non-trivial point with integral coordinates. This proof extends naturally to simultaneous approximations by considering the set

{\displaystyle S=\left\{(x,y_{1},\dots ,y_{d})\in \mathbb {R} ^{1+d}:-N-{\frac {1}{2}}\leq x\leq N+{\frac {1}{2}},|\alpha _{i}x-y_{i}|\leq {\frac {1}{N^{1/d}}}\right\}.}
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Recognize the region as a linear shear of a box, verify central symmetry and convexity, and compute volume greater than 2 to the dimension. Linear algebra and multivariable volume suffice to apply the supplied lattice-point theorem.

Warning

This proof is essentially a specialization of a (potentially much) more general result and may therefore be considered unsatisfactory by some readers.
