Statement

For a finite simple graph H, let K(H) be the least nonnegative constant comparing the maximum of \sum_{uv\in E(H)}a_{uv}\langle x_u,x_v\rangle over unit vectors in a common real Euclidean space with the maximum of \sum_{uv\in E(H)}a_{uv}\varepsilon_u\varepsilon_v over signs, for every real edge-weight system. Then K_G^{\mathbb R}=\sup\{K(H):H\text{ is finite and bipartite}\}, where K_G^{\mathbb R} is the least uniform constant in the analogous bilinear two-family comparison for all real matrices.

Proof 1: The real Grothendieck constant from bipartite graphs — outline

The Grothendieck inequality of a graph is an extension of the Grothendieck inequality because the bilinear inequality is the special case of the graph inequality when the graph is bipartite with two copies of \{1,\ldots,n\} as its bipartition classes. Thus,

K_G^{\mathbb R}=\sup_{n\in\mathbb N}\{K(H):H\text{ is an }n\text{-vertex bipartite graph}\}.
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A bipartite weighted graph gives a coefficient matrix by filling missing cross-part edges with zeros; a matrix conversely gives a complete weighted bipartite graph. The vector and sign optimizations then agree term by term, giving the equality of best constants. Euclidean inner products, finite sums and suprema are undergraduate material. No proof of Grothendieck’s inequality or functional analysis is required.
