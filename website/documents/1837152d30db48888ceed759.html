Statement

Define f:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C by f(0)=0 and f(z)=\exp(-z^{-4}) for z\ne0. Its real partial derivatives exist everywhere and satisfy f_x+i f_y=0, but f is not continuous at zero.

Proof 1: Check the axes and a diagonal ray — outline

Looman pointed out that the function given by f(z)=\exp(-z^{-4}) for z\ne0, f(0)=0 satisfies the Cauchy–Riemann equations everywhere but is not analytic (or even continuous) at z=0.

The source supplies this example and its asserted properties without the partial-derivative and continuity checks.
