Statement

Let R be a GCD domain with fraction field F, and let f\in R[x] be nonconstant. Then f is irreducible in R[x] if and only if its coefficient gcd is a unit and f is irreducible in F[x].

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory ring theory supplies polynomial division, gcds, units, irreducibility and characteristic. A fraction field uses ordinary fractions over a domain; content is the gcd of coefficients. These closely related definitions suffice without advanced commutative algebra.

Proof 1: Irreducibility over a GCD domain and its fraction field — proof

Write \operatorname{cont}(h) for the ideal generated by the coefficients of h, and \operatorname{gcd}(I) for the principal ideal generated by a gcd of the generators of I. Here c(h) denotes a coefficient gcd, so \operatorname{gcd}(\operatorname{cont}(h))=(c(h)).

({\displaystyle \Rightarrow }) First note that the gcd of the coefficients of {\displaystyle f} is 1 since, otherwise, we can factor out some element {\displaystyle c\in R} from the coefficients of {\displaystyle f} to write {\displaystyle f=cf'}, contradicting the irreducibility of {\displaystyle f}. Next, suppose {\displaystyle f=gh} for some non-constant polynomials {\displaystyle g,h} in {\displaystyle F[x]}. Then, for some nonzero {\displaystyle d\in R}, the polynomial {\displaystyle dg} has coefficients in {\displaystyle R} and so, by factoring out the gcd {\displaystyle q} of the coefficients, we write {\displaystyle dg=qg'}. Do the same for {\displaystyle h} and we can write {\displaystyle f=cg'h'} for some {\displaystyle c\in F}. Now, let {\displaystyle c=a/b} for some {\displaystyle a,b\in R} with b\ne0. Then {\displaystyle bf=ag'h'}. From this, using multiplicativity of coefficient gcds, we get:

{\displaystyle (b)\supset \operatorname {gcd} (\operatorname {cont} (bf))=(a)}.

That is, {\displaystyle b} divides {\displaystyle a}. Thus, {\displaystyle c\in R} and then the factorization {\displaystyle f=cg'h'} constitutes a contradiction to the irreducibility of {\displaystyle f}.

({\displaystyle \Leftarrow }) If {\displaystyle f} is irreducible over {\displaystyle F}, then either it is irreducible over {\displaystyle R} or it contains a nonunit constant polynomial as a factor, the second possibility is ruled out by the assumption. {\displaystyle \square }
