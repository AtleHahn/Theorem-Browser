Statement

Let S\subset\mathbb Z be finite, let \gamma_m\in\mathbb R for m\in S, and define (Tu)_j=\sum_{m\in S}\gamma_m u_{j+m} for every real sequence (u_j)_{j\in\mathbb Z}. If p\in S and \gamma_p<0, then for any integer k, the nondecreasing sequence u_i=\mathbf1_{\{i\ge k\}} satisfies (Tu)_{k-p}-(Tu)_{k-p-1}=\gamma_p<0.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The operator is explicitly a finite indexed sum, and the bi-infinite step sequence is defined by an integer inequality.

Proof 1: Place the step at the chosen stencil offset

Assume that \gamma_p<0 and choose the following nondecreasing sequence:

u_i=0,\quad i<k;\qquad u_i=1,\quad i\geq k.

Then from the definition of T we get

(Tu)_j-(Tu)_{j-1} =\sum_{m\in S}\gamma_m(u_{j+m}-u_{j+m-1}).

Each summand is 0 if j+m\ne k, and \gamma_m if j+m=k. Now choose j=k-p, to give

(Tu)_{k-p}-(Tu)_{k-p-1} =\gamma_p(u_k-u_{k-1})=\gamma_p<0,

which implies that (Tu)_j is not nondecreasing. Thus monotonicity is not preserved for \gamma_p<0.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Subtract two finite sums and observe that the step sequence has just one nonzero consecutive difference. Elementary indexed arithmetic and inequalities suffice.
