Statement

Work in Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory with the axiom of choice (ZFC). Suppose 0^\sharp exists. Let

\aleph_\omega=\sup_{n<\omega}\aleph_n,\qquad A=\{\kappa<\aleph_\omega:\kappa\text{ is a cardinal}\}.

Then A is countable, but for every constructible set B containing A,

|B|\geq\aleph_\omega.

All cardinals and cardinalities here are computed in the ambient universe, not internally in the constructible universe. The set A includes the finite cardinals as well as the infinite cardinals \aleph_n for n<\omega.

An ordinal is a von Neumann ordinal, and a cardinal is the least ordinal of its cardinality. The symbol \omega is the first infinite ordinal; \aleph_0 is its cardinality and \aleph_{n+1} is the next larger infinite cardinal. Constructible means membership in

L=\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathrm{Ord}}L_\alpha,\quad L_0=\varnothing,\quad L_{\alpha+1}=\operatorname{Def}(L_\alpha),\quad L_\lambda=\bigcup_{\alpha<\lambda}L_\alpha\text{ for limit }\lambda,

where \operatorname{Def}(Y) consists of subsets definable over (Y,\in) by first-order formulas with parameters in Y. The hypothesis that 0^\sharp exists can equivalently be stated as existence of an uncountable set of ordinal order-indiscernibles for L: every parameter-free set-theoretic formula has the same truth value in L on all increasing tuples of the same finite length from that set.
