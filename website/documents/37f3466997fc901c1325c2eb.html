Statement

For an integer n\ge1, call A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\} primitive if no two distinct elements of A divide one another. The set \{\lceil(n+1)/2\rceil,\ldots,n\} is primitive and has maximum size \lceil n/2\rceil.

Proof 1: Powers-of-two chain partition — outline

A primitive set contains no two distinct integers one of which divides the other. The chain-partition bound used here says that such a set has at most as many elements as there are divisibility chains in a partition.

In the subset {\displaystyle \{\lceil (n+1)/2\rceil ,\dots n\}}, all pairs of numbers are within a factor of less than two of each other, so no two can be multiples. It includes approximately half of the numbers from {\displaystyle 1} to {\displaystyle n}. By Dilworth's theorem (using a partition of the integers into chains of powers of two multiplied by an odd number) this subset has maximum cardinality among all subsets in which no two are multiples.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Partition by odd part into doubling chains and compare with the upper-half interval. Prime divisibility and finite counting suffice; the full Dilworth theorem is unnecessary for the stated chain bound.
