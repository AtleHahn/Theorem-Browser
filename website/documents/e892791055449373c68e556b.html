Statement

Let n\ge2 and let \omega\subset\mathbb R^n be a connected C^2 submanifold. Set T(S)=S+i\mathbb R^n\subset\mathbb C^n. Let H be the affine hull of \omega, let V=\operatorname{int}_H\operatorname{conv}(\omega), and put A=\omega\cup V. Every continuous CR function f:T(\omega)\to\mathbb C extends to a continuous function F:T(A)\to\mathbb C whose restriction to T(V) is CR. Thus F=f on T(\omega) and is CR on both tube-manifold pieces of the almost convex hull. Here \operatorname{conv}(\omega) consists of finite convex combinations of points of \omega, and \operatorname{int}_H means interior in the affine subspace H, not necessarily in \mathbb R^n. All tube subsets have their subspace topologies from \mathbb C^n. For a tube submanifold M\subset\mathbb C^n, its antiholomorphic complex tangent bundle is T^{0,1}M=(\mathbb C TM)\cap T^{0,1}\mathbb C^n. A continuous function is CR when Lf=0 in the distributional sense for every local C^1 vector field L with values in this bundle; T^{0,1}\mathbb C^n is spanned by \partial/\partial\bar z_j. Distributional differentiation means testing against compactly supported smooth functions in local coordinates and using the formal transpose of the vector field. The target T(A) need not itself be a manifold: continuity on the union and the CR property on the two stated pieces give the meaning of the extension there.
