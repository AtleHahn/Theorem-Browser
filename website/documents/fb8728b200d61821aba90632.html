Statement

Let X be a nonnegative real m\times n matrix, where m,n\ge1 are integers, whose row sums r_i and column sums c_j are positive integers. Then X is a finite convex combination of nonnegative integer matrices with the same row and column sums. A finite convex combination is a sum \sum_{\ell=1}^k\theta_\ell Y_\ell with \theta_\ell\ge0 and \sum_\ell\theta_\ell=1.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Convex decompositions of arbitrary matrices require undergraduate linear algebra and the notion of a convex combination. A bright US college student should be able to understand the statement after about 30 minutes of focused background research; this rating concerns understanding the claim, not proving it.

Proof 1: Replicate both rows and columns — outline

Replicate columns as well as rows by the splitting procedure: split row i into r_i equal rows and column j into c_j equal columns. Apply the Birkhoff–von Neumann decomposition to the resulting doubly stochastic square matrix, and recombine the row and column groups. The resulting matrices need not be limited to entries 0 and 1.

Level justification

The outline uses row and column splitting, a finite doubly stochastic matrix decomposition and recombination. These are undergraduate finite matrix and convex-combination concepts. The stated equal-splitting recipe does not in general give a doubly stochastic matrix, so this level assessment does not certify the outlined construction.
