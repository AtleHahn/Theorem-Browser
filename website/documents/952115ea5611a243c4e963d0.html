Statement

Let \mathbb N=\{0,1,2,\ldots\} and I_m=\{0,1,\ldots,m-1\}. For a set X, [X]^n denotes its n-element subsets. Consider the uniform assertion:

For every integer n\geq1 and integer k\geq0, some integer m\geq0 has the property that every function f:[I_m]^n\to\mathbb N satisfying f(s)<\min(s) whenever 0\notin s admits a set H\subseteq I_m of cardinality k such that

s,t\in[H]^n\text{ and }\min(s)=\min(t)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad f(s)=f(t).

Here \min(s) is the least element of s; all such sets are nonempty because n\geq1.

This assertion, formulated as a first-order arithmetic sentence by coding finite sets and finite functions by natural numbers, is not provable in Peano arithmetic (PA). PA is classical first-order arithmetic with zero, successor, addition and multiplication, their usual defining axioms, the axioms that successor is injective and never zero, and the induction schema for every formula in this language. Nonprovability concerns the single uniform sentence quantifying over n and k, rather than a claim that each fixed numerical instance is unprovable.
