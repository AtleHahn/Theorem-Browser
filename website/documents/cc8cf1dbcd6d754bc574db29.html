Statement

Let \theta range over an open real interval and let X have a density f(x;\theta) on a parameter-independent support S\subseteq\mathbb R. Suppose the score V=\partial_\theta\log f(X;\theta) exists almost surely and I(\theta)=E_\theta[V^2] is finite and positive. Let T=t(X) have finite variance and mean \psi(\theta)=E_\theta T=\theta. Assume differentiation and integration commute for both normalization and the estimator mean: \partial_\theta\int_S f(x;\theta)\,dx=\int_S\partial_\theta f(x;\theta)\,dx,\qquad \partial_\theta\int_S t(x)f(x;\theta)\,dx=\int_S t(x)\partial_\theta f(x;\theta)\,dx. Then \operatorname{Var}_\theta(T)\ge1/I(\theta) and its efficiency e(T)=I(\theta)^{-1}/\operatorname{Var}_\theta(T) satisfies 0<e(T)\le1.

Level justification

Unbiasedness is explicitly mean theta and efficiency is explicitly a ratio of finite positive quantities. Understanding the density expectation, variance and supplied score/information formulas adds linked integral definitions to undergraduate calculus. The two interchange conditions are hypotheses, not theorems the reader must prove.

Proof 1: Zero bias and the efficiency ratio

The unbiased version of the bound is a special case of the prescribed-bias Cramér–Rao bound

\operatorname{var}_\theta(T)\geq\frac{[1+b'(\theta)]^2}{I(\theta)},

with b(\theta)=0.

The efficiency of an unbiased estimator T measures how close this estimator's variance comes to this lower bound; estimator efficiency is defined as

e(T)=\frac{I(\theta)^{-1}}{\operatorname{var}_\theta(T)}.

The Cramér–Rao lower bound thus gives

e(T)\leq1.

Since 0<I(\theta)<\infty and \operatorname{var}_\theta(T)\geq I(\theta)^{-1}>0 is finite, the efficiency is positive.
