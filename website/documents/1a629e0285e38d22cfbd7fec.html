Statement

In \mathrm{PG}(2,K), the projective plane over a field K, a nonempty oval \mathcal O meets each line in at most two points and has exactly one tangent (a line meeting it only there) at each of its points. Assume \operatorname{char}K\ne2. For three distinct oval points P_1,P_2,P_3, write t_i for the tangent at P_i and set P_4=t_1\cap P_2P_3, P_5=t_2\cap P_1P_3, P_6=t_3\cap P_1P_2. Property (P3) says these three intersection points are collinear for every such triangle. Then \mathcal O is a nondegenerate conic if and only if it has (P3).

Proof 1: Normalize coordinates and solve the tangent-slope identity — outline

Let the projective plane be coordinatized inhomogeneously over K such that P_3 is the vertical point at infinity, the line at infinity g_\infty is the tangent at P_3, (0,0)\in\mathcal O, the x-axis is the tangent at (0,0), and \mathcal O contains (1,1). Furthermore, set P_1=(x_1,y_1) and P_2=(x_2,y_2). The oval \mathcal O can be described by a function f:K\to K such that

\mathcal O=\{(x,y)\in K^2:y=f(x)\}\cup\{P_3\}.

The tangent at (x_0,f(x_0)) will be described using a function d such that its equation is

y=d(x_0)(x-x_0)+f(x_0).

Here d is a tangent-slope function, not a derivative. Hence

P_5=(x_1,d(x_2)(x_1-x_2)+f(x_2)),\qquad P_4=(x_2,d(x_1)(x_2-x_1)+f(x_1)).

I: If \mathcal O is a nondegenerate conic we have f(x)=x^2 and d(x)=2x, and one calculates that P_4,P_5,P_6 are collinear.

II: If \mathcal O is an oval with property (P3), the slope of the line P_4P_5 is equal to the slope of the line P_1P_2. For x_1\ne x_2, this means

d(x_2)+d(x_1)-\frac{f(x_2)-f(x_1)}{x_2-x_1} =\frac{f(x_2)-f(x_1)}{x_2-x_1},

and hence

(d(x_2)+d(x_1))(x_2-x_1) =2(f(x_2)-f(x_1))\tag{i}

for all x_1,x_2\in K. The identity also holds when x_1=x_2, since both sides are zero.

With f(0)=d(0)=0 one gets

d(x_2)x_2=2f(x_2),\tag{ii}

and from f(1)=1 we get

d(1)=2.\tag{iii}

Equations (i) and (ii) yield

d(x_2)x_1=d(x_1)x_2,\tag{iv}

and with (iii), taking x_1=1, we get

d(x_2)=2x_2\qquad\text{for all }x_2\in K.\tag{v}

A consequence of (ii) and (v), since \operatorname{char}K\ne2, is

f(x_2)=x_2^2,\qquad x_2\in K.

Hence \mathcal O is a nondegenerate conic.
