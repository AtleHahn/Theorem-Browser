Statement

For every Riemannian metric g on the two-dimensional torus T^2,

\operatorname{sys}(T^2,g)^2\le\frac{2}{\sqrt3}\operatorname{area}(T^2,g),

where the systole is the least length of a closed loop that is not contractible to a point. Equality holds if and only if the Riemannian torus is isometric, after multiplying all lengths by a positive constant, to the flat torus

\mathbb C/(\mathbb Z+\mathbb Z e^{2\pi i/3})

with its Euclidean quotient metric. In particular, the constant 2/\sqrt3 is optimal.

Proof 1: Conformal uniformization and averaging closed loops (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Chapter12, Theorem12.3.2 and Sections12.4–12.7, especially Proposition12.7.3 and Corollaries12.7.4–12.7.5).

The horizontal loops need only be geodesics for the flat metric: their noncontractibility gives the required lower bound on their lengths for g. The rectangle is a fundamental region up to boundary identifications, whose edges have area zero. Equality forces both \operatorname{Var}(f)=0 and \operatorname{Im}\tau=\sqrt3/2, since the systole is positive. Thus f is constant and the reduced lattice is hexagonal. Conversely, the lattice generated by 1 and e^{2\pi i/3} has shortest nonzero vector of length one and area \sqrt3/2, proving equality and optimality.

The source sketches a variance approach, but only says that the mean conformal factor can be related to the systole by Fubini’s theorem. It supplies neither that required estimate nor the full equality argument. The existing flat-torus calculation proves sharpness only, so no complete proof of the stated theorem is retained.
