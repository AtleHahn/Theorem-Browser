Statement

Let \zeta denote the Riemann zeta function. There is a real number a<1/2 such that, for every fixed 0<\varepsilon<0.001, there exist c=c(\varepsilon)>0 and T_0=T_0(\varepsilon)>0 for which every T\ge T_0, with H=T^{a+\varepsilon}, has at least cH\log T zeros of \zeta(1/2+it) with real ordinate t\in(T,T+H). Thus the base exponent 1/2 can be lowered in this stated short-interval sense.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The result counts zeros of zeta on its critical line, requiring the meromorphic continuation and its zero meaning in addition to elementary exponent bounds. That preparation remains uncertain. The cumulative prerequisite preparation cannot confidently fit approximately 30 minutes beyond the stipulated undergraduate baseline. Under the conservative threshold rule, the classification is therefore assessed as Beyond undergraduate level. Uncertainty about the exact preparation time is retained in this justification but no longer leaves the educational assessment provisional.

Proof 1: Compare the Karatsuba exponent with one half — outline

Use Karatsuba’s zero-count estimate for H=T^{a+\varepsilon} with a=27/82=1/3-1/246 and 0<\varepsilon<0.001: for sufficiently large T, the interval (T,T+H) contains at least cH\log T critical-line zeros. This value of a is below 1/2, so the stated lower-exponent assertion follows.
