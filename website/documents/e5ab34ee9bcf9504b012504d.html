Statement

Let ABC be a nondegenerate plane triangle. Construct equilateral triangles BXC, CYA and AZB externally on its sides, and let L, M and N be their respective centroids. Then LMN is equilateral.

Proof 1: Compare two thirty-degree spiral similarities — outline

Choose the orientation of the plane so that A,B,C occur counterclockwise. A quick way to see that \triangle LMN is equilateral is to observe that MN becomes CZ under a clockwise rotation of 30^\circ around A and a homothety of ratio \sqrt3 with the same center, and that LN also becomes CZ after a counterclockwise rotation of 30^\circ around B and a homothety of ratio \sqrt3 with the same center. The respective spiral similarities are

A(\sqrt3,-30^\circ),\qquad B(\sqrt3,30^\circ).

That implies MN=LN and the angle between them must be 60^\circ.

Proof 2: Specialize the hexagon theorem by identifying three pairs of vertices — outline

Use the source-stated Dao Than Oai generalization, including the repeated-vertex specialization explicitly prescribed there: for a hexagon PQRSTU with equilateral triangles PQG, SHR and ITU constructed consistently on alternate sides, the centroids of UGR, QHT and SIP form an equilateral triangle. Set P=A, Q=B, R=B, S=C, T=C and U=A. Then the three erected triangles are ABG, CHB and ICA, so in the outer Napoleon construction G=Z, H=X and I=Y. The three centroid triangles UGR, QHT and SIP are consequently AZB, BXC and CYA. Their centroids are N,L,M, respectively. The generalized theorem therefore says that NLM is equilateral.
