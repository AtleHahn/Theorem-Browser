Statement

In the affine plane over a commutative field K, let A,B,C be three distinct points on a line g and a,b,c three distinct points on a distinct line h, none at g\cap h when that intersection exists. If Ab\parallel aB and Bc\parallel bC, then Ac\parallel aC.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The affine plane over a field is K2 with lines given by linear equations. Introductory linear algebra supplies intersections and parallel directions, so the incidence implication adds no projective or algebraic-geometric machinery.

Proof 1: Coordinates for intersecting and parallel base lines

Because of parallelism in an affine plane one has to distinguish two cases: g\not\parallel h and g\parallel h. The key for a simple proof is the possibility of introducing a suitable coordinate system.

Case 1: The lines g,h intersect at the point S=g\cap h. In this case coordinates are introduced such that S=(0,0), A=(0,1), c=(1,0). The points B,C have the coordinates B=(0,\gamma), C=(0,\delta), \gamma,\delta\notin\{0,1\}.

From the parallelism of the lines Bc,Cb one gets b=(\delta/\gamma,0), and the parallelism of the lines Ab,Ba yields a=(\delta,0). Hence line Ca has slope -1 and is parallel to line Ac.

Case 2: g\parallel h (little theorem). In this case the coordinates are chosen such that c=(0,0), b=(1,0), A=(0,1), B=(\gamma,1), \gamma\ne0. From the parallelism of cB\parallel bC and Ab\parallel Ba one gets C=(\gamma+1,1) and a=(\gamma+1,0), respectively, and finally the parallelism Ac\parallel Ca.

Level justification

Normalize affine coordinates in the intersecting and parallel cases and compare direction vectors over the given commutative field. Undergraduate linear algebra and abstract fields suffice.
