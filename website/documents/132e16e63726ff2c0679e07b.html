Statement

Let I=[-h,h], let functions F_k satisfy |F_k|\leq2C on a rectangle, and suppose y_{k,j+1}(t)=\int_0^tF_k(s,y_{k,j}(s))\,ds are defined there, starting with y_{k,0}=0. Then all iterates have Lipschitz constant at most 2C on I.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves differential equations; integration; uniform estimates. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Subtract integrals over ordered endpoints

The functions y_{k,j} are equicontinuous as, for -h\le t<t'\le h, we have

\begin{aligned} |y_{k,j+1}(t')-y_{k,j+1}(t)| &\le\int_t^{t'}|F_k(t'',y_{k,j}(t''))|\,dt''\\ &\le |t'-t|\sup_R|F_k|\le2C|t'-t|, \end{aligned}

where R is the rectangle on which the iterates are defined. The zero initial iterate satisfies the same bound.

Level justification

Subtract the two integral equations and bound the integrand by its common supremum. Undergraduate calculus suffices.
