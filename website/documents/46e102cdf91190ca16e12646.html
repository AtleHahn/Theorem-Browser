Statement

Every k-algebra automorphism \alpha of a finite-dimensional central simple k-algebra B is inner: \alpha(a)=bab^{-1} for some b\in B^\times and every a\in B.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Abstract algebra and linear algebra supply rings, ideals, vector spaces and homomorphisms. A finite-dimensional k-algebra is a vector space with compatible multiplication; simple excludes proper nonzero two-sided ideals and central identifies the center with k. These closely related definitions, units and the displayed conjugation formula fit about 30 minutes without Brauer or representation theory.

Proof 1: Specialize the two homomorphisms

Apply Skolem–Noether with A=B, f=\operatorname{id}_B and g=\alpha. Its conclusion is exactly \alpha(a)=bab^{-1}. Here the invoked theorem says that two unital k-algebra homomorphisms from a simple k-algebra into a finite-dimensional central simple k-algebra are conjugate by a unit of the target.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Starting from introductory rings and vector spaces, a finite-dimensional k-algebra is a vector space with compatible multiplication; simple means no proper nonzero two-sided ideal and central means its center is k. These closely related definitions suffice to apply the explicitly stated conjugacy theorem with identity and alpha. No structure or Brauer theory is used.
