Statement

Let T preserve a probability space and f\in L^1. Then n^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}f(T^jx) has an almost-everywhere finite T-invariant limit.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement requires measure theory and ergodic transformations.

Proof 1: Additive specialization of Kingman (outline)

Set g_n(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}f(T^jx) as in the source application. This process is additive, hence subadditive, so Kingman’s theorem gives an invariant limit. Applying the same theorem to the additive process -g_n excludes a negative infinite limit for g_n/n; Kingman already excludes a positive infinite limit.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof concerns L1 functions on a measure-preserving probability system and almost-everywhere invariant limits. These measure-theoretic ergodic prerequisites are substantial even though additivity immediately implies subadditivity.
