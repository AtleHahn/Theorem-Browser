Statement

For an open set \Omega\subset\mathbb R^n, W^{1,p}(\Omega) denotes the Sobolev space of L^p functions whose first weak partial derivatives are in L^p. For p<\infty, W_0^{1,p}(\Omega) is the closure of smooth compactly supported functions in the W^{1,p} norm. All norms and integrals use Lebesgue measure.

Zero boundary values. If 1\le p<\infty and \Omega is bounded in at least one direction (contained in a slab between two parallel hyperplanes), there is C=C(\Omega,p) such that

\|u\|_{L^p(\Omega)}\le C\|\nabla u\|_{L^p(\Omega)} \quad\text{for every }u\in W_0^{1,p}(\Omega).

Subtracting the mean (Poincaré–Wirtinger). If 1\le p\le\infty and \Omega is a nonempty bounded connected open set with Lipschitz boundary, there is C=C(\Omega,p) such that

\|u-u_\Omega\|_{L^p(\Omega)}\le C\|\nabla u\|_{L^p(\Omega)} \quad\text{for every }u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega),\qquad u_\Omega=\frac1{|\Omega|}\int_\Omega u(x)\,dx.

Here |\Omega| is its volume and a Lipschitz boundary is locally a graph of a Lipschitz function.

Proof 1: Integration along segments and overlapping domain pieces (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Corollary1.3 and Sections3.1–3.3, with the finite-p density and infinity-endpoint adaptations below).

For zero boundary values, use Royer, Theorem2.50, pp.30–31. Integrating along the slab direction and applying Hölder gives the estimate for smooth compactly supported functions, including p=1; the defining W_0^{1,p} density gives the general case.

For the mean inequality, take constant exponent p in Corollary1.3 and pass to W^{1,p} by smooth density. Keep the radial integration in Lemma3.1 restricted to r\le\operatorname{diam}D before discarding the endpoint indicator. In LemmaA.4 the sphere-area factor is n\omega_n; the larger displayed (n+1)\omega_n still gives a valid upper bound. A finite star-shaped cover can be obtained directly from Lipschitz graph patches: choose a small base ball and a horizontal top above the graph by more than its Lipschitz oscillation, making the patch star-shaped with respect to a ball near the top; finitely many such patches cover a boundary neighborhood, and finitely many interior balls cover the remainder.

For p=\infty, the constants in (1.4) and Remark1.4 have bounded pth roots as p\to\infty. Apply the finite-p inequalities to u\in W^{1,\infty} and take the limits of both norms. If the cover has one member, repeat that member so that the overlap minimum in (1.5) is defined.
