Statement

Let G be a finite undirected graph and A,B\subseteq V(G). An AB-path starts in A, ends in B, and has no internal vertex in A\cup B; a single vertex in A\cap B is allowed. An AB-separator is a vertex set meeting every such path, and may meet A or B. Then the minimum separator size equals the maximum number of pairwise vertex-disjoint AB-paths.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Paths, disjointness and separators are explicitly finite combinatorial definitions. The extremal equality needs no college prerequisite.

Proof 1: Edge deletion, paired separators and path concatenation — outline

An AB-connector of size k is a union of k vertex-disjoint AB-paths. Let k be the minimum size of an AB-separator. Every separator meets every path of a connector, so a connector has size at most k.

Induction on the number of edges in G. For G with no edges, the minimum AB-separator is A ∩ B, which is itself an AB-connector consisting of single-vertex paths.

For G having an edge e, we may assume by induction that the Theorem holds for G−e. If G−e has a minimum AB-separator of size k, then there is an AB-connector of size k in G−e, and hence in G.

Otherwise, let S be an AB-separator of G−e of size less than k, so that every AB-path in G contains a vertex of S or the edge e. The size of S must be k−1, since if it was less, S together with either endpoint of e would be a better AB-separator of G. In G−S there is an AB-path through e, since S alone is too small to be an AB-separator of G. Let v1 be the earlier and v2 be the later vertex of e on such a path. Then v1 is reachable from A but not from B in G−S−e, while v2 is reachable from B but not from A.

Now, let S1 = S ∪ {v1}, and consider a minimum AS1-separator T in G−e. Since v2 is not reachable from A in G−S1, T is also an AS1-separator in G. Then T is also an AB-separator in G (because every AB-path intersects S1). Hence it has size at least k. By induction, G−e contains an AS1-connector C1 of size k. Because of its size, the endpoints of the paths in it must be exactly S1.

Similarly, letting S2 = S ∪ {v2}, a minimum S2B-separator has size k, and there is an S2B-connector C2 of size k, with paths whose starting points are exactly S2.

Furthermore, since the interiors of the paths in the two connectors lie on the disjoint A-reachable and B-reachable sides of G−S−e, every path in C1 is internally disjoint from every path in C2, and we can define an AB-connector of size k in G by concatenating paths (k−1 paths through S and one path going through e=v1v2). Trim each concatenated path to its last encounter with A and its first subsequent encounter with B, if necessary. Q.E.D.
