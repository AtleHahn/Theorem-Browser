Statement

Let A be a real random variable with \Pr(m\le A\le M)=1 for finite real bounds m\le M. With \mu=\mathbb E[A] and \sigma^2=\mathbb E[(A-\mu)^2], one has \sigma^2\le(M-\mu)(\mu-m).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

From real analysis, a bounded real random variable can be read as a measurable real-valued quantity with a probability law, and expectation as its probability-weighted integral. The displayed formulas define its mean and variance; probability one means exceptional outcomes of probability zero can be ignored. Learning these closely related bounded-average notions is a small extension within about 30 minutes. The statement does not require convergence of random variables, construction of measures or limit theorems.

Proof 1: Expand the nonnegative endpoint product

Since {\displaystyle m\leq A\leq M} almost surely,

{\displaystyle 0\leq \mathbb {E} [(M-A)(A-m)]=-\mathbb {E} [A^{2}]-mM+(m+M)\mu }.

Thus,

{\displaystyle \sigma ^{2}=\mathbb {E} [A^{2}]-\mu ^{2}\leq -mM+(m+M)\mu -\mu ^{2}=(M-\mu )(\mu -m)}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof uses positivity and linearity of expectation for a bounded real random variable, then the elementary variance identity and quadratic factorization. Introductory undergraduate probability suffices; all moments exist because the variable is bounded almost surely. No measure-space construction or limiting expectation argument is used.
