Statement

Let X_1,X_2,\ldots be independent discrete symbols, with mass functions p_i, and suppose \operatorname{Var}(\log p_i(X_i))<M for one finite M>0 and all i. Set p_n(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=\prod_{i=1}^n p_i(x_i) and \overline H_n=n^{-1}H(X_1,\ldots,X_n). Then for every \varepsilon>0, \Pr(|-n^{-1}\log p_n(X_1,\ldots,X_n)-\overline H_n|>\varepsilon)\to0.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The formulation combines arbitrary discrete distributions, independence, variance of logarithmic information, joint entropy and a limiting tail probability. Proof 1: Variance bound and Markov’s inequality

The proof follows from a simple application of Markov's inequality applied to the squared centered sum of the independent information variables, whose second moment is the variance displayed below.

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\Pr \left[\left|-{\frac {1}{n}}\log p_n(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots ,X_{n})-{\overline {H}}_{n}\right|>\varepsilon \right]&\leq {\frac {1}{n^{2}\varepsilon ^{2}}}\mathrm {Var} \left[\sum _{i=1}^{n}\log(p_i(X_{i}))\right]\\&\leq {\frac {M}{n\varepsilon ^{2}}}\to 0{\text{ as }}n\to \infty \end{aligned}}}

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Centering the logarithmic information variables uses their expectations and the definition of joint entropy. Independence makes the variance additive, and Markov applied to the squared average gives M/(n epsilon^2), tending to zero.
