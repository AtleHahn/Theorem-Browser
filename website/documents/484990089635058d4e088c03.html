Statement

Let d\geq1 and let F be a real homogeneous polynomial of degree 2d whose complex projective zero set C\subseteq\mathbb{CP}^2 is nonsingular. Suppose its real locus C(\mathbb R)\subseteq\mathbb{RP}^2 has g+1 connected components, where g is the genus of the compact complex curve C; this is the maximal, or M-curve, condition. Let p be the number of its even ovals and n the number of its odd ovals, defined by nesting parity below. Then

p-n\equiv d^2\pmod8.

An oval is a component of the real curve that bounds a topological disk in \mathbb{RP}^2; in this even-degree nonsingular setting all real components are ovals. An oval is even if it lies inside the disk bounded by an even number of other ovals, and odd otherwise. These are the positive and negative oval counts in the congruence; they refer to nesting, not a choice of orientation.

The real or complex projective plane consists of nonzero triples over the indicated field modulo nonzero scalar multiplication. Nonsingularity means that the three first partial derivatives of F do not vanish simultaneously at any complex projective point of F=0. The genus g is determined by the Euler characteristic 2-2g of the underlying compact oriented real surface of C. Congruence modulo 8 means that p-n-d^2 is divisible by 8.
