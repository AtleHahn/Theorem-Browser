Statement

Let 0<\varepsilon<1/2, P=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2) and Q=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2+\varepsilon) on \{0,1\}, where a Bernoulli measure of parameter t assigns probability t to 1. For positive probability masses, put D_{\rm KL}(P\parallel Q)=\sum_{x=0}^1P(x)\log(P(x)/Q(x)). For each integer n\ge1, let P^n,Q^n be the distributions of n independent tosses: for w=(w_1,\ldots,w_n)\in\{0,1\}^n, P^n(w)=\prod_iP(w_i) and Q^n(w)=\prod_iQ(w_i). Define d_{\rm TV}(P^n,Q^n)=\sup_{A\subseteq\{0,1\}^n}|P^n(A)-Q^n(A)|. Then d_{\rm TV}(P^n,Q^n)^2\le1-\exp\left[-\frac n2\log\frac1{1-4\varepsilon^2}\right].

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Relative entropy, total variation, product distributions or infinite-value conventions require introductory undergraduate probability/information theory, even when given by finite sums. A bright US college student should be able to understand the statement after about 30 minutes of focused background research; this rating concerns understanding the claim, not proving it.

Proof 1: Bretagnolle–Huber and product entropy — outline

Use the Bretagnolle–Huber bound d_{\rm TV}(R,S)^2\le1-e^{-D_{\rm KL}(R\parallel S)} and additivity of relative entropy for independent product laws. The calculation is

\begin{aligned} d_{\rm TV}(P^n,Q^n)^2&\le1-e^{-D_{\rm KL}(P^n\parallel Q^n)}\\ &=1-e^{-nD_{\rm KL}(P\parallel Q)}\\ &=1-\exp\left[-\frac n2\log\frac1{1-4\varepsilon^2}\right]. \end{aligned}

Level justification

All laws are finite probability tables and the distance is a maximum over finitely many events. Apply the supplied entropy bound and product additivity, then compute a two-term logarithmic sum. These explicitly defined information quantities form a small extension of undergraduate discrete probability.
