Statement

Let a,b,c be the positive side lengths of a nondegenerate Euclidean triangle, opposite interior angles \alpha,\beta,\gamma. Put s=(a+b+c)/2. Then \dfrac{\cot(\alpha/2)}{s-a}=\dfrac{\cot(\beta/2)}{s-b}=\dfrac{\cot(\gamma/2)}{s-c}=\sqrt{\dfrac{s}{(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All objects and manipulations are elementary triangle trigonometry.

Proof 1: Take the positive root and cyclically relabel

The stated half-angle square identity becomes

\tan^2(\alpha/2)=\frac{(s-b)(s-c)}{s(s-a)}.

All factors are positive, and \tan(\alpha/2)>0. Taking the positive square root, inverting and dividing by s-a gives

\frac{\cot(\alpha/2)}{s-a}=\sqrt{\frac{s}{(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}}.

Cyclically relabeling proves the other equalities.
