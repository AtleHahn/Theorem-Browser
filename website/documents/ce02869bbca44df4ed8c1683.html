Medium

Choice guarantees maximal extensions of families whose admissibility is determined by finite subsets. This connects finite compatibility with global nonconstructive existence and explains the foundational role of maximality principles beyond particular algebraic applications.

Statement

Assume the axiom of choice. Let Z be a set, let \mathcal F\subseteq\mathcal P(Z) be a family of subsets of Z, and let X\in\mathcal F. Suppose \mathcal F has finite character: for every A\subseteq Z,

A\in\mathcal F\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{every finite subset of }A\text{ belongs to }\mathcal F.

Then there exists Y\in\mathcal F with X\subseteq Y that is maximal under inclusion in \mathcal F: if Y\subseteq Y'\in\mathcal F, then Y'=Y. Here \mathcal P(Z) denotes the set of all subsets of Z.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Undergraduate set language suffices for all displayed quantifiers. Finite character and inclusion-maximality are defined explicitly and do not require ordinal or cardinal arithmetic. Understanding the axiom of choice as the existence of a choice function for a family of nonempty sets is a small additional reading burden; no proof of its independence or equivalence to other principles is needed.

Proof 1: Chain unions and maximal extension (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Section2, Lemma2.1 and Theorems2.2–2.3, pp.5–6).

Apply the chain-union argument to \mathcal F_X=\{Y\in\mathcal F:X\subseteq Y\}. It contains X. For a nonempty chain, every finite subset of its union lies in one chain member, so finite character puts the union in \mathcal F_X. The empty chain has upper bound X. Zorn’s lemma gives a maximal member of \mathcal F_X, and every larger member of \mathcal F would also contain X, so maximality holds in all of \mathcal F.
