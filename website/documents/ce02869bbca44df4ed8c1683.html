Statement

Assume the axiom of choice. Let Z be a set, let \mathcal F\subseteq\mathcal P(Z) be a family of subsets of Z, and let X\in\mathcal F. Suppose \mathcal F has finite character: for every A\subseteq Z,

A\in\mathcal F\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{every finite subset of }A\text{ belongs to }\mathcal F.

Then there exists Y\in\mathcal F with X\subseteq Y that is maximal under inclusion in \mathcal F: if Y\subseteq Y'\in\mathcal F, then Y'=Y. Here \mathcal P(Z) denotes the set of all subsets of Z.

Level justification

Undergraduate set language suffices for all displayed quantifiers. Finite character and inclusion-maximality are defined explicitly and do not require ordinal or cardinal arithmetic. Understanding the axiom of choice as the existence of a choice function for a family of nonempty sets is a small additional reading burden; no proof of its independence or equivalence to other principles is needed.
