Statement

Assume the Frey–Mazur conjecture in the form that, for primes p>17, two elliptic curves over \mathbb Q with isomorphic mod-p Galois representations are isogenous over \mathbb Q. Then such curves have the same conductor; in particular their isomorphic residual representations cannot give a strict conductor decrease between elliptic curves.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Conductors, isogenies and residual Galois representations together require substantial arithmetic-geometry background. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Use conductor invariance under the assumed isogeny

By the assumed Frey–Mazur conjecture the two curves are isogenous. Isogenous elliptic curves over \mathbb Q have the same conductor. Therefore a strict decrease in conductor is impossible.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the explicitly assumed conjecture and conductor invariance. Elliptic curves, isogenies, residual Galois representations and conductors require advanced arithmetic geometry despite the two-step implication.
