Statement

There is a bijection from [0,1] onto [0,1). Here a bijection is a function assigning each target point to exactly one domain point.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Both real intervals and the meaning of bijection are given explicitly. The assertion asks for a one-to-one pairing covering exactly the half-open target interval. Understanding that statement needs elementary functions and interval membership; a theory of infinite cardinal numbers is unnecessary.

Proof 1: Shift the powers-of-two sequence — outline

Let {\displaystyle f:[0,1]\to [0,1)} with {\displaystyle f(x)=x/2}; and {\displaystyle g:[0,1)\to [0,1]} with {\displaystyle g(x)=x}; the two injective functions.

For the sequence-shifting construction, put {\displaystyle C_{0}=\{1\},\;C_{k}=\{2^{-k}\},\;C=\bigcup _{k=0}^{\infty }C_{k}=\{1,{\tfrac {1}{2}},{\tfrac {1}{4}},{\tfrac {1}{8}},...\}}

Then {\displaystyle h(x)={\begin{cases}{\frac {x}{2}},&\mathrm {for} \ x\in C\\x,&\mathrm {for} \ x\in [0,1]\smallsetminus C\end{cases}}} is a bijective function from {\displaystyle [0,1]\to [0,1)}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The formula shifts 1,1/2,1/4,... one place and fixes every other point. Checking that the two parts have disjoint images and cover the half-open interval uses only powers of two and one-to-one correspondence.
