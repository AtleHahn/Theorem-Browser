Statement

Let (Y_n)_{n\geq1} be real random variables on a probability space, and put S_n=\sum_{j=1}^nY_j.

If the variables are independent and identically distributed, with \mathbb E Y_n=0 and \operatorname{Var}(Y_n)=1, then

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{|S_n|}{\sqrt{2n\log\log n}}=1\quad\text{with probability }1.

The following formulation allows different distributions. Suppose the variables are independent, have mean zero and finite variances, and put B_n=\sum_{j=1}^n\operatorname{Var}(Y_j). Assume B_n\to\infty and that there are positive constants M_n with |Y_n|\leq M_n with probability 1 for every n, such that

\frac{M_n}{\sqrt{B_n/\log\log B_n}}\longrightarrow0.

Then

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{|S_n|}{\sqrt{2B_n\log\log B_n}}=1\quad\text{with probability }1.

All logarithms are natural; the expressions involving iterated logarithms are taken for sufficiently large n that their radicands are positive. The limit superior is the limit of the suprema over successive tails of the sequence.

Level justification

Undergraduate probability and real-analysis sequences suffice; probability-one equality and limsup are spelled out, and the small-o condition is written as an ordinary limit. No martingale or stochastic-process theory is needed for these precise statements.
