Statement

Let X\subset\mathbf P^n_k be a nonempty smooth quasi-projective variety over an algebraically closed field k, and let |H|=(\mathbf P^n_k)^* be the projective space parametrizing hyperplanes. There is a dense Zariski-open subset U\subset |H| such that every H\in U does not contain X and its section X\cap H is smooth (possibly empty).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves smooth quasi-projective varieties; algebraically closed fields; dual projective space; Zariski-open dense subsets; hyperplane sections. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Incidence-family dimension argument — outline

We consider the subfibration of the product variety {\displaystyle X\times |H|} with fiber above {\displaystyle x\in X} the linear system of hyperplanes that intersect X non-transversally at x.

For d=\dim X<n, the fiber dimension is n-d-1, one less than the codimension of {\displaystyle X\subset \mathbf {P} ^{n}}, so that the total space has dimension n-1, less than {\displaystyle n} and so its projection is contained in a divisor of the complete system {\displaystyle |H|}.

The complementary open subset has transverse, hence smooth, sections; hyperplanes containing X are excluded by the same incidence condition. If d=n, no hyperplane contains the embedded tangent space, so the incidence fibers are empty.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof uses embedded tangent spaces of smooth varieties, an incidence family as a projective bundle, dimensions of morphism images and the algebraic transversality criterion. These are linked algebraic-geometric prerequisites beyond the undergraduate allowance. The final dimension inequality is not a substitute for understanding this framework.

Hyperplanes containing a fixed projective d-plane form a projective space of dimension n−d−1 when d<n
Kind:

Theorem

Treatment:

Implicitly assumed

Statement used:

Hyperplanes in P^n containing a fixed projective d-plane form P^(n-d-1) when d<n; when d=n there are none.

Application in proof:

Compute the dimension of each bad-incidence fibre.

Application level:

The calculation is linear algebra, but interpreting the tangent-space incidence family requires projective algebraic geometry.

Dimension of a projective bundle and dimension bound for the closure of a morphism image
Kind:

Theorem

Treatment:

Implicitly assumed

Statement used:

A projective bundle of fibre dimension r over a d-dimensional variety has dimension d+r; the closure of the image of a finite-type morphism has dimension at most its source.

Application in proof:

Bound the closure of the bad-hyperplane image by n-1, leaving a dense open subset of the n-dimensional dual projective space.

Application level:

Beyond undergraduate level

Level justification:

This is algebraic dimension theory for bundles and morphism images.

Review notes:

For quasi-projective X, the incidence projection need not have closed image. Taking its Zariski closure and using the dimension bound supplies the required open complement.
