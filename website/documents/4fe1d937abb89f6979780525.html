Statement

Let f:\mathbb N\to\mathbb N be time-constructible: a deterministic Turing machine on input 1^n halts after exactly f(n) steps. For a deterministic Turing machine M with encoding [M], define

H_f=\{([M],x):M\text{ accepts }x\text{ within }f(|x|)\text{ steps}\}.

Then H_f is decidable.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Time constructibility is explicitly a clocked halting machine. Encoded transition tables and finite simulation add a limited computational model without resource-class hierarchy prerequisites.

Proof 1: Finite clocked simulation

We can decide membership of H_f by way of a deterministic Turing machine R, that simulates M for f(|x|) steps by first calculating f(|x|) and then writing out a row of 0s of that length, and then using this row of 0s as a "clock" or "counter" to simulate M for at most that many steps. At each step, the simulating machine needs to look through the definition of M to decide what the next action would be. It accepts precisely when M accepts within the clock limit, and rejects otherwise.

Level justification

Compute the given finite clock and simulate one transition at a time until acceptance or expiry. Elementary algorithms and a finite transition-table machine model suffice; time constructibility is defined explicitly and no complexity hierarchy is used.
