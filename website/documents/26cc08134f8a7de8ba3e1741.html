Statement

Let K be a field of characteristic two or three and f(x)=ax^3+bx^2+cx+d\in K[x], a\ne0. Suppose its formal derivative f' is nonconstant. In an algebraic closure, f' has exactly one distinct root r. If f has a multiple root, it must be r, and the remaining third root counted after two copies of r is -b/a-2r.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory algebra supplies polynomial factorization, multiplicity and field characteristic. An algebraic closure only supplies all polynomial roots here, and a primitive cube root is a nonidentity root of X cubed minus one. The displayed finite formulas need no Galois or extension-degree theory.

Proof 1: Use the unique derivative root and Vieta’s sum — outline

Use the fact that a multiple root is a common root of the polynomial and its formal derivative. In these characteristics, if the derivative is not a constant, it is a linear polynomial in characteristic 3, and is the square of a linear polynomial in characteristic 2. Therefore, for either characteristic 2 or 3, the derivative has only one root. This allows computing the multiple root, and the third root can be deduced from the sum of the roots, which is provided by Vieta's formulas.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Compute the formal derivative in characteristics two and three, use its unique distinct root and recover the remaining root by Vieta. An algebraic closure is used only to contain polynomial roots. This is introductory field and polynomial algebra.
