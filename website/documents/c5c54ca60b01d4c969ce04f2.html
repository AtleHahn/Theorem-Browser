Statement

Let G=(V,E) be a finite undirected graph, allowing repeated edges and loops. Its vertices form V, and each edge has two ends attached to vertices. Write d(v) for the number of attached ends, counting a loop twice. If every vertex has the same odd degree r, then |V| is even.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Graph vertices, edges and degrees are locally explained; proofs use finite counting, parity and induction.

Proof 1: Parity in the degree-sum formula

The degree sum formula implies that every r-regular graph with n vertices has nr/2 edges. Because the number of edges must be an integer, it follows that when r is odd the number of vertices must be even.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The degree sum nr is even, so odd r forces even n. Elementary parity and endpoint counting suffice.
