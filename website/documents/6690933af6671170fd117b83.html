Statement

In classical first-order logic, for a binary predicate p, \exists y\,\forall x\,p(x,y)\ \vdash\ \forall x\,\exists y\,p(x,y).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Ordinary undergraduate quantifiers suffice: a single witness working for every x supplies a witness for each x. No sequent-calculus machinery is needed to understand this displayed entailment.

Proof 1: Introduce the four quantifiers with fresh eigenvariables

A sequent \Gamma\vdash\Delta means that if all formulas in the finite list \Gamma are true, at least one in the finite list \Delta is true. An empty left list is true and an empty right disjunction is false. Use the four standard quantifier introduction rules of LK. Universal left and existential right may instantiate a formula by a term free for its variable. Existential left and universal right use an eigenvariable that is not free in their conclusion. Starting from identity, the derivation is

p(x,y)\vdash p(x,y),\forall x\,p(x,y)\vdash p(x,y)\qquad(\forall L),\forall x\,p(x,y)\vdash\exists y\,p(x,y)\qquad(\exists R),\exists y\,\forall x\,p(x,y)\vdash\exists y\,p(x,y)\qquad(\exists L),\exists y\,\forall x\,p(x,y)\vdash\forall x\,\exists y\,p(x,y)\qquad(\forall R).

The eigenvariable y of the existential-left step and x of the universal-right step are not free in their respective conclusions.
