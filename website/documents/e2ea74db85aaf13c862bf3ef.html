Statement

Let p range over primes, and let \log denote the natural logarithm. The following three assertions hold.

  1. For every integer n\ge2,
\left|\sum_{p\le n}\frac{\log p}{p}-\log n\right|\le2.
  1. There is a real constant M, called the Meissel–Mertens constant, such that for every integer n\ge2,
\left|\sum_{p\le n}\frac1p-\log\log n-M\right| \le\frac4{\log(n+1)}+\frac2{n\log n}.

In particular, \sum_{p\le n}1/p-\log\log n\to M. 3. With \gamma=\lim_{m\to\infty}(\sum_{j=1}^m1/j-\log m), the Euler–Mascheroni constant,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\log n\prod_{p\le n}\left(1-\frac1p\right)=e^{-\gamma}.

Level justification

The exact estimates use elementary arithmetic and logarithms, while the full product asymptotic and constants require standard undergraduate limits of sequences. No analytic number theory is needed to understand them.
