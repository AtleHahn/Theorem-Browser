Statement

Let p range over primes, and let \log denote the natural logarithm. The following three assertions hold.

  1. For every integer n\ge2,
\left|\sum_{p\le n}\frac{\log p}{p}-\log n\right|\le2.
  1. There is a real constant M, called the Meissel–Mertens constant, such that for every integer n\ge2,
\left|\sum_{p\le n}\frac1p-\log\log n-M\right| \le\frac4{\log(n+1)}+\frac2{n\log n}.

In particular, \sum_{p\le n}1/p-\log\log n\to M. 3. With \gamma=\lim_{m\to\infty}(\sum_{j=1}^m1/j-\log m), the Euler–Mascheroni constant,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\log n\prod_{p\le n}\left(1-\frac1p\right)=e^{-\gamma}.
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The exact estimates use elementary arithmetic and logarithms, while the full product asymptotic and constants require standard undergraduate limits of sequences. No analytic number theory is needed to understand them.

Proof 1: Factorial estimates, partial summation and the Euler product (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Sections3.2–3.5,3.6.2,3.7–3.8).

All prime sums here include 2. In §3.5, use the displayed Stirling comparison for x\ge5 and check 1<x<5 directly; this gives \psi(x)<2x and the first estimate. For the second estimate, a convenient corrected remainder calculation is as follows. Put A(m)=\sum_{p\le m}\log p/p, E_m=A(m)-\log m, and h_m=m^{-\rho}/\log m. Summation by parts gives

\sum_{p>n}p^{-1-\rho}-\sum_{m>n}\frac{m^{-1-\rho}}{\log m} =-E_nh_{n+1}+\sum_{m>n}E_m(h_m-h_{m+1})+\sum_{m>n}\left(\log\frac m{m-1}-\frac1m\right)h_m.

Since |E_m|\le2, the right side has absolute value at most 4/\log(n+1)+1/(2n\log(n+1)). Replacing the integer sum by its integral costs at most 1/(n\log n). As \rho\downarrow0, that integral is \log(1/\rho)-\log\log n-\gamma+o(1). The Euler product gives \sum_p p^{-1-\rho}=\log(1/\rho)-H+o(1), where H=\sum_p\sum_{k\ge2}1/(kp^k). Hence M=\gamma-H and the stated error bound follows. Finally, taking logarithms of the finite product gives \log\log n-\sum_{p\le n}1/p-\sum_{p\le n,k\ge2}1/(kp^k)\to-\gamma, proving the third assertion.
