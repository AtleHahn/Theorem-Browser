Statement

Primitive recursive arithmetic, augmented by quantifier-free transfinite induction up to \varepsilon_0, proves the consistency of first-order Peano arithmetic:

\mathrm{PRA}+\mathrm{TI}_{\mathrm{qf}}(\varepsilon_0)\ \vdash\ \operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{PA}).

Here \mathrm{PA} is first-order arithmetic with the Peano axioms and induction for every first-order formula. \mathrm{PRA} is arithmetic with symbols and defining equations for primitive recursive functions and quantifier-free induction. The ordinal \varepsilon_0 is the least solution of \omega^\alpha=\alpha, the supremum of \omega,\omega^\omega,\omega^{\omega^\omega},\ldots.

Use the usual effective Cantor-normal-form notations for ordinals below \varepsilon_0, encoded by natural numbers, with their primitive recursive ordering <_{\varepsilon_0}. The additional induction schema says, for each quantifier-free predicate A on these notations (allowing parameters), that if A(a) follows from A(b) for every b<_{\varepsilon_0}a, for every notation a, then A holds for all such notations. The conclusion \operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{PA}) is the arithmetical assertion that no natural number codes a formal PA derivation of a contradiction, such as 0=1.

Proof availability

Source proof sketch and limitations

The source describes a primitive recursive reduction procedure on PA derivations and an assignment of ordinal notations below \varepsilon_0. A derivation of contradiction would yield further contradiction derivations with strictly decreasing assigned ordinals. Quantifier-free transfinite induction rules out that descent, so no contradiction derivation exists.

The article does not define the reduction procedure or ordinal assignment, nor prove that every reduction preserves the relevant derivation and strictly decreases its ordinal. Those are the substantive ingredients of Gentzen's proof. This section retains the source's ordinal-descent strategy without supplying an invented reduction construction.
