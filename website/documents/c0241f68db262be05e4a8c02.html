Statement

Let a,b,c be positive integers with a^2+b^2=c^2 and \gcd(a,b,c)=1. Each of the following triples is again positive and primitive Pythagorean: T_1(a,b,c)=(a-2b+2c,2a-b+2c,2a-2b+3c), T_2(a,b,c)=(a+2b+2c,2a+b+2c,2a+2b+3c), T_3(a,b,c)=(-a+2b+2c,-2a+b+2c,-2a+2b+3c).

Proof 1: Three transformations preserve primitive Pythagorean triples — outline

The three linear transformations are:

Transformation new side a new side b new side c
T1: a − 2b + 2c 2a − b + 2c 2a − 2b + 3c
T2: a + 2b + 2c 2a + b + 2c 2a + 2b + 3c
T3: −a + 2b + 2c −2a + b + 2c −2a + 2b + 3c

In other words, every primitive triple will be a "parent" to three additional primitive triples. Starting from the initial node with a = 3, b = 4, and c = 5, the operation T1 produces the new triple

(3 − (2×4) + (2×5), (2×3) − 4 + (2×5), (2×3) − (2×4) + (3×5)) = (5, 12, 13),

and similarly T2 and T3 produce the triples (21, 20, 29) and (15, 8, 17).

The source asserts preservation of primitive triples and illustrates it here; it does not supply the general verification.
