Statement

Let X be an algebraic variety over a field, regarded as a scheme, and suppose X is quasi-compact and H^1(X,\mathcal I)=0 for every quasi-coherent ideal sheaf \mathcal I\subseteq\mathcal O_X. Then X is an affine variety.

Level

Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Algebraic varieties as schemes; Quasi-coherent ideal sheaves; First sheaf cohomology; Affine varieties; Quasi-compact schemes. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Proof 1: Specialize the scheme criterion

Serre's theorem on affineness states that a scheme X is affine if X is quasi-compact and, for every quasi-coherent ideal sheaf \mathcal I of \mathcal O_X-modules, H^1(X,\mathcal I)=0.

A special case of this theorem arises when X is an algebraic variety, in which case the conditions of the theorem imply that X is an affine variety.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Specialize the quoted affine-scheme criterion to a variety. Quasi-coherent ideal sheaves and sheaf cohomology on schemes are essential graduate algebraic geometry, even when the specialization is immediate.
