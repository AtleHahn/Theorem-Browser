Statement

Let (X,\mathcal A,P) be a probability space and H a nonempty finite or countable family of measurable maps h:X\to\{0,1\}. For an m-sample r, write \widehat Q_r(h)=m^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^m h(r_i) and Q(h)=\int h\,dP. For \varepsilon>0 and an integer m\ge2/\varepsilon^2, define V=\{r:\exists h\in H,\ |Q(h)-\widehat Q_r(h)|\ge\varepsilon\} and R=\{(r,s):\exists h\in H,\ |\widehat Q_r(h)-\widehat Q_s(h)|\ge\varepsilon/2\}. Then P^m(V)\le2P^{2m}(R).

Level justification

The statement uses arbitrary probability measures and random samples.

Proof 1: An independent sample and Chebyshev

By the triangle inequality, if {\displaystyle |Q(h)-{\widehat {Q}}_{r}(h)|\geq \varepsilon } and {\displaystyle |Q(h)-{\widehat {Q}}_{s}(h)|\leq \varepsilon /2} then {\displaystyle |{\widehat {Q}}_{r}(h)-{\widehat {Q}}_{s}(h)|\geq \varepsilon /2}.

Therefore,

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}&P^{2m}(R)\\[5pt]\geq {}&P^{2m}\{\exists h\in H,|Q(h)-{\widehat {Q}}_{r}(h)|\geq \varepsilon {\text{ and }}|Q(h)-{\widehat {Q}}_{s}(h)|\leq \varepsilon /2\}\\[5pt]={}&\int _{V}P^{m}\{s:\exists h\in H,|Q(h)-{\widehat {Q}}_{r}(h)|\geq \varepsilon {\text{ and }}|Q(h)-{\widehat {Q}}_{s}(h)|\leq \varepsilon /2\}\,dP^{m}(r)\\[5pt]={}&A\end{aligned}}}

since {\displaystyle r} and {\displaystyle s} are independent.

Now for {\displaystyle r\in V} fix an {\displaystyle h\in H} such that {\displaystyle |Q(h)-{\widehat {Q}}_{r}(h)|\geq \varepsilon }. For this {\displaystyle h}, we shall show that

{\displaystyle P^{m}\left\{|Q(h)-{\widehat {Q}}_{s}(h)|\leq {\frac {\varepsilon }{2}}\right\}\geq {\frac {1}{2}}.}

Thus, integrating the pointwise bound over r\in V, {\displaystyle A\geq {\frac {P^{m}(V)}{2}}} and hence {\displaystyle P^{2m}(R)\geq {\frac {P^{m}(V)}{2}}}.

Notice, {\displaystyle m\cdot {\widehat {Q}}_{s}(h)} is a binomial random variable with expectation {\displaystyle m\cdot Q(h)} and variance {\displaystyle m\cdot Q(h)(1-Q(h))}. By Chebyshev's inequality we get

{\displaystyle P^{m}\left\{|Q(h)-{\widehat {Q_{s}(h)}}|>{\frac {\varepsilon }{2}}\right\}\leq {\frac {m\cdot Q(h)(1-Q(h))}{(\varepsilon m/2)^{2}}}\leq {\frac {1}{\varepsilon ^{2}m}}\leq {\frac {1}{2}}}

for the mentioned bound on {\displaystyle m}. Here we use the fact that {\displaystyle x(1-x)\leq 1/4} for 0\leq x\leq1.

All the events are measurable because H is finite or countable.
