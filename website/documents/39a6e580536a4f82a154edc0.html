Statement

Let n\ge1 be an integer and order \mathbb N_0^n coordinatewise: a\le b means a_i\le b_i for every i. Every nonempty subset S\subseteq\mathbb N_0^n has a finite nonempty set of minimal elements, where a\in S is minimal if b\in S and b\le a imply b=a.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Partial orders, minimal elements, chains and antichains are undergraduate discrete-mathematical concepts; the finite or coordinatewise setting does not remove that abstraction. A bright US college student should be able to understand the statement after about 30 minutes of focused background research; this rating concerns understanding the claim, not proving it.

Proof 1: Monomial encoding and Hilbert’s basis theorem — outline

The tuples {\displaystyle (a_{1},a_{2},\dots ,a_{n})} in {\displaystyle \mathbb {N}_0 ^{n}} correspond one-for-one with the monomials {\displaystyle x_{1}^{a_{1}}x_{2}^{a_{2}}\dots x_{n}^{a_{n}}} over a set of {\displaystyle n} variables {\displaystyle x_{1},x_{2},\dots x_{n}}. Under this correspondence, Dickson's lemma may be seen as a special case of Hilbert's basis theorem stating that every polynomial ideal has a finite basis, for the ideals generated by monomials.

Here the polynomial ring is over a field; coordinatewise comparison of exponent tuples is monomial divisibility.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Starting with undergraduate polynomial rings and ideals, encode tuples by monomials. The extra ingredients are the stated finite-generation conclusion and the explicit divisibility criterion; these are a small related extension, without learning a proof of the Hilbert basis theorem or Groebner theory.
