Statement

Given n noncollinear Euclidean planar points, an ordinary line can be found with O(n^3) distance evaluations and comparisons, treating each real-coordinate geometric operation as constant cost. An ordinary line contains exactly two of the given points.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The plane geometry and ordinary-line condition are elementary. The cost model counts explicitly bounded distance evaluations and comparisons on finite triples.

Proof 1: Enumerate point-line triples

Kelly's proof of the existence of an ordinary line can be turned into an algorithm that finds an ordinary line by searching for the closest pair of a point and a line through two other points, restricting to positive distances. The time for this closest-pair search is O(n^3), based on a brute-force search of all triples of points.

Here the imported Kelly criterion says that a connecting line having the minimum positive distance from one of the given points is ordinary.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Enumerate the finitely many point-line triples and apply the explicitly supplied minimum-positive-distance criterion. Elementary plane geometry and counting at most a constant times n cubed operations suffice; Big-O is only the stated cubic bound.
