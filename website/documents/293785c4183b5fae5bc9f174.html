Statement

Let f:\mathbb N_{>0}\to(0,\infty), and let \varphi(q) be Euler’s totient, the number of integers 1\le a\le q coprime to q. If \sum_{q=1}^\infty\varphi(q)f(q)/q<\infty, then for Lebesgue-almost every real \alpha the inequality |\alpha-p/q|<f(q)/q has only finitely many solutions in integers p,q with q>0 and \gcd(p,q)=1.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Totient and the approximation inequalities are explicit. On the real line, almost everywhere means outside a set coverable by intervals of arbitrarily small total length, a short extension of undergraduate real analysis. The statement needs this null-set meaning and convergent nonnegative series, not general measure integration.

Proof 1: Borel–Cantelli convergence implication — outline

This direction follows from the first Borel–Cantelli lemma: if measurable sets have a finite sum of measures, their limsup has measure zero. Apply it to the rational-approximation sets for the denominators q.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

On a unit interval the approximation sets are finite unions of intervals whose total lengths are bounded by a constant times phi(q)f(q)/q. The supplied first Borel-Cantelli conclusion concerns a null limsup; this interval-length covering application is a small extension of real analysis, without general probability processes or integration.
