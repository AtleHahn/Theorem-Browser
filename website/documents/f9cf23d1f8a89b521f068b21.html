Statement

Let S be a closed surface and A\subset S a closed annular neighbourhood of a simple closed curve. Let f:A\to A_0=\{z\in\mathbb C:1\le|z|\le2\} be a homeomorphism, and put \tau_0(z)=e^{2\pi i|z|}z. Then the map \tau:S\to S equal to f^{-1}\tau_0f on A and to the identity on S\setminus A is a homeomorphism.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Basic topology supplies homeomorphisms and subspaces, and complex arithmetic supplies rotations. A surface and an annular neighborhood can be understood through a locally planar space containing a copy of the given circle strip. The map is specified explicitly on that strip and is the identity elsewhere, without bundle or differential-geometric prerequisites.

Proof 1: Conjugate the annular map and paste along its fixed boundary

On the canonical annulus A_0=\{z\in\mathbb C:1\leq|z|\leq2\}, one can define a diffeomorphism \tau_0 by the formula

\tau_0(z)=e^{2i\pi|z|}z,

which is the identity on both boundary components \{|z|=1\},\{|z|=2\}.

The homeomorphism f from A to the canonical annulus A_0 is used to define a homeomorphism \tau of S as follows: on S\setminus A it is the identity, and on A it is equal to f^{-1}\circ\tau_0\circ f.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The canonical map preserves radius and has inverse z maps to exp(-2 pi i |z|)z. Both maps fix the boundary radii, so conjugation by the given homeomorphism and the closed-set pasting lemma produce continuous inverse maps on S. Only basic topology, complex arithmetic and the concrete meaning of an annular neighborhood are needed; no surface classification or mapping-class theory is used.
