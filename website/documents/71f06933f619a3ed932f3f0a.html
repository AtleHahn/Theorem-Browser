Statement

Let U\subseteq\mathbb C be open, let f:U\to\mathbb C be holomorphic, and suppose the closed disk \overline D=\{z:|z-z_0|\le r\}, with r>0, lies in U. Let \gamma be its counterclockwise boundary circle. For every integer n\ge0 and every a inside the disk,

f^{(n)}(a)=\frac{n!}{2\pi i}\oint_\gamma\frac{f(z)}{(z-a)^{n+1}}\,dz.
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Multivariable calculus supplies parametrized line integrals, and real analysis supplies derivatives. A complex contour integral is the integral of f(gamma(t))gamma-prime(t); holomorphic means complex differentiable. For the explicitly given circles or smooth curves, these are closely related extensions of familiar calculus, with no residue or Laurent-series machinery needed.

Proof 1: Differentiate the geometric-kernel representation — outline

Cauchy's integral formula gives

f(a)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_\gamma\frac{f(z)}{z-a}\,dz.

Because {\textstyle {\frac {1}{z-a}}} can be expanded as a power series in the variable {\displaystyle a} as

{\displaystyle {\frac {1}{z-a}}={\frac {1+{\frac {a-z_0}{z-z_0}}+\left({\frac {a-z_0}{z-z_0}}\right)^{2}+\cdots }{z-z_0}},}

it follows that holomorphic functions are analytic, i.e. they can be expanded as convergent power series. In particular {\displaystyle f} is actually infinitely differentiable, with

{\displaystyle f^{(n)}(a)={\frac {n!}{2\pi i}}\oint _{\gamma }{\frac {f(z)}{\left(z-a\right)^{n+1}}}\,dz.}

The geometric series converges uniformly on the contour for a in each smaller closed disk centered at z_0; dominated convergence justifies the integration and differentiation.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof combines Cauchy’s holomorphic integral formula with locally uniform geometric-kernel expansion and complex differentiation. This requires a complex-analysis framework rather than only real integral estimates.
