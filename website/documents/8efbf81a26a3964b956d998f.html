Statement

Let K\subset\mathbb C be compact and have two-dimensional Lebesgue measure zero. For every continuous h:K\to\mathbb C and every \varepsilon>0, there is a rational function r with no pole on K such that \sup_{z\in K}|h(z)-r(z)|<\varepsilon.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex differentiability and rational functions are direct extensions of calculus. In the approximation statement, planar measure zero can be understood by arbitrarily small rectangle covers; compactness and uniform approximation are familiar analysis/topology concepts.

Proof 1: Smooth cutoff, Cauchy integral and rational Riemann sums — outline

By the Stone–Weierstrass theorem any complex-valued continuous function on K can be uniformly approximated by a polynomial in {\displaystyle z} and {\displaystyle {\overline {z}}}.

So it suffices to show that {\displaystyle {\overline {z}}} can be uniformly approximated by a rational function on K.

Let g(z) be a smooth function of compact support on C equal to 1 on K and set

{\displaystyle f(z)=g(z)\cdot {\overline {z}}.}

By the generalized Cauchy integral formula

{\displaystyle f(z)={\frac {1}{2\pi i}}\iint _{\mathbb C\backslash K}{\frac {\partial f}{\partial {\bar {w}}}}{\frac {dw\wedge d{\bar {w}}}{w-z}},}

since K has measure zero.

Restricting z to K and taking Riemann approximating sums for the integral on the right hand side yields the required uniform approximation of {\displaystyle {\bar {z}}} by a rational function.

The source leaves the uniform error estimate near K implicit; sample points for the rational sums are chosen outside K.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Reduce to conjugation by Stone–Weierstrass, apply the generalized Cauchy formula to a smooth cutoff and approximate its singular integral uniformly. Area measure, Cauchy kernels and uniform singular-integral estimates constitute substantial advanced analysis.
