Statement

Let a<b be real. Write H_0^1(a,b) for the real Sobolev space of square-integrable functions with square-integrable weak derivative and zero endpoint traces. If f\in C([a,b]) and u\in C^2([a,b]) satisfies -u''=f and u(a)=u(b)=0, then \int_a^b u'(x)v'(x)\,dx=\int_a^b f(x)v(x)\,dx for every v\in H_0^1(a,b).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The test functions lie in a Sobolev space and use weak derivatives and traces, not merely ordinary differentiable functions.

Proof 1: Multiply and integrate by parts

After multiplying the boundary value problem -u''=f, u(a)=u(b)=0, by v in H_0^1(a,b) and performing an integration by parts, one obtains

a(u,v)=L(v)\qquad\text{for all }v\in H_0^1(a,b),

with

a(u,v)=\int_a^b u'(x)v'(x)\,dx

and

L(v)=\int_a^b f(x)v(x)\,dx.

The boundary terms vanish because v has zero endpoint traces.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Integrate against a Sobolev test function and eliminate boundary traces. Weak derivatives, H_0^1 and trace-based integration by parts require graduate analysis despite the one-dimensional domain.
