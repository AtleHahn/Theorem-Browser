Statement

For a real series \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n and any fixed positive integer N, the series converges if and only if its tail \sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}a_n converges. Consequently, a convergence criterion for positive-term series applies after discarding a finite initial segment of an eventually positive series.

Proof 1: Separate the finite prefix

The series may be split as

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n=\sum_{n=1}^N a_n+\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}a_n.

The first expression on the right is a partial sum which will be finite, and so the convergence of the entire series will be determined by the convergence properties of the second expression on the right. For an eventually positive series, choose N beyond all nonpositive terms; the second expression may then be re-indexed to form a series of all positive terms beginning at n=1.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Partial sums of the full series differ from tail partial sums by one fixed finite constant. Their convergence is therefore equivalent, and eventual positivity permits discarding the exceptional prefix. Standard calculus of real series suffices.
