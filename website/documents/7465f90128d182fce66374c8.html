Statement

Let X have gamma density p(x)=\beta^\alpha x^{\alpha-1}e^{-\beta x}/\Gamma(\alpha) for x>0, where \alpha,\beta>0 and \Gamma(a)=\int_0^\infty t^{a-1}e^{-t}\,dt. \operatorname{Var}X=\alpha/\beta^2.

Level justification

Proof 1: Differentiate the gamma log-partition function

As an introductory example, consider the gamma distribution, whose distribution is defined by

{\displaystyle p(x)={\frac {\beta ^{\alpha }}{\Gamma (\alpha )}}x^{\alpha -1}e^{-\beta x}.}

The natural-parameter form shows that the natural parameter is given by

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\eta _{1}&=\alpha -1,\\\eta _{2}&=-\beta ,\end{aligned}}}

the reverse substitutions are

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\alpha &=\eta _{1}+1,\\\beta &=-\eta _{2},\end{aligned}}}

the sufficient statistics are (log x, x), and the log-partition function is

{\displaystyle A(\eta _{1},\eta _{2})=\log \Gamma (\eta _{1}+1)-(\eta _{1}+1)\log(-\eta _{2}).}

Using the exponential-family covariance identity \operatorname{Cov}(T_i,T_j)=\partial_i\partial_j A, we obtain the variance from the second derivative.

To compute the variance of x, we just differentiate again:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\operatorname {Var} (x)&={\frac {\partial ^{2}}{\partial \eta _{2}^{2}}}A{\left(\eta _{1},\eta _{2}\right)}={\frac {\partial }{\partial \eta _{2}}}{\frac {\eta _{1}+1}{-\eta _{2}}}\\[1ex]&={\frac {\eta _{1}+1}{\eta _{2}^{2}}}={\frac {\alpha }{\beta ^{2}}}.\end{aligned}}}
