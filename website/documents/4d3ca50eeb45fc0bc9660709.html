Statement

Let g(z)=z+a_2z^2+a_3z^3+\cdots be holomorphic and injective on |z|<1. Then |a_2|\le2.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Calculus and complex arithmetic suffice after learning that holomorphic means complex differentiable. The statements concern explicit constant or polynomial maps, or coefficients of a given convergent power series; no further complex-analytic machinery is needed to understand the claim.

Proof 1: Apply the area theorem to the odd transform — outline

First, Grönwall’s area theorem and its proof are as follows.

Suppose that

{\displaystyle F(z)=z+b_{1}z^{-1}+b_{2}z^{-2}+\cdots }

is univalent in {\displaystyle |z|>1}. Then

{\displaystyle \sum _{n=1}^{\infty }n|b_{n}|^{2}\leq 1.}

In fact, if {\displaystyle r>1}, the complement of the image of the disk {\displaystyle |z|>r} is a bounded domain {\displaystyle X(r)}. Its area is given by

{\displaystyle \int _{X(r)}dx\,dy={1 \over 2i}\int _{\partial X(r)}{\overline {z}}\,dz={1 \over 2i}\int _{|z|=r}{\overline {F}}\,dF=\pi r^{2}-\pi \sum _{n=1}^{\infty }n|b_{n}|^{2}r^{-2n}.}

Since the area is positive, the result follows by letting {\displaystyle r} decrease to {\displaystyle 1}.

The claimed second-coefficient inequality follows by applying Gronwall's area theorem to the odd univalent function

{\displaystyle g(z^{-2})^{-1/2}=z-{1 \over 2}a_{2}z^{-1}+\cdots .}

The square-root branch is fixed by g(z^{-2})^{-1/2}/z\to1 at infinity. The source asserts that this transform is univalent; its admissibility is a prerequisite here.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The supplied argument includes a proof of the area theorem using contour integration, Laurent expansions and images of exterior domains, and then uses a normalized holomorphic square-root branch whose univalence is asserted. Together these require a substantial complex-analysis background, beyond a small extension of ordinary undergraduate calculus. Applying the coefficient inequality alone would be simpler, but that is not the whole argument supplied.
