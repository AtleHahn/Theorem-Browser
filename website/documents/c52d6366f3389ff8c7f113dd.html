Statement

For each positive integer N, choose a permutation \pi_N of \{1,\ldots,N\} uniformly from its N! permutations. Let \ell(\pi_N) be the largest k for which there exist 1\le i_1<\cdots<i_k\le N with \pi_N(i_1)<\cdots<\pi_N(i_k). For every real x,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\Pr\!\left(\frac{\ell(\pi_N)-2\sqrt N}{N^{1/6}}\le x\right)=F_2(x).

Here F_2 is the Tracy–Widom cumulative distribution function for the Gaussian unitary ensemble. Its normalization can be specified as follows: let H_n be a random complex Hermitian n\times n matrix with density proportional to \exp(-\operatorname{tr}(H_n^2)/2) relative to Lebesgue measure on the real vector space of Hermitian matrices, and let \lambda_{\max}(H_n) be its largest eigenvalue. Then F_2(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Pr(n^{1/6}(\lambda_{\max}(H_n)-2\sqrt n)\le x).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The combinatorial object is elementary, but the complete target-law definition requires matrix-valued Gaussian distributions and spectral scaling limits. Their cumulative burden exceeds the conservative undergraduate allowance.
