Statement

For a pointed space Y, write \operatorname{SP}(Y) for its infinite symmetric product: finite unordered tuples of points of Y, with basepoint entries ignored, with the usual direct-limit topology of finite symmetric products. Let G be an abelian group and n\geq1 an integer. Let M(G,n) be a connected pointed CW Moore space, meaning \widetilde H_n(M(G,n);\mathbb Z)=G and all its other reduced integral homology groups vanish. Then \operatorname{SP}(M(G,n)) is an Eilenberg–MacLane space K(G,n), meaning its positive-degree homotopy groups are G in degree n and zero otherwise.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Abelian groups; CW Moore spaces; Infinite symmetric products; Eilenberg–MacLane spaces. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Apply Dold–Thom to the Moore homology groups

The Dold–Thom theorem identifies each homotopy group of \operatorname{SP}(M(G,n)) with the corresponding reduced homology group of M(G,n). Hence it is K(G,n).
