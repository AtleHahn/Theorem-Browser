Statement

Let G=(V,E) be a finite simple undirected d-regular graph with n=|V|\ge1 and adjacency matrix A (entry A_{xy}=1 when xy\in E, and 0 otherwise). Let \lambda\ge0 bound the absolute values of the eigenvalues of A on the subspace perpendicular to the all-ones vector \mathbf1. Assume d>0 and \lambda>0. A proper coloring assigns colors to vertices with different colors at adjacent vertices; let \chi(G) be the least number of colors in such a coloring. Then \chi(G)\ge d/\lambda.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Finite simple regular graphs; Real symmetric adjacency matrices; Eigenvalues and orthogonality; Proper vertex coloring. Proof 1: Count the sizes of the color classes

Each color class is an independent set, so has at most \lambda n/d vertices by the independent-set bound. At least d/\lambda such sets are therefore needed to cover all n vertices.

Level justification

Apply the supplied spectral independent-set bound to each color class and count the classes. The full bound uses eigenvalues and orthogonality of the adjacency matrix, requiring linear algebra despite the final division.
