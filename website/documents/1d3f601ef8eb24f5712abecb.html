Statement

For a homomorphism f:G\to H of abelian groups, its cokernel is the quotient H/\operatorname{im}f with the canonical quotient map.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory group theory supplies homomorphisms, kernels and quotient groups. The additional universal property asks that any compatible homomorphism factor uniquely through the displayed subgroup or quotient. This is one concrete extension of familiar maps, accessible with a short worked group example.

Proof 1: Normal closure of an abelian subgroup

In the category of groups, the cokernel of a group homomorphism f : G → H is the quotient of H by the normal closure of the image of f. In the case of abelian groups, since every subgroup is normal, the cokernel is just H modulo the image of f:

{\displaystyle \operatorname {coker} (f)=H/\operatorname {im} (f).}
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory group theory supplies images, normal subgroups, quotient groups and the fact that every subgroup of an abelian group is normal. The only added universal-property language is a cokernel: the universal homomorphism that kills the given image. The supplied group-cokernel formula specializes immediately. No general theory of abelian categories is needed for this concrete argument.

Treatment:

Cited without proof

Statement used:

For a group homomorphism f:G->H, the cokernel is the quotient map H->H/N, where N is the smallest normal subgroup of H containing im(f).

Application in proof:

Since H is abelian, im(f) is already normal and N=im(f), so the displayed quotient is the cokernel.

Application level:

Undergraduate level

Level justification:

The universal quotient killing an image is a small extension of the first isomorphism theorem and quotient groups. A categorical development is not needed.
