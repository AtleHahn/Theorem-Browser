Statement

Given a polynomial A(x)\in\mathbb Z[x], there are algorithms deciding each of the questions \exists x\in\mathbb R\; A(x)>0 and \exists x\in\mathbb R\; A(x)=0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Computably enumerable means an algorithm lists the members, while decidable means an algorithm always answers yes or no. These simple procedure notions, together with integer polynomials and increasing sequences, suffice without formal machine indexing.

Proof 1: Apply real-field decidability after removing sine — outline

The Tarski–Seidenberg decidability theorem says that the first-order theory of the ordered real field is decidable. In particular, it decides the two sentences

\exists x\in\mathbb R\;A(x)>0, \qquad \exists x\in\mathbb R\;A(x)=0

for an integer polynomial A.

In the source's comparison, removing the sine generators from the ring generated by the integers, x, \sin x^n, and \sin(x\sin x^n) for positive integers n leaves precisely \mathbb Z[x].

Level

Level justification

Apply the stated decision theorem to the two explicit existential polynomial sentences. Undergraduate polynomials and basic quantified logic suffice with decidable meaning an algorithm returning a correct yes/no answer; neither quantifier elimination nor general model theory is proved.
