Statement

Every infinite partially ordered set P contains a countably infinite antichain or a chain of cardinality |P|. A chain is a subset whose elements are pairwise comparable; an antichain has no comparable distinct elements.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Partial orders, chains, antichains and countability are undergraduate set/order concepts. A chain means pairwise comparable and an antichain pairwise incomparable; selection principles, where assumed, are explicit hypotheses. These short definitions suffice for the quantified existence or partition claim.

Proof 1: Apply the graph theorem to the comparability graph — outline

Form the comparability graph of P: distinct elements are adjacent exactly when they are comparable. Apply the Erdős–Dushnik–Miller graph theorem in the form giving a countably infinite independent set or a clique of cardinality |P|. Cliques are chains and independent sets are antichains, giving the assertion.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Translate the explicit infinite graph alternative through the comparability relation. Undergraduate orders, graphs and equal cardinality as a bijection suffice; the proof of the partition theorem is not required.
