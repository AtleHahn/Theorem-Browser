Statement

Let V be a vector space over a field. If B\subset V is linearly independent and no strictly larger subset of V is linearly independent, then B spans V and hence is a basis.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear independence, span and basis belong to undergraduate linear algebra. The assertion allows an arbitrary set of vectors but uses finite linear combinations, with no new topological or algebraic structure.

Proof 1: Use dependence of every added vector

If B is maximal linearly independent, every vector outside B is linearly dependent with the vectors of B; since B itself is independent, that vector is a finite linear combination of members of B. Thus B spans the space and is a basis.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use a finite dependence relation after adding any vector and solve for its coefficient. Undergraduate abstract linear algebra suffices, with no appeal to existence of maximal sets.
