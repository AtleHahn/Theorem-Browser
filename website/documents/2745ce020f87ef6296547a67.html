Statement

For a group G, write \operatorname{cd}_{\mathbb Z}G=\sup\{j\ge0:H^j(G,M)\ne0\text{ for some }\mathbb Z[G]\text{-module }M\}. This equals the smallest length of a projective \mathbb Z[G]-resolution of the trivial module \mathbb Z, or infinity if no finite such resolution exists.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Group cohomology; Group rings and coefficient modules; Projective resolutions. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Compare the vanishing criterion with the supremum definition

Use the following fact: \mathbb Z as a trivial \mathbb Z[G]-module has a projective resolution of length at most n if and only if H^i(G,M)=0 for all \mathbb Z[G]-modules M and for all i>n.

Therefore, we have an alternative definition of cohomological dimension as follows: the cohomological dimension of G with coefficients in \mathbb Z is the smallest n (possibly infinity) such that \mathbb Z has a projective resolution of length n as a trivial \mathbb Z[G]-module.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The supremum/minimum comparison uses a theorem about group cohomology with arbitrary coefficient modules and projective resolutions over group rings. Those homological-algebra objects exceed the baseline by much more than a short extension.
