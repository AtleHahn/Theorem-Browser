A smooth function unequal to its Taylor series

A smooth function can have every derivative zero at a point and still be nonzero arbitrarily nearby. Thus even complete derivative data at one point need not determine local behavior without stronger assumptions such as analyticity, clarifying limits on reconstructing a continuum from local information.

Statement

Define f(x)=e^{-1/x^2} for x>0 and f(x)=0 for x\le0. Then f is smooth on \mathbb R and f^{(j)}(0)=0 for all j\ge0. Its Taylor series at zero is identically zero and does not equal f on any neighborhood of zero.

Proof 1: Differentiate the flat exponential and use rapid decay — outline

Using the chain rule repeatedly by mathematical induction, one shows that for any positive order k,

f^{(k)}(x)= \begin{cases} \dfrac{p_k(x)}{x^{3k}}\cdot e^{-1/x^2}&x>0,\\ 0&x\leq0, \end{cases}

for some polynomial p_k of degree 2(k-1). The function e^{-1/x^2} tends to zero faster than any positive power of x as x\to0, so f is infinitely many times differentiable and f^{(k)}(0)=0 for every positive integer k.

  • The Taylor series of f converges uniformly to the zero function T_f(x)=0, which is analytic with all coefficients equal to zero.
  • The function f is unequal to this Taylor series on every neighborhood of zero, and hence non-analytic.
