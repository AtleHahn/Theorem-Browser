Statement

The region \{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb R^3:x^2+y^2<1,\ y^2+z^2<1\} has volume \int_{-1}^1 4(1-y^2)\,dy.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves intersecting cylinders; coordinate inequalities; solid volume; definite integrals. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Integrate square cross-sections

For the intersection of two cylinders, if the x-z plane is the slice direction, the equations for the cylinder give that {\displaystyle x^{2}<1-y^{2}} while {\displaystyle z^{2}<1-y^{2}}, which defines a region which is a square in the x-z plane of side length {\displaystyle 2{\sqrt {1-y^{2}}}}, so that the total volume is:

{\displaystyle \displaystyle \int _{-1}^{1}4(1-y^{2})\,dy.}
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

At each fixed y in (-1,1), the two inequalities independently bound x and z by sqrt(1-y^2), producing a square of area 4(1-y^2). Integrating this area is standard single-variable volume calculus. No measure-theoretic integration or geometry beyond coordinates is needed.
