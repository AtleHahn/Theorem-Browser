Statement

Let F be a finite field of q elements, n\ge1 an integer, and P\in F[X_1,\ldots,X_n] have total degree less than n(q-1). Then \sum_{x\in F^n}P(x)=0 in F.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory algebra supplies finite fields, polynomial evaluation, irreducibility and vector subspaces. The Mobius function, asymptotic notation or uniform finite counting probability is a limited numerical definition where used. These statements require no field-extension norm or Galois theory.

Proof 1: Factor the monomial sums — outline

If 0\leq i<q-1, then

\sum_{x\in F}x^i=0

(with the polynomial convention x^0=1), so the sum over F^n of any polynomial in X_1,\ldots,X_n of degree less than n(q-1) also vanishes.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Expand into monomials, observe one exponent must be below q-1 and factor the finite grid sum into one-variable power sums. Finite fields and multivariate polynomial algebra are ordinary undergraduate abstract algebra.
