Statement

Let p be prime, f\in\mathbb Z[X], k,m positive integers with m\le k, and r\in\mathbb Z satisfy p^k\mid f(r) and p\nmid f^\prime(r), where the prime denotes the formal polynomial derivative. There is exactly one residue s\bmod p^{k+m} with s\equiv r\pmod {p^k} and f(s)\equiv0\pmod {p^{k+m}}. It is s\equiv r-f(r)a\pmod {p^{k+m}},\qquad a f^\prime(r)\equiv1\pmod {p^m}. Here the formal derivative is (\sum_j c_jX^j)^\prime=\sum_{j\ge1}jc_jX^{j-1}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The hypotheses are integer divisibility and the formal derivative rule is given. The conclusion describes one residue and its formula using a modular inverse; these are elementary polynomial and remainder calculations, not infinite p-adic analysis.

Proof 1: Hensel lifting of a simple modular root — proof

Put N=\deg f. We use the Taylor expansion of f around r to write:

{\displaystyle f(s)=\sum _{n=0}^{N}c_{n}(s-r)^{n},\qquad c_{n}=f^{(n)}(r)/n!.}

From {\displaystyle r\equiv s{\bmod {p}}^{k},} we see that s − r = tpk for some integer t. Let

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}f(s)&=\sum _{n=0}^{N}c_{n}\left(tp^{k}\right)^{n}\\&=f(r)+tp^{k}f'(r)+\sum _{n=2}^{N}c_{n}t^{n}p^{kn}\\&=f(r)+tp^{k}f'(r)+p^{2k}t^{2}g(t)&&g(t)\in \mathbb {Z} [t]\\&=zp^{k}+tp^{k}f'(r)+p^{2k}t^{2}g(t)&&f(r)\equiv 0{\bmod {p}}^{k}\\&=(z+tf'(r))p^{k}+p^{2k}t^{2}g(t)\end{aligned}}}

For {\displaystyle m\leqslant k,} we have:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}f(s)\equiv 0{\bmod {p}}^{k+m}&\Longleftrightarrow (z+tf'(r))p^{k}\equiv 0{\bmod {p}}^{k+m}\\&\Longleftrightarrow z+tf'(r)\equiv 0{\bmod {p}}^{m}\\&\Longleftrightarrow tf'(r)\equiv -z{\bmod {p}}^{m}\\&\Longleftrightarrow t\equiv -z[f'(r)]^{-1}{\bmod {p}}^{m}&&p\nmid f'(r)\end{aligned}}}

The assumption that {\displaystyle f'(r)} is not divisible by p ensures that {\displaystyle f'(r)} has an inverse mod {\displaystyle p^{m}} which is necessarily unique. Hence a solution for t exists uniquely modulo {\displaystyle p^{m},} and s exists uniquely modulo {\displaystyle p^{k+m}.}

Substitution in s=r+tp^k gives s\equiv r-f(r)a\pmod{p^{k+m}}.

Level justification

Expand the finite polynomial after substituting r+t p^k, discard divisible higher terms and solve one invertible linear congruence. The formal derivatives encode finite polynomial coefficients; calculus and completions are unnecessary.
