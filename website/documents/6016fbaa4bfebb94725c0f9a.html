Statement

Assume the axiom of choice. For the action of a group H on a set X, there is a subset M\subseteq X meeting each orbit in exactly one point, and HM=\{hm:h\in H,\ m\in M\}=X.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory group theory supplies subgroups, quotients, actions, products and homomorphisms. Perfect means generated by commutators, torsion means finite element orders, and the chain conditions are explicit sequences of subgroups. The relevant one of these closely related definitions is a small extension, without representation theory.

Proof 1: Choose one point from every orbit

Partition the acted-on set into orbits. The axiom of choice selects one point from each orbit; call the set of selected points M. Acting on these representatives covers every orbit, hence covers X.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the orbit partition and apply the explicitly assumed choice principle to select one point per orbit, then recover X by the action. Undergraduate group actions and elementary set theory suffice; no proof of choice is required.
