Statement

For A\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}, \det(AA^T)=\sum_{S\subseteq[n],\,|S|=m}\det(A_{[m],S})^2, with increasing selected columns and the usual empty conventions.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Real matrices; Transpose; Gram determinants; Minors. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Pair each minor with its transpose

For real matrices A\in\mathbb R^{m\times n} and B\in\mathbb R^{n\times m}, use Cauchy–Binet: \det(AB)=\sum_{|S|=m}\det(A_{[m],S})\det(B_{S,[m]}), with indices within each selected subset increasing. Substitute B=A^T. For each subset S, B_{S,[m]}=(A_{[m],S})^T, whose determinant equals that of A_{[m],S}. Every term in Cauchy–Binet is therefore the corresponding square.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Substitute the transpose into Cauchy–Binet and use invariance of determinant under transpose. Undergraduate linear algebra suffices.
