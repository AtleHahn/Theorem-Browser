Statement

Let X be a complex Banach space and x^{**}\in X^{**} satisfy \|x^{**}\|\le1. Let \varphi_1,\ldots,\varphi_n\in X^*, n\ge1, and suppose x\in X is given with \varphi_i(x)=x^{**}(\varphi_i) for every i. Put Y=\bigcap_{i=1}^n\ker\varphi_i. For every \delta>0, the coset x+Y contains an element of norm at most 1+\delta.

Proof 1: A given finite bidual interpolant has near-unit representatives — proof

Let B_X be the closed unit ball of X. Every element z\in(x+Y)\cap(1+\delta)B_X satisfies z\in(1+\delta)B_X and \varphi_i(z)=\varphi_i(x)=x^{**}(\varphi_i), so it suffices to show that the intersection is nonempty.

Assume for contradiction that it is empty. Then \operatorname{dist}(x,Y)\ge1+\delta and by the Hahn–Banach theorem there exists a linear form \varphi\in X^* such that

\varphi|_Y=0,\qquad \varphi(x)\ge1+\delta,\qquad \|\varphi\|_{X^*}=1,

where \varphi(x) is real. The annihilator of the common kernel Y is \operatorname{span}\{\varphi_1,\ldots,\varphi_n\}. Thus \varphi belongs to this span, and the given interpolation equalities imply

1+\delta\le\varphi(x)=x^{**}(\varphi) =|x^{**}(\varphi)| \le\|\varphi\|_{X^*}\|x^{**}\|_{X^{**}}\le1,

which is a contradiction.
