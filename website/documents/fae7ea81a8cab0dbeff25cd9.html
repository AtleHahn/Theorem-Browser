Statement

Let K,L be finite geometric simplicial complexes and f:|K|\to|L| continuous. For all sufficiently large n, there is a simplicial map s:\operatorname{Bd}^nK\to L whose realization g is a simplicial approximation: g(x) lies in the smallest closed simplex containing f(x). In particular f and g are homotopic. Here \operatorname{Bd} is barycentric subdivision.

Beyond undergraduate level

Proof 1: Small stars and vertex selection

Precisely, let {\displaystyle \operatorname {St} ^{0}(w)} denote the open star of {\displaystyle w}; i.e., the union of all relatively-open simplexes containing {\displaystyle w} in the closure. Note {\displaystyle \operatorname {St} ^{0}(w)} is the complement of the union of all simplexes disjoint from {\displaystyle \operatorname {St} ^{0}(w)}; in particular, is an open subset and thus {\displaystyle U_{w}:=f^{-1}(\operatorname {St} ^{0}(w))}, {\displaystyle w} vertices, form an open cover of {\displaystyle |K|}. Let {\displaystyle \delta } be a Lebesgue number of this open cover (which exists by compactness of |K|); i.e., a positive real number such that if {\displaystyle A\subset |K|} is a subset of diameter {\displaystyle <\delta }, then {\displaystyle A} is contained in some open set in the cover.

Now, let {\displaystyle K'} be a sufficiently iterated barycentric subdivision of {\displaystyle K} with the property that the diameter of each simplex in {\displaystyle K'} is less than {\displaystyle \delta /2} (see below for how to find such a refinement). Then the diameter of {\displaystyle \operatorname {St} ^{0}(v)} is less than {\displaystyle \delta }, since {\displaystyle |x-y|\leq |x-v|+|v-y|<\delta } for each {\displaystyle x,y} in {\displaystyle \operatorname {St} ^{0}(v)}. Thus, for each vertex {\displaystyle v}, we have {\displaystyle \operatorname {St} ^{0}(v)\subset f^{-1}(\operatorname {St} ^{0}(w))} or

{\displaystyle f(\operatorname {St} ^{0}(v))\subset \operatorname {St} ^{0}(w)}

for some vertex {\displaystyle w}. Let {\displaystyle s(v)} denote some such {\displaystyle w}. Then {\displaystyle s} is a map between the sets of the vertices. We note that {\displaystyle s} extends by linearity to {\displaystyle s:K'\to L}; i.e., it maps simplexes to simplexes {\displaystyle \Leftrightarrow } for each set of the vertices {\displaystyle v_{i}} of a simplex in {\displaystyle K'}, the convex hull {\displaystyle \langle s(v_{1}),\cdots ,s(v_{r})\rangle } is a (closed) simplex in {\displaystyle L} {\displaystyle \Leftrightarrow } for each set of the vertices {\displaystyle v_{i}} of a simplex in {\displaystyle K'}, there is a relatively-open simplex in {\displaystyle L} whose closure contains all {\displaystyle s(v_{i})}’s as vertices, possibly with repetition.

Now, the last condition in the above holds since given a set of the vertices {\displaystyle v_{i}} of a simplex in {\displaystyle K'}, we have:

{\displaystyle \emptyset \neq f(\cap _{i}\operatorname {St} ^{0}(v_{i}))\subset \cap _{i}f(\operatorname {St} ^{0}(v_{i}))\subset \cap _{i}\operatorname {St} ^{0}(s(v_{i})).}

And then we get the (necessary continuous) map {\displaystyle g=|s|:|K|\to |L|}. Next, for each {\displaystyle x} in {\displaystyle |K|}, {\displaystyle f(x)} belongs to a unique relatively-open simplex {\displaystyle \tau } in {\displaystyle L}. Let {\displaystyle x=\sum \lambda _{i}v_{i}} be a convex combination with nonzero coefficients for some vertices v_i of the smallest simplex of K' containing x. Then {\displaystyle g(x)=\sum \lambda _{i}g(v_{i})}. Let {\displaystyle w_{i}=g(v_{i})}. For each {\displaystyle i}, we have {\displaystyle x\in \operatorname {St} ^{0}(v_{i})} and so

{\displaystyle f(x)\in f(\operatorname {St} ^{0}(v_{i}))\subset \operatorname {St} ^{0}(w_{i}).}

Thus, {\displaystyle f(x)} belongs to some {\displaystyle \tau '} whose closure contains {\displaystyle w_{i}} and by uniqueness, {\displaystyle \tau '=\tau }. Then we have

{\displaystyle g(x)\in \sum \lambda _{i}{\overline {\tau }}\subset {\overline {\tau }}.}

Hence, {\displaystyle f(x),g(x)} belongs to the same simplex {\displaystyle {\overline {\tau }}}. So, if we let {\displaystyle h_{t}=(1-t)f+tg}, then {\displaystyle h_{t}} is a homotopy {\displaystyle f\sim g}.

Here, we shall give some rigorous argument on how to find a refinement in which the diameter of each simplex is arbitrarily small (of course, this is intuitively entirely obvious.) The argument is by estimating the diameter of a simplex in a barycentric subdivision as follows.

Lemma—Let {\displaystyle \sigma } be a {\displaystyle p}-simplex. Then each simplex in the barycentric subdivision of {\displaystyle \sigma } has diameter {\displaystyle \leq {\frac {p}{p+1}}\operatorname {diam} (\sigma )}.

Since {\displaystyle p/(p+1)} is less than {\displaystyle 1}, we can make the maximal diameter of a simplex arbitrary small by iterating barycentric subdivisions.

Proof: By construction, each simplex in the barycentric subdivision has the vertices of the form {\displaystyle b(F_{0}),\cdots } for a chain of faces

{\displaystyle \sigma =F_{p}\supset \cdots \supset F_{0}}

where {\displaystyle F<G} means {\displaystyle F} is a proper face of {\displaystyle G}, and, thinking {\displaystyle F} consisting of vertices, the barycenter {\displaystyle b(F)} of {\displaystyle F} is

{\displaystyle b(F)={\frac {1}{\#F}}\sum _{v\in F}v.}

Now, given faces {\displaystyle F<G}, with {\displaystyle g:=\#G,f:=\#F}, we have:

{\displaystyle b(F)-b(G)={\frac {g-f}{g}}\left({\frac {1}{f}}\sum _{v\in F}v-{\frac {1}{g-f}}\sum _{v\in G-F}v\right).}

Then the expression after {\displaystyle {\frac {g-f}{g}}} is in

{\displaystyle \langle v\mid v\in G\rangle +\langle -v\mid v\in G\rangle \subset \langle v-w\mid v,w\in G\rangle }

where the bracket means convex hull. Thus, since {\displaystyle (g-f)/g\leq (g-1)/g\leq p/(p+1)},

{\displaystyle |b(F)-b(G)|\leq {\frac {p}{p+1}}\max _{v,w\in G}|v-w|.}

This implies the diameter of {\displaystyle \langle b(F_{i})\mid i\rangle } is bounded by the claimed bound.
