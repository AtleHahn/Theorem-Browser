Statement

Let F\in L^2(0,\infty), extended by zero to the negative half-line. For \eta>0, set f(\xi+i\eta)=\int_0^\infty F(x)e^{ix(\xi+i\eta)}\,dx. Let f(\xi) denote the L2 Fourier transform of F in this positive-exponent, unnormalized convention. Then \int_\mathbb R|f(\xi+i\eta)-f(\xi)|^2\,d\xi\to0 as \eta\downarrow0.

Proof 1: Plancherel and dominated convergence — outline

Apply Plancherel’s theorem to the Fourier transforms of F(x)e^{-\eta x} and F(x), and use dominated convergence as \eta\downarrow0. This gives the stated L2 limit.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Use Plancherel to transfer the L2 boundary error to the exponentially damped function and dominated convergence to remove damping. General L2 Fourier extensions and measure-theoretic convergence require graduate analysis.
