Statement

Let \lambda_1,\lambda_2\in\mathbb C satisfy e^{\lambda_1},e^{\lambda_2}\in\overline{\mathbb Q} and \lambda_2\ne0. Then \lambda_1/\lambda_2 is rational or transcendental. Here \overline{\mathbb Q} denotes the algebraic numbers.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex exponentials and polynomial roots are undergraduate concepts. Algebraic means a root of a nonzero rational polynomial, and transcendental means not algebraic. These definitions suffice for the rational-or-transcendental alternative.

Proof 1: Two-logarithm independence

Write \mathbb L=\{\lambda\in\mathbb C:e^\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb Q}\}.

The Gelfond–Schneider theorem, in its logarithmic form, says that if {\displaystyle \lambda _{1},\lambda _{2}\in \mathbb {L} } are linearly independent over the rational numbers, then they are linearly independent over the algebraic numbers. So if {\displaystyle \lambda _{1},\lambda _{2}\in \mathbb {L} } and {\displaystyle \lambda _{2}} is not zero, then the quotient {\displaystyle \lambda _{1}/\lambda _{2}} is either a rational number or transcendental. It cannot be an algebraic irrational number like {\displaystyle {\sqrt {2}}}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the explicit two-logarithm independence theorem: an algebraic ratio gives an algebraic linear relation, hence rational dependence and a rational ratio. Undergraduate linear algebra and complex exponentials suffice; algebraic coefficients mean numbers satisfying rational polynomials, without field-extension machinery.
