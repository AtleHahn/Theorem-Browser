Statement

Let N,a,b,m be integers with \gcd(a,b)=1 and k=a^2-Nb^2\ne0. If k\mid a+bm, put A=(am+Nb)/k, B=(a+bm)/k, K=(m^2-N)/k. Then A,B,K are integers and A^2-NB^2=K.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The integer constructions use finite algebra and explicitly stated recurrences.

Proof 1: Scaled composition and divisibility outline

Use Brahmagupta's identity:

{\displaystyle (x_{1}^{2}-Ny_{1}^{2})(x_{2}^{2}-Ny_{2}^{2})=(x_{1}x_{2}+Ny_{1}y_{2})^{2}-N(x_{1}y_{2}+x_{2}y_{1})^{2},}

The chakravala (cyclic) method starts by choosing two relatively prime integers {\displaystyle a} and {\displaystyle b}, then composing the triple {\displaystyle (a,b,k)} (that is, one which satisfies {\displaystyle a^{2}-Nb^{2}=k}) with the trivial triple {\displaystyle (m,1,m^{2}-N)} to get the triple {\displaystyle {\big (}am+Nb,a+bm,k(m^{2}-N){\big )}}, which can be scaled down to

{\displaystyle \left({\frac {am+Nb}{k}},{\frac {a+bm}{k}},{\frac {m^{2}-N}{k}}\right).}

When {\displaystyle m} is chosen so that {\displaystyle {\frac {a+bm}{k}}} is an integer, so are the other two numbers in the triple.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The displayed composition identity is a polynomial expansion. For integrality, gcd(a,b)=1 implies gcd(b,k)=1; a=-bm modulo k gives b²(m²-N)=0 modulo k and hence k divides m²-N. Also b(am+Nb)=a(a+bm)-k, so k divides am+Nb. These are elementary congruences and coprimality cancellation.
