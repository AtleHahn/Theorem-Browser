Statement

Let a convex three-dimensional polyhedron mean a bounded convex solid with finitely many polygonal faces. For every such polyhedron, its graph of vertices and edges has an edge uv such that

\deg(u)+\deg(v)\leq13,

where \deg(v) is the number of edges incident with the vertex v. The bound is sharp: there exists a convex polyhedron for which every edge has endpoint degree sum at least 13.

In the dual formulation, every convex three-dimensional polyhedron has two faces sharing an edge such that the sum of their numbers of sides is at most 13.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Counting incident edges and face sides of convex solids uses elementary spatial geometry; degree is locally defined and no graph-theory background is necessary.
