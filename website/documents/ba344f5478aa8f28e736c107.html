Statement

For coprime integers a>b>0, a primitive prime divisor of a^n-b^n means a prime dividing a^n-b^n that divides none of a^k-b^k for 1\leq k<n. If a+b is a power of two, then a^2-b^2 has no primitive prime divisor.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Only finite integer arithmetic, factorization and divisibility are needed.

Proof 1: Exceptional-case factorization

Since a+b is a power of two, any odd prime factors of {\displaystyle a^{2}-b^{2}=(a+b)(a^{1}-b^{1})} must be contained in {\displaystyle a^{1}-b^{1}}, which is also even.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Factor the difference of squares; all odd divisors come from a-b, and parity makes two divide a-b as well. Elementary divisibility suffices.
