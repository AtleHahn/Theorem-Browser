Statement

For r\ge 1, let W_r be the connected sum of r copies of \mathbf{RP}^2. A smooth real cubic surface X is rational over \mathbb R precisely in the four cases X(\mathbb R)\cong W_7,W_5,W_3,W_1; it is not rational in the case X(\mathbb R)\cong W_1\sqcup S^2. The symbols here denote diffeomorphism types.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Rationality of real algebraic surfaces together with the classification of their real-locus smooth types requires substantial algebraic geometry. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Apply the connectedness criterion to the five types

The real classification gives exactly these five types. The first four are connected and the fifth is disconnected. The stated rationality criterion says that a smooth real cubic surface is rational exactly when its real-point space is connected, so precisely the first four cases are rational.

Level justification

Combine the classification of real cubic surfaces with their birational rationality criterion. Smooth real algebraic surfaces, real loci, connected sums and diffeomorphism types require substantial algebraic and differential topology beyond basic connectedness.
