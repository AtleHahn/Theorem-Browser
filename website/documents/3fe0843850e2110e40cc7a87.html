Statement

Let ABCD be a rectangle with vertices in consecutive order, and let P be any point in its plane. Writing UV for the Euclidean distance between points U,V, one has

AP^2+CP^2=BP^2+DP^2.
Level
High school only

The statement involves a plane rectangle and real squared distances.

Proof 1: Four Pythagorean distance identities

Drop perpendicular lines from the point P to the supporting lines of the sides of the rectangle, meeting the lines AB, BC, CD, and AD at points W, X, Y and Z respectively. By applying the Pythagorean theorem to the right triangle AWP, and observing that WP = AZ, it follows that

{\displaystyle AP^{2}=AW^{2}+WP^{2}=AW^{2}+AZ^{2}}

and by a similar argument the squares of the lengths of the distances from P to the other three corners can be calculated as

{\displaystyle PC^{2}=WB^{2}+ZD^{2},}

{\displaystyle BP^{2}=WB^{2}+AZ^{2},} and

{\displaystyle PD^{2}=ZD^{2}+AW^{2}.}

These distance identities also hold when a foot lies on an extended side or a leg has length zero. Therefore:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}AP^{2}+PC^{2}&=\left(AW^{2}+AZ^{2}\right)+\left(WB^{2}+ZD^{2}\right)\\[4pt]&=\left(WB^{2}+AZ^{2}\right)+\left(ZD^{2}+AW^{2}\right)\\[4pt]&=BP^{2}+PD^{2}\end{aligned}}}
Level
High school only
Level justification

Apply Pythagoras to the four perpendicular coordinate distances and regroup the four squared legs. The identities remain valid for external or zero-length projections. This is elementary Euclidean geometry.
