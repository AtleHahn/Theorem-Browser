Statement

Let n\ge3 be an integer. Suppose that a group G satisfies either of the following hypotheses:

  • G is finitely presented and \operatorname{cd}_{\mathbb Z}(G)\le n;
  • G is finitely generated and 3\le\operatorname{cd}_{\mathbb Z}(G)\le n.

Then there exists a connected aspherical CW complex X of dimension n with fundamental group \pi_1(X)\cong G.

Finitely generated means generated by a finite set; finitely presented means admitting finitely many generators and defining relations. A CW complex is a space built by attaching cells (open balls) along their boundaries, with the closure-finite weak topology; its dimension is the supremum of its cell dimensions. Aspherical means all homotopy groups \pi_j(X) for j\ge2 vanish. The fundamental group \pi_1(X) consists of based loops modulo based homotopy.

Here \mathbb Z[G] is the integral group ring and

\operatorname{cd}_{\mathbb Z}(G)=\sup\{j\ge0:H^j(G,M)\ne0\text{ for some }\mathbb Z[G]\text{-module }M\}.

Group cohomology H^j(G,M) is computed by applying \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Z[G]}(-,M) to a projective resolution of the trivial module \mathbb Z and taking degree-j cohomology.
