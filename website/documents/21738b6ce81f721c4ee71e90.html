Statement

Let \Delta^{d+1} be a simplex, the convex hull of d+2 affinely independent vertices. Every affine map f:\Delta^{d+1}\to\mathbb R^d maps two disjoint nonempty faces to sets with nonempty intersection. A face is the convex hull of a subset of the vertices.

Proof 1: Apply Radon to the vertex images

Formally, let ƒ be an affine function from Δd+1 to Rd. Let {\displaystyle v_{1},v_{2},\dots ,v_{d+2}} be the vertices of Δd+1, and let {\displaystyle x_{1},x_{2},\dots ,x_{d+2}} be their images under ƒ. By Radon’s theorem in its indexed-point formulation, the {\displaystyle x_{1},x_{2},\dots ,x_{d+2}} can be partitioned, by their indices, into two disjoint subsets, e.g. (xi)i in I and (xj)j in J, with overlapping convex hull. Because f is affine, the convex hull of (xi)i in I is the image of the face spanned by the vertices (vi)i in I, and similarly the convex hull of (x_j)_{j\in J} is the image of the face spanned by the vertices (v_j)_{j\in J}. These two faces are disjoint, and their images under f intersect — as claimed.

Repeated vertex images cause no extra burden: equal images can be placed in opposite parts.
