Simpler proof assessment

Likely alternative prerequisite level: High school only; lowest recorded proof level: Undergraduate level. Nonzero square residues form a multiplicatively closed set Q of size (p-1)/2 by pairing opposite square roots modulo p. For a nonsquare a, aQ is disjoint from Q by cancellation and has the same size, so it is the complement. Multiplication by a square preserves each set, and the product of two nonsquares lies in Q by the same coset counting. Euclid's lemma and finite modular arithmetic give all three rules without a cyclic-group generator.

Statement

For an odd prime p, write (a/p)=0 if p\mid a, 1 if a is a nonzero square modulo p, and -1 otherwise. For integers a,b not divisible by p, (ab/p)=(a/p)(b/p). Equivalently, two residues or two nonresidues have residue product, and a residue times a nonresidue has nonresidue product.

Level
High school only

Proof 1: Use the cyclic multiplicative group — outline

The multiplicative group of nonzero residues modulo p is cyclic of order p-1. The stated residue/nonresidue product rules follow from this cyclic structure.

Level justification
