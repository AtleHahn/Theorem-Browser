Statement

Let \pi(x) count primes below x, and let \pi_{10,a}(x) count those congruent to a modulo 10. For each a\in\{1,3,7,9\}, \pi_{10,a}(x)/\pi(x)\to1/4 as x\to\infty.

Proof 1: Arithmetic-progression prime theorem specialization (outline)

Use the arithmetic-progression prime theorem \pi_{d,a}(x)\sim\operatorname{Li}(x)/\varphi(d) for coprime a,d, where \varphi is Euler’s totient and \operatorname{Li}(x)=\int_2^xdt/\log t. Since \varphi(10)=4, each of the four counts is asymptotic to \operatorname{Li}(x)/4. Apart from 2 and 5, every prime ends in one of these four digits. Thus \pi(x)=\sum_{a\in\{1,3,7,9\}}\pi_{10,a}(x)+2 for x>5; dividing gives the stated limit.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the stated progression asymptotic to four residue classes, sum their counts and discard the fixed contribution of two primes in the limit. Undergraduate asymptotic calculus suffices; no proof of the prime theorem is used.
