Statement

Let a finite group G act on a finite set X. Put X^g=\{x\in X:g\cdot x=x\} and G_x=\{g\in G:g\cdot x=x\}. The orbit of x is G\cdot x=\{g\cdot x:g\in G\}, and X/G is the set of these orbits. Then \sum_{g\in G}|X^g|=\sum_{x\in X}|G_x|.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory group theory supplies subgroups, quotients, actions, products and homomorphisms. Perfect means generated by commutators, torsion means finite element orders, and the chain conditions are explicit sequences of subgroups. The relevant one of these closely related definitions is a small extension, without representation theory.

Proof 1: Count fixed pairs by each coordinate

Count the set of fixed pairs in two ways:

\sum_{g\in G}|X^g|=\#\{(g,x)\in G\times X:g\cdot x=x\}=\sum_{x\in X}|G_x|.
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Count the same finite fixed-pair set by either coordinate. Undergraduate groups and actions supply the objects; the counting itself is elementary.
