Statement

Every real nonnegative square matrix A has a nonzero vector v\geq0 with Av=\rho(A)v, where \rho(A)\geq0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Proof 1: Source spectral argument outline

For a positive matrix, the Perron theorem gives a positive eigenvector whose eigenvalue is its spectral radius. Since any nonnegative matrix can be obtained as a limit of positive matrices, one obtains the existence of an eigenvector with nonnegative components; the corresponding eigenvalue will be nonnegative and greater than or equal, in absolute value, to all other eigenvalues.

The source indicates this limiting argument without giving its details.

Level justification

Approximate by positive matrices, normalize their Perron eigenvectors and pass to a convergent subsequence. Undergraduate linear algebra and finite-dimensional compactness suffice; spectral-radius continuity is the specific additional supplied fact.
