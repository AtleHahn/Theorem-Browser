Statement

Let n\ge1 and let A,B\subseteq\mathbb R^n be disjoint nonempty convex sets. There are a unit vector v and c\in\mathbb R such that \langle v,a\rangle\le c\le\langle v,b\rangle for every a\in A,b\in B.

Proof 1: Compact exhaustion and a limiting unit normal — outline

Use the relative-interior facts: a nonempty finite-dimensional convex set has nonempty relative interior (interior within its affine span), is contained in the closure of its relative interior, and its relative interior admits an increasing exhaustion by nonempty compact convex sets.

Approach both A,B from the inside by A_1\subseteq A_2\subseteq\cdots\subseteq A and B_1\subseteq B_2\subseteq\cdots\subseteq B, such that each A_k,B_k is nonempty, compact and convex, and the unions are the relative interiors \operatorname{relint}(A),\operatorname{relint}(B).

By positive-gap separation of disjoint compact convex sets, for each pair A_k,B_k there exists some unit vector v_k and real number c_k, such that

\langle v_k,a\rangle<c_k<\langle v_k,b\rangle\qquad(a\in A_k,\ b\in B_k).

Since the unit sphere is compact, we can take a convergent subsequence, so that v_k\to v. Let

c_A:=\sup_{a\in A}\langle v,a\rangle,\qquad c_B:=\inf_{b\in B}\langle v,b\rangle.

We claim that c_A\leq c_B, thus separating A,B.

Assume not, then there exist some a\in A,b\in B such that \langle v,a\rangle>\langle v,b\rangle. By relative-interior density and continuity, we may choose such a,b in the respective relative interiors. They belong to A_k,B_k for all sufficiently large k. Since v_k\to v, for large enough k we have \langle v_k,a\rangle>\langle v_k,b\rangle, a contradiction.

The limits satisfy \|v\|=1. Nonemptiness of both sets and c_A\le c_B ensure that both bounds are finite. Choose c\in[c_A,c_B].
