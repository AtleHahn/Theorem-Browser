Statement

If a first-order theory T has an infinite model, its models are not all isomorphic. Consequently, a satisfiable first-order theory with exactly one model up to isomorphism has a finite model.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement concerns general infinite structures in first-order model theory.

Proof 1: Larger models from upward Löwenheim–Skolem

Let M be an infinite model of T. By the upward Löwenheim–Skolem theorem there is a model of T of cardinality larger than |M| (choose the cardinal also at least the size of the language). Models of distinct cardinalities cannot be isomorphic. Thus T cannot have a unique model up to isomorphism.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply upward Lowenheim–Skolem and distinguish models by cardinality. Understanding arbitrary first-order models, language size and the extension theorem is a substantial model-theory prerequisite; retain beyond undergraduate in doubt.
