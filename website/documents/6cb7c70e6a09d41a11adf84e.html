Statement

Let \Sigma be a finite alphabet and L\subseteq\Sigma^* a language, where \Sigma^* is the set of finite words including the empty word. Then L is context-free if and only if there are a finite set T of opening bracket types, a disjoint matching set \overline T of closing brackets, a regular language R\subseteq(T\cup\overline T)^* and a homomorphism h:(T\cup\overline T)^*\to\Sigma^* with L=h(D_T\cap R).

Here D_T consists of all correctly nested, type-matched bracket words, including the empty word. A homomorphism replaces each bracket symbol by a fixed word, possibly empty, and concatenates the replacements. A regular language is one accepted by a finite-state automaton, a finite directed graph with symbol-labeled transitions, a starting state and accepting states. Context-free means generated by a finite grammar whose productions replace a single nonterminal by a word of terminals and nonterminals, starting from a designated nonterminal and ending with only terminals.
