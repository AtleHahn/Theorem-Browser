Statement

Let G be a group with identity e acting on a set X from the left, so ex=x and g(hx)=(gh)x. Write Gx=\{gx:g\in G\} and G_x=\{g\in G:gx=x\}. If G is finite, then |Gx|=|G|/|G_x| for every x, so each orbit size divides |G|.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory group and ring theory supplies subgroups, quotients, commutators and homomorphisms. Any added terms such as perfect, nilpotent or virtually free describe explicit subgroup conditions; they are small algebraic extensions without representation or geometric-group theory.

Proof 1: Finite orbit–stabilizer formula

If G is finite then the orbit–stabilizer theorem, which identifies G/G_x with Gx by gG_x\mapsto gx, together with Lagrange's theorem, gives

|Gx|=[G:G_x]=|G|/|G_x|.

In other words, the length of the orbit of x times the order of its stabilizer is the order of the group. In particular that implies that the orbit length is a divisor of the group order.
