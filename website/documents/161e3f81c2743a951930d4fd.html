Simpler proof assessment

Likely alternative prerequisite level: Undergraduate level; lowest recorded proof level: Beyond undergraduate level. Let E be the space of finitely supported sequences and let W be the linear forms in E vanishing on the closed subspace C. Closedness and a finite-coordinate cylinder separating x from C show C=W-perp: separate the finite-dimensional projected subspace by a linear form. Choose an algebraic projection Q:E->W by extending a basis. Its transpose acts continuously on R^N because each output coordinate uses only finitely many input coordinates. P=I-Q-transpose has range W-perp=C and P^2=P. This uses algebraic bases and product topology, not classification of topological vector spaces.

Statement

Every closed vector subspace of \mathbb R^{\mathbb N}, with its product topology, is the range of a continuous linear projection.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Basic topology supplies product spaces, compactness, connected components, metrics and continuity; linear algebra supplies projections where present. The evaluation, finite-consistency or covering conditions are explicit uses of those concepts. No weak dual topology, manifold structure or further specialist theory is needed for comprehension.

Proof 1: Closed subspaces of the real sequence product are complemented

Use the following theorem: a vector subspace of a Hausdorff locally convex space that is topologically linearly isomorphic to a product \mathbb R^I is closed and complemented. Here complemented means it is the range of a continuous linear projection.

The product-of-fields subspace theorem may be used to show that every closed vector subspace of {\displaystyle \mathbb {R} ^{\mathbb {N} }} is complemented because any such space is either finite dimensional or else TVS–isomorphic to {\displaystyle \mathbb {R} ^{\mathbb {N} }.}

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

This applies classification of closed subspaces of a sequence product and a complemented-subspace theorem in Hausdorff locally convex spaces. The cumulative topological-vector-space and projection framework exceeds a small undergraduate extension.
