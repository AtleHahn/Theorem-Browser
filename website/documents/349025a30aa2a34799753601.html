Statement

Consider formulas built with \neg and \to, with uniform substitution and modus ponens (from A and A\to B infer B), and axiom schemes A1:\ \phi\to(\psi\to\phi),\qquad A2:\ (\phi\to(\psi\to\xi))\to((\phi\to\psi)\to(\phi\to\xi)),\qquad A3:\ (\neg\phi\to\neg\psi)\to(\psi\to\phi). A formula is derivable if it is the last line of a finite sequence of axiom instances and applications of these rules. For formulas p,q, the formula (p\to q)\to(\neg q\to\neg p) is derivable.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Derivability from axiom schemas and modus ponens is a syntactic proof-system concept from undergraduate mathematical logic, distinct from simply checking a truth table. A bright US college student should be able to understand the statement after about 30 minutes of focused background research; this rating concerns understanding the claim, not proving it.

Proof 1: Derive transposition from double negation and syllogism

Use the following externally established schemes as lemmas: DN1:\neg\neg p\to p, DN2:p\to\neg\neg p, HS1:(q\to r)\to((p\to q)\to(p\to r)), and HS2:(p\to q)\to((q\to r)\to(p\to r)). The hypothetical-syllogism metatheorem permits composing derivable implications.

  1. {\displaystyle q\to \neg \neg q}       (instance of the (DN2))
  2. {\displaystyle (q\to \neg \neg q)\to ((p\to q)\to (p\to \neg \neg q))}       (instance of (HS1))
  3. {\displaystyle (p\to q)\to (p\to \neg \neg q)}       (from (1) and (2) by modus ponens)
  4. {\displaystyle \neg \neg p\to p}       (instance of the (DN1))
  5. {\displaystyle (\neg \neg p\to p)\to ((p\to \neg \neg q)\to (\neg \neg p\to \neg \neg q))}       (instance of the (HS2))
  6. {\displaystyle (p\to \neg \neg q)\to (\neg \neg p\to \neg \neg q)}       (from (4) and (5) by modus ponens)
  7. {\displaystyle (p\to q)\to (\neg \neg p\to \neg \neg q)}       (from (3) and (6) using the hypothetical syllogism metatheorem)
  8. {\displaystyle (\neg \neg p\to \neg \neg q)\to (\neg q\to \neg p)}       (instance of (A3))
  9. {\displaystyle (p\to q)\to (\neg q\to \neg p)}       (from (7) and (8) using the hypothetical syllogism metatheorem)
