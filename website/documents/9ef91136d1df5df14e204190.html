Statement

Let d\ge1 be an integer, \rho_1,\ldots,\rho_d>0, and c_\alpha\in\mathbb C for \alpha\in\mathbb N_0^d. Write |\alpha|=\sum_i\alpha_i and \rho^\alpha=\prod_i\rho_i^{\alpha_i}. For integers m\ge0, put M_m=\max_{|\alpha|=m}|c_\alpha|\rho^\alpha and S_m=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}|c_\alpha|\rho^\alpha. Then, with extended nonnegative limsups, \limsup_{m\to\infty}S_m^{1/m}=\limsup_{m\to\infty}M_m^{1/m}=\limsup_{|\alpha|\to\infty}(|c_\alpha|\rho^\alpha)^{1/|\alpha|}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves complex modulus; finite sums and maxima; integer tuples; limit superior. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Take roots of the homogeneous maximum bounds — outline

Choose an index attaining M_m=\max_{|\alpha|=m}|c_\alpha|\rho^\alpha. The source estimate is

M_m\le S_m\le(m+1)^dM_m.

Each summand is nonnegative and at most M_m; the indices have all coordinates between 0 and m.

For m\ge1, take mth roots of the displayed bounds. Since (m+1)^{d/m}\to1, the limiting upper growth rates of S_m^{1/m} and M_m^{1/m} agree. The maximum over each degree also gives

\limsup_{|\alpha|\to\infty}(|c_\alpha|\rho^\alpha)^{1/|\alpha|}=\limsup_{m\to\infty}M_m^{1/m}.

Level justification

Bound a finite homogeneous sum between its maximum and the number of possible indices times that maximum, take roots and compare limsups. Undergraduate sequence limits and multi-index notation suffice.
