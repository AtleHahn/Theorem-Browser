Statement

Let \gamma(t)=e^{it} for 0\le t\le2\pi. With the parametrized contour integral defined by \int_\gamma h(z)\,dz=\int_0^{2\pi}h(\gamma(t))\gamma'(t)\,dt, one has \int_\gamma dz/z=2\pi i\ne0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Complex numbers; Real-parameter differentiation; Definite integrals. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Parametrize and cancel the exponential

For \gamma(t)=e^{it}, one has \gamma'(t)=ie^{it}. Hence

\int_\gamma\frac{dz}{z}=\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{1}{e^{it}}ie^{it}\,dt=\int_0^{2\pi}i\,dt=2\pi i.
