Statement

Let m,n\geq0 be integers and R=\mathbb C[x_{ij}:1\leq i\leq m,\ 1\leq j\leq n], the polynomial ring on complex m\times n matrices A. The prime ideals of R invariant under all invertible changes A\mapsto hAg^{-1}, where h\in\mathrm{GL}_m(\mathbb C) and g\in\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb C), are exactly

I_k=(\text{all }k\times k\text{ minors of }(x_{ij})),\qquad 1\leq k\leq\min(m,n)+1.

In particular each I_k is prime. The ideal I_{\min(m,n)+1} is the zero ideal, since there are no minors of that size.

An ideal P is prime if it is proper and ab\in P implies a\in P or b\in P for all a,b\in R. Invariant means that for each g,h, substitution f(A)\mapsto f(hAg^{-1}) sends P onto itself. A minor of size k is the determinant of the submatrix obtained by choosing k rows and k columns. The notation (S) denotes all finite sums of polynomial multiples of elements of S.

Level justification

Linear algebra and introductory ring theory suffice. Learning the explicit substitution-stability condition for ideals is one small extension, and a basic example is the determinant ideal of square singular matrices. No algebraic geometry or proof prerequisites enter the statement.
