Statement

Let U\subseteq\mathbb R^3 be open and let F be smooth with \nabla\times F=0. Suppose piecewise smooth loops c_0,c_1:[0,1]\to U are joined by a piecewise smooth map H:[0,1]^2\to U satisfying H(t,0)=c_0(t), H(t,1)=c_1(t) and H(0,s)=H(1,s). Then \int_{c_0}F\cdot dc_0=\int_{c_1}F\cdot dc_1.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Open subsets of R³; Curl and line integrals; Piecewise smooth maps of a square. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Cancellation of the two side paths

In what follows, we abuse notation and use "{\displaystyle \oplus }" for concatenation of paths and "{\displaystyle \ominus }" for reversing the orientation of a path.

Let D = [0, 1] × [0, 1], and split ∂D into four line segments γj.

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\gamma _{1}:[0,1]\to D;\quad &\gamma _{1}(t)=(t,0)\\\gamma _{2}:[0,1]\to D;\quad &\gamma _{2}(s)=(1,s)\\\gamma _{3}:[0,1]\to D;\quad &\gamma _{3}(t)=(1-t,1)\\\gamma _{4}:[0,1]\to D;\quad &\gamma _{4}(s)=(0,1-s)\end{aligned}}}

so that

{\displaystyle \partial D=\gamma _{1}\oplus \gamma _{2}\oplus \gamma _{3}\oplus \gamma _{4}}

For the given piecewise smooth homotopy H:D\to U, put \gamma=\gamma_1\oplus\gamma_2\oplus\gamma_3\oplus\gamma_4 and define

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\Gamma _{i}(t)&=H(\gamma _{i}(t))&&i=1,2,3,4\\\Gamma (t)&=H(\gamma (t))=(\Gamma _{1}\oplus \Gamma _{2}\oplus \Gamma _{3}\oplus \Gamma _{4})(t)\end{aligned}}}

Let S denote the parametrized surface given by H on D. That

{\displaystyle \iint _{S}\nabla \times \mathbf {F} \,\mathrm {d} S=\oint _{\Gamma }\mathbf {F} \,\mathrm {d} \Gamma }

follows immediately from the parametrized Stokes theorem. F is curl-free, so the left side vanishes, i.e.

{\displaystyle 0=\oint _{\Gamma }\mathbf {F} \,\mathrm {d} \Gamma =\sum _{i=1}^{4}\int _{\Gamma _{i}}\mathbf {F} \,\mathrm {d} \Gamma }

As H(0,s)=H(1,s), \Gamma_2=\ominus\Gamma_4. Thus the line integrals along Γ2(s) and Γ4(s) cancel, leaving

{\displaystyle 0=\int _{\Gamma _{1}}\mathbf {F} \,\mathrm {d} \Gamma +\int _{\Gamma _{3}}\mathbf {F} \,\mathrm {d} \Gamma }

On the other hand, c_0=\Gamma_1 and c_1=\ominus\Gamma_3, so that the desired equality follows almost immediately.
