Statement

Let F be a field and let a,b,c\in F[t] be nonzero pairwise coprime polynomials with a+b=c. Suppose their formal derivatives are not all zero. If \operatorname{rad}(f) denotes the product of the distinct monic irreducible factors of a nonzero polynomial f, then \max\{\deg a,\deg b,\deg c\}\leq\deg\operatorname{rad}(abc)-1.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory polynomial algebra supplies factorization, degree and coprimality. Formal derivatives are the usual coefficient operation over any field, and the radical is explicitly the product of distinct irreducible factors. These limited definitions suffice.

Proof 1: Wronskians, coprime derivative factors and root multiplicities

Replace c by -c, which changes neither its degree nor its roots, to use the source’s convention a+b+c=0.

Step 1. The condition a + b + c = 0 implies that the Wronskians W(a, b) = ab′ − a′b, W(b, c), and W(c, a) are all equal. Write W for their common value.

Step 2. The condition that at least one of the derivatives a′, b′, or c′ is nonzero and that a, b, and c are coprime is used to show that W is nonzero. For example, if W = 0 then ab′ = a′b so a divides a′ (as a and b are coprime) so a′ = 0 (as deg a > deg a′ unless a is constant).

Step 3. W is divisible by each of the greatest common divisors (a, a′), (b, b′), and (c, c′). Since these are coprime it is divisible by their product, and since W is nonzero we get

deg (a, a′) + deg (b, b′) + deg (c, c′) ≤ deg W.

Step 4. Substituting in the inequalities

deg (a, a′) ≥ deg a − (number of distinct roots of a)

deg (b, b′) ≥ deg b − (number of distinct roots of b)

deg (c, c′) ≥ deg c − (number of distinct roots of c)

(where the roots are taken in some algebraic closure) and

deg W ≤ deg a + deg b − 1

we find that

deg c ≤ (number of distinct roots of abc) − 1

which is what we needed to prove.

The same argument using the other two Wronskians bounds \deg a and \deg b. Each distinct irreducible factor has at most its degree many distinct roots in the algebraic closure, so the number of distinct roots of abc is at most \deg\operatorname{rad}(abc). This gives the stated bound also over an imperfect field.
