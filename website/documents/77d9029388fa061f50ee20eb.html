Statement

Let (\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P) be a probability space with an increasing family of sub-\sigma-algebras (\mathcal F_t), called a filtration. A real supermartingale (X_t) is adapted (X_t is \mathcal F_t-measurable), has \mathbb E|X_t|<\infty, and satisfies \mathbb E[X_t\mid\mathcal F_s]\le X_s almost surely for s\le t. A submartingale has the reverse inequality; a martingale has equality. Write X^-=\max(-X,0) and X^+=\max(X,0).

  1. Discrete time. If (X_n)_{n\ge1} is a supermartingale and \sup_n\mathbb E[X_n^-]<\infty, then X_n converges almost surely to an integrable finite random variable. The symmetric assertion holds for submartingales under \sup_n\mathbb E[X_n^+]<\infty.
  2. Continuous time (first convergence theorem). If (N_t)_{t\ge0} is a right-continuous supermartingale and \sup_{t>0}\mathbb E[N_t^-]<\infty, then \lim_{t\to\infty}N_t exists and is finite almost surely. Right-continuity means N_s(\omega)\to N_t(\omega) as s\downarrow t along the sample paths.
  3. Continuous time (second convergence theorem). For such a right-continuous supermartingale, the family (N_t)_{t>0} is uniformly integrable if and only if there is an integrable random variable N with N_t\to N almost surely and in L^1 as t\to\infty.
  4. Discrete martingale terminal values. For a martingale (X_n,\mathcal F_n)_{n\ge0}, the following are equivalent: uniform integrability; existence of an integrable \mathcal F_\infty-measurable X_\infty with X_n\to X_\infty almost surely and in L^1; and existence of an integrable \mathcal F_\infty-measurable X_\infty such that X_n=\mathbb E[X_\infty\mid\mathcal F_n] almost surely for every n. Here \mathcal F_\infty=\sigma(\bigcup_n\mathcal F_n).

Almost surely means outside a set of probability zero. Convergence in L^1 means \mathbb E|X_n-X|\to0 (or its continuous-time counterpart). A family (Y_t) is uniformly integrable when

\lim_{C\to\infty}\sup_t\mathbb E\bigl[|Y_t|\mathbf1_{\{|Y_t|>C\}}\bigr]=0.
