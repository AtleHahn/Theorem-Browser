Simpler proof assessment

Likely alternative prerequisite level: High school only; lowest recorded proof level: Undergraduate level. Partition the sample space into the finitely many event-membership atoms E_J, with weights p_J. Apply finite Cauchy-Schwarz to |J|sqrt(p_J) and sqrt(p_J) over nonempty J. The three resulting finite sums are sum_i P(A_i), P(union A_i), and sum_(i,j)P(A_i intersect A_j). The denominator is positive by hypothesis. This uses only finite probabilities and the school sum-of-squares inequality even when the original probability space is infinite.

Statement

Let A_1,\ldots,A_n be events in a probability space, with integer n\ge1 and \Pr(A_i)>0 for at least one i. Then \Pr\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^n A_i\right)\ge\frac{\left(\sum_{i=1}^n\Pr(A_i)\right)^2}{\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{j=1}^n\Pr(A_i\cap A_j)}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Only finitely many events, their probabilities, unions and pairwise intersections occur. These are elementary probability concepts, and the finite sums and inequality are explicit. This rating concerns understanding the statement, independently of the difficulty of its proof.

Proof 1: Apply Paley–Zygmund to the event count — outline

The inequality can be obtained by applying the Paley–Zygmund inequality to the number of A_i which occur.

Here the count is Y=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf1_{A_i}, and the form of Paley–Zygmund used is \Pr(Y>0)\ge(\mathbb EY)^2/\mathbb E(Y^2) for a nonnegative variable with positive finite first and second moments.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

For the bounded event count Y, expand its first and second moments using indicators and apply the explicitly stated zero-threshold moment bound. Basic probability and finite expectations suffice; no proof of the imported inequality is required.
