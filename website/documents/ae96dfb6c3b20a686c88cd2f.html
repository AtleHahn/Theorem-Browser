Statement

Let ABC be a non-equilateral nondegenerate Euclidean triangle. Its incircle and the circle through the three side midpoints are internally tangent at its Feuerbach point F. Write a=BC,b=CA,c=AB, and let x,y,z be the distances from F to the respective midpoints of BC,CA,AB. Then x+y+z=2\max(x,y,z).

Level
High school only
Level justification

All geometric objects and the distance comparison have elementary Euclidean definitions.

Proof 1: Absolute side differences — outline

The source supplies the distance identities x=\frac{R}{2OI}|b-c|,\qquad y=\frac{R}{2OI}|c-a|,\qquad z=\frac{R}{2OI}|a-b|, where R is the circumradius, O the circumcenter and I the incenter. These are the specific formulas underlying its asserted sum-of-distances property. The routine absolute-difference calculation for three real side lengths is left implicit.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Apply the supplied midpoint-distance formulas and order the three side lengths: the largest absolute difference is the sum of the other two. Elementary triangle geometry and real absolute values suffice.
