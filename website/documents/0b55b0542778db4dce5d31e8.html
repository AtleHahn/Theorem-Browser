Statement

Let \phi be complex analytic near 0 with \phi(0)\ne0. Put f(w)=w/\phi(w), and let g be its local analytic inverse with g(0)=0. If [w^r] denotes Taylor coefficient extraction, then for every integer n\ge1, [z^n]g(z)=\frac1n[w^{n-1}]\phi(w)^n.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Calculus and power series supply composition, coefficients, differentiation and local inversion. Formal series retain the same coefficient operations without analytic convergence; Lambert W is specified by its inverse equation. These direct definitions suffice for the displayed coefficient identities.

Proof 1: Derivative-to-coefficient substitution

Apply the Lagrange inversion theorem when {\displaystyle f(w)=w/\phi (w)} for some analytic {\displaystyle \phi (w)} with {\displaystyle \phi (0)\neq 0.} Take {\displaystyle a=0} to obtain {\displaystyle f(a)=f(0)=0.} Then for the inverse {\displaystyle g(z)} (satisfying {\displaystyle f(g(z))\equiv z}), we have

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}g(z)&=\sum _{n=1}^{\infty }\left[\lim _{w\to 0}{\frac {d^{n-1}}{dw^{n-1}}}\left(\left({\frac {w}{w/\phi (w)}}\right)^{n}\right)\right]{\frac {z^{n}}{n!}}\\{}&=\sum _{n=1}^{\infty }{\frac {1}{n}}\left[{\frac {1}{(n-1)!}}\lim _{w\to 0}{\frac {d^{n-1}}{dw^{n-1}}}(\phi (w)^{n})\right]z^{n},\end{aligned}}}

which can be written alternatively as

{\displaystyle [z^{n}]g(z)={\frac {1}{n}}[w^{n-1}]\phi (w)^{n},}

where {\displaystyle [w^{r}]} is an operator which extracts the coefficient of {\displaystyle w^{r}} in the Taylor series of a function of w.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the explicit local inverse expansion and convert derivatives to Taylor coefficients. Undergraduate complex power series and the local analytic inverse suffice; Lagrange inversion is a supplied coefficient formula.
