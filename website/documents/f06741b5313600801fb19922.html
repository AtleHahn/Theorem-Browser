Statement

For every integer n\ge1, the open cube (-1,1)^n is convex, symmetric about zero, has volume 2^n, and contains no integer point except zero.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The explicitly described coordinate cube has side length two, and each coordinate of an integer point in it must be zero; the claim uses elementary coordinate geometry. This rating concerns understanding the statement, independently of the difficulty of its proof.

Proof 1: Check the open cube at the volume threshold

If S is the interior of the square with vertices (\pm1,\pm1), then S is symmetric and convex, and has area 4, but the only lattice point it contains is the origin. This example, showing that the volume bound is sharp, generalizes to the hypercube (-1,1)^n in every dimension n.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Check coordinatewise that the cube has only the zero integer point and use its product volume, symmetry and convexity. Undergraduate finite-dimensional Euclidean geometry suffices for the arbitrary-dimensional formulation.
