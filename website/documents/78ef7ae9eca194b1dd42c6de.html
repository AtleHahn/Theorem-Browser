Statement

Let G be a locally compact group and \Gamma a discrete subgroup such that \Gamma\backslash G is compact. For a compactly supported test function f on G, the right regular action R(f) on functions on \Gamma\backslash G has kernel K_f(x,y)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}f(x^{-1}\gamma y), with respect to compatible Haar and quotient measures. Moreover, writing O for the conjugacy classes of \Gamma, one has K_f=\sum_{o\in O}K_o, where for \gamma\in o and \Gamma_\gamma its centralizer in \Gamma, K_o(x,y)=\sum_{\delta\in\Gamma_\gamma\backslash\Gamma}f(x^{-1}\delta^{-1}\gamma\delta y).

Proof 1: Unfolding and regrouping by conjugacy class

The right regular representation is (R(g)\phi)(x)=\phi(xg). Integrating it against f gives the source calculation

{\displaystyle \displaystyle R(f)(\phi )(x)=\int _{G}f(y)\phi (xy)\,dy=\int _{\Gamma \backslash G}\sum _{\gamma \in \Gamma }f(x^{-1}\gamma y)\phi (y)\,dy.}

In other words, R(f) is an integral operator on L2(Γ*G*) (the space of functions on Γ*G*) with kernel

{\displaystyle \displaystyle K_{f}(x,y)=\sum _{\gamma \in \Gamma }f(x^{-1}\gamma y).}

The kernel K can be written as

{\displaystyle K_{f}(x,y)=\sum _{o\in O}K_{o}(x,y)}

where O is the set of conjugacy classes in Γ, and

{\displaystyle K_{o}(x,y)=\sum _{\gamma \in o}f(x^{-1}\gamma y)=\sum _{\delta \in \Gamma _{\gamma }\backslash \Gamma }f(x^{-1}\delta ^{-1}\gamma \delta y)}

where {\displaystyle \gamma } is an element of the conjugacy class {\displaystyle o}, and {\displaystyle \Gamma _{\gamma }} is its centralizer in {\displaystyle \Gamma }.
