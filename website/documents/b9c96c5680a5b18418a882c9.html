Statement

Let a_n\to0 and b_n\to0 be real sequences with b_n strictly decreasing. If (a_{n+1}-a_n)/(b_{n+1}-b_n)\to\ell\in[-\infty,+\infty], then a_n/b_n\to\ell.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves real sequences converging to zero; strict monotonicity; difference quotients; extended real limits. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Telescope a tail and let its endpoint tend to infinity — outline

Since b_n strictly decreases to zero, b_n>0 for every n.

Write l=\ell. All sequence indices and tail lengths below are integers.

Case 1: we first consider the case with {\displaystyle -\infty<l<\infty } and {\displaystyle (b_{n})} strictly decreasing. This time, for each {\displaystyle \nu >0}, we can write

{\displaystyle a_{n}=(a_{n}-a_{n+1})+\dots +(a_{n+\nu -1}-a_{n+\nu })+a_{n+\nu },}

and for any {\displaystyle \epsilon /2>0,} {\displaystyle \exists n_{0}} such that for all {\displaystyle n>n_{0}} we have

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}&(l-\epsilon /2)(b_{n}-b_{n+\nu })+a_{n+\nu }=(l-\epsilon /2)[(b_{n}-b_{n+1})+\dots +(b_{n+\nu -1}-b_{n+\nu })]+a_{n+\nu }<a_{n}\\&a_{n}<(l+\epsilon /2)[(b_{n}-b_{n+1})+\dots +(b_{n+\nu -1}-b_{n+\nu })]+a_{n+\nu }=(l+\epsilon /2)(b_{n}-b_{n+\nu })+a_{n+\nu }.\end{aligned}}}

For each fixed n>n_0, the two sequences

{\displaystyle c_{\nu }^{\pm }:={\frac {a_{n+\nu }-b_{n+\nu }(l\pm \epsilon /2)}{b_{n}}}}

are infinitesimal since by hypothesis {\displaystyle a_{n+\nu },b_{n+\nu }\to 0} as {\displaystyle \nu \to \infty }, thus for all {\displaystyle \epsilon /2>0} there are {\displaystyle \nu _{\pm }>0} such that

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}&|c_{\nu }^{+}|<\epsilon /2,\quad \forall \nu >\nu _{+},\\&|c_{\nu }^{-}|<\epsilon /2,\quad \forall \nu >\nu _{-},\end{aligned}}}

thus, choosing {\displaystyle \nu } appropriately for this fixed n (which is to say, taking the limit with respect to {\displaystyle \nu }) we obtain

{\displaystyle l-\epsilon <l-\epsilon /2+c_{\nu }^{-}<{\frac {a_{n}}{b_{n}}}<l+\epsilon /2+c_{\nu }^{+}<l+\epsilon ,\quad \forall n>n_{0}}

which concludes the proof.

Case 2: we assume {\displaystyle l=+\infty } and {\displaystyle (b_{n})} strictly decreasing. For all {\displaystyle 2M>0} there exists {\displaystyle n_{0}>0} such that for all {\displaystyle n>n_{0},}

{\displaystyle {\frac {a_{n+1}-a_{n}}{b_{n+1}-b_{n}}}>2M\implies a_{n}-a_{n+1}>2M(b_{n}-b_{n+1}).}

Therefore, for each {\displaystyle \nu >0,}

{\displaystyle {\frac {a_{n}}{b_{n}}}>2M+{\frac {a_{n+\nu }-2Mb_{n+\nu }}{b_{n}}},\quad \forall n>n_{0}.}

The sequence

{\displaystyle c_{\nu }:={\frac {a_{n+\nu }-2Mb_{n+\nu }}{b_{n}}}}

converges to {\displaystyle 0} (keeping {\displaystyle n} fixed). Hence

{\displaystyle \forall M>0\,~\exists {\bar {\nu }}>0} such that {\displaystyle -M<c_{\nu }<M,\,\forall \nu >{\bar {\nu }},}

and, choosing {\displaystyle \nu } conveniently, we conclude the proof

{\displaystyle {\frac {a_{n}}{b_{n}}}>2M+c_{\nu }>M,\quad \forall n>n_{0}.}

The case l=-\infty follows by the same argument with signs reversed.
