Statement

Let X_1,\ldots,X_n be independent zero-one variables with common success probability 1/2\le p<1. For 0<x<1-p,

\Pr\left(n^{-1}\sum_iX_i>p+x\right)\le\exp\left(-\frac{nx^2}{2p(1-p)}\right).

Proof 1: Insert the stated divergence comparison

Use the Chernoff–Hoeffding bound

\Pr\left(n^{-1}\sum_iX_i\ge p+x\right)\le e^{-nD(p+x\Vert p)},

where D(u\Vert v)=u\ln(u/v)+(1-u)\ln((1-u)/(1-v)) is the Kullback–Leibler divergence between Bernoulli distributed random variables with parameters u and v respectively.

Taking \varepsilon=x, if p ≥ ⁠1/2⁠, then {\displaystyle D(p+\varepsilon \parallel p)\geq {\tfrac {\varepsilon ^{2}}{2p(1-p)}}} which means

{\displaystyle \Pr \left({\frac {1}{n}}\sum X_{i}>p+x\right)\leq \exp \left(-{\frac {x^{2}n}{2p(1-p)}}\right).}
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the explicit Bernoulli tail theorem and then substitute the supplied scalar divergence lower bound into its negative exponential. Introductory undergraduate probability of sample averages and logarithmic functions suffice; no information-theoretic framework is needed. The parameter p>=1/2 is important for this stronger one-sided comparison, and the strict-tail event is contained in the non-strict event bounded by the theorem.
