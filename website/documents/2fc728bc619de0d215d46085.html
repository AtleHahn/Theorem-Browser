Statement

Let i:H\to E be a real abstract Wiener space, where E is a separable Banach space, H is its Cameron–Martin Hilbert space, and \gamma is its abstract Wiener probability measure. For h\in H, write W_h(x)=\langle h,x\rangle^{\sim} for the Paley–Wiener integral. Let F:E\to\mathbb R be Fréchet differentiable with bounded derivative: \sup_{x\in E}\lVert DF(x)\rVert<\infty. For every h\in H and t\in\mathbb R,

\int_E F(x+t i(h))\,d\gamma(x)=\int_E F(x)e^{tW_h(x)-t^2\lVert h\rVert_H^2/2}\,d\gamma(x).
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Abstract Wiener spaces, Cameron–Martin embeddings and Paley–Wiener stochastic integrals require substantial infinite-dimensional probability foundations, beyond a half-hour introduction. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Shifted-integral form of the Cameron–Martin formula (outline)

Use the Cameron–Martin formula: the pushforward of \gamma under x\mapsto x+t i(h) has density e^{tW_h(x)-t^2\lVert h\rVert_H^2/2} with respect to \gamma. Integrating the Cameron–Martin formula with respect to Wiener measure on both sides gives

\int_E F(x+t i(h))\,d\gamma(x)=\int_E F(x)e^{tW_h(x)-t^2\lVert h\rVert_H^2/2}\,d\gamma(x).
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The change-of-measure step presupposes an abstract Wiener space, its Cameron–Martin subspace and Paley–Wiener random variables. Bounded Fréchet derivative gives linear growth, but integrability still uses Gaussian measure theory.
