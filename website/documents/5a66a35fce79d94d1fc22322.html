Statement

Let K\subseteq\mathbb R be a field. If finitely many real lengths \ell_1,\ldots,\ell_s are constructed by compass and straightedge from given lengths in K, then the generated field K(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_s) has degree a power of two over K.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A generated subfield and its degree as a vector-space dimension are undergraduate abstract-algebra notions. Compass-and-straightedge construction is an elementary geometric input; the statement does not require Galois groups or a classification of constructible numbers.

Proof 1: Quadratic towers and degree multiplicativity

It is straightforward to show from analytic geometry that constructible lengths must come from base lengths by the solution of some sequence of quadratic equations. In terms of field theory, such lengths must be contained in a field extension generated by a tower of quadratic extensions. It follows that a field generated by finitely many constructions will always have degree over the base field that is a power of two.

The tower law gives a power of two for the degree of the finite tower over K; the intermediate field K(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_s) has degree dividing that degree.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Place the finitely many lengths in a quadratic tower, multiply its extension degrees, and use the tower law for the generated intermediate field. Finite field extensions and degrees are an ordinary undergraduate algebra extension, without Galois correspondence.
