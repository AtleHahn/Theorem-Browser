Statement

The circumcenter of a nondegenerate Euclidean triangle lies inside it if the triangle is acute, at the midpoint of the hypotenuse if it is right, and outside it if it is obtuse.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement uses elementary Euclidean lengths and angles.

Proof 1: Read coordinate signs and use equal endpoint distances — outline

Trilinear coordinates are proportional signed distances to the opposite sidelines, using the inward-positive convention.

The locational features in the statement can be seen by considering the trilinear coordinates \cos\alpha:\cos\beta:\cos\gamma of the circumcenter, where \alpha,\beta,\gamma are the vertex angles: all three coordinates are positive for any interior point, at least one coordinate is negative for any exterior point, and one coordinate is zero and two are positive for a non-vertex point on a side of the triangle.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The given signed-distance coordinates reduce the location to signs of cosines of triangle angles. In the right case the center lies on the hypotenuse and its equal distances to the endpoints identify the midpoint. Only elementary geometry and trigonometry are required.
