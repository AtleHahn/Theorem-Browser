Statement

A family of axis-aligned 1\times2\times4 bricks with integer-lattice vertices, contained in a 6\times6\times6 cube and with pairwise disjoint interiors, has at most 26 bricks. Permutations of brick side directions are allowed.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement bounds a finite number of explicitly sized lattice bricks inside a cube.

Proof 1: Count the scarce-color cells — outline

Color the twenty-seven 2\times2\times2 blocks alternately black and white. The cube has 216 unit cells, with eight more of one color than the other, while each brick covers four of each color. The smaller color class has 104 cells, so at most 104/4=26 bricks can be present.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Count the 104 scarce-color cells and divide by the four used per brick. School integer arithmetic and grid geometry suffice.
