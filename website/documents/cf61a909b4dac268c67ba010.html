Statement

If a real function f is defined near a, differentiable at a, and f(a)\ne0, then (1/f)'(a)=-f'(a)/f(a)^2.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Real derivatives; Limits; Products and quotients of real functions. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Substitute in the quotient rule

The reciprocal rule is a special case of the quotient rule in which the numerator is 1. Applying the quotient rule gives

{\displaystyle \left(\frac1f\right)'(x)={\frac {d}{dx}}\left[{\frac {1}{f(x)}}\right]={\frac {0\cdot f(x)-1\cdot f'(x)}{f(x)^{2}}}={\frac {-f'(x)}{f(x)^{2}}}.}

Here the derivative is evaluated at x=a.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the quotient rule with constant numerator one. Single-variable calculus suffices.
