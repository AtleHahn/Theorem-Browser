Statement

Let a,b,c be the legs and hypotenuse of a Euclidean right triangle. Three similar plane figures of finite areas A,B,C have corresponding linear dimensions a,b,c. Then A+B=C.

Proof 1: Scale areas by squared corresponding lengths

The basic idea is that the area of a plane figure is proportional to the square of any linear dimension, and in particular is proportional to the square of the length of any side. Thus, if similar figures with areas A,B,C have corresponding lengths a,b,c, then

\begin{aligned} &\frac{A}{a^2}=\frac{B}{b^2}=\frac{C}{c^2}\\ &\quad\implies A+B=\frac{a^2}{c^2}C+\frac{b^2}{c^2}C. \end{aligned}

But, by the Pythagorean theorem, a^2+b^2=c^2, so A+B=C.
