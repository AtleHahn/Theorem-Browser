Statement

Let n,m\ge1 be integers, and let a_1\ge\cdots\ge a_n\ge0 and b_1,\ldots,b_m\ge0 be integers. There exists a simple bipartite graph with labeled vertex parts \{u_1,\ldots,u_n\} and \{v_1,\ldots,v_m\} in which u_i has degree a_i and v_j has degree b_j if and only if

\sum_{i=1}^n a_i=\sum_{j=1}^m b_j, \qquad \sum_{i=1}^k a_i\le\sum_{j=1}^m\min(b_j,k)\quad(1\le k\le n).

A simple bipartite graph permits at most one edge between each pair of vertices in opposite parts and no edges within a part; the degree counts incident edges. The sequence (b_j) need not be sorted.

High school only
Level justification

The graph terminology is explicitly defined using finite sets and counting; all inequalities use elementary arithmetic.

Proof 1: Ferrers degrees lowered by edge rotations (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Section3, Theorem3 and Lemmas4–6, pp.35–38).

For the sorted column list \beta, its conjugate satisfies \sum_{i\le k}\beta_i^*=\sum_j\min(\beta_j,k). The conditions at k=n give b_j\le n, so the Ferrers construction fits the prescribed row part. Sort b and restore its labels afterwards; isolated vertices supply any missing zero entries, and zero total degree gives the empty graph. In Lemma5(2), the intended conclusion is \operatorname{dpt}_{H_2}(V_1)=\mu, with the V_2 degrees unchanged.

The transfer step can be made explicit. For a current decreasing list \lambda dominating a, take the first index where \lambda_i>a_i, then the first later deficit \lambda_j<a_j. Decrease the last entry in the constant block containing \lambda_i and increase \lambda_j. The list stays decreasing and still dominates a, while \sum_i|\lambda_i-a_i| decreases by two. Repeat until it equals a; each transfer is realized by Lemma5’s edge rotation.
