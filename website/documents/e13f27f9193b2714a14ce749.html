Statement

For each positive integer N, with angles in radians, \sum_{n=1}^N\sin n=\frac{\sin1+\sin N-\sin(N+1)}{2-2\cos1}. In particular these partial sums are bounded independently of N.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The finite trigonometric formula and uniform bound use elementary trigonometry and integer indices.

Proof 1: Evaluate two geometric sums — outline

Euler’s sine formula and the finite geometric sums give

\sum_{n=1}^N\sin n=\sum_{n=1}^N\frac{e^{in}-e^{-in}}{2i}=\frac{\sum_{n=1}^N(e^i)^n-\sum_{n=1}^N(e^{-i})^n}{2i}=\frac{\sin1+\sin N-\sin(N+1)}{2-2\cos1}.
Level
High school only
Level justification

Use the supplied Euler sine identity and finite geometric sums, then simplify and bound the sine terms. Complex-number algebra and high-school trigonometry suffice; no convergence theorem is involved.
