Statement

Let m\geq1, let z\in\mathbb C satisfy z^m=1, and let F be a complex random variable with \mathbb EF=0 and \mathbb E|F|^2=1. Set F_n=z^nF for n\in\mathbb Z. Then the sequence is stationary in covariance, its autocovariance is g(k)=z^k, and its spectral measure on the dual of \mathbb Z is the unit point mass at the character \xi_z(k)=z^k. Also F_{n+m}=F_n.

Level justification

The statement uses probability and spectral-measure terminology even though the root-of-unity algebra is elementary.

Proof 1: Covariance computation and a Dirac spectral measure

Since |z|=1, the mean is zero and the covariance computation in the source gives

\mathbb E(F_n\overline{F_j})=z^{n-j}\mathbb E|F|^2=z^{n-j}.

The unit point mass at \xi_z has Fourier coefficient \int\xi(k)\,d\delta_{\xi_z}(\xi)=z^k, so it gives the spectral measure (uniqueness is Bochner's theorem). Finally z^{n+m}F=z^nF, since z^m=1.
