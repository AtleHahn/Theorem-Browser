Statement

For real r\ne-1 and x>0, \int x^r\,dx=x^{r+1}/(r+1)+C.

Proof 1: Differentiate the proposed antiderivative

For x>0, write x^r=\exp(r\log x). The chain rule and the derivatives of exponential and logarithm give (x^r)'=\exp(r\log x)r/x=rx^{r-1}. Apply the result to r+1 and divide by r+1\ne0: the derivative of x^{r+1}/(r+1) is x^r. Antiderivatives on an interval differ by a constant.
