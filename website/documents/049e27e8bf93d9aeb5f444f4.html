Statement

For a finite family F of nonempty sets, a subfamily K pins M\subseteq F if every member of M meets a member of K. Its width w(F) is the minimum size of K\subseteq F pinning all of F; its matching width mw(F) is the maximum over disjoint subfamilies M of their minimum pinning size using members of F. For a finite set U and F=\{\{u\}:u\in U\}, its maximum matching size \nu(F), minimum vertex-cover size \tau(F), width and matching width all equal |U|. For a simple bipartite graph with parts X,Y, define N(S) as the neighbors in X of S\subseteq Y and F_S=\{\{x\}:x\in N(S)\}. The conditions \nu(F_S)\ge|S|, \tau(F_S)\ge|S|, mw(F_S)\ge|S|, and [mw(F_S)\ge|S|\text{ or }w(F_S)\ge2|S|-1], each required for every S\subseteq Y, are each equivalent to Hall’s inequalities |N(S)|\ge|S|.

Proof 1: Count disjoint singletons and their necessary pinners

The neighborhood-hypergraph F_S contains just singletons—a singleton for every neighbor of S. Since singletons do not intersect, the entire set of singletons is a matching. Hence,

\nu(F_S)=|N(S)|.

In a hypergraph of singletons, a transversal must contain all vertices. Hence,

\tau(F_S)=|N(S)|.

Thus each of the first two conditions becomes, for every subset S of Y,

|N(S)|\ge |S|.

This is exactly Hall's marriage condition.

Since singletons do not intersect, the entire family F_S is a matching, and its only pinning-set is the family F_S itself, i.e., its matching-width is |N(S)|, and its width is the same:

w(F_S)=mw(F_S)=|N(S)|.

The same arguments give all four equalities for the singleton family on U. Thus both remaining conditions are equivalent to Hall's marriage condition: for nonempty S, 2|S|-1\ge |S|, and the empty-set condition is automatic.
