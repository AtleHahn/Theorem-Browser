Statement

Let H be a complex Hilbert space and B(H) its bounded operators. For S\subseteq B(H) put S'=\{T\in B(H):TO=OT\ \forall O\in S\}, and S''=(S')'. For every subset M\subseteq B(H) its weak operator closure is contained in M''.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The conclusion compares weak operator closure with a double commutant.

Proof 1: Apply local commutant closedness to the bicommutant

For any x and y in H, the map T → <Tx, y> is continuous in the weak operator topology, by its definition. Therefore, for any fixed operator O, so is the map

{\displaystyle T\to \langle (OT-TO)x,y\rangle =\langle Tx,O^{*}y\rangle -\langle TOx,y\rangle }

Let S be any subset of B(H), and S′ its commutant. For any operator T in S′, this function is zero for all O in S. For any T not in S′, it must be nonzero for some O in S and some x and y in H. By its continuity there is an open neighborhood of T for the weak operator topology on which it is nonzero, and which therefore is also not in S′. Hence any commutant S′ is closed in the weak operator topology. In particular, so is M′′; since it contains M, it also contains its weak operator closure.
