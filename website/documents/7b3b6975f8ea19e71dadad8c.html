Statement

Let (X_i,d_i) be a sequence of nonempty compact metric spaces. Suppose their diameters are bounded above by one finite constant, and for each \varepsilon>0 there is an integer N_\varepsilon such that every X_i can be covered by at most N_\varepsilon metric balls of radius \varepsilon. Then some subsequence converges to a compact metric space X in Gromov–Hausdorff distance. For compact metric spaces Y,Z, this distance is the infimum of d_H(f(Y),g(Z)) over distance-preserving embeddings into a common metric space M, where d_H(A,B)=\max\{\sup_{a\in A}\inf_{b\in B}d_M(a,b),\ \sup_{b\in B}\inf_{a\in A}d_M(a,b)\}. Convergence means that these distances to X tend to zero.
