Statement

Let (F_n)_{n\in\mathbb Z} be complex random variables on one probability space with mean zero and finite second moments. Assume stationarity of their covariance: \mathbb E(F_n\overline{F_m})=g(n-m) for a function g:\mathbb Z\to\mathbb C. Then there exists a unique finite positive Borel measure \mu on the circle \mathbb T=\mathbb R/\mathbb Z satisfying

g(k)=\int_{\mathbb T}e^{-2\pi ikx}\,d\mu(x).

Proof 1: Gram positivity followed by Bochner representation

In the Hilbert space of random variables with finite second moments, use \langle F,H\rangle=\mathbb E(F\overline H). The mean-zero assumption identifies covariance with this inner product, so

g(n-m)=\langle F_n,F_m\rangle.

The finite matrix of these values is a Gram matrix and is positive semidefinite. This gives precisely positive definiteness of g.

Apply the finite-positive-measure version of Bochner's theorem stated at the beginning of the source: positive-definite functions on \mathbb Z are Fourier transforms of unique finite positive measures on its dual circle. This gives the displayed representation of g.
