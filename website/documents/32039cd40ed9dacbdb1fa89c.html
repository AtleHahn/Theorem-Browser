Statement

Projection from (0,1) gives a bijection from rational points of the unit circle other than (0,1) to rational points (t,0) of the horizontal axis. Its inverse is (2t/(1+t^2),(t^2-1)/(1+t^2)).

Proof 1: Source calculation — outline

For the stereographic approach, suppose that P′ is a point on the x-axis with rational coordinates, writing t=m/n with integers m,n and n\ne0,

{\displaystyle P'=\left({\frac {m}{n}},0\right).}

Then, it can be shown by basic algebra that the other intersection P of the line through P′ and the north pole N=(0,1) with the circle has coordinates

{\displaystyle P=\left({\frac {2\left({\frac {m}{n}}\right)}{\left({\frac {m}{n}}\right)^{2}+1}},{\frac {\left({\frac {m}{n}}\right)^{2}-1}{\left({\frac {m}{n}}\right)^{2}+1}}\right)=\left({\frac {2mn}{m^{2}+n^{2}}},{\frac {m^{2}-n^{2}}{m^{2}+n^{2}}}\right).}

This establishes that each rational point of the x-axis goes over to a rational point of the unit circle. The converse, that every rational point of the unit circle other than N comes from such a point of the x-axis, follows by applying stereographic projection. Suppose that P(x, y) is a point of the unit circle other than N, with x and y rational numbers. Then the point P′ obtained by stereographic projection onto the x-axis has coordinates

{\displaystyle \left({\frac {x}{1-y}},0\right)}

which is rational.

High school only
Level justification

Solve the line-circle intersection and check the rational forward and inverse coordinate ratios. School coordinate geometry and fractions suffice; abstract projective geometry is unnecessary.
