Statement

A finite simple bipartite graph on n vertices has at most n^2/4 edges. Here bipartite means that its vertices split into two sets and every edge has one endpoint in each set.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement supplies the bipartition definition; the bound concerns finite counts of edges and vertices.

Proof 1: AM–GM for the two vertex-class sizes

Two-number inequality

For nonnegative real x,y, (x+y)/2\ge\sqrt{xy}, with equality exactly when x=y.

This follows from the fact that the square of a real number is always non-negative (greater than or equal to zero) and from the identity (a ± b)2 = a2 ± 2ab + b2:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}0&\leq (x-y)^{2}\\&=x^{2}-2xy+y^{2}\\&=x^{2}+2xy+y^{2}-4xy\\&=(x+y)^{2}-4xy.\end{aligned}}}

Hence (x + y)2 ≥ 4xy, with equality when (x − y)2 = 0, i.e. x = y. The AM–GM inequality then follows from taking the positive square root of both sides and then dividing both sides by 2.

Edge count

In graph theory, the AM-GM inequality can be used to prove that the number of edges of a bipartite graph on {\displaystyle n} vertices is bounded from above by {\displaystyle {\frac {n^{2}}{4}}}. The vertices of a bipartite graph with {\displaystyle n} vertices may be divided into two classes {\displaystyle A} and {\displaystyle B}. Since we can only draw edges between vertices in one class to another, the maximum number of edges is given by {\displaystyle |A||B|}. By the AM-GM inequality, we can bound {\displaystyle |A||B|} from above like so:

{\displaystyle |A||B|\leq ({\frac {|A|+|B|}{2}})^{2}\leq {\frac {n^{2}}{4}}}

Level
High school only
Level justification

There are at most |A||B| possible cross-part edges. The locally proved two-number inequality bounds this product by (|A|+|B|)^2/4=n^2/4. Only elementary counting and squares are used.
