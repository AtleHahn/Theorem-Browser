Statement

Let n\geq2. If f is holomorphic near 0\in\mathbb C^n, f(0)=0, and f(z,0,\ldots,0) is not identically zero, then the zero at the origin is not isolated.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement concerns zeros in a neighborhood in complex affine space, with holomorphicity and a nonzero coordinate-axis restriction.

Mathematical Field

Proof 1: Varying the remaining variables (outline)

Use the Weierstrass preparation theorem: near the origin f(z,z')=h(z,z')W(z,z'), where h does not vanish and W is monic in z of positive degree k, with every lower coefficient zero at z'=0. The source finds zeros by varying the remaining variables and solving this polynomial. Polynomial roots vary continuously as a multiset with the coefficients, so roots approach zero as the remaining variables approach zero. Since there is at least one remaining variable, this gives zeros other than the origin arbitrarily close to it.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Use Weierstrass preparation for a germ in several complex variables and vary the transverse parameter to obtain nearby roots. Several-variable holomorphic germs and analytic preparation exceed ordinary undergraduate complex analysis.
