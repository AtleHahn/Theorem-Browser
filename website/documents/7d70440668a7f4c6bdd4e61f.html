Statement

Let \to be a terminating relation on a set. Suppose it has the polygon property: any two one-step reductions u\to v, u\to w extend to a common descendant d by reduction chains from v and w of the same finite length. Then the relation is confluent, every element has a unique normal form, and every maximal reduction chain starting at a fixed element has the same length.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A terminating relation is one with no infinite reduction sequence. The polygon condition is explicitly defined; confluent and normal form mean that reductions can be joined and end at an irreducible element. These are concrete extensions of relations and finite chains.

Proof 1: Induction on shortest reduction length outline

The polygon property implies local confluence. Newman’s lemma says that a terminating, locally confluent rewriting system is confluent.

By Newman's lemma, the system is confluent, and every element has a unique normal form. For each element {\displaystyle u}, define {\displaystyle rl(u)} be the length of the shortest reduction chain from {\displaystyle u} to its normal form.

Induct on {\displaystyle rl(u)}. If {\displaystyle rl(u)=0}, then {\displaystyle u} is already in normal form. Otherwise, {\displaystyle rl(u)\geq 1}, and we apply the polygonal property and the induction hypothesis.
