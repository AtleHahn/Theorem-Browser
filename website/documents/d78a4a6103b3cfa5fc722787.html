Statement

Let X be a nonsingular quasi-projective variety over a field, and let Y,Z be algebraic cycles on X. There exists an algebraic cycle Z^{\prime} rationally equivalent to Z such that Y and Z^{\prime} intersect properly. Quasi-projective means a locally closed subvariety of a projective space. An algebraic cycle is a finite integer linear combination of integral closed subvarieties. Two cycles are rationally equivalent if their difference is a sum of divisors of nonzero rational functions on integral subvarieties. Two cycles intersect properly if, for every pair of components of dimensions a,b in an n-dimensional ambient variety, their intersection is empty or has dimension at most a+b-n.

Proof 1: Moving cycles and fibre specialization (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorem 3.1 and proof, pp. 19–21; Theorem 3.4 and proof, pp. 22–23; finite-field push-pull and coprime-degree descent on p. 23.).

Here is the dimension and field extension argument supplementing the source's moving construction. Take projective closures and restrict the moved cycles to the smooth open variety. Integer coefficients are handled componentwise. If component dimensions r,s satisfy r+s<m=\dim X, apply the construction on X\times\mathbb P^d, with d=m, to Z\times\mathbb P^d against Y\times\mathbb P^d. Their moved intersection has dimension at most r+s+d-m. A general fibre over \mathbb P^d has intersection dimension at most r+s-m, and hence is empty when this is negative. Generic flatness gives proper fibre sections, and refined fibre pullback preserves rational equivalence and sends Z\times\mathbb P^d to Z.

Over a finite field choose good fibres and moving parameters over two sufficiently large extensions of coprime degrees a,b. Finite pushforward gives proper cycles in classes a[Z],b[Z]. An integer combination with coefficients u,v satisfying ua+vb=1 gives the required class. Finite pushforward preserves the intersection dimension bound. Work separately on the connected components.
