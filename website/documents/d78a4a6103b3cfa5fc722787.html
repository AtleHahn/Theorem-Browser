Statement

Let X be a nonsingular quasi-projective variety over a field, and let Y,Z be algebraic cycles on X. There exists an algebraic cycle Z^{\prime} rationally equivalent to Z such that Y and Z^{\prime} intersect properly. Quasi-projective means a locally closed subvariety of a projective space. An algebraic cycle is a finite integer linear combination of integral closed subvarieties. Two cycles are rationally equivalent if their difference is a sum of divisors of nonzero rational functions on integral subvarieties. Two cycles intersect properly if, for every pair of components of dimensions a,b in an n-dimensional ambient variety, their intersection is empty or has dimension at most a+b-n.
