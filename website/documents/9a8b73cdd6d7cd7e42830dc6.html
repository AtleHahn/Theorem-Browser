Statement

Let p,q be real continuous functions on [a,b] with p>0. If u,v are linearly independent real solutions of (py')'+qy=0 and z_1<z_2 are successive zeros of u, then v has exactly one zero in (z_1,z_2). Solutions are understood to have y and py' continuously differentiable.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Real continuously differentiable functions; Divergence-form differential equation; Linear independence of two functions; Successive zeros. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. Proof 1: Specializing comparison to identical equations (outline)

Use the stated Sturm–Picone comparison principle: for (p_1u')'+q_1u=0 and (p_2v')'+q_2v=0 with 0<p_2\leq p_1 and q_1\leq q_2, between successive zeros of u the second solution has a zero unless the two solutions are proportional on that interval.

In the special case where both equations are identical, one obtains the Sturm separation theorem: the zeros of linearly independent solutions alternate.

Level justification

Apply the explicitly stated comparison to identical scalar equations and use uniqueness to exclude proportional independent solutions; applying comparison in the reverse direction excludes two intervening zeros. Undergraduate ODEs are a small extension of differential calculus here.
