Statement

Let g:I\to\mathbb R be continuously differentiable on an open interval I=(a,b). If \int_a^b g(x)h'(x)\,dx=0 for every smooth compactly supported h, then g is constant on I.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Continuous real functions; Smooth compactly supported test functions; Integration; Differentiation. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Reduce to zero derivative by integration by parts

Since continuous differentiability of g is assumed, integration by parts reduces the statement to the basic version of the fundamental lemma of the calculus of variations: if a continuous function F on (a,b) satisfies

\int_a^b F(x)h(x)\,dx=0

for all compactly supported smooth functions h on (a,b), then F is identically zero. A function with zero derivative on an interval is constant.

Level justification
