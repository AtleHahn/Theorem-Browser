Statement

Let \pi(x) be the number of primes at most x. As x\to\infty, \pi(2x)-\pi(x)\sim x/\log x.

Level

Level justification

Proof 1: Scale the prime number theorem and subtract

The prime number theorem (PNT) implies that the number of primes up to {\displaystyle x}, denoted {\displaystyle \pi (x)}, is roughly {\displaystyle x/{\text{log}}(x)}, so if we replace {\displaystyle x} with {\displaystyle 2x} then we see the number of primes up to {\displaystyle 2x} is asymptotically twice the number of primes up to {\displaystyle x} (the terms {\displaystyle {\text{log}}(2x)} and {\displaystyle {\text{log}}(x)} are asymptotically equivalent). Therefore, the number of primes in the interval from {\displaystyle x} to {\displaystyle 2x} is roughly {\displaystyle x/{\text{log}}(x)} when {\displaystyle x} is large.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Normalize both prime counts by x/log x, use the prime-number asymptotic and log(2x)/log x tending to one, then subtract the limits two and one. Elementary asymptotic real analysis suffices to apply the theorem.
