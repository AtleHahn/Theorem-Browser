Statement

Let u,f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty) be continuous, and let w:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty) be continuous and nondecreasing with w(x)>0 for x>0. Suppose \alpha\ge0 and u(t)\le\alpha+\int_0^t f(s)w(u(s))\,ds for all t\ge0. Fix x_0>0 and set G(x)=\int_{x_0}^x dy/w(y) for x>0. If G(0):=\lim_{x\downarrow0}G(x) is finite, include 0 in the domain of G; otherwise take its domain to be (0,\infty). Suppose \alpha belongs to this domain. Since G is strictly increasing, it has an inverse on its range. For every T\ge0 such that G(\alpha)+\int_0^t f(s)\,ds belongs to that range for all 0\le t\le T,

u(t)\le G^{-1}\left(G(\alpha)+\int_0^t f(s)\,ds\right)\quad(0\le t\le T).

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Calculus and real analysis suffice for the explicitly defined inverse-domain condition; no differential-equation existence theory is needed to read the statement.
