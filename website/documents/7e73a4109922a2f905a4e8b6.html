Likely alternative prerequisite level: High school only; lowest recorded proof level: Undergraduate level. If a permutation places two y-values in reverse order relative to increasing x, exchanging them changes the sum by (x_j-x_i)(y_high-y_low)>=0. Repeated swaps give the matching-order maximum; applying the same argument to reversed y gives the minimum. Ties and n=1 yield zero changes. Finite algebra and permutations replace ordered derivatives and the functional theorem.

Statement

Let n\ge1 be an integer and let permutations act on \{1,\ldots,n\}. For ordered real sequences x_1\le\cdots\le x_n and y_1\le\cdots\le y_n, and any permutation \sigma, \sum_i x_i y_{n-i+1}\le\sum_i x_i y_{\sigma(i)}\le\sum_i x_i y_i.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Understanding the two finite-sum bounds uses elementary real order and finite permutations, with no derivative prerequisite.

High School Mathematics

Proof 1: Substitute linear functions into the functional inequality

Use the functional rearrangement theorem: if continuously differentiable functions satisfy f_1'\le\cdots\le f_n' on [x_1,x_n], their evaluation sums at the ordered x_i are bounded by the reverse and matching orders. Put f_i(x)=xy_i. Their derivatives are the ordered constants y_i, so substitution gives the displayed bounds.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Differentiate the linear functions f_i(x)=xy_i and apply the functional rearrangement theorem. Single-variable undergraduate calculus suffices.
