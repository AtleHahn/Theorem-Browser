Statement

Let \Gamma\subset\mathbb R^2 be a Jordan curve, meaning the image of an injective continuous map from the circle, and let \Omega be the bounded component of \mathbb R^2\setminus\Gamma.

Suppose that for every p\in\Gamma there are a closed Euclidean disk D_p of radius 1 and an open neighborhood U_p of p such that

p\in\partial D_p,\qquad D_p\cap U_p\subseteq\overline\Omega.

Thus the curve has a locally interior supporting unit disk at each point; no differentiability of the curve is required in this formulation.

Then there is a closed Euclidean disk D of radius 1 such that D\subseteq\overline\Omega.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Plane topology, circles and closure are familiar undergraduate concepts. The supporting-disk condition is given explicitly in elementary set and neighborhood terms; learning the Jordan-domain convention is a small extension of point-set topology.

  • Extends the inscribed-unit-disk conclusion from pointwise smooth curvature bounds to local supporting-disk geometry.

Proof 1: Nested regions and disjoint disks (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorem 1; §3 Lemmas 3–8 and proof of Theorem 1, pp.4–10; Appendix A for the Jordan-curve orientation lemma.).

The following hypothesis bridge is supplied here: choose a ball B_epsilon(p) contained in the supplied neighborhood U_p. The open set int(D_p) intersect B_epsilon(p) lies in closure(Omega). Every boundary point of a Jordan domain is approached by its exterior, so an open subset of closure(Omega) lies in Omega. This is exactly the paper’s bounded-convex-curvature condition. Use its §3 proof: two-contact inscribed disks have a positive lower radius bound; if all inscribed disks have radius below 1, compactness supplies a uniform upper gap. A recursive construction produces nested boundary regions whose successive differences contain pairwise disjoint disks of a fixed positive radius, contradicting bounded area. Finally take the closure of the resulting open unit disk to obtain the required closed disk in closure(Omega). No differentiability, tangent, or rectifiability assumption is added.
