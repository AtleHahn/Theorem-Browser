Statement

For f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R defined by f(x)=1/x when x\ne0 and f(0)=0, the graph is closed in the usual Euclidean plane but f is discontinuous at zero.

Level

Level justification

Proof 1: Reciprocal with an isolated zero-value construction (outline)

Take the function

f(x)=\begin{cases}1/x,&x\ne0,\\0,&x=0.\end{cases}

This gives a discontinuous function with a closed graph for the noncompact target \mathbb R.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Check the reciprocal graph away from zero and exclude finite limiting heights as nonzero arguments approach zero. Undergraduate real limits and Euclidean closedness suffice; the source asserts the example without these checks.
