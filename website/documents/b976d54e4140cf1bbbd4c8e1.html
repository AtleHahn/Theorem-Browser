Statement

Let A be a finite nonzero associative ring with identity and without zero divisors. Then every nonzero element is invertible, so A is a division ring.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory group and ring theory supplies subgroups, quotients, commutators and homomorphisms. Any added terms such as perfect, nilpotent or virtually free describe explicit subgroup conditions; they are small algebraic extensions without representation or geometric-group theory.

Proof 1: Injective multiplication maps

Let {\displaystyle A} be a finite domain. For each nonzero {\displaystyle b} in {\displaystyle A}, the two maps

{\displaystyle a\mapsto ab,a\mapsto ba:A\to A}

are injective by the cancellation property, and thus, surjective by counting. It follows from elementary group theory that the nonzero elements of {\displaystyle A} form a group under multiplication. Thus, {\displaystyle A} is a division ring.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Left and right multiplication by a nonzero element are injective on the finite ring, hence surjective and give inverses. Associative rings and invertibility are standard introductory abstract algebra.
