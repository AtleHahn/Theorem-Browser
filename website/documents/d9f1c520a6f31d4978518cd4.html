Statement

Let g(z)=\sum_{m\ge1}a_mz^m/m! enumerate positive-size objects whose internal nodes are labelled 1,\ldots,m. Form unordered sets of n such objects by distributing the global labels into nonempty disjoint subsets and relabelling each object in increasing order. Let f_n(z) count these configurations exponentially by total number of labels, and set f_0(z)=1. Then the generating function, exponential in total labels and ordinary in number of objects, is F(z,t)=\sum_{n\ge0}f_n(z)t^n=\exp(tg(z)).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Undergraduate combinatorics supplies labeled finite objects, permutations and orbit counts. An exponential generating function uses coefficients divided by factorials; this short definition lets the displayed product and exponential identities be read as counting formulas.

Proof 1: Specialize to the symmetric group and sum the exponential series

The labelled enumeration theorem says that the exponential generating function for relabelled configurations in n slots modulo G\le S_n is g(z)^n/|G|. For unordered sets the slot group is S_n, of order n!, so f_n(z)=g(z)^n/n!. Including the empty configuration,

F(z,t)=\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{(tg(z))^n}{n!}=\exp(tg(z)).

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Specialize the slot action to the symmetric group and sum its factorial-weighted powers. Undergraduate combinatorics and formal power series suffice; the absence of zero-size objects makes every coefficient computation finite.
