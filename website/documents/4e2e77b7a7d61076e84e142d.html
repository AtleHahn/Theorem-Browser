Statement

For the closed orientable surface S of genus n\ge1,

\pi_1(S)\cong\langle A_1,B_1,\ldots,A_n,B_n\mid\prod_{i=1}^n A_iB_iA_i^{-1}B_i^{-1}=1\rangle.

Proof 1: Polygon, puncture and boundary relation — outline

Use the Seifert–Van Kampen theorem: if X=U\cup V, with U,V open and path connected and U\cap V nonempty and path connected, then at a basepoint in the intersection, \pi_1(X) is the pushout of \pi_1(U)\leftarrow\pi_1(U\cap V)\to\pi_1(V).

One can construct the genus-n orientable surface S using its standard fundamental polygon. For the first open set A, pick a disk within the center of the polygon. Pick B to be the complement in S of the center point of A. Then the intersection of A and B is a punctured disk, which is homotopy equivalent to (and so has the same fundamental group as) a circle. Then \pi_1(A\cap B)\cong\pi_1(S^1), which is the integers, and \pi_1(A)=\{1\}. Thus the homomorphism induced by inclusion from \pi_1(A\cap B) to \pi_1(A) sends any generator to the trivial element. However, the homomorphism induced by inclusion from \pi_1(A\cap B) to \pi_1(B) is not trivial. In order to understand this, first one must calculate \pi_1(B). This is easily done as one can deformation retract B (which is S with one point deleted) onto the edges labeled by

A_1B_1A_1^{-1}B_1^{-1}A_2B_2A_2^{-1}B_2^{-1}\cdots A_nB_nA_n^{-1}B_n^{-1}.

This space is known to be the wedge sum of 2n circles (also called a bouquet of circles), which further is known to have fundamental group isomorphic to the free group with 2n generators, which in this case can be represented by the edges themselves: \{A_1,B_1,\dots,A_n,B_n\}. The generator of \pi_1(A\cap B) maps to the displayed boundary word. We now have enough information to apply Van Kampen's theorem. The generators are the loops \{A_1,B_1,\dots,A_n,B_n\} (A is simply connected, so it contributes no generators) and there is exactly one relation:

A_1B_1A_1^{-1}B_1^{-1}A_2B_2A_2^{-1}B_2^{-1}\cdots A_nB_nA_n^{-1}B_n^{-1}=1.

Using generators and relations, this group is denoted

\left\langle A_1,B_1,\dots,A_n,B_n\mid A_1B_1A_1^{-1}B_1^{-1}\cdots A_nB_nA_n^{-1}B_n^{-1}=1\right\rangle.
