Medium

Any NP claim has a suitably encoded proof that a randomized verifier can check with a constant number of queries and controlled error. This challenges the idea that reliable verification must inspect an entire proof, while distinguishing probabilistic assurance from a deterministic reading of every step.

Statement

The complexity classes satisfy

\mathsf{NP}=\mathsf{PCP}[O(\log n),O(1)].

Here n is the input length in bits. A language belongs to \mathsf{NP} if its yes-instances have certificates of length polynomial in n whose validity can be checked by a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm, and no certificate is valid for a no-instance.

The class on the right consists of languages with a randomized polynomial-time verifier having access to a binary proof string. The verifier uses at most O(\log n) independent fair random bits and reads at most a constant number of proof bits. Queries are nonadaptive: their locations depend only on the input and random bits, not on answers to other queries. Its guarantees are:

  • For every yes-instance there exists a proof accepted for every random choice.
  • For every no-instance and every purported proof, the probability of acceptance is at most 1/2.

The constants in the bounds depend on the verifier, not on the input. The proof can be chosen to have polynomial length.
