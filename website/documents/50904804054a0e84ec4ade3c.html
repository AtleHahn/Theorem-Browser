Statement

Let K be an algebraically closed field and let P_1,\ldots,P_6 be distinct projective points. Write L_{ij} for the line through P_i,P_j. Set

C_1=L_{12}\cup L_{34}\cup L_{56},\qquad C_2=L_{23}\cup L_{45}\cup L_{61},

and

Q_1=L_{12}\cap L_{45},\quad Q_2=L_{23}\cap L_{56},\quad Q_3=L_{34}\cap L_{61}.

Assume all these lines and intersection points are defined, and C_1\cap C_2 consists of the nine distinct points P_1,\ldots,P_6,Q_1,Q_2,Q_3. Assume P_1,P_3,P_5 lie on a line A, P_2,P_4,P_6 lie on a distinct line B, and none of the Q_i lies on A\cup B. Then Q_1,Q_2,Q_3 are collinear.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Fields and homogeneous coordinates give the projective points and lines; unions of lines are zeros of products of linear forms. These limited extensions of undergraduate algebra suffice for the specified nine-point configuration.

Proof 1: Use the two vertex-lines as the conic — outline

Use the three-line cubics C_1,C_2 defined in the statement with the degenerate conic Q=A\cup B through the six vertices. Let M join Q_1,Q_2. The cubic Q\cup M passes through eight of the nine intersections of C_1,C_2, so Cayley–Bacharach places Q_3 on it. As Q_3\notin Q, it lies on M.

Level justification

Regard each union of lines as the zero set of the product of their linear equations and apply the fully stated nine-point cubic theorem. Undergraduate fields, homogeneous coordinates and polynomial degree suffice with basic projective curves as a small related extension; no intersection multiplicities or scheme theory enter.
