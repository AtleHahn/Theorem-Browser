Statement

Let (A_i)_{i\in I} be a finite indexed family of sets, allowing repetitions and allowing individual sets to be infinite. There exist pairwise distinct representatives a_i\in A_i for all i\in I if and only if, for every J\subseteq I, the union \bigcup_{i\in J}A_i contains at least |J| distinct elements. Equivalently, there is an injective map f:I\to\bigcup_{i\in I}A_i with f(i)\in A_i exactly when

\left|\bigcup_{i\in J} A_i\right|\ge |J|\qquad(J\subseteq I).
Level
High school only
Level justification

Only elementary set membership and finite counting are required. Even infinite member sets are tested by containing a specified finite number of distinct elements; no infinite-cardinal arithmetic is needed.

Proof 1: Finite exchange chains and forced representatives (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorem1 and preceding lemma, pp.26–29).

The source explicitly allows infinite member sets and repeated sets, treating their positions as distinct indices. Its induction only changes finitely many representatives, so it requires no finiteness of the union. For clarity, the exchange lemma can be read as follows: fix representatives a_i, let R be the elements present in every representative set, and close R under the rule a_i\mapsto A_i. A shortest exchange chain from a new element outside \{a_i\} to R would give a representative system omitting an element of R. Thus the closure stays inside \{a_i\}; its corresponding sets have exactly that many available representatives, forcing the closure to equal R. The empty indexed family has the empty representative map.
