Statement

For every positive integer d there is a finite set Y_d of primes such that, whenever p\notin Y_d, every homogeneous polynomial of degree d over the p-adic field \mathbb Q_p in at least d^2+1 variables has a nonzero zero vector.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement involves p-adic fields and homogeneous forms over them, beyond calculus and real/complex linear algebra.

Proof 1: Transfer from Laurent-series fields through ultraproducts (outline)

Dependencies

Use Serge Lang's theorem, stating that the analogous theorem is true for the field Fp((t)) of formal Laurent series over a finite field Fp with {\displaystyle Y_{d}=\varnothing }. In other words, every homogeneous polynomial of degree d with more than d2 variables has a non-trivial zero (so Fp((t)) is a C2 field).

Use the following valued-field elementary-equivalence theorem: if two Henselian valued fields have equivalent valuation groups and residue fields, and the residue fields have characteristic 0, then they are elementarily equivalent (which means that a first-order sentence is true for one if and only if it is true for the other).

Ultraproduct transfer

Use the same arbitrary nonprincipal ultrafilter on the primes in both ultraproducts.

Next one applies this to two fields, one given by an ultraproduct over all primes of the fields Fp((t)) and the other given by an ultraproduct over all primes of the p-adic fields Qp. Both residue fields are given by an ultraproduct over the fields Fp, so are isomorphic and have characteristic 0, and both value groups are the same, so the ultraproducts are elementarily equivalent. (Taking ultraproducts is used to force the residue field to have characteristic 0; the residue fields of Fp((t)) and Qp both have non-zero characteristic p.)

The elementary equivalence of these ultraproducts implies that for any sentence in the language of valued fields, there is a finite set Y of exceptional primes, such that for any p not in this set the sentence is true for Fp((t)) if and only if it is true for the field of p-adic numbers. Applying this to the sentence stating that every non-constant homogeneous polynomial of degree d in d2+1 variables has a nontrivial zero, and using Lang's theorem, one gets the Ax–Kochen theorem.

For more variables, restrict to d^2+1 variables by setting the others to zero.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof combines finite-field Laurent series, p-adic and Henselian valued fields, residue fields and value groups, elementary equivalence and ultraproduct transfer. This cumulative algebraic and model-theoretic preparation is well beyond the undergraduate allowance. The final fixed-degree polynomial sentence and extra-variable restriction are elementary compared with that framework. The arbitrariness of the nonprincipal ultrafilter is essential to the finite-exception conclusion.

Theorem

Statement used:

Application in proof:

Statement used:

For fixed positive integers d and r, the claim that every homogeneous degree-d form in r variables has a nonzero zero is a first-order field sentence: quantify over the finitely many coefficients, then over r coordinates, and assert a nonzero coordinate and vanishing of the finite polynomial sum.

Application in proof:
