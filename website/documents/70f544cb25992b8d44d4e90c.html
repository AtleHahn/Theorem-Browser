Statement

Let (X_t,\mathcal F_t)_{t=0}^n be a real-valued submartingale, n\ge1. Suppose real \mathcal F_{t-1}-measurable random variables A_t,B_t and deterministic constants 0<c_t<\infty satisfy

A_t\le X_t-X_{t-1}\le B_t,\qquad B_t-A_t\le c_t\quad\text{almost surely},\quad 1\le t\le n.

Then, for every \epsilon>0,

\mathbb P(X_n-X_0\le-\epsilon)\le \exp\left(-\frac{2\epsilon^2}{\sum_{t=1}^nc_t^2}\right).
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The hypotheses require conditional expectation, filtrations and predictable processes in general probability theory.

Proof 1: Reverse the sign of the process

Upper-tail Azuma inequality (dependency)

Let (X_t,\mathcal F_t)_{t=0}^n be a real-valued supermartingale, n\ge1. Suppose real \mathcal F_{t-1}-measurable random variables A_t,B_t and deterministic constants 0<c_t<\infty satisfy

A_t\le X_t-X_{t-1}\le B_t,\qquad B_t-A_t\le c_t\quad\text{almost surely},\quad 1\le t\le n.

Then, for every \epsilon>0,

\mathbb P(X_n-X_0\ge\epsilon)\le \exp\left(-\frac{2\epsilon^2}{\sum_{t=1}^nc_t^2}\right).

Argument

Since a submartingale is a supermartingale with signs reversed, apply the upper-tail inequality to -X_t, whose increment interval is [-B_t,-A_t] and has the same width. We have

\mathbb P(X_n-X_0\le-\epsilon)\le \exp\left(-\frac{2\epsilon^2}{\sum_{t=1}^nc_t^2}\right).
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Negation changes a submartingale into a supermartingale and reverses its increment endpoints without changing interval widths. The manipulation is simple, but verifying its general hypotheses requires measure-theoretic conditional expectation, adaptedness and predictability. Unlike the finite fair-coin example, the statement is not restricted to a finite probability tree; this framework exceeds the short undergraduate extension.
