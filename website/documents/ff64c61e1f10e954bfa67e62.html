Statement

Let p_j be the jth prime and P_k=\prod_{j=1}^k p_j its primorial. If d(n) counts positive divisors, then \lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\log d(P_k)}{\log P_k/\log\log P_k}=\log2.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Prime products; Divisor counts; Logarithms; Limits of real sequences. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Substitute the primorial estimate — outline

Use the primorial estimate P_k=\exp((1+o(1))k\log k). Since the prime factors of P_k each have exponent one, its divisor count is 2^k. These expressions in the displayed ratio give the limit \log2.
