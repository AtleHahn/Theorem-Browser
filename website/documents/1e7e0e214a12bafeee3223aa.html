Statement

In a Euclidean triangle with side lengths a,b,c, an interior cevian of length d meets the side of length a in segments of lengths m,n, where m is adjacent to side c and n to side b. Then a=m+n and b^2m+c^2n=a(d^2+mn).

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement is an elementary Euclidean length identity, included in rubric1.1 high-school geometry.

Proof 1: Cancellation in two cosine-law equations

The theorem can be proved as an application of the law of cosines.

Let θ be the angle between m and d and θ' the angle between n and d. Then θ' is the supplement of θ, and so cos θ' = −cos θ. Applying the law of cosines in the two small triangles using angles θ and θ' produces

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}c^{2}&=m^{2}+d^{2}-2dm\cos \theta ,\\b^{2}&=n^{2}+d^{2}-2dn\cos \theta '\\&=n^{2}+d^{2}+2dn\cos \theta .\end{aligned}}}

Multiplying the first equation by n and the third equation by m and adding them eliminates cos θ. One obtains

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}b^{2}m+c^{2}n&=nm^{2}+n^{2}m+(m+n)d^{2}\\&=(m+n)(mn+d^{2})\\&=a(mn+d^{2}),\\\end{aligned}}}

which is the required equation.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Multiply and add two cosine-law equations so the supplementary-angle terms cancel. School triangle trigonometry and algebra suffice.

Proof 2: Altitude and Pythagorean expansions — outline

The theorem can be proved by drawing a perpendicular from the vertex of the triangle to the line of the base and using the Pythagorean theorem to write the distances b, c, d in terms of the altitude. The left and right hand sides of the equation then reduce algebraically to the same expression.
