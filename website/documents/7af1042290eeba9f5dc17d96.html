Likely alternative prerequisite level: Undergraduate level; lowest recorded proof level: Beyond undergraduate level. The statement already supplies a power series. For r<R, choose a larger radius still below R to get uniform absolute convergence on |z|=r. Integrate f(r exp(it))exp(-int) over 0<=t<=2pi and use elementary exponential orthogonality: the integral is 2pi a_n r^n. Taking moduli gives the stated estimate for every n>=0. Only real integration and power-series convergence are needed, without Cauchy's contour theorem.

Statement

Let f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_nz^n be holomorphic for |z|<R. For 0<r<R and every integer n\ge0, |a_n|\le r^{-n}\sup_{|z|=r}|f(z)|.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex differentiability and entire functions add one direct extension of calculus; power-series coefficients, derivatives, absolute values and growth bounds are familiar analysis. The explicit inequalities require no specialist complex-analytic constructions to understand.

Proof 1: Bound the coefficient integral by circle length

Let C be the positively oriented circle |z|=r. The coefficient form of Cauchy's integral formula gives

{\displaystyle |a_{n}|={\bigg |}\underbrace {{\frac {1}{2\pi i}}\int _{C}{\frac {f(z)}{z^{n+1}}}\,dz\,} _{=a_{n}}{\bigg |}\leq r^{-n}\sup _{|z|=r}|f(z)|.}
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

This application requires holomorphic power-series coefficients and their recovery by Cauchy contour integration. The final length estimate is simple, but the linked complex-analytic framework exceeds the ordinary calculus/real-analysis baseline.
