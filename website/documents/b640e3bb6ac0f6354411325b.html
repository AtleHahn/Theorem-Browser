Statement

Let Q_1,\ldots,Q_N,q,k be real constants and p_1,\ldots,p_N\in\mathbb R^3. On D=\mathbb R^3\setminus\{p_1,\ldots,p_N\} define F(r)=kq\sum_{i=1}^N Q_i\frac{r-p_i}{|r-p_i|^3}. For an oriented differentiable curve \gamma\subset D from a to b assume each ordinary integral \int_\gamma (r-p_i)|r-p_i|^{-3}\cdot dr exists, \int_\gamma F(r)\cdot dr=kq\sum_{i=1}^NQ_i\left(\frac1{|a-p_i|}-\frac1{|b-p_i|}\right).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Euclidean vectors and distances; Finite sums; Parametrized curves and line integrals. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Sum reciprocal-distance potentials

Let γ ⊂ R3 − {p1, ..., pN} be an arbitrary differentiable curve from a to b. Then the work done on the particle is

{\displaystyle W=\int _{\gamma }\mathbf {F} (\mathbf {r} )\cdot \mathrm {d} \mathbf {r} =\int _{\gamma }\left(kq\sum _{i=1}^{N}{\frac {Q_{i}(\mathbf {r} -\mathbf {p} _{i})}{\left|\mathbf {r} -\mathbf {p} _{i}\right|^{3}}}\right)\cdot \mathrm {d} \mathbf {r} =kq\sum _{i=1}^{N}\left(Q_{i}\int _{\gamma }{\frac {\mathbf {r} -\mathbf {p} _{i}}{\left|\mathbf {r} -\mathbf {p} _{i}\right|^{3}}}\cdot \mathrm {d} \mathbf {r} \right)}

Now for each i, direct computation shows that

{\displaystyle {\frac {\mathbf {r} -\mathbf {p} _{i}}{\left|\mathbf {r} -\mathbf {p} _{i}\right|^{3}}}=-\nabla {\frac {1}{\left|\mathbf {r} -\mathbf {p} _{i}\right|}}.}

Thus, continuing from above and using the gradient theorem,

{\displaystyle W=-kq\sum _{i=1}^{N}\left(Q_{i}\int _{\gamma }\nabla {\frac {1}{\left|\mathbf {r} -\mathbf {p} _{i}\right|}}\cdot \mathrm {d} \mathbf {r} \right)=kq\sum _{i=1}^{N}Q_{i}\left({\frac {1}{\left|\mathbf {a} -\mathbf {p} _{i}\right|}}-{\frac {1}{\left|\mathbf {b} -\mathbf {p} _{i}\right|}}\right)}

Level justification

Differentiate each reciprocal-distance potential and apply the gradient theorem to each term of the finite sum. This is standard vector calculus.
