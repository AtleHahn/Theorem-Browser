Proof 1: The eta series at two is a logarithmic integral

The interchange is absolutely convergent because \sum_n\int_0^1|(-1)^{n-1}x^{n-1}/n|\,dx=\sum_n1/n^2<\infty.

{\displaystyle \eta (2)=\sum _{n=1}^{\infty }(-1)^{n-1}{\frac {1}{n^{2}}}=\sum _{n=1}^{\infty }\int _{0}^{1}(-1)^{n-1}{\frac {1}{n}}{x}^{n-1}\,\mathrm {d} x=\int _{0}^{1}\sum _{n=1}^{\infty }(-1)^{n-1}{\frac {1}{n}}{x}^{n-1}\,\mathrm {d} x=\int _{0}^{1}{\frac {\ln(x+1)}{x}}\,\mathrm {d} x}
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The explicit sum of integral absolute values is the convergent reciprocal-square series. Apply the corresponding termwise integration criterion and the logarithm power series. Calculus and introductory series analysis suffice.
