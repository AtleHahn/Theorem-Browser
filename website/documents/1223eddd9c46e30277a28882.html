Statement

For real x and integer n\ge0, \cos(nx)=\sum_{0\le k\le n,\ k\text{ even}}(-1)^{k/2}\binom nk\cos^{n-k}x\sin^kx and \sin(nx)=\sum_{0\le k\le n,\ k\text{ odd}}(-1)^{(k-1)/2}\binom nk\cos^{n-k}x\sin^kx. For integers n\ge0 and 0\le k\le n, write \binom nk=n!/(k!(n-k)!), with 0!=1.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The integer multiple-angle formulas concern real trigonometric functions and elementary algebra with finite even/odd index sums.

Proof 1: Separate real and imaginary parts

For the complex numbers the binomial theorem can be combined with de Moivre's formula to yield multiple-angle formulas for the sine and cosine. According to De Moivre's formula,

{\displaystyle \cos \left(nx\right)+i\sin \left(nx\right)=\left(\cos x+i\sin x\right)^{n}.}

Using the binomial theorem, the expression on the right can be expanded, and then the real and imaginary parts can be taken to yield formulas for cos(nx) and sin(nx). In general,

{\displaystyle \cos(nx)=\sum _{k{\text{ even}}}(-1)^{k/2}{\binom {n}{k}}\cos ^{n-k}x\sin ^{k}x}

and

{\displaystyle \sin(nx)=\sum _{k{\text{ odd}}}(-1)^{(k-1)/2}{\binom {n}{k}}\cos ^{n-k}x\sin ^{k}x.}

The sums range over 0\le k\le n with the indicated parity.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Expand the finite nth power, use the four-cycle of powers of i, and collect even and odd indices as real and imaginary parts. The supplied de Moivre formula identifies these parts with the desired trigonometric functions. Elementary algebra, complex arithmetic and trigonometry suffice.
