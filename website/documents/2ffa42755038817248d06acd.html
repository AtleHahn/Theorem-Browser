Philosophical significance

Every positive-density subset of the integers contains arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions. Such patterns can be forced by size alone, so their presence does not necessarily indicate design or a special causal mechanism.

Statement

For every integer k\ge1 and real \delta\in(0,1] there exists an integer N\ge1 such that every A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\} with |A|\ge\delta N contains a,a+d,\ldots,a+(k-1)d for integers a,d\ge1. Equivalently, every A\subseteq\mathbb N of positive upper density contains such an arithmetic progression for every k. Positive upper density means there is \varepsilon>0 for which |A\cap\{1,\ldots,N\}|\ge\varepsilon N for infinitely many integers N.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Density is expressed through elementary counts and infinitely many integers, without requiring advanced analysis.

Level justification

The linked progression encoding invokes hypergraph removal. Its finite proof uses conditional averages on finite partitions, orthogonal projection identities, Cauchy–Schwarz, bounded energy increments and nested asymptotic parameter choices. This cumulative linear-algebra and real-analysis machinery is undergraduate; finite sigma-algebra terminology denotes partitions and does not require measure-theoretic probability.
