Statement

For complex z and real θ, with J_n the Bessel function of the first kind, e^{iz\cos\theta}=J_0(z)+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}i^nJ_n(z)\cos(n\theta).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The Bessel functions of integer order can be introduced by their explicit factorial power series. Together with familiar complex exponentials and convergence, this single special-function definition suffices for the displayed expansion without differential-equation theory.

Proof 1: Pair positive and negative orders

Use the imported cosine exponential expansion e^{iz\cos\theta}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}i^nJ_n(z)e^{in\theta} and pair the n and −n terms. For n≥1, i^{-n}J_{-n}(z)e^{-in\theta}=i^nJ_n(z)e^{-in\theta} by J_{-n}(z)=(-1)^nJ_n(z). The two terms sum to 2i^nJ_n(z)\cos(n\theta); the unpaired term is J_0(z).

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Pair positive and negative Bessel orders using the supplied symmetry and absolute convergence. The rearrangement is undergraduate analysis, but the companion does not define the complex Bessel family or establish its convergence setting; without a confident small-background justification retain beyond undergraduate provisionally.
