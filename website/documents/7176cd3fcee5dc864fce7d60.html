Statement

Let S be a semigroup, a set with an associative operation xy; a monoid is a semigroup with a two-sided identity. A homomorphism satisfies f(xy)=f(x)f(y). A congruence \sim is an equivalence relation with x\sim y,u\sim v\Rightarrow xu\sim yv. If f:S\to L is a surjective homomorphism to a semilattice and x\sim y means f(x)=f(y), then S/{\sim} is isomorphic to L.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Semigroups and monoids are direct variants of group axioms, and their definitions are supplied. Congruences, fibers and quotients use familiar equivalence relations and multiplication compatibility, so no specialist semigroup theory is required.

Proof 1: Identify the quotient with the surjective image

Use the equivalence relation x\sim y exactly when f(x)=f(y). It is the kernel congruence. The first isomorphism theorem identifies the quotient by this relation with the image, and surjectivity makes that image L.

Level justification

Identify equality-of-images classes with the image using the supplied first isomorphism theorem. Undergraduate quotient algebra suffices; semilattice structure introduces no additional argument.
