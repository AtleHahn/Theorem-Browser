Statement

A group is cyclic if it is generated by one element. Every subgroup of a cyclic group is cyclic.

If a cyclic group G has finite order n\ge1, every subgroup has order dividing n, and for each positive divisor d\mid n there is exactly one subgroup of order d.

An infinite cyclic group is isomorphic to the additive group \mathbb Z. Its subgroups are precisely d\mathbb Z=\{dk:k\in\mathbb Z\} for integers d\ge0. The case d=0 is the trivial subgroup; every other subgroup is infinite cyclic.
