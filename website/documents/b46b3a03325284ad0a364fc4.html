Statement

Let P,Q be probability measures on a measurable space (X,\Sigma). Put \delta(P,Q)=\sup_{A\in\Sigma}|P(A)-Q(A)|. Let D_{\rm KL}(P\Vert Q)=\int\log(dP/dQ)\,dP if P\ll Q, with natural logarithms and the usual zero-mass convention, and let it be +\infty otherwise. Then \delta(P,Q)\le\sqrt{D_{\rm KL}(P\Vert Q)/2}. Equivalently, the total variation norm V=2\delta satisfies D_{\rm KL}\ge V^2/2.

Proof 1: Density ratio and weighted Cauchy–Schwarz — outline

For the density ratio in the source, write 1+r=dP/dQ when P\ll Q, so r\ge-1, E_Q r=0 and E_Q|r|=V. The non-absolutely-continuous case has infinite divergence and is immediate. The source uses the scalar logarithmic bound

(1+r)\log(1+r)-r\ge \frac{r^2}{2(1+r/3)}\qquad(r\ge-1),

with the limiting convention at r=-1, without deriving that bound. Its calculation is:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}D_{KL}(P\parallel Q)&=E_{Q}[(1+r(x))\log(1+r(x))-r(x)]\\&\geq {\frac {1}{2}}E_{Q}\left[{\frac {r(x)^{2}}{1+r(x)/3}}\right]\\&\geq {\frac {1}{2}}{\frac {E_{Q}[|r(x)|]^{2}}{E_{Q}[1+r(x)/3]}}&{\text{(from Titu's lemma)}}\\&={\frac {1}{2}}E_{Q}[|r(x)|]^{2}&{\text{(As }}E_{Q}[1+r(x)/3]=1{\text{ )}}\\&={\frac {1}{2}}V^{2}.\end{aligned}}}

Here Titu's lemma is also known as Sedrakyan's inequality.
