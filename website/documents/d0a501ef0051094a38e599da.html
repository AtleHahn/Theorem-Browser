Statement

Let A be a commutative ring, I a nilpotent ideal and M an A-module. Put A_0=A/I, M_0=M/IM, \operatorname{gr}_I A=\bigoplus_{i\ge0}I^i/I^{i+1} and \operatorname{gr}_I M=\bigoplus_{i\ge0}I^iM/I^{i+1}M. Then M is flat over A if and only if M_0 is flat over A_0 and the natural map \operatorname{gr}_I A\otimes_{A_0}M_0\to\operatorname{gr}_I M is an isomorphism.

Proof 1: Descending Tor kernels in the graded diagram — outline

If M is flat, then M_0 is A_0-flat by base change, and \operatorname{Tor}_1^A(A/I^n,M)=0 for every n>0. This implies that I^n\otimes_A M\simeq I^nM and so, since tensor product commutes with base extension,

\begin{aligned} \operatorname{gr}_I(A)\otimes_{A_0}M_0 &\simeq\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}(I^n)_0\otimes_{A_0}M_0\\ &\simeq\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}(I^n\otimes_A M)_0\\ &\simeq\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}(I^nM)_0 =\operatorname{gr}_I M, \end{aligned}

where N_0=N/IN for an A-module N.

Conversely, suppose M_0 is A_0-flat and the natural graded map is an isomorphism. Let \alpha_i denote the exact sequence

0\to\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(A/I^i,M) \to I^i\otimes_A M\to I^iM\to0

and let

\gamma_i:\quad 0\to0\to(I^i/I^{i+1})\otimes_A M \xrightarrow{\simeq}I^iM/I^{i+1}M\to0.

Consider the natural sequence of complexes

\alpha_{i+1}\to\alpha_i\to\gamma_i.

The graded isomorphism implies that the map

\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(A/I^{i+1},M) \longrightarrow\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(A/I^i,M)

is surjective: a kernel element in I^i\otimes_A M lifts to I^{i+1}\otimes_A M by right exactness, and its image in I^{i+1}M is zero since I^{i+1}M\hookrightarrow I^iM. Thus \operatorname{Tor}_1^A(A/I^i,M)=0 for i>0 (it is so for large i, and then use descending induction).

Use the Tor base-change lemma: if M_0 is A_0-flat and \operatorname{Tor}_1^A(A/I,M)=0, then \operatorname{Tor}_1^A(X,M)=0 for every A-module X killed by some power of I. Since I is nilpotent, this applies to every X, and the Tor criterion for flatness implies that M is flat.
