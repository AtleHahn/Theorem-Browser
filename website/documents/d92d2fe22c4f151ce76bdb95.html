Statement

Let f(T)=\sum_{k\ge k_0}c_kT^{k/n} with complex coefficients and n\ge1. For t\ne0 choose x with x^n=t, and evaluate as \sum c_kx^k. There is a radius r\in[0,\infty] such that this converges for 0<|t|<r and diverges for |t|>r.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Complex power series; Laurent series; Radius of convergence. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. Proof 1: Reduction to an ordinary power series

Factor out x^{k_0}. The remaining series \sum_{k\ge k_0}c_kx^{k-k_0} is a power series with radius R\in[0,\infty]. Multiplication by the nonzero factor x^{k_0} does not change convergence. Since |x|=|t|^{1/n}, the required radius is r=R^n. No assertion at the boundary is needed.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Choose an nth root variable, remove the finite initial Laurent factor and apply the ordinary power-series radius theorem. Undergraduate complex series suffice; fractional exponents are explicitly interpreted through that variable.
