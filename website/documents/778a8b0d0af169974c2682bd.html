Binomial family is an exponential family

Statement

Fix an integer n\ge1 and 0<p<1. The masses f(x)=\binom nx p^x(1-p)^{n-x}, x=0,\ldots,n, have exponential-family form \binom nx\exp(x\eta-A) with \eta=\log(p/(1-p)) and A=-n\log(1-p).

Level
High school only
Level justification

The binomial masses and the target exponential-family expression are both supplied, as are eta and A. The complete assertion is equality of these formulas for 0<p<1 and integer x in the stated finite range. Binomial coefficients, positive powers and logarithm laws are high-school mathematics; no inference or general statistical-family theory is needed.

Proof 1: Rewrite the powers using the logit parameter

As an example of a discrete exponential family, consider the binomial distribution with known number of trials n. The probability mass function for this distribution is

{\displaystyle f(x)={\binom {n}{x}}p^{x}{\left(1-p\right)}^{n-x},\quad x\in \{0,1,2,\ldots ,n\}.}

This can equivalently be written as

{\displaystyle f(x)={\binom {n}{x}}\exp \left[x\log \left({\frac {p}{1-p}}\right)+n\log(1-p)\right],}

which shows that the binomial distribution is an exponential family, whose natural parameter is

{\displaystyle \eta =\log {\frac {p}{1-p}}.}

This function of p is known as logit.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The exponential-family form is specified explicitly. Rewriting the two positive powers with logarithms gives the required eta and A by elementary logarithm laws. No statistical inference or general theory of exponential families is needed.
