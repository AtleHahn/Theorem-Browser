Statement

A positive integer N is a congruum if N=y^2-x^2=z^2-y^2 for integers x,y,z. For positive integers m>n, put N=4mn(m^2-n^2) and Y=(m^2+n^2)^2. Then Y-N,Y,Y+N are three integer squares in arithmetic progression, so N is a congruum.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement specifies integer polynomial expressions and squares in an arithmetic progression.

Proof 1: Choosing the middle square and spacing — outline

The congruum problem may be solved by choosing two distinct positive integers m and n (with m>n); then the number 4mn(m^2-n^2) is a congruum. The middle square of the associated arithmetic progression of squares is (m^2+n^2)^2, and the other two squares may be found by adding or subtracting the congruum.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The outer squares are (m^2-n^2-2mn)^2 and (m^2-n^2+2mn)^2. Expanding them gives Y minus or plus N, so the outlined construction needs only polynomial arithmetic.
