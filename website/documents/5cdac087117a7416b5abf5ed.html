Statement

Fix 0<T<\infty and a filtered probability space satisfying the usual conditions, with a P-Brownian motion W. Let Y be an admissible Itô integrand on [0,T], so X_t=\int_0^tY_s\,dW_s is a continuous local martingale, and suppose Z_t=\exp(X_t-\tfrac12\int_0^tY_s^2\,ds) is a true martingale. Define Q on \mathcal F_T by dQ/dP=Z_T. Then \widetilde W_t=W_t-\int_0^tY_s\,ds is a Q-Brownian motion on [0,T].

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Brownian motion; Stochastic integrals; Local martingales; Change of probability measure. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Combine the source stochastic-integral case with Lévy characterization — outline

Use the continuous-local-martingale form of Girsanov’s theorem: with density \mathcal E(X), a P local martingale M becomes M-[M,X] under Q. In particular, W-[W,X] is a Q local martingale. The Itô covariation identity gives [W,X]_t=\int_0^tY_s\,ds, a continuous finite-variation process. Thus \widetilde W is continuous, starts at zero and has quadratic variation [\widetilde W]_t=[W]_t=t. Lévy’s characterization makes it a Q-Brownian motion.
