Statement

Let A be a commutative ring, I an ideal and M an A-module. Assume either I is nilpotent, or A is Noetherian and \bigcap_{r\ge1}I^r(\mathfrak a\otimes_A M)=0 for every ideal \mathfrak a\subseteq A. Set A_n=A/I^{n+1} and M_n=M/I^{n+1}M for n\ge0. The following are equivalent: (1) M is A-flat; (2) M_0 is A_0-flat and \operatorname{Tor}_1^A(A/I,M)=0; (3) every M_n is A_n-flat; (4) M_0 is A_0-flat and \operatorname{gr}_I A\otimes_{A_0}M_0\to\operatorname{gr}_I M is an isomorphism. Here \operatorname{gr}_I A=\bigoplus_{j\ge0}I^j/I^{j+1} and \operatorname{gr}_I M=\bigoplus_{j\ge0}I^jM/I^{j+1}M, and the map is induced by multiplication.

Proof 1: Tor, graded reduction and separated kernel cycle — outline

Use the Tor base-change lemma: if M/IM is flat over A/I and \operatorname{Tor}_1^A(A/I,M)=0, then \operatorname{Tor}_1^A(X,M)=0 for every X killed by a power of I. We also use the ideal tensor criterion: M is flat if and only if \mathfrak a\otimes_A M\to M is injective for every ideal \mathfrak a.

The proof uses the cycle of implications {\displaystyle 1.\Rightarrow 4.\Rightarrow 3.\Rightarrow 2.\Rightarrow 1.}

{\displaystyle 2.\Rightarrow 1.}: If {\displaystyle I} is nilpotent, then, by the Tor base-change lemma stated above, {\displaystyle \operatorname {Tor} _{1}^{A}(-,M)=0} and {\displaystyle M} is flat over {\displaystyle A}. Thus, assume that A is Noetherian and M is idealwise separated for I. Let {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {a}}\subset A} be an ideal and we shall show {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {a}}\otimes M\to M} is injective. For an integer {\displaystyle k>0}, consider the exact sequence

{\displaystyle 0\to {\mathfrak {a}}/(I^{k}\cap {\mathfrak {a}})\to A/I^{k}\to A/({\mathfrak {a}}+I^{k})\to 0.}

Since {\displaystyle \operatorname {Tor} _{1}^{A}(A/({\mathfrak {a}}+I^{k}),M)=0} by the Tor base-change lemma stated above (note {\displaystyle I^{k}} kills {\displaystyle A/({\mathfrak {a}}+I^{k})}), tensoring the above with {\displaystyle M}, we get:

{\displaystyle 0\to {\mathfrak {a}}/(I^{k}\cap {\mathfrak {a}})\otimes M\to A/I^{k}\otimes M=M/I^{k}M}.

Tensoring {\displaystyle M} with {\displaystyle 0\to I^{k}\cap {\mathfrak {a}}\to {\mathfrak {a}}\to {\mathfrak {a}}/(I^{k}\cap {\mathfrak {a}})\to 0}, we also have:

{\displaystyle (I^{k}\cap {\mathfrak {a}})\otimes M{\overset {f}{\to }}{\mathfrak {a}}\otimes M{\overset {g}{\to }}{\mathfrak {a}}/(I^{k}\cap {\mathfrak {a}})\otimes M\to 0.}

We combine the two to get the exact sequence:

{\displaystyle (I^{k}\cap {\mathfrak {a}})\otimes M{\overset {f}{\to }}{\mathfrak {a}}\otimes M{\overset {g'}{\to }}M/I^{k}M.}

Now, if {\displaystyle x} is in the kernel of {\displaystyle {\mathfrak {a}}\otimes M\to M}, then, a fortiori, {\displaystyle x} is in {\displaystyle \operatorname {ker} (g')=\operatorname {im} (f)}. By the Artin–Rees lemma, given {\displaystyle n>0}, we can find {\displaystyle k>0} such that {\displaystyle I^{k}\cap {\mathfrak {a}}\subset I^{n}{\mathfrak {a}}}. Since {\displaystyle \cap _{n\geq 1}I^{n}({\mathfrak {a}}\otimes M)=0}, we conclude {\displaystyle x=0}.

For (1)\Rightarrow(4), use base-change flatness and tensor exactness: I^j\otimes_A M\simeq I^jM for all j, and reduction modulo I gives the graded isomorphism. For (4)\Rightarrow(3), apply the nilpotent graded flatness criterion: a module N over a ring B with nilpotent ideal J is flat precisely when N/JN is B/J-flat and \operatorname{gr}_J B\otimes_{B/J}(N/JN)\to\operatorname{gr}_J N is an isomorphism. Take B=A_n, N=M_n, and J=I/I^{n+1}; condition (4) descends to this truncated graded map.

For (3)\Rightarrow(2), n=0 already gives flatness of M_0. The Tor exact sequence identifies \operatorname{Tor}_1^A(A/I,M) with the kernel of I\otimes_A M\to M. Let x be in this kernel. For k\ge1, flatness of M/I^kM over A/I^k makes (I/I^k)\otimes_{A/I^k}(M/I^kM)\longrightarrow M/I^kM injective. Tensor right exactness then places x in the image of I^k\otimes_A M\to I\otimes_A M, hence in I^{k-1}(I\otimes_A M). For all k this forces x=0, by nilpotence or idealwise separation for the ideal I. This proves (2).
