Statement

Let S be the class of holomorphic injective functions f(z)=z+\sum_{n\ge2}a_nz^n on the open complex unit disk. For every integer n\ge2, \max_{f\in S}|a_n|=n, attained by the Koebe function K(z)=z/(1-z)^2.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex differentiability is the complex difference-quotient extension of calculus; univalent means injective. Power-series coefficients, disks and derivative scale are familiar analysis concepts. These definitions suffice for the image or coefficient bound without complex-geometric theory.

Proof 1: The Koebe coefficients attain the upper bound

Use the Bieberbach upper bound |a_n|\le n for the class S. The Koebe function belongs to S and has expansion K(z)=\sum_{n\ge1}nz^n. Its nth coefficient therefore equals n, so the bound is attained.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the supplied coefficient bound and differentiate a geometric series to read off the coefficients of the explicit extremizer. Undergraduate complex analysis suffices: univalent means injective, and no theory of extremal conformal maps is developed.
