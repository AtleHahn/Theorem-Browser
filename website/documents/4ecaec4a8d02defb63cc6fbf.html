Statement

Let S=\{z\in\mathbb C:|\operatorname{Im}z|<\pi/2\}. Suppose f is holomorphic in S, continuous on \overline S, satisfies |f|\le1 on \partial S, and |f(z)|<\exp(A\exp(c|\operatorname{Re}z|)) throughout S for real constants c<1 and finite A. Then |f(z)|\le1 throughout S.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex differentiability extends the calculus derivative. Power series, circle parametrization, bounds and convergence are familiar analysis; the hypotheses and formulas are explicit, with no abstract complex geometry or distribution theory.

Proof 1: Exponential damping on expanding rectangles — outline

We choose a positive {\displaystyle b\in(c,1)} and define for each {\displaystyle \epsilon >0} the auxiliary function {\displaystyle h_{\epsilon }} by {\textstyle h_{\epsilon }(z)=e^{-\epsilon (e^{bz}+e^{-bz})}}. Moreover, for a given {\displaystyle a>0}, we define {\displaystyle S_{a}} to be the open rectangle in the complex plane enclosed within the vertices {\displaystyle \{a\pm i\pi /2,-a\pm i\pi /2\}}. Now, fix {\displaystyle \epsilon >0} and consider the function {\displaystyle fh_{\epsilon }}. Because one can show that {\displaystyle |h_{\epsilon }(z)|\leq 1} for all {\displaystyle z\in {\overline {S}}}, it follows that {\displaystyle |f(z)h_{\epsilon }(z)|\leq 1} for {\displaystyle z\in \partial S}. Moreover, one can show for {\displaystyle z\in {\overline {S}}} that {\displaystyle |f(z)h_{\epsilon }(z)|\to 0} uniformly as {\displaystyle |\Re (z)|\to \infty }. This allows us to find an {\displaystyle x_{0}} such that {\displaystyle |f(z)h_{\epsilon }(z)|\leq 1} whenever {\displaystyle z\in {\overline {S}}} and {\displaystyle |\Re (z)|\geq x_{0}}. Now consider the bounded rectangular region {\displaystyle S_{x_{0}}}. We have established that {\displaystyle |f(z)h_{\epsilon }(z)|\leq 1} for all {\displaystyle z\in \partial S_{x_{0}}}. Hence, the maximum modulus principle implies that {\displaystyle |f(z)h_{\epsilon }(z)|\leq 1} for all {\displaystyle z\in {\overline {S_{x_{0}}}}}. Since {\displaystyle |f(z)h_{\epsilon }(z)|\leq 1} also holds whenever {\displaystyle z\in S} and {\displaystyle |\Re (z)|>x_{0}}, we have in fact shown that {\displaystyle |f(z)h_{\epsilon }(z)|\leq 1} holds for all {\displaystyle z\in S}. Finally, because {\displaystyle fh_{\epsilon }\to f} as {\displaystyle \epsilon \to 0}, we conclude that {\displaystyle |f(z)|\leq 1} for all {\displaystyle z\in S}. Q.E.D.
