Statement

Let \mathfrak g be a finite-dimensional real semisimple Lie algebra and let \theta be a Cartan involution. Put \mathfrak k=\ker(\theta-I) and \mathfrak p=\ker(\theta+I), and choose a maximal abelian subspace \mathfrak a\subseteq\mathfrak p. For \lambda\in\mathfrak a^*\setminus\{0\} set

\mathfrak g_\lambda=\{X\in\mathfrak g:[H,X]=\lambda(H)X\text{ for every }H\in\mathfrak a\},

and let \Sigma be those \lambda with \mathfrak g_\lambda\ne0. Set \mathfrak m=\{X\in\mathfrak k:[H,X]=0\text{ for every }H\in\mathfrak a\}. Then the real vector-space direct sum decomposition is

\mathfrak g=\mathfrak m\oplus\mathfrak a\oplus\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Sigma}\mathfrak g_\lambda.

A Lie algebra is semisimple if it has no nonzero solvable ideal; solvable means that repeated commutator subalgebras eventually become zero. A Cartan involution is a Lie algebra automorphism \theta with \theta^2=I for which -B(X,\theta Y) is positive definite, where B(X,Y)=\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{ad}X\operatorname{ad}Y) and \operatorname{ad}X(Y)=[X,Y]. Abelian means brackets between its elements vanish, and maximal is by inclusion among such subspaces of \mathfrak p. The dual \mathfrak a^* consists of real linear functionals. Direct sum means that each element has a unique sum of components in the indicated subspaces.
