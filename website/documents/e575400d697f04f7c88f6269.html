Statement

A graph here is finite, simple and undirected. A cycle has at least three vertices, and cycles are vertex-disjoint when they share no vertices. A feedback vertex set is a set of vertices meeting every cycle, equivalently a set whose deletion leaves an acyclic graph.

There exists a function F:\mathbb N_{>0}\to\mathbb N_{\geq0} such that, for every positive integer k, every graph either contains k vertex-disjoint cycles or has a feedback vertex set of size at most F(k).

Let F_*(k) be the least nonnegative integer bound that works for all graphs with no k vertex-disjoint cycles. Then

F_*(k)=\Theta(k\log k).

Precisely, there are constants c,C>0 and an integer k_0\geq2 such that

c\,k\log k\leq F_*(k)\leq C\,k\log k\qquad(k\geq k_0).

Here \log is the natural logarithm; its base does not affect the assertion. The constants are independent of both k and the graph.

Proof 1: Specialize planar-minor packing and its sharp bound to the triangle

Use the following results stated in the source. For a fixed planar graph H, let \mathcal F(H) consist of graphs containing H as a minor. The Robertson–Seymour planar-minor packing theorem says that there is a function b_H(k) such that every graph either contains k vertex-disjoint subgraphs in \mathcal F(H) or has a vertex set of size at most b_H(k) meeting every such subgraph. The source also states the later sharp bound: when H is planar and is not a forest, the least such bounding function has order \Theta(k\log k).

A graph is planar if it can be drawn in the plane without edge crossings. A minor is obtained by deleting vertices or edges and contracting edges; contracting an edge identifies its two endpoints. A forest is an acyclic graph.

Take H=K_3, the triangle, which is planar and is not a forest. As the source observes, a graph contains K_3 as a minor exactly when it contains a cycle. Consequently, k vertex-disjoint subgraphs in \mathcal F(K_3) exist exactly when k vertex-disjoint cycles exist: select a cycle in each of the disjoint subgraphs, or take the cycles themselves as the subgraphs. A vertex set meeting every member of \mathcal F(K_3) in a graph is exactly a feedback vertex set.

Thus the packing and deletion alternatives coincide with those in the statement, for every graph and every k. Their least bounding functions also coincide. The existence assertion and the sharp \Theta(k\log k) bound therefore follow from the stated planar-minor results.

This is the source’s deduction from external theorems; proofs of those general planar-minor results are not supplied in the article.
