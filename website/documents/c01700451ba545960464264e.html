Statement

The inverse f(y)=\sqrt[3]{y} of g(x)=x^3 is not differentiable at 0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves real derivatives; real cubic and cube-root functions; inverse functions. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. Proof 1: Contradict the inverse identity with the zero cubic derivative — outline

Here f(0)=0 and g^{\prime}(0)=0. If f were differentiable at zero, the differentiable-inverse identity would require f^{\prime}(0)g^{\prime}(0)=1, which is impossible. Equivalently the proposed reciprocal derivative would require 1/g^{\prime}(f(0))=1/0.
