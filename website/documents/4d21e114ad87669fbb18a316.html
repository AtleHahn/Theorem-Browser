Statement

For a finite undirected graph, its edges can be partitioned into simple cycles if and only if every vertex has even degree. A simple cycle has no repeated vertex except its common starting and ending vertex; isolated vertices are allowed.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The finite graph objects and parity condition can be understood through elementary combinatorics.

Proof 1: Split Euler tours into simple cycles (outline)

Use the theorem of Euler that a finite connected graph with at least one edge has an Euler tour (a closed trail that covers each edge exactly once) if and only if every vertex has even degree. A representation of a graph as a union of simple cycles may be obtained from an Euler tour by repeatedly splitting the tour into smaller cycles whenever there is a repeated vertex. Apply this to each component containing an edge; isolated vertices contribute no edges.

Conversely, each cycle incident to a vertex contributes two to its degree. The source leaves this necessity check and the componentwise application implicit.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Use the explicitly stated Euler-tour criterion, split repeated vertices finitely many times and count two incident edges per cycle. Finite graph definitions and parity suffice.
