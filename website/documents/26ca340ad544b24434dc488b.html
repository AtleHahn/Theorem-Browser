Statement

Let X be a smooth connected compact complex curve of genus g, D a divisor and K_X a canonical divisor. Write \ell(D)=\dim_{\mathbb C}H^0(X,\mathcal O(D)). Then \ell(D)-\ell(K_X-D)=\deg D+1-g.

Proof 1: Extract the degree-two characteristic class and apply Serre duality

Write h^i(\mathcal O(D))=\dim_{\mathbb C}H^i(X,\mathcal O(D)) and K=K_X.

For curves, the Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem is essentially the classical Riemann–Roch theorem. To see this, recall that for each divisor D on a curve there is an invertible sheaf O(D) (which corresponds to a line bundle) such that the space L(D) of meromorphic sections is H^0(X,\mathcal O(D)). For curves the Todd class is {\displaystyle 1+c_{1}(T(X))/2,} and the Chern character of a sheaf O(D) is just 1+c1(O(D)), so the Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem states that

{\displaystyle h^{0}({\mathcal {O}}(D))-h^{1}({\mathcal {O}}(D))=c_{1}({\mathcal {O}}(D))+c_{1}(T(X))/2\ \ \ } (integrated over X).

But h0(O(D)) is just l(D), the dimension of H^0(X,\mathcal O(D)), and by Serre duality h1(O(D)) = h0(O(K − D)) = l(K − D) where K is the canonical divisor. Moreover, c1(O(D)) integrated over X is the degree of D, and c1(T(X)) integrated over X is the Euler class 2 − 2g of the curve X, where g is the genus. So we get the classical Riemann Roch theorem

{\displaystyle \ell (D)-\ell (K-D)={\text{deg}}(D)+1-g.}
