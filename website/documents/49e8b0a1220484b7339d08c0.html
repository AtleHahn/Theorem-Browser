A logical consequence can be mediated by a statement using only vocabulary shared by premises and conclusion. This gives a precise sense in which valid inference depends on common conceptual content.

Statement

Let \varphi and \psi be formulas of classical first-order logic, with logical equality and truth and falsity available. If \varphi\models\psi, then there exists a first-order formula \rho such that

\varphi\models\rho\quad\text{and}\quad\rho\models\psi,

and every nonlogical symbol occurring in \rho occurs in both \varphi and \psi.

The nonlogical symbols are the relation, function and constant symbols of the language; logical connectives, quantifiers and equality are not counted among them. Here \alpha\models\beta means that in every structure interpreting the symbols of both formulas, every variable assignment satisfying \alpha also satisfies \beta. The formula \rho is called an interpolant.

Level justification

Proof 1: Common-language consequences and joint consistency (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorem 21.15, p.139; Theorem 21.13 and Lemma 21.14, pp.137–138).

Replace the finitely many free variables in \varphi,\psi by distinct fresh constants. Entailment under all assignments becomes sentence entailment in the expanded languages. Apply Theorem 21.15, rename bound variables to avoid capture, and replace the fresh constants back by their variables. The resulting formula has only common original nonlogical symbols. Use falsity if \varphi is unsatisfiable and truth for an empty conjunction.
