Statement

Let f:X\to Y be a birational projective morphism between Noetherian integral schemes. For every point y\in Y whose local ring \mathcal O_{Y,y} is normal, the fiber

X_y=X\times_Y\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(y)

is connected. Here \kappa(y) is the residue field at y and connected refers to the underlying Zariski topological space. Integral means irreducible and reduced; a normal local ring is an integrally closed domain; a Noetherian scheme admits a finite cover by spectra of Noetherian rings. Birational means inducing an isomorphism of function fields. Projective means factoring as a closed immersion into some projective space over Y, followed by its projection.

In the variety formulation also stated in the source, a proper birational morphism of algebraic varieties has connected fiber over each normal point of the target. Here varieties are integral separated schemes of finite type over a field, and proper means separated, of finite type and universally closed.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Scheme fibers, residue fields, normality, projectivity and properness together require substantial algebraic geometry. A short definition of each does not remove their cumulative conceptual burden.
