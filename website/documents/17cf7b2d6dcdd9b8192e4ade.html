Random isolation connects a solver for uniquely satisfiable formulas to randomized decision of general satisfiability. This makes precise why having a unique answer need not remove computational difficulty and how randomness can transform the search landscape without introducing false acceptance.

Statement

Assume a randomized polynomial-time procedure maps an n-variable Boolean formula F to formulas G_0,\ldots,G_n such that every satisfying assignment of any G_i satisfies F, and, whenever F is satisfiable, with probability at least 1/4 at least one G_i has exactly one satisfying assignment. If a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm accepts formulas with exactly one satisfying assignment and rejects unsatisfiable formulas (with unrestricted behavior otherwise), then SAT has a randomized polynomial-time algorithm that never accepts an unsatisfiable formula and accepts any satisfiable formula with probability at least 1/2. Consequently \mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{RP}.

Level justification

The randomized algorithm and its error promise are specified. NP as polynomial certificates, RP as the given one-sided-error model and polynomial reductions are limited related operational definitions.

Proof 1: Testing all formulas and independent repetition

Assuming Unambiguous-SAT is solvable by a polynomial time algorithm A, we obtain a randomized algorithm for SAT by running A on G_i for each i\le n and accepting if any run accepts. If F is unsatisfiable, then A rejects all G_i as they are unsatisfiable, whereas if F is satisfiable, then A accepts some G_i with probability at least 1/4. We can improve the acceptance probability by repeating the reduction several times: three independent repetitions give success probability at least 1-(3/4)^3>1/2, while rejection on unsatisfiable inputs remains certain.

Thus SAT is in \mathsf{RP}. By the NP-completeness of SAT and the inclusion \mathsf{RP}\subseteq\mathsf{NP}, this gives \mathsf{NP}=\mathsf{RP}.
