Statement

For a complex Hilbert space H, write \mathbf P(H) for its nonzero one-dimensional complex subspaces. For nonzero representatives v,w, put c([v],[w])=|\langle v,w\rangle|/(\|v\|\|w\|). A ray symmetry is a bijection of ray spaces preserving c. Let \dim H\ge2, and let a group G act on \mathbf P(H) by ray symmetries T(g), so T(f)T(g)=T(fg). Choose compatible unitary or antiunitary representatives U(g) supplied by Wigner’s theorem. Then for each f,g\in G there is a scalar \omega(f,g) of modulus one with U(f)U(g)=\omega(f,g)U(fg).

Proof 1: Phase uniqueness for a composite ray map

Use Wigner’s theorem: when \dim H\ge2, compatible unitary or antiunitary representatives exist and are unique up to a scalar of modulus one.

If G acts by symmetries on ray space, and if f, g, h ∈ G with fg = h, then

{\displaystyle T(f)T(g)=T(h),}

where the T are ray transformations. From the uniqueness part of Wigner's theorem, one has for the compatible representatives U,

{\displaystyle U(f)U(g)=\omega (f,g)U(fg)=e^{i\xi (f,g)}U(fg),}

where ω(f, g) is a phase factor.
