Statement

Let N\ge1 be an integer. For m=3 or m=4, let q_{ri}\in\{-1,1\} for 1\le r\le N, 1\le i\le m, and define the temporal correlations by the common-sample averages

C_{ij}=\frac1N\sum_{r=1}^N q_{ri}q_{rj}.

For three times,

C_{12}+C_{23}-C_{13}\le1.

For four times,

C_{12}+C_{23}+C_{34}-C_{14}\le2.

Here q_{ri} is the definite two-state value at time t_i, with t_1<\cdots<t_m, in realization r. In the physical interpretation, the common array assumes that measurements do not change these values or their subsequent evolution.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The complete mathematical assertions involve only finite arrays of signs and arithmetic averages. The physical interpretation is explained locally and adds no quantum-mechanical prerequisites.

Proof 1: Average the pointwise sign bounds — outline

The source writes the three-time expression as

\frac1N\sum_{r=1}^N(q_{r1}q_{r2}+q_{r2}q_{r3}-q_{r1}q_{r3}).

For each realization the expression in parentheses is at most one, so its average is at most one. The source then gives the four-time bound by the same sign-averaging method. It leaves the finite sign checks, including the four-time pointwise bound, implicit; these are routine omissions, not an additional externally supplied proof.
