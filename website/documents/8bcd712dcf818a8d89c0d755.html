Simpler proof assessment

Likely alternative prerequisite level: Undergraduate level; lowest recorded proof level: Beyond undergraduate level. On each compact subannulus choose a smaller radius in the inner annulus and a larger radius in the outer annulus. Absolute convergence there bounds negative and positive coefficient tails by geometric majorants throughout the compact subannulus. The same estimates with polynomial factors in the index control differentiated series. Termwise differentiation gives the holomorphic extension, including across |z|=1. Undergraduate power-series estimates and complex differentiation suffice without a general Laurent-annulus theorem.

Statement

Let 0<r<1<R. Suppose f and g are holomorphic respectively on r<|z|<1 and 1<|z|<R, with absolutely convergent Laurent expansions f(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}a_nz^n and g(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}a_nz^n having the same coefficients. The common series converges absolutely on r<|z|<R and defines a holomorphic function extending both.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Analysis supplies absolute convergence and power series; a Laurent series simply allows negative integer powers on an annulus. With complex differentiability as a direct calculus extension, the stated gluing assertion requires no residue or Riemann-surface theory.

Proof 1: Common Laurent coefficients glue adjacent annuli — outline

The Laurent expansions of f and g are absolutely convergent in the regions r<|z|<1 and 1<|z|<R, respectively. Since their coefficients agree, it is then elementary that the common Laurent series converges absolutely in the whole region r<|z|<R. This series gives the common holomorphic extension.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof glues holomorphic functions through convergence of a bilateral Laurent series, including holomorphy from local uniform convergence. This requires complex analytic series and continuation beyond the agreed baseline.
