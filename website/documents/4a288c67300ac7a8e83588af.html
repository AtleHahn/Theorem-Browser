Statement

Let n\ge1 be an integer, let a\in\mathbb Z satisfy \gcd(a,n)=1, and let R contain exactly one representative of each residue class coprime to n. Then aR=\{ar:r\in R\} represents exactly the same residue classes.

High school only

Proof 1: Use cancellation and finiteness

Let m be the number of reduced residue classes modulo n.

Let R = {x1, x2, ... , xm} be a reduced residue system (mod n) and let a be any integer coprime to n. The proof hinges on the fundamental fact that multiplication by a permutes the xi: in other words if axj ≡ axk (mod n) then j = k. (This law of cancellation is proved in the article Multiplicative group of integers modulo n.) That is, the sets R and aR = {ax1, ax2, ... , axm}, considered as sets of congruence classes (mod n), are identical (as sets—they may be listed in different orders).

Level
High school only
Level justification

Use coprime cancellation to make multiplication injective and finite counting to obtain a permutation. Elementary integer congruences suffice.
