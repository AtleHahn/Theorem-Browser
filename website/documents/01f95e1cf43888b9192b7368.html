Statement

Let d\geq2 be an integer. Assign to each p\in\mathbb R^d a nonempty subset F(p)\subseteq\mathbb R^d. Suppose that whenever \|p-q\|=1, every u\in F(p) and v\in F(q) satisfy \|u-v\|=1, where \|\cdot\| is the Euclidean norm. Then each F(p) is a singleton \{f(p)\}, and the resulting function is an isometry:

\|f(p)-f(q)\|=\|p-q\|\qquad(p,q\in\mathbb R^d).

In particular, every function f:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^d preserving unit distances preserves all distances and is one-to-one.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Euclidean distance is the square root of the sum of squared coordinate differences. The multivalued version is explicitly described as assigning a nonempty set of possible images; its assertion uses elementary sets and distance comparisons, not topology or linear algebra.

  • Rigidity from preservation of one distance, without continuity assumptions, is an independently significant geometric theorem.

Proof 1: Simplex distances and triangle-inequality approximation (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorem 1 and proof, pp.1–3, including footnotes 1–4).

For the multivalued assertion, choose one selection f(p)\in F(p) and apply the single-valued theorem. Fix a unit vector e. The images f(p-e),f(p+e) are distance 2 apart. Every u\in F(p) is distance 1 from both, so equality in the triangle inequality forces u to be their unique midpoint, namely f(p). In the paper’s polygonal-chain observation, use lengths whose largest member is at most the sum of the others; this holds for the repeated small lengths in the final step and, by choosing the final power sufficiently large, for the mixed-length chain in the noncontraction step.

Level justification

The linked argument uses triangle inequalities, similar triangles, regular-simplex geometry, integer residues and arbitrarily fine numerical bounds. Its density dependency follows from pigeonholes, and simplex rigidity follows from subtracting squared-distance equations. These finite coordinate calculations can be explained locally without a linear algebra course or a theory of continuous maps.
