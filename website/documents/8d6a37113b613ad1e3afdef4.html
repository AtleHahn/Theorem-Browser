Simpler proof assessment

Likely alternative prerequisite level: High school only; lowest recorded proof level: Undergraduate level. Set B=0 and fix absolute distance differences i,j. For signed choices u=+/-i,v=+/-j, squared-distance subtraction gives X=P-rQ, r=|X|, and a quadratic in r. Opposite sign pairs give Q and -Q and therefore roots r and -r; together they contribute at most two nonnegative-distance points, with r=0 counted only once. The quadratic cannot be identically zero, since P=0 and |Q|=1 would force A,B,C collinear. At most four points occur per (i,j), including zero and extreme differences. Finite coordinate algebra yields the exact stated bound.

Statement

Let S be a set of points in the Euclidean plane with integer distances between every pair. If A,B,C\in S are noncollinear, then |S|\le4(d(A,B)+1)(d(B,C)+1), where d denotes Euclidean distance.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement concerns integer distances between points in the Euclidean plane and an explicit bound using two of those distances. This rating concerns understanding the statement, independently of the difficulty of its proof.

Proof 1: Count intersections of two finite conic families — outline

The proof uses a system of curves for which each point of the given set lies on a crossing of two of the curves. In more detail, it consists of the following steps:

  • Choose the triangle ABC formed by the three noncollinear given points.

  • Let d denote the Euclidean distance function. For any point X of the given set, the integer |d(A,X)-d(B,X)| is at most d(A,B), by the triangle inequality. Thus, X lies on one of d(A,B)+1 loci defined by equations of the form

    |d(A,X)-d(B,X)|=i,\qquad 0\le i\le d(A,B).

    For 0<i<d(A,B) these are hyperbolas. For i=0 the locus is the perpendicular bisector of AB, and for i=d(A,B) the equation instead defines two rays extending in opposite directions on the line through A and B. These last two loci can be treated as degenerate hyperbolas for the purposes of the proof.

  • By a symmetric argument, X must also lie on one of d(B,C)+1 hyperbolas or degenerate hyperbolas defined by equations of the form

    |d(B,X)-d(C,X)|=j,\qquad 0\le j\le d(B,C).

  • The nondegenerate hyperbolas from the two families containing X cannot coincide, because they have different pairs of foci. The degenerate loci have distinct supporting lines because A,B,C are noncollinear. Thus there is no common curve component, and X must be a point where the two loci intersect. Each pair has at most four intersection points, by Bézout's theorem (also when one or both loci lie on lines).

  • Because each given point must be one of these intersection points, the number of given points is at most the product of the number of pairs of loci and the number of intersections per pair. This is at most

    4\bigl(d(A,B)+1\bigr)\bigl(d(B,C)+1\bigr)

    points, a finite number.
