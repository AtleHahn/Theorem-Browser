Statement

Let X=\mathbb C/\Lambda for a lattice \Lambda of rank two, and let P\in X. With \ell(nP) denoting the dimension of meromorphic functions with only an allowed pole of order at most n at P, one has \ell(0)=1 and \ell(nP)=n for every integer n>0.

Proof 1: Descend dz and separate the zero-pole case — outline

The torus \mathbb C/\Lambda, where \Lambda is a two-dimensional lattice (a group isomorphic to \mathbb Z^2), has genus one. The standard complex coordinate z on \mathbb C yields a one-form \omega=dz on X that is everywhere holomorphic and nowhere zero. Therefore, K, the divisor of \omega, is zero.

We use Riemann–Roch in the form

\ell(D)-\ell(K-D)=\deg D-g+1.

For D=0, holomorphic functions on the compact connected surface are constant, so \ell(K-D)=\ell(0)=1. For D=nP with n>0, the degree of K-D is strictly negative, so that the correction term \ell(K-D) is 0. Riemann–Roch therefore gives \ell(nP)=n. On this surface, the sequence \ell(0),\ell(P),\ell(2P),\ldots is

1,\ 1,\ 2,\ 3,\ 4,\ 5,\ldots.
