Statement

Let X_1,\ldots,X_n be independent real random variables with 0\le X_i\le1 almost surely, and let \overline X=n^{-1}\sum_iX_i. For t>0, \mathbb P(\overline X-\mathbb E\overline X\ge t)\le e^{-2nt^2} and \mathbb P(|\overline X-\mathbb E\overline X|\ge t)\le2e^{-2nt^2}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The mean is a finite arithmetic average. Event probabilities, finite independence, expectation and almost-sure interval membership are the limited additional probability definitions over undergraduate integration; there is no convergence assertion or statistical model to learn.

Proof 1: Substitute unit interval lengths and rescale the sum

Apply Hoeffding’s inequality for bounded independent variables.

Suppose {\displaystyle a_{i}=0} and {\displaystyle b_{i}=1} for all i. This can occur when Xi are independent Bernoulli random variables, though they need not be identically distributed. Then we get the inequality

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\operatorname {P} \left(S_{n}-\mathrm {E} \left[S_{n}\right]\geq t\right)&\leq \exp(-2t^{2}/n)\\\operatorname {P} \left(\left|S_{n}-\mathrm {E} \left[S_{n}\right]\right|\geq t\right)&\leq 2\exp(-2t^{2}/n)\end{aligned}}}

or equivalently,

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\operatorname {P} \left((S_{n}-\mathrm {E} \left[S_{n}\right])/n\geq t\right)&\leq \exp(-2nt^{2})\\\operatorname {P} \left(\left|(S_{n}-\mathrm {E} \left[S_{n}\right])/n\right|\geq t\right)&\leq 2\exp(-2nt^{2})\end{aligned}}}

Here S_n=\sum_iX_i and (S_n-\mathbb ES_n)/n=\overline X-\mathbb E\overline X. The inequalities hold for t>0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Set every interval length to one in Hoeffding and replace the sum threshold by nt. Undergraduate random variables and expectations suffice to apply the supplied bounded-variable inequality.
