Statement

For every integer n>3, there is a prime number p such that n<p<2n-2.

Review notes

  • A classical uniform prime-existence theorem and foundational result on gaps between primes.

Proof 1: Two-prime interval bound and endpoint exclusion (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorem 1 and Example 1, pp.1–2).

For n\ge7, set x=2n-2. The bound R_2=11 supplies two primes in (n-1,2n-2]; at most one is at most n, and the even upper endpoint is composite. Thus one lies in (n,2n-2). For n=4,5,6, take p=5,7,7. The analytic estimate used in Example 1 is proved in Ramanujan’s paper, equations (7)–(17).
