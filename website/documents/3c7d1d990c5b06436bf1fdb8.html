Statement

Let X be an \mathbb R^2-valued centered Gaussian random vector, let D be a disk centered at the origin, and let R be a rectangle centered at the origin. Then \mathbb P(X\in D\cap R)\geq\mathbb P(X\in D)\mathbb P(X\in R). If \mathbb P(X\in R)>0, equivalently \mathbb P(X\in D\mid X\in R)\geq\mathbb P(X\in D).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A centered Gaussian vector in the plane can be understood as a linear image of independent standard normal coordinates, including singular images. Event probabilities and conditional probability as a ratio add a limited density/probability extension to undergraduate integration and linear algebra; the geometric sets are ordinary convex symmetric plane figures.

Proof 1: Positive Gaussian correlation of a centered disk and rectangle — outline

The Gaussian correlation inequality states that a centered Gaussian probability measure \mu satisfies \mu(E\cap F)\geq\mu(E)\mu(F) for convex sets E,F symmetric about the origin. Apply it in dimension two to the centered disk and rectangle. The source describes the resulting intersection probability and its equivalent conditional-probability interpretation.

Level justification

Check that a centered disk and rectangle are convex and symmetric, then apply the supplied Gaussian event inequality. Finite-dimensional Gaussian vectors and ordinary conditional probability fit undergraduate probability; no proof of the general correlation theorem is required.
