Statement

For an n\times n matrix N with entries in \{-1,1\}, |\det N|\le n^{n/2}. Equality holds exactly when its columns are pairwise orthogonal; such a sign matrix is called a Hadamard matrix.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Real matrices and determinants; Column norms and orthogonality. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Determinant bound for sign matrices (outline)

Every column has norm \sqrt n. Hadamard’s inequality gives the bound, and its nonzero-column equality criterion gives precisely the stated orthogonality characterization.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the column-norm determinant inequality with each norm equal to sqrt(n), including its equality criterion. Undergraduate linear algebra suffices.
