Statement

Let a<b be real numbers, let n\geq0 be an integer, and let f:[a,b]\to\mathbb R be continuous. For a continuous real function u on this interval define

\|u\|_\infty=\max_{x\in[a,b]}|u(x)|.

A real polynomial g of degree at most n is a best uniform approximation to f, meaning that

\|f-g\|_\infty\leq\|f-p\|_\infty

for every real polynomial p of degree at most n, if and only if there are n+2 points

a\leq x_0<x_1<\cdots<x_{n+1}\leq b

and a sign \sigma\in\{-1,1\} such that

f(x_i)-g(x_i)=\sigma(-1)^i\|f-g\|_\infty\qquad(0\leq i\leq n+1).

Polynomials of degree at most n include the zero polynomial.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Continuity on a compact interval, maxima, polynomial degree and minimization of a displayed error norm are ordinary introductory real-analysis concepts. No approximation-theory machinery is needed to understand this complete iff statement.
