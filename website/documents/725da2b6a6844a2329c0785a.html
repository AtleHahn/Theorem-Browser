Statement

For a solvable Lie algebra \mathfrak g over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, the following properties are equivalent: every finite-dimensional \mathfrak g-module has a complete invariant flag; every nonzero finite-dimensional simple \mathfrak g-module has dimension one.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Lie algebras, solvability and their linear actions add a short set of related algebraic definitions. Simple modules mean no proper invariant subspaces and flags are nested subspaces, so the full assertion is accessible from undergraduate algebra and linear algebra.

Proof 1: Invariant flags and one-dimensional simple modules — source deduction

A complete invariant flag in a nonzero simple module must have no nonzero proper invariant term, so that module has dimension one. Conversely, in a nonzero finite-dimensional module V choose a maximal proper submodule V_1. The quotient V/V_1 is simple and hence one-dimensional. Induction on \dim V, applied to V_1, gives a full flag, with V adjoined as its first term. The zero-dimensional case has the empty flag.
