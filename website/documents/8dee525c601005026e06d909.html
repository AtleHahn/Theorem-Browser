Statement

Graphs below are finite, undirected and simple. A clique is a set of pairwise adjacent vertices; an independent set has no edges within it. The complete r-partite graph joins all vertices in different parts and none in the same part. Write T(n,r) for this graph on n vertices with r part sizes differing by at most 1 (empty parts allowed). If n\ge0 and r\ge1 are integers, every n-vertex graph with no (r+1)-vertex clique has at most e(T(n,r)) edges, and this bound is attained. Here e(G) denotes the number of edges of G.

Proof 1: Induction after deleting an r-clique — outline

Induct on n. For n\le r, the complete graph is T(n,r), so the claim is immediate. The case r=1 also follows immediately. For n>r and r>1, proceed as follows.

Take a {\displaystyle K_{r+1}}-free graph on {\displaystyle n} vertices with the maximal number of edges. Find a {\displaystyle K_{r}} (which exists by maximality), and partition the vertices into the set {\displaystyle A} of the {\displaystyle r} vertices in the {\displaystyle K_{r}} and the set {\displaystyle B} of the {\displaystyle n-r} other vertices.

Now, one can bound edges above as follows:

  • There are exactly {\displaystyle {\binom {r}{2}}} edges within {\displaystyle A}.
  • There are at most {\displaystyle (r-1)|B|=(r-1)(n-r)} edges between {\displaystyle A} and {\displaystyle B}, since no vertex in {\displaystyle B} can connect to all of {\displaystyle A}.
  • The number of edges within {\displaystyle B} is at most the number of edges of {\displaystyle T(n-r,r)} by the inductive hypothesis.

Adding these bounds gives the result.

The graph T(n,r) itself has no (r+1)-clique. The source leaves the maximality assertion and the balanced-graph edge-count recurrence implicit.

Proof 2: Maximum-degree multipartite reduction — outline

For an empty graph or r=1 the assertion is immediate.

Take the vertex {\displaystyle v} of largest degree. Consider the set {\displaystyle A} of vertices, including v, not adjacent to {\displaystyle v} and the set {\displaystyle B} of vertices adjacent to {\displaystyle v}.

Now, delete all edges within {\displaystyle A} and draw all edges between {\displaystyle A} and {\displaystyle B}. This does not decrease the number of edges by our maximality assumption and keeps the graph {\displaystyle K_{r+1}}-free. Now, {\displaystyle B} is {\displaystyle K_{r}}-free, so the same argument can be repeated on {\displaystyle B}.

Repeating this argument eventually produces a graph in the same form as a Turán graph, which is a collection of independent sets, with edges between each two vertices from different independent sets. A simple calculation shows that the number of edges of this graph is maximized when all independent set sizes are as close to equal as possible. There are at most r nonempty parts, since a clique can be formed by choosing one vertex from each part; allow empty parts to obtain r parts.

Balance the parts

Let {\displaystyle S_{1},S_{2},\ldots ,S_{r}} be the independent sets of the multipartite graph. Since two vertices have an edge between them if and only if they are not in the same independent set, the number of edges is

{\displaystyle \sum _{i<j}\left|S_{i}\right|\left|S_{j}\right|={\frac {1}{2}}\left(n^{2}-\sum _{i}\left|S_{i}\right|^{2}\right),}

where the left hand side follows from direct counting, and the right hand side follows from complementary counting. To show the {\displaystyle \left(1-{\frac {1}{r}}\right){\frac {n^{2}}{2}}} bound, applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality to the {\textstyle \sum \limits _{i}\left|S_{i}\right|^{2}} term on the right hand side suffices, since {\textstyle \sum \limits _{i}\left|S_{i}\right|=n}.

To prove the Turán Graph is optimal, one can argue that no two {\displaystyle S_{i}} differ by more than one in size. In particular, supposing that we have {\displaystyle \left|S_{i}\right|\geq \left|S_{j}\right|+2} for some {\displaystyle i\neq j}, moving one vertex from {\displaystyle S_{i}} to {\displaystyle S_{j}} (and adjusting edges accordingly) would increase the value of the sum. This can be seen by examining the changes to either side of the above expression for the number of edges, or by noting that the degree of the moved vertex increases.

A copy of a vertex has the same neighbors as that vertex. In particular, given a {\displaystyle K_{r+1}}-free graph, the following steps are applied:

  • If {\displaystyle u,v} are non-adjacent vertices and {\displaystyle u} has a higher degree than {\displaystyle v}, replace {\displaystyle v} with a copy of {\displaystyle u}. Repeat this until all non-adjacent vertices have the same degree.
  • If {\displaystyle u,v,w} are vertices with {\displaystyle u,v} and {\displaystyle v,w} non-adjacent but {\displaystyle u,w} adjacent, then replace both {\displaystyle u} and {\displaystyle w} with copies of {\displaystyle v}.

Repeat the steps until neither applies. Now, non-adjacency, including equality of vertices, forms an equivalence relation.
