Statement

Let G be a finite group and p a prime. Define its p-residual by

O^p(G)=\bigcap\{N\trianglelefteq G:G/N\text{ has order a power of }p\}.

Then O^p(G) is generated by all Sylow q-subgroups of G, as q ranges over the prime divisors of |G| different from p.

Here N\trianglelefteq G means that N is normal. A Sylow q-subgroup has order equal to the largest power of q dividing |G|. The subgroup generated by a family is the smallest subgroup containing every member of the family; an empty family generates the trivial subgroup. The power p^0=1 is allowed in the defining intersection.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory finite-group theory suffices; the residual is an explicitly defined intersection, with no additional specialist structure.

Proof 1: Sylow generation and the quotient characterization (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Definition4.5 p.60 and Lemma4.7(i)–(iii),(v), proof p.61.).

Use Definition 4.5 and Lemma 4.7, rather than the different terminology in Definition 4.6. Let H be generated by the Sylow subgroups for primes other than p. It is normal by conjugation invariance, and G/H is a p-group. For every normal N with p-group quotient, each of those Sylow subgroups maps trivially to G/N, so H\subseteq N. These two inclusions identify H with the stated intersection. The argument includes an empty generating family and primes not dividing |G|.
