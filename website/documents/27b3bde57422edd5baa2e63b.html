Statement

In a nondegenerate right triangle, the hypotenuse is strictly longer than either leg.

Proof 1: Compare the three positions of an equal-length point

Let ADC be a right triangle with right angle at D. On the ray from A through D, choose B with AB=AC, and write \gamma=\angle BAC.

The proof, based upon the triangle angle-sum postulate, proceeds by considering three positions for point B: (i) B beyond D (which is to be proved), or (ii) B coincident with D (which would mean the isosceles triangle had two right angles as base angles plus the vertex angle γ, which would violate the triangle postulate), or lastly, (iii) B interior to the right triangle between points A and D (in which case angle ABC is an exterior angle of a right triangle BDC and therefore larger than π/2, meaning the other base angle of the isosceles triangle also is greater than π/2 and their sum exceeds π in violation of the triangle postulate).

Thus B lies beyond D, and AC=AB>AD. A similar construction shows AC>DC, establishing the theorem.

Level justification
