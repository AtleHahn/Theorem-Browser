Statement

Let F be a field with a non-Archimedean absolute value |\cdot|, and let \mathcal O=\{x\in F:|x|\leq1\}. For every integer n\geq1,

\mathrm{GL}_n(F)=B_n(F)\,\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal O),

where B_n(F) is the group of invertible upper triangular matrices. Thus every invertible matrix over F is a product of an invertible upper triangular matrix and a matrix whose entries and inverse entries belong to \mathcal O.

An absolute value has |x|\geq0, |x|=0 exactly when x=0, |xy|=|x||y|, and |1|=1; non-Archimedean means |x+y|\leq\max(|x|,|y|). The group \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal O) consists of matrices invertible over the ring \mathcal O, so both the matrix and its inverse have entries in \mathcal O. Uniqueness of these two factors is not asserted.

Level justification

Linear algebra over a field and introductory ring theory suffice; the valuation ring is specified by a single strong-triangle norm inequality and invertibility by explicit entry conditions, a small additional reading burden.
