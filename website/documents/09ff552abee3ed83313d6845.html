Statement

For smooth regular surfaces in \mathbb R^3, Gaussian curvature is preserved by every local isometry. Here a local isometry is a smooth diffeomorphism between open surface neighborhoods whose differential preserves inner products of tangent vectors.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement uses differential geometry of surfaces and curvature, beyond the calculus/linear-algebra baseline.

Proof 1: Metric coefficients and equality of mixed third derivatives (outline)

Following Do Carmo we can express the second derivative of a parametrisation of a surface, in terms of the first fundamental form, second fundamental form and Christoffel symbols, then find equations linking the Christoffel symbols to the coefficients of the first fundamental form and their derivatives, showing that these Christoffel symbols are invariant under isometries. Finally, an equation linking Gaussian curvature to Christoffel symbols shows that it is also invariant under isometries.

Let {\displaystyle S,{\tilde {S}}} be regular surfaces, and let {\displaystyle \mathbf {r} =\mathbf {r} (u,v)} be a parametrisation of a patch of the surface {\displaystyle S}, with unit normal {\displaystyle \mathbf {N} }. Denote the first derivatives of {\displaystyle \mathbf {r} } with respect to {\displaystyle u} and {\displaystyle v} by {\displaystyle \mathbf {r} _{u}} and {\displaystyle \mathbf {r} _{v}} and the second derivatives by {\displaystyle \mathbf {r} _{uu},\mathbf {r} _{uv},\mathbf {r} _{vv}}. (As our surface is regular, {\displaystyle \mathbf {r} _{vu}=\mathbf {r} _{uv}}.)

Definition:

A diffeomorphism {\displaystyle \phi :S\to {\tilde {S}}} is an isometry if for all {\displaystyle p\in S} and all pairs {\displaystyle \mathbf {w} _{1},\mathbf {w} _{2}\in T_{p}(S)} the tangent space to {\displaystyle S} we have

{\displaystyle \langle \mathbf {w} _{1},\mathbf {w} _{2}\rangle _{p}=\langle d\phi _{p}(\mathbf {w} _{1}),d\phi _{p}(\mathbf {w} _{2})\rangle _{\phi (p)}.}

In other words, the differential map between tangent spaces, {\displaystyle d\phi _{p}:T_{p}(S)\to T_{\phi (p)}({\tilde {S}})} preserves the inner product.

This definition of isometry applies to the whole surface, for the theorem we only need a weaker definition, defined for small neighbourhoods.

Definition:

A map {\displaystyle \phi :V\to {\tilde {S}}} of a neighbourhood {\displaystyle V} of {\displaystyle p\in S} is a local isometry if there is a neighbourhood {\displaystyle {\tilde {V}}} of {\displaystyle \phi (p)\in {\tilde {S}}} such that {\displaystyle \phi :V\to {\tilde {V}}} is an isometry.

If there exists local isometries for each {\displaystyle p\in S} then {\displaystyle S} and {\displaystyle {\tilde {S}}} are said to be locally isometric.

An immediate consequence is if {\displaystyle \mathbf {x} (u,v)} is a parametrisation of {\displaystyle V\subset S} and {\displaystyle \phi \circ \mathbf {x} (u,v)} is a parametrisation of {\displaystyle {\tilde {V}}\subset {\tilde {S}}} then the coefficients of the first fundamental form for both surfaces agree:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}E&=\langle \mathbf {x} _{u},\mathbf {x} _{u}\rangle &={\tilde {E}}&=\langle d\phi _{p}(\mathbf {x} _{u}),d\phi _{p}(\mathbf {x} _{u})\rangle \\F&=\langle \mathbf {x} _{u},\mathbf {x} _{v}\rangle &={\tilde {F}}&=\langle d\phi _{p}(\mathbf {x} _{u}),d\phi _{p}(\mathbf {x} _{v})\rangle \\G&=\langle \mathbf {x} _{v},\mathbf {x} _{v}\rangle &={\tilde {G}}&=\langle d\phi _{p}(\mathbf {x} _{v}),d\phi _{p}(\mathbf {x} _{v})\rangle \\\end{aligned}}}

Furthermore, as {\displaystyle E-{\tilde {E}}=0} for all point {\displaystyle (u,v)\in \mathbf {x} ^{-1}(V)} in the parameter space the derivatives are equal {\displaystyle E_{u}-{\tilde {E}}_{u}=0}, etc., as are all higher derivatives.

Now consider the second derivatives of a parametrisation {\displaystyle \mathbf {r} =\mathbf {r} (u,v)}, these can be expressed in terms of the basis {\displaystyle \mathbf {r} _{u},\mathbf {r} _{v},\mathbf {N} } and Christoffel symbols {\displaystyle \Gamma _{ij}^{k}},

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\mathbf {r} _{uu}&=\Gamma _{11}^{1}\mathbf {r} _{u}+\Gamma _{11}^{2}\mathbf {r} _{v}+L\mathbf {N} \\\mathbf {r} _{uv}&=\Gamma _{12}^{1}\mathbf {r} _{u}+\Gamma _{12}^{2}\mathbf {r} _{v}+M\mathbf {N} \\\mathbf {r} _{vv}&=\Gamma _{22}^{1}\mathbf {r} _{u}+\Gamma _{22}^{2}\mathbf {r} _{v}+N\mathbf {N} .\\\end{aligned}}}

(1)

In the arguments of the fundamental forms below, u,v denote the coordinate tangent vectors. Taking the dot product of each equation with {\displaystyle \mathbf {N} } shows that the coefficients {\displaystyle L,M,N} are the coefficients of the second fundamental form, {\displaystyle L=\mathrm {I\!I} (u,u),M=\mathrm {I\!I} (u,v),N=\mathrm {I\!I} (v,v)}.

Let {\displaystyle E,F,G} be the coefficients of the first fundamental form {\displaystyle E=\mathrm {I} (u,u)=\mathbf {r} _{u}\cdot \mathbf {r} _{u}}, {\displaystyle F=\mathrm {I} (u,v)=\mathbf {r} _{u}\cdot \mathbf {r} _{v}}, {\displaystyle G=\mathrm {I} (v,v)=\mathbf {r} _{v}\cdot \mathbf {r} _{v}}. Their derivatives with respect to {\displaystyle u,v} are

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}E_{u}&=2\mathbf {r} _{uu}\cdot \mathbf {r} _{u}\\E_{v}&=2\mathbf {r} _{uv}\cdot \mathbf {r} _{u}\\F_{u}&=\mathbf {r} _{uu}\cdot \mathbf {r} _{v}+\mathbf {r} _{uv}\cdot \mathbf {r} _{u}\\F_{v}&=\mathbf {r} _{uv}\cdot \mathbf {r} _{v}+\mathbf {r} _{vv}\cdot \mathbf {r} _{u}\\G_{u}&=2\mathbf {r} _{uv}\cdot \mathbf {r} _{v}\\G_{v}&=2\mathbf {r} _{vv}\cdot \mathbf {r} _{v}.\end{aligned}}}

Now take dot products of the second derivatives of the surface with {\displaystyle \mathbf {r} _{u}} and {\displaystyle \mathbf {r} _{v}} to obtain expressions for the Christoffel symbols.

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\mathbf {r} _{uu}\cdot \mathbf {r} _{u}&=\Gamma _{11}^{1}E+\Gamma _{11}^{2}F&={\tfrac {1}{2}}E_{u}\\\mathbf {r} _{uu}\cdot \mathbf {r} _{v}&=\Gamma _{11}^{1}F+\Gamma _{11}^{2}G&=F_{u}-{\tfrac {1}{2}}E_{v}\\\mathbf {r} _{uv}\cdot \mathbf {r} _{u}&=\Gamma _{12}^{1}E+\Gamma _{12}^{2}F&={\tfrac {1}{2}}E_{v}\\\mathbf {r} _{uv}\cdot \mathbf {r} _{v}&=\Gamma _{12}^{1}F+\Gamma _{12}^{2}G&={\tfrac {1}{2}}G_{u}\\\mathbf {r} _{vv}\cdot \mathbf {r} _{u}&=\Gamma _{22}^{1}E+\Gamma _{22}^{2}F&=F_{v}-{\tfrac {1}{2}}G_{u}\\\mathbf {r} _{vv}\cdot \mathbf {r} _{v}&=\Gamma _{22}^{1}F+\Gamma _{22}^{2}G&={\tfrac {1}{2}}G_{v}\\\end{aligned}}}

Each pair of equations can be written as a matrix, for the first two

{\displaystyle {\begin{pmatrix}E&F\\F&G\end{pmatrix}}{\begin{pmatrix}\Gamma _{11}^{1}\\\Gamma _{11}^{2}\end{pmatrix}}={\begin{pmatrix}{\tfrac {1}{2}}E_{u}\\F_{u}-{\tfrac {1}{2}}E_{v}\end{pmatrix}}}

and for non-singular surfaces the matrix is invertible with determinant {\displaystyle EG-F^{2}\neq 0}, showing the Christoffel symbols can be expressed in terms of the coefficient of first fundamental form and their derivatives. This is a key result showing all geometric concepts and properties expressed in terms of the Christoffel symbols are invariant under local isometries. We now show the Gaussian curvature can be expressed in this way.

We use the standard Gaussian-curvature formula K=(LN-M^2)/(EG-F^2) and the normal-derivative formulas. The derivatives of the unit normal can be written as {\displaystyle \mathbf {N} _{u}=a_{11}\mathbf {r} _{u}+a_{21}\mathbf {r} _{v},\mathbf {N} _{v}=a_{12}\mathbf {r} _{u}+a_{22}\mathbf {r} _{v}} and equations for these coefficients can be expressed in terms of the coefficient of the first and second fundamental forms,

{\displaystyle a_{11}={\frac {MF-LG}{EG-F^{2}}},\quad a_{12}={\frac {NF-MG}{EG-F^{2}}},\quad a_{21}={\frac {LF-ME}{EG-F^{2}}},\quad a_{22}={\frac {MF-NE}{EG-F^{2}}},}

The third derivatives of our parameterisation {\displaystyle \mathbf {r} _{uuv}} can be expressed as either {\displaystyle (\mathbf {r} _{uu})_{v}} or {\displaystyle (\mathbf {r} _{uv})_{u}} differentiating the first two equations in (1) and equating these gives

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}&\Gamma _{11}^{1}\mathbf {r} _{uv}+\Gamma _{11}^{2}\mathbf {r} _{vv}+L\mathbf {N} _{v}+(\Gamma _{11}^{1})_{v}\mathbf {r} _{u}+(\Gamma _{11}^{2})_{v}\mathbf {r} _{v}+L_{v}\mathbf {N} \\&=\Gamma _{12}^{1}\mathbf {r} _{uu}+\Gamma _{12}^{2}\mathbf {r} _{vu}+M\mathbf {N} _{u}+(\Gamma _{12}^{1})_{u}\mathbf {r} _{u}+(\Gamma _{12}^{2})_{u}\mathbf {r} _{v}+M_{u}\mathbf {N} \end{aligned}}}

Substitution in expressions from (1) and equating coefficients of {\displaystyle \mathbf {r} _{v}} gives

{\displaystyle \Gamma _{11}^{1}\Gamma _{12}^{2}+\Gamma _{11}^{2}\Gamma _{22}^{2}+La_{22}+(\Gamma _{11}^{2})_{v}=\Gamma _{12}^{1}\Gamma _{11}^{2}+\Gamma _{12}^{2}\Gamma _{12}^{2}+Ma_{21}+(\Gamma _{12}^{2})_{u}}

Rearranging gives

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\Gamma _{11}^{1}\Gamma _{12}^{2}+\Gamma _{11}^{2}\Gamma _{22}^{2}+(\Gamma _{11}^{2})_{v}-\Gamma _{12}^{1}\Gamma _{11}^{2}-\Gamma _{12}^{2}\Gamma _{12}^{2}-(\Gamma _{12}^{2})_{u}&=+Ma_{21}-La_{22}\\&=M{\frac {LF-ME}{EG-F^{2}}}-L{\frac {MF-NE}{EG-F^{2}}}\\&=E{\frac {LN-M^{2}}{EG-F^{2}}}\\&=EK\end{aligned}}}

Giving the required expression for the Gaussian Curvature {\displaystyle K} in terms of coefficients of the first fundamental form and its derivatives, so it is invariant by local isometries.
