Statement

Let M=(m_{ij})\in\mathbb C^{n\times n} be Hermitian positive semidefinite, with n\ge1. Then \det M\le\prod_{j=1}^n m_{jj}.

Proof 1: Take singleton principal blocks

Use the multiple-principal-block Fischer inequality: for a Hermitian positive-semidefinite matrix partitioned into principal diagonal blocks M_1,\ldots,M_k, \det M\leq\prod_{j=1}^k\det M_j. Take every block to have size 1\times1. Its determinant is its diagonal entry, so \det M\leq\prod_{j=1}^n m_{jj}.

Proof 2: A positive semidefinite determinant is bounded by its diagonal product — source argument

Factor M=N^*N. If v_i are the columns of N, then m_{ii}=\|v_i\|^2. Using the Gram determinant identity \det(N^*N)=|\det N|^2 and Hadamard gives \det M=|\det N|^2\le\prod_i\|v_i\|^2=\prod_i m_{ii}.
