Statement

Let (S,\Sigma,\mu) be a measure space and f_n:S\to\mathbb R measurable, with |f_n|\le g for a nonnegative integrable g. Then f_n, \liminf f_n and \limsup f_n are integrable, \int_S|\liminf_n f_n|\,d\mu\le\int_Sg\,d\mu and \int_S|\limsup_n f_n|\,d\mu\le\int_Sg\,d\mu, and \int_S\liminf_n f_n\,d\mu\le\liminf_n\int_S f_n\,d\mu\le\limsup_n\int_S f_n\,d\mu\le\int_S\limsup_n f_n\,d\mu.

Proof 1: Apply Fatou to both sides of the dominator

All fn as well as the limit inferior and the limit superior of the fn are measurable and dominated in absolute value by g, hence integrable.

Using linearity of the Lebesgue integral and applying Fatou's lemma to the non-negative functions {\displaystyle f_{n}+g} we get

{\displaystyle \int _{S}\liminf _{n\to \infty }f_{n}\,d\mu +\int _{S}g\,d\mu =\int _{S}\liminf _{n\to \infty }(f_{n}+g)\,d\mu\leq \liminf _{n\to \infty }\int _{S}(f_{n}+g)\,d\mu =\liminf _{n\to \infty }\int _{S}f_{n}\,d\mu +\int _{S}g\,d\mu .}

Cancelling the finite(!) {\displaystyle \int _{S}g\,d\mu } term we get the first inequality. The second inequality is the elementary inequality between {\displaystyle \liminf } and {\displaystyle \limsup }. The last inequality follows by applying reverse Fatou lemma, i.e. applying the Fatou lemma to the non-negative functions {\displaystyle g-f_{n}}, and again (up to sign) cancelling the finite {\displaystyle \int _{S}g\,d\mu } term.

Finally, since {\displaystyle \limsup _{n}|f_{n}|\leq g},

{\displaystyle \max \left(\left|\int _{S}\liminf _{n\to \infty }f_{n}\,d\mu \right|,\left|\int _{S}\limsup _{n\to \infty }f_{n}\,d\mu \right|\right)\leq \int _{S}\max \left(\left|\liminf _{n\to \infty }f_{n}\right|,\left|\limsup _{n\to \infty }f_{n}\right|\right)\,d\mu \leq \int _{S}\limsup _{n\to \infty }|f_{n}|\,d\mu \leq \int _{S}g\,d\mu }

by the monotonicity of the Lebesgue integral. In particular, since each of |\liminf f_n| and |\limsup f_n| is bounded by the maximum inside the integral, their absolute integrals are at most \int_S g\,d\mu.
