Statement

Let A be a nonzero complex unital Banach algebra in which every nonzero element is invertible. Here a unital Banach algebra is an associative complex algebra with a complete norm satisfying \|xy\|\le\|x\|\|y\| and a multiplicative identity 1 with \|1\|=1. Then the scalar map

\mathbb C\longrightarrow A,\qquad \lambda\longmapsto\lambda1,

is an isometric algebra isomorphism, where \mathbb C has its usual absolute-value norm.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The joint normed-algebra and division-algebra setting requires combining completeness, multiplicative norm control and algebraic structure. Accessibility within a short undergraduate extension is uncertain, so the conservative beyond-undergraduate classification is retained.

Proof 1: Apply nonemptiness of the spectrum

Use the cited fact that every element of a nonzero complex unital Banach algebra has nonempty spectrum: for each a\in A there is a scalar \lambda\in\mathbb C for which \lambda1-a is not invertible. Since every nonzero element of A is invertible, \lambda1-a=0. Thus every element of A is a scalar multiple of 1.

The scalar map preserves the algebra operations and is injective because 1\ne0. For the isometry assertion, norm homogeneity and the unit convention give \|\lambda1\|=|\lambda|\|1\|=|\lambda|.
