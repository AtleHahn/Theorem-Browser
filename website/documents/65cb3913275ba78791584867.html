Statement

Let f:X\to S and g:S^{\prime}\to S be continuous maps, X^{\prime}=X\times_S S^{\prime}, and let g^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to X and f^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to S^{\prime} be the projections. Work with sheaves of abelian groups; h^* means inverse image and R^r h_* is the rth right derived direct image. For every sheaf \mathcal F on X and integer r\ge0, there is a natural base-change morphism g^*R^rf_*\mathcal F\to R^rf^{\prime}_*g^{\prime*}\mathcal F.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Derived direct images and natural base-change morphisms of sheaves require substantial sheaf cohomology. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Adjunction units and spectral-sequence edge maps

This map exists without any assumptions on the maps f and g. It is constructed as follows: since {\displaystyle g'^{*}} is left adjoint to {\displaystyle g'_{*}}, there is a natural map (called unit map)

{\displaystyle \operatorname {id} \to g'_{*}\circ g'^{*}}

and so

{\displaystyle R^{r}f_{*}\to R^{r}f_{*}\circ g'_{*}\circ g'^{*}.}

The Grothendieck spectral sequence then gives the first map and the last map (they are edge maps) in:

{\displaystyle R^{r}f_{*}\circ g'_{*}\circ g'^{*}\to R^{r}(f\circ g')_{*}\circ g'^{*}=R^{r}(g\circ f')_{*}\circ g'^{*}\to g_{*}\circ R^{r}f'_{*}\circ g'^{*}.}

Combining this with the above yields

{\displaystyle R^{r}f_{*}\to g_{*}\circ R^{r}f'_{*}\circ g'^{*}.}

Using the adjointness of {\displaystyle g^{*}} and {\displaystyle g_{*}} finally yields the desired map.
