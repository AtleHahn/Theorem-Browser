Statement

Let U be a nonempty simply connected open subset of \mathbb C with U\ne\mathbb C. Then there is a biholomorphic map f:U\to\mathbb D, where \mathbb D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}. Biholomorphic means bijective and holomorphic with a holomorphic inverse. Here simply connected means path connected with every closed path continuously deformable to a point within U.

More precisely, for every z_0\in U and every real angle \theta modulo 2\pi, there is exactly one such map with f(z_0)=0 and f'(z_0)/|f'(z_0)|=e^{i\theta}. In particular, there is a unique map normalized by f(z_0)=0 and f'(z_0)>0.

Proof 1: Bounded injection and extremal normal family (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Lecture25, Sections25.1–25.2, printed pp.149–153 (PDF pp.154–158)).

The bounded injection is the reciprocal of a shifted holomorphic logarithm in Lemma25.2.1; it need only map into the disk. Before taking an extremal sequence, Cauchy's estimate on a closed disk of radius r about z_0 gives |f'(z_0)|\le1/r. In Section25.2.2 use the disk automorphism \psi_\alpha(w)=(\alpha-w)/(1-\overline\alpha w). Once the positive-derivative map is obtained, multiplication by e^{i\theta} gives the prescribed angle; applying the same rotation backwards reduces uniqueness to the normalized case.

Its Dirichlet-problem sketch treats only smooth bounded domains and leaves the final mapping properties to be checked.
