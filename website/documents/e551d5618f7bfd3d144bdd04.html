For positive x,y,z, x³+y³+z³≥3xyz.

Proof 1: Compare (3,0,0) with (1,1,1)

Write \sum_{\mathrm{sym}}x^\alpha y^\beta z^\gamma for the sum over all six permutations of x,y,z. Muirhead's inequality compares these symmetric sums when one decreasing exponent sequence majorizes the other: its partial sums are at least those of the other sequence, and their total sums are equal.

We can prove the inequality

x^3+y^3+z^3\ge3xyz

by writing it using the symmetric-sum notation as

\sum_{\mathrm{sym}}x^3y^0z^0 \ge\sum_{\mathrm{sym}}x^1y^1z^1,

which is the same as

2x^3+2y^3+2z^3\ge6xyz.

Since the sequence (3,0,0) majorizes the sequence (1,1,1), the inequality holds by Muirhead's inequality.
