Statement

Let G be a finite simple d-regular graph, where d\ge1 is odd and edge connectivity is at least d-1. Then every edge of G belongs to a perfect matching. Edge connectivity is zero for a disconnected graph and otherwise the minimum number of edges whose deletion disconnects it; thus for d=1 the lower bound zero imposes no restriction.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Regular degree, edge connectivity and perfect matching are finite edge-count and incidence conditions; the connectivity convention is explicit.

Proof 1: Discharge the even-order hypothesis

Use the following matching theorem as an external premise: if G is a regular graph of degree d whose edge connectivity is at least d-1, and G has an even number of vertices, then every edge of G belongs to at least one perfect matching.

The condition on the number of vertices can be omitted from this result when the degree is odd, because in that case (by the handshaking lemma) the number of vertices is always even.

Level justification

Use the degree-sum parity to supply even vertex count, then apply the explicit matching criterion. Finite graph definitions, counting and parity suffice; the matching theorem proof is not used.
