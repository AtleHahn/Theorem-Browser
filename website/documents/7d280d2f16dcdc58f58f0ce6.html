Statement

Let p(n) be the number of partitions of a nonnegative integer n into positive integer summands, with the order of the summands ignored. For every integer k\ge0,

\begin{aligned} p(5k+4)&\equiv0\pmod5,\\ p(7k+5)&\equiv0\pmod7,\\ p(11k+6)&\equiv0\pmod{11}. \end{aligned}
Level
High school only
Level justification

Partitions are defined by elementary counting and the conclusions use only integer divisibility.

Review notes

  • Ramanujan’s classical three partition congruences, central in partition number theory.

Proof 1: Eta-power theta identities and residue exclusion (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorem and proof, p.1; identity discussion and bibliography, pp.1–2).

Interpret the calculation coefficientwise in formal Laurent series over \mathbb F_\ell. The leading monomial of \eta(\ell\tau) is invertible, and the remaining factor has constant term1 and only exponents divisible by \ell; division therefore preserves the absence of exponents divisible by \ell. The condition 24n\equiv1\pmod\ell gives respectively n\equiv4,5,6 for \ell=5,7,11. The tenth-power theta identity used for \ell=11 is a substantive dependency: see Serre, equation(27), Sections2.5 and2.8(i) for its modular-form decomposition.

Level justification

The coefficient extraction and quadratic-residue arguments are elementary. The substantive eta-power identity for the modulus11 case is supplied by weight-five modular forms and Hecke-character theta series, including their modular transformation and eigenspace theory. This makes this linked proof, including its identity dependencies, beyond undergraduate level.
