Statement

On a probability space (\Omega,\mathcal F,P), let (B_n) be a finite or countable measurable partition of \Omega. For events A,C with P(C)>0, P(A\mid C)=\sum_{n:P(B_n\cap C)>0}P(A\mid B_n\cap C)P(B_n\mid C). Here P(D\mid E)=P(D\cap E)/P(E) for P(E)>0. For C=\Omega this gives P(A)=\sum_{n:P(B_n)>0}P(A\mid B_n)P(B_n). Null cells contribute zero and are omitted.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The conditional probabilities are defined as ratios of event probabilities, and the partition consists of disjoint events. Countable additivity and ordinary nonnegative series explain the sum. These closely related probability rules are a limited addition to undergraduate sets and analysis, without conditional expectations of functions.

Proof 1: Divide the disjoint-cell probabilities by the conditioning mass

The law of total probability can also be stated for conditional probabilities:

\begin{aligned} P(A\mid C)&=\frac{P(A\cap C)}{P(C)} =\frac{\sum_nP(A\cap B_n\cap C)}{P(C)}\\[2ex] &=\frac{\sum_{n:P(B_n\cap C)>0}P(A\mid B_n\cap C)P(B_n\mid C)P(C)}{P(C)}\\[2ex] &=\sum_{n:P(B_n\cap C)>0}P(A\mid B_n\cap C)P(B_n\mid C). \end{aligned}

Here countable additivity applies to the disjoint events A\cap B_n\cap C; cells with P(B_n\cap C)=0 contribute zero and are omitted before taking conditional probabilities. Taking C=\Omega gives the unconditional form.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Split A intersect C into disjoint partition cells, use countable additivity, and divide by P(C). This is basic probability with a convergent nonnegative series; conditional probabilities are explicit event ratios.
