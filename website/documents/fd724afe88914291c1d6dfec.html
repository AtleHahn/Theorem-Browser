Statement

Suppose six random variables A,B,C,D,E,F have positive joint mass or density proportional to f_1(A,B,D)f_2(A,C,D)f_3(C,D,F)f_4(C,E,F), with positive factors. For fixed C=c,D=d where the usual conditional mass or density is defined and normalized, the pairs (A,B) and (E,F) are conditionally independent.

Proof 1: Group the fixed-condition factors into two disjoint variable sets

With {\displaystyle C} and {\displaystyle D} constant, {\displaystyle \Pr(A,B,E,F|C=c,D=d)\propto [f_{1}(A,B,d)f_{2}(A,c,d)]\cdot [f_{3}(c,d,F)f_{4}(c,E,F)]=g_{1}(A,B)g_{2}(E,F)} where {\displaystyle g_{1}(A,B)=f_{1}(A,B,d)f_{2}(A,c,d)} and {\displaystyle g_{2}(E,F)=f_{3}(c,d,F)f_{4}(c,E,F)}. This implies that {\displaystyle A,B\perp E,F|C,D}.

Here the last implication uses the factorization criterion for independence of the two variable groups in a normalized conditional mass or density.
