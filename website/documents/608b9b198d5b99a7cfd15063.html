Statement

Let K be a field and G\subset K^\times a finite subgroup of order n, with the characteristic of K not dividing n. Acting on Kx by scalar multiplication, G is generated by pseudoreflections (nonidentity linear maps whose fixed subspace has codimension one). Its invariant polynomials are exactly K[x^n]\subset K[x], so this invariant algebra is a polynomial algebra in one generator.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Fields, finite multiplicative subgroups and polynomials are introductory algebra. Invariant means unchanged by the stated scalar action, and pseudoreflection is explicitly a fixed-subspace codimension condition. These are direct applications of linear algebra.

Proof 1: Scalar roots of unity and the invariant generator — outline

Let V=Kx be one-dimensional. Then any finite group faithfully acting on V is a subgroup of the multiplicative group of the field K, and hence a cyclic group. It follows that G consists of roots of unity of order dividing n, where n is its order, so G is generated by pseudoreflections. In this case, K[V]=K[x] is the polynomial ring in one variable and the algebra of invariants of G is the subalgebra generated by x^n, hence it is a polynomial algebra.

The cyclicity of finite multiplicative subgroups of fields is used without proof. The source leaves the invariant-coefficient check implicit.

Level justification

Use cyclicity of the finite scalar group, then compare polynomial coefficients under a generator to retain exponents divisible by n. Undergraduate fields, cyclic groups and polynomial algebra suffice; pseudoreflections are explicitly defined and in one dimension just nonidentity scalars.
