Statement

Let T be a first-order theory in a language L. A sentence is a formula with no free variables, and T\vdash\psi means that \psi is derivable from T in first-order logic.

  • If T\vdash\exists x_1\cdots\exists x_m\,\varphi(x_1,\ldots,x_m) is a sentence of L, and a_1,\ldots,a_m are distinct fresh constant symbols, then the theory T\cup\{\varphi(a_1,\ldots,a_m)\} in the expanded language is conservative over T.
  • If T\vdash\forall x_1\cdots\forall x_n\exists y\,\varphi(y,x_1,\ldots,x_n) is a sentence of L, and f is a fresh n-ary function symbol, then the theory obtained by adjoining the axiom
\forall x_1\cdots\forall x_n\,\varphi(f(x_1,\ldots,x_n),x_1,\ldots,x_n)

is conservative over T. The case n=0 treats a constant as a function with no arguments.

Conservative means that for every sentence \psi in the original language L, the expanded theory proves \psi if and only if T proves \psi. Fresh symbols do not occur in L; the formulas displayed have no free variables other than those quantified. No uniqueness of the witnesses is required.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Quantified elementary formulas are familiar, but distinguishing a theory from its language, formal derivability and conservativity under added function symbols requires a cumulative metamathematical foundation not confidently supplied in a short extension of undergraduate logic.
