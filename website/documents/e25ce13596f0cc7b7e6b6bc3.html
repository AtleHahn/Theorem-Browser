Statement

For an integer n>0 define E_n(z)=(1-z)\exp(\sum_{j=1}^n z^j/j). For |z|<1, E_n(z)=\exp\left(-\frac{z^{n+1}}{n+1}\sum_{k=0}^\infty\frac{z^k}{1+k/(n+1)}\right).

Level

Level justification

Complex differentiation and exponential functions extend calculus directly; real analysis supplies series, compact convergence and growth limits. All special functions or growth parameters used here are explicitly given by formulas, so no meromorphic continuation or abstract complex geometry is needed.

Proof 1: Cancellation of the initial logarithmic terms

For |z|<1, use the logarithm series

1-z=\exp(\ln(1-z))=\exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^j}{j}\right).

The elementary factor is

E_n(z)=(1-z)\exp\left(\frac{z}{1}+\frac{z^2}{2}+\cdots+\frac{z^n}{n}\right).

For |z|<1 and n>0, one may express it as

E_n(z)=\exp\left(-\frac{z^{n+1}}{n+1}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^k}{1+k/(n+1)}\right).

The source leaves the cancellation and reindexing implicit.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Cancel finitely many terms of the holomorphic logarithm series and reindex the remaining power series. Introductory complex analysis suffices.
