Statement

Let \theta\in C^\infty(\mathbb R) vanish for t\ge1, put \rho(x)=\theta(|x|^2) on \mathbb R^n, and define \Theta(t)=\frac12\int_t^\infty\theta(s)\,ds and \Phi(x)=\Theta(|x|^2). Then \Phi is smooth, supported in the closed unit ball, and

\Delta\Phi(x)=-n\rho(x)-\nabla\rho(x)\cdot x.
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Smooth radial functions; Divergence; Laplacian; Single-variable integration. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Radial integration and the divergence identity

Put K(x)=-n\rho(x)-\nabla\rho(x)\cdot x.

In terms of {\displaystyle \rho (x)=\theta \left(|x|^{2}\right)} we get

{\displaystyle K(x)=-\operatorname {div} (\rho (x)x)=-\operatorname {div} \left(\theta \left(|x|^{2}\right)x\right)}

and if we set

{\displaystyle \Theta (t)={\frac {1}{2}}\int _{t}^{\infty }\theta (s)\mathrm {d} s}

then {\displaystyle \Theta \in \mathrm {C} ^{\infty }(\mathbb {R} )} with {\displaystyle \Theta (t)=0} for {\displaystyle t\geqslant 1}, and {\displaystyle \Theta ^{\prime }(t)=-{\frac {1}{2}}\theta (t)}. Consequently

{\displaystyle -\theta \left(|x|^{2}\right)x=\nabla \left(\Theta \left(|x|^{2}\right)\right)}

and so {\displaystyle K(x)=\operatorname {div} \nabla \left(\Theta \left(|x|^{2}\right)\right)=(\Delta \Phi )(x)}, where {\displaystyle \Phi (x)=\Theta \left(|x|^{2}\right)}. The function \Phi is smooth and vanishes outside the closed unit ball.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Differentiate the one-variable integral, apply the multivariable chain rule and take a divergence. Undergraduate multivariable calculus suffices; support follows directly from the vanishing of theta for t>=1.
