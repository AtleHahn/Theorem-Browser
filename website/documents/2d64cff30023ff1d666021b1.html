Statement

Let f be a nonzero entire function of finite order \rho. Here entire means holomorphic on \mathbb C, and its order can be defined by

\rho=\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{\log\log\max(e,M_f(r))}{\log r}, \qquad M_f(r)=\max_{|z|=r}|f(z)|.

Let m\ge0 be the multiplicity of its zero at the origin, taking m=0 when f(0)\ne0, and list its nonzero zeros (a_k) with multiplicity. Let p be the least nonnegative integer such that \sum_k|a_k|^{-(p+1)} converges. Define

E_p(w)=(1-w)\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^p\frac{w^j}{j}\right),

with empty sum when p=0. Then there is a polynomial Q such that

f(z)=z^m e^{Q(z)}\prod_k E_p(z/a_k),

with the product converging uniformly on compact subsets of \mathbb C. Finite and empty zero lists are allowed, with an empty product equal to 1.

Put q=\deg Q, taking q=0 for a constant polynomial, and define the genus g=\max(p,q). Then

g\le\rho\le g+1.

Consequently, if \rho is not an integer then g=\lfloor\rho\rfloor; if \rho is a positive integer then g=\rho-1 or g=\rho.
