Statement

Let f be a nonzero entire function of finite order \rho. Here entire means holomorphic on \mathbb C, and its order can be defined by

\rho=\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{\log\log\max(e,M_f(r))}{\log r}, \qquad M_f(r)=\max_{|z|=r}|f(z)|.

Let m\ge0 be the multiplicity of its zero at the origin, taking m=0 when f(0)\ne0, and list its nonzero zeros (a_k) with multiplicity. Let p be the least nonnegative integer such that \sum_k|a_k|^{-(p+1)} converges. Define

E_p(w)=(1-w)\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^p\frac{w^j}{j}\right),

with empty sum when p=0. Then there is a polynomial Q such that

f(z)=z^m e^{Q(z)}\prod_k E_p(z/a_k),

with the product converging uniformly on compact subsets of \mathbb C. Finite and empty zero lists are allowed, with an empty product equal to 1.

Put q=\deg Q, taking q=0 for a constant polynomial, and define the genus g=\max(p,q). Then

g\le\rho\le g+1.

Consequently, if \rho is not an integer then g=\lfloor\rho\rfloor; if \rho is a positive integer then g=\rho-1 or g=\rho.

Proof 1: Minimal canonical products and logarithmic growth (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Section3.1, Theorems3.14–3.15 and3.17, pp.79–82; preceding zero-count estimates).

The source’s p is the least summability index; the genus here is g=\max(p,\deg Q). With zero convergence exponent \alpha, its estimates give p\le\alpha\le p+1, \deg Q\le\rho and \rho\le\max(\deg Q,\alpha), hence g\le\rho\le g+1. Finite or empty zero lists have p=\alpha=0. In the factorization proof read \rho(P)=\alpha\le\rho(f), and first divide out z^m.

For the minimum-modulus estimate, split factors at |a_k|=2r. In the tail use |\log E_p(z/a_k)|\le C_p|z/a_k|^{p+1}. For the remaining factors choose circles avoiding |z-a_k|<|a_k|^{-N} with N>\alpha; their linear factors contribute O(n(2r)\log r), and their exponential factors have the power-sum bounds used in Theorem3.14. This supplies the estimate without dropping factors solely because |z-a_k|\ge r.
