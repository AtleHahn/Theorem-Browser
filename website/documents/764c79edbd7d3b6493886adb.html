Statement

Let I_n=[a_n,b_n] be nonempty bounded closed real intervals with I_{n+1}⊆I_n and b_n−a_n→0. Then their intersection contains exactly one point.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Closed real intervals; Nested sequences; Lengths tending to zero. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Compare two hypothetical common points with shrinking lengths

This statement can easily be verified by contradiction. Assume that there exist two different numbers {\displaystyle x,y\in \cap _{n\in \mathbb {N} }I_{n}}. From {\displaystyle x\neq y} it follows that they differ by {\displaystyle |x-y|>0.} Since both numbers have to be contained in every interval, it follows that {\displaystyle |I_{n}|\geq |x-y|} for all {\displaystyle n\in \mathbb {N} }. This contradicts the hypothesis that the interval lengths tend to zero; therefore, the intersection can contain at most one number {\displaystyle x}. The nested-interval completeness axiom guarantees that such a real number {\displaystyle x} exists. {\displaystyle \;\square }
