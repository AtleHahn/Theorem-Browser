Statement

Let f,g:I\to\mathbb R be continuous on a real interval I and differentiable in its interior. If f'=g' there, then f-g is constant on I.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Continuous real functions; Differentiation on intervals. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Differentiate the difference

Let {\displaystyle F(x)=f(x)-g(x)}, then {\displaystyle F'(x)=f'(x)-g'(x)=0} in the interior of the interval {\displaystyle I}. By the zero-derivative constancy theorem (a continuous function on an interval whose interior derivative is zero is constant), {\displaystyle F} is constant over {\displaystyle I}, so {\displaystyle f=g+c} over {\displaystyle I}, where {\displaystyle c} is a constant.
