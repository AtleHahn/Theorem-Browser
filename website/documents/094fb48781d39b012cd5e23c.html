Proof 1: Divide the Pythagorean identity by the squared hypotenuse

In a right triangle with sides a, b and hypotenuse c, trigonometry determines the sine and cosine of the angle θ between side a and the hypotenuse as:

{\displaystyle \sin \theta ={\frac {b}{c}},\quad \cos \theta ={\frac {a}{c}}.}

From that it follows:

{\displaystyle {\cos }^{2}\theta +{\sin }^{2}\theta ={\frac {a^{2}+b^{2}}{c^{2}}}=1,}

where the last step applies Pythagoras's theorem.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Use the right-triangle definitions sin(theta)=b/c and cos(theta)=a/c, then divide a²+b²=c² by c². The hypotenuse is nonzero because the triangle is nondegenerate. This is standard high-school geometry and algebra.

Proof 2: Set opposite half-arc angles equal

For positive half-arc angles \theta_1,\theta_2,\theta_3,\theta_4 of a cyclic quadrilateral, with \theta_1+\theta_2+\theta_3+\theta_4=\pi, Ptolemy's theorem has the trigonometric form

\sin\theta_1\sin\theta_3+\sin\theta_2\sin\theta_4 =\sin(\theta_1+\theta_2)\sin(\theta_1+\theta_4).

Let {\displaystyle \theta _{1}=\theta _{3}} and {\displaystyle \theta _{2}=\theta _{4}}. Then {\displaystyle \theta _{1}+\theta _{2}=\theta _{3}+\theta _{4}=90^{\circ }} (since the four half-arc angles sum to 180^\circ). Then:

{\displaystyle \sin \theta _{1}\sin \theta _{3}+\sin \theta _{2}\sin \theta _{4}=\sin(\theta _{1}+\theta _{2})\sin(\theta _{1}+\theta _{4})}{\displaystyle \sin ^{2}\theta _{1}+\sin ^{2}\theta _{2}=\sin ^{2}(\theta _{1}+\theta _{2})}{\displaystyle \sin ^{2}\theta _{1}+\cos ^{2}\theta _{1}=1}

Take \theta_1=\theta and \theta_2=\pi/2-\theta.
