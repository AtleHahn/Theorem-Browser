Statement

Let q\ge1 be an integer, let X be a complex analytic space, not necessarily reduced, and let \mathcal F be a coherent analytic sheaf on X. The following finiteness and vanishing assertions hold:

  • If X is q-pseudoconvex, then \dim_{\mathbb C}H^i(X,\mathcal F)<\infty for every integer i\ge q.
  • If X is q-pseudoconcave, then \dim_{\mathbb C}H^i(X,\mathcal F)<\infty for every integer 0\le i<\operatorname{codh}(\mathcal F)-q.
  • If X is q-complete, then H^i(X,\mathcal F)=0 for every integer i\ge q.

Here q-pseudoconvex means that X admits a smooth real exhaustion function \varphi which is q-convex outside a compact subset. Exhaustion means that \{x:\varphi(x)<c\} is relatively compact in X for every real c. The space is q-complete when it admits such an exhaustion that is q-convex everywhere.

Here q-pseudoconcave uses the superlevel convention: there is a smooth function \varphi:X\to(0,\infty), q-convex outside a compact subset, for which \{x:\varphi(x)>c\} is relatively compact for every c>0. The homological codimension is \operatorname{codh}(\mathcal F)=\inf_{x\in X}\operatorname{depth}_{\mathcal O_{X,x}}\mathcal F_x. For a nonzero finitely generated module M over a local Noetherian ring (R,\mathfrak m), depth is the maximum length of an M-regular sequence in \mathfrak m: each element acts injectively on the quotient by the preceding elements and the final quotient is nonzero. The zero module has depth +\infty.

Here a complex analytic space may have nilpotents in its structure sheaf; it is not required to be reduced or smooth. A coherent analytic sheaf is a locally finitely presented module over that structure sheaf. H^i(X,\mathcal F) denotes sheaf cohomology, the ith right derived functor of global sections. Use the following q-convexity convention. A real C^\infty function on an analytic space is q-convex at a point if, in a local embedding into an open subset of \mathbb C^N, it is the restriction of a smooth function whose Levi matrix (\partial^2\varphi/\partial z_j\partial\bar z_k) has at least N-q+1 positive eigenvalues at that point. Smooth functions are understood on the underlying reduced space through such local embeddings; the sheaf \mathcal F and its cohomology still use the full analytic-space structure.

Level justification

All three clauses require coherent sheaves and derived-functor cohomology on possibly nonreduced analytic spaces, q-convexity and pseudoconcavity, and the local algebra of depth. These are several interdependent theories beyond the cumulative undergraduate reading allowance, even with explicit definitions.
