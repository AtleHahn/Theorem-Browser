Statement

Let s\in\mathbb Z and let p_1,\ldots,p_m be a finite list of primes congruent to 1 modulo 4, where m\ge0 and repetitions are allowed. Then s^2p_1\cdots p_m is a sum of two integer squares.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement involves only elementary divisibility and integer squares.

Proof 1: Combine Fermat’s theorem with two-square multiplication outline

Use Fermat’s theorem that each prime congruent to 1 modulo 4 is a sum of two integer squares. In conjunction with the Brahmagupta–Fibonacci identity (a^2+b^2)(c^2+d^2)=(ac-bd)^2+(ad+bc)^2, this proves that a square times any number of such primes is a sum of two squares. The identity itself is verified by expansion.
