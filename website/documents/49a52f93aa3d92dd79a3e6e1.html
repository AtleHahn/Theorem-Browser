Statement

If H is a normal subgroup of a finite group G with \gcd(|H|,[G:H])=1, all complements of H in G are conjugate. A complement is a subgroup K with G=HK and H\cap K=\{1\}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory group theory supplies normal subgroups, cosets, kernels, actions and automorphisms. Complement, normalizer, centralizer or fixed-point sets are explicitly defined by these operations, so no representation or extension-field theory is needed.

Proof 1: Remove the solvability condition with Feit–Thompson

Use the external conjugacy part of Schur–Zassenhaus under the additional assumption that H or G/H is solvable. Their orders are coprime, so at least one is odd. Feit–Thompson says every finite odd-order group is solvable, so the additional assumption always holds.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Coprime orders make either the subgroup or quotient odd, so apply odd-order solvability and then the conditional conjugacy theorem. Undergraduate group theory suffices; complements are defined locally, and neither deep theorem is proved.
