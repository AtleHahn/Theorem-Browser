Statement

Let A,B,C,D be the vertices, in order, of a plane quadrilateral. The segment joining the midpoints of AB,CD and the segment joining the midpoints of BC,DA both have midpoint S=(A+B+C+D)/4, the centroid of its four vertices.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Edge midpoints and the average of four planar vertices use elementary coordinate geometry.

Proof 1: Specialize the equal-pair construction to a plane — outline

Use the weighted-centroid identity: if groups of m and k points have centroids S_X,S_Y, their joint centroid is

S=\frac{m}{m+k}S_X+\frac{k}{m+k}S_Y.

Take the coplanar specialization of the two-pair centroid construction. For either pairing into opposite edges, the weighted-centroid identity with equal group sizes places the centroid of the four vertices at the midpoint of the segment joining the two edge midpoints. Thus both bimedians are bisected by this centroid.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Apply the supplied centroid arithmetic with two equally sized groups, separately for each opposite-edge pairing. School coordinate averages and midpoint geometry suffice.

The source supplies the coplanar specialization; the equal-pair substitution is routine. No uniqueness of intersection is claimed when the two segments degenerate.
