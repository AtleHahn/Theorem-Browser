Statement

Let H,G be finite simple graphs with the same number of vertices. Let B\subseteq V(H) consist of vertices at pairwise graph distance at least 4 (vertices in distinct components may have infinite distance). Suppose an injective map \phi:V(H)\setminus B\to V(G) already preserves every edge whose endpoints are outside B. For b\in B, let F_b be the unused vertices of G adjacent to \phi(y) for every neighbor y of b in H. Then \phi extends to an injective edge-preserving map of all of H into G if and only if

\left|\bigcup_{b\in S}F_b\right|\geq |S|\qquad(S\subseteq B).

Proof 1: Match buffer vertices to their free spots

Consider the set of vertices left to be embedded, which is precisely B, and the set of free spots \bigcup_{b\in B}F_b. Form a bipartite graph between these two sets, joining each b\in B to F_b, and find a matching covering B in this bipartite graph. Embed according to this matching.

By Hall's marriage theorem, this phase fails if and only if Hall's condition is violated. For this to happen, there must be some S\subseteq B such that

\left|\bigcup_{b\in S}F_b\right|<|S|.

Here the buffer vertices are pairwise nonadjacent, and their free spots impose all adjacency conditions to the already embedded neighbors.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Construct a bipartite graph of unembedded vertices and their permissible unused images, then apply the stated Hall criterion. The separation assumption implies no two buffer vertices are adjacent, so no additional edge constraints arise between newly assigned vertices. Finite graph embeddings and Hall matching are a small extension of undergraduate discrete mathematics, without the general blow-up lemma.
