Statement

On S=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\} let f_{2k}(z)=1/2 and f_{2k-1}(z)=-1/2 for k\ge1. All maps are analytic and have images in the common compact subset \{-1/2,1/2\}\subset S, but G_n=f_n\circ\cdots\circ f_1 does not converge at any point of S.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Calculus and complex arithmetic suffice after learning that holomorphic means complex differentiable. The statements concern explicit constant or polynomial maps, or coefficients of a given convergent power series; no further complex-analytic machinery is needed to understand the claim.

Proof 1: Alternating constant outer maps

The example f_{2n}(z)=1/2 and f_{2n-1}(z)=-1/2 for S=\{z:|z|<1\} demonstrates the inadequacy of simply requiring contraction into a compact subset for convergence of the backward compositions G_n=f_n\circ\cdots\circ f_1.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Each composition equals its outermost constant map, so the values alternate between -1/2 and 1/2 and cannot converge. Verifying the full example also uses that constant complex functions are analytic and finite subsets are compact. These are direct undergraduate analysis facts; the complex derivative of a constant is zero, so no substantial complex-analysis course is needed.
