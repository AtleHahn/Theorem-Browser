Statement

Let R be a unique factorization domain and f,g nonzero polynomials in R[x_1,\ldots,x_n]. Let c(h) denote a gcd of the coefficients of h, defined up to a unit. Then c(fg) is associated to c(f)c(g). In particular, a product of polynomials with coefficient gcd 1 also has coefficient gcd 1.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory ring theory supplies polynomial division, gcds, units, irreducibility and characteristic. A fraction field uses ordinary fractions over a domain; content is the gcd of coefficients. These closely related definitions suffice without advanced commutative algebra.

Proof 1: Multiplicativity of polynomial content over a UFD — proof

Write \operatorname{cont}(h) for the ideal generated by the coefficients of h, and \operatorname{gcd}(I) for the principal ideal generated by a gcd of the generators of I. Thus \operatorname{gcd}(\operatorname{cont}(h))=(c(h)).

Use the content radical inclusions \operatorname{cont}(f)\operatorname{cont}(g)\subseteq\sqrt{\operatorname{cont}(fg)}.

Factoring out the gcd's from the coefficients, we can write {\displaystyle f=af'} and {\displaystyle g=bg'} where the gcds of the coefficients of {\displaystyle f',g'} are both 1. Clearly, it is enough to prove the assertion when {\displaystyle f,g} are replaced by {\displaystyle f',g'}; thus, we assume the gcd's of the coefficients of {\displaystyle f,g} are both 1. If {\displaystyle \gcd(\operatorname {cont} (fg))=(1)}, then there is nothing to prove. So, assume otherwise; then there is a non-unit element dividing the coefficients of {\displaystyle fg}. Factorizing that element into a product of prime elements, we can take that element to be a prime element {\displaystyle \pi }. Now, we have:

{\displaystyle (\pi )={\sqrt {(\pi )}}\supset {\sqrt {\operatorname {cont} (fg)}}\supset \operatorname {cont} (f)\operatorname {cont} (g)}.

Thus, either {\displaystyle (\pi )} contains {\displaystyle \operatorname {cont} (f)} or {\displaystyle \operatorname {cont} (g)}; contradicting the gcd's of the coefficients of {\displaystyle f,g} are both 1. {\displaystyle \square }
