Statement

Let H be a nonempty family of functions X\to\{0,1\}. Define \Pi_H(k)=\max_{x\in X^k}|\{(h(x_1),\ldots,h(x_k)):h\in H\}|. Fix x\in X^{2m}, m\ge1 an integer, and independently swap coordinates i,m+i with probability 1/2. Writing r,s for the two resulting halves, for every \varepsilon>0 one has \Pr_\sigma(\exists h\in H:|\widehat Q_r(h)-\widehat Q_s(h)|\ge\varepsilon/2)\le2\Pi_H(2m)e^{-m\varepsilon^2/8}, where \widehat Q denotes the average of the labels in the corresponding half.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The random experiment consists of finitely many independent fair swaps. Boolean labels, sample averages and the explicitly defined pattern count are elementary discrete/probability concepts; the assertion uses no general distribution or measurable-function integration.

Proof 1: Finite label patterns and independent signs

Let R denote the event that some h\in H has |\widehat Q_r(h)-\widehat Q_s(h)|\geq\varepsilon/2 for the two halves r,s.

Fix x=(x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_{2m}) and let t be the number of distinct label patterns of H on x, which is at most \Pi_H(2m). This means there are functions h_1,h_2,\ldots,h_t\in H such that for any h\in H, there exists j between 1 and t with h_j(x_k)=h(x_k) for 1\leq k\leq2m.

Let \sigma be chosen uniformly from the 2^m permutations that swap i and m+i for a subset of i=1,\ldots,m, and write \sigma(x)=(x_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(2m)}). Define w_i^j=1 if h_j(x_i)=1 and w_i^j=0 otherwise. The two-sample event R occurs for \sigma(x) if and only if some j\in\{1,\ldots,t\} satisfies

\left|\frac1m\left(\sum_{i=1}^m w_{\sigma(i)}^j-\sum_{i=1}^m w_{\sigma(m+i)}^j\right)\right| \geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

By the union bound we get

\begin{aligned} \Pr_\sigma[\sigma(x)\in R] &\leq t\max_{1\leq j\leq t} \Pr_\sigma\left[ \left|\frac1m\left(\sum_{i=1}^m w_{\sigma(i)}^j-\sum_{i=1}^m w_{\sigma(m+i)}^j\right)\right| \geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right]\\ &\leq\Pi_H(2m)\max_{1\leq j\leq t} \Pr_\sigma\left[ \left|\frac1m\left(\sum_{i=1}^m w_{\sigma(i)}^j-\sum_{i=1}^m w_{\sigma(m+i)}^j\right)\right| \geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right]. \end{aligned}

Since the distribution over the permutations \sigma is uniform, for each i the difference w_{\sigma(i)}^j-w_{\sigma(m+i)}^j equals \pm|w_i^j-w_{m+i}^j|, with equal probability. Thus

\begin{aligned} &\Pr_\sigma\left[ \left|\frac1m\sum_{i=1}^m(w_{\sigma(i)}^j-w_{\sigma(m+i)}^j)\right| \geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right]\\ &\qquad=\Pr_\beta\left[ \left|\frac1m\sum_{i=1}^m|w_i^j-w_{m+i}^j|\beta_i\right| \geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right], \end{aligned}

where the \beta_i are independent random signs and both possibilities are equally likely. By Hoeffding's inequality, this is at most 2e^{-m\varepsilon^2/8}. Consequently

\Pr_\sigma[\sigma(x)\in R]\leq2\Pi_H(2m)e^{-m\varepsilon^2/8}.
