Statement

Let d\ge1 be an integer, let f:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb C be Lebesgue integrable, meaning \int_{\mathbb R^d}|f(x)|\,dx<\infty, and let \alpha>0. There is an at most countable family of pairwise disjoint open cubes Q_j\subset\mathbb R^d, each of positive finite side length, such that, writing m for Lebesgue measure and \Omega=\bigcup_j Q_j,

\alpha\le\frac{1}{m(Q_j)}\int_{Q_j}|f(x)|\,dx\le2^d\alpha \quad\text{for every }j,

and

|f(x)|\le\alpha\quad\text{for almost every }x\in\mathbb R^d\setminus\Omega.

The union may be empty. Almost every means except on a set of Lebesgue measure zero.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The full assertion concerns arbitrary Lebesgue-integrable functions, null exceptional sets and countably many measurable pieces. From the specified baseline of introductory real analysis, the cumulative measure/integration preparation is not confidently within the short reading allowance, even though the geometric cubes and inequalities are elementary.

Proof 1: Dyadic stopping cubes and differentiation (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (§2.5.2, Lemma 2.17, p.21).

Apply the construction to |f|. Replace each selected half-open cube by its interior; countably many boundaries have measure zero, so neither the averages nor the almost-everywhere conclusion changes. The final differentiation display has limit |f(x)|, rather than f(x).
