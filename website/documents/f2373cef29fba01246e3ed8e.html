Statement

Every finitely generated module M over a principal ideal domain R is isomorphic to R^n/K for a submodule K and a finite integer n\geq0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Modules replace field scalars by ring scalars in vector-space axioms; finite generation, submodules and quotients parallel undergraduate linear algebra and ring ideals. A PID is an integral domain with principal ideals, an additional familiar ring condition, not a demand to know the module classification theorem.

Proof 1: Map a free basis onto the generators

It suffices to construct the morphism that sends the elements of the canonical basis of R^n to the generators of the module, and take the quotient by its kernel. The morphism is surjective because the chosen elements generate M, so the first isomorphism theorem gives M\cong R^n/K, where K is its kernel.
