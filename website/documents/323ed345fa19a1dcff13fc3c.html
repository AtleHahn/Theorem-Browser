Statement

Let C,b,h>0 with 2Ch\leq b, and let F:[0,h]\times[-b,b]\to\mathbb R be continuous, bounded in absolute value by 2C, and Lipschitz in its second variable with constant L. Set y_0=0 and y_{j+1}(t)=\int_0^tF(s,y_j(s))\,ds. For 0\leq t\leq h these iterates are defined, satisfy |y_j(t)|\leq b, and \sup_{0\leq s\leq t}|y_{j+1}(s)-y_j(s)|\leq2CL^j t^{j+1}/(j+1)!.

Proof 1: Induction on the integral differences

Write R=[0,h]\times[-b,b]. The iterates are well-defined by induction: as

\begin{aligned} |y_{j+1}(t)| &=\left|\int_0^t F(s,y_j(s))\,ds\right|\\ &\le\int_0^t|F(s,y_j(s))|\,ds\\ &\le t\sup_R|F|\\ &\le h\cdot2C\le b, \end{aligned}

the point (t,y_{j+1}(t)) is within the domain of F.

We have, for j\ge1,

\begin{aligned} |y_{j+1}(t)-y_j(t)| &=\left|\int_0^t\bigl(F(s,y_j(s))-F(s,y_{j-1}(s))\bigr)\,ds\right|\\ &\le\int_0^t|F(s,y_j(s))-F(s,y_{j-1}(s))|\,ds\\ &\le L\int_0^t|y_j(s)-y_{j-1}(s)|\,ds, \end{aligned}

where L is the Lipschitz constant of F. Thus for the maximal difference

M_j(t)=\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|y_{j+1}(s)-y_j(s)|,

we have a bound M_j(t)\le L\int_0^t M_{j-1}(s)\,ds, and

\begin{aligned} M_0(t)&\le\int_0^t|F(s,0)|\,ds\\ &\le t\sup_R|F|\le2Ct. \end{aligned}

By induction, this implies the bound M_j(t)\le2CL^j t^{j+1}/(j+1)!.
