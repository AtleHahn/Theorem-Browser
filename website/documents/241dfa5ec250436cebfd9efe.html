Statement

For K=\mathbb R or \mathbb C, let H=\{(x,y)\in K^2:xy=1\} and \pi:K^2\to K, \pi(x,y)=x. The set H is closed in both the usual and Zariski topologies, while \pi(H)=K\setminus\{0\} is closed in neither. Thus the projection is not a closed map in either topology.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Basic topology supplies closed sets and projections; Zariski closed sets are common polynomial zero sets. This single added topology is used on an explicit hyperbola and line, requiring no further algebraic geometry.

Proof 1: Evaluate the hyperbola projection

The equation xy-1=0 makes H Zariski closed; it is also the zero set of a continuous polynomial in the usual topology. A point with xy=1 has x\ne0, and each x\ne0 occurs with y=1/x, so \pi(H)=K\setminus\{0\}. This omits the usual limit point 0. In the Zariski topology of the affine line over the infinite field K, proper closed sets are finite, whereas K\setminus\{0\} is infinite and proper.

Compute the projection as the punctured line and compare usual and polynomial-zero closedness. Undergraduate topology and polynomial algebra suffice with the Zariski closed-set definition as a small concrete extension.
