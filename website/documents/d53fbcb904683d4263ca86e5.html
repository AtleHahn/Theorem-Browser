Statement

Let k be a nonzero commutative ring with identity and let I=(z^2-xy-yx,zx-xz,zy-yz) in k\langle x,y,z\rangle. Use degree-lexicographic order with x<y<z and the corresponding reductions z^2\mapsto xy+yx, zx\mapsto xz, zy\mapsto yz. The word z^2x has two distinct reduced expressions, so these three relations do not form a Gröbner basis for I. Here Gröbner basis means the reduced-word basis criterion of the monic diamond lemma.

Proof 1: Exhibit distinct reduced expressions

Diamond lemma (external prerequisite)

For monic word reductions decreasing such an order, if every overlap or inclusion ambiguity is resolvable, the reduced words form a basis of the quotient algebra. Equivalently, every element has a unique reduced expression. An overlap ambiguity consists of two overlapping occurrences of leading words, and an inclusion ambiguity of one occurrence inside another. It is resolvable when reducing either occurrence can be followed by reductions to the same expression.

Two reductions

The word z^2x can first be reduced at z^2, giving

{\displaystyle z^{2}x=(xy+yx)x=xyx+yx^{2},}

Alternatively reduce at zx first:

{\displaystyle z^{2}x=zxz=xz^{2}=x(xy+yx)=x^{2}y+xyx.}

Both answers avoid all three leading words and hence are reduced. They are distinct in the free algebra over nonzero k. Thus reduction is not unique; by the diamond lemma these three relations do not form a Gröbner basis.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Compare the two word reductions and invoke their interpretation via a reduced-word basis of a quotient free algebra. Free associative algebras, two-sided ideals and the noncommutative Groebner/diamond framework exceed a small extension of ordinary undergraduate polynomial algebra.
