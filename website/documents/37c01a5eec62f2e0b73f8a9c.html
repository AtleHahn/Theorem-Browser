Statement

Let \theta=\sqrt[3]2 be real, \omega=(-1+i\sqrt3)/2, and K=\mathbb Q(\theta,\omega), the splitting field of X^3-2. Its proper nontrivial intermediate fields are exactly \mathbb Q(\omega) and \mathbb Q(\theta),\mathbb Q(\omega\theta),\mathbb Q(\omega^2\theta). The first has degree two and is Galois with group C_2; the latter three have degree three, are not normal over \mathbb Q, and have trivial rational automorphism groups.

Proof 1: Apply the subgroup correspondence to the six-element symmetric group — outline

Consider the splitting field K of the irreducible polynomial {\displaystyle x^{3}-2} over {\displaystyle \mathbb {Q} }; that is, {\displaystyle K=\mathbb {Q} (\theta ,\omega )} where θ is a cube root of 2, and ω is a cube root of 1 (but not 1 itself). If we consider K inside the complex numbers, we may take {\displaystyle \theta ={\sqrt[{3}]{2}}}, the real cube root of 2, and {\displaystyle \omega =-{\tfrac {1}{2}}+i{\tfrac {\sqrt {3}}{2}}.} Since ω has minimal polynomial {\displaystyle x^{2}+x+1}, the extension {\displaystyle \mathbb {Q} \subset K} has degree:

{\displaystyle [\,K:\mathbb {Q} \,]=[\,K:\mathbb {Q} (\,\theta \,)\,]\cdot [\,\mathbb {Q} (\,\theta \,):\mathbb {Q} \,]=2\cdot 3=6,}

with {\displaystyle \mathbb {Q} }-basis {\displaystyle \{1,\theta ,\theta ^{2},\omega ,\omega \theta ,\omega \theta ^{2}\}}. Therefore, the Galois group {\displaystyle G=\mathrm {Gal} (K/\mathbb {Q} )} has six elements, determined by all permutations of the three roots of {\displaystyle x^{3}-2}:

{\displaystyle \alpha _{1}=\theta ,\ \alpha _{2}=\omega \theta ,\ \alpha _{3}=\omega ^{2}\theta .}

Since there are only 3! = 6 such permutations, G must be isomorphic to the symmetric group of all permutations of three objects. The group can be generated by two automorphisms f and g defined by:

{\displaystyle f(\theta )=\omega \theta ,\quad f(\omega )=\omega ,}{\displaystyle g(\theta )=\theta ,\quad g(\omega )=\omega ^{2},}

and {\displaystyle G=\left\{1,f,f^{2},g,gf,gf^{2}\right\}}, obeying the relations {\displaystyle f^{3}=g^{2}=(gf)^{2}=1}. Their effect as permutations of {\displaystyle \alpha _{1},\alpha _{2},\alpha _{3}} is (in cycle notation): {\displaystyle f=(123),g=(23)}. Also, g can be considered as the complex conjugation mapping.

We use the finite Galois correspondence: subgroups H correspond to their fixed fields K^H, with [K^H:\mathbb Q]=|G|/|H|; a fixed field is normal over \mathbb Q exactly when H is normal in G, in which case its Galois group is G/H. Products of automorphisms mean composition, so gf=g\circ f.

The subgroups of G and corresponding subfields are as follows:

  • As always, the trivial group {1} corresponds to the whole field K, while the entire group G to the base field {\displaystyle \mathbb {Q} }.
  • The unique subgroup of order 3, {\displaystyle H=\{1,f,f^{2}\}}, corresponds to the subfield {\displaystyle \mathbb {Q} (\omega )} of degree two, since the subgroup has index two in G: i.e. {\displaystyle [\mathbb {Q} (\omega ):\mathbb {Q} ]={\tfrac {|G|}{|H|}}=2}. Also, this subgroup is normal, so the subfield is normal over {\displaystyle \mathbb {Q} }, being the splitting field of {\displaystyle x^{2}+x+1}. Its Galois group over the base field is the quotient group {\displaystyle G/H=\{[1],[g]\}}, where [g] denotes the coset of g modulo H; that is, its only non-trivial automorphism is the complex conjugation g.
  • There are three subgroups of order 2, {\displaystyle \{1,g\},\{1,gf\}} and {\displaystyle \{1,gf^{2}\},} corresponding respectively to the subfields {\displaystyle \mathbb {Q} (\theta ),\mathbb {Q} (\omega \theta ),\mathbb {Q} (\omega ^{2}\theta ).} These subfields have degree 3 over {\displaystyle \mathbb {Q} } since the subgroups have index 3 in G. The subgroups are not normal in G, so the subfields are not Galois or normal over {\displaystyle \mathbb {Q} }. In fact, each subfield contains only a single one of the roots {\displaystyle \alpha _{1},\alpha _{2},\alpha _{3}}, so none has any non-trivial automorphisms.
