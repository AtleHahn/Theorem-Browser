Statement

Let G be a finite triangle-free planar graph. Let C be the Clebsch graph. There is a homomorphism G\to K_3\times C, and K_3\times C is triangle-free and three-colorable. Here a homomorphism preserves edges, and the tensor-product graph has vertices (i,c), adjacent exactly when both respective coordinates are adjacent in their factors.

Level justification

Finite graphs, edge-preserving maps and products are explicitly described. The Clebsch graph can be specified by its finite 16-vertex adjacency rule, a small concrete addition rather than a new theory.

Proof 1: Pair two homomorphisms and project to the triangle (outline)

A 3-coloring of a graph G may be described by a graph homomorphism from G to a triangle K_3. In the language of homomorphisms, Grötzsch's theorem states that every triangle-free planar graph has a homomorphism to K_3. Naserasr showed that every triangle-free planar graph also has a homomorphism to the Clebsch graph, a 4-chromatic graph.

By combining these two results, it may be shown that every triangle-free planar graph has a homomorphism to a triangle-free 3-colorable graph, the tensor product of K_3 with the Clebsch graph. The coloring of the graph may then be recovered by composing this homomorphism with the homomorphism from this tensor product to its K_3 factor.

The two existence theorems and the triangle-free property of the Clebsch graph are external prerequisites. The source leaves the product-map and triangle-free checks implicit.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Combine the two supplied graph maps coordinatewise, project to K3 for coloring and use triangle-freeness of the other factor. Finite graphs and the explicit product definition suffice with a small introduction to graph homomorphisms; no proof of the existence theorems is needed.
