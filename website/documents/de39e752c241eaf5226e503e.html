Statement

Let d,r\ge1 be integers and N=(d+1)(r-1). For every affine map f:\Delta^N\to\mathbb R^d on an N-simplex, there exist r nonempty pairwise disjoint faces F_1,\ldots,F_r such that \bigcap_{j=1}^r f(F_j)\ne\varnothing. A face is the convex hull of a subset of the simplex vertices, and convex hull means all finite convex combinations.

Proof 1: Apply the point theorem to the vertex images

Write [r]=\{1,\ldots,r\}. Formally, let {\displaystyle f} be an affine function from {\displaystyle \Delta ^{N}} to {\displaystyle \mathbb {R} ^{d}}. Let {\displaystyle v_{1},v_{2},\dots ,v_{N+1}} be the vertices of {\displaystyle \Delta ^{N}} and {\displaystyle x_{1},x_{2},\dots ,x_{N+1}} be their images under {\displaystyle f}. By Tverberg’s point-partition theorem, the {\displaystyle x_{1},x_{2},\dots ,x_{N+1}} can be partitioned, by their indices, into {\displaystyle r} disjoint subsets, e.g. {\displaystyle (\{x_{i}:i\in A_{j}\})_{j\in [r]}} with overlapping convex hull. Because {\displaystyle f} is affine, the convex hull of {\displaystyle \{x_{i}:i\in A_{j}\}} is the image of the face spanned by the vertices {\displaystyle \{v_{i}:i\in A_{j}\}} for all {\displaystyle j\in [r]}. These faces are pairwise-disjoint, and their images under {\displaystyle f} intersect, as claimed by the reformulation.
