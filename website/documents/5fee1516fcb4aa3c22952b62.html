Statement

For a topological space X, let L=C(X,[0,1]) and define e:X\to[0,1]^L by e(x)=(f(x))_{f\in L}. The closure of e(X) in the product topology is compact.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Basic topology supplies product spaces, compactness, connected components, metrics and continuity; linear algebra supplies projections where present. The evaluation, finite-consistency or covering conditions are explicit uses of those concepts. No weak dual topology, manifold structure or further specialist theory is needed for comprehension.

Proof 1: Closed subset of a compact product

Each factor [0,1] is compact. Tychonoff’s theorem makes [0,1]^L compact, and the closure of e(X) is a closed subset of this compact space. Therefore it is compact.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply compactness of the product of intervals and then closed-subset compactness. The evaluation map adds no new machinery beyond indexed products and continuous functions in point-set topology.
