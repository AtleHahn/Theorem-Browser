Statement

Let n\ge0 be an integer and let p_0,\ldots,p_n,q_0,\ldots,q_n be nonnegative real numbers with \sum_{i=0}^np_i=\sum_{i=0}^nq_i=1. There is an index j with p_j>0 and p_j\ge q_j.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All hypotheses and the conclusion are finite real-coordinate inequalities.

Mathematical Field
High School Mathematics
Required topics
nonnegative real coordinatesfinite sumsinequalities
Review notes
none

Proof 1: Equal sums on the supporting face — outline

Write p=(p_0,\ldots,p_n) and q=(q_0,\ldots,q_n) in the simplex \Delta^n=\{x:x_i\ge0,\sum_i x_i=1\}. Their coordinate sums are equal.

{\displaystyle \sum _{i=0}^{n}{p_{i}}=1=\sum _{i=0}^{n}{q_{i}}}

Hence, by the pigeonhole principle, for every p\in\Delta^n there must be an index {\displaystyle j\in \{0,\ldots ,n\}} such that the {\displaystyle j}th coordinate of p is greater than or equal to the {\displaystyle j}th coordinate of the vector q:

{\displaystyle p_{j}\geq q_{j}.}

Moreover, if p lies on a k-dimensional sub-face of {\displaystyle \Delta ^{n},} then by the same argument, the index {\displaystyle j} can be selected from among the k + 1 coordinates which are not zero on this sub-face.

Apply this to the smallest face containing p: its allowed coordinates are exactly those with p_i>0 (all other coordinates are fixed at zero). Thus the selected index also has p_j>0.
