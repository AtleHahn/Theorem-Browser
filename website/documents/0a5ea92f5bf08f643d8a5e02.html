Statement

Write \mathbb N_{>0}=\{1,2,\ldots\} and \mathbb N_0=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}. Let f:\mathbb N_{>0}\to\mathbb N_0 be nondecreasing and unbounded, and define

f^*(n)=\#\{m\in\mathbb N_{>0}:f(m)<n\}.

Then f^* is also nondecreasing and unbounded, and the strictly increasing sequences

F(n)=n+f(n),\qquad F^*(n)=n+f^*(n)

partition the positive integers: every positive integer occurs exactly once in exactly one of these sequences. Each sequence lists its set in increasing order.

Conversely, for every partition of the positive integers into two infinite sets with increasing enumerations s_1<s_2<\cdots and s_1^*<s_2^*<\cdots, the functions

f(n)=s_n-n,\qquad f^*(n)=s_n^*-n

are nonnegative, nondecreasing and unbounded and satisfy the counting relation above. Thus every such partition arises from this construction.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All functions take integer values; monotonicity, finite counting and complementary enumeration are explicitly described and require only elementary reasoning.
