Statement

Write \mathbb N_{>0}=\{1,2,\ldots\} and \mathbb N_0=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}. Let f:\mathbb N_{>0}\to\mathbb N_0 be nondecreasing and unbounded, and define

f^*(n)=\#\{m\in\mathbb N_{>0}:f(m)<n\}.

Then f^* is also nondecreasing and unbounded, and the strictly increasing sequences

F(n)=n+f(n),\qquad F^*(n)=n+f^*(n)

partition the positive integers: every positive integer occurs exactly once in exactly one of these sequences. Each sequence lists its set in increasing order.

Conversely, for every partition of the positive integers into two infinite sets with increasing enumerations s_1<s_2<\cdots and s_1^*<s_2^*<\cdots, the functions

f(n)=s_n-n,\qquad f^*(n)=s_n^*-n

are nonnegative, nondecreasing and unbounded and satisfy the counting relation above. Thus every such partition arises from this construction.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All functions take integer values; monotonicity, finite counting and complementary enumeration are explicitly described and require only elementary reasoning.

Proof 1: Interlacing integer crossings of an increasing polygonal line (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Sections2–4, Lemma2, Corollary1 and equations(3)–(4)).

For the finite unbounded case here, a concrete choice in Lemma 2 is linear interpolation through (0,0) and (n,f(n)+n/(n+1)). It is strictly increasing and unbounded, and never has an integer value at a positive integer. Its horizontal and vertical integer crossings therefore give the two complementary lists in the source. The counting function is finite because f is unbounded and nondecreasing; it is unbounded because, for each r, taking n>f(r) counts at least the first r terms. Conversely, s_n-n counts complementary entries below s_n. It is nonnegative, nondecreasing and unbounded when that complement is infinite. Apply the forward construction and uniqueness of the increasing enumeration of the complement to obtain the stated inverse relation.
