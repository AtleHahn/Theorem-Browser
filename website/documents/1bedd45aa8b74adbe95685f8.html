Statement

For an odd prime r, let (a/r) be 0 if r\mid a, 1 if a is a nonzero square modulo r, and -1 otherwise. There are no distinct primes b,B,p with b\equiv B\equiv3\pmod4, p\equiv1\pmod4, and (B/b)=(b/B)=(p/b)=(p/B)=-1.

Proof 1: Use the auxiliary prime in a conic and reduce modulo four — outline

Use Legendre’s conic theorem: for pairwise coprime nonzero integers A,C,D not all of the same sign, solvability of u^2\equiv-CD\pmod A, v^2\equiv-DA\pmod C, and w^2\equiv-AC\pmod D implies a nonzero integer solution of Ax^2+Cy^2+Dz^2=0. Also use the mixed-prime case: if primes r\equiv1\pmod4 and s\equiv3\pmod4 satisfy (s/r)=1, then (r/s)=1. Its contrapositive applied with r=p and s=b,B gives (b/p)=(B/p)=-1.

The assumed characters and multiplicativity give (bp/B)=(Bp/b)=1. The first supplement gives (-1/p)=1, and hence (-Bb/p)=1. Thus Legendre’s conic theorem applies to coefficients B,b,-p, giving a nonzero integer solution of Bx^2+by^2-pz^2=0. Divide out the common divisor to choose a primitive solution. Modulo four the equation is -(x^2+y^2+z^2)\equiv0. Each square is zero or one modulo four, so all three must be zero; all three integers are even, a contradiction.
