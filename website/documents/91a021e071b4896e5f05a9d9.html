Statement

On four-dimensional Minkowski space with constant metric \eta, let \phi be a smooth real scalar field satisfying the Euler–Lagrange equation for L=\tfrac12\partial^\mu\phi\,\partial_\mu\phi-\lambda\phi^4,\qquad\lambda\in\mathbb R. Indices are raised with \eta. Then j^\mu=\partial^\mu\phi(x^\nu\partial_\nu\phi+\phi)-x^\mu L has zero divergence.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement invokes the field Euler–Lagrange equation and Minkowski raised-index derivatives; the equations are not written out locally.

Proof 1: Scaling generator and quartic homogeneity

For Q, consider the generator of a spacetime rescaling. In other words,

{\displaystyle Q[\phi (x)]=x^{\mu }\partial _{\mu }\phi (x)+\phi (x).}

The second term on the right hand side is due to the "conformal weight" of {\displaystyle \phi }. And

{\displaystyle Q[L]=\partial ^{\mu }\phi \left(\partial _{\mu }\phi +x^{\nu }\partial _{\mu }\partial _{\nu }\phi +\partial _{\mu }\phi \right)-4\lambda \phi ^{3}\left(x^{\mu }\partial _{\mu }\phi +\phi \right).}

This has the form of

{\displaystyle \partial _{\mu }\left[{\frac {1}{2}}x^{\mu }\partial ^{\nu }\phi \partial _{\nu }\phi -\lambda x^{\mu }\phi ^{4}\right]=\partial _{\mu }\left(x^{\mu }L\right)}

(where we have performed a change of dummy indices and used the four-dimensional identity \partial_\mu x^\mu=4) so set

{\displaystyle f^{\mu }=x^{\mu }L.}

Then

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}j^{\mu }&=\left[{\frac {\partial }{\partial (\partial _{\mu }\phi )}}L\right]Q[\phi ]-f^{\mu }\\&=\partial ^{\mu }\phi \left(x^{\nu }\partial _{\nu }\phi +\phi \right)-x^{\mu }\left({\frac {1}{2}}\partial ^{\nu }\phi \partial _{\nu }\phi -\lambda \phi ^{4}\right).\end{aligned}}}

Noether's theorem states that {\displaystyle \partial _{\mu }j^{\mu }=0} (as one may explicitly check by substituting the Euler–Lagrange equations into the left hand side).
