Statement

Let H be a Hilbert space and U:H\to H a linear isometry, not necessarily onto. If P is the orthogonal projection onto \ker(I-U), then for every x\in H, N^{-1}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}U^nx\to Px in norm as N\to\infty.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A Hilbert/Banach setting adds completeness to normed linear algebra. Operator powers, orthogonal projection, continuous scalar functionals and unit-ball norm bounds are direct extensions. The statements use only these explicit identities/convergence conditions, without weak topology or spectrum.

Proof 1: Decompose into fixed vectors and telescoping differences — outline

Indeed, it is not difficult to see that in this case any {\displaystyle x\in H} admits an orthogonal decomposition into parts from {\displaystyle \ker(I-U)} and {\displaystyle {\overline {\operatorname {ran} (I-U)}}} respectively. The former part is invariant in all the partial sums as {\displaystyle N} grows, while for the latter part, from the telescoping series one would have:

{\displaystyle \lim _{N\to \infty }{1 \over N}\sum _{n=0}^{N-1}U^{n}(I-U)=\lim _{N\to \infty }{\frac {1}{N}}\left(I-U^{N}\right)=0}

The orthogonal decomposition is used as a prerequisite. Passage from the range to its closure uses the uniform bound on the averaging operators; the source leaves that step implicit.

Use the explicitly supplied orthogonal decomposition, telescope on the range and approximate its closure using norm bounds. Inner-product linear algebra and metric completeness suffice with the stated decomposition consequence; no spectral or measure-theoretic ergodic theory is used.
