Statement

For a prime p and nonzero integer u, let \nu_p(u) be the largest k\ge0 with p^k\mid u. Let p be odd, x,y\in\mathbb Z, n\ge1, p\nmid xy, p\mid x-y, and x^n\ne y^n. Then \nu_p(x^n-y^n)=\nu_p(x-y)+\nu_p(n).

Level
High school only
Level justification

The valuation is explicitly defined using elementary divisibility; the result is high-school number theory.

Proof 1: Binomial one-step lift and induction — outline

First use the coprime-exponent factorization: if p\nmid b and u\equiv v\not\equiv0\pmod p, then

u^{b-1}+u^{b-2}v+\cdots+v^{b-1}\equiv bu^{b-1}\not\equiv0\pmod p,

so \nu_p(u^b-v^b)=\nu_p(u-v).

Via the binomial expansion, the substitution {\displaystyle y=x+kp} can be used in the difference-of-powers quotient Q=x^{p-1}+x^{p-2}y+\cdots+y^{p-1} to show that {\displaystyle \nu _{p}(x^{p}-y^{p})=\nu _{p}(x-y)+1} because Q is a multiple of {\displaystyle p} but not {\displaystyle p^{2}}. Then, if {\displaystyle n} is written as {\displaystyle p^{a}b} where {\displaystyle p\nmid b}, the base case gives {\displaystyle \nu _{p}(x^{n}-y^{n})=\nu _{p}((x^{p^{a}})^{b}-(y^{p^{a}})^{b})=\nu _{p}(x^{p^{a}}-y^{p^{a}})}. By induction on {\displaystyle a},

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\nu _{p}(x^{p^{a}}-y^{p^{a}})&=\nu _{p}(((\dots (x^{p})^{p}\dots ))^{p}-((\dots (y^{p})^{p}\dots ))^{p})\ {\text{(exponentiation used }}a{\text{ times per term)}}\\&=\nu _{p}(x-y)+a\end{aligned}}}

Since a=\nu_p(n), this is the asserted formula. The source leaves the collection of binomial coefficients modulo p^2 implicit.

Level justification

Remove the coprime exponent by factorization, use a binomial calculation modulo p squared for one lift and iterate. Elementary modular arithmetic, binomial expansion and induction suffice, including the coefficient collection left implicit.
