Statement

Let X be a real martingale or a nonnegative real submartingale on a finite discrete index set ending at T, or on [0,T]. In continuous time assume almost surely right-continuous paths. If p\ge1 and \mathbb E|X_t|^p<\infty for every index, then |X_t|^p is a submartingale.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the assertion requires stochastic-process martingale definitions and integrability of absolute powers.

Proof 1: Apply conditional Jensen to the convex power — outline

Conditional Jensen's inequality implies that {\displaystyle |X_{t}|^{p}} is a submartingale for any number {\displaystyle p\geq 1}, provided that these new random variables all have finite integral.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the argument requires a filtration, conditional expectation and the martingale/submartingale inequalities, all interpreted for integrable random variables. Conditional Jensen supplies the convex-power inequality; for a nonnegative submartingale one additionally uses that t^p is increasing on the nonnegative half-line. Adaptedness and, in continuous time, right continuity are preserved by the continuous power map. These prerequisites exceed elementary probability and the undergraduate baseline.

Statement used:

For an integrable random variable Y with integrable convex phi(Y), phi(E[Y|F])<=E[phi(Y)|F] almost surely.

Application in proof:

Use phi(t)=|t|^p and F=F_s. For a martingale E[X_t|F_s]=X_s. For a nonnegative submartingale, X_s<=E[X_t|F_s] and monotonicity of phi on [0,infinity) gives the remaining inequality.

Application level:

Beyond undergraduate level

Level justification:

The conditional expectation is relative to a sigma-algebra and the comparison is almost sure. Understanding the actual hypothesis check requires measure-theoretic probability, not merely a scalar convexity inequality.
