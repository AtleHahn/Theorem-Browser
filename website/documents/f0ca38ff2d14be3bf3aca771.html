Statement

Let \Omega\subset\mathbb R^d be a domain for which the Poincaré inequality \|v\|_{L^2}\le C\|\nabla v\|_{L^2} holds on V=H_0^1(\Omega). Equip V with the Hilbert norm \|v\|_V=\|\nabla v\|_{L^2}. For every bounded real linear functional f\in V', there is exactly one u\in V with \int_\Omega\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\,dx=f(v) for all v\in V, and \|\nabla u\|_{L^2}\le\|f\|_{V'}, where the dual norm uses this gradient norm.

Proof 1: Coercivity and Lax–Milgram

The Poincaré inequality makes the gradient norm equivalent to the usual norm on H_0^1(\Omega); use its Hilbert-space completeness. Put a(u,v)=\int_\Omega\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\,dx. Directly, a(u,u)=\|\nabla u\|_{L^2}^2 and, by Cauchy–Schwarz, |a(u,v)|\le\|\nabla u\|_{L^2}\|\nabla v\|_{L^2}. Apply the stated Lax–Milgram theorem: a bounded bilinear form on a real Hilbert space with a(u,u)\ge c\|u\|^2, c>0, represents every bounded functional uniquely, with solution norm at most \|f\|/c. Here c=1, giving the result.
