Statement

An equitable k-coloring of a finite simple undirected graph assigns one of k colors to every vertex, gives adjacent vertices different colors, and has any two color-class sizes differing by at most one; empty classes are allowed. Let a graph consist of one central vertex adjacent to five leaves, with no edges between leaves. Its ordinary chromatic number (least number of colors with different colors at adjacent vertices) is two, while its equitable chromatic number is four.

Proof 1: Compare bipartition sizes and split five leaves — outline

The star K_{1,5}—a single central vertex connected to five others—is a complete bipartite graph, and therefore may be colored with two colors. However, the resulting coloring has one vertex in one color class and five in another, and is therefore not equitable. The smallest number of colors in an equitable coloring of this graph is four: the central vertex must be the only vertex in its color class, so the other five vertices must be split among at least three color classes in order to ensure that the other color classes all have at most two vertices.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The center occupies a singleton color class, so each leaf class has at most two members; five leaves need at least three such classes. Coloring them in sizes two, two and one attains four total colors.
