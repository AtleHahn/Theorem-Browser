Statement

The lower bound t_2(n)\ge\lceil6n/13\rceil, where t_2(n) is the minimum number of ordinary lines for n noncollinear Euclidean points, cannot hold at n=7. An ordinary line is a line containing exactly two of the given points.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The locally defined minimum and displayed ceiling inequality use finite geometry and arithmetic.

Proof 1: Substitute seven into the numerical bound

The n=7 case is an exception because otherwise the Kelly–Moser construction would be a counterexample. This construction consists of the vertices, edge midpoints, and centroid of an equilateral triangle; these seven points determine only three ordinary lines. Thus t_2(7)\leq3. However, were the Csima–Sawyer bound valid for n=7, it would claim that t_2(7)\geq\lceil42/13\rceil=4.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The supplied seven-point configuration has three ordinary lines, while the proposed numerical bound is four. Finite plane geometry and ceiling arithmetic suffice.
