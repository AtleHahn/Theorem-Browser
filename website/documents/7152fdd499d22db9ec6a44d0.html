Statement

Let (P,\le) be a partially ordered set and p\in P. A subset E\subseteq P is dense if every q\in P has an e\in E with e\le q. Let \mathcal D be a countable family of dense subsets of P. There exists an upward-closed, downward-directed nonempty subset F\subseteq P containing p and meeting every member of \mathcal D.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Partial orders, countability and subsets are undergraduate foundations. Dense, upward closed and downward directed are direct order conditions, and density is explicitly specified; no forcing semantics is required to understand the existence claim.

Proof 1: Descending sequence of dense-set choices — outline

A \mathcal D-generic filter means a filter meeting every member of \mathcal D. For the empty family, use \{q:q\ge p\}; a nonempty finite family can be enumerated with repetitions.

Let p ∈ P be given. Since 𝒟 is countable, 𝒟 = { 𝒟i | i ∈ N }, i.e., one can enumerate the dense subsets of P as 𝒟1, 𝒟2, ... and, by density, there exists p1 ≤ p with p1 ∈ 𝒟1. Iterating that, one gets ... ≤ p2 ≤ p1 ≤ p with pi ∈ 𝒟i. Then G = { q ∈ P | ∃i. q ≥ pi } is a 𝒟-generic filter.
