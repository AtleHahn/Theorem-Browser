Statement

In a nondegenerate triangle ABC, let the internal bisector AD meet BC at D. Put a=|BC|, b=|AC|, c=|AB|, m=|BD|, n=|DC|, and d=|AD|. With k=b/n=c/m=(b+c)/a,

d^2=bc-mn=mn(k^2-1)=bc(1-1/k^2).

Level
High school only
Level justification

All terms are elementary Euclidean triangle geometry, angle bisectors and segment lengths or ratios.

Proof 1: Stewart identity and the bisector ratio

The angle bisector theorem gives b/n=c/m=k and a=m+n, hence k=(b+c)/a.

By Stewart's theorem (which is more general than Apollonius's theorem), we have

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}b^{2}m+c^{2}n&=a(d^{2}+mn)\\(kn)^{2}m+(km)^{2}n&=a(d^{2}+mn)\\k^{2}(m+n)mn&=(m+n)(d^{2}+mn)\\k^{2}mn&=d^{2}+mn\\(k^{2}-1)mn&=d^{2}\\\end{aligned}}}

Level
High school only
Level justification

Insert the angle-bisector side ratio into Stewart’s identity and cancel the positive length m+n. The remaining equivalent expressions follow from b=kn, c=km and bc=k^2 mn. All prerequisites are Euclidean triangle geometry and high-school algebra.
