Statement

For a prime p, the additive topological group \mathbb Z_p of p-adic integers does not have the no-small-subgroups property: every neighborhood of 0 contains a nontrivial subgroup.

Proof 1: Neighborhoods contain powers of p

Every neighborhood N of 0 in \mathbb Z_p contains p^k\mathbb Z_p for all sufficiently large integers k. These are nonzero additive subgroups, so no neighborhood of 0 excludes all nontrivial subgroups.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the p-power subgroup neighborhood basis in the topological ring of p-adic integers. The proof is short, but the underlying completed ring and its topology are not developed in the companion and require more than the allowed small extension; retain beyond undergraduate conservatively.
