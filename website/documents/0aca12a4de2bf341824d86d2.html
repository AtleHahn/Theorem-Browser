Statement

Let f:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb R be continuous, infinitely differentiable on (0,\infty), and satisfy (-1)^n f^{(n)}(t)\ge0 for every integer n\ge0 and every t>0, where f^{(0)}=f. Then there exists a finite nonnegative Borel measure \mu on [0,\infty) such that

f(t)=\int_{[0,\infty)}e^{-tx}\,d\mu(x)\qquad(t\ge0).

A Borel measure is a countably additive measure on the sigma-algebra generated by open subsets; nonnegative means \mu(E)\ge0 for every Borel set E, and finite means \mu([0,\infty))<\infty.

Level justification

Although alternating derivative signs are elementary, arbitrary Borel measures and their integrals require measure theory beyond the standard undergraduate baseline.

Proof 1: Derivative measures and a tight weak limit (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorem 1 and proof, pp.3–6).

For f(0)>0, apply the normalized theorem to f/f(0) and rescale its measure; if f(0)=0, monotonicity and nonnegativity give f=0. The proof’s missing tightness check is (1-e^{-xR})\sigma_n([R,\infty))\le f(0)-f(x): use \phi_n(xt)\le e^{-xt} and the displayed total mass. First choose x>0 small by continuity at zero, then R large. In the preliminary estimates, (-1)^kf^{(k)} is nonincreasing, the differences are f(t/2^k)-f(t/2^{k-1}), and (1)–(2) concern f^{(k)}, not f. These give the vanishing boundary terms in the integrations by parts.
