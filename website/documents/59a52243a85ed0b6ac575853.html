Statement

For a positive integer n, let \varphi(n) count the integers 1\le k\le n with \gcd(k,n)=1. For a prime p and integer k\ge1, \varphi(p^k)=p^k-p^{k-1}=p^{k-1}(p-1).

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement uses only positive integer powers and the locally defined totient.

Proof 1: Count the multiples of the only prime factor

For 1\le m\le p^k, since p is a prime number, the only possible values of {\displaystyle \gcd(p^{k},m)} are {\displaystyle 1,p,p^{2},\dots ,p^{k}}, and the only way to have {\displaystyle \gcd(p^{k},m)>1} is if m is a multiple of p, that is, {\displaystyle m\in \{p,2p,3p,\ldots ,p^{k-1}p=p^{k}\}}, and there are {\displaystyle p^{k-1}} such multiples not greater than {\displaystyle p^{k}}. Therefore, the other {\displaystyle p^{k}-p^{k-1}} numbers are all relatively prime to {\displaystyle p^{k}}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Exclude exactly the multiples of the single prime factor and count them. School divisibility and integer powers suffice.
