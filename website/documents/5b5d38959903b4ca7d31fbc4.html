Statement

Let n\geq3, let \Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^n be a connected open set, and let f:\Omega\to\mathbb R^n be smooth and conformal. Here conformality means that at every point x the derivative Df(x) is a positive scalar multiple of an orthogonal linear transformation.

Then f is the restriction to \Omega of a Möbius transformation of \mathbb R^n\cup\{\infty\}: a finite composition of translations x\mapsto x+b, nonzero homotheties x\mapsto c x, orthogonal linear transformations, and inversion x\mapsto x/\|x\|^2 (extended by interchanging 0 and \infty). The resulting transformation has no pole on \Omega.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Multivariable calculus and linear algebra describe the conformal derivative and all generators explicitly; the Möbius terminology needs only the given finite-composition definition. No differential geometry or Sobolev theory is needed for this classical signature.
