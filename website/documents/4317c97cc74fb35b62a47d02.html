Statement

For the closed Euclidean disk D of radius R>0, with positively oriented boundary and geodesic curvature k_g, \int_{\partial D}k_g\,ds=2\pi.

Proof 1: Boundary curvature of a Euclidean disk — proof

Let K denote Gaussian curvature. Suppose we flatten a hemisphere of radius R, whose Euler characteristic is 1, to make it into a disk. This transformation is a homeomorphism, so the Euler characteristic is still 1. However, on the left hand side of the Gauss–Bonnet theorem we now have {\displaystyle K=0} and {\displaystyle k_{g}=1/R}, because a circumference is not a geodesic of the plane. Then, using the Gauss–Bonnet theorem, we can conclude that {\displaystyle \int _{\partial D}k_{g}ds=2\pi }.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The actual proof invokes Gauss-Bonnet and topological invariance of Euler characteristic, relating Gaussian and geodesic curvature. This requires surface differential geometry beyond a small baseline extension, even though the disk integral also has an elementary direct calculation.
