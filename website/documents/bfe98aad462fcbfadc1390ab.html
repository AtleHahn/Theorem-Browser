Statement

For p>0, N\geq1 and x_1,\ldots,x_N\in\mathbb C, set

M_p(x)=\left(2^{-N}\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^N}\left|\sum_{j=1}^N\varepsilon_jx_j\right|^p\right)^{1/p},\qquad \|x\|_2=\left(\sum_{j=1}^N|x_j|^2\right)^{1/2}.

Let p_0 be the unique number in (1,2) satisfying

\Gamma\left(\frac{p_0+1}{2}\right)=\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2}\qquad(p_0\approx1.847),

where \Gamma(t)=\int_0^\infty u^{t-1}e^{-u}\,du for t>0, and put G_p=\sqrt2\bigl(\Gamma((p+1)/2)/\sqrt\pi\bigr)^{1/p}. Then

A_p\|x\|_2\leq M_p(x)\leq B_p\|x\|_2,

where

A_p=\begin{cases}2^{1/2-1/p},&0<p\leq p_0,\\G_p,&p_0<p<2,\\1,&p\geq2,\end{cases}\qquad B_p=\begin{cases}1,&0<p\leq2,\\G_p,&p>2.\end{cases}

For each fixed p, A_p is the largest lower-bound constant and B_p the smallest upper-bound constant that work for every N and every such complex vector x.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Replacing probabilistic expectation by its finite average leaves finite sums, complex numbers, powers and a defined improper integral. These are within calculus, linear algebra and introductory analysis; no measure-theoretic prerequisite is needed for this finite formulation.
