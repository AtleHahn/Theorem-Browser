Statement

Let k,n_1,\ldots,n_k be positive integers and put n=n_1+\cdots+n_k. For each i, introduce a block of variables x_{i,0},\ldots,x_{i,n_i}. Let F_1,\ldots,F_n be complex polynomials that are homogeneous of degree d_{j,i}\geq0 in the ith block for each j.

Consider their common zero locus in

\mathbb P^{n_1}(\mathbb C)\times\cdots\times\mathbb P^{n_k}(\mathbb C).

In each factor the coordinates form a nonzero vector and are identified under multiplication by a nonzero scalar. The zero locus either has positive dimension or is finite. In the finite case its points, counted with their algebraic intersection multiplicities, number

B=[t_1^{n_1}\cdots t_k^{n_k}]\prod_{j=1}^n\left(\sum_{i=1}^k d_{j,i}t_i\right),

where the bracket denotes the indicated polynomial coefficient. At an isolated common zero the multiplicity is the complex dimension of the local ring modulo the ideal generated by the dehomogenized equations in an affine coordinate chart.

Proof 1: Koszul resolution and multidegree finite differences (external)

The following editorial argument completes the stated scope. A supporting source is The Stacks Project: Koszul regular sequences and cohomology of projective space (Tag 062F, regular sequence implies Koszul exactness; tag 01XS projective-space cohomology; supplied multiprojective Euler-characteristic calculation.). Supporting sources: Stacks Project, cohomology of projective space, Tag 01XS.

Here is an editorial synthesis of the cited standard lemmas. On X=product P^(n_i), each F_j is a section of L_j=O(d_(j,1),...,d_(j,k)). If the common zero scheme Z is finite, at each zero the n equations are a system of parameters in the regular local ring of dimension n; hence a regular sequence because the ring is Cohen–Macaulay. Outside Z one equation is a unit. Thus the Koszul complex of these sections is a locally free resolution of O_Z (also when Z is empty). Twist it by O(m_1,...,m_k) for all m_i sufficiently large, and take Euler characteristics. Projective-space cohomology and Kunneth give length(Z)=sum_{J subset {1,...,n}}(-1)^|J| product_i binomial(m_i+n_i-sum_{j in J}d_(j,i), n_i). The left side is the sum of the local complex dimensions in the statement, including nilpotents. The right side is the iterated finite difference, in directions d_1,...,d_n, of the polynomial P(m)=product_i binomial(m_i+n_i,n_i), of total degree n. Only its top homogeneous part product_i m_i^(n_i)/n_i! survives; order-n finite differences equal the corresponding directional derivatives. Applying product_j(sum_i d_(j,i) partial/partial m_i) yields exactly [t_1^(n_1)...t_k^(n_k)]product_j(sum_i d_(j,i)t_i). This proves the claimed equality without any genericity assumption. A zero equation with a nonempty finite zero scheme is impossible by the local dimension bound; empty intersections are already handled by the exact Koszul complex. Nonnegative and zero multidegrees therefore cause no exception.
