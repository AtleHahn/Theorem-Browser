Statement

Let k,n_1,\ldots,n_k be positive integers and put n=n_1+\cdots+n_k. For each i, introduce a block of variables x_{i,0},\ldots,x_{i,n_i}. Let F_1,\ldots,F_n be complex polynomials that are homogeneous of degree d_{j,i}\geq0 in the ith block for each j.

Consider their common zero locus in

\mathbb P^{n_1}(\mathbb C)\times\cdots\times\mathbb P^{n_k}(\mathbb C).

In each factor the coordinates form a nonzero vector and are identified under multiplication by a nonzero scalar. The zero locus either has positive dimension or is finite. In the finite case its points, counted with their algebraic intersection multiplicities, number

B=[t_1^{n_1}\cdots t_k^{n_k}]\prod_{j=1}^n\left(\sum_{i=1}^k d_{j,i}t_i\right),

where the bracket denotes the indicated polynomial coefficient. At an isolated common zero the multiplicity is the complex dimension of the local ring modulo the ideal generated by the dehomogenized equations in an affine coordinate chart.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The full multiplicity-counted conclusion requires local algebra and projective intersection theory; counting formal coefficients alone does not determine its statement level.
