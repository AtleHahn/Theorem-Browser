Statement

Let n\geq1 be an integer and let \mu=(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_r) be a partition of n, meaning \mu_1\geq\cdots\geq\mu_r\geq1 and \sum_i\mu_i=n. Its diagram is the set of n pairs

D(\mu)=\{(i-1,j-1):1\leq i\leq r,\ 1\leq j\leq\mu_i\}.

List these pairs in any order as (p_1,q_1),\ldots,(p_n,q_n), and form the polynomial in 2n independent variables

\Delta_\mu(x_1,\ldots,x_n,y_1,\ldots,y_n)=\det\bigl(x_i^{p_j}y_i^{q_j}\bigr)_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\in\mathbb C[x_1,\ldots,x_n,y_1,\ldots,y_n].

Let V_\mu be the complex vector space spanned by all its mixed partial derivatives of all orders, including the order-zero derivative:

V_\mu=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}\left\{\left(\prod_{i=1}^n\partial_{x_i}^{a_i}\partial_{y_i}^{b_i}\right)\Delta_\mu:\ a_i,b_i\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\right\}.

Then

\dim_{\mathbb C}V_\mu=n!.

The space is independent of the ordering of the diagram pairs, since reordering them changes the determinant only by a sign.

Level justification

The actual statement uses determinants, partial derivatives and dimensions from standard undergraduate linear algebra and calculus. The only extra indexing device is a partition diagram, defined explicitly as a finite set of integer pairs. No Hilbert schemes, representation theory or Macdonald-polynomial background is needed to understand this dimension assertion.
