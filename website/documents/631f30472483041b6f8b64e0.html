Statement

Let R\subseteq A\times B be a relation. Products are read left to right: for P\subseteq U\times V and Q\subseteq V\times W, (u,w)\in PQ if some v\in V satisfies (u,v)\in P and (v,w)\in Q. Put R^{\mathsf T}=\{(b,a):(a,b)\in R\}, \bar R=(A\times B)\setminus R and

X=R\backslash R=\overline{R^{\mathsf T}\bar R},

where the outer complement is in B\times B and \backslash here denotes a residual, not set difference. Then X is a preorder on B: it is reflexive and transitive.

Sets, relations, composition and complements are familiar foundations. Every nonstandard query/residual term is explicitly defined, so the assertions can be understood by ordinary quantifiers and set operations.

Proof 1: Zero diagonal and residual transitivity

Use the residual characterization recalled in the source: X=R\backslash R is the largest relation Q\subseteq B\times B such that RQ\subseteq R. Also use the Schröder rule, in the typed instance

RQ\subseteq R\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad R^{\mathsf T}\bar R\subseteq\bar Q.

These are explicit relation-calculus prerequisites.

Reflexivity

Put I_B=\{(b,b):b\in B\}.

Forming the diagonal of {\displaystyle R^{\textsf {T}}{\bar {R}}}, the corresponding row of {\displaystyle R^{\textsf {T}}} and column of {\displaystyle {\bar {R}}} will be of opposite logical values, so the diagonal is all zeros. Then

{\displaystyle R^{\textsf {T}}{\bar {R}}\subseteq {\bar {I}_B}\implies I_B\subseteq {\overline {R^{\textsf {T}}{\bar {R}}}}=R\backslash R}, so that {\displaystyle R\backslash R} is a reflexive relation.

For arbitrary indexing sets, this zero-diagonal observation means that no a simultaneously satisfies aRb and \neg aRb.

Transitivity

To show transitivity, one requires that {\displaystyle (R\backslash R)(R\backslash R)\subseteq R\backslash R.} Recall that {\displaystyle X=R\backslash R} is the largest relation such that {\displaystyle RX\subseteq R.} Then

{\displaystyle R(R\backslash R)\subseteq R}

{\displaystyle R(R\backslash R)(R\backslash R)\subseteq R} (repeat)

{\displaystyle \equiv R^{\textsf {T}}{\bar {R}}\subseteq {\overline {(R\backslash R)(R\backslash R)}}} (Schröder's rule)

{\displaystyle \equiv (R\backslash R)(R\backslash R)\subseteq {\overline {R^{\textsf {T}}{\bar {R}}}}} (complementation)

{\displaystyle \equiv (R\backslash R)(R\backslash R)\subseteq R\backslash R.} (definition)
