Statement

Let X\subseteq\mathbb R be open and (\Omega,\mathcal A,\mu) a measure space. Suppose f:X\times\Omega\to\mathbb R has each section f(x,\cdot) integrable; outside one null set f_x(x,\omega) exists for every x\in X; and outside one null set |f_x(x,\omega)|\le\theta(\omega) for every x, where \theta\ge0 is integrable. Then F(x)=\int_\Omega f(x,\omega)\,d\mu is differentiable and F'(x)=\int_\Omega f_x(x,\omega)\,d\mu. The derivative section may be defined as zero on the exceptional null set.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement quantifies over a measure space and almost-everywhere parameter derivatives dominated by a Lebesgue-integrable function.

Proof 1: Dominate difference quotients using the mean value theorem

Fix x\in X and restrict all increments to be small enough that the interval between x and x+h lies in X. Let N be a common measurable null set outside which the derivative exists and the bound by \theta holds for every x.

Let

u(x)=\int_\Omega f(x,\omega)\,d\mu(\omega).

By the definition of the derivative,

u'(x)=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{u(x+h)-u(x)}h,

provided the limit exists. Substitute the integral expression for u. The difference of two integrals equals the integral of the difference, and 1/h is a constant, so

\begin{aligned} u'(x)&=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{\int_\Omega f(x+h,\omega)\,d\mu(\omega)-\int_\Omega f(x,\omega)\,d\mu(\omega)}h\\ &=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{\int_\Omega(f(x+h,\omega)-f(x,\omega))\,d\mu(\omega)}h\\ &=\lim_{h\to0}\int_\Omega\frac{f(x+h,\omega)-f(x,\omega)}h\,d\mu(\omega). \end{aligned}

We now show that the limit can be passed through the integral sign.

For each sufficiently small \delta\ne0, consider the difference quotient

f_\delta(x,\omega)=\frac{f(x+\delta,\omega)-f(x,\omega)}{\delta}.

For \omega\notin N fixed, the mean value theorem implies there exists z between x and x+\delta such that

f_\delta(x,\omega)=f_x(z,\omega).

The difference quotients converge pointwise to the partial derivative f_x by the assumption that the partial derivative exists.

There is an integrable function \theta:\Omega\to\mathbb R such that |f_x(x,\omega)|\le\theta(\omega) outside N. Then

|f_\delta(x,\omega)|=|f_x(z,\omega)|\le\theta(\omega).

The quotients are measurable, and their pointwise limit outside N, defined to be zero on N, is measurable and dominated by \theta.

The dominated convergence theorem allows us to move the limit inside the integral. In particular, the limit and integral may be exchanged for every sequence \delta_n\to0 of nonzero increments. Therefore, the limit as \delta\to0 may be passed through the integral sign, giving

u'(x)=\int_\Omega f_x(x,\omega)\,d\mu(\omega).
