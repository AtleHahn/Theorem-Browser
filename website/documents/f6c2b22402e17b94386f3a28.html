Statement

Let p=(p_x) and q=(q_x) be probability vectors on a finite set U. With base-two logarithms, put D(p\Vert q)=\sum_xp_x\log_2(p_x/q_x) and V(p,q)=\sum_x|p_x-q_x|, with 0\log(0/q)=0 and positive mass divided by zero giving infinite divergence. Then D(p\Vert q)\ge V(p,q)^2/(2\ln2).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Relative entropy, total variation, product distributions or infinite-value conventions require introductory undergraduate probability/information theory, even when given by finite sums. A bright US college student should be able to understand the statement after about 30 minutes of focused background research; this rating concerns understanding the claim, not proving it.

Proof 1: Binary derivative bound and coarse-graining — outline

All logarithms in the following binary calculation are base two.

Binary case

{\displaystyle P={\begin{cases}1,&{\text{w.p. }}p\\0,&{\text{w.p. }}1-p\end{cases}}}

and,

{\displaystyle Q={\begin{cases}1,&{\text{w.p. }}q\\0,&{\text{w.p. }}1-q\end{cases}}}

We assume {\displaystyle p\geq q} (other case is similar), and let

{\displaystyle f(p,q)=p\log {\frac {p}{q}}+(1-p)\log {\frac {1-p}{1-q}}-{\frac {1}{2\ln 2}}(2(p-q))^{2}.}

Since

{\displaystyle {\frac {\partial f}{\partial q}}=-{\frac {p-q}{\ln 2}}\left({\frac {1}{q(1-q)}}-4\right)\leq 0,}

and {\displaystyle f=0} when {\displaystyle q=p}, we conclude that {\displaystyle f(p,q)\geq 0} where {\displaystyle q\leq p.} Thus we have that {\displaystyle D(P||Q)\geq {\tfrac {1}{2\ln 2}}||P-Q||_{1}^{2}} for this special case.

Coarse-graining a finite distribution

Let {\displaystyle P} and {\displaystyle Q} be distributions on {\displaystyle U.} Let {\displaystyle A\subset U} be

{\displaystyle A=\{x|p(x)\geq q(x)\}.}

and {\displaystyle P_{A}} and {\displaystyle Q_{A}} be

{\displaystyle P_{A}={\begin{cases}1,&{\text{w.p. }}\sum \limits _{x\in A}p(x)\\0,&{\text{w.p. }}\sum \limits _{x\not \in A}p(x)\end{cases}}}

{\displaystyle Q_{A}={\begin{cases}1,&{\text{w.p. }}\sum \limits _{x\in A}q(x)\\0,&{\text{w.p. }}\sum \limits _{x\not \in A}q(x)\end{cases}}}

Then,

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}||P-Q||_{1}&=\sum \limits _{x}|p(x)-q(x)|\\&=\sum \limits _{x\in A}(p(x)-q(x))+\sum \limits _{x\notin A}(q(x)-p(x))\\&=\left|\sum \limits _{x\in A}p(x)-\sum \limits _{x\in A}q(x)\right|+\left|\sum \limits _{x\notin A}p(x)-\sum \limits _{x\notin A}q(x)\right|\\||P-Q||_{1}&=||P_{A}-Q_{A}||_{1}&{\text{(1)}}\end{aligned}}}

Here \|P-Q\|_1 means V(p,q), and P_A,Q_A denote the binary distributions just defined. Define a random variable {\displaystyle Z} as {\displaystyle Z={\begin{cases}1,&{\text{if }}x\in A\\0,&{\text{if }}x\notin A\end{cases}}.} Write D(P\Vert Q\mid Z) for the P-weighted average of the relative entropies of the conditional distributions on the two partition cells (zero-weight cells contribute zero). The relative-entropy chain rule gives {\displaystyle D(P||Q)=D(P(Z)||Q(Z))+D(P||Q|Z).} Since {\displaystyle D(P(Z)||Q(Z))=D(P_{A}||Q_{A})} and {\displaystyle D(P||Q|Z)\geq 0,} we have

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}D(P||Q)&\geq D(P_{A}\Vert Q_{A})\\&\geq {\frac {1}{2\ln 2}}\cdot ||P_{A}-Q_{A}|{|_{1}}^{2}&{\text{(use the special case)}}\\&={\frac {1}{2\ln 2}}\cdot ||P-Q|{|_{1}}^{2}&{\text{(use equation 1)}}\end{aligned}}}

The boundary values are understood by the same extended-divergence conventions and limiting values of the binary expression.
