Proof 1: Choose the complementary remaining half-arc

For positive half-arc angles \theta_1,\theta_2,\theta_3,\theta_4 of a cyclic quadrilateral, with \theta_1+\theta_2+\theta_3+\theta_4=\pi, Ptolemy's theorem has the trigonometric form

\sin\theta_1\sin\theta_3+\sin\theta_2\sin\theta_4 =\sin(\theta_1+\theta_2)\sin(\theta_1+\theta_4).

Let {\displaystyle \theta _{3}=90^{\circ }}. Then {\displaystyle \theta _{1}+(\theta _{2}+\theta _{4})=90^{\circ }}. Hence

{\displaystyle \sin \theta _{1}\sin \theta _{3}+\sin \theta _{2}\sin \theta _{4}=\sin(\theta _{3}+\theta _{2})\sin(\theta _{3}+\theta _{4})}{\displaystyle \cos(\theta _{2}+\theta _{4})+\sin \theta _{2}\sin \theta _{4}=\cos \theta _{2}\cos \theta _{4}}{\displaystyle \cos(\theta _{2}+\theta _{4})=\cos \theta _{2}\cos \theta _{4}-\sin \theta _{2}\sin \theta _{4}}

Formula for compound angle cosine (+)

Here take \theta_2=\alpha, \theta_4=\beta and \theta_1=\pi/2-\alpha-\beta.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The given trigonometric version of Ptolemy is applied with one angle pi/2 and the remaining complementary angle positive. Complementary/supplementary sine identities and elementary algebra yield the cosine formula. This is high-school mathematics.
