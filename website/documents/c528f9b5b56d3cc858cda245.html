Statement

Let E,F be finite-rank smooth vector bundles on a smooth manifold M, and let D be a real-linear morphism of their sheaves of smooth sections, satisfying \operatorname{supp}(Ds)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(s). The k-jet j^ks(y) records derivatives through order k in local coordinates; its vanishing is independent of the coordinates. For every point p there are a neighborhood U and an integer k such that D restricted to U is a differential operator of order at most k: D=i_D\circ j^k for a smooth linear bundle map i_D:J^kE|_U\to F|_U.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves smooth manifolds; vector bundles; jets; differential operators. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Proof 1: Local jet bound, scaling and coefficient extension — outline

The problem is invariant under local diffeomorphism, so it is sufficient to work on an open set in \mathbb R^n with E and F trivial bundles.

First we prove the local bound: for every x and C>0, there are a neighborhood V of x and a positive integer k such that j^ks(y)=0 implies |Ds(y)|<C whenever y\in V\setminus\{x\}.

Fix x and C>0 and suppose the local vanishing-jet bound stated above is false. Then there is a sequence xk tending to x, and a sequence of very disjoint balls Bk around the xk (meaning that the geodesic distance between any two such balls is non-zero), and sections sk of E over each Bk such that jksk(xk)=0 but |Dsk(xk)|≥C>0.

Let ρ(x) denote a standard bump function for the unit ball at the origin: a smooth real-valued function which is equal to 1 on B1/2(0), which is zero outside the unit ball and vanishes to infinite order on its boundary.

Consider every other section s2k. At x2k, these satisfy

j2ks2k(x2k)=0.

Suppose that 2k is given. Then, since these functions are smooth and each satisfy j2k(s2k)(x2k)=0, it is possible to specify a smaller ball B′δ(x2k) with 0<\delta\le1 such that the higher order derivatives obey the following estimate:

{\displaystyle \sum _{|\alpha |\leq k}\ \sup _{y\in B'_{\delta }(x_{2k})}|\nabla ^{\alpha }s_{2k}(y)|\leq {\frac {1}{M_{k}}}\left({\frac {\delta }{2}}\right)^{k}}

where \nabla^\alpha denotes a coordinate partial derivative, and

{\displaystyle M_{k}=2^k\sum _{|\alpha |\leq k}\sup |\nabla ^{\alpha }\rho |.}

Now

{\displaystyle \rho _{2k}(y):=\rho \left({\frac {y-x_{2k}}{\delta }}\right)}

is a standard bump function supported in B′δ(x2k), and the derivative of the product s2kρ2k is bounded, using the product rule (the factor 2^k in M_k accounts for its binomial coefficients), in such a way that

{\displaystyle \max _{|\alpha |\leq k}\ \sup _{y\in B'_{\delta }(x_{2k})}|\nabla ^{\alpha }(\rho _{2k}s_{2k})|\leq 2^{-k}.}

As a result, because the following series and all of the partial sums of its derivatives converge uniformly

{\displaystyle q(y)=\sum _{k=1}^{\infty }\rho _{2k}(y)s_{2k}(y),}

q(y) is a smooth section on a fixed coordinate neighborhood V of x, extending each summand by zero outside its ball.

We now observe that since s2k and {\displaystyle \rho }2ks2k are equal in a neighborhood of x2k,

{\displaystyle \lim _{k\rightarrow \infty }|Dq(x_{2k})|\geq C}

So by continuity |Dq(x)|≥ C>0. On the other hand,

{\displaystyle \lim _{k\rightarrow \infty }Dq(x_{2k+1})=0}

since Dq(x2k+1)=0 because q is identically zero in B2k+1 and D is support non-increasing. So Dq(x)=0. This is a contradiction.

First, let us dispense with the constant C from the local bound. We show that, under the same hypotheses as the local bound, |Ds(y)|=0. Pick a y in V\setminus\{x\} so that jks(y)=0 but |Ds(y)|=g>0. Rescale s by a factor of 2C/g. Then if g is non-zero, by the linearity of D, |Ds(y)|=2C>C, which is impossible by the local bound. Using the standard characterization of differential operators by dependence on finite jets, this proves the theorem in the punctured neighborhood V\setminus\{x\}.

Now, we must continue the differential operator to the central point x in the punctured neighborhood. D is a linear differential operator with smooth coefficients. Furthermore, it sends germs of smooth functions to germs of smooth functions at x as well. Thus the coefficients of D are also smooth at x. Here one uses the standard recovery of differential-operator coefficients from its values on coordinate polynomial sections, which are smooth across x.
