Statement

Let d,n be integers with n\ge d>1, and let V_0,\ldots,V_d be affinely independent points in \mathbb R^n. Put S=(V_0+\cdots+V_d)/(d+1) and F_i=d^{-1}\sum_{j\ne i}V_j. Then all median segments V_iF_i contain S, and |V_iS|:|SF_i|=d:1 for every i.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves finite-dimensional Euclidean geometry; affine independence; centroids of simplices; segment ratios. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Separate one vertex from its opposite facet — outline

Use the weighted-centroid identity: two groups of m and k points, with centroids S_X,S_Y, have joint centroid (mS_X+kS_Y)/(m+k). For each median, group the single vertex separately from the d vertices of its opposite facet. The identity places their common centroid on that median in the ratio d:1.
