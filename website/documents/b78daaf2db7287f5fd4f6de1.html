Statement

Let H_A,H_B be finite-dimensional complex inner-product spaces, and let V_1,\ldots,V_m be operators on H_A with \sum_kV_kV_k^*=I_A, where * is the adjoint. Define P(\sigma)=\sum_k(V_k\otimes I_B)^*\sigma(V_k\otimes I_B). For every operator \sigma on H_A\otimes H_B, \operatorname{tr}_A P(\sigma)=\operatorname{tr}_A\sigma, where the partial trace is the linear map defined by \operatorname{tr}_A(T\otimes S)=\operatorname{tr}(T)S. In particular, for a density operator (positive, trace one), the reduced state on H_B is unchanged by this unconditioned local operation on H_A.

Proof 1: Expand in tensor operators and use trace cyclicity

Any operator \sigma on H_A\otimes H_B is a sum of the form

\sigma=\sum_i T_i\otimes S_i,

where T_i and S_i are operators on H_A and H_B respectively. For the following, it is not required to assume that T_i and S_i are state projection operators: they need not necessarily be nonnegative, nor have a trace of one.

After Alice's operation P, the reduced state of Bob's system is given by the partial trace of the overall state with respect to Alice's system. One can directly calculate this operator:

\begin{aligned} \operatorname{tr}_A(P(\sigma)) &=\operatorname{tr}_A\left(\sum_k(V_k\otimes I_B)^*\sigma(V_k\otimes I_B)\right)\\ &=\operatorname{tr}_A\left(\sum_k\sum_i V_k^*T_iV_k\otimes S_i\right)\\ &=\sum_i\sum_k\operatorname{tr}(V_k^*T_iV_k)S_i\\ &=\sum_i\sum_k\operatorname{tr}(T_iV_kV_k^*)S_i\\ &=\sum_i\operatorname{tr}\left(T_i\sum_k V_kV_k^*\right)S_i\\ &=\sum_i\operatorname{tr}(T_i)S_i\\ &=\operatorname{tr}_A(\sigma). \end{aligned}

Thus, when \sigma is a density operator, statistically Bob cannot tell whether Alice performed this unconditioned local operation or did nothing at all.
