Statement

Let f:X\to Y be a function and L,R arbitrary sets. For any set A, use f(A)=\{f(x):x\in A\cap X\} , f^{-1}(B)=\{x\in X:f(x)\in B\}, and F_y=f^{-1}(\{y\})=\{x\in X:f(x)=y\}. Write L\triangle R=(L\cup R)\setminus(L\cap R). Then f(L\triangle R)=Y\setminus\{y\in Y:L\cap F_y=R\cap F_y\}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Fibers and symmetric difference are locally defined set operations; unequal fiber sections mean an input lies in exactly one of L,R.

Proof 1: Replace symmetric difference by union minus intersection

Apply the stated image-difference formula to A=L\cup R and B=L\cap R. The excluded fiber condition is (L\cup R)\cap F_y\subseteq L\cap R. This holds exactly when the two sections L\cap F_y and R\cap F_y are equal: every element of either section must be in both. Substitution gives the formula.

High school only
Level justification

Write symmetric difference as union minus intersection and identify the excluded condition with equal fiber sections. Elementary sets suffice.
