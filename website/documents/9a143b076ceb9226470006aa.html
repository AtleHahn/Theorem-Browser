Statement

For complex z and real θ, with J_n the Bessel function of the first kind of integer order, e^{iz\sin\theta}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}J_n(z)e^{in\theta}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The Bessel functions of integer order can be introduced by their explicit factorial power series. Together with familiar complex exponentials and convergence, this single special-function definition suffices for the displayed expansion without differential-equation theory.

Proof 1: Shift the angle by a quarter turn

In the cosine exponential identity e^{iz\cos\phi}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}i^nJ_n(z)e^{in\phi}, substitute φ=θ−π/2. Then cosφ=sinθ and i^ne^{in(\theta-\pi/2)}=e^{in\theta}, giving the stated expansion.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Shift the real angle by pi/2 in the supplied Bessel expansion. The substitution is elementary, but the complex Bessel function and its convergence framework are undefined in the companion; retain the inherited advanced level provisionally rather than assume these prerequisites are negligible.
