Statement

Fix a ring R and use either left R-modules throughout or right R-modules throughout. A module is an abelian group with a compatible distributive scalar action of R; a submodule is an additive subgroup closed under that action. Quotient modules have the induced scalar action, and a module isomorphism is a bijective additive map preserving it.

Let T\subseteq S be submodules of an R-module M. Then S/T is a submodule of M/T and

(M/T)/(S/T)\cong M/S,

by (m+T)+(S/T)\mapsto m+S.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Modules are vector spaces with scalars from a ring. For a student familiar with vector spaces, rings and quotient groups, the given module, submodule and quotient definitions are a single closely related extension reasonably learned within 30 minutes.
