Statement

Let a\le b be real numbers, and let f:[a,b]\to(-\infty,\infty] satisfy \liminf_{y\to x}f(y)\ge f(x) for every x\in[a,b]. Then f is bounded below and attains its infimum (possibly +\infty).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Lower semicontinuity defined by liminf; Extended real values; Real interval compactness. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Negate and use the upper semicontinuous theorem — outline

Apply the upper semicontinuous maximum theorem to -f:[a,b]\to[-\infty,\infty), which is upper semicontinuous. That theorem gives an upper bound and an attained supremum for -f, hence a lower bound and an attained infimum for f.
