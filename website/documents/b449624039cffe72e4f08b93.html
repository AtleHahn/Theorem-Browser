Statement

Let A be an infinite subset of the nonnegative integers. For an integer n\geq 0, define

s_A(n)=\#\{(a,b)\in A\times A:a+b\leq n\},

where ordered pairs are counted, including pairs with a=b. For every real constant c>0, it is impossible that

s_A(n)=cn+o\!\left(\frac{n^{1/4}}{\sqrt{\log n}}\right)\qquad(n\to\infty).

Here \log is the natural logarithm, and the displayed little-o relation would mean that (s_A(n)-cn)\sqrt{\log n}/n^{1/4} tends to zero along positive integers.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The counting is elementary; asymptotic comparison requires undergraduate limits and is defined explicitly. No analytic number theory machinery is needed to read the statement.
