Statement

Let G be a finite simple undirected graph in which every vertex has the known degree r, and let H be obtained by deleting one vertex and all its incident edges. The graph G can be reconstructed up to isomorphism by adding a new vertex to H and joining it to exactly the vertices of H whose degree is less than r.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Graph vertices, edges and degrees are locally explained; proofs use finite counting, parity and induction.

Proof 1: Reconstruct a regular graph from a vertex-deleted graph and its degree — outline

The source reconstruction recipe adds a new neighbor to every vertex of too-low degree. Deleting one vertex lowers the degree precisely of its neighbors, so the supplied recipe restores exactly the missing adjacency relations.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Identify the deleted vertex’s neighbors by their one-unit degree deficit and restore those edges. Finite graph counting suffices.
