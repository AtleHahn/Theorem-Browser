Statement

Fix a finite field \mathbb F_q and a prime \ell\ne\operatorname{char}\mathbb F_q. Assume the following weak estimate over the fixed finite field \mathbb F_q: for every even-dimensional smooth projective variety Y/\mathbb F_q of dimension D, every eigenvalue \gamma of Frobenius on H^D_{\mathrm{et}}(Y_{\overline{\mathbb F}_q},\mathbb Q_\ell) satisfies |\gamma|\le q^{D/2+1/2}, for compatible complex embeddings of the algebraic eigenvalues. Then for every smooth projective X/\mathbb F_q of any dimension d, every Frobenius eigenvalue \alpha on H^d_{\mathrm{et}}(X_{\overline{\mathbb F}_q},\mathbb Q_\ell) satisfies |\alpha|=q^{d/2}.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves smooth projective varieties over finite fields; etale cohomology; Frobenius eigenvalues; complex absolute values. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Even products, roots and duality outline

The assumed estimate is that the eigenvalues \gamma of Frobenius on the middle cohomology of an even-dimensional variety of dimension D satisfy

|\gamma|\leq q^{D/2+1/2}.

To obtain the asserted purity one needs to eliminate the 1/2 from the exponent. This can be done as follows. Applying this estimate to any even power X^k of X and using the Künneth formula shows that the eigenvalues of Frobenius on the middle cohomology of X, of any dimension d, satisfy

|\alpha^k|\leq q^{kd/2+1/2}.

Here \alpha^k occurs in the tensor product of k copies of H^d(X), in middle degree kd, and the product stays over \mathbb F_q.

As this is true for arbitrarily large even k, taking k-th roots implies that

|\alpha|\leq q^{d/2}.

Poincaré duality then implies that

|\alpha|=q^{d/2},

since q^d/\alpha is also a middle-degree eigenvalue and satisfies the same upper bound.
