Statement

Every real u\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb R^3) satisfies \|u\|_4\le C\|u\|_2^{\frac14}\|\nabla u\|_2^{\frac34}, with a constant independent of u. Norms are Lebesgue norms on \mathbb R^3, and W^{1,2} consists of square-integrable functions with square-integrable weak first derivatives.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement uses Lebesgue norms and first-order weak differentiability, rather than only classical calculus.

Proof 1: Substitute the dimension and fourth-power exponent

Use the Gagliardo–Nirenberg theorem as an external prerequisite: for integers 0\le j<m, 1\le q,r\le\infty, 1\le P<\infty, and j/m\le\theta\le1 with

\frac1P=\frac jn+\theta\left(\frac1r-\frac mn\right)+\frac{1-\theta}{q},

it gives \|D^ju\|_P\le C\|D^mu\|_r^\theta\|u\|_q^{1-\theta} for real u\in L^q(\mathbb R^n) with weak derivatives D^mu\in L^r. If j=0,q=\infty,rm<n, additionally require decay at infinity or membership in some finite L^s. If r>1 and m-j-n/r is a nonnegative integer, require \theta<1. The constant is independent of u, and 1/\infty=0.

Set n=3,j=0,m=1,P=4,q=r=2,\theta=\frac34. These parameters satisfy the displayed relation, and q<\infty and \theta<1 avoid the exceptional cases. The external inequality is exactly the stated estimate.

Level justification

Check the interpolation relation and exceptional parameter restrictions. Weak derivatives and general Lebesgue-Sobolev spaces are substantial graduate prerequisites even for this direct substitution.
