Statement

Independently draw with replacement from n\geq1 coupon types, each with probability 1/n, and let T be the number of draws until every type has appeared. Here n is an integer. Write H_m=\sum_{j=1}^m1/j, H_m^{(r)}=\sum_{j=1}^m j^{-r} and H_0=0. If k distinct types have already been obtained, 0\leq k\leq n, the expected additional number T_k of draws is \mathbb ET_k=nH_{n-k}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The sampling experiment is explicit and discrete. Expectation and variance are weighted sums over possible integer waiting times; analysis supplies infinite sums and asymptotic notation, and algebra supplies permutation cycles where used. These concrete additions do not require general stochastic-process theory.

Proof 1: Sum only the uncompleted geometric stages

Let t_i be the time to collect the i-th new coupon after i-1 coupons have been collected. This is geometric with parameter p_i=(n-i+1)/n and expectation 1/p_i=n/(n-i+1).

Let k be the number of coupons already collected, then:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\operatorname {E} (T_{k})&{}=\operatorname {E} (t_{k+1}+t_{k+2}+\cdots +t_{n})\\&{}=n\cdot \left({\frac {1}{1}}+{\frac {1}{2}}+\cdots +{\frac {1}{n-k}}\right)\\&{}=n\cdot H_{n-k}\end{aligned}}}

And when {\displaystyle k=0} then we get \operatorname E(T)=nH_n.

When k=n, the sum is empty and the remaining time is zero.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Sum only the remaining geometric-stage means and reindex the finite harmonic sum. The empty sum handles a completed collection. Basic discrete probability suffices.
