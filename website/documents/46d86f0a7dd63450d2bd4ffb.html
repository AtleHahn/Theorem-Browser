Statement

Let G\subset\mathbb C be a domain, a\in G, and D=\{z:|z|<1\}. There is at most one biholomorphism f:G\to D with f(a)=0 and f^{\prime}(a)>0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex differentiation is a direct calculus extension. Biholomorphism means a bijective holomorphic map with holomorphic inverse, and the square root is explicitly a holomorphic function satisfying h squared equals q. The remaining image, derivative and injectivity conditions are familiar analysis.

Proof 1: Uniqueness of a normalized Riemann map

If f and g satisfied the same conditions, h=f\circ g^{-1} would be a univalent holomorphic map of the unit disk onto itself with h(0)=0 and h'(0)>0. But by the Schwarz lemma, the univalent holomorphic maps of the unit disk onto itself are given by the Möbius transformations

k(z)=e^{i\theta}\frac{z-\alpha}{1-\overline\alpha z}

with |\alpha|<1. So h must be the identity map and f=g.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Compose the two maps and apply the explicit disk-automorphism formula; fixing zero removes the translation and the positive derivative removes the rotation. Undergraduate complex analysis suffices.
