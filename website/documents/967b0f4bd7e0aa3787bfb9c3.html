Statement

Let m\geq1 be an integer and let h be a real polynomial. If x_0<\cdots<x_m are real numbers with h(x_i)h(x_{i+1})<0 for every 0\leq i<m, then h has at least m distinct real roots and has degree at least m.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement uses only elementary real-polynomial algebra and finite inequalities.

Proof 1: Count roots forced by alternating signs

The values h(x_i) alternate sign on these m+1 points, and thus h has at least m distinct roots. However, if h were a polynomial of degree at most m-1, it would have at most m-1 distinct roots. This is a contradiction.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the intermediate value theorem on each of the m disjoint intervals between consecutive sample points, then use the degree bound on distinct roots. Continuity, the intermediate value theorem and the polynomial root bound are standard undergraduate calculus and algebra. No approximation theory is needed.
