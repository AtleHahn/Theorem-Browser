Statement

Let X be a Polish space and A\subseteq X. If both A and X\setminus A are analytic subsets, then A is Borel.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Analytic subsets of Polish spaces require descriptive set theory beyond calculus and linear algebra.

Proof 1: Separate a set from its complement

Use Lusin separation: two disjoint analytic subsets U,V of a Polish space admit a Borel set C with U\subseteq C and V\cap C=\varnothing. Apply it to U=A and V=X\setminus A. Then A\subseteq C\subseteq A, so A=C is Borel.

Level justification

Separate the analytic set from its analytic complement and force equality with the Borel separator. Polish spaces, analytic subsets and Borel hierarchy form a descriptive-set-theoretic framework beyond a small undergraduate extension.
