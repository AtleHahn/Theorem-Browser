Statement

For measurable f_n:X\to[0,\infty] on a measurable space, g_k=\inf_{n\ge k}f_n and f=\liminf_n f_n=\sup_k g_k are measurable.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Sets, inverse images and countable operations are familiar from analysis and topology. A sigma-algebra is a family closed under complements and countable unions, and measurability is the corresponding inverse-image condition. Here no general integration, null-set quotient or stochastic process is required; the limited set-family definitions suffice.

Proof 1: Express threshold preimages as countable intersections

Write \mathcal F for the sigma-algebra of the measurable space X. In the proof, write g_n=\inf_{k\ge n}f_k.

Recall the closed intervals generate the Borel σ-algebra. Thus it suffices to show, for every {\displaystyle t\in [-\infty ,+\infty ]}, that {\displaystyle g_{n}^{-1}([t,+\infty ])\in {\mathcal {F}}}. Now observe that

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}g_{n}^{-1}([t,+\infty ])&=\left\{x\in X\mid g_{n}(x)\geq t\right\}\\[3pt]&=\left\{x\in X\;\left|\;\inf _{k\,\geq \,n}f_{k}(x)\geq t\right.\right\}\\[3pt]&=\bigcap _{k\,\geq \,n}\left\{x\in X\mid f_{k}(x)\geq t\right\}\\[3pt]&=\bigcap _{k\,\geq \,n}f_{k}^{-1}([t,+\infty ])\end{aligned}}}

Every set on the right-hand side is from {\displaystyle {\mathcal {F}}}, which is closed under countable intersections. Thus the left-hand side is also a member of {\displaystyle {\mathcal {F}}}.

Similarly, it is enough to verify that {\displaystyle f^{-1}([0,t])\in {\mathcal {F}}}, for every {\displaystyle t\in [-\infty ,+\infty ]}. Since the sequence {\displaystyle \{g_{n}(x)\}} pointwise non-decreases,

{\displaystyle f^{-1}([0,t])=\bigcap _{n}g_{n}^{-1}([0,t])\in {\mathcal {F}}}.
