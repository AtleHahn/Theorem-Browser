Statement

Let A:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^m be a nonzero linear map, with smallest nonzero singular value \sigma. Let g:\mathbb R^m\to\mathbb R be continuously differentiable and \mu-strongly convex, and let f=g\circ A attain its global minimum f^*. A continuously differentiable function g is \mu-strongly convex if g(v)\geq g(u)+\langle\nabla g(u),v-u\rangle+\frac\mu2\|v-u\|^2 for all u,v. Then \frac12\|\nabla f(x)\|^2\geq\mu\sigma^2(f(x)-f^*) for every x.

Proof 1: Projection and singular-value bound — outline

{\displaystyle g(Ay)\geq g(Ax)+\langle \nabla g(Ax),Ay-Ax\rangle +{\frac {\mu }{2}}\|Ay-Ax\|^{2}}

Now, since {\textstyle \nabla f(x)=A^{T}\nabla g(Ax)}, we have

{\displaystyle f(y)\geq f(x)+\langle \nabla f(x),y-x\rangle +{\frac {\mu }{2}}\|A(y-x)\|^{2}}

Set {\textstyle y} to be the projection of {\textstyle x} to the optimum subspace, then we have {\textstyle \|A(y-x)\|\geq \sigma \|y-x\|}. Thus, we have

{\displaystyle f(y)-f(x)\geq \langle \nabla f(x),y-x\rangle +{\frac {\mu \sigma ^{2}}{2}}\|y-x\|^{2}}

Vary the {\textstyle y} on the right side to minimize the right side, we have the desired result.
