Statement

For positive real numbers x_1,\ldots,x_n, n\ge1, \frac{x_1+\cdots+x_n}{n}\ge(x_1\cdots x_n)^{1/n}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Understanding the inequality requires positive real numbers, finite sums and products, integer roots and comparison of real quantities.

Mathematical Field
High School Mathematics

Proof 1: Apply concavity of the logarithm

For instance, the function log(x) is concave, so substituting {\displaystyle \varphi (x)=\log(x)} in the equal-weight concave form of Jensen’s inequality establishes the (logarithm of the) familiar arithmetic-mean/geometric-mean inequality:

{\displaystyle \log \!\left({\frac {\sum _{i=1}^{n}x_{i}}{n}}\right)\geq {\frac {\sum _{i=1}^{n}\log \!\left(x_{i}\right)}{n}}}{\displaystyle \exp \!\left(\log \!\left({\frac {\sum _{i=1}^{n}x_{i}}{n}}\right)\right)\geq \exp \!\left({\frac {\sum _{i=1}^{n}\log \!\left(x_{i}\right)}{n}}\right)}{\displaystyle {\frac {x_{1}+x_{2}+\cdots +x_{n}}{n}}\geq {\sqrt[{n}]{x_{1}\cdot x_{2}\cdots x_{n}}}}
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The logarithm and exponential manipulations are elementary, but the supplied proof uses concavity and the finite Jensen inequality. Those concepts belong to undergraduate calculus; they should receive the same rating here as in the identical Jensen proof of the general AM–GM theorem. No measure-theoretic expectation is used.
