Statement

Let X be a complex normed vector space and F:X\to\mathbb C linear. Define \|F\|=\sup_{\|x\|\le1}|F(x)| and \|\operatorname{Re}F\|=\sup_{\|x\|\le1}|\operatorname{Re}F(x)|, allowing infinity. Then \|F\|=\|\operatorname{Re}F\|.

Proof 1: A complex functional and its real part have equal norms — outline

If the domain is a normed space then \|F\|=\|\operatorname{Re}F\| (where one side is infinite if and only if the other side is infinite).

The phase argument behind this assertion is as follows. The inequality |\operatorname{Re}F|\leq|F| gives \|\operatorname{Re}F\|\leq\|F\|. For the converse, fix x\in X with \|x\|\leq1. Let r=|F(x)| and pick any \theta\in\mathbb R such that F(x)=re^{i\theta}. Homogeneity of F implies F(e^{-i\theta}x)=r is real so that

\operatorname{Re}F(e^{-i\theta}x)=F(e^{-i\theta}x).

Since \|e^{-i\theta}x\|=\|x\|\leq1, we have

r=\operatorname{Re}F(e^{-i\theta}x)\leq\|\operatorname{Re}F\|.

Taking the supremum gives the reverse inequality, including infinite values.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Rotate x by a unit complex scalar to make F(x) real and nonnegative without changing its norm, then compare the two suprema, including infinity. Normed linear spaces and supremum bounds are a small extension of linear algebra and real analysis.
