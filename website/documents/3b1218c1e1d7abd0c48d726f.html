Statement

Let 1\le p<q\le\infty and let (N,\nu) be a sigma-finite measure space. Suppose W:\ell^p(\mathbb Z)\to L^q(N,\nu) is bounded and linear. For a=(a_k)_{k\in\mathbb Z} define (P_na)_k=a_k if |k|\le n and zero otherwise, for integers n\ge0. Then W^*a=\sup_{n\ge0}|W(P_na)| belongs to L^q(N,\nu) and there exists a finite constant C such that \|W^*a\|_q\le C\|a\|_{\ell^p} for all a\in\ell^p(\mathbb Z). The sequence norm is (\sum_k|a_k|^p)^{1/p}; L^q uses the integral norm, or essential supremum for q=\infty. Sigma-finite means a countable union of finite-measure sets. The countable supremum is well-defined up to a null set, independently of the chosen representatives.
