Statement

Let X be a real vector space, M\subseteq X a vector subspace, f:M\to\mathbb R linear, and p:X\to\mathbb R sublinear: p(u+v)\le p(u)+p(v) and p(tu)=tp(u) for t\ge0. Assume f\le p on M. There exists a linear F:X\to\mathbb R extending f with F\le p on X.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra supplies subspaces and linear functionals; analysis supplies norm bounds and countability. Sublinear, convex or seminorm conditions are explicitly given inequalities, and the functional norm is a unit-ball supremum. These direct extensions suffice without locally convex or dual-topology theory.

Proof 1: Real Hahn–Banach dominated extension — outline

Fix x\in X\setminus M. Given any real number {\displaystyle b,} the map {\displaystyle F_{b}:M\oplus \mathbb {R} x\to \mathbb {R} } defined by {\displaystyle F_{b}(m+rx)=f(m)+rb} is always a linear extension of {\displaystyle f} to {\displaystyle M\oplus \mathbb {R} x} but it might not satisfy {\displaystyle F_{b}\leq p.} It will be shown that {\displaystyle b} can always be chosen so as to guarantee that {\displaystyle F_{b}\leq p,} which will complete the proof.

If {\displaystyle m,n\in M} then

{\displaystyle f(m)-f(n)=f(m-n)\leq p(m-n)=p(m+x-x-n)\leq p(m+x)+p(-x-n)}

which implies

{\displaystyle -p(-n-x)-f(n)~\leq ~p(m+x)-f(m).}

So define

{\displaystyle a=\sup _{n\in M}[-p(-n-x)-f(n)]\qquad {\text{ and }}\qquad c=\inf _{m\in M}[p(m+x)-f(m)]}

where {\displaystyle a\leq c} are real numbers. To guarantee {\displaystyle F_{b}\leq p,} it suffices that {\displaystyle a\leq b\leq c} (in fact, this is also necessary) because then {\displaystyle b} satisfies "the decisive inequality"

{\displaystyle -p(-n-x)-f(n)~\leq ~b~\leq ~p(m+x)-f(m)\qquad {\text{ for all }}\;m,n\in M.}

To see that {\displaystyle f(m)+rb\leq p(m+rx)} follows, assume {\displaystyle r\neq 0} and substitute {\displaystyle {\tfrac {1}{r}}m} in for both {\displaystyle m} and {\displaystyle n} to obtain

{\displaystyle -p\left(-{\tfrac {1}{r}}m-x\right)-{\tfrac {1}{r}}f\left(m\right)~\leq ~b~\leq ~p\left({\tfrac {1}{r}}m+x\right)-{\tfrac {1}{r}}f\left(m\right).}

If {\displaystyle r>0} (respectively, if {\displaystyle r<0}) then the right (respectively, the left) hand side equals {\displaystyle {\tfrac {1}{r}}\left[p(m+rx)-f(m)\right]} so that multiplying by {\displaystyle r} gives {\displaystyle rb\leq p(m+rx)-f(m).}

Maximal extension. By Zorn’s lemma, the set of all possible dominated linear extensions of {\displaystyle f} are partially ordered by extension of each other, so there is a maximal extension {\displaystyle F.} By the one-dimensional construction just proved, if {\displaystyle F} is not defined on all of {\displaystyle X,} then it can be further extended. Thus {\displaystyle F} must be defined everywhere, as claimed.

Proof 2: Extend a positive functional on the epigraph cone

For this proof, write V=X, U=M, N=p, and \phi=f in the statement.

Use the M. Riesz extension theorem: if a convex cone K and a subspace F of a real vector space E satisfy K+F=E, every linear functional on F nonnegative on F\cap K has a linear extension nonnegative on K.

Define a convex cone K ⊂ R×V by

{\displaystyle K=\left\{(a,x)\,\mid \,N(x)\leq a\right\}.}

Define a functional φ1 on R×U by

{\displaystyle \phi _{1}(a,x)=a-\phi (x).}

One can see that φ1 is K-positive, and that K + (R × U) = R × V. Therefore φ1 can be extended to a K-positive functional ψ1 on R×V. Then

{\displaystyle \psi (x)=-\psi _{1}(0,x)}

is the desired extension of φ. Indeed, if ψ(x) > N(x), we have: (N(x), x) ∈ K, whereas

{\displaystyle \psi _{1}(N(x),x)=N(x)-\psi (x)<0,}

leading to a contradiction.
