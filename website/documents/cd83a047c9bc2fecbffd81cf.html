Statement

Let n>m\ge0 be integers and let U be a nonempty open subset of \mathbb R^n. There is no continuous injective map f:U\to\mathbb R^m.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Euclidean open sets; continuous injective maps; dimension. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Coordinate inclusion and invariance of domain

Suppose there exists such a map {\displaystyle f.} Composing {\displaystyle f} with the standard inclusion of {\displaystyle \mathbb {R} ^{m}} into {\displaystyle \mathbb {R} ^{n}} would give a continuous injection from U into {\displaystyle \mathbb {R} ^{n}}, but with an image with empty interior in {\displaystyle \mathbb {R} ^{n}}. This would contradict invariance of domain.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Compose with the coordinate inclusion and use the supplied open-image conclusion, which contradicts containment in a lower-dimensional coordinate subspace. Basic Euclidean topology and linear algebra suffice to apply invariance of domain.
