Statement

Let f be continuously differentiable on an open neighbourhood of (x_0,y_0)\in\mathbb R^2, with f(x_0,y_0)=0 and f_y(x_0,y_0)\ne0. There are open intervals U\ni x_0 and V\ni y_0 and a unique differentiable function \varphi:U\to V such that \varphi(x_0)=y_0 and, for (x,y)\in U\times V, f(x,y)=0 exactly when y=\varphi(x). Its derivative is \varphi^{\prime}(x)=-f_x(x,\varphi(x))/f_y(x,\varphi(x)).

Proof 1: Peano existence and monotonicity in the dependent variable

By differentiating the equation ⁠{\displaystyle f(x,\varphi (x))=0}⁠, one gets

{\displaystyle {\frac {\partial f}{\partial x}}(x,\varphi (x))+\varphi '(x)\,{\frac {\partial f}{\partial y}}(x,\varphi (x))=0.}

and thus

{\displaystyle \varphi '(x)=-{\frac {{\frac {\partial f}{\partial x}}(x,\varphi (x))}{{\frac {\partial f}{\partial y}}(x,\varphi (x))}}.}

This gives an ordinary differential equation for ⁠{\displaystyle \varphi }⁠, with the initial condition ⁠{\displaystyle \varphi (x_{0})=y_{0}}⁠.

Since {\textstyle {\frac {\partial f}{\partial y}}(x_{0},y_{0})\neq 0,} continuity gives a rectangle on which this derivative has constant nonzero sign, and the right-hand side of the differential equation is continuous. Hence, the Peano existence theorem applies so there is a (possibly non-unique) solution. Along a solution with the prescribed initial condition, the chain rule above gives \frac{d}{dx}f(x,\varphi(x))=0, so f(x,\varphi(x))=0. Shrink the intervals so the solution stays in the rectangle. To see why {\textstyle \varphi } is unique, note that the function {\textstyle g_{x}(y)=f(x,y)} is strictly monotone in a neighbourhood of {\textstyle x_{0},y_{0}} (as {\textstyle {\frac {\partial f}{\partial y}}(x_{0},y_{0})\neq 0}), thus it is injective. If {\textstyle \varphi ,\phi } are solutions to the differential equation with the prescribed initial condition, then {\textstyle g_{x}(\varphi (x))=g_{x}(\phi (x))=0} and by injectivity we get, {\textstyle \varphi (x)=\phi (x)}.

The same injectivity shows that these are all the zeros in the chosen product neighborhood.
