Statement

Let (X_t)_{t\in T} be a centered real Gaussian process on a nonempty index set T: every finite linear combination of its variables is normally distributed and \mathbb EX_t=0. Put d(s,t)=(\mathbb E|X_s-X_t|^2)^{1/2}, a pseudometric, and let N(T,d;\varepsilon) be the least number of open d-balls of radius \varepsilon covering T, or \infty if no finite cover exists. If \int_0^\infty\sqrt{\log N(T,d;\varepsilon)}\,d\varepsilon<\infty, then X has a version Y whose sample paths are almost surely bounded and uniformly continuous on (T,d). Version means \mathbb P(Y_t=X_t)=1 for each fixed t; the boundedness and continuity conclusion holds simultaneously for all indices outside one null event.
