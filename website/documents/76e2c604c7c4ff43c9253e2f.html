Statement

Let A_1,\ldots,A_m\subseteq\Omega, m\ge0, and put N(\omega)=\sum_{j=1}^m1_{A_j}(\omega) and N_k(\omega)=\sum_{J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\},\ |J|=k}1_{\cap_{j\in J}A_j}(\omega). An empty intersection is \Omega, so N_0=1. Let T be a linear operator on a vector space over a field and I its identity. Then T^{N(\omega)}=\sum_{k=0}^mN_k(\omega)(T-I)^k.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Sets, injections and linear/polynomial operations are familiar undergraduate material. Indicator counts and all operators are explicitly specified, with central indeterminate meaning it commutes with coefficients. No specialist structures are needed.

Proof 1: Polynomial substitution for a general operator

The pointwise polynomial identity x^{N(\omega)}=\sum_{k=0}^mN_k(\omega)(x-1)^k can be evaluated at any linear operator T. The constants act as scalar multiples of the identity, so the polynomial substitution is valid and yields the stated formula.

Level justification

Evaluate a finite polynomial identity at one linear operator. Undergraduate linear algebra and polynomial evaluation suffice, including for an arbitrary field; no operator topology is involved.
