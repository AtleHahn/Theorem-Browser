Statement

For differentiable f,g:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R^n, (f\cdot g)'=f'\cdot g+f\cdot g'.

Proof 1: Apply the dot-product bilinear calculation

The only properties of multiplication used in the proof using the limit definition of derivative are that multiplication is continuous and bilinear. The Euclidean dot product has these properties.

Let h(x)=f(x)\cdot g(x) and suppose that f and g are each differentiable at x. We want to prove that h is differentiable at x and that its derivative, h'(x), is given by f'(x)\cdot g(x)+f(x)\cdot g'(x). To do this, f(x)\cdot g(x+\Delta x)-f(x)\cdot g(x+\Delta x) (which is zero, and thus does not change the value) is added to the numerator to permit its factoring, and then properties of limits are used.

\begin{aligned} h'(x)&=\lim_{\Delta x\to0}\frac{h(x+\Delta x)-h(x)}{\Delta x}\\[5pt] &=\lim_{\Delta x\to0}\frac{f(x+\Delta x)\cdot g(x+\Delta x)-f(x)\cdot g(x)}{\Delta x}\\[5pt] &=\lim_{\Delta x\to0}\frac{f(x+\Delta x)\cdot g(x+\Delta x)-f(x)\cdot g(x+\Delta x)+f(x)\cdot g(x+\Delta x)-f(x)\cdot g(x)}{\Delta x}\\[5pt] &=\lim_{\Delta x\to0}\frac{[f(x+\Delta x)-f(x)]\cdot g(x+\Delta x)+f(x)\cdot[g(x+\Delta x)-g(x)]}{\Delta x}\\[5pt] &=\lim_{\Delta x\to0}\frac{f(x+\Delta x)-f(x)}{\Delta x}\cdot\lim_{\Delta x\to0}g(x+\Delta x)+\lim_{\Delta x\to0}f(x)\cdot\lim_{\Delta x\to0}\frac{g(x+\Delta x)-g(x)}{\Delta x}\\[5pt] &=f'(x)\cdot g(x)+f(x)\cdot g'(x). \end{aligned}

The fact that \lim_{\Delta x\to0}g(x+\Delta x)=g(x) follows from the fact that differentiable functions are continuous.
