Statement

Let D⊂C be open and f:D→C continuous. If ∮_γf(z)dz=0 for every closed piecewise C¹ curve γ in D, then f is holomorphic on D.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex differentiation extends the calculus difference quotient, and a contour integral is an ordinary parametrized integral along a piecewise smooth planar curve. With these two direct definitions, the vanishing-integral criterion requires no further complex geometry.

Proof 1: Construct a path-independent primitive and differentiate

To prove the theorem, we construct an anti-derivative for f. Since the anti-derivative is holomorphic by construction, and since holomorphic functions are analytic and have holomorphic derivatives, it follows that f is holomorphic.

Without loss of generality, it can be assumed that D is connected, by working on each component. Fix a point z_0 in D, and for any z\in D, let \gamma:[0,1]\to D be a piecewise C^1 curve such that \gamma(0)=z_0 and \gamma(1)=z. Such curves exist because connected open subsets of \mathbb C are polygonally path connected. Then define the function F to be

F(z)=\int_\gamma f(\zeta)\,d\zeta.

To see that the function is well-defined, suppose \tau:[0,1]\to D is another piecewise C^1 curve such that \tau(0)=z_0 and \tau(1)=z. The curve \gamma\tau^{-1} (i.e. the curve combining \gamma with \tau in reverse) is a closed piecewise C^1 curve in D. Then,

\int_\gamma f(\zeta)\,d\zeta+\int_{\tau^{-1}}f(\zeta)\,d\zeta =\oint_{\gamma\tau^{-1}}f(\zeta)\,d\zeta=0.

And it follows that

\int_\gamma f(\zeta)\,d\zeta=\int_\tau f(\zeta)\,d\zeta.

Then using the continuity of f to estimate difference quotients, we get that F'(z)=f(z). Had we chosen a different z_0 in D, F would change by a constant: namely, the result of integrating f along any piecewise regular curve from the new z_0 to the old, and this does not change the derivative.
