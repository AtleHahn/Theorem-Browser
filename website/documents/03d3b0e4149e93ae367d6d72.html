Statement

Let s be Schwartz on \mathbb R, S(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb R}s(x)e^{-2\pi ix\xi}\,dx, and \delta>0. Then \left|\delta\sum_ns(n\delta)-\int_{\mathbb R}s(x)\,dx\right|\le\sum_{k\ne0}|S(k/\delta)|.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Calculus supplies improper Fourier integrals and analysis supplies convergent series. Schwartz means smooth with each derivative decaying faster than every inverse power; this is a direct quantified growth condition. The transform is explicitly given, with no distribution or L2 completion involved.

Proof 1: Remove the zero Fourier mode

Poisson summation with period \delta at x=0 gives \delta\sum_ns(n\delta)=\sum_kS(k/\delta). Since S(0)=\int_{\mathbb R}s(x)\,dx, subtracting this term leaves \sum_{k\ne0}S(k/\delta). The triangle inequality gives the displayed bound.

Level justification

Apply the explicitly stated Poisson sum identity, subtract its zero Fourier mode and use absolute convergence and the triangle inequality. Undergraduate classical Fourier analysis suffices with Schwartz meaning smooth rapid decay, a small related function-class extension; no distributions are used.
