Statement

Let X be a simply connected Riemannian manifold of bounded nonpositive sectional curvature, with distance d. Suppose C,\varepsilon>0 have the following property: for every discrete subgroup \Gamma\le\operatorname{Isom}(X) and every x\in X, the subgroup generated by F_x=\{g\in\Gamma:d(x,gx)<\varepsilon\} has a nilpotent subgroup of index less than C. If F\subset\operatorname{Isom}(X) generates a discrete group and there is x\in X with d(x,gx)<\varepsilon for every g\in F, then \langle F\rangle has a nilpotent subgroup of index at most C.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The full hypothesis combines sectional curvature, discrete isometry groups and nilpotent subgroup structure, requiring substantial Riemannian and geometric-group background. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Use the subgroup generated by the prescribed isometries

The source gives the following as an immediate consequence of the small-displacement subgroup property: for any subset F of the isometry group, if it satisfies that

  • there exists an x\in X such that \forall g\in F:d(x,gx)<\varepsilon;
  • the group \langle F\rangle generated by F is discrete,

then \langle F\rangle contains a nilpotent subgroup of index \leq C.

The source states the implication without writing out the subgroup-inclusion argument. Only this implication of the assumed property is asserted here.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the assumed property to the group generated by F, whose small-displacement elements already include its generators. The subgroup deduction is short, but Riemannian curvature, discrete isometry groups and nilpotence form a substantial prerequisite.
