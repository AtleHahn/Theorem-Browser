Statement

Let a<b, let f:[a,b]\to\mathbb R be continuous, and let F:[a,b]\to\mathbb R be continuous with F'=f on (a,b). Then \int_a^b f(t)\,dt=F(b)-F(a).

Proof 1: Newton–Leibniz formula for a continuous integrand — proof

Suppose F is an antiderivative of f, with f continuous on [a,b]. Let

G(x)=\int_a^x f(t)\,dt.

By the first fundamental theorem of calculus, G is continuous on [a,b] and is also an antiderivative of f on (a,b). Since F'-G'=0, the mean value theorem implies that F-G is a constant function, that is, there is a number c such that G(x)=F(x)+c for all x in [a,b]. Letting x=a, we have

F(a)+c=G(a)=\int_a^a f(t)\,dt=0,

which means c=-F(a). In other words, G(x)=F(x)-F(a), and so

\int_a^b f(x)\,dx=G(b)=F(b)-F(a).
