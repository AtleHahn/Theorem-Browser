Statement

The number \operatorname{var}(q) counts sign changes in the coefficient sequence of a real polynomial q, ignoring zero coefficients. Let p be a nonzero real polynomial of degree d. Let a,b,c,d_0 be nonnegative integers, with c+d_0>0 and ad_0-bc\ne0, and put M(x)=(ax+b)/(cx+d_0) and F(x)=(cx+d_0)^dp(M(x)). If \operatorname{var}(F)=1, then p has exactly one root in the open interval with endpoints M(0) and M(\infty), interpreted as extended limits. That root corresponds to the unique positive root of F.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The relevant statement concepts are Real polynomials; Fractional-linear maps; Coefficient sign variations; Extended endpoint limits. Besides polynomial algebra and sign variations, the full formulation uses extended endpoint limits of a fractional-linear map and an interval root-isolation condition. These are accessible with undergraduate analysis and a brief introduction to the notation. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Positive-ray root correspondence

Represent the fractional-linear transformation by

M(x)=\frac{ax+b}{cx+d_0},

leading to the transformed polynomial

F(x)=(cx+d_0)^d p\left(\frac{ax+b}{cx+d_0}\right)

with one sign variation in the sequence of its coefficients. By Descartes' rule of signs, F has a single positive root. This root (in the interval (0,\infty)) corresponds to that positive root of p(x) that is in the open interval with endpoints b/d_0 and a/c. These endpoints are not ordered and correspond to M(0) and M(\infty) respectively, interpreted as extended limits when a denominator is zero. The nonzero determinant and positivity of cx+d_0 on x>0 ensure this interval correspondence.
