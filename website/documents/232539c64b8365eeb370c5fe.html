Statement

Let T=(T_1,\ldots,T_m)^T be a real random vector with finite covariance matrix \Sigma, let I be a real positive-definite d\times d matrix, and let \Phi be a real m\times d matrix. Suppose that for every v\in\mathbb R^m the scalar statistic h_v=v^TT satisfies \operatorname{Var}(h_v)\ge(\Phi^Tv)^TI^{-1}(\Phi^Tv). Then \Sigma\ge\Phi I^{-1}\Phi^T, where the inequality means positive semidefiniteness of the difference.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Finite-vector covariance adds scalar moment definitions to undergraduate positive-definite matrices and quadratic forms.

Proof 1: Apply the scalar inequality to every linear combination

We can take any v\in\mathbb R^m, then, defining h:=\sum_jv_jT_j, the assumed scalar inequality gives

\operatorname{Var}[h]=v^T\Sigma v\geq v^T\Phi I^{-1}\Phi^Tv.

This holds for all v\in\mathbb R^m, so we can conclude

\Sigma\geq\Phi I^{-1}\Phi^T.
