Statement

A finite simple graph has finitely many vertices, at most one edge per vertex pair, and no loops. A proper coloring assigns distinct colors to adjacent vertices; a plane graph is drawn without crossings. If every finite simple plane triangulation is four-colorable, then every finite simple planar graph is four-colorable. A plane triangulation is a connected plane graph whose faces, including the outer face, all have triangular boundaries.

Level
High school only
Level justification

A graph is a finite set of vertices joined by edges; planarity means it can be drawn without crossings, and proper coloring assigns different colors across each edge. These finite combinatorial and planar notions are explicit.

Proof 1: Add edges and restrict the coloring — outline

Graphs with fewer than three vertices are trivially four-colorable. For a plane drawing with at least three vertices, if planar regions separated by the graph are not triangulated (i.e., do not have exactly three edges in their boundaries), we can add edges without introducing new vertices in order to make every region triangular, including the unbounded outer region. If this triangulated graph is colorable using four colors or fewer, so is the original graph since the same coloring is valid if edges are removed. So it suffices to prove the four color theorem for triangulated graphs to prove it for all planar graphs.
