Statement

Assume that every product of compact topological spaces is compact. Then for every indexed family (A_i)_{i\in I} of nonempty sets, \prod_i A_i is nonempty; equivalently, the axiom of choice holds.

Proof 1: Adjoined points and finite intersections

Let \{A_i\} be an indexed family of nonempty sets, for i ranging in I (where I is an arbitrary indexing set). We wish to show that the cartesian product of these sets is nonempty. Now, for each i, take X_i to be A_i with a new point *_i tacked on, so X_i=A_i\cup\{*_i\}.

Now define the cartesian product

X=\prod_{i\in I}X_i

along with the natural projection maps \pi_i which take a member of X to its ith term. The point (*_i)_{i\in I} belongs to X.

We give each X_i the topology whose open sets are: the empty set, the singleton \{*_i\}, the set X_i. This makes X_i compact, and by Tychonoff's theorem, X is also compact (in the product topology). The projection maps are continuous; all the A_i's are closed, being complements of the singleton open set \{*_i\} in X_i. So the inverse images \pi_i^{-1}(A_i) are closed subsets of X. We note that

\prod_{i\in I}A_i=\bigcap_{i\in I}\pi_i^{-1}(A_i)

and prove that these inverse images have the finite intersection property (FIP). Let i_1,\ldots,i_N be a finite collection of distinct indices in I. Then the finite product A_{i_1}\times\cdots\times A_{i_N} is nonempty (only finitely many choices here, so AC is not needed); it merely consists of N-tuples. Let a=(a_1,\ldots,a_N) be such an N-tuple. We extend a to the whole index set: take a to the function f defined by f(j)=a_k if j=i_k, and f(j)=*_j otherwise. This step is where the addition of the extra point to each space is crucial, for it allows us to define f for everything outside of the N-tuple in a precise way without choices (we can already choose, by construction, *_j from X_j). \pi_{i_k}(f)=a_k is obviously an element of each A_{i_k} so that f is in each inverse image; thus we have

\bigcap_{k=1}^N\pi_{i_k}^{-1}(A_{i_k})\ne\varnothing.

By the FIP characterization of compactness, the entire intersection over I must be nonempty, and the proof is complete.
