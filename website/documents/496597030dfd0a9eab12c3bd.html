Statement

For x>0 define \Gamma(x)=\int_0^\infty u^{x-1}e^{-u}\,du. Then x^{-x}e^x\Gamma(x)=\int_{\mathbb R}\exp(x(1+t-e^t))\,dt.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Real exponentials and powers; Improper integrals; Locally defined gamma function. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Exponential substitution followed by scaling

Two changes of variables give

{\displaystyle \int _{\mathbb {R} }e^{x\left(1+t-e^{t}\right)}\,dt\ {\overset {t\mapsto \ln t}{=}}\ e^{x}\int _{0}^{\infty }t^{x-1}e^{-xt}\,dt\ {\overset {t\mapsto {\frac {t}{x}}}{=}}\ x^{-x}e^{x}\int _{0}^{\infty }e^{-t}t^{x-1}\,dt=x^{-x}e^{x}\Gamma (x).}
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the exponential substitution and then a positive scaling in the improper real integral. This is ordinary calculus.
