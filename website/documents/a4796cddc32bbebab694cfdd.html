Statement

The real space C[a,b] with supremum norm is separable for a compact real interval [a,b].

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Real analysis supplies continuous functions on a compact interval and the supremum norm. Countability, cardinal bounds and a countable dense subset are basic set/topology concepts, so no additional function-space theory is needed.

Proof 1: Approximate polynomial coefficients by rationals — outline

As a consequence of the Weierstrass approximation theorem, one can show that the space C[a,b] is separable: the polynomial functions are dense, and each polynomial function can be uniformly approximated by one with rational coefficients; there are only countably many polynomials with rational coefficients.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Weierstrass approximation reduces to polynomials, whose finitely many coefficients can be approximated rationally uniformly on a bounded interval. Countably many finite rational lists supply a dense set. This is standard analysis.
