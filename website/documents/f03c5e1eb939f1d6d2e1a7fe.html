Statement

Let G be a nontrivial finite group whose centralizer C_G(g)=\{h\in G:hg=gh\} is abelian for every g\ne1. Then the sets A\setminus\{1\}, as A ranges over the maximal abelian subgroups of G, partition G\setminus\{1\}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory abstract algebra supplies finite groups, actions, normal subgroups, quotient groups and Sylow subgroups. The displayed definitions of orbits, centralizers, solvability or series equivalence are direct uses or small extensions of these familiar structures; no representation theory is needed for comprehension.

Proof 1: Partition by the commutation equivalence relation — outline

In the CA case the relation "a commutes with b" is an equivalence relation on the non-identity elements. So the elements break up into equivalence classes, such that each equivalence class is the set of non-identity elements of a maximal abelian subgroup.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

For nonidentity b, two elements commuting with b lie in its abelian centralizer and therefore commute with each other. This proves transitivity; each class with the identity adjoined is the relevant centralizer and a maximal abelian subgroup. Only undergraduate groups, centralizers and equivalence relations are needed.
