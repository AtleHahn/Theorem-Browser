Statement

Let D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\} and let h:D\to D be holomorphic and injective with h(0)=0. For p>0, put \|f\|_{H^p}^p=\sup_{0<r<1}(2\pi)^{-1}\int_0^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^p\,d\theta and let H^p(D) consist of holomorphic functions for which this is finite. For every f\in H^2(D), f\circ h\in H^2(D) and \|f\circ h\|_{H^2}\le\|f\|_{H^2}.

Proof 1: Backward-shift induction followed by polynomial density — outline

Write C_hf=f\circ h, and use the H^2 norm throughout.

To prove the result for H2 it suffices to show that for f a polynomial

{\displaystyle \displaystyle {\|C_{h}f\|^{2}\leq \|f\|^{2},}}

Let U be the unilateral shift defined by

{\displaystyle \displaystyle {Uf(z)=zf(z)}.}

This has adjoint U* given by

{\displaystyle U^{*}f(z)={f(z)-f(0) \over z}.}

Since f(0) = a0, this gives

{\displaystyle f=a_{0}+zU^{*}f}

and hence

{\displaystyle C_{h}f=a_{0}+hC_{h}U^{*}f.}

Constants are orthogonal to functions vanishing at zero, and multiplication by h is a contraction because |h|\le1. Thus

{\displaystyle \|C_{h}f\|^{2}=|a_{0}|^{2}+\|hC_{h}U^{*}f\|^{2}\leq |a_{0}^{2}|+\|C_{h}U^{*}f\|^{2}.}

Since U*f has degree less than f, it follows by induction that

{\displaystyle \|C_{h}U^{*}f\|^{2}\leq \|U^{*}f\|^{2}=\|f\|^{2}-|a_{0}|^{2},}

and hence

{\displaystyle \|C_{h}f\|^{2}\leq \|f\|^{2}.}

The induction starts with constants. The reduction to polynomials uses their density in H^2 and continuity of point evaluations: the contractive extension agrees pointwise with composition.
