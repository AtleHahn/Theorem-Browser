Statement

Let r be a nonconstant rational function of one complex variable. Write its distinct finite zeros as a_1,\ldots,a_s with multiplicities m_1,\ldots,m_s, and its distinct finite poles as b_1,\ldots,b_t with orders n_1,\ldots,n_t. At a point z different from these zeros and poles, regard complex numbers as plane vectors and define the force

F(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{s}m_j\frac{z-a_j}{|z-a_j|^2}-\sum_{j=1}^{t}n_j\frac{z-b_j}{|z-b_j|^2}.

If r'(z)=0, then F(z)=0. Thus a finite critical point away from the zeros and poles is an equilibrium for repulsive particles of mass m_j at the zeros and attractive particles of mass n_j at the poles, with force magnitude equal to mass divided by distance. Empty sums are zero. Multiple zeros of r, where the force is undefined, are excluded; the multiplicity of a zero of r' away from the particles is unrestricted.

Level justification

Complex numbers, rational-function differentiation and the explicit force formula are accessible from undergraduate calculus and algebra; zeros/poles with order are a small local extension.
