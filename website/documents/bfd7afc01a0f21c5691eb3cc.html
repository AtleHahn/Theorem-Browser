Statement

Let (a_n)_{n\geq1} be nonzero complex numbers with |a_n|\to\infty. The choice p_n=n gives \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(r/|a_n|)^{1+p_n}<\infty for every r>0. More generally, for any nonnegative integer sequence having this property, finite changes preserving nonnegativity, or replacement by nonnegative integers p'_n\geq p_n, preserve the property.

Proof 1: Geometric-tail verification outline

The sequence {\displaystyle \{p_{n}\}} with the summability property in the statement always exists. For example, we could always take {\displaystyle p_{n}=n} and have the convergence. Such a sequence is not unique: changing it at finite number of positions, or taking another sequence p′n ≥ pn, will not break the convergence.
