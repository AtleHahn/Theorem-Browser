Statement

Let K be a field and a_0,a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4\in K, with a_4 not a square in K (there is no b\in K with b^2=a_4). If the equation y^2=a_4x^4+a_3x^3+a_2x^2+a_1x+a_0 has a solution (x,y)\in L^2 for a finite extension L/K of odd degree, then it has a solution in K^2. Here the degree [L:K] is the dimension of L as a K-vector space.

Review notes

  • Choose least odd degree d>1. Since [K(x,y):K(x)] divides two and is odd, y lies in K(x), so y=h(x) with degree s<d. The nonzero polynomial h squared-f has even degree max(4,2s); nonsquare leading coefficient prevents cancellation when s=2. Dividing by the minimal polynomial of x leaves positive odd degree less than d. Choose an odd-degree irreducible factor of this quotient; its root and h evaluated there give the contradiction. This corrects the source shorthand degree calculation and arbitrary-root wording.
