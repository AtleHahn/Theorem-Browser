Identifying all subsets with binary-valued characteristic functions uses classical decidability of membership. The result exposes a logical commitment behind a familiar set representation that is not automatic in constructive mathematics.

Statement

Assuming classical excluded middle, for every set S the map sending u:S\to\{0,1\} to \{s\in S:u(s)=1\} is a bijection from \{0,1\}^S to \mathcal P(S).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Undergraduate sets, functions, orders and infinite sequences suffice. Choice, well-ordering or maximal-chain principles are explicit set-selection/order assertions where invoked, not a demand to learn their independence proofs. The displayed quantifiers and constructions can be understood through familiar subsets and functions.

Proof 1: Recover a subset from its characteristic function (outline)

The characteristic function of A\subseteq S takes the value 1 on A and 0 off A.

Assuming the law of excluded middle, characteristic functions surject onto powersets, and then {\displaystyle |2^{S}|=|{\mathcal {P}}(S)|}.

Here 2^S denotes the set of functions S\to\{0,1\}; a function into \{0,1\} is determined by its set of 1-values.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The inverse assignments send a subset to its indicator and an indicator to its one-set. This requires only introductory function sets, power sets and bijections; the classical axiom is used solely to decide membership versus nonmembership.
