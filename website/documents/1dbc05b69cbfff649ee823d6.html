Statement

Let d\ge1 and 1\le p,q,r\le\infty satisfy

\frac1p+\frac1q=1+\frac1r,\qquad \frac1\infty=0.

For real- or complex-valued f\in L^p(\mathbb R^d) and g\in L^q(\mathbb R^d), their convolution

(f*g)(x)=\int_{\mathbb R^d}f(y)g(x-y)\,dy

is defined almost everywhere, belongs to L^r(\mathbb R^d), and satisfies

\|f*g\|_r\le\|f\|_p\|g\|_q.

The spaces use Lebesgue measure; for a finite exponent s, \|h\|_s=(\int|h|^s)^{1/s}, and \|h\|_\infty is the essential supremum.
