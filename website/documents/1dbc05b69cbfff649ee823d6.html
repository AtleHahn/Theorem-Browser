Statement

Let d\ge1 and 1\le p,q,r\le\infty satisfy

\frac1p+\frac1q=1+\frac1r,\qquad \frac1\infty=0.

For real- or complex-valued f\in L^p(\mathbb R^d) and g\in L^q(\mathbb R^d), their convolution

(f*g)(x)=\int_{\mathbb R^d}f(y)g(x-y)\,dy

is defined almost everywhere, belongs to L^r(\mathbb R^d), and satisfies

\|f*g\|_r\le\|f\|_p\|g\|_q.

The spaces use Lebesgue measure; for a finite exponent s, \|h\|_s=(\int|h|^s)^{1/s}, and \|h\|_\infty is the essential supremum.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Undergraduate real analysis plus the explicitly given Lp and essential-supremum definitions suffices for the statement; no group harmonic analysis is required.

Proof 1: Endpoint estimates and complex interpolation (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorem7.22, printed pp.84–85; interpolation in Theorem7.19 and Lemmas7.20–7.21).

The proof does not require the printed restriction p<q. For fixed f\in L^p, its endpoint maps are L^1\to L^p and L^{p'}\to L^\infty. When 1\leq p<\infty, choose \theta=p(1-1/q): then 1/q=1-\theta/p and 1/r=(1-\theta)/p. Thus every admissible q\in[1,p'], including q=p when admissible, is covered. Endpoint values of \theta use the endpoint estimates directly. If p=\infty, the exponent relation forces q=1,r=\infty, which follows from the essential-supremum bound. For general real or complex inputs use |f*g|\leq |f|*|g|; the same estimates show absolute convergence almost everywhere. The displayed L^0 in the proof is L^p.
