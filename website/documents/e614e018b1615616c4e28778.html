Statement

Let \mathcal A be a small abelian category and \mathcal L the full subcategory of functors \mathcal A\to\mathrm{Ab} consisting of additive left exact functors. Then H:\mathcal A^{op}\to\mathcal L, defined by H(A)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal A}(A,-) and precomposition on morphisms, is fully faithful.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Small abelian categories; Left exact functors; Natural transformations; Yoneda embedding. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Proof 1: Representable functors and Yoneda

Let \mathcal L\subset\operatorname{Fun}(\mathcal A,\mathrm{Ab}) be the category of additive left exact functors from the abelian category \mathcal A to the category of abelian groups \mathrm{Ab}. We construct a contravariant embedding H:\mathcal A\to\mathcal L by setting H(A)=h^A for all A\in\mathcal A, where h^A is the covariant hom-functor, h^A(X)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal A}(A,X). Each h^A is additive and left exact, so it belongs to \mathcal L. The Yoneda lemma states that H is fully faithful.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof concerns an arbitrary abelian category and its category of additive left-exact functors. Understanding the construction and full faithfulness requires morphisms, functors, natural transformations, exactness and representable functors together. Quoting Yoneda does not remove those prerequisites; they exceed the stated undergraduate allowance.

Yoneda lemma: Nat(Hom(A,−),Hom(B,−)) is naturally Hom(B,A)
Kind:

Theorem

Treatment:

Cited without proof

Statement used:

For objects A,B, natural transformations Hom(A,-) -> Hom(B,-) correspond bijectively to morphisms B -> A by precomposition.

Application in proof:

The bijection identifies the morphism sets of A^op with those between H(A) and H(B), proving full faithfulness.

Application level:

Beyond undergraduate level

Level justification:

Natural transformations and representable functors must already be meaningful to follow the variance and fullness claim. This is a substantial category-theoretic prerequisite.
