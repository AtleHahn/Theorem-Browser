Statement

A graph is planar if its vertices can be drawn as distinct points in the plane and edges as simple curves meeting only at common endpoints. A subdivision replaces each edge by a path, with the new internal vertices distinct for different edges. If a finite graph contains a subgraph that is a subdivision of K_5 or K_{3,3}, it is nonplanar. Here K_5 is the complete graph on five vertices and K_{3,3} is the complete bipartite graph on two sets of three vertices.

Proof 1: Suppress subdivision paths in a hypothetical planar drawing

The two graphs {\displaystyle K_{5}} and {\displaystyle K_{3,3}} are nonplanar, as may be shown either by a case analysis or an argument involving Euler's formula. Additionally, subdividing a graph cannot turn a nonplanar graph into a planar graph: if a subdivision of a graph {\displaystyle G} has a planar drawing, the paths of the subdivision form curves that may be used to represent the edges of {\displaystyle G} itself. A subgraph of a planar graph inherits its planar drawing. Therefore, a graph that contains a subgraph which is a subdivision of K_5 or K_{3,3} cannot be planar.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Restrict a hypothetical drawing and suppress subdivision paths to contradict the supplied nonplanarity of the two small graphs. Elementary finite drawings and paths suffice.
