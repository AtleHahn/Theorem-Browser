Statement

Let L be the long line and give L\times\mathbb R the product topology. Then L\times\mathbb R is a connected Hausdorff space in which every point has a neighborhood homeomorphic to an open subset of \mathbb R^2, but it has no countable topological base and is noncompact. Thus it is a surface when second countability is omitted from the definition.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement describes a product of a nonmetrizable manifold and its local and countability properties in general topology.

Proof 1: Take the product of the long line with the real line — outline

Use the standard properties of the long line: it is connected, Hausdorff and locally homeomorphic to the real line, but is not second-countable. Take its Cartesian product with \mathbb R. This is the stated non-second-countable, noncompact locally Euclidean surface.
