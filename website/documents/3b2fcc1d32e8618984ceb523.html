Statement

Let r_0,r_1,r_2,r_3 be the complex roots with multiplicity of x^4+cx^2+dx+e. Put s_1=(r_0-r_1+r_2-r_3)/2, s_2=(r_0+r_1-r_2-r_3)/2, and s_3=(r_0-r_1-r_2+r_3)/2. Then \prod_{j=1}^3(z^2-s_j^2)=z^6+2cz^4+(c^2-4e)z^2-d^2. The roots r_i are recovered by the inverse Hadamard transform once the correct signs of the s_j are selected.

Proof 1: Transform roots by signed half-sums — outline

If we now set

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}s_{0}&={\tfrac {1}{2}}(r_{0}+r_{1}+r_{2}+r_{3}),\\s_{1}&={\tfrac {1}{2}}(r_{0}-r_{1}+r_{2}-r_{3}),\\s_{2}&={\tfrac {1}{2}}(r_{0}+r_{1}-r_{2}-r_{3}),\\s_{3}&={\tfrac {1}{2}}(r_{0}-r_{1}-r_{2}+r_{3}),\end{aligned}}}

then since the transformation is an involution, we may express the roots in terms of the four si in exactly the same way. Since the quartic is depressed, we know the value s_0=0, so we really only need the values for s1, s2 and s3. These we may find by expanding the polynomial

{\displaystyle \left(z^{2}-s_{1}^{2}\right)\left(z^{2}-s_{2}^{2}\right)\left(z^{2}-s_{3}^{2}\right)}

which for the depressed quartic in the statement is equal to

{\displaystyle z^{6}+2cz^{4}+\left(c^{2}-4e\right)z^{2}-d^{2}}

This polynomial is of degree six, but only of degree three in z2, and so the corresponding equation is solvable. By trial we can determine which three roots are the correct ones, and hence find the solutions of the quartic.

The source leaves the expansion using the coefficient relations implicit.
