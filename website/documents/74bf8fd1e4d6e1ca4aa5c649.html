Statement

Let A,B,C\in\mathbb R^d, d\ge2, be noncollinear. Set a=A-C, b=B-C and \Delta=|a|^2|b|^2-(a\cdot b)^2>0. Their circumcircle lies in their affine plane, with radius and center

r=\frac{|a||b||a-b|}{2\sqrt\Delta},\qquad O=C+\frac{|a|^2|b|^2(a+b)-(a\cdot b)(|a|^2b+|b|^2a)}{2\Delta}.
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves real Euclidean vectors; inner products; norms; affine planes. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Replace cross products by dot products — outline

The circumcircle of a triangle embedded in three dimensions can be found using a generalized method. Let A, B, C be three-dimensional points, which form the vertices of a triangle. We start by translating the system to place C at the origin:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\mathbf {a} &=\mathbf {A} -\mathbf {C} ,\\\mathbf {b} &=\mathbf {B} -\mathbf {C} .\end{aligned}}}

The circumradius r is then

{\displaystyle r={\frac {\left\|\mathbf {a} \right\|\left\|\mathbf {b} \right\|\left\|\mathbf {a} -\mathbf {b} \right\|}{2\left\|\mathbf {a} \times \mathbf {b} \right\|}}={\frac {\left\|\mathbf {a} -\mathbf {b} \right\|}{2\sin \theta }}={\frac {\left\|\mathbf {A} -\mathbf {B} \right\|}{2\sin \theta }},}

where θ is the interior angle between a and b. The circumcenter, p_0=O, is given by

{\displaystyle p_{0}={\frac {\left(\left\|\mathbf {a} \right\|^{2}\mathbf {b} -\left\|\mathbf {b} \right\|^{2}\mathbf {a} \right)\times (\mathbf {a} \times \mathbf {b} )}{2\left\|\mathbf {a} \times \mathbf {b} \right\|^{2}}}+\mathbf {C} .}

This cross-product formula is three-dimensional, but it can be generalized to the other dimensions by replacing the cross products with following identities:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\mathbf {u} \times (\mathbf {v} \times \mathbf {w} )&=(\mathbf {u} \cdot \mathbf {w} )\mathbf {v} -(\mathbf {u} \cdot \mathbf {v} )\mathbf {w} ,\\\left\|\mathbf {u} \times \mathbf {v} \right\|^{2}&=\left\|\mathbf {u} \right\|^{2}\left\|\mathbf {v} \right\|^{2}-(\mathbf {u} \cdot \mathbf {v} )^{2}.\end{aligned}}}

This gives us the following equation for the circumradius r:

{\displaystyle r={\frac {\left\|\mathbf {a} \right\|\left\|\mathbf {b} \right\|\left\|\mathbf {a} -\mathbf {b} \right\|}{2{\sqrt {\left\|\mathbf {a} \right\|^{2}\left\|\mathbf {b} \right\|^{2}-(\mathbf {a} \cdot \mathbf {b} )^{2}}}}}}

and the following equation for the circumcenter p0:

{\displaystyle p_{0}={\frac {\left(\left(\left\|\mathbf {a} \right\|^{2}\mathbf {b} -\left\|\mathbf {b} \right\|^{2}\mathbf {a} \right)\cdot \mathbf {b} \right)\mathbf {a} -\left(\left(\left\|\mathbf {a} \right\|^{2}\mathbf {b} -\left\|\mathbf {b} \right\|^{2}\mathbf {a} \right)\cdot \mathbf {a} \right)\mathbf {b} }{2\left(\left\|\mathbf {a} \right\|^{2}\left\|\mathbf {b} \right\|^{2}-(\mathbf {a} \cdot \mathbf {b} )^{2}\right)}}+\mathbf {C} }

which can be simplified to:

{\displaystyle p_{0}={\frac {\left\|\mathbf {a} \right\|^{2}\left\|\mathbf {b} \right\|^{2}(\mathbf {a} +\mathbf {b} )-(\mathbf {a} \cdot \mathbf {b} )\left(\left\|\mathbf {a} \right\|^{2}\mathbf {b} +\left\|\mathbf {b} \right\|^{2}\mathbf {a} \right)}{2\left(\left\|\mathbf {a} \right\|^{2}\left\|\mathbf {b} \right\|^{2}-(\mathbf {a} \cdot \mathbf {b} )^{2}\right)}}+\mathbf {C} }
