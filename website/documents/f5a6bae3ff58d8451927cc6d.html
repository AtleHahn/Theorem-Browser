Statement

Graphs below are finite, undirected and simple. An independent set has no edges within it. A graph with vertex degrees d_1,\ldots,d_n has an independent set of size at least \sum_{i=1}^n1/(d_i+1).

Level
High school only
Level justification

The assertion compares the size of a locally defined independent set to an elementary finite sum.

Proof 1: Keep vertices preceding all their neighbors

Write S=\sum_{i=1}^n1/(d_i+1). The proof attempts to find such an independent set as follows:

  • Consider a random permutation of the vertices of the graph
  • Select every vertex that is adjacent to none of the vertices before it.

A vertex of degree {\displaystyle d} is included in this with probability {\displaystyle {\frac {1}{d+1}}}, so this process gives an average of {\displaystyle S} vertices in the chosen set.

Level
High school only
Level justification

In a finite random ordering, each vertex is earliest among itself and its neighbors in exactly a 1/(d+1) fraction of orders. Selected vertices cannot be adjacent, and at least one ordering attains the average selected count. This is finite counting and averaging.
