Statement

Let r>1 be irrational and m a positive integer. Set \mathcal B_r=\{\lfloor nr\rfloor:n\ge1\} and \{x\}=x-\lfloor x\rfloor. Then m\in\mathcal B_r if and only if 1-1/r<\{m/r\}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Only elementary real-number arithmetic, integer inequalities and floors are needed.

Proof 1: Translate the floor interval

\begin{aligned} m\in\mathcal B_r &\Longleftrightarrow \exists n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}:\ m=\lfloor nr\rfloor\\ &\Longleftrightarrow \exists n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}:\ m<nr<m+1\\ &\Longleftrightarrow \exists n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}:\ \frac mr<n<\frac mr+\frac1r\\ &\Longleftrightarrow \exists n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}:\ n-\frac1r<\frac mr<n\\ &\Longleftrightarrow 1-\frac1r<\left\{\frac mr\right\}. \end{aligned}

Here \{x\}=x-\lfloor x\rfloor denotes the fractional part of x.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Rewrite m=floor(nr) as m<nr<m+1 using irrationality, divide by r and use 1/r<1. The only possible integer in the resulting interval is floor(m/r)+1, and its membership is exactly the stated fractional-part inequality. Elementary inequalities, irrationality and floors suffice.
