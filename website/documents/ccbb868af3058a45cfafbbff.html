Statement

Let X be a smooth complex projective variety of complex dimension d, let \eta\in H^2(X,\mathbb Q) be the cohomology class of a hyperplane section, and let 0\le i\le d be an integer. Then cup product with \eta^i gives an isomorphism H^{d-i}(X,\mathbb Q)\to H^{d+i}(X,\mathbb Q).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Cohomology classes of hyperplane sections and cup-product isomorphisms require substantial algebraic topology and projective geometry. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Take the target of relative hard Lefschetz to be a point

Use the relative hard Lefschetz theorem as an external prerequisite: for a smooth proper map f:X\to Y of relative dimension d between complex projective varieties, cup product with the relative hyperplane class gives R^{d-i}f_*\mathbb Q\cong R^{d+i}f_*\mathbb Q. Take Y to be a point and f the structure map. The higher direct images over that point are the cohomology groups of X, so this is the stated isomorphism.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Specialize relative hard Lefschetz to a point and identify higher direct images with cohomology. Projective varieties, sheaf direct images and cup products require graduate algebraic geometry and topology.
