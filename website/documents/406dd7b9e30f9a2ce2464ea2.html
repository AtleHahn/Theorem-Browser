Statement

Let V be an n-dimensional normed vector space over \mathbb R or \mathbb C, where n\ge0. There is a basis e_1,\ldots,e_n of V whose dual coordinate functionals e^1,\ldots,e^n\in V^* satisfy

e^i(e_j)=\delta_{ij},\qquad \|e_i\|=\|e^i\|=1\quad(1\le i,j\le n).

Here V^* is the space of linear functionals on V, with dual norm \|f\|=\sup_{\|v\|\le1}|f(v)|, and \delta_{ij} is 1 when i=j and 0 otherwise. Such a basis is called an Auerbach basis.

The source also gives the real geometric formulation: for n\ge1 and every convex body K\subset\mathbb R^n symmetric about the origin (a compact convex set with nonempty interior and K=-K), there is an invertible linear map T:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^n such that

\left\{x:\sum_{i=1}^n|x_i|\le1\right\}\ \subseteq\ T(K)\ \subseteq\ \left\{x:\max_{1\le i\le n}|x_i|\le1\right\}.
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra and elementary real analysis give bases, coordinate functionals, norms and compact convex sets. The dual norm is defined locally as a supremum; the two coordinate balls and containment are explicit. These form a small extension of that baseline, without infinite-dimensional functional analysis or a Hahn–Banach prerequisite.

Proof availability

The source gives an induction attempt, not a usable proof of the lemma. After choosing a unit vector and a supporting functional, it applies induction to the functional's kernel. It does not establish that the resulting coordinate functionals on the whole space still have norm one while vanishing on the chosen vector. This is an essential gap, not just a missing notation change. Its description of the unit sphere as a convex body is also inaccurate. No full proof is retained. The subsequent Hahn–Banach argument proves a projection corollary while assuming the lemma; it is not a proof of the lemma.
