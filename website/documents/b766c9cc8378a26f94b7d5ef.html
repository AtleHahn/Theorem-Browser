Statement

Let m,n be positive integers, A\in\mathbb R^{m\times n} and b\in\mathbb R^m. Inequalities between vectors are componentwise. Let S\subset\mathbb R^n be a nonempty closed convex cone and define S^*=\{z:z^Tx\ge0\text{ for every }x\in S\}. Suppose C=\{Ax:x\in S\} is closed. Exactly one holds: (i) Ax=b for some x\in S; (ii) A^Ty\in S^* and b^Ty<0 for some y\in\mathbb R^m. Here a cone is closed under multiplication by nonnegative scalars, and closedness is in the Euclidean topology.

Proof 1: Generalized Farkas' lemma — outline

The first alternative is membership of b in the closed convex cone C=AS. By closed-cone separation, either b\in C, or a hyperplane separates it with y^Tc\ge0 for every c\in C and b^Ty<0. The former inequalities say exactly that A^Ty\in S^*. There are no other possibilities.
