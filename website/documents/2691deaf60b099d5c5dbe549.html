Statement

An infinite closed cover need not allow continuous pasting: the inclusion \iota:\mathbb Z\to\mathbb R, with cofinite topology on \mathbb Z and usual topology on \mathbb R, is continuous on each closed singleton but not continuous on \mathbb Z.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Basic point-set topology and real analysis supply compactness, closed sets, continuity, metrics and completeness. The Cantor-set construction or cofinite topology, where present, is a direct set-based example learnable briefly. The complete assertion needs no algebraic or differential topology.

Proof 1: A finite nonempty preimage in the cofinite topology

The singletons form a closed cover in the cofinite topology, whose nonempty open sets have finite complement.

The inclusion map {\displaystyle \iota :\mathbb {Z} \to \mathbb {R} } from the integers to the real line (with the integers equipped with the cofinite topology) is continuous when restricted to a singleton integer, but the preimage of a bounded open interval containing an integer in the reals with this map is a nonempty finite set of points, so not open in {\displaystyle \mathbb {Z} .}

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the explicitly defined cofinite topology and the inverse-image criterion for continuity. A nonempty finite preimage of a bounded interval is not open, although restrictions to singletons are continuous. This is basic point-set topology.
