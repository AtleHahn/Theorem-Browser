Statement

Let \Omega\subset\mathbb R^d be a domain and let real functions u,v,f have the regularity and integrability needed for Green’s first identity, with -\Delta u=f on \Omega and u=v=0 on \partial\Omega. Then \int_\Omega\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\,dx=\int_\Omega fv\,dx.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Gradients and Laplacians; Multiple integrals; Boundary conditions. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Testing and integration by parts

Testing the differential equation against v gives

{\displaystyle -\int _{\Omega }(\nabla ^{2}u)v\,dx=\int _{\Omega }fv\,dx.}

The left side of this equation can be made more symmetric by integration by parts using Green's identity and assuming that {\displaystyle v=0} on {\displaystyle \partial \Omega }:

{\displaystyle \int _{\Omega }\nabla u\cdot \nabla v\,dx=\int _{\Omega }fv\,dx.}

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Multiply the classical differential equation by the test function and apply Green with vanishing boundary values. Undergraduate multivariable calculus suffices under the explicitly assumed classical regularity; no Sobolev theory is used here.
