Statement

Let A_1,\ldots,A_m\subseteq\Omega, m\ge0, and put N(\omega)=\sum_{j=1}^m1_{A_j}(\omega) and N_k(\omega)=\sum_{J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\},\ |J|=k}1_{\cap_{j\in J}A_j}(\omega). An empty intersection is \Omega, so N_0=1. On \mathbb R^{m+1} let E(c_0,\ldots,c_m)=(c_1,\ldots,c_m,0) and \Delta=E-I. For every c\in\mathbb R^{m+1}, c_{N(\omega)}=\sum_{k=0}^mN_k(\omega)(\Delta^kc)_0.

Proof 1: Only the first m plus one coordinates matter

Use the combinatorial finite-difference identity c_{N(\omega)}=\sum_{k=0}^mN_k(\omega)(\Delta^kc)_0 for an infinite sequence, where (Ec)_j=c_{j+1} and \Delta=E-I. For k\le m, the zeroth component of E^kc is c_k, and the zeroth component of (E-I)^kc uses only c_0,\ldots,c_k. Therefore replacing the infinite shift by the truncated shift matrix changes none of the components used in the finite combinatorial identity. Applying that identity gives the formula.
