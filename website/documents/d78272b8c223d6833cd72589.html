Statement

Let A be a nonzero complex unital Banach algebra in which every nonzero element is invertible. The scalar map \mathbb C\to A, \lambda\mapsto\lambda1, is an algebra isomorphism.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Banach algebras and algebra isomorphisms are beyond the finite-dimensional calculus/linear-algebra baseline.

Proof 1: Apply nonemptiness of the spectrum

The theorem follows from the fact that the spectrum of any element of a complex Banach algebra is nonempty: for every element {\displaystyle a} of a complex Banach algebra {\displaystyle A} there is some complex number {\displaystyle \lambda } such that {\displaystyle \lambda 1-a} is not invertible. This is a consequence of the complex-analyticity of the resolvent function. By assumption, {\displaystyle \lambda 1-a=0}. So {\displaystyle a=\lambda \cdot 1}. Since 1\ne0, the scalar map is also injective and preserves the algebra operations. Its inverse gives an isomorphism from {\displaystyle A} to {\displaystyle \mathbb {C} }.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the nonempty-spectrum assertion in a complex Banach division algebra and identify all elements as scalars. Retain beyond undergraduate provisionally for the combined complete normed algebra and spectrum setting, despite the short algebraic inference.
