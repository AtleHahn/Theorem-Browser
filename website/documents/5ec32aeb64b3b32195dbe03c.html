Statement

For each prime p and positive integer r, there is an integer N=N(p,r) such that every homogeneous polynomial f\in\mathbb Q_p[x_1,\ldots,x_n] of degree r, with n\ge N, has a zero in \mathbb Q_p^n other than the zero vector.

Proof 1: Specialize Brauer’s theorem to one equation — outline

Use the source-stated diagonal solvability fact for \mathbb Q_p: for each positive integer d, all diagonal degree-d forms over \mathbb Q_p in sufficiently many variables have a nontrivial zero. Use also Brauer's theorem on forms: over a field with this property, finitely many homogeneous equations of fixed degrees vanish identically on an affine subspace of any prescribed finite dimension once the number of variables is sufficiently large.

Choose one equation, as in the source application, and choose a one-dimensional affine subspace. It follows that a homogeneous equation of degree r over \mathbb Q_p has a nontrivial solution if its number of variables is sufficiently large.

Level justification

The argument specializes Brauer to one equation, but the full objects are forms over the p-adic field and its diagonal solvability theory. Understanding that field and the simultaneous-form hypotheses exceeds a small extension of ordinary undergraduate algebra.
