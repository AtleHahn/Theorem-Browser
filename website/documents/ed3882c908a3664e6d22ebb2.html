Statement

Use real projective coordinates [x:y:t], meaning a nonzero real triple considered up to nonzero common scaling; t=0 is the line at infinity. Let two distinct lines be given by y=sx+mt and ax+by+ct=0, where s,m,a,b,c are real and (a,b)\ne(0,0). They have exactly one projective intersection. If a+bs\ne0, it has t=1 and x=-(c+bm)/(a+bs), y=sx+m. Otherwise it is [1:s:0].

Level
High school only
Level justification

Projective coordinates are explicitly defined as proportional nonzero real triples. The asserted intersection and its cases use only linear equations and scaling.

Proof 1: Unique intersection of two distinct projective lines

One can verify this with equations. The equation of the first line is y=sx+mt in projective coordinates. If the equation of the second line is ax+by+ct=0, by substituting sx+mt for y in it, one gets

(a+bs)x+(c+bm)t=0.

If a+bs\ne0, one gets the x-coordinate of the intersection point by solving the latter equation in x and putting t=1.

If a+bs=0, then b\ne0 because (a,b)\ne(0,0). Thus s=-a/b, and the two lines are parallel as having the same slope. Since they are distinct, m\ne-c/b, and the substituted equation gives t=0. This gives the point at infinity of projective coordinates [1:s:0].

Level
High school only
Level justification

Substitute one line equation into the other and distinguish a zero coefficient, using the locally defined scaling convention. School linear equations and proportional coordinates suffice.
