Statement

For every integer d\ge1, the family of all positive-radius Euclidean balls centered at zero in \mathbb R^d has no disjoint subfamily whose fivefold dilates cover the union of the entire family. A fivefold dilate has the same center and five times the radius.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves finite-dimensional Euclidean balls; bounded sets; disjoint families. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Concentric-ball counterexample

Consider the family of all positive-radius balls centered at 0 in \mathbb R^d; any nonempty disjoint subfamily consists of only one ball B, and 5B does not contain all the balls in this family. The empty subfamily cannot cover either.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Every ball contains the origin, so a disjoint selection has at most one member, whose finite dilation remains bounded. Basic Euclidean metric spaces suffice in arbitrary dimension.
