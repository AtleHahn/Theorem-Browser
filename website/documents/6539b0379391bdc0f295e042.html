Statement

Let W be a d-dimensional Brownian motion on (\Omega,\mathcal F,P), with integer d\ge2. For each outcome \omega, let A_\omega\subset[0,\infty) be a chosen set of times, possibly depending on \omega, and put W_\omega(A_\omega)=\{W_\omega(t):t\in A_\omega\}. There is a P-null set N, independent of this assignment, such that \dim_H W_\omega(A_\omega)=2\dim_H A_\omega for every \omega\notin N.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Brownian paths in Euclidean space; Hausdorff dimension; Probability-null exceptional sets; Random subsets of nonnegative time. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Specialize the simultaneous pathwise identity

Kaufman's dimension-doubling theorem states that P-almost surely, for any set A\subset[0,\infty), we have

\dim_H W(A)=2\dim_H A.

Kaufman's result is true for all choices of A simultaneously. This means Kaufman's theorem can also be applied to random sets A: outside the same null set, use A=A_\omega at outcome \omega.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The universal-outside-one-null-set substitution is logical, but the theorem concerns Brownian sample paths and Hausdorff dimension of arbitrary time sets. These stochastic and fractal-geometric concepts exceed a small undergraduate extension.
