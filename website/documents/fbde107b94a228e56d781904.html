Statement

For every integer n\ge1, there is a constant C_n>0 such that every real u\in L^1(\mathbb R^n)\cap W^{1,2}(\mathbb R^n) satisfies \|u\|_2^{1+2/n}\le C_n\|u\|_1^{2/n}\|Du\|_2. Here Du is the weak gradient and all norms are Lebesgue norms on \mathbb R^n.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement is measure-theoretic functional analysis, not only classical calculus.

Proof 1: Split frequencies at an optimized radius — outline

Use the unitary angular-frequency Fourier transform, so that Plancherel's theorem and the weak-derivative Fourier identity apply. The inequality follows from basic properties of the Fourier transform. Indeed, integrating over the complement of the ball of radius \rho>0,

\int_{|x|\geq\rho}|\widehat u(x)|^2\,dx \leq\int_{|x|\geq\rho}\frac{|x|^2}{\rho^2}|\widehat u(x)|^2\,dx \leq\rho^{-2}\int_{\mathbb R^n}|Du|^2\,dx

because 1\leq |x|^2/\rho^2. On the other hand, one has

|\widehat u|\leq\|u\|_{L^1}

which, when integrated over the ball of radius \rho, gives

\int_{|x|\leq\rho}|\widehat u(x)|^2\,dx \leq\rho^n\omega_n\|u\|_{L^1}^2,

where \omega_n is the volume of the unit n-ball. Choosing \rho to minimize the sum of these two bounds and applying Parseval's theorem,

\|\widehat u\|_{L^2}=\|u\|_{L^2},

gives the inequality.

Proof 2: Interpolation parameters followed by a power

Use the Gagliardo–Nirenberg theorem as an external prerequisite: for integers 0\le j<m, 1\le q,r\le\infty, 1\le P<\infty, and j/m\le\theta\le1 with

\frac1P=\frac jn+\theta\left(\frac1r-\frac mn\right)+\frac{1-\theta}{q},

it gives \|D^ju\|_P\le C\|D^mu\|_r^\theta\|u\|_q^{1-\theta} for real u\in L^q(\mathbb R^n) with weak derivatives D^mu\in L^r. If j=0,q=\infty,rm<n, additionally require decay at infinity or membership in some finite L^s. If r>1 and m-j-n/r is a nonnegative integer, require \theta<1. The constant is independent of u, and 1/\infty=0.

Indeed, choosing

j=0,\quad m=1,\quad n\geq1,\quad P=2,\quad q=1,\quad r=2,\quad \theta=\frac{n}{n+2},

one gets

\|u\|_{L^2(\mathbb R^n)} \leq C\|u\|_{L^1(\mathbb R^n)}^{2/(n+2)} \|\nabla u\|_{L^2(\mathbb R^n)}^{n/(n+2)}.

This is recast, by raising both sides to the power (n+2)/n and renaming the constant, as

\|u\|_{L^2(\mathbb R^n)}^{1+2/n} \leq C\|u\|_{L^1(\mathbb R^n)}^{2/n}\|\nabla u\|_{L^2(\mathbb R^n)}.
