Statement

Let (P_t)_{t\in I} be a nonempty family of three-dimensional convex polyhedra with fixed corresponding vertex-edge-face incidences. Suppose every corresponding face has the same shape throughout the family. Then all the polyhedra P_t are congruent.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement quantifies over finite three-dimensional solids and compares their corresponding polygonal faces and congruence. Its family indexing adds no independent infinite-set or topological prerequisite.

Proof 1: Comparing convex realizations by Cauchy’s theorem

Cauchy's theorem states that convex polytopes in three dimensions with congruent corresponding faces must be congruent to each other, where the correspondence preserves their vertex-edge-face incidences.

Cauchy's rigidity theorem is a corollary from Cauchy's theorem stating that a convex polytope cannot be deformed among convex realizations so that its faces remain rigid. For the family of convex realizations in the statement, any pair P_s,P_t has congruent corresponding faces and the same incidences, so Cauchy's theorem gives P_s\cong P_t.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Compare any two family members using the supplied face-congruence theorem. No continuous deformation or differential rigidity theory is invoked; the deduction concerns elementary solid polyhedra.
