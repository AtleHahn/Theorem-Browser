Statement

Let n\ge1 and let T be a finite triangulation of the closed ball B^n=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:\|x\|\le1\}. Suppose its boundary triangulation is antipodally symmetric: with every boundary simplex \sigma, it contains -\sigma. Label every vertex by a nonzero integer, by a map L:V(T)\to\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}, satisfying L(-v)=-L(v) for every boundary vertex.

An edge is complementary if its endpoint labels are a,-a. An n-simplex is alternating if its n+1 vertex labels have distinct absolute values and, when ordered by increasing absolute value, their signs alternate. If T has no complementary edge, the number of its alternating n-simplices is odd.

A triangulation means a finite simplicial complex together with a homeomorphism from its realization onto the ball; its boundary subcomplex maps onto the sphere. Simplices meet along common faces, and the antipodal condition refers to their images on that sphere. An n-simplex has n+1 vertices.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Finite simplicial complexes and ball triangulations are a small combinatorial extension of undergraduate geometry and point-set topology; label parity and all quantifiers are explicit. No homology machinery is needed to understand the statement.
