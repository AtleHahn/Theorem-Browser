Statement

Let n\ge1 and let T be a finite triangulation of the closed ball B^n=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:\|x\|\le1\}. Suppose its boundary triangulation is antipodally symmetric: with every boundary simplex \sigma, it contains -\sigma. Label every vertex by a nonzero integer, by a map L:V(T)\to\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}, satisfying L(-v)=-L(v) for every boundary vertex.

An edge is complementary if its endpoint labels are a,-a. An n-simplex is alternating if its n+1 vertex labels have distinct absolute values and, when ordered by increasing absolute value, their signs alternate. If T has no complementary edge, the number of its alternating n-simplices is odd.

A triangulation means a finite simplicial complex together with a homeomorphism from its realization onto the ball; its boundary subcomplex maps onto the sphere. Simplices meet along common faces, and the antipodal condition refers to their images on that sphere. An n-simplex has n+1 vertices.

Level justification

Finite simplicial complexes and ball triangulations are a small combinatorial extension of undergraduate geometry and point-set topology; label parity and all quantifiers are explicit. No homology machinery is needed to understand the statement.

Proof 1: Boundary intersection parity and facet counting (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorem 1.4 p.2; Theorem 3.1 and proof pp.3–5, alternating-matroid specialization; boundary face-count bridge given below.).

Apply Zivaljevic Theorem 1.4 on the boundary sphere S^(n-1), where the labelling defines an antipodal simplicial map to the crosspolytope boundary because no edge is complementary. It gives an odd count of positive alternating (n-1)-faces for ANY antipodal triangulation, without a hemisphere flag. The elementary mod-2 identity on an n-simplex is: the number of its positive alternating facets is odd exactly when the simplex is alternating. For distinct absolute labels, remove one sign from the ordered sign string to check the identity; repeated equal labels produce paired facets, and a simplex cannot contain opposite labels. Sum over the ball: every interior facet appears twice, leaving precisely the boundary count. Hence the original number of alternating n-simplices is odd. For n=1 the boundary S^0 has one positive vertex, giving the same argument. Theorem 1.4 follows from Theorem 3.1 and its alternating-matroid specialization, using characteristic classes and intersection theory. No subdivision or change in the counted simplices is involved.
