Semantic consequences of an effective first-order theory can be enumerated

For effectively axiomatized first-order theories, all semantic consequences can be mechanically enumerated through proofs. This joins semantic truth in every model to effective proof search, while enumeration alone need not decide when a proposed statement is not a consequence.

Statement

Let T be a computably enumerable first-order theory in a computable language with an effective usual sound and complete proof system. Then the sentences true in all models of T form a computably enumerable set.

Proof 1: Enumerate finite proofs from generated axioms (outline)

A consequence of the completeness theorem is that it is possible to computably enumerate the semantic consequences of any computably enumerable first-order theory, by enumerating all the possible formal deductions from the axioms of the theory, and using this to produce an enumeration of their conclusions.

Here deductions are generated from the enumerated axioms of T. Soundness and completeness identify their conclusions with exactly the sentences true in all models of T.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Enumerating deductions is a straightforward algorithm, but identifying them with truth in all models uses formal first-order theories, effective languages, soundness and completeness together. That cumulative logical background is not confidently a small extension; retain beyond undergraduate provisionally.
