Statement

Let n\ge1 be an integer and z=r(\cos x+i\sin x) with r\ge0 and real x. The set of solutions of w^n=z is \{r^{1/n}(\cos((x+2\pi k)/n)+i\sin((x+2\pi k)/n)):k=0,\ldots,n-1\}, where r^{1/n} is the nonnegative real root.

Proof 1: Divide the polar angle and take the radial root — outline

A modest extension of de Moivre's formula

(\cos x+i\sin x)^n=\cos(nx)+i\sin(nx)

can be used to find the n-th roots of a complex number for a positive integer n. If z is a complex number, written in polar form as

z=r(\cos x+i\sin x),

then the n-th roots of z are given by

r^{1/n}\left(\cos\frac{x+2\pi k}{n}+i\sin\frac{x+2\pi k}{n}\right)

where k varies over the integer values from 0 to n-1. When r=0, all these expressions give the same root 0. The source states this recipe without the power and exhaustion checks.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Take the positive real radial root and divide the angle after adding full turns. De Moivre verifies each root, and polar periodicity gives exhaustion; zero is handled separately. This is elementary complex trigonometry.
