Statement

Let \Gamma be a finitely generated Kleinian group, and let \Omega be its region of discontinuity. The quotient \Omega/\Gamma has finitely many connected components, each of which is a compact Riemann surface with finitely many points removed.

Review notes

  • The named finiteness theorem controls the conformal type of quotients for all finitely generated Kleinian groups.

Proof 1: Quasiconformal deformation and finite-index descent (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Section 4.8, Theorem 4.29 and proof, pp.43–45.).

Use section 4.8, Theorem 4.29. Selberg’s lemma gives a torsion-free finite-index subgroup; take its normal core. It is finitely generated and has the same discontinuity domain. Its quotient is a finite union of compact Riemann surfaces with finitely many punctures. The finite quotient group acts conformally, permuting components and punctures; these maps extend across punctures to the compactifications. Taking the finite quotient gives compact Riemann surfaces with finitely many punctures (branch points are allowed in the underlying surface). Elementary torsion-free groups are trivial, cyclic parabolic, cyclic loxodromic, or rank-two parabolic, giving respectively sphere, cylinder, torus, torus; finite quotients preserve finiteness. Empty Omega is immediate.
