Statement

For 0<p<1 and 0\le\varepsilon<1-p, define

D(p+\varepsilon\Vert p)=(p+\varepsilon)\log\frac{p+\varepsilon}{p}+(1-p-\varepsilon)\log\frac{1-p-\varepsilon}{1-p}.

Then D(p+\varepsilon\Vert p)\ge2\varepsilon^2.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The function is fully defined by real arithmetic and logarithms; no probability meaning is needed.

Proof 1: Compare the second derivatives — outline

The inequality D(p+\varepsilon\Vert p)\ge2\varepsilon^2 follows from the convexity of D(p+\varepsilon\Vert p) and the fact that

{\displaystyle {\frac {d^{2}}{d\varepsilon ^{2}}}D(p+\varepsilon \parallel p)={\frac {1}{(p+\varepsilon )(1-p-\varepsilon )}}\geq 4={\frac {d^{2}}{d\varepsilon ^{2}}}(2\varepsilon ^{2}).}

The two functions have equal value and equal first derivative at \varepsilon=0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Differentiate the explicit logarithmic function twice. With q=p+epsilon, q(1-q)<=1/4 makes the second derivative at least four. Subtract 2epsilon^2 and integrate the nonnegative second derivative twice using the zero initial value and slope. This is undergraduate single-variable calculus; relative entropy is only a name for the displayed formula.
