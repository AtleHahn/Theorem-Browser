Statement

Let A be a real nonnegative n\times n matrix that is irreducible in the positive-length walk sense: for every i,j there is an integer m\ge1 with (A^m)_{ij}>0. Let h be the greatest common divisor of the lengths of closed directed walks in the graph with an edge i\to j whenever a_{ij}>0. Let r=\rho(A), the largest modulus of its complex eigenvalues.

Then r>0 is an algebraically simple eigenvalue. There are strictly positive vectors v,w with Av=rv and w^TA=rw^T. Every nonzero nonnegative right eigenvector is a positive multiple of v, and likewise every nonzero nonnegative left eigenvector is a positive multiple of w.

The eigenvalues of modulus r are exactly

r e^{2\pi i k/h}\quad(k=0,\ldots,h-1),

and each is algebraically simple. Moreover, A is similar over \mathbb C to e^{2\pi i/h}A, so its entire spectrum is invariant under this rotation.

If A is primitive, meaning that some positive power of A is strictly positive entrywise, then h=1, all other eigenvalues have modulus less than r, and, with w^Tv=1,

\lim_{k\to\infty}r^{-k}A^k=vw^T.

In particular these conclusions apply when A itself is strictly positive.
