Statement

Let \pi(x) count primes at most x. For every fixed \varepsilon>0, \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\pi((1+\varepsilon)n)-\pi(n)}{n/\log n}=\varepsilon. In particular, the number of primes in (n,(1+\varepsilon)n] tends to infinity, and for all sufficiently large n there is a prime strictly between n and (1+\varepsilon)n.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves prime-counting function; real logarithm; asymptotic ratios. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Prime number theorem and the positive limiting count — outline

Use the prime number theorem \pi(x)\sim x/\log x.

It follows from the prime number theorem that for any real {\displaystyle \varepsilon >0} there is a {\displaystyle n_{0}>0} such that for all {\displaystyle n>n_{0}} there is a prime {\displaystyle p} such that {\displaystyle n<p<(1+\varepsilon )n}. It can be shown, for instance, that

{\displaystyle \lim _{n\to \infty }{\frac {\pi ((1+\varepsilon )n)-\pi (n)}{n/\log n}}=\varepsilon ,}

which implies that {\displaystyle \pi ((1+\varepsilon )n)-\pi (n)} goes to infinity (and, in particular, is greater than 1 for sufficiently large {\displaystyle n}).
