A Gödel sentence can be recast as the assertion that one integer polynomial has no integer solution. This connects abstract limits of formal provability with ordinary arithmetic equations, showing that self-referential coding can have a concrete number-theoretic expression.

Statement

For the standard Gödel sentence asserting that no natural number satisfies its primitive-recursive proof-code relation, there is an integer-coefficient polynomial in finitely many variables such that the Gödel sentence is equivalent in the standard interpretation to that polynomial having no integer zero.

Proof 1: A Diophantine form of the Gödel sentence — source argument

The source applies the MRDP theorem to the primitive-recursive relation expressing a proof code. That theorem expresses the existence of such a code by an existential polynomial-zero condition. Negating the two equivalent existence assertions gives the claimed nonvanishing formulation.
