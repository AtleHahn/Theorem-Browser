Statement

Let S be a quasi-compact and quasi-separated scheme, and let f:X\to S be a separated morphism of finite type. There exist a scheme \overline X, an open immersion j:X\hookrightarrow\overline X, and a proper morphism p:\overline X\to S such that f=p\circ j. A scheme is a locally ringed space locally isomorphic to the prime spectrum of a commutative ring with its structure sheaf. Quasi-compact means that every open cover has a finite subcover; quasi-separated means that intersections of two affine open subsets are quasi-compact. A morphism is separated when its diagonal is a closed immersion, and is of finite type when it is quasi-compact and locally given by finitely generated algebras. An open immersion identifies its source with an open subscheme. Proper means separated, of finite type, and universally closed: every base change is a closed map on underlying spaces.
