Statement

Let D\subseteq\mathbb C^n be a domain with piecewise smooth boundary and f\in C^1(\overline D). Write \omega(\zeta,z) for the Bochner–Martinelli kernel (a differential form in \zeta of bidegree (n,n-1)), and \bar\partial f for the antiholomorphic differential. Suppose that at z\in D the indicated integrals exist and the representation

f(z)=\int_{\partial D}f(\zeta)\omega(\zeta,z)-\int_D\bar\partial f(\zeta)\wedge\omega(\zeta,z)

holds. If f is holomorphic on D, then

f(z)=\int_{\partial D}f(\zeta)\omega(\zeta,z).
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The Bochner–Martinelli kernel and integration of forms of mixed bidegree in several complex variables require substantial differential-form and complex-geometric preparation; merely recognizing the zero term does not explain the full statement. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Vanishing of the antiholomorphic derivative

In particular if f is holomorphic, \bar\partial f=0, so the second term of the assumed representation vanishes, and

f(z)=\int_{\partial D}f(\zeta)\omega(\zeta,z).
