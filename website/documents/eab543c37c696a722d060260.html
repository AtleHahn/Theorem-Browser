Statement

Let R be a commutative unital ring containing \mathbb Q and A\in M_2(R). Put s_j=\operatorname{tr}(A^j). If A is invertible, A^{-1}=-2(A-s_1I_2)/(s_1^2-s_2).

Proof 1: A two-by-two inverse from traces

Put n=2 and write \det(tI_n-A)=\sum_{i=0}^n c_it^i, with c_n=1.

Use the characteristic-coefficient identity c_{n-k}=(-1)^k B_k(s_1,-1!s_2,2!s_3,\ldots,(-1)^{k-1}(k-1)!s_k)/k!, where B_k is the complete exponential Bell polynomial and B_0=1.

For the first terms, B_1(x_1)=x_1 and B_2(x_1,x_2)=x_1^2+x_2.

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}c_{2}=B_{0}=1,\\[4pt]c_{1}={\frac {-1}{1!}}B_{1}(s_{1})=-s_{1}=-\operatorname {tr} (A),\\[4pt]c_{0}={\frac {1}{2!}}B_{2}(s_{1},-1!s_{2})={\frac {1}{2}}(s_{1}^{2}-s_{2})={\frac {1}{2}}((\operatorname {tr} (A))^{2}-\operatorname {tr} (A^{2})).\end{aligned}}}

The coefficient c_0 gives the determinant of the 2\times2 matrix, c_1 minus its trace, while its inverse is given by the characteristic-coefficient inverse formula:

A^{-1}=\frac{-1}{\det A}(A+c_1I_2) =\frac{-2(A-\operatorname{tr}(A)I_2)} {(\operatorname{tr}(A))^2-\operatorname{tr}(A^2)}.

Level justification

Compute the two trace coefficients and substitute them into the stated characteristic inverse formula. Linear algebra over a commutative rational algebra suffices, with invertibility ensuring the determinant is a unit.
