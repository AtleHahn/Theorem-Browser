Statement

For any polynomial P\in\mathbb R[X] and any z\in\mathbb C, P(\overline z)=\overline{P(z)}.

Level
High school only

Proof 1: Conjugate each polynomial term

Consider the polynomial

{\displaystyle P(z)=a_{0}+a_{1}z+a_{2}z^{2}+\cdots +a_{n}z^{n}}

where all ar are real.

Take any \zeta\in\mathbb C.

Now

{\displaystyle P{\big (}\,{\overline {\zeta }}\,{\big )}=\sum _{r=0}^{n}a_{r}{\big (}\,{\overline {\zeta }}\,{\big )}^{r}}

and given the properties of complex conjugation,

{\displaystyle \sum _{r=0}^{n}a_{r}{\big (}\,{\overline {\zeta }}\,{\big )}^{r}=\sum _{r=0}^{n}a_{r}{\overline {\zeta ^{r}}}=\sum _{r=0}^{n}{\overline {a_{r}\zeta ^{r}}}={\overline {\sum _{r=0}^{n}a_{r}\zeta ^{r}}}.}

The last expression is \overline{P(\zeta)}. This works because all a_r are real, so \overline{a_r}=a_r, and does not require P(\zeta)=0.
