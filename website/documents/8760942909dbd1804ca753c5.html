Statement

Let (S,\rho) be a separable metric space, and let \mathcal P(S) be its Borel probability measures. Equip \mathcal P(S) with the weak topology, the coarsest topology making \mu\mapsto\int_S f\,d\mu continuous for every bounded continuous real function f on S. A family K\subseteq\mathcal P(S) is tight if for every \varepsilon>0 there is a compact set C\subseteq S such that \mu(C)\geq1-\varepsilon for every \mu\in K.

  1. If K is tight, its closure in \mathcal P(S) is sequentially compact: every sequence in that closure has a subsequence converging weakly to a member of the closure.
  2. The weak topology on \mathcal P(S) is metrizable.
  3. If (S,\rho) is also complete, the converse in part 1 holds. Moreover, the weak topology on \mathcal P(S) is induced by a complete metric d_0, and K is tight if and only if its closure in (\mathcal P(S),d_0) is compact.
