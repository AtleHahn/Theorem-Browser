Statement

Let a simple planar polygon be triangulated by diagonals without adding vertices. Its vertices can be colored with three colors so that the endpoints of every polygon edge and every triangulation diagonal have different colors.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Coloring is specified explicitly as a finite geometric condition.

Proof 1: Remove a leaf triangle and extend the coloring

To prove 3-colorability of polygon triangulations, we observe that the weak dual graph to the triangulation (the undirected graph having one vertex per triangle and one edge per pair of adjacent triangles) is a tree, since any cycle in the dual graph would form the boundary of a hole in the polygon, contrary to the assumption that it has no holes. Whenever there is more than one triangle, the dual graph (like any tree) must have a vertex with only one neighbor, corresponding to a triangle that is adjacent to other triangles along only one of its sides. The smaller polygon formed by removing this triangle has a 3-coloring by mathematical induction, and this coloring is easily extended to the one additional vertex of the removed triangle.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Use the finite triangle-adjacency tree to remove a leaf triangle, color the smaller polygon inductively and give the added vertex the remaining color. School finite graph and polygon reasoning suffice.
