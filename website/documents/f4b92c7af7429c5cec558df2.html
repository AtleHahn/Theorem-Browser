Statement

Fix 0<\lambda<1, y_0\in\mathbb R^n, and a nonnegative finite measurable function f. Put g=1_{\{y_0\}}. Then s(x)=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{y\in\mathbb R^n}f((x-y)/(1-\lambda))^{1-\lambda}g(y/\lambda)^\lambda=0 for every x, whereas the ordinary supremum equals f((x-\lambda y_0)/(1-\lambda))^{1-\lambda}.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The precise measurable-function and probability-density statements require measure theory.

Proof 1: Evaluate the singleton-supported expression

For fixed x, the expression is zero except possibly at the single point y=\lambda y_0. Thus it vanishes almost everywhere and its essential supremum is zero. Its ordinary supremum is its displayed value at that point.

Level justification

The singleton calculation is simple, but the full assertion uses arbitrary measurable functions and essential supremum under Lebesgue measure. Retain beyond undergraduate provisionally for the measure-theoretic prerequisite burden.
