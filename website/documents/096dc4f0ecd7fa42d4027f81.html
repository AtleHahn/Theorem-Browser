Statement

Let E\subseteq\mathbb R^N and let \mathcal A_1,\ldots,\mathcal A_c be families of open balls, each family pairwise disjoint, whose union covers E. Set \mathcal G=\bigcup_{i=1}^c\mathcal A_i and S_{\mathcal G}(x)=\sum_{B\in\mathcal G}\mathbf1_B(x). Then \mathbf1_E(x)\le S_{\mathcal G}(x)\le c for every x\in\mathbb R^N.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Metric balls, bounded radii, disjointness and separability are basic analysis/topology. The enlarged balls are explicitly radius multiples, and all covering or multiplicity conditions are ordinary set inclusions/counts, without measure integration.

Proof 1: Count at most one ball from each disjoint family

Each point belongs to at most one ball from each disjoint family, hence to at most c balls from \mathcal G. Every point of E belongs to at least one of these balls because the families cover E. This gives the two indicator inequalities.

Level justification

Count at most one containing ball per disjoint family and at least one for points covered. Basic set and function reasoning suffices, with the modest extension that the possibly infinite indicator sum has at most c nonzero terms at each point.
