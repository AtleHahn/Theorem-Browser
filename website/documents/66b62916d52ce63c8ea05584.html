Statement

Let M\subseteq B(H) be a von Neumann algebra on a complex Hilbert space H, and let \Omega be a cyclic separating unit vector: M\Omega is dense in H, and x\Omega=0 for x\in M implies x=0. Here B(H) denotes the bounded linear operators, and

M'=\{T\in B(H):Tx=xT\text{ for all }x\in M\}

is the commutant. A von Neumann algebra is a unital adjoint-closed operator algebra closed in the weak operator topology.

Define the densely defined antilinear operators S_0(x\Omega)=x^*\Omega for x\in M and F_0(y\Omega)=y^*\Omega for y\in M'. They are closable. Write S=\overline{S_0}, F=\overline{F_0}=S^* and S=J\Delta^{1/2} for the polar decomposition, with \Delta=S^*S and J the antiunitary modular conjugation. Then

J^2=I,\qquad J\Delta J=\Delta^{-1},\qquad \Delta^{it}M\Delta^{-it}=M\quad(t\in\mathbb R),\qquad JMJ=M'.

The powers of the positive self-adjoint modular operator are defined by spectral calculus. In particular \sigma_t(x)=\Delta^{it}x\Delta^{-it} is a one-parameter group of automorphisms of M, and J\Delta^{it}J=\Delta^{it}.

The same commutation theorem has the following left-Hilbert-algebra formulation. Let \mathfrak A be a complex algebra with an antilinear involution a\mapsto a^\sharp and inner product such that left multiplication by each element is bounded, (ab,c)=(b,a^\sharp c), the involution is closable in the Hilbert completion H, and the span of products is dense. Let \lambda(a) be left multiplication on H and put M=\lambda(\mathfrak A)''. If S is the closure of \sharp and S=J\Delta^{1/2} its polar decomposition, then again \Delta^{it}M\Delta^{-it}=M for every real t and JMJ=M', with the same modular identities above.
