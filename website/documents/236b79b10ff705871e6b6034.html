Statement

Let h:X\to[0,\infty) and T:X\to\mathbb R^s be measurable on a fixed observation space X with reference measure \mu. For \eta\in\mathbb R^s let Z(\eta)=\int h(x)e^{\eta^TT(x)}\,d\mu(x), and wherever 0<Z(\eta)<\infty put A(\eta)=\log Z(\eta) and p_\eta(x)=h(x)e^{\eta^TT(x)-A(\eta)}. At an interior point of the natural parameter domain (where Z is finite on a neighborhood), \mathbb E_\eta[T_j(X)]=\partial A(\eta)/\partial\eta_j for 1\le j\le s.

Proof 1: Differentiate the cumulant generating function

The moment-generating function of T(X) is

\begin{aligned} M_T(u)&=\mathbb E_\eta\left[\exp\left(u^{\mathsf T}T(X)\right)\right]\\ &=\int_X h(x)\exp\left[(\eta+u)^{\mathsf T}T(x)-A(\eta)\right]\,d\mu(x)\\ &=e^{A(\eta+u)-A(\eta)}. \end{aligned}

Thus

K_T(u)=\log M_T(u)=A(\eta+u)-A(\eta)

is the cumulant generating function for T(X).

In particular, using the property that first derivatives of the cumulant generating function at zero are means,

\mathbb E_\eta(T_j(X))=\frac{\partial A(\eta)}{\partial\eta_j}.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof differentiates a cumulant function for measurable statistics on an arbitrary reference-measure space. General integrability and expectation, in addition to the derivative theorem, require measure-theoretic probability.
