Statement

Every finitely generated module M over a principal ideal domain R has, for some integer n\ge0, a decomposition M\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^n R/(d_i), with proper ideals (d_1)\supseteq\cdots\supseteq(d_n). These ideals are uniquely determined; generators are determined up to units. Zero d_i give free summands.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Cyclic module quotients, ideals and uniqueness up to units are abstract-algebra notions.

Proof 1: Diagonalize a finite presentation — outline

Every finitely generated module over a PID is also finitely presented because a PID is Noetherian. Take a presentation, which is a map

R^r\longrightarrow R^g

(relations to generators), and put it in Smith normal form.

This yields the invariant factor decomposition, and the diagonal entries of Smith normal form are the invariant factors. Unit diagonal entries give zero quotients and are discarded; zero entries give free summands. The divisibility and uniqueness assertions use those of Smith normal form.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Pass from a finitely generated PID module to a finite presentation via Noetherian theory, then read cyclic summands and uniqueness from Smith form. General modules, presentations and Noetherian finiteness together exceed a small extension of ordinary undergraduate algebra.
