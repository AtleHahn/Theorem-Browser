Statement

Let n\ge1 be an integer, \mu Lebesgue measure on \mathbb R^n, A+B=\{a+b:a\in A,b\in B\} and tA=\{ta:a\in A\}. The following universal assertions for nonempty compact A,B are equivalent: (i) \mu(A+B)^{1/n}\ge\mu(A)^{1/n}+\mu(B)^{1/n}; (ii) for every 0<\lambda<1, \mu(\lambda A+(1-\lambda)B)\ge\mu(A)^\lambda\mu(B)^{1-\lambda}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Basic topology supplies compact Euclidean sets and calculus supplies volumes and real powers. Lebesgue volume for compact sets can be understood through approximating box covers, including sets of zero volume. The sum and scaling operations are explicitly defined. Adding that concrete volume extension and reading the two universal inequalities fits about 30 minutes; constructing Lebesgue measure or integration theory is unnecessary.

Proof 1: Scale the sets and optimize the weight outline

Forward implication

For the forward implication, apply the Brunn–Minkowski inequality to sets {\textstyle \lambda A} and {\textstyle (1-\lambda )B}, which gives

{\textstyle \mu (\lambda A+(1-\lambda )B)\geq (\mu (\lambda A)^{1/n}+\mu ((1-\lambda )B)^{1/n})^{n}=(\lambda \mu (A)^{1/n}+(1-\lambda )\mu (B)^{1/n})^{n},}

where we used the fact that {\textstyle \mu (\cdot )^{1/n}} is homogeneous. Using now the inequality {\textstyle \lambda x+(1-\lambda )y\geq x^{\lambda }y^{1-\lambda }}, which holds for all {\textstyle x,y\geq 0,\lambda \in [0,1]} (coming from concavity of the logarithm {\textstyle \log(\lambda x+(1-\lambda )y)\geq \lambda \log(x)+(1-\lambda )\log(y)}), we find that {\textstyle (\lambda \mu (A)^{1/n}+(1-\lambda )\mu (B)^{1/n})^{n}\geq \mu (A)^{\lambda }\mu (B)^{1-\lambda }}, concluding the proof.

Reverse implication

For positive-volume A,B:

Using this multiplicative inequality for the scaled sets A/\lambda and B/(1-\lambda), we find

{\textstyle \mu (A+B)=\mu (\lambda {\frac {A}{\lambda }}+(1-\lambda ){\frac {B}{1-\lambda }})\geq {\bigl (}{\frac {\mu (A)}{\lambda ^{n}}}{\bigr )}^{\lambda }{\bigl (}{\frac {\mu (B)}{(1-\lambda )^{n}}}{\bigr )}^{1-\lambda }.}

The right side is maximized at {\textstyle \lambda ={\frac {\mu (A)^{1/n}}{\mu (A)^{1/n}+\mu (B)^{1/n}}}}, which gives

{\textstyle \mu (A+B)\geq (\mu (A)^{1/n}+\mu (B)^{1/n})^{n}} concluding the proof.

Level justification

The equivalence uses volume only through scaling, translation invariance and monotonicity on compact sets. The main work is weighted AM–GM and choosing lambda from the two volume roots. These are undergraduate real analysis and Euclidean scaling; no measure construction or integration theorem is needed. Zero-volume cases follow by containing a translate of the other nonempty set.
