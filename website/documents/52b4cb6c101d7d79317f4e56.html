Statement

Let a,b,c>0 be the side lengths of a nondegenerate Euclidean triangle, opposite angles \alpha,\beta,\gamma\in(0,\pi), and let R be its circumradius. Put S=(\sin\alpha+\sin\beta+\sin\gamma)/2. Then the area is T=4R^2\sqrt{S(S-\sin\alpha)(S-\sin\beta)(S-\sin\gamma)}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement uses elementary Euclidean geometry and real trigonometry.

Proof 1: Scale Heron’s four factors by the sine law outline

The sine rule can be used in deriving the formula for the triangle's area: denoting the semi-sum of the angles' sines as

S=\tfrac12(\sin\alpha+\sin\beta+\sin\gamma),

we have

T=4R^2\sqrt{S(S-\sin\alpha)(S-\sin\beta)(S-\sin\gamma)},

where R is the radius of the circumcircle:

2R=\frac a{\sin\alpha}=\frac b{\sin\beta}=\frac c{\sin\gamma}.

The source places this deduction next to Heron's formula T=\sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}, with s=(a+b+c)/2, but leaves the substitution and simplification implicit.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Replace every side and the semiperimeter in Heron by their extended-sine-law expressions and extract the positive radius factor. School trigonometry and algebra suffice.
