Statement

Let X be a real random variable with \mathbb EX=0, \mathbb EX^2=1, and finite fourth moment m_4=\mathbb EX^4. Then \Pr(X\ge0)\ge\frac{2\sqrt3-3}{m_4}.

Proof 1: Zero-threshold specialization with sign reflection (outline)

Use the stated fourth-moment theorem of He, Zhang and Zhang: for a real random variable Y with \mathbb EY=0, \mathbb EY^2=1 and finite fourth moment, and a\ge0, \Pr(Y\ge a)\le1-(2\sqrt3-3)\frac{(1+a^2)^2}{\mathbb EY^4+6a^2+a^4}. Apply this with Y=-X and a=0. Negation preserves the second and fourth moments and preserves the zero mean. Thus \Pr(X\le0)\le1-(2\sqrt3-3)/m_4. Taking the complementary event gives \Pr(X>0)\ge(2\sqrt3-3)/m_4, and \Pr(X\ge0)\ge\Pr(X>0) yields the displayed lower bound.
