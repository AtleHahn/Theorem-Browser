Statement

Let X,Y be F-spaces (topological vector spaces whose topology comes from a complete translation-invariant metric), and let T:X\to Y be linear. Assume that whenever x_i\to0 and Tx_i\to y, one has y=0. Then \Gamma_T=\{(x,Tx):x\in X\} is closed in X\times Y.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

F-spaces are explicitly complete metrizable topological vector spaces. Continuous vector operations and metric-product sequential closedness are undergraduate topology; no closed-graph theorem is required.

Proof 1: Translate a graph-convergent sequence to zero

In fact, for the graph of T to be closed, it is enough that if {\displaystyle x_{i}\to 0,\,Tx_{i}\to y}, then {\displaystyle y=0}. Indeed, assuming that condition holds, if {\displaystyle (x_{i},Tx_{i})\to (x,y)}, then {\displaystyle x_{i}-x\to 0} and {\displaystyle T(x_{i}-x)\to y-Tx}. Thus, {\displaystyle y=Tx}; i.e., {\displaystyle (x,y)} is in the graph of T.

Since X\times Y is metrizable, containing the limits of its convergent sequences makes the graph closed.

Level justification

Subtract the limit point, apply the zero-sequence assumption and use sequential closedness in a metric product. The explicit metrizable-vector-space setting requires only basic metric topology and continuous addition; no functional-analytic closed graph theorem is used.
