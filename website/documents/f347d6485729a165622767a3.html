Statement

For every real x, define G(x)=\int_0^x e^{-t^2}\,dt, with the usual oriented Riemann integral. Then G is differentiable on \mathbb R and G'(x)=e^{-x^2}.

Proof 1: Derivative of the Gaussian integral function — proof

The function f(x)=e^{-x^2} is continuous. By the first fundamental theorem of calculus, G(x)=\int_0^x f(t)\,dt is an antiderivative of f.
