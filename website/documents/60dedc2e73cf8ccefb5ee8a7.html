Statement

Let \Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^3 be open and \mathbf B=(B_1,B_2,B_3):\Omega\to\mathbb R^3 be a classical C^2 vector field. If \nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0 and \nabla\times\mathbf B=0, then \Delta B_i=0 for i=1,2,3.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Second partial derivatives; Divergence and curl; Vector Laplacian. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Apply the vector Laplacian identity

Use the classical identity \Delta\mathbf B=\nabla(\nabla\cdot\mathbf B)-\nabla\times(\nabla\times\mathbf B). Both terms in parentheses vanish, so every component of \Delta\mathbf B is zero.
