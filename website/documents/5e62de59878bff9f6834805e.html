Statement

A minor is obtained from a subgraph by contracting edges, up to isomorphism. A clique is a set of pairwise adjacent vertices. A k-clique-sum identifies cliques of equal size at most k in two graphs and may delete edges within the identified clique. Define treewidth \operatorname{tw}(G) as the least k\ge0 for which G is obtainable by successive k-clique-sums of graphs on at most k+1 vertices. For finite graphs G,H, if \operatorname{tw}(G)<\operatorname{tw}(H), then H is not a minor of G.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Treewidth, clique sums and excluded minors involve interacting graph constructions beyond elementary graph terminology. A bright US college student should be able to understand the statement after about 30 minutes of focused background research; this rating concerns understanding the claim, not proving it.

Proof 1: Contrapose minor monotonicity

Use minor monotonicity of treewidth: if H were a minor of G, then \operatorname{tw}(H)\le\operatorname{tw}(G). This contradicts the assumed strict inequality.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the exact minor-monotonicity theorem and contrapose its integer inequality. Finite graphs and the locally defined minor and width notions are a small extension of elementary discrete mathematics.
