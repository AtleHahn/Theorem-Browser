Statement

Let n\ge1, let 0\le j<m be integers, and let 1\le q,r\le\infty, 1\le p<\infty and \theta\in[0,1] satisfy

\frac1p=\frac jn+\theta\left(\frac1r-\frac mn\right)+\frac{1-\theta}{q}, \qquad \frac jm\le\theta\le1,

where 1/\infty=0. Let u:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R belong to L^q(\mathbb R^n) and have weak derivatives of order m in L^r(\mathbb R^n). Impose the following exceptional-case conditions:

  • If j=0, q=\infty and rm<n, require that u tends to zero at infinity or belongs to L^s(\mathbb R^n) for some finite s\ge1.
  • If r>1 and m-j-n/r is a nonnegative integer, require \theta<1.

Then

\|D^j u\|_{L^p(\mathbb R^n)} \le C\|D^m u\|_{L^r(\mathbb R^n)}^{\theta} \|u\|_{L^q(\mathbb R^n)}^{1-\theta},

where C>0 depends only on j,m,n,q,r,\theta, not on u. Here D^k u is the array of all weak partial derivatives of order k, with D^0u=u; L^a uses Lebesgue measure and the usual integral norm for finite a and essential-supremum norm for a=\infty.
