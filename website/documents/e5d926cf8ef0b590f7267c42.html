Statement

For an integer n\ge1 and real numbers u_1,\ldots,u_n,v_1,\ldots,v_n, (\sum_i u_iv_i)^2\le(\sum_i u_i^2)(\sum_i v_i^2).

High school only
Level justification

Only finite real sums and elementary algebra occur.

Proof 1: Sum of squared two-coordinate determinants — outline

The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality can be proved using only elementary algebra for finite real sequences by observing that the difference of the right and the left hand side of the stated inequality is

{\displaystyle {\tfrac {1}{2}}\sum _{i=1}^{n}\sum _{j=1}^{n}(u_{i}v_{j}-u_{j}v_{i})^{2}\geq 0}
Level
High school only
Level justification

Expanding the displayed sum of squared two-coordinate expressions gives the difference of the two sides. Every summand is nonnegative, so only finite algebra is needed.

Proof 2: Nonnegative quadratic discriminant — outline

If all u_i=0, the inequality is immediate. Otherwise, consider the following quadratic polynomial in {\displaystyle x}

{\displaystyle (u_{1}x+v_{1})^{2}+\cdots +(u_{n}x+v_{n})^{2}={\biggl (}\sum _{i}u_{i}^{2}{\biggr )}x^{2}+2{\biggl (}\sum _{i}u_{i}v_{i}{\biggr )}x+\sum _{i}v_{i}^{2}.}

Since the latter polynomial is nonnegative, it has at most one real root, hence its discriminant is less than or equal to zero. That is,

{\displaystyle {\biggl (}\sum _{i}u_{i}v_{i}{\biggr )}^{2}-{\biggl (}\sum _{i}{u_{i}^{2}}{\biggr )}{\biggl (}\sum _{i}{v_{i}^{2}}{\biggr )}\leq 0.}
Level
High school only
Level justification

After excluding the all-zero leading coefficients, the displayed quadratic has positive leading coefficient and is nonnegative everywhere. Its discriminant is therefore nonpositive, exactly the claimed inequality.
