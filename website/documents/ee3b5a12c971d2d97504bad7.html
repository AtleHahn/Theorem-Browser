Statement

Let n\geq1 and let (\Omega,\Sigma,\mathbb P) be a probability space. Let X be a measurable map into the nonempty compact subsets of \mathbb R^n, with their Borel sigma-algebra for the Hausdorff distance

d_H(K,L)=\max\left\{\sup_{x\in K}\inf_{y\in L}\|x-y\|,\ \sup_{y\in L}\inf_{x\in K}\|x-y\|\right\}.

Assume

\mathbb E\|X\|<\infty,\qquad \|K\|=\max_{v\in K}\|v\|.

Define its set-valued expectation by

\mathbb E[X]=\{\mathbb E[V]: V:\Omega\to\mathbb R^n\text{ is measurable},\ \mathbb P(V\in X)=1,\ \mathbb E\|V\|<\infty\}.

Then

\operatorname{vol}_n(\mathbb E[X])^{1/n}\geq\mathbb E\left[\operatorname{vol}_n(X)^{1/n}\right],

where \|\cdot\| is the Euclidean norm and \operatorname{vol}_n is n-dimensional Lebesgue measure. The compact sets need not be convex.

Level justification

Proof 1: Atomic decomposition and Aumann convexification (external)

A supporting source can be found here (Section 4.2, pp.46–48; Balder Appendix Theorem A.4). The deduction below supplies the argument for the stored statement.

Supporting passages: Balder, Comments on purification in continuum games, Theorem A.4.

Use Aumann convexification on the nonatomic part as a named dependency (Balder Appendix Theorem A.4; translate X by ||X||(1,...,1) to meet its nonnegative formulation, then translate the integral back). Completeness is unnecessary for almost-everywhere selections, as Balder explicitly notes. The following atomic decomposition argument is supplied here: split the probability space into countably many atoms A_i of masses p_i and a nonatomic remainder A_0 of mass p_0. A measurable compact-set-valued map is almost surely constant K_i on each atom. Let B be its unnormalized Aumann integral on A_0 (or {0} if p_0=0). On A_0 convexification is valid; Brunn–Minkowski/Jensen for the Minkowski integral of compact convex sets gives vol(B)^(1/n)>=integral_A0 vol(conv X)^(1/n)>=integral_A0 vol(X)^(1/n). This Jensen step follows by simple convex-set approximation in expected Hausdorff distance, continuity of volume on compact convex sets, and the bound vol(K)^(1/n)<=c_n||K||. Now EX=B+sum_i p_i K_i, with uniformly convergent tails because sum_i p_i||K_i||<infinity; this set is compact. Fix x_i in K_i. For each m, B+sum_(i<=m)p_iK_i+sum_(i>m)p_ix_i is contained in EX. Finite Brunn–Minkowski therefore bounds vol(EX)^(1/n) below by vol(B)^(1/n)+sum_(i<=m)p_i vol(K_i)^(1/n). Let m increase. This is precisely the requested inequality, without convexifying the atomic K_i.
