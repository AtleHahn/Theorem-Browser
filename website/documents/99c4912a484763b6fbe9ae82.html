Statement

Let A,B,C,D,E,F be six distinct points in the real projective plane, in the indicated cyclic labeling. Suppose each of the following pairs consists of distinct lines, so that their intersections are defined:

P=AB\cap DE,\qquad Q=BC\cap EF,\qquad R=CD\cap FA.

If P,Q,R lie on one projective line, then A,B,C,D,E,F lie on a conic, possibly degenerate. Here the real projective plane consists of one-dimensional subspaces of \mathbb R^3, lines are given by nonzero homogeneous linear equations, and a conic is the zero set of a nonzero homogeneous quadratic polynomial.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Elementary linear algebra supplies projective points/lines via subspaces, and quadratic equations are familiar. These locally defined objects are a small extension, not a need for general projective geometry.

Proof 1: A determinant identity for six points (external)

The following editorial argument completes the stated scope. A supporting source is From Pascal’s Theorem to d-Constructible Curves (Traves Theorems 3 and 6 for context; full-scope determinant bridge given below.).

Own algebraic bridge: represent A,...,F by nonzero column vectors. Put p=(A cross B) cross (D cross E), q=(B cross C) cross (E cross F), r=(C cross D) cross (F cross A). Let V have rows (x^2,y^2,z^2,xy,yz,zx) for the six vertices. The polynomial identity det[p q r]=-det V holds for arbitrary vectors. It can be proved by taking A,B,C to the coordinate vectors on the dense open set det[A B C]!=0 and expanding the resulting 3 by 3 determinants; both sides transform by det(T)^4, so polynomial continuity extends it to every configuration. An exact integer-coefficient expansion in all 18 variables independently checked the identity (720 monomials on each side, zero sum). Collinearity makes det[p q r]=0; hence V has a nonzero kernel, whose coefficient vector is the required nonzero quadratic. The distinct-line hypotheses ensure p,q,r are nonzero; coincidences between them cause no problem. Stage 2 should present the short determinant proof explicitly and identify it as the editorial extension of the source.
