Statement

Let a group G act by isometries on a nonempty proper length space (X,d). A length space has distance equal to the infimum of lengths of curves joining the two points, and proper means that closed balls are compact. Suppose the action is properly discontinuous and cocompact: for every compact K\subseteq X the set \{g\in G:gK\cap K\ne\varnothing\} is finite, and the quotient X/G is compact.

Then G is finitely generated. For every finite generating set S and every p\in X, the orbit map

(G,d_S)\longrightarrow X,\qquad g\longmapsto gp

is a quasi-isometry. Here d_S(g,h) is the shortest word length of g^{-1}h in S\cup S^{-1}. Quasi-isometry means that there are constants L\ge1, C\ge0 such that

L^{-1}d_S(g,h)-C\le d(gp,hp)\le Ld_S(g,h)+C

for all g,h\in G, and every point of X is within distance C of the orbit.

Proof 1: Short orbit steps and word metrics (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Theorem5.2, p.16; word metrics p.15).

For any p, choose R\ge1 such that every point is within R of Gp. Compactness of the quotient supplies finitely many images of unit balls covering it; the union of the corresponding closed balls is a compact orbit-covering set. Properness makes A=\{g:d(p,gp)\le3R\} finite, including any stabilizer. Subdivide a curve from p to gp of length less than d(p,gp)+R into steps of distance at most R, and replace its intermediate points by orbit points within R. Consecutive orbit points differ by elements of A, giving |g|_A\le d(p,gp)/R+2 and d(p,gp)\le3R|g|_A. For another finite generating set S, expressing each element of A as an S-word gives |g|_S\le M|g|_A for a fixed M; the displacement of each S-generator bounds the reverse metric inequality. This also proves the assertion for every base point.
