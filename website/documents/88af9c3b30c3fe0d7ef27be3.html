Statement

Let \psi be a smooth complex scalar field on a four-dimensional Minkowski coordinate domain, let \eta^{\mu\nu} be the constant inverse Minkowski metric, and let m\in\mathbb R. For the density L=\eta^{\nu\mu}\partial_\nu\psi\,\partial_\mu\psi^*+m^2\psi\psi^*, assume the Euler–Lagrange equations for the field and its conjugate. Then j^\nu=i\eta^{\nu\mu}\bigl((\partial_\mu\psi)\psi^*-(\partial_\mu\psi^*)\psi\bigr) has \partial_\nu j^\nu=0.

Proof 1: Opposite phase generators for conjugate fields

The density L is invariant under transformations of the \psi field and its complex conjugate field \psi^* of the form

\psi\longrightarrow e^{i\theta}\psi,\qquad \psi^*\longrightarrow e^{-i\theta}\psi^*,

a complex rotation, with constant real \theta. In the limit when the phase \theta becomes infinitesimally small, \delta\theta, it may be taken as the parameter \varepsilon, while the generators \Psi are equal to i\psi and -i\psi^*, respectively.

For fields \varphi=(\psi,\psi^*), the field form of Noether's theorem states that if the infinitesimal transformation changes L by \varepsilon\partial_\mu\Lambda^\mu, then

j^\nu=\Lambda^\nu-\frac{\partial L}{\partial(\partial_\nu\varphi)}\cdot\Psi

is conserved on Euler–Lagrange solutions; the dot contracts the field indices. Here \Lambda^\nu=0. For the Lagrangian density

L=\partial_\nu\psi\,\partial_\mu\psi^*\,\eta^{\nu\mu}+m^2\psi\psi^*,

Noether's theorem states that the conserved current equals

j^\nu =i\left(\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x^\mu}\psi^* -\frac{\partial\psi^*}{\partial x^\mu}\psi\right)\eta^{\nu\mu}, \qquad \partial_\nu j^\nu=0.
