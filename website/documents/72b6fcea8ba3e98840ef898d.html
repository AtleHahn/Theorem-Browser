Truth in all finite structures has no sound and complete effectively enumerable proof calculus for the stated relational languages. Restricting attention to finite worlds therefore does not remove foundational limits, and first-order completeness for unrestricted models does not transfer to finite-model validity.

Statement

Let \tau be a finite relational first-order vocabulary, and use effectively coded sentences and finite nonempty \tau-structures. Assume \tau contains a binary relation symbol. There is no proof calculus with an effective enumeration of its theorems that is both sound and complete for truth in all finite nonempty \tau-structures.

Proof 1: Completeness would enumerate all finite validities

Use the theorem that finite validity for this vocabulary is not recursively enumerable. An effective sound and complete calculus would enumerate exactly those finitely valid sentences, contradicting that theorem.

Identify the enumerated theorems of a sound complete calculus with finite-model validities. Finite first-order semantics, effective syntax and recursive enumerability form cumulative logic prerequisites not confidently within one small extension of ordinary undergraduate background.
