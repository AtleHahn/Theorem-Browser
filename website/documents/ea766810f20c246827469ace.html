Statement

Let P(x)=a_0\prod_{i=1}^D(x-\alpha_i) be a nonzero polynomial with integer coefficients and leading coefficient a_0\ne0. Define its multiplicative Mahler measure by M(P)=|a_0|\prod_{i=1}^D\max(1,|\alpha_i|). Then M(P) is a positive real algebraic number.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The measure is explicitly a finite product involving polynomial roots and their complex absolute values. Algebraic means satisfying an integer-coefficient polynomial; understanding the claim requires elementary polynomial algebra. This rating concerns understanding the statement, independently of the difficulty of its proof.

Proof 1: Algebraic factors in the root-product formula

Every root \alpha_i is algebraic. Algebraic numbers are closed under complex conjugation, multiplication and taking square roots, so |\alpha_i|=\sqrt{\alpha_i\overline{\alpha_i}} is algebraic. Each \max(1,|\alpha_i|) is therefore algebraic, as is the finite product multiplied by the integer |a_0|. Its factors are positive, so M(P) is positive.

Within undergraduate level
