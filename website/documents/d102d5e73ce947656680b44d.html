A lattice has finitely many rotational symmetries fixing a point

Statement

Let L=\{k_1v_1+\cdots+k_dv_d:k_i\in\mathbb Z\} for a basis v_1,\ldots,v_d of \mathbb R^d, where d\ge1 is an integer. For each fixed c\in\mathbb R^d, only finitely many Euclidean rotations fixing c preserve L.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Real Euclidean lattices; Rotations fixing a point; Finite families of transformations. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Discreteness bounds the rotation orbits — outline

Discreteness gives a positive lower bound on the distance between lattice points. Rotation orbits about a fixed point are bounded, and this spacing bound allows only finitely many lattice points in each orbit. It follows that the family of lattice rotational symmetries fixing that point is finite.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Bound each orbit by lattice separation and then use d+1 affinely independent lattice points to determine the rotation. Undergraduate Euclidean linear algebra and compactness suffice.
