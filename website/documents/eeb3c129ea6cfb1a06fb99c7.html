Statement

Let p:V\to[0,\infty) on a real or complex vector space satisfy p(\lambda u)=|\lambda|p(u) and p(u)=0 exactly when u=0. Then p(u+v)\le p(u)+p(v) for all u,v if and only if |p(u)-p(v)|\le p(u-v) for all u,v. In particular every norm satisfies the reverse triangle inequality.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The full metric/norm axioms and inequalities are displayed. Real analysis and linear algebra suffice to understand the equivalence, without any added structure.

Proof 1: Rearrange the inequalities in both directions

The proof of the reverse triangle inequality from the usual one uses

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}p(v-u)&=p({-}1(u-v))\\[2pt]&=|{-}1|\cdot p(u-v)\\[2pt]&=p(u-v)\end{aligned}}}

to find:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}p(u)=p((u-v)+v)&\leq p(u-v)+p(v)\\[4pt]\Rightarrow \quad p(u)-p(v)&\leq p(u-v),\\[10pt]p(v)=p((v-u)+u)&\leq p(v-u)+p(u)\\[4pt]\Rightarrow \quad p(u)-p(v)&\geq -p(u-v),\end{aligned}}}

Combining these two statements gives:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}&-p(u-v)\leq p(u)-p(v)\leq p(u-v)\\[4pt]&\Rightarrow \quad {\big |}p(u)-p(v){\big |}\leq p(u-v).\end{aligned}}}

In the converse, the proof of the triangle inequality from the reverse triangle inequality works in two cases:

  • If {\displaystyle p(u+v)-p(u)\geq 0,} then by the reverse triangle inequality,

    {\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}&p(u+v)-p(u)={\big |}p(u+v)-p(u){\big |}\leq p((u+v)-u)=p(v)\\[4pt]&\Rightarrow \quad p(u+v)\leq p(u)+p(v)\end{aligned}}}

  • And if {\displaystyle p(u+v)-p(u)<0,} then trivially

    {\displaystyle p(u)+p(v)\geq p(u)>p(u+v)}

    by the nonnegativity of p.

Thus, in both cases, we find that

{\displaystyle p(u)+p(v)\geq p(u+v).}
