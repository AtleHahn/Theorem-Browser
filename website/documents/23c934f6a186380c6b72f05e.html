Statement

Let X,Y be nonnegative real random variables with finite expectations \mathbb E X<\mathbb E Y, and suppose X\le_{st}Y, meaning \Pr(X>x)\le\Pr(Y>x) for every real x. Then the function h(x)=(\Pr(Y>x)-\Pr(X>x))/(\mathbb E Y-\mathbb E X) for x\ge0, and h(x)=0 for x<0, is a probability density. In particular there exists a nonnegative absolutely continuous random variable Z with this density.

Proof 1: Normalize the ordered tail difference

Let X and Y be non-negative random variables such that E[X] < E[Y] < ∞ and {\displaystyle X\leq _{st}Y} (i.e. X is smaller than Y in the usual stochastic order). Then there exists an absolutely continuous non-negative random variable Z having probability density function

{\displaystyle f_{Z}(x)={\Pr(Y>x)-\Pr(X>x) \over {\rm {E}}[Y]-{\rm {E}}[X]}\,,\qquad x\geqslant 0.}

The source asserts that this formula defines the density; it does not supply a proof of normalization.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the ordered tails for nonnegativity and the stated tail-expectation identity for normalization. Undergraduate real-variable probability and monotone tail functions suffice; the proof needs no construction of a general measure space or conditional expectation.
