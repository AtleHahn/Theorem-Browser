Statement

For every nonconstant polynomial P\in\mathbb C[z], every zero of P' lies in the convex hull of the zeros of P. The convex hull is the set of all convex combinations of those finitely many points in the complex plane.

Level justification

Proof 1: Express a critical point as a convex combination

By the fundamental theorem of algebra, {\displaystyle P} is a product of linear factors as

{\displaystyle P(z)=\alpha \prod _{i=1}^{n}(z-a_{i})}

where the complex numbers {\displaystyle a_{1},a_{2},\ldots ,a_{n}} are the – not necessarily distinct – zeros of the polynomial P, the complex number α is the leading coefficient of P and n is the degree of P.

For any root {\displaystyle z} of {\displaystyle P'}, if it is also a root of {\displaystyle P}, then the theorem is trivially true. Otherwise, we have for the logarithmic derivative

0=\frac{P^{\prime}(z)}{P(z)}=\sum_{i=1}^n\frac{1}{z-a_i}=\sum_{i=1}^n\frac{\overline z-\overline{a_i}}{|z-a_i|^2}.

Hence

{\displaystyle \sum _{i=1}^{n}{\frac {\overline {z}}{|z-a_{i}|^{2}}}=\sum _{i=1}^{n}{\frac {\overline {a_{i}}}{|z-a_{i}|^{2}}}}.

Taking their conjugates, and dividing, we obtain {\displaystyle z} as a convex sum of the roots of {\displaystyle P}:

{\displaystyle z=\sum _{i=1}^{n}{\frac {|z-a_{i}|^{-2}}{\sum _{j=1}^{n}|z-a_{j}|^{-2}}}a_{i}}

The weights are positive and sum to one.
