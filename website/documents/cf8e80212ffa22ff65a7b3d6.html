Statement

Let A,B,C be distinct points on a Euclidean circle with diameter AC. Then \angle ABC=90^\circ.

Proof 1: Equal-radius isosceles triangles

The following facts are used: the sum of the angles in a triangle is equal to 180° and the base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal.

Let O be the center of the circle.

Since OA = OB = OC, △OBA and △OBC are isosceles triangles, and by the equality of the base angles of an isosceles triangle, ∠ OBC = ∠ OCB and ∠ OBA = ∠ OAB.

Let α = ∠ BAO and β = ∠ OBC. The three internal angles of the ∆ABC triangle are α, (α + β), and β. Since the sum of the angles of a triangle is equal to 180°, we have

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\alpha +(\alpha +\beta )+\beta &=180^{\circ }\\2\alpha +2\beta &=180^{\circ }\\2(\alpha +\beta )&=180^{\circ }\\\therefore \alpha +\beta &=90^{\circ }.\end{aligned}}}

Q.E.D.

Split the angle into the base angles of two equal-radius isosceles triangles and use the triangle angle sum. School geometry and algebra suffice.

Proof 2: Trigonometric slope product

The theorem may also be proven using trigonometry: By a similarity, let O = (0, 0), A = (−1, 0), and C = (1, 0). Then B is a point on the unit circle (cos θ, sin θ). We will show that △ABC forms a right angle by proving that AB and BC are perpendicular — that is, the product of their slopes is equal to −1. Since B is distinct from A and C, the slope denominators and \sin\theta are nonzero. We calculate the slopes for AB and BC:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}m_{AB}&={\frac {y_{B}-y_{A}}{x_{B}-x_{A}}}={\frac {\sin \theta }{\cos \theta +1}}\\[2pt]m_{BC}&={\frac {y_{C}-y_{B}}{x_{C}-x_{B}}}={\frac {-\sin \theta }{-\cos \theta +1}}\end{aligned}}}

Then we show that their product equals −1:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}&m_{AB}\cdot m_{BC}\\[4pt]={}&{\frac {\sin \theta }{\cos \theta +1}}\cdot {\frac {-\sin \theta }{-\cos \theta +1}}\\[4pt]={}&{\frac {-\sin ^{2}\theta }{-\cos ^{2}\theta +1}}\\[4pt]={}&{\frac {-\sin ^{2}\theta }{\sin ^{2}\theta }}\\[4pt]={}&{-1}\end{aligned}}}

Note the use of the Pythagorean trigonometric identity {\displaystyle \sin ^{2}\theta +\cos ^{2}\theta =1.}

Proof 3: Half-turn and equal diagonals

Let \triangle ABC be a triangle in a circle where AC is a diameter in that circle. Then construct a new triangle \triangle ACD by rotating \triangle ABC by 180^\circ about the center of the circle O. Since we rotated through 180^\circ, lines AB and CD are parallel, likewise for AD and CB. It follows that the quadrilateral ABCD is a parallelogram. The points B and D are opposite under the half-turn about O, so BD is a straight line through O, meaning BD is a diameter of the circle.

Since lines AC and BD, the diagonals of the parallelogram, are both diameters of the circle and therefore have equal length, the parallelogram must be a rectangle. All angles in a rectangle are right angles.

Proof 4: Cancel the radius terms in a dot product

Let O and R be the center and radius of the circle.

The theorem can be proved using vector algebra. Let's take the vectors {\displaystyle {\overrightarrow {AB}}} and {\displaystyle {\overrightarrow {CB}}}. These vectors satisfy

{\displaystyle {\overrightarrow {AB}}={\overrightarrow {AO}}+{\overrightarrow {OB}}\qquad \qquad {\overrightarrow {CB}}={\overrightarrow {CO}}+{\overrightarrow {OB}}}

and their dot product can be expanded as

{\displaystyle {\overrightarrow {AB}}\cdot {\overrightarrow {CB}}=\left({\overrightarrow {AO}}+{\overrightarrow {OB}}\right)\cdot \left({\overrightarrow {CO}}+{\overrightarrow {OB}}\right)={\overrightarrow {AO}}\cdot {\overrightarrow {CO}}+({\overrightarrow {AO}}+{\overrightarrow {CO}})\cdot {\overrightarrow {OB}}+{\overrightarrow {OB}}\cdot {\overrightarrow {OB}}}

but

{\displaystyle {\overrightarrow {AO}}=-{\overrightarrow {CO}}\qquad \qquad {\overrightarrow {AO}}\cdot {\overrightarrow {CO}}=-R^{2}\qquad \qquad {\overrightarrow {OB}}\cdot {\overrightarrow {OB}}=R^{2}}

and the dot product vanishes

{\displaystyle {\overrightarrow {AB}}\cdot {\overrightarrow {CB}}=-R^{2}+{\overrightarrow {0}}\cdot {\overrightarrow {OB}}+R^{2}=0}

and then the vectors {\displaystyle {\overrightarrow {AB}}} and {\displaystyle {\overrightarrow {CB}}} are orthogonal and the angle ABC is a right angle.
