Statement

Let (X,\mathcal F,\mu) be a measure space with positive measure \mu, and let A_n\in\mathcal F satisfy \sum_n\mu(A_n)<\infty. Then \mu(\bigcap_{N\ge1}\bigcup_{n\ge N}A_n)=0.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The hypothesis involves arbitrary measure spaces, not only finite probability calculations.

Proof 1: Finite-measure tail unions and subadditivity (outline)

The sequence of sets \{\bigcup_{n=N}^\infty A_n\}_{N=1}^\infty is non-increasing:

\bigcup_{n=1}^\infty A_n\supseteq \bigcup_{n=2}^\infty A_n\supseteq\cdots\supseteq \bigcup_{n=N}^\infty A_n\supseteq \bigcup_{n=N+1}^\infty A_n\supseteq\cdots\supseteq \limsup_{n\to\infty}A_n.

By subadditivity, \mu(\bigcup_{n=1}^\infty A_n)\leq\sum_{n=1}^\infty\mu(A_n)<\infty, so continuity from above applies and gives

\mu\left(\limsup_{n\to\infty}A_n\right) =\lim_{N\to\infty}\mu\left(\bigcup_{n=N}^\infty A_n\right).

By subadditivity,

\mu\left(\bigcup_{n=N}^\infty A_n\right) \leq\sum_{n=N}^\infty\mu(A_n).

By the original assumption, \sum_{n=1}^\infty\mu(A_n)<\infty. As this series converges,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{n=N}^\infty\mu(A_n)=0,

as required.
