Statement

Let X be a real vector space, M\subseteq X a vector subspace, f:M\to\mathbb R linear, and p:X\to\mathbb R convex, meaning p(tu+(1-t)v)\le tp(u)+(1-t)p(v) for 0\le t\le1. Assume f\le p on M. There exists a linear F:X\to\mathbb R extending f with F\le p on X.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra supplies subspaces and linear functionals; analysis supplies norm bounds and countability. Sublinear, convex or seminorm conditions are explicitly given inequalities, and the functional norm is a unit-ball supremum. These direct extensions suffice without locally convex or dual-topology theory.

Proof 1: Real Hahn–Banach extension dominated by a convex function — outline

Assume that {\displaystyle p} is convex, which means that {\displaystyle p(ty+(1-t)z)\leq tp(y)+(1-t)p(z)} for all {\displaystyle 0\leq t\leq 1} and {\displaystyle y,z\in X.} Let {\displaystyle M,} {\displaystyle f:M\to \mathbb {R} ,} and {\displaystyle x\in X\setminus M} be as in the lemma's statement. Given any {\displaystyle m,n\in M} and any positive real {\displaystyle r,s>0,} the positive real numbers {\displaystyle t:={\tfrac {s}{r+s}}} and {\displaystyle {\tfrac {r}{r+s}}=1-t} sum to {\displaystyle 1} so that the convexity of {\displaystyle p} on {\displaystyle X} guarantees

{\displaystyle {\begin{alignedat}{9}p\left({\tfrac {s}{r+s}}m+{\tfrac {r}{r+s}}n\right)~&=~p{\big (}{\tfrac {s}{r+s}}(m-rx)&&+{\tfrac {r}{r+s}}(n+sx){\big )}&&\\&\leq ~{\tfrac {s}{r+s}}\;p(m-rx)&&+{\tfrac {r}{r+s}}\;p(n+sx)&&\\\end{alignedat}}}

and hence

{\displaystyle {\begin{alignedat}{9}sf(m)+rf(n)~&=~(r+s)\;f\left({\tfrac {s}{r+s}}m+{\tfrac {r}{r+s}}n\right)&&\qquad {\text{ by linearity of }}f\\&\leq ~(r+s)\;p\left({\tfrac {s}{r+s}}m+{\tfrac {r}{r+s}}n\right)&&\qquad f\leq p{\text{ on }}M\\&\leq ~sp(m-rx)+rp(n+sx)\\\end{alignedat}}}

thus proving that {\displaystyle -sp(m-rx)+sf(m)~\leq ~rp(n+sx)-rf(n),} which after multiplying both sides by {\displaystyle {\tfrac {1}{rs}}} becomes

{\displaystyle {\tfrac {1}{r}}[-p(m-rx)+f(m)]~\leq ~{\tfrac {1}{s}}[p(n+sx)-f(n)].}

This implies that the values defined by

{\displaystyle a=\sup _{\stackrel {m\in M}{r>0}}{\tfrac {1}{r}}[-p(m-rx)+f(m)]\qquad {\text{ and }}\qquad c=\inf _{\stackrel {n\in M}{s>0}}{\tfrac {1}{s}}[p(n+sx)-f(n)]}

are real numbers that satisfy {\displaystyle a\leq c.} For any real {\displaystyle b\in \mathbb {R} } define {\displaystyle F_{b}:M\oplus \mathbb {R} x\to \mathbb {R} } by {\displaystyle F_{b}(m+rx)=f(m)+rb.} It can be verified that if {\displaystyle a\leq b\leq c} then {\displaystyle F_{b}\leq p} where {\displaystyle rb\leq p(m+rx)-f(m)} follows from {\displaystyle b\leq c} when {\displaystyle r>0} (respectively, follows from {\displaystyle a\leq b} when {\displaystyle r<0}). Maximal extension. By Zorn’s lemma, the set of all possible dominated linear extensions of {\displaystyle f} are partially ordered by extension of each other, so there is a maximal extension {\displaystyle F.} By the one-dimensional construction just proved, if {\displaystyle F} is not defined on all of {\displaystyle X,} then it can be further extended. Thus {\displaystyle F} must be defined everywhere, as claimed. {\displaystyle \blacksquare }
