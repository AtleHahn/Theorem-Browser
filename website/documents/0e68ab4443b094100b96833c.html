Statement

Let X be a smooth projective surface over an algebraically closed field k of any characteristic. The vector space V=\operatorname{NS}(X)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Q is finite-dimensional; write \rho=\dim_{\mathbb Q}V. The intersection pairing on V has signature (1,\rho-1). If H is the class of a hyperplane section in a projective embedding of X, then H\cdot H=\deg X>0 and V has a direct-sum decomposition into \mathbb QH and a subspace on which the pairing is negative definite.

Here \operatorname{NS}(X) is the group of divisors on X modulo algebraic equivalence. A divisor is a finite integer linear combination of irreducible curves. Algebraic equivalence is generated by differences of fibers in algebraic families of divisors parametrized by a smooth connected algebraic curve; equivalently, for the associated line bundles it is generated by \mathcal L|_{X\times\{s\}}\otimes(\mathcal L|_{X\times\{t\}})^{-1}, where \mathcal L is a line bundle on X\times C and s,t\in C(k). A line bundle is a locally free sheaf of rank one. Smooth projective surface means a smooth integral projective variety of dimension two over k.

For precision, the intersection of two divisor classes with associated line bundles L,M can be defined by

L\cdot M=\chi(\mathcal O_X)-\chi(L^{-1})-\chi(M^{-1})+\chi(L^{-1}\otimes M^{-1}),

where \chi(E)=\sum_{i=0}^2(-1)^i\dim_k H^i(X,E) is the Euler characteristic of coherent sheaf cohomology. Extend this pairing rationally to V. Signature means that after extension to real scalars its matrix has one positive and \rho-1 negative eigenvalues, with no zero eigenvalues. The degree is the number of intersections with a general linear subspace of complementary dimension, counted with multiplicity.
