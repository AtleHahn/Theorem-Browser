Statement

Let A\in\mathbb R^{m\times n} and B\in\mathbb R^{n\times k}, where m,n,k are positive integers, and S=\{Bx:x\in\mathbb R^k,\ x\ge0\}. Then AS is closed in Euclidean space \mathbb R^m.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra supplies linear maps, subspaces, images, quotients and matrix products. Norm bounds use a unit-ball supremum, and closedness is ordinary Euclidean topology. All additional objects are explicitly defined in these familiar terms.

Proof 1: A linear image of a finitely generated cone is closed — outline

For a polyhedral convex cone represented as S=\{Bx:x\ge0\}, the closedness condition on its linear image AS holds automatically, by the closedness of finitely generated cones.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Observe AS is generated by the finitely many columns of AB and apply the supplied closed-cone theorem. Undergraduate linear algebra and Euclidean topology suffice; the cone is explicitly a set of nonnegative linear combinations.
