Statement

Let V be a real Hilbert space with norm \|\cdot\|, and let a:V\times V\to\mathbb R be bilinear with |a(v,w)|\le\gamma\|v\|\|w\| and a(v,v)\ge\alpha\|v\|^2 for all v,w\in V, where \gamma,\alpha>0. Let L:V\to\mathbb R be bounded and linear, and let V_h\subseteq V be finite-dimensional. Assume a is symmetric. Let u\in V and u_h\in V_h satisfy a(u,v)=L(v) for all v\in V and a(u_h,v)=L(v) for all v\in V_h. Write \|w\|_a=\sqrt{a(w,w)}. Then \|u-u_h\|_a\le\|u-v\|_a for every v\in V_h, so u_h is the best approximation to u in this norm.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra supplies linear functionals and bilinear forms; real analysis supplies norm bounds and completeness. The interpolation or variational equations are explicit. These direct normed/Hilbert-space extensions require no dual-space topology or PDE background.

Proof 1: Orthogonality and Cauchy–Schwarz

For every w\in V_h, the two variational equations give a(u,w)=L(w)=a(u_h,w), hence a(u-u_h,w)=0.

The bilinear form is symmetric, so

{\displaystyle a(v,w)=a(w,v)} for all {\displaystyle v,w} in {\displaystyle V.}

This, together with the continuity and coercivity properties of this form in the statement, implies that {\displaystyle a(\cdot ,\cdot )} is an inner product on {\displaystyle V.} The resulting norm

{\displaystyle \|v\|_{a}={\sqrt {a(v,v)}}}

is called the energy norm, since it corresponds to a physical energy in many problems. This norm is equivalent to the original norm {\displaystyle \|\cdot \|.}

Using the {\displaystyle a}-orthogonality of {\displaystyle u-u_{h}} and {\displaystyle V_{h}} and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality

{\displaystyle \|u-u_{h}\|_{a}^{2}=a(u-u_{h},u-u_{h})=a(u-u_{h},u-v)\leq \|u-u_{h}\|_{a}\cdot \|u-v\|_{a}} for all {\displaystyle v} in {\displaystyle V_{h}}.

If u=u_h the conclusion is immediate; otherwise divide by \|u-u_h\|_a. Hence

{\displaystyle \|u-u_{h}\|_{a}\leq \|u-v\|_{a}} for all {\displaystyle v} in {\displaystyle V_{h}}

This states that the subspace solution {\displaystyle u_{h}} is the best approximation to the full-space solution {\displaystyle u} in respect to the energy norm. Geometrically, this means that {\displaystyle u_{h}} is the projection of the solution {\displaystyle u} onto the subspace {\displaystyle V_{h}} in respect to the inner product {\displaystyle a(\cdot ,\cdot )}.

Subtract the two given equations to get orthogonality, view the explicitly positive symmetric form as an inner product and apply Cauchy-Schwarz. Hilbert completeness and existence theory are not used; norms and bilinear forms are a small extension of undergraduate inner-product algebra.
