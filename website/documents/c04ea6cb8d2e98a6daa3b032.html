Statement

Let p_\eta(x)=g(\eta)h(x)e^{\eta T(x)} be normalized densities relative to a fixed measure \mu, for a scalar natural parameter \eta in an open interval of finite normalizers. Put A(\eta)=-\log g(\eta). Then \mathbb E_\eta T(X)=-g'(\eta)/g(\eta)=A'(\eta).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement includes measure probability and a parameter-dependent density.

Proof 1: Differentiate the integral of the density

Writing p(x)=p_\eta(x), in the one-dimensional case we have

{\displaystyle p(x)=g(\eta )h(x)e^{\eta T(x)}.}

This must be normalized, so

{\displaystyle 1=\int _{x}p(x)\,d\mu(x)=\int _{x}g(\eta )h(x)e^{\eta T(x)}\,d\mu(x)=g(\eta )\int _{x}h(x)e^{\eta T(x)}\,d\mu(x).}

Take the derivative of both sides with respect to η:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}0&=g(\eta ){\frac {d}{d\eta }}\int _{x}h(x)e^{\eta T(x)}\,d\mu(x)+g'(\eta )\int _{x}h(x)e^{\eta T(x)}\,d\mu(x)\\[1ex]&=g(\eta )\int _{x}h(x)\left({\frac {d}{d\eta }}e^{\eta T(x)}\right)\,d\mu(x)+g'(\eta )\int _{x}h(x)e^{\eta T(x)}\,d\mu(x)\\[1ex]&=g(\eta )\int _{x}h(x)e^{\eta T(x)}T(x)\,d\mu(x)+g'(\eta )\int _{x}h(x)e^{\eta T(x)}\,d\mu(x)\\[1ex]&=\int _{x}T(x)g(\eta )h(x)e^{\eta T(x)}\,d\mu(x)+{\frac {g'(\eta )}{g(\eta )}}\int _{x}g(\eta )h(x)e^{\eta T(x)}\,d\mu(x)\\[1ex]&=\int _{x}T(x)p(x)\,d\mu(x)+{\frac {g'(\eta )}{g(\eta )}}\int _{x}p(x)\,d\mu(x)\\[1ex]&=\operatorname {E} [T(x)]+{\frac {g'(\eta )}{g(\eta )}}\\[1ex]&=\operatorname {E} [T(x)]+{\frac {d}{d\eta }}\log g(\eta )\end{aligned}}}

Therefore,

{\displaystyle \operatorname {E} [T(x)]=-{\frac {d}{d\eta }}\log g(\eta )={\frac {d}{d\eta }}A(\eta ).}

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The algebraic differentiation is elementary, but the proof interchanges differentiation and integration for densities relative to an arbitrary measure using finite exponential normalizers. General measure integration and a dominated parameter-integral argument exceed a small extension of ordinary calculus.
