Statement

A finite simple graph consists of finitely many vertices joined by edges, with no loops or repeated edges. It is planar if it has a drawing in the plane whose edges meet only at common endpoints. In a fixed plane drawing, suppose a vertex v has exactly five distinct neighbors v_1,\ldots,v_5 in cyclic order. Then at least one of the pairs \{v_1,v_3\} and \{v_2,v_4\} is nonadjacent.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The claim concerns two specified pairs of five neighbors in a finite plane drawing; vertices, edges and noncrossing incidence are locally defined elementary finite geometry.

Proof 1: Use the separating three-cycle — outline

If v_1 is not adjacent to v_3, the first pair suffices. Otherwise the cycle v,v_1,v_3 separates v_2 from v_4 in the plane drawing. An edge from v_2 to v_4 would cross this cycle. Hence the second pair is nonadjacent.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the cycle through v, v1 and v3 to separate the alternating neighbors. The embedding and cyclic-order application of simple-cycle separation requires basic undergraduate planar topology.
