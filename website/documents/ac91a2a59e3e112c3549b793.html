Statement

Let (X,\mathcal A,\mu) and (Y,\mathcal B,\nu) be sigma-finite measure spaces, with product measure \mu\times\nu. If f:X\times Y\to\mathbb R is measurable and \int|f|\,d(\mu\times\nu)<\infty, then the two iterated integrals exist after arbitrary definition of undefined inner integrals on null sets, and both equal \int f\,d(\mu\times\nu).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The assertion is about Lebesgue integration on product measure spaces.

Proof 1: Fubini’s theorem — outline

Write f=f^+-f^-, where f^+=\max(f,0) and f^-=\max(-f,0) are nonnegative integrable functions. Apply Tonelli’s theorem to each, and subtract the resulting equalities.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Split a Lebesgue-integrable function into its positive and negative parts and apply Tonelli. The full assertion concerns sigma-finite product measures and almost-everywhere sections, not ordinary continuous Riemann integrals.
