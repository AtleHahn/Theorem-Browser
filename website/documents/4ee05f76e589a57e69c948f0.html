Statement

Propositions are finite Boolean expressions, AB denotes conjunction and \neg A negation. Write v(A\mid X) for a numerical plausibility assigned to A under information X; assume logically equivalent propositions and conditioning information give the same values whenever the expressions used are defined. Assume these values lie in [0,1] and satisfy v(AB\mid X)=v(A\mid X)v(B\mid AX) for the displayed conditioning expressions. Assume also a function f:[0,1]\to[0,1] represents negation: v(\neg A\mid X)=f(v(A\mid X)). If v(A\mid X)>0 and v(B\mid X)>0, then v(A\mid X)f\!\left(\frac{v(A\neg B\mid X)}{v(A\mid X)}\right)=v(B\mid X)f\!\left(\frac{v(B\neg A\mid X)}{v(B\mid X)}\right).

Level
High school only
Level justification

Plausibility is an explicitly axiomatized real assignment to Boolean expressions; all product and negation laws needed are supplied.

Proof 1: Symmetry of the complement-product expression

Double negation gives f(f(v(B\mid AX)))=v(B\mid AX). The product and negation rules yield v(AB\mid X)=v(A\mid X)f(v(\neg B\mid AX))=v(A\mid X)f\!\left(\frac{v(A\neg B\mid X)}{v(A\mid X)}\right). Interchanging A,B and using AB\equiv BA gives the same value and hence equality of the two expressions.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Apply the explicitly assumed product and negation rules, use double negation and commute the conjunction. School function substitution, division by positive values and Boolean reasoning suffice; no measure-theoretic probability is involved.
