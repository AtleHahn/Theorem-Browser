Statement

Suppose there is a deterministic algorithm which, given the length n in unary, constructs in time polynomial in n a polynomial-size Boolean circuit deciding satisfiability of every encoded Boolean formula of length n. Then \mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}, where \mathsf P and \mathsf{NP} denote deterministic and nondeterministic polynomial time.

Proof 1: Construct and evaluate a SAT circuit

Suppose that polynomial sized circuits for SAT not only exist, but also that they could be constructed by a polynomial time algorithm. Then this supposition implies that SAT itself could be solved by a polynomial time algorithm that constructs the circuit and then applies it. That is, efficiently constructible circuits for SAT would lead to \mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}, using the NP-completeness of SAT (the Cook–Levin theorem).
