Statement

For s>0, define \Gamma(s)=\int_0^\infty t^{s-1}e^{-t}\,dt. Then \Gamma(1/2)=\sqrt\pi.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Real improper integrals; Gamma function defined by an integral. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Gamma function at one half — proof

The integrand is an even function,

{\displaystyle \int _{-\infty }^{\infty }e^{-x^{2}}dx=2\int _{0}^{\infty }e^{-x^{2}}dx}

Thus, after the change of variable {\textstyle x={\sqrt {t}}}, this turns into the Euler integral

{\displaystyle 2\int _{0}^{\infty }e^{-x^{2}}dx=2\int _{0}^{\infty }{\frac {1}{2}}\ e^{-t}\ t^{-{\frac {1}{2}}}dt=\Gamma {\left({\frac {1}{2}}\right)}={\sqrt {\pi }}}

where {\textstyle \Gamma (z)=\int _{0}^{\infty }t^{z-1}e^{-t}dt} is the gamma function. Here the Gaussian integral evaluation \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-x^2}\,dx=\sqrt\pi is used.

Level justification

Use evenness of the Gaussian integrand and substitute t=x squared to identify the gamma integral. This is ordinary improper integration.
