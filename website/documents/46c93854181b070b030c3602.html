Statement

Let F be a field and let A(x)=\sum_{i\ge m}a_ix^i and B(x)=\sum_{j\ge n}b_jx^j, with integer lower bounds m,n and coefficients in F. Each coefficient of their formal product C(x) is a finite sum: [x^k]C(x)=\sum_{i=m}^{k-n}a_i b_{k-i}, interpreted as zero when k<m+n. Hence multiplication is well defined and its product is again bounded below.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Formal series use polynomial-style coefficient arithmetic without a convergence requirement. Coefficient extraction and partitions/compositions are explicitly described finite counts for each degree. This is a small extension of familiar power series and polynomial algebra.

Proof 1: Source calculation

The coefficient of x^k only receives terms with i+j=k, i\ge m and j\ge n. Thus only the finitely many indices m\le i\le k-n contribute. There are no such indices for k<m+n. This is the source’s finite-coefficient-sum reason for allowing multiplication of Laurent series with only finitely many negative terms.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

For a fixed coefficient the lower bounds leave only finitely many contributing index pairs. Formal series over an arbitrary field are a small algebraic extension of undergraduate polynomial algebra; no analytic convergence is involved.
