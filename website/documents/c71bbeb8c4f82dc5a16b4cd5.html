Statement

Let 1\le r\le n and V be an n-dimensional vector space over a field of q elements. Fix 0\ne v\in V. The r-dimensional subspaces containing v form a family of size \binom{n-1}{r-1}_q, every two meeting in a nonzero subspace. Here the Gaussian binomial counts subspaces of the specified dimension.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory algebra supplies finite fields, polynomial evaluation, irreducibility and vector subspaces. The Mobius function, asymptotic notation or uniform finite counting probability is a limited numerical definition where used. These statements require no field-extension norm or Galois theory.

Proof 1: Choose subspaces through a nonzero vector — outline

An intersecting family of subspaces may be obtained by choosing any nonzero vector and constructing the family of subspaces of the given dimension that all contain the chosen vector. Its size is

\binom{n-1}{r-1}_q,

where the subscript q marks the notation for the Gaussian binomial coefficient, the number of subspaces of a given dimension within a vector space of a larger dimension over a finite field of order q.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Every subspace contains the chosen line, and quotienting by that line gives (r-1)-subspaces of an (n-1)-space. The Gaussian binomial is defined as this count. Undergraduate vector-space quotients suffice.
