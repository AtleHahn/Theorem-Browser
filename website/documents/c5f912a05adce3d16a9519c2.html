Statement

Let X be a real or complex Banach space and let V_1\subsetneq V_2\subsetneq\cdots be finite-dimensional linear subspaces. For every real sequence \epsilon_1\ge\epsilon_2\ge\cdots tending to zero, there exists x\in X such that

\inf_{v\in V_i}\|x-v\|=\epsilon_i\qquad(i\ge1).

A Banach space is a normed vector space complete in its norm metric. The infimum is the distance from x to the subspace; monotone convergence to zero implies all \epsilon_i\ge0.

Proof 1: Finite flags and compactness (external)

A historical or contextual reference can be found here (Theorem 1.1, for context; the proof is given below). The complete argument below is supplied here and is not attributed to that reference.

The following direct proof is supplied here; Micherda is a contextual reference for the theorem. For each N construct x_N in V_(N+1) with norm epsilon_1 and dist(x_N,V_i)=epsilon_i for i≤N. Start with y in V_(N+1) whose distance to V_N equals epsilon_N and whose norm equals epsilon_N: subtract a best V_N-approximant from any vector outside V_N and scale (use zero if epsilon_N=0). Induct downward. If y has norm epsilon_i and the required distances for indices j≥i, choose v in V_i outside V_(i-1). The continuous function t↦dist(y+tv,V_(i-1)) starts at most epsilon_i≤epsilon_(i-1) and tends to infinity; choose t giving epsilon_(i-1). Subtract a best V_(i-1)-approximant, making the norm epsilon_(i-1). All distances to V_j for j≥i remain unchanged. Best approximants exist because the subspaces are finite-dimensional. This gives x_N. The sequence is bounded, and for each fixed i its tail lies within epsilon_i of a bounded subset of V_i. Since epsilon_i→0, it is totally bounded. Completeness gives a convergent subsequence; continuity of every distance function yields the desired limit. Real t suffices also in complex spaces.
