Statement

Let p(x,y,t) be a nonzero complex homogeneous polynomial of degree n\ge1, and let L be the projective line y=sx+mt, with s,m\in\mathbb C. Assume L is not a component of p=0, so q(x,t)=p(x,sx+mt,t) is not identically zero. Then L meets p=0 in exactly n points counted with multiplicity, where a point [x:y:t] is a nonzero coordinate triple up to common scaling and its multiplicity is the multiplicity of the corresponding linear factor of q.

Proof 1: Intersection count of a line and a complex plane curve

One may write the equation of the line in projective coordinates as {\displaystyle y=sx+mt.} If the curve is defined in projective coordinates by a homogeneous polynomial {\displaystyle p(x,y,t)} of degree n, the substitution of y provides a homogeneous polynomial of degree n in x and t. The fundamental theorem of algebra implies that it can be factored in linear factors. Each factor gives the ratio of the x and t coordinates of an intersection point, and the multiplicity of the factor is the multiplicity of the intersection point.

If t is viewed as the coordinate of infinity, a factor equal to t represents an intersection point at infinity.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Substituting the line equation gives an explicit nonzero homogeneous polynomial in two variables. Undergraduate complex polynomial algebra and linear algebra suffice; projective coordinates add only the supplied identification under common scaling. If q(z,1) has degree d<=n, ordinary factorization gives q(x,t)=c t^(n-d) product_(j=1)^d(x-alpha_j t), so the missing degree is counted at infinity. Multiplicity is explicitly defined by these factors, with no general intersection-theory machinery.
