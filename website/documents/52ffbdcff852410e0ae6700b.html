Statement

Let A be Hermitian on \mathbb C^n, n\ge1, with eigenvalues \lambda_1\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_n, repeated with multiplicity. Write q_A(x)=x^*Ax for unit vectors x. For 1\le k\le n and every k-dimensional subspace M, there are unit vectors x,y\in M with q_A(x)\le\lambda_k and q_A(y)\ge\lambda_{n-k+1}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Hermitian matrices; Eigenvalues; Finite-dimensional inner product spaces; Subspaces; Rayleigh quotients. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Dimension intersection and the negative operator

Let v_1,\ldots,v_n be the corresponding unit-length orthogonal eigenvectors. The second inequality is a corollary of the first, using -A, whose kth eigenvalue in descending order is -\lambda_{n-k+1}.

M is a k-dimensional subspace, so the span

N:=\operatorname{span}(v_k,\ldots,v_n)

of these n-k+1 linearly independent vectors must intersect M in at least a single line.

Take unit x\in M\cap N. That's what we need.

Since x\in N,

x=\sum_{i=k}^n a_i v_i.

Since \sum_{i=k}^n|a_i|^2=1, we find

q_A(x)=\langle x,Ax\rangle =\sum_{i=k}^n|a_i|^2\lambda_i\leq\lambda_k.

Level justification

Intersect the given subspace with the bottom-eigenvector span, expand a unit vector and average eigenvalues; apply the result to minus A for the reverse bound. Undergraduate spectral linear algebra suffices.
