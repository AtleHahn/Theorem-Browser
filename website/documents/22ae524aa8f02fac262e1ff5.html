Statement

Let X={u∈C1([0,1],R):u(0)=0} with the C1 norm, and Y=C([0,1],R) with the supremum norm. For all sufficiently small g∈Y, the equation u′+u²=g, u(0)=0 has a unique sufficiently small solution u∈X. The solution depends continuously Fréchet differentiably on g.

Proof 1: Small-forcing Riccati equations from Banach inversion — outline

Consider the Banach spaces {\displaystyle X\subset C^{1}([0,1])} of continuously-differentiable real-valued functions on the unit interval such that {\displaystyle f(0)=0}, and {\displaystyle Y=C([0,1])} of continuous functions on the unit interval. Define

{\displaystyle F:X\to Y,\qquad F(u)=u'+u^{2}.}

Then {\displaystyle F} is continuously Fréchet differentiable, and

{\displaystyle DF(u)h=h'+2uh.}

At {\displaystyle u=0}, the derivative is the operator {\displaystyle h\mapsto h'}. This is an isomorphism from {\displaystyle X} to {\displaystyle Y}, with inverse

{\displaystyle g\mapsto \left(t\mapsto \int _{0}^{t}g(s)\,ds\right).}

Therefore the inverse function theorem for Banach spaces implies that {\displaystyle F} is locally invertible near {\displaystyle 0}. In particular, for every sufficiently small {\displaystyle g\in C([0,1])}, the initial-value problem

{\displaystyle u'(t)+u(t)^{2}=g(t),\qquad u(0)=0,}

has a unique small solution {\displaystyle u\in C^{1}([0,1])}, and this solution depends continuously differentiably on {\displaystyle g}.
