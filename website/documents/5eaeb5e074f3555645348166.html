Statement

Let X,Y be Fréchet spaces (complete metrizable Hausdorff locally convex vector spaces), and let T:X\to Y be linear. Then T is continuous if and only if its graph is closed in X\times Y.

Level

Level justification

General locally convex complete metrizable vector spaces are outside calculus/linear algebra.

Proof 1: Applying Ursescu to the inverse relation

Use Ursescu’s theorem in the full-image case: a relation R:E\rightrightarrows F with closed convex graph, with E a complete metrizable locally convex space and F barrelled, and with image all of F, sends every neighborhood of x to a neighborhood of every y\in R(x). The full-image specialization applies because the relative algebraic interior and affine span of the image are both F. Fréchet spaces are barrelled.

For the non-trivial direction, assume that the graph of {\displaystyle T} is closed and let {\displaystyle {\mathcal {R}}:=T^{-1}:Y\rightrightarrows X.} It is easy to see that {\displaystyle \operatorname {gr} {\mathcal {R}}} is closed and convex and that its image is {\displaystyle X.} Given {\displaystyle x\in X,} {\displaystyle (Tx,x)} belongs to {\displaystyle Y\times X} so that, by Ursescu’s theorem, for every open neighborhood {\displaystyle V} of {\displaystyle Tx} in {\displaystyle Y,} {\displaystyle {\mathcal {R}}(V)=T^{-1}(V)} is a neighborhood of {\displaystyle x} in {\displaystyle X.} Thus {\displaystyle T} is continuous at {\displaystyle x.}

Conversely, a continuous map into a Hausdorff space has closed graph.
