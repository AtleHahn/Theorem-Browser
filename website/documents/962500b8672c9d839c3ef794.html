Statement

Let Y=\delta(X) be an integrable real random variable on a probability space, \mathcal G a sub-sigma-algebra, and L:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R convex with L(Y) integrable. Then \mathbb E\left[L(\mathbb E[Y\mid\mathcal G])\right]\le\mathbb E[L(Y)].

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Conditional expectation relative to an arbitrary sub-sigma-algebra requires measure-theoretic probability.

Proof 1: Conditional Jensen followed by expectation

If L is a convex function and \mathcal G a sub-sigma-algebra, then, from the conditional version of Jensen's inequality, we get

L(\mathbb E[\delta(X)\mid\mathcal G]) \leq\mathbb E[L(\delta(X))\mid\mathcal G] \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mathbb E[L(\mathbb E[\delta(X)\mid\mathcal G])] \leq\mathbb E[L(\delta(X))].

The implication takes expectations and uses the tower property; here \delta(X)=Y as in the statement.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply conditional Jensen and then the tower rule for a general sub-sigma-algebra. Conditional expectation in this measure-theoretic sense is substantially beyond elementary conditioning on finite events.
