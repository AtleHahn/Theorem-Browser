Statement

Let a<b be real, let (x_n) be distinct points of (a,b), and let \lambda_n,\mu_n\geq0 have finite total sum, with \lambda_n+\mu_n>0 for every n. Define the normalized nonnegative jump function f:[a,b]\to\mathbb R by f(x)=\sum_{x_n\leq x}\lambda_n+\sum_{x_n<x}\mu_n. Then the ordinary derivative exists and equals zero at Lebesgue-almost every point of (a,b).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Real analysis supplies convergent nonnegative series, ordinary derivatives and secant slopes. Null sets mean interval covers of arbitrarily small length, and the length of an open set is the sum of its disjoint interval lengths. These limited additions suffice without general integration.

Proof 1: Separate finitely many jumps from a small tail — outline

Local large-slope estimate

For c>0, let U_c(f) be the set of points x such that

\frac{f(t)-f(s)}{t-s}>c

for some a\leq s<x<t\leq b. We first show that U_c(f) is open and has total length \ell(U_c(f))\leq4c^{-1}(f(b)-f(a)).

By definition U_c(f) is an open subset of (a,b), so can be written as a disjoint union of at most countably many open intervals I_k=(a_k,b_k). Let J_k be an interval with closure in I_k and \ell(J_k)=\ell(I_k)/2. By compactness, there are finitely many open intervals of the form (s,t), with (f(t)-f(s))/(t-s)>c, covering the closure of J_k.

Use the elementary interval-cover thinning fact: the finite cover can be taken as adjacent open intervals (s_{k,1},t_{k,1}), (s_{k,2},t_{k,2}), … only intersecting at consecutive intervals. Hence

\ell(J_k)\leq\sum_m(t_{k,m}-s_{k,m}) \leq\sum_m c^{-1}(f(t_{k,m})-f(s_{k,m})) \leq2c^{-1}(f(b_k)-f(a_k)).

Finally sum both sides over k. Since \ell(I_k)=2\ell(J_k), monotonicity gives

\ell(U_c(f))\leq4c^{-1}(f(b)-f(a)).

Control the shrinking secant slopes

To prove the derivative assertion, define

Df(x)=\limsup_{s,t\to x,\ s<x<t}\frac{f(t)-f(s)}{t-s},

a variant of the Dini derivative of f. It will suffice to prove that for any fixed c>0, the Dini derivative satisfies Df(x)\leq c almost everywhere, i.e. except on a null set.

Choose \varepsilon>0, arbitrarily small. Starting from the definition of the jump function f=\sum f_n, where f_n is the elementary jump at x_n with values 0,\lambda_n,\lambda_n+\mu_n, write f=g+h with g=\sum_{n\leq N}f_n and h=\sum_{n>N}f_n, where N\geq1. Thus g is a step function having only finitely many discontinuities at x_n for n\leq N and h is a non-negative jump function. It follows that Df=g'+Dh=Dh except at the N points of discontinuity of g. Choosing N sufficiently large so that \sum_{n>N}(\lambda_n+\mu_n)<\varepsilon, it follows that h is a jump function such that h(b)-h(a)<\varepsilon and, by the local large-slope estimate, Dh\leq c off an open set with length less than 4\varepsilon/c.

By construction Df\leq c off that open set together with the finitely many discontinuities of g. A finite set is null and can be covered by an open set of arbitrarily small length. Since \varepsilon is arbitrarily small, \{x:Df(x)>c\} is a null set. Thus Df\leq c almost everywhere. Taking a countable sequence of positive c tending to zero, Df and hence also f' must vanish almost everywhere.
