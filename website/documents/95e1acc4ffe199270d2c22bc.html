Statement

For integers r,s\geq1, there exists a sequence of (r-1)(s-1) distinct real numbers with no increasing subsequence of length r and no decreasing subsequence of length s. Thus the Erdős–Szekeres length threshold is sharp.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The sharpness statement concerns only finite sequences and numerical inequalities.

Proof 1: Rotate a rectangular grid — outline

Take an (r-1)-by-(s-1) rectangular grid of points and apply a sufficiently small rotation to obtain (r-1)(s-1) points without a path of r-1 positive-slope edges or s-1 negative-slope edges. Order the rotated points by their first coordinates and use their second coordinates as the sequence values.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Order a slightly rotated finite rectangular grid by first coordinate and read second coordinates. The construction uses plane slopes, finite orders and counting; no calculus or infinite limiting argument is required.

Routine omissions

The source gives no angle bound or slope-count verification. With r-1 horizontal positions and s-1 vertical positions a sufficiently small positive rotation has the required ordering; the choice of orientation and strict smallness are routine recipe parameters. If one dimension is zero the construction is the empty sequence.
