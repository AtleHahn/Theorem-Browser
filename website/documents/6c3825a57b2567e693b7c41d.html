Statement

Let K be a number field with ring of integers \mathcal O_K, let \mathfrak p be a nonzero prime ideal, and let \zeta\in\mathcal O_K be a primitive nth root of unity, where n\ge1 and n\notin\mathfrak p. Put q=|\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak p|. There is a set A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_m\}\subset\mathcal O_K, where m=(q-1)/n, such that the residues a_i\zeta^j for 1\le i\le m and 0\le j<n represent each nonzero residue exactly once.

Proof 1: Construction of a one-over-n system by removing cosets — outline

Use the fact that the residues of 1,\zeta,\ldots,\zeta^{n-1} form a subgroup of order n in (\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak p)^\times, whose cosets partition this finite group. A 1/n system means a set of representatives of these cosets.

Constructing a 1/n system is straightforward: let M be a representative set for (\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak p)^\times. Pick any a_1\in M and remove the numbers congruent to a_1,a_1\zeta,a_1\zeta^2,\dots,a_1\zeta^{n-1} from M. If any elements remain, pick a_2 from M and remove the numbers congruent to a_2,a_2\zeta,a_2\zeta^2,\dots,a_2\zeta^{n-1}. Repeat until M is exhausted. Then \{a_1,a_2,\dots,a_m\} is a 1/n system mod \mathfrak p.
