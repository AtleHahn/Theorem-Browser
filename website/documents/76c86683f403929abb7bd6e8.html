Statement

Let 0<p<q<\infty and let (a_i)_{i\in I} be real or complex numbers indexed by a countable set, with \sum_i|a_i|^p<\infty. For an integer N\ge1 not exceeding |I|, choose I_N containing indices of N largest absolute values. Then (\sum_{i\notin I_N}|a_i|^q)^{1/q}\le N^{1/q-1/p}(\sum_i|a_i|^p)^{1/p}.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Proof 1: Compare consecutive blocks of N terms — outline

Reorder the nonzero absolute values, appending zeros if only finitely many remain. W.l.o.g. we assume that the sequence {\displaystyle (a_{i})_{i\in I}} is sorted by {\displaystyle |a_{i+1}|\leq |a_{i}|,i\in I} and we set {\displaystyle I=\mathbb {N} } for notation, with \mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\} and I_N=\{1,\ldots,N\}.

First, we reformulate the statement of the lemma to

{\displaystyle \left({\frac {1}{N}}\sum _{i\in I\setminus I_{N}}|a_{i}|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\leq \left({\frac {1}{N}}\sum _{j\in I}|a_{j}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}.}

Now, we notice that for {\displaystyle d\in \mathbb {N} }

{\displaystyle |a_{i}|\leq |a_{dN}|,\quad {\text{for}}\quad i=dN+1,\dots ,(d+1)N;}{\displaystyle |a_{dN}|\leq |a_{j}|,\quad {\text{for}}\quad j=(d-1)N+1,\dots ,dN;}

Using this, we can estimate

{\displaystyle \left({\frac {1}{N}}\sum _{i\in I\setminus I_{N}}|a_{i}|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\leq \left({\frac {1}{N}}\sum _{d\in \mathbb {N} }N|a_{dN}|^{q}\right)^{1/q}=\left(\sum _{d\in \mathbb {N} }|a_{dN}|^{q}\right)^{1/q}}

as well as

{\displaystyle \left(\sum _{d\in \mathbb {N} }|a_{dN}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}=\left({\frac {1}{N}}\sum _{d\in \mathbb {N} }N|a_{dN}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq \left({\frac {1}{N}}\sum _{i\in I}|a_{i}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}.}

Also, we get by the sequence-sum comparison for 0<p<q (also valid for quasi-norms below 1):

{\displaystyle \left(\sum _{d\in \mathbb {N} }|a_{dN}|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\leq \left(\sum _{d\in \mathbb {N} }|a_{dN}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}.}

Putting all these ingredients together completes the proof.
