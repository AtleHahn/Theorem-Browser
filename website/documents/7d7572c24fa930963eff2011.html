Statement

Let X be a vector space over \mathbb K=\mathbb C, M\subseteq X a linear subspace, and p:X\to[0,\infty) a seminorm, so p(x+y)\le p(x)+p(y) and p(\lambda x)=|\lambda|p(x). Let f:M\to\mathbb K be linear with |f(m)|\le p(m) for m\in M. Taking real parts bijects the complex-linear extensions F of f with |F|\le p and the real-linear extensions R of \operatorname{Re}f with R\le p. Its inverse is F(x)=R(x)-iR(ix).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra supplies norms, inner products and scalar-valued linear maps; analysis supplies suprema and norm closure. Seminorm or cyclic-vector conditions are explicitly given by norm inequalities or the closed span of iterates. These direct definitions suffice without spectral or weak-topology theory.

Proof 1: Bijection between dominated complex extensions and real-part extensions — outline

If {\displaystyle z=a+ib\in \mathbb {C} } has real part {\displaystyle \operatorname {Re} z=a} then {\displaystyle -\operatorname {Re} (iz)=b,} which proves that {\displaystyle z=\operatorname {Re} z-i\operatorname {Re} (iz).} Substituting {\displaystyle F(x)} in for {\displaystyle z} and using {\displaystyle iF(x)=F(ix)} gives {\displaystyle F(x)=\operatorname {Re} F(x)-i\operatorname {Re} F(ix).}

In particular, a linear functional on {\displaystyle X} extends another one defined on {\displaystyle M\subseteq X} if and only if their real parts are equal on {\displaystyle M} (in other words, a linear functional {\displaystyle F} extends {\displaystyle f} if and only if {\displaystyle \operatorname {Re} F} extends {\displaystyle \operatorname {Re} f}). The real part of a linear functional on {\displaystyle X} is always a real-linear functional (meaning that it is linear when {\displaystyle X} is considered as a real vector space) and if {\displaystyle R:X\to \mathbb {R} } is a real-linear functional on a complex vector space then {\displaystyle x\mapsto R(x)-iR(ix)} defines the unique linear functional on {\displaystyle X} whose real part is {\displaystyle R.}

If {\displaystyle |F|\leq p} then {\displaystyle \operatorname {Re} F\leq |\operatorname {Re} F|\leq |F|\leq p.} For the converse, assume {\displaystyle \operatorname {Re} F\leq p} and fix {\displaystyle x\in X.} Let {\displaystyle r=|F(x)|} and pick any {\displaystyle \theta \in \mathbb {R} } such that {\displaystyle F(x)=re^{i\theta };} it remains to show {\displaystyle r\leq p(x).} Homogeneity of {\displaystyle F} implies {\displaystyle F\left(e^{-i\theta }x\right)=r} is real so that {\displaystyle \operatorname {Re} F\left(e^{-i\theta }x\right)=F\left(e^{-i\theta }x\right).} By assumption, {\displaystyle \operatorname {Re} F\leq p} and {\displaystyle p\left(e^{-i\theta }x\right)=p(x),} so that {\displaystyle r=\operatorname {Re} F\left(e^{-i\theta }x\right)\leq p\left(e^{-i\theta }x\right)=p(x),} as desired.

Thus taking real parts gives the stated one-to-one correspondence.
