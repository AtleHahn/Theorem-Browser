Statement

For integers n\ge r\ge0, \sum_{i=r}^n\binom ir=\binom{n+1}{r+1}. Here \binom ik counts the k-element subsets of an i-element set, and is 0 when k>i.

Proof 1: Induction using Pascal’s identity

Use Pascal’s identity \binom nk=\binom{n-1}{k-1}+\binom{n-1}k.

This identity can be proven by mathematical induction on {\displaystyle n}.

Base case Let {\displaystyle n=r};

{\displaystyle \sum _{i=r}^{n}{i \choose r}=\sum _{i=r}^{r}{i \choose r}={r \choose r}=1={r+1 \choose r+1}={n+1 \choose r+1}.}

Inductive step Suppose, for some {\displaystyle k\in \mathbb {N} ,k\geqslant r},

{\displaystyle \sum _{i=r}^{k}{i \choose r}={k+1 \choose r+1}}

Then

{\displaystyle \sum _{i=r}^{k+1}{i \choose r}=\left(\sum _{i=r}^{k}{i \choose r}\right)+{k+1 \choose r}={k+1 \choose r+1}+{k+1 \choose r}={k+2 \choose r+1}.}

Proof 2: Telescope adjacent binomial coefficients

Write k=r. Use Pascal’s identity \binom nk=\binom{n-1}{k-1}+\binom{n-1}k.

We use a telescoping argument to simplify the computation of the sum:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\sum _{t=\color {blue}0}^{n}{\binom {t}{k}}=\sum _{t=\color {blue}k}^{n}{\binom {t}{k}}&=\sum _{t=k}^{n}\left[{\binom {t+1}{k+1}}-{\binom {t}{k+1}}\right]\\&=\sum _{t=\color {green}k}^{\color {green}n}{\binom {\color {green}{t+1}}{k+1}}-\sum _{t=k}^{n}{\binom {t}{k+1}}\\&=\sum _{t=\color {green}{k+1}}^{\color {green}{n+1}}{\binom {\color {green}{t}}{k+1}}-\sum _{t=k}^{n}{\binom {t}{k+1}}\\&={\binom {n+1}{k+1}}-\underbrace {\binom {k}{k+1}} _{0}&&{\text{by telescoping}}\\&={\binom {n+1}{k+1}}.\end{aligned}}}

Proof 3: Count distributions by the oldest child’s share

Put m=n-r and k=r+2, so k\ge2. The following counting argument uses m for the number of candies.

Imagine that we are distributing {\displaystyle m} indistinguishable candies to {\displaystyle k} distinguishable children. By a direct application of the stars and bars method, there are

{\displaystyle {\binom {m+k-1}{k-1}}}

ways to do this. Alternatively, we can first give {\displaystyle 0\leqslant i\leqslant m} candies to the oldest child so that we are essentially giving {\displaystyle m-i} candies to {\displaystyle k-1} kids and again, with stars and bars and double counting, we have

{\displaystyle {\binom {m+k-1}{k-1}}=\sum _{i=0}^{m}{\binom {m+k-2-i}{k-2}},}

which simplifies to the desired result by taking {\displaystyle m'=m+k-2} and {\displaystyle r=k-2}, and noticing that {\displaystyle m'-m=k-2=r}:

{\displaystyle {\binom {m'+1}{r+1}}=\sum _{i=0}^{m}{\binom {m'-i}{r}}=\sum _{i=r}^{m'}{\binom {i}{r}}.}

Here m'=n, the upper index in the statement.

Proof 4: Partition subsets by their largest element

Write k=r for the fixed lower index; the letter r in the following count is a summation index.

Count the {\displaystyle (k+1)}-element subsets of the set {\displaystyle \{1,2,\ldots ,n+1\}} in two ways.

There are clearly {\displaystyle {\binom {n+1}{k+1}}} such subsets in total.

Alternatively, partition these subsets according to their largest element. If the largest element is {\displaystyle r+1}, where {\displaystyle k\leq r\leq n}, then the remaining {\displaystyle k} elements must be chosen from {\displaystyle \{1,2,\ldots ,r\}}, which can be done in {\displaystyle {\binom {r}{k}}} ways.

Therefore,

{\displaystyle \sum _{r=k}^{n}{\binom {r}{k}}={\binom {n+1}{k+1}}.}

Proof 5: Compare coefficients in a finite geometric sum

Let {\displaystyle X=1+x}. Then, by the partial sum formula for geometric series, we find that

{\displaystyle X^{r}+X^{r+1}+\dots +X^{n}={\frac {X^{r}-X^{n+1}}{1-X}}={\frac {X^{n+1}-X^{r}}{x}}}.

Further, by the binomial theorem, we also find that

{\displaystyle X^{r+k}=(1+x)^{r+k}=\sum _{i=0}^{r+k}{\binom {r+k}{i}}x^{i}}.

Note that this means the coefficient of {\displaystyle x^{r}} in {\displaystyle X^{r+k}} is given by {\displaystyle {\binom {r+k}{r}}}.

Thus, the coefficient of {\displaystyle x^{r}} in the left hand side of our first equation can be obtained by summing over the coefficients of {\displaystyle x^{r}} from each term, which gives

{\displaystyle \sum _{k=0}^{n-r}{\binom {r+k}{r}}}

Similarly, we find that the coefficient of {\displaystyle x^{r}} on the right hand side is given by the coefficient of {\displaystyle x^{r+1}} in {\displaystyle X^{n+1}-X^{r}}, which is

{\displaystyle {\binom {n+1}{r+1}}-{\binom {r}{r+1}}={\binom {n+1}{r+1}}}

Therefore, we can compare the coefficients of {\displaystyle x^{r}} on each side of the equation to find that

{\displaystyle \sum _{k=0}^{n-r}{\binom {r+k}{r}}={\binom {n+1}{r+1}}}

The quotient by x is interpreted as a polynomial after cancellation; putting i=r+k gives the stated sum.

Level justification
