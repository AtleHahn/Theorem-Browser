Statement

For a finite sequence of real numbers let V count changes of sign after deleting all zero terms. For a nonzero real polynomial f of degree n, put W_t(f)=V(f(t),f^{\prime}(t),\ldots,f^{(n)}(t)) and let v_t(f) count sign changes in the coefficients of f(x+t), read in increasing powers. Roots are counted with multiplicity. For real \ell<r, W_\ell(f)-W_r(f)-N_{(\ell,r]}(f) is a nonnegative even integer, where N_{(\ell,r]}(f) is the number of roots in (\ell,r] counted with multiplicity.

Proof 1: Fourier’s sign-variation bound on polynomial roots outline

Local sign changes

Let {\displaystyle n} be the degree of {\displaystyle f}, so that {\displaystyle f,f',...,f^{(n-1)}} are nonconstant polynomials, {\displaystyle f^{(n)}} is a nonzero constant, and {\displaystyle f^{(n+1)},...} are all identically zero.

As a function of {\displaystyle t,} the sign variation {\displaystyle W_{t}(f)} can only vary at a root of at least one of {\displaystyle f,f',...,f^{(n-1)}.}

If {\displaystyle W_{t}(f)} varies at {\displaystyle t=r}, then for some {\displaystyle k}, {\displaystyle f^{(k)}(x)} has a root at {\displaystyle r}, and each of {\displaystyle f,f',...,f^{(k-1)}} has no root at {\displaystyle r}.

If {\displaystyle k=0}, then {\displaystyle f(x)=(x-r)^{s}p(x-r)} for some {\displaystyle s\geq 1} and some polynomial {\displaystyle p} that satisfies {\displaystyle p(0)\neq 0}. By explicitly computing {\displaystyle f,f',...,f^{(n)}} at {\displaystyle r} and {\displaystyle r-\epsilon } for a small {\displaystyle \epsilon }, we have {\displaystyle W_{r}(f)=W_{r-\epsilon }(f)-s-2s',\quad \exists s'\geq 0.}

In this equation, the term {\displaystyle -s} is due to the signs of {\displaystyle f,f',...,f^{(s)}} changing from {\displaystyle (-1)^{s}\operatorname {sign} (p(0)),(-1)^{s-1}\operatorname {sign} (p(0)),...,-\operatorname {sign} (p(0)),\operatorname {sign} (p(0))} to {\displaystyle 0,0,...,0,\operatorname {sign} (p(0))}. The term {\displaystyle -2s',\quad \exists s'\geq 0} is due to the higher derivative signs possibly becoming zero.

If {\displaystyle k\geq 1}, then since some derivatives are zeroed at {\displaystyle r}, but both {\displaystyle f^{(k-1)}(x)} and {\displaystyle f^{(n)}(x)} remain nonzero, we only lose an even number of sign changes:

{\displaystyle W_{r}(f)=W_{r-\epsilon }(f)-2s',\quad \exists s'\geq 0}

If {\displaystyle W_{t}(f)} varies at {\displaystyle t=l}, then arguing similarly, we find that for both cases, we can take a small {\displaystyle \epsilon } such that {\displaystyle W_{l+\epsilon }(f)=W_{l}(f)}.
