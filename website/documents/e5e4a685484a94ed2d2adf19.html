Statement

Let X be a normal complex projective variety of dimension n, and let D=\sum_i d_iD_i be a boundary \mathbb Q-divisor: the D_i are distinct prime divisors and d_i\in\mathbb Q\cap[0,1]. Suppose that K_X+D is \mathbb Q-Cartier and (X,D) is log canonical. For every integer 0\le p\le n and every rank-one reflexive \mathbb Q-Cartier coherent subsheaf

\mathcal A\subseteq\Omega_X^{[p]}(\log\lfloor D\rfloor),

one has \kappa(\mathcal A)\le p. Here K_X is a canonical divisor and \mathbb Q-Cartier means some positive integral multiple is Cartier. Log canonical means that on a log resolution f:Y\to X, writing K_Y+\Gamma=f^*(K_X+D) with compatible canonical divisors, every coefficient of \Gamma is at most 1. A log resolution is a proper birational morphism from a smooth variety for which the exceptional divisors and strict transform of the boundary have simple normal crossings (locally their union is a union of coordinate hyperplanes). Write j:U\hookrightarrow X for the largest open subset on which X is smooth and the support of D has simple normal crossings. Then \Omega_X^{[p]}(\log\lfloor D\rfloor)=j_*\Omega_U^p(\log\lfloor D\rfloor|_U); the latter logarithmic forms are exterior products of the local generators dz_i/z_i along components of the reduced divisor \lfloor D\rfloor and dz_i in the other coordinate directions. For a coherent sheaf \mathcal F, put \mathcal F^*=\mathcal Hom_{\mathcal O_X}(\mathcal F,\mathcal O_X). Reflexive means the canonical map \mathcal A\to\mathcal A^{**} is an isomorphism; rank one refers to its generic rank. Put \mathcal A^{[m]}=(\mathcal A^{\otimes m})^{**}. The sheaf is \mathbb Q-Cartier when some \mathcal A^{[m]} is invertible. Its Iitaka dimension \kappa(\mathcal A) is the maximum of the dimensions of the images of rational maps given by the nonzero complete systems of sections of \mathcal A^{[m]}, over positive integers m; it is -\infty if all these spaces of sections vanish.
