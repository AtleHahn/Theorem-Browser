Statement

Let R be a commutative ring with identity. For a,b,c,d\in R and A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}, its characteristic polynomial is t^2-(a+d)t+ad-bc, and A^2-(a+d)A+(ad-bc)I_2=0.

Proof 1: Explicit Cayley–Hamilton identity for two-by-two matrices

The characteristic polynomial is given by p(λ) = λ2 − (a + d)λ + (ad − bc).

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}&{}A^{2}-(a+d)A+(ad-bc)I_{2}\\[1ex]&={\begin{pmatrix}a^{2}+bc&ab+bd\\ac+cd&bc+d^{2}\\\end{pmatrix}}-{\begin{pmatrix}a(a+d)&b(a+d)\\c(a+d)&d(a+d)\end{pmatrix}}+(ad-bc)I_{2}\\[1ex]&={\begin{pmatrix}bc-ad&0\\0&bc-ad\\\end{pmatrix}}+(ad-bc)I_{2}\\[1ex]&={\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}}\end{aligned}}}
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra and introductory abstract algebra suffice: interpret a two-by-two matrix over a commutative ring, multiply its entries, and cancel using the ring laws. The displayed computation supplies every substantive step. The extension from numerical entries to arbitrary ring entries is small and uses no division; no general Cayley–Hamilton theorem is assumed. The arbitrary-ring formulation prevents a high-school-only rating.
