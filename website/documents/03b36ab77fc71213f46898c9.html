Statement

Let complex coefficients a_{n,m} satisfy a_{n,m}\to0 for each fixed m and \sup_n\sum_{m=1}^n|a_{n,m}|<\infty. Assume \sum_{m=1}^n a_{n,m}\to1. If z_m\to z_\infty\ne0, then \sum_{m=1}^n a_{n,m}z_m\to z_\infty.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves complex sequences; limits; finite weighted sums. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Center the sequence and apply the null-sequence lemma

Use the null-sequence Toeplitz lemma as an explicit prerequisite: under the coefficient hypotheses a_{n,m}\to0 for each fixed m and uniformly bounded truncated absolute row sums, w_m\to0 implies \sum_{m=1}^n a_{n,m}w_m\to0.

We have \lim_{m\to\infty}(z_m-z_\infty)=0. Applying the null-sequence lemma yields

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{m=1}^n a_{n,m}(z_m-z_\infty)=0.

Finally, writing S_n=\sum_{m=1}^n a_{n,m}z_m,

\begin{aligned} \lim_{n\to\infty}S_n &=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{m=1}^n a_{n,m}z_m\\ &=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{m=1}^n a_{n,m}(z_m-z_\infty) +z_\infty\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{m=1}^n a_{n,m}\\ &=0+z_\infty\cdot1=z_\infty, \end{aligned}

which completes the proof.
