Statement

Let P\subseteq\mathbb R^n and c\in\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\}. No point x^*\in\operatorname{int}(P) minimizes c^Tx over P.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Finite-dimensional Euclidean vectors; Interior points and open balls; Linear objectives. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Move a short distance against the objective vector

Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that an optimal solution x^* belongs to \operatorname{int}(P). Then there exists some \epsilon>0 such that the ball of radius \epsilon centered at x^* is contained in P, that is, B_\epsilon(x^*)\subseteq P. Therefore,

x^*-\frac{\epsilon}{2}\frac{c}{\|c\|}\in P

and

c^T\left(x^*-\frac{\epsilon}{2}\frac{c}{\|c\|}\right) =c^Tx^*-\frac{\epsilon}{2}\frac{c^Tc}{\|c\|} =c^Tx^*-\frac{\epsilon}{2}\|c\|<c^Tx^*.

Hence x^* is not an optimal solution, a contradiction.
