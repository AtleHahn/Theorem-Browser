Statement

Let f be injective and holomorphic on D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\} with f(0)=0. For every 0<r\le\tanh(\pi/4), the image f(\{|z|<r\}) is starlike with respect to zero: if w is in that image and 0<t<1, then tw is also in it.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex differentiability extends the calculus derivative. Starlikeness is explicitly radial segment containment, the primary factor is an explicit exponential-polynomial formula, and the remaining bounds use ordinary suprema and convexity. No canonical infinite products or complex geometry are required.

Proof 1: Grunsky starlikeness radius

At z=0 interpret zf'(z)/f(z) as 1, and use its continuous argument equal to 0 there.

Let f be a univalent function on D with f(0) = 0. By Nevanlinna's criterion, f is starlike on |z| < r if and only if

{\displaystyle \Re {zf^{\prime }(z) \over f(z)}\geq 0}

for |z| < r. Equivalently

{\displaystyle \left|\arg {zf^{\prime }(z) \over f(z)}\right|\leq {\pi \over 2}.}

On the other hand, by Grunsky’s argument inequality,

{\displaystyle \left|\arg {zf^{\prime }(z) \over f(z)}\right|\leq \log {1+|z| \over 1-|z|}.}

Thus if

{\displaystyle \log {1+|z| \over 1-|z|}\leq {\pi \over 2},}

the inequality holds at z. This condition is equivalent to

{\displaystyle |z|\leq \tanh {\pi \over 4}}

and hence f is starlike on any disk |z| < r with r ≤ tanh π/4.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Combine the analytic argument bound with the starlikeness criterion and solve the scalar radius inequality. Univalent maps, normalized argument branches and geometric analytic criteria require substantial complex analysis.
