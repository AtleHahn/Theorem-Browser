Statement

For a completely positive map \Phi:A\to B(H) on a unital C-star algebra A and complex Hilbert space H, a Stinespring representation is a triple (\pi,V,K) with K a complex Hilbert space, \pi:A\to B(K) a unital star homomorphism, V:H\to K bounded, and \Phi(a)=V^*\pi(a)V. It is minimal if K=\overline{\operatorname{span}\pi(A)VH}. If (\pi_1,V_1,K_1) and (\pi_2,V_2,K_2) are two minimal representations of the same \Phi, there is a unitary W:K_1\to K_2 with WV_1=V_2 and W\pi_1(a)=\pi_2(a)W for all a\in A.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves C-star algebras; Minimal Stinespring representations defined locally; Unitary equivalence; Intertwining operators. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Match the two dense families of generators — outline

Let (\pi_1,V_1,K_1) and (\pi_2,V_2,K_2) be two minimal Stinespring representations of a given \Phi. Define W:K_1\to K_2 on the generating vectors by

W\pi_1(a)V_1h=\pi_2(a)V_2h,

extending linearly and continuously to K_1. This gives the intertwining relation

W\pi_1(a)=\pi_2(a)W.

Taking a=1 in the defining formula gives WV_1=V_2. Since both Stinespring representations are minimal, W is unitary. Thus minimal Stinespring representations are unique up to a unitary transformation.
