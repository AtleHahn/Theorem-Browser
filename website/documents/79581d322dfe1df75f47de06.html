Statement

A finite graph is planar if it can be drawn in the plane with vertices as points and edges as curves intersecting only at common endpoints. A minor is obtained by deleting vertices or edges and contracting edges (identifying their endpoints), with loops and repeated edges removable. Every minor of a finite planar graph is planar.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Planarity and each minor operation are explicitly described through finite plane drawings and edge identifications.

Proof 1: Delete and reroute edges in a planar drawing — outline

Vertex and edge deletion preserve a planar drawing. To contract an edge, retain one endpoint and reroute the edges at its other endpoint along the contracted edge. This can be done without introducing crossings, yielding a planar drawing of the contraction. Repeating these operations yields planarity of every minor.
