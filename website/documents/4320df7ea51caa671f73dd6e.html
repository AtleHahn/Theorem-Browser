Statement

Let G be a finite subgroup of \operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb C). If n\ge3, there is an abelian normal subgroup H\le G with [G:H]\le n!\,6^{n(\pi(n+1)+1)}, where \pi(x) is the number of primes at most x and [G:H] counts left cosets. If n\ge71, there is such a subgroup with [G:H]\le(n+1)!. Abelian means its elements commute, and normal means it is invariant under conjugation by G.

Proof 1: Primitive constituents and wreath-product bounds (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Collins Proposition C and Theorem D pp.412–414 (only degrees 2,3,4,6 needed); Theorems A–B; concluding proofs pp.420–423. Coarse comparison supplied here.).

Apply Collins Theorems A, B, D and their concluding proof. For 3<=n<=19, Theorem D realizes the maximum index using direct/wreath products of the blocks G_2,G_3,G_4,G_6 and possibly one fixed coordinate. Their indices modulo center are respectively 60,360,25920,6531840 (Proposition C). Each block index is <=216^m in its degree m. Products of block indices are therefore <=216^n, while every permutation factor in the direct/wreath product construction is bounded by n! (the product embeds into a permutation group on at most n coordinates). Thus J(n)<=n!216^n in this range. For 20<=n<=70 Theorem B gives either 60^r r!, r=floor(n/2), or (n+1)!, both <=n!216^n. For n>=71 Theorem A gives the exact requested (n+1)! and it too is <=n!216^n. Finally pi(n+1)>=2 for n>=3, so n!216^n=n!6^(3n)<=n!6^(n(pi(n+1)+1)). This handles the entire first clause and the sharper second clause, with the same abelian normal subgroup from the applicable Collins theorem.
