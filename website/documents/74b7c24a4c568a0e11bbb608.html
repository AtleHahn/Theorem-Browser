Statement

For finite posets P,Q, let J(P),J(Q) be their lower-set lattices under inclusion. There is a bijection between order-preserving maps g:Q\to P and bounded lattice homomorphisms h:J(P)\to J(Q), given by h(S)=g^{-1}(S). Here bounded homomorphisms preserve unions, intersections, empty sets and the whole posets.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Order infima/suprema and set unions/intersections are undergraduate concepts. The lattice, lower-set and prime/irreducible conditions are explicitly defined using them; a finite ordered-set example suffices to interpret the added terminology.

Proof 1: Composition with point evaluations — outline

Use the following lower-set facts: inverse image under an order-preserving map is a bounded lattice homomorphism; and every bounded homomorphism J(P)\to\mathbf2 is evaluation j_x(S)=1 if x\in S, 0 otherwise, at a unique x\in P.

Given h:J(P)\to J(Q), compose it with the evaluation j_q:J(Q)\to\mathbf2 for each q\in Q. The point-evaluation correspondence identifies j_q\circ h with a point h^*(q)\in P. This gives the order-preserving map h^*:Q\to P described in the source. The two constructions are inverse: (g^*)^*=g for an order-preserving g, and (h^*)^*=h for a bounded lattice homomorphism h, where g^* is inverse image on lower sets.
