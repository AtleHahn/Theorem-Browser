Statement

For real or complex formal power series F(x)=\sum_{k\ge0}a_kx^k and G(x)=\sum_{j\ge1}b_jx^j, write F(G(x))=\sum_{n\ge0}c_nx^n. Then c_0=a_0 and, for n\ge1, c_n=\sum_{k=1}^na_k\sum_{\substack{i_1+\cdots+i_k=n\\i_j\ge1}}b_{i_1}\cdots b_{i_k}=\sum_{k=1}^na_k\sum_{\substack{\sum_jm_j=k\\\sum_jjm_j=n}}\frac{k!}{\prod_jm_j!}\prod_{j=1}^nb_j^{m_j}. The second inner sum has nonnegative integer m_j.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Formal series use polynomial-style coefficient arithmetic without a convergence requirement. Coefficient extraction and partitions/compositions are explicitly described finite counts for each degree. This is a small extension of familiar power series and polynomial algebra.

Proof 1: Extract ordered exponents and collect their frequencies

The first form is obtained by picking out the coefficient of x^n in

(b_1x+b_2x^2+\cdots)^k

“by inspection”, and the second form is then obtained by collecting like terms, or alternatively, by applying the multinomial theorem. Here the ordered positive parts i_1,\dots,i_k specify the terms chosen from the k factors, and m_j is the number of parts equal to j. The constant term is c_0=a_0 since G(0)=0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Extract the finite coefficient sum using the zero constant term of G, then group exponent choices by multiplicities. Formal series and coefficientwise composition are a small discrete-algebra extension of polynomial multiplication.
