Philosophical significance
Medium

Orthomodularity together with an infinite orthonormal family forces a generalized inner-product space to be a real, complex or quaternionic Hilbert space. This is central to attempts to reconstruct quantum theory from structural assumptions about propositions and orthogonality, while not singling out complex quantum mechanics without further assumptions.

Statement

Let K be a division ring with an additive involution a\mapsto a^* satisfying (ab)^*=b^*a^* and (a^*)^*=a. Let V be a left vector space over K with a Hermitian form \langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:V\times V\to K, linear in its first variable and satisfying \langle u,v\rangle=\langle v,u\rangle^*. Suppose the form is nondegenerate: if \langle u,v\rangle=0 for every u, then v=0. For a subspace S, put S^\perp=\{v:\langle s,v\rangle=0\ \forall s\in S\}. Assume V=S+S^\perp for every subspace S=S^{\perp\perp}. If V contains an infinite set (u_i) with \langle u_i,u_j\rangle=\delta_{ij}, then K is isomorphic as a division ring to \mathbb R, \mathbb C, or the quaternion division ring \mathbb H. A division ring is a nonzero ring with identity in which every nonzero element has a multiplicative inverse. The Kronecker delta is 1 for i=j and 0 otherwise; \mathbb H has real basis 1,i,j,k with i^2=j^2=k^2=ijk=-1.

Moreover, under an identification with the corresponding classical scalar division ring, V with its Hermitian form is a Hilbert space over \mathbb R, \mathbb C or \mathbb H: the form is positive definite and V is complete in the norm \|v\|=\sqrt{\langle v,v\rangle}. The scalar involution corresponds respectively to the identity, complex conjugation or quaternionic conjugation.

Proof 1: Ordered scalar classification from orthomodularity (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Chapter Soler’s Theorem pp.373-387; statement p.373, orthomodularity p.375, completeness pp.376-377, Lemmas 5-7 pp.380-384, noncommutative Section 4 pp.385-387.).

First deduce anisotropy from the hypotheses here. A line L=Kx is biorthogonally closed: nondegeneracy makes the functionals associated to independent vectors independent, so vectors outside L are separated by an element of L^\perp. If x\ne0 were isotropic, then L\subseteq L^\perp, and V=L+L^\perp would imply V=L^\perp, contradicting nondegeneracy. Thus the form is anisotropic and the orthogonal sums are direct.

Choose a countably infinite subsequence of the orthonormal set and apply Prestel's argument. It constructs a semi-order and then an Archimedean Baer order in the skew-field case. Holland's classification and realization of Dedekind cuts determine the scalars and involution. Positivity and the completion argument yield the asserted Hilbert space. Section 4 invokes Solèr's noncommutative lemma and Holland's classification; their proofs are dependencies of this exposition.
