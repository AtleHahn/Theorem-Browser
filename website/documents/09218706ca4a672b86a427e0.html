Statement

If A is a nonnegative primitive square matrix, let r=\rho(A) and choose its positive right and left Perron vectors with w^Tv=1. Then A^k/r^k\to P=vw^T>0 entrywise; P is a rank-one projection commuting with A.

Proof 1: Source spectral argument outline

A nonnegative matrix is primitive if some positive integer power is entrywise positive. By the primitive Perron–Frobenius theorem, A has a positive simple eigenvalue r strictly larger in absolute value than its other eigenvalues, and positive left and right eigenvectors. Apply the following argument with M=A.

Given that M is diagonalizable, M is conjugate to a diagonal matrix with eigenvalues r1, ... , rn on the diagonal (denote r1 = r). The matrix Mk/rk will be conjugate to the diagonal matrix with diagonal (1, (r2/r)k, ... , (rn/r)k), which tends to (1,0,0,...,0), for k → ∞, so the limit exists. The same method works for general M (without assuming that M is diagonalizable), using Jordan form; the source leaves the decay of the other Jordan blocks implicit.

Denote the limit by P. The projection and commutativity properties are elementary corollaries of the definition: MMk/rk = Mk/rk M ; P2 = lim M2k/r2k = P. The image assertion is also elementary: M(Pu) = M lim Mk/rk u = lim rMk+1/rk+1u, so taking the limit yields M(Pu) = r(Pu), so image of P lies in the r-eigenspace for M, which is one-dimensional by the assumptions.

Denoting by v, r-eigenvector for M (by w for MT). Columns of P are multiples of v, because the image of P is spanned by it. Applying the same argument to M^T, the rows are multiples of w^T. So P takes a form (a v wT), for some a. Hence its trace equals to (a wT v). Trace of projector equals the dimension of its image. It was proved before that it is not more than one-dimensional. From the definition one sees that P acts identically on the r-eigenvector for M. So it is one-dimensional. So choosing (wTv) = 1, implies P = vwT.

Since v,w are positive, P=vw^T is entrywise positive.
