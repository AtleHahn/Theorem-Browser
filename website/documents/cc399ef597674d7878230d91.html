Statement

Let P=(p_i)_{i\in\mathbb N} and Q=(q_i)_{i\in\mathbb N} be nonnegative real sequences with \sum_i p_i=\sum_iq_i=1. Their Kullback–Leibler divergence D(P\Vert Q)=\sum_i p_i\log_2(p_i/q_i) is nonnegative and equals zero exactly when P=Q. Terms with p_i=0 are zero, and a term with p_i>0,q_i=0 is +\infty; the sum is understood in its usual extended sense.

Proof 1: Substitute two probability mass functions into log sum

Use the countable log sum inequality, stated here as an external prerequisite: for nonnegative summable sequences a_i,b_i with totals a,b>0,

\sum_i a_i\log_2(a_i/b_i)\ge a\log_2(a/b),

with the same zero conventions as in the statement; when the left side is finite, equality holds precisely when the positive-denominator ratios are all a/b and positive numerators have positive denominators. Apply it with a_i=p_i, b_i=q_i, and a=b=1. The lower bound is zero. Equality forces p_i/q_i=1 wherever q_i>0, with p_i=0 wherever q_i=0; hence P=Q. Conversely, when P=Q each term is zero.
