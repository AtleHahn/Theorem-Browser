Statement

The intervals (0,1) and [0,1], equipped with their Borel sigma-algebras, admit a bimeasurable bijection.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Basic topology and set operations supply intervals and inverse images. Borel sets are generated from open sets by countable unions and complements; bimeasurable means both directions preserve measurability. No integration theory is needed for this concrete bijection claim.

Proof 1: Two interval embeddings — outline

The closed interval is evidently isomorphic to a shorter closed subinterval of the open interval, by an affine map. Also the open interval is evidently isomorphic to a part of the closed interval (just itself, for instance).

These are bimeasurable embeddings for the Borel sigma-algebras, so the measurable Schröder–Bernstein theorem gives the isomorphism.

Level justification

Embed each interval bimeasurably in the other and apply the supplied measurable Schroder-Bernstein theorem. Undergraduate point-set topology suffices with Borel sigma-algebras as the sets generated by opens and measurable preimages as small related extensions; no measure or integration theory is used.
