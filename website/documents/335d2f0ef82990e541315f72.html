Statement

Let H be a complex Hilbert space of dimension at least two and fix a unit vector e\in H. There is no unitary U on H\otimes H such that for every unit vector \phi\in H there is a real phase \alpha_\phi with U(\phi\otimes e)=e^{i\alpha_\phi}\phi\otimes\phi. Thus even with a fixed blank state, one unitary cannot clone all pure states up to phase.

Proof 1: Inner-product obstruction to a universal copier

To prove the theorem, we select an arbitrary pair of unit vectors \phi and \psi in the Hilbert space H. Because U is supposed to be unitary, we would have

\begin{aligned} \langle\phi,\psi\rangle\langle e,e\rangle &=\langle\phi\otimes e,\psi\otimes e\rangle\\ &=\langle\phi\otimes e,U^*U(\psi\otimes e)\rangle\\ &=e^{-i(\alpha_\phi-\alpha_\psi)} \langle\phi\otimes\phi,\psi\otimes\psi\rangle\\ &=e^{-i(\alpha_\phi-\alpha_\psi)}\langle\phi,\psi\rangle^2. \end{aligned}

Here the inner product is conjugate-linear in its first argument. Since e is assumed to be normalized, we thus get

|\langle\phi,\psi\rangle|^2=|\langle\phi,\psi\rangle|.

This implies that either |\langle\phi,\psi\rangle|=1 or |\langle\phi,\psi\rangle|=0. Hence by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality either \phi=e^{i\beta}\psi for some real \beta, or \phi is orthogonal to \psi. However, this cannot be the case for two arbitrary unit vectors in a Hilbert space of dimension at least two. Therefore, a single universal U cannot clone a general quantum state. This proves the no-cloning theorem.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The scalar inner-product obstruction is elementary, but the full statement and proof use arbitrary Hilbert tensor products, unitaries and their adjoints, not merely finite matrices. That combined infinite-dimensional framework is not confidently a small extension of ordinary undergraduate linear algebra; retain beyond undergraduate in doubt.
