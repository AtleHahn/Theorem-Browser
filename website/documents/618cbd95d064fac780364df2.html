Statement

For x>0 and y\in\mathbb R, define A(y,x)=\arctan(y/x), the two-argument angle on the right half-plane. Then \partial A/\partial y=x/(x^2+y^2) and \partial A/\partial x=-y/(x^2+y^2).

Proof 1: Differentiate the supplied arctangent coordinate recipe — outline

For the first and fourth quadrant (i.e., x>0), one has A(y,x)=\arctan(y/x). Its partial derivatives are:

\frac{\partial A(y,x)}{\partial y}=\frac{x}{x^2+y^2} \qquad\text{and}\qquad \frac{\partial A(y,x)}{\partial x}=\frac{-y}{x^2+y^2}.
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Hold one coordinate fixed and apply the chain rule to arctan(y/x). The inner derivatives are 1/x and -y/x^2; simplifying by 1+(y/x)^2 gives the stated formulas. The condition x>0 ensures the given angle recipe and all divisions are valid. This is ordinary multivariable calculus.
