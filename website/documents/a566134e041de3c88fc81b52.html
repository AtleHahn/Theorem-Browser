Statement

Let f be meromorphic on 0<|z-z_0|<r_0. If there exist b\in\mathbb C and \varepsilon>0 with |f(z)-b|\ge\varepsilon at every point where f is finite, then f extends meromorphically over z_0; the missing point is removable or a pole.

Proof 1: Bounded reciprocal and the zero/nonzero dichotomy — outline

Write V=\{z:|z-z_0|<r_0\}. There is some complex value b and some \varepsilon>0 such that |f(z)-b|\geq\varepsilon at every point of V\setminus\{z_0\} where f is finite.

Then the new function:

g(z)=\frac1{f(z)-b}

must be holomorphic on V\setminus\{z_0\}, with zeroes at the poles of f, and bounded by 1/\varepsilon. It can therefore be holomorphically extended to all of V by Riemann's removable-singularity theorem. So the original function can be expressed in terms of g:

f(z)=\frac1{g(z)}+b

on V\setminus\{z_0\}, as an identity of meromorphic functions. Consider the two possible cases for

\lim_{z\to z_0}g(z).

If the limit is 0, then f has a pole at z_0. If the limit is not 0, then z_0 is a removable singularity of f. Here g is not identically zero, since it is a nonzero reciprocal wherever f is finite, so any zero of g has finite order.

Level justification

The argument turns poles into zeros, removes a bounded holomorphic singularity and then uses finite-order zeros to recover a meromorphic extension. These interconnected holomorphic and meromorphic local theories require substantial complex-analysis background.
