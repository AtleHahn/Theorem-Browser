Statement

Let C be a smooth simple closed curve in \mathbb C, with the positive boundary orientation, and let \varphi be holomorphic on a neighborhood of C. For w\notin C put

F(w)=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_C\frac{\varphi(\zeta)}{\zeta-w}\,d\zeta.

For each z\in C the limits from the interior and exterior exist and satisfy

\lim_{\substack{w\to z\\w\text{ inside }C}}F(w) =\frac1{2\pi i}\operatorname{pv}\int_C\frac{\varphi(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}\,d\zeta+\frac12\varphi(z),\lim_{\substack{w\to z\\w\text{ outside }C}}F(w) =\frac1{2\pi i}\operatorname{pv}\int_C\frac{\varphi(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}\,d\zeta-\frac12\varphi(z).

The principal value is obtained by deleting a symmetric arc-length neighborhood of z and letting its radius decrease to zero.

Proof 1: Removal of the density singularity and the contour jump (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Section46.2, Theorem46.2 and its proof, pp.172–174).

For the analytic density in this statement, the curved-contour extension can be made explicit. For w near C, set

H(w)=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_C\frac{\varphi(\zeta)-\varphi(w)}{\zeta-w}\,d\zeta.

The divided difference extends continuously across its diagonal, so H is continuous across C, and F(w)=H(w)+\varphi(w)\operatorname{Ind}(C,w). Symmetric arc-length deletion gives \operatorname{pv}\int_C d\zeta/(\zeta-z)=\pi i: the small interior indentation has clockwise angle tending to \pi, and its endpoint radii have ratio tending to 1. Hence the normalized principal value is H(z)+\varphi(z)/2, yielding both formulas for arbitrary interior or exterior approach. This uses analyticity only near C, not throughout its interior.

For the analytic density, local power series remove the diagonal singularity. The remaining Jordan and winding dependencies follow from disk fixed points, interval extension and local continuous-angle increments; the half-jump is an elementary arclength and logarithm calculation.
