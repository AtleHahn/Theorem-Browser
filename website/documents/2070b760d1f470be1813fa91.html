Statement

Let (X_\alpha) be a family of compact convex subsets of \mathbb R^d. If every subfamily of at most d+1 members has nonempty intersection, then the whole family has nonempty intersection.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Basic topology and real analysis supply compactness, completeness, continuity and sequence convergence; linear algebra supplies inner products and orthogonality. Hilbert completeness and the explicitly specified bounds or maps are direct extensions, with no spectral or weak-topology theory needed.

Proof 1: Finite Helly and compactness

Use the finite Helly theorem: a finite family of at least d+1 convex sets in \mathbb R^d has nonempty total intersection if every d+1 members intersect. Thus every finite subfamily of the given family intersects. Fix one member X_{\alpha_0}. The intersections X_\alpha\cap X_{\alpha_0} are closed subsets of the compact space X_{\alpha_0} and have the finite intersection property. Its compactness gives a common point, also common to the original family.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply finite Helly to get the finite intersection property inside one fixed compact member. Undergraduate Euclidean convexity and point-set compactness suffice.
