Statement

For an integrable real random variable X and integer n\ge1, (\mathbb EX)^{2n}\le\mathbb E[X^{2n}], allowing the right side to be infinite.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Expectation of a scalar random variable and its nonnegative powers is a limited integration/probability extension of undergraduate analysis. An infinite upper moment is an extended nonnegative value, not a new convergence theorem.

Proof 1: Convexity of an even power

The function g(x)=x^{2n} is convex since g^{\prime\prime}(x)=2n(2n-1)x^{2n-2}\ge0. Jensen’s inequality therefore yields g(\mathbb EX)\le\mathbb E g(X), which is the claim.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Check convexity of the even power by its second derivative and apply the explicit expectation form of Jensen. Undergraduate calculus and probability suffice; allowing an infinite upper moment does not require any limiting argument in this application.
