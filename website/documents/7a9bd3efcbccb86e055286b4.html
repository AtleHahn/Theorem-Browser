Statement

Let I,J be nondegenerate real intervals and f:I\to J a continuous bijection. Let F be an antiderivative of f (continuous on I and with F^{\prime}=f in its interior). Assume additionally that f^{-1} is differentiable in the interior of J. Then H(y)=yf^{-1}(y)-F(f^{-1}(y)) is continuous on J and satisfies H^{\prime}(y)=f^{-1}(y) in its interior. All antiderivatives of f^{-1} are H+C for real constants C.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves real antiderivatives; continuous inverse functions. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Inverse integral when the inverse is differentiable — outline

Differentiate the source expression H(y)=yf^{-1}(y)-F(f^{-1}(y)) by product and chain rules: H'(y)=f^{-1}(y)+y(f^{-1})'(y)-f(f^{-1}(y))(f^{-1})'(y)=f^{-1}(y). Continuity gives the endpoint values, and antiderivatives differ by a constant.
