Statement

For n\geq1, define f_n:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R by f_n(x)=1 for n\leq x\leq n+1 and f_n(x)=0 otherwise. Then f_n(x)\to0 for every x\in\mathbb R, but for every Lebesgue-measurable set B of finite measure, the convergence is not uniform on \mathbb R\setminus B.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Pointwise and uniform convergence are undergraduate real analysis. Lebesgue-measurable sets carry the extension of interval length, with finite measure meaning finite total length in that sense; this small additional measure definition suffices for the complete real-line assertion, without the general Egorov theorem.

Proof 1: Translate unit-interval indicators — outline

Take f_n=1_{[n,n+1]} on \mathbb R. These functions converge pointwise everywhere to zero, but they do not converge uniformly on \mathbb R\setminus B for any measurable B of finite Lebesgue measure.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Every fixed point eventually leaves the translated intervals. Uniform convergence outside B would force B to contain every sufficiently late unit interval, hence a half-line of infinite length. Only interval lengths and monotonicity are needed beyond elementary uniform convergence, not a general Egorov proof.
