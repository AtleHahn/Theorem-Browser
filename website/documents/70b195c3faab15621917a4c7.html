Statement

Let k be a field of characteristic zero, G a reductive algebraic group and V a finite-dimensional rational representation of G over k. Then the invariant ring k[V]^G is Cohen–Macaulay.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Reductive algebraic groups, rational representations and Cohen-Macaulay invariant rings require substantial algebraic geometry and commutative algebra. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Boutot’s quotient theorem — outline

Use Boutot’s theorem: in characteristic zero, a quotient of a variety with rational singularities by a reductive group again has rational singularities. Affine space V is smooth and has rational singularities. Its quotient has coordinate ring k[V]^G, so this ring has rational singularities. Rings with rational singularities are Cohen–Macaulay, giving the conclusion.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply Boutot to affine space and use Cohen–Macaulayness of rational singularities. Reductive quotients, rational singularities and commutative-algebra depth require graduate algebraic geometry.
