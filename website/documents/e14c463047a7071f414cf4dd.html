Statement

An equitable k-coloring of a finite simple undirected graph assigns one of k colors to every vertex, gives adjacent vertices different colors, and has any two color-class sizes differing by at most one; empty classes are allowed. For n\geq1, let K_{1,n} have a central vertex adjacent to each of n leaves and no other edges. The least number of colors in an equitable coloring is 1+\lceil n/2\rceil.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Proof 1: Isolate the center and pair the leaves — outline

The central vertex must be alone in its color class. Therefore every other color class can have at most two vertices, and the n leaves must be split among at least \lceil n/2\rceil classes. Splitting the leaves into classes of at most two gives the claimed number.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The center occupies its own class, limiting each other class to two vertices. Pairing the leaves achieves the resulting ceiling bound. Elementary finite counting suffices.
