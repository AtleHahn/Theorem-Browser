Statement

For a uniformly random permutation of \{1,\ldots,m\} let N_m count its fixed points. Then \mathbb E[x^{N_m}]=\sum_{k=0}^m(x-1)^k/k! for real x, and N_m converges in distribution to a Poisson random variable of mean one, whose probability of value r is e^{-1}/r!.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The experiment uses independent choices from a finite alphabet or a uniform finite permutation. Elementary probability supplies products and averages, while analysis supplies infinite sequences and limits. The Poisson mass formula is explicit where used; no general stochastic-process machinery is needed.

Proof 1: Count fixed subsets and pass to the exponential generating limit

Write N=N_m.

Let σ be a uniformly random permutation of the set {1, ..., m} and let Aj denote the event that j is a fixed point of σ, meaning that Aj = {σ(j) = j}. When the numbers in J, which is a subset of {1, ..., m}, are fixed points, then there are (m – |J|)! ways to permute the remaining m – |J| numbers, hence

{\displaystyle \mathbb {P} {\biggl (}\bigcap _{j\in J}A_{j}{\biggr )}={\frac {(m-|J|)!}{m!}}.}

By the combinatorical interpretation of the binomial coefficient, there are {\displaystyle \textstyle {\binom {m}{k}}} different choices of a subset J of {1, ..., m} with k elements, hence S_k=\sum_{|J|=k}\mathbb P(\bigcap_{j\in J}A_j) simplifies to

{\displaystyle S_{k}={\binom {m}{k}}{\frac {(m-k)!}{m!}}={\frac {1}{k!}}.}

Therefore, using the event-count generating identity \mathbb E[x^N]=\sum_{k=0}^m S_k(x-1)^k, the probability-generating function of the number N of fixed points is given by

{\displaystyle \mathbb {E} [x^{N}]=\sum _{k=0}^{m}{\frac {(x-1)^{k}}{k!}},\qquad x\in \mathbb {R} .}

This is the partial sum of the infinite series giving the exponential function at x – 1, which in turn is the probability-generating function of the Poisson distribution with parameter 1. By the continuity theorem for probability-generating functions, as m tends to infinity, the distribution of N converges to the Poisson distribution with parameter 1.
