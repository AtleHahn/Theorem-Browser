Statement

Let A be a compact self-adjoint operator on a complex Hilbert space H. List its positive eigenvalues, with multiplicity, in decreasing order \lambda_1\ge\lambda_2\ge\cdots>0. For every index k for which \lambda_k exists,

\lambda_k=\max_{\dim S=k}\ \min_{x\in S,\ \|x\|=1}\langle Ax,x\rangle =\min_{\dim S=k-1}\ \max_{x\in S^\perp,\ \|x\|=1}\langle Ax,x\rangle.

Here S ranges over linear subspaces of H. If the negative eigenvalues are listed in increasing order \mu_1\le\mu_2\le\cdots<0, then for every existing \mu_k,

\mu_k=\min_{\dim S=k}\ \max_{x\in S,\ \|x\|=1}\langle Ax,x\rangle =\max_{\dim S=k-1}\ \min_{x\in S^\perp,\ \|x\|=1}\langle Ax,x\rangle.

Only indices of existing positive or negative eigenvalues are asserted; zero eigenvalues are not inserted into either list.

Proof 1: Spectral complements and dimension intersections (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (§4.3, Theorem 4.10, pp.117–119; Theorem 4.12(i), pp.119–120).

For positive eigenvalues apply the source to -A; for negative ones reverse the sign. The complement of the first k-1 eigenvectors includes the kernel and opposite-sign spectral subspace. Spectral expansion bounds every Rayleigh quotient there; choosing the kth eigenvector proves attainment, completing the step following (4.31). Every k-dimensional test space meets this complement, giving the other variational formula. For each codimension-(k-1) complement, the compressed operator is compact and has a strictly positive largest Rayleigh value, hence attains it. No separability is needed: the closed range of a compact operator is separable, and adjoining the kernel projections of any finite test space gives a separable invariant subspace.
