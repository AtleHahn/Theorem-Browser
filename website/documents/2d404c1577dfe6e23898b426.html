Statement

Let a<b, k\ge1, and f:[a,b]\to\mathbb R^k be continuous and differentiable on (a,b), with the Euclidean norm \|\cdot\|_2. Some c\in(a,b) satisfies \|f(b)-f(a)\|_2\le(b-a)\|f'(c)\|_2.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Real-parameter vector differentiation; Euclidean norm and dot product. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Project onto the endpoint difference

Write \lvert\cdot\rvert for the Euclidean norm \|\cdot\|_2.

Take {\displaystyle \varphi (t)=({\textbf {f}}(b)-{\textbf {f}}(a))\cdot {\textbf {f}}(t)}. Then {\displaystyle \varphi } is real-valued and thus, by the mean value theorem,

{\displaystyle \varphi (b)-\varphi (a)=\varphi '(c)(b-a)}

for some {\displaystyle c\in (a,b)}. Now,

{\displaystyle \varphi (b)-\varphi (a)=|{\textbf {f}}(b)-{\textbf {f}}(a)|^{2}}

and

{\displaystyle \varphi '(c)=({\textbf {f}}(b)-{\textbf {f}}(a))\cdot {\textbf {f}}'(c).}

Hence, using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, from the above equation, we get:

{\displaystyle |{\textbf {f}}(b)-{\textbf {f}}(a)|^{2}\leq |{\textbf {f}}(b)-{\textbf {f}}(a)||{\textbf {f}}'(c)|(b-a).}

If {\displaystyle {\textbf {f}}(b)={\textbf {f}}(a)}, the theorem holds trivially. Otherwise, dividing both sides by {\displaystyle |{\textbf {f}}(b)-{\textbf {f}}(a)|} yields the theorem.
