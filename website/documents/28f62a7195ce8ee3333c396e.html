Statement

Let A be a set, B a Banach space, and f_n:A\to B. If \|f_n(x)\|\le M_n for all x,n and \sum_nM_n<\infty with M_n\ge0, then \sum_nf_n converges uniformly and absolutely, meaning \sum_n\|f_n(x)\|<\infty.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Real analysis supplies complete metric spaces, closedness, compactness and uniform convergence; linear algebra supplies linear maps. A Banach space is complete for its norm metric, and the operator bound or compactness condition is explicitly given. These statements use that single extension without dual spaces or weak topologies.

Proof 1: Norm version of the Cauchy estimate (outline)

Replace absolute values by norms for the Banach-space version of the test.

Consider the sequence of functions

{\displaystyle S_{n}(x)=\sum _{k=1}^{n}f_{k}(x).}

Since the series {\displaystyle \sum _{n=1}^{\infty }M_{n}} converges and Mn ≥ 0 for every n, then by the Cauchy criterion,

{\displaystyle \forall \varepsilon >0:\exists N:\forall m>n>N:\sum _{k=n+1}^{m}M_{k}<\varepsilon .}

For the chosen N,

{\displaystyle \forall x\in A:\forall m>n>N}{\displaystyle \left\|S_{m}(x)-S_{n}(x)\right\|=\left\|\sum _{k=n+1}^{m}f_{k}(x)\right\|{\overset {(1)}{\leq }}\sum _{k=n+1}^{m}\|f_{k}(x)\|\leq \sum _{k=n+1}^{m}M_{k}<\varepsilon .}

(Inequality (1) follows from the triangle inequality.)

For each x, the sequence Sn(x) is thus a Cauchy sequence in the Banach space B, and by completeness, it converges to some element S(x) that depends on x. For n > N we can write

{\displaystyle \left\|S(x)-S_{n}(x)\right\|=\left\|\lim _{m\to \infty }S_{m}(x)-S_{n}(x)\right\|=\lim _{m\to \infty }\left\|S_{m}(x)-S_{n}(x)\right\|\leq \varepsilon .}

Since N does not depend on x, this means that the sequence Sn of partial sums converges uniformly to the function S. Hence, by definition, the series {\displaystyle \sum _{k=1}^{\infty }f_{k}(x)} converges uniformly.

Analogously, one can prove that {\displaystyle \sum _{k=1}^{\infty }\|f_{k}(x)\|} converges uniformly.

A Banach space here adds only a norm-induced complete metric on a vector space; duals, operator topology and measure theory do not enter.
