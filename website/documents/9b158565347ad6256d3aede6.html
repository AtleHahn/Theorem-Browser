Likely alternative prerequisite level: High school only; lowest recorded proof level: Undergraduate level. Wilf-Zeilberger give an explicit rational certificate for Dixon's finite summation identity. Specializing the parameters to the equal binomial-cube case yields a finite telescoping/induction verification of this identity for n>=0. Checking such a certificate requires factorial ratios, polynomial arithmetic and cancellation of boundary terms; finding it algorithmically is not a reader prerequisite. This replaces MacMahon's matrix/formal-series theorem with a school-algebra verification route.

Statement

For every nonnegative integer n, \sum_{k=0}^{2n}(-1)^k\binom{2n}{k}^3=(-1)^n\frac{(3n)!}{(n!)^3}.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All terms and quantifiers involve finite integer arithmetic.

Proof 1: Compute one coefficient by binomial expansion and by a determinant

Use MacMahon’s master theorem: for a complex m\times m matrix A=(a_{ij}), define G(k)=[x_1^{k_1}\cdots x_m^{k_m}]\prod_{i=1}^m(\sum_j a_{ij}x_j)^{k_i}. Here brackets mean monomial coefficient. For T=\operatorname{diag}(t_1,\ldots,t_m), the formal identity is \sum_{k\in\mathbb N_0^m}G(k)t_1^{k_1}\cdots t_m^{k_m}=\det(I-TA)^{-1}.

Consider a matrix

{\displaystyle A={\begin{pmatrix}0&1&-1\\-1&0&1\\1&-1&0\end{pmatrix}}.}

Compute the coefficients G(2n, 2n, 2n) directly from the definition:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}G(2n,2n,2n)&={\bigl [}x_{1}^{2n}x_{2}^{2n}x_{3}^{2n}{\bigl ]}(x_{2}-x_{3})^{2n}(x_{3}-x_{1})^{2n}(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2n}\\[6pt]&=\,\sum _{k=0}^{2n}(-1)^{k}{\binom {2n}{k}}^{3},\end{aligned}}}

where the last equality follows from the fact that on the right-hand side we have the product of the following coefficients:

{\displaystyle [x_{2}^{k}x_{3}^{2n-k}](x_{2}-x_{3})^{2n},\ \ [x_{3}^{k}x_{1}^{2n-k}](x_{3}-x_{1})^{2n},\ \ [x_{1}^{k}x_{2}^{2n-k}](x_{1}-x_{2})^{2n},}

which are computed from the binomial theorem. On the other hand, we can compute the determinant explicitly:

{\displaystyle \det(I-TA)\,=\,\det {\begin{pmatrix}1&-t_{1}&t_{1}\\t_{2}&1&-t_{2}\\-t_{3}&t_{3}&1\end{pmatrix}}\,=\,1+{\bigl (}t_{1}t_{2}+t_{1}t_{3}+t_{2}t_{3}{\bigr )}.}

Only the term of total degree 6n in the reciprocal geometric series can contribute. Therefore, by the MMT, we have a new formula for the same coefficients:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}G(2n,2n,2n)&={\bigl [}t_{1}^{2n}t_{2}^{2n}t_{3}^{2n}{\bigl ]}(-1)^{3n}{\bigl (}t_{1}t_{2}+t_{1}t_{3}+t_{2}t_{3}{\bigr )}^{3n}\\[6pt]&=(-1)^{n}{\binom {3n}{n,n,n}},\end{aligned}}}

where the last equality follows from the fact that we need to use an equal number of times all three terms in the power. Now equating the two formulas for coefficients G(2n, 2n, 2n) we obtain an equivalent version of Dixon's identity:

{\displaystyle \sum _{k=0}^{2n}(-1)^{k}{\binom {2n}{k}}^{3}=(-1)^{n}{\binom {3n}{n,n,n}}.}

Here \binom{3n}{n,n,n}=(3n)!/(n!)^3.
