Statement

Let ABC be a nondegenerate Euclidean triangle, P an interior point, a=BC, b=CA, c=AB, p=PA, q=PB, r=PC, and let x,y,z be the perpendicular distances from P to the supporting lines BC,CA,AB, respectively. Then p+q+r\ge2(x+y+z), with equality if and only if ABC is equilateral and P is its centroid.

Proof 1: Area comparison, reflection and AM–GM — outline

  • Let the sides of {\displaystyle ABC} be {\displaystyle a}, {\displaystyle b}, and {\displaystyle c}, which are opposite to the vertices {\displaystyle A}, {\displaystyle B}, and {\displaystyle C}. Let us denote the distance between internal point {\displaystyle P} to all such three vertices as {\displaystyle PA=p}, {\displaystyle PB=q}, and {\displaystyle PC=r}, and the distance between internal point {\displaystyle P} to all three sides of a triangle {\displaystyle ABC}, namely the perpendicular distance to the supporting line of each side: from {\displaystyle P} to {\displaystyle BC} is {\displaystyle x}, to {\displaystyle CA} is {\displaystyle y}, and to {\displaystyle AB} is {\displaystyle z}. First, we prove that

    {\displaystyle cr\geq ax+by.}

    This is equivalent to

    {\displaystyle {\frac {c(r+z)}{2}}\geq {\frac {ax+by+cz}{2}}.}

  • The right side is the area of triangle ABC, but on the left side, {\displaystyle r+z} is at least the height of the triangle; consequently, the left side cannot be smaller than the right side. Now reflect {\displaystyle P} in the angle bisector at {\displaystyle C}. We find that {\displaystyle cr\geq ay+bx} for the reflection of {\displaystyle P}. Similarly, {\displaystyle bq\geq az+cx} and {\displaystyle ap\geq bz+cy}. We solve these inequalities for {\displaystyle r}, {\displaystyle q}, and {\displaystyle p}:

    {\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}r&\geq (a/c)y+(b/c)x,\\q&\geq (a/b)z+(c/b)x,\\p&\geq (b/a)z+(c/a)y.\end{aligned}}}

  • Adding the three up, we get

    {\displaystyle p+q+r\geq \left({\frac {b}{c}}+{\frac {c}{b}}\right)x+\left({\frac {a}{c}}+{\frac {c}{a}}\right)y+\left({\frac {a}{b}}+{\frac {b}{a}}\right)z.}

  • Since the sum of a positive number and its reciprocal is at least 2 by AM–GM inequality, we are finished. Equality holds only for the equilateral triangle, where {\displaystyle P} is its centroid.

Routine omissions

The reflection may lie outside the triangle but remains in the vertex angle wedge. Extending the same area-height comparison requires the routine signed-base-distance case when the point crosses the opposite side; that domain check is omitted in the source, not a claim that the reflected point stays interior. The source states the equality case without tracing equality in each height and reciprocal-sum inequality; these are routine elementary equality checks.
