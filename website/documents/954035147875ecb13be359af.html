Statement

Let ABC be a nondegenerate Euclidean triangle and D strictly between B and C. Using ordinary nonnegative lengths and ordinary angles between the indicated rays,

{\displaystyle {\frac {|BD|}{|CD|}}={\frac {|AB|\sin \angle DAB}{|AC|\sin \angle DAC}}.}
Level
High school only
Level justification

All terms are elementary plane geometry and trigonometry.

Proof 1: Law of sines without the bisection assumption

Applying the law of sines to ABD and ACD and rearranging gives

{\displaystyle {{\frac {|AB|}{|BD|}}\sin \angle DAB=\sin \angle ADB},}{\displaystyle {{\frac {|AC|}{|CD|}}\sin \angle DAC=\sin \angle ADC}.}

Angles ADB and ADC are supplementary, so their sines are equal. Therefore

{\displaystyle {{\frac {|AB|}{|BD|}}\sin \angle DAB={\frac {|AC|}{|CD|}}\sin \angle DAC},}

which rearranges to the stated ratio.

High school only
Level justification

Apply the sine law in the two triangles and equate sines of supplementary angles, then rearrange. Elementary Euclidean trigonometry suffices.
