Statement

Let m\ge1 be an integer and let b_{i,j}\in\{0,1\} for integer indices 0\le i<m and 0\le j<17. Put N=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\sum_{j=0}^{16}b_{i,j}2^{17i+j} and k=17N. Define \operatorname{mod}(t,q)=t-q\lfloor t/q\rfloor for real t and q>0. On each cell i\le x<i+1, k+j\le y<k+j+1, the inequality \frac12<\left\lfloor\operatorname{mod}\left(\left\lfloor y/17\right\rfloor2^{-17\lfloor x\rfloor-\operatorname{mod}(\lfloor y\rfloor,17)},2\right)\right\rfloor holds exactly when b_{i,j}=1. Thus every finite monochrome bitmap of height 17 is encoded by some rectangle at vertical position k of this formula.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Binary encoding, integer powers, floors and remainders are explicitly defined finite arithmetic operations. Complexity of the formula adds no college concept.

Proof 1: Binary digit extraction — outline

The formula is a general-purpose method of decoding a bitmap stored in the constant k, and it could be used to draw any other image. When applied to the unbounded positive range 0\le y, the formula tiles a vertical swath of the plane with a pattern that contains all possible 17-pixel-tall bitmaps.

The constant k is a simple monochrome bitmap image treated as a binary number and multiplied by 17. If k is divided by 17, the least significant bit encodes the cell at (0,k); the 17 least significant bits encode the first column of pixels; the next 17 least significant bits encode the second column, and so on.

Here columns and rows follow increasing x and y, respectively; the source's illustrated plot reverses both axes. The floor/remainder verification is implicit in the source.
