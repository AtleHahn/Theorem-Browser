Statement

Let f be analytic on a neighborhood of the closed disk |z|\le r, where r>0, and f(0)\ne0. List its zeros in |z|<r, with multiplicity, as a_1,\ldots,a_n. Then \log|f(0)|=-\sum_{k=1}^n\log\frac r{|a_k|}+\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log|f(re^{i\theta})|\,d\theta. Boundary zeros are allowed; the integral is understood as an improper integral.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex differentiability and Laurent expansions extend calculus and power series; contour integrals on the given semicircles are ordinary parametrized integrals. The displayed identities use no abstract complex geometry or residue theory.

Proof 1: Remove zeros and apply the harmonic mean-value property

By rescaling, it suffices to prove the case for {\displaystyle r=1}.

  1. If {\displaystyle f} contains zeros on the circle boundary, then we can define {\displaystyle g(z)={\frac {f(z)}{\prod _{k}(z-e^{i\theta _{k}})}}}, where {\displaystyle e^{i\theta _{k}}} are the zeros on the circle boundary, repeated with multiplicity; fill in the removable singularities. The removed factors have modulus one at zero. Since

    {\displaystyle \int _{0}^{2\pi }\ln |e^{i\theta }-e^{i\theta _{k}}|d\theta =2\int _{0}^{\pi }\ln(2\sin \theta )d\theta =0,}

    we have reduced to proving the theorem for {\displaystyle g}, that is, the case with no zeros on the circle boundary.

  2. Define

    {\displaystyle F(z):={\frac {f(z)}{\prod _{k=1}^{n}(z-a_{k})}}}

    and fill in all the removable singularities. We obtain a function {\displaystyle F} that is analytic in {\displaystyle B(0,1+\epsilon )}, for some \epsilon>0, and it has no roots on the closed unit disk.

  3. Since {\displaystyle \log |F|=Re(\log F)} is a harmonic function, we can apply Poisson integral formula to it, and obtain

    {\displaystyle \log |F(0)|={\frac {1}{2\pi }}\int _{0}^{2\pi }\log |F(e^{i\theta })|\,d\theta ={\frac {1}{2\pi }}\int _{0}^{2\pi }\log |f(e^{i\theta })|\,d\theta -\sum _{k=1}^{n}{\frac {1}{2\pi }}\int _{0}^{2\pi }\log |e^{i\theta }-a_{k}|\,d\theta .}

    where {\displaystyle \int _{0}^{2\pi }\log |e^{i\theta }-a_{k}|\,d\theta } can be written as

    {\displaystyle \int _{0}^{2\pi }\log |e^{i\theta }-a_{k}|\,d\theta =\int _{0}^{2\pi }\log |1-a_{k}e^{-i\theta }|\,d\theta =Re\int _{0}^{2\pi }\log(1-a_{k}e^{-i\theta })\,d\theta .}

  4. Now, {\displaystyle \int _{0}^{2\pi }\log(1-a_{k}e^{-i\theta })\,d\theta } is a multiple of a contour integral of function {\displaystyle \log(1-z)/z} along a circle of radius {\displaystyle |a_{k}|<1}. Choose the analytic logarithm of 1-z on |z|<1 with value 0 at z=0. The apparent singularity of \log(1-z)/z at zero is removable, so the contour integral is zero by Cauchy's theorem.

Finally, \log|F(0)|=\log|f(0)|-\sum_k\log|a_k|, which gives the required formula.
