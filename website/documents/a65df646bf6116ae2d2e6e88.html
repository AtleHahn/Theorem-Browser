Statement

Let A be a reduced commutative Noetherian ring, and let K be its total ring of fractions. If A is integrally closed in K, then A is a finite direct product of integrally closed domains.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The claim concerns integral closure inside the total fraction ring and a direct-product decomposition of commutative rings.

Commutative algebra

Proof 1: Integral coordinate idempotents — outline

If \mathfrak p_i, 1\leq i\leq r, are the minimal prime ideals of A, then the total ring of fractions K of A is the direct product of the residue fields \kappa(\mathfrak p_i)=Q(A/\mathfrak p_i), by the total-fraction-ring decomposition for a reduced Noetherian ring. That means we can write 1=e_1+\dots+e_r, where e_i are the coordinate idempotents in K and e_i e_j=0 for i\neq j. Now, if A is integrally closed in K, then each e_i is integral over A (it satisfies e_i^2-e_i=0) and so is in A; consequently, A is a direct product of integrally closed domains Ae_i.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Decompose the total quotient ring at minimal primes, bring coordinate idempotents into A by integral closedness and split into normal factors. Noetherian commutative algebra, total fractions and normalization are substantial advanced prerequisites.
