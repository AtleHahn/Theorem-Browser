Statement

Let B be Brownian motion on a probability space and (\mathcal G_t)_{t\ge0} its usual augmented natural filtration, with \mathcal G_\infty=\sigma(\bigcup_{t\ge0}\mathcal G_t). If X\in L^2 is \mathcal G_\infty-measurable, there is a predictable process C with \mathbb E\int_0^\infty C_s^2\,ds<\infty such that, for every t\ge0,

\mathbb E[X\mid\mathcal G_t]=\mathbb E[X]+\int_0^t C_s\,dB_s

almost surely.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Brownian motion; Augmented filtrations; Conditional expectation; Predictable processes; Square-integrable Itô integrals. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Condition the terminal Itô representation

By the terminal-variable form of the Brownian martingale representation theorem, if X is a square integrable random variable measurable with respect to \mathcal G_\infty, then there exists a predictable process C with \mathbb E\int_0^\infty C_s^2\,ds<\infty such that

X=\mathbb E(X)+\int_0^\infty C_s\,dB_s.

The infinite integral is understood as an L^2 limit. Consequently,

\mathbb E(X\mid\mathcal G_t)=\mathbb E(X)+\int_0^t C_s\,dB_s.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The proof conditions a terminal Brownian representation and passes an infinite Itô integral through an L2 limit. Filtrations, predictability, conditional expectation and stochastic integration require substantial graduate probability.
