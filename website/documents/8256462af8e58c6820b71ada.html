Statement

The function f(z)=e^{1/z} is holomorphic on \mathbb C\setminus\{0\} and has an isolated essential singularity at zero, with Laurent expansion f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}z^{-k}/k!.

Proof 1: The Laurent series and punctured-domain derivative

Consider the function

{\displaystyle f(z)=e^{1/z}.}

This function has the following Laurent series about the essential singular point at 0:

{\displaystyle f(z)=\sum _{n=0}^{\infty }{\frac {1}{n!}}z^{-n}.}

Because {\displaystyle f'(z)=-{\frac {e^{{1}/{z}}}{z^{2}}}} exists for all points z ≠ 0 we know that f(z) is analytic in a punctured neighborhood of z = 0. Hence it is an isolated singularity. It is essential because its Laurent series has infinitely many nonzero negative-degree terms.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Substitution in the exponential series is straightforward, but the full proof invokes the Laurent classification of isolated singularities and holomorphic differentiation. Retain beyond undergraduate provisionally for that cumulative complex-analysis prerequisite.
