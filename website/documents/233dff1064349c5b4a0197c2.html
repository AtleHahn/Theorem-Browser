Statement

Let ABC be a nondegenerate Euclidean triangle with a=|BC|, b=|CA|, c=|AB| and opposite interior angles \alpha,\beta,\gamma. The side a satisfies a=b\cos\gamma\pm\sqrt{c^2-b^2\sin^2\gamma}, with the sign chosen so that it equals the positive side length. In particular, the radicand is nonnegative for the given triangle.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement and argument concern elementary Euclidean triangles, trigonometry and algebra.

Proof 1: Solve the cosine-law quadratic

The cosine law gives a^2-2ab\cos\gamma+b^2-c^2=0. Solving this quadratic in a gives a=b\cos\gamma\pm\sqrt{b^2\cos^2\gamma-b^2+c^2}=b\cos\gamma\pm\sqrt{c^2-b^2\sin^2\gamma}. Since a is a real positive side of the triangle, its root has nonnegative radicand and positive value.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Solve the cosine-law quadratic and simplify with the sine-square identity. School algebra and trigonometry suffice.
