The familiar rules for addition, multiplication and exponentiation do not derive every identity true of positive integers. This gives a concrete limit of a natural algebraic rule system, without implying that the exhibited identity is unprovable in stronger arithmetic theories.

Statement

There are identities built from variables, the constant 1, addition, multiplication and exponentiation that hold for all positive integer substitutions but are not derivable in equational logic from the following eleven identities:

\begin{gathered} x+y=y+x,\qquad (x+y)+z=x+(y+z),\\ x\cdot1=x,\qquad xy=yx,\qquad (xy)z=x(yz),\qquad x(y+z)=xy+xz,\\ 1^x=1,\qquad x^1=x,\qquad x^{y+z}=x^yx^z,\qquad (xy)^z=x^zy^z,\qquad (x^y)^z=x^{yz}. \end{gathered}

In particular, Wilkie's identity

\begin{aligned} &\big((1+x)^y+(1+x+x^2)^y\big)^x\big((1+x^3)^x+(1+x^2+x^4)^x\big)^y\\ ={}&\big((1+x)^x+(1+x+x^2)^x\big)^y\big((1+x^3)^y+(1+x^2+x^4)^y\big)^x \end{aligned}

is true for all positive integers x,y, but cannot be derived from those eleven identities.

Equational derivation permits substitution of terms for variables, replacement of equals within a larger term, and reflexivity, symmetry and transitivity of equality. Numerals abbreviate sums of 1; subtraction and extra arithmetic axioms are not among the allowed rules.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The syntax and allowed finite rewrite rules are explicitly stated and use only elementary algebra. Understanding that no such derivation exists is an elementary quantified assertion, even though proving it requires deeper methods.

Proof 1: A twelve-element countermodel and equational soundness (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (Section1 and Sections7.1–7.3, printed pp.466,478–479 (PDF pp.2,14–15)).

Choose \alpha-option 1, all \beta_j=8, \gamma-option 1, all \delta_j=6, and (\epsilon_1,\epsilon_3)=(6,6), (\epsilon_2,\epsilon_4)=(6,7) in the displayed tables. The eleven identities hold for every triple of labels. Numerals 2,3,4 denote labels 9,8,12, respectively; at labels x=3,y=4, Wilkie’s two sides evaluate to labels 12 and 7. Every allowed equality rule preserves validity in these tables, so this inequality rules out a derivation. Truth on positive integers follows separately by setting A=1+x, B=1+x+x^2, Q=1-x+x^2>0 and factoring both sides as Q^{xy}(A^y+B^y)^x(A^x+B^x)^y.

Source verification and nonderivability limitation

Put A=1+x, B=1+x+x^2 and Q=1-x+x^2. The factorizations 1+x^3=AQ and 1+x^2+x^4=BQ make both sides equal to

Q^{xy}(A^y+B^y)^x(A^x+B^x)^y.

This is the source's ordinary-arithmetic verification of truth.

For nonderivability, the article gives an intuition based on the absence of subtraction from the allowed language. That does not exclude every possible equational derivation by another route. The source attributes the nonderivability theorem to Wilkie but does not supply its proof or a countermodel. The truth calculation is therefore only a partial proof of the stated result.

Level justification

The proof uses finite table lookup and induction on the explicitly stated substitution and equality rules. Any allowed derivation preserves equality in every table system satisfying the eleven axioms; one such system makes the two sides unequal. Ordinary polynomial factorization proves truth for positive integers. No SAT-solving theory is needed to verify the exhibited countermodel.
