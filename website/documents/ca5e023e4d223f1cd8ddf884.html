Statement

A Weil cohomology theory for varieties over finite fields, with the usual functorial action of algebraic correspondences and elliptic-curve first cohomology of dimension 2, cannot have coefficient field \mathbb Q.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Weil cohomology theories; algebraic correspondences; coefficient fields; elliptic-curve cohomology. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Quaternion division-algebra action obstructs rational coefficients

To see this consider the case of a supersingular elliptic curve over a finite field of characteristic p. The endomorphism ring of this is an order in a quaternion division algebra over the rationals, and should act on the first cohomology group, which should be a 2-dimensional vector space over the coefficient field by analogy with the case of a complex elliptic curve. However a quaternion division algebra over the rationals cannot act on a 2-dimensional vector space over the rationals.

The action here is unital, and the endomorphisms are taken over a finite extension where all geometric endomorphisms are defined.
