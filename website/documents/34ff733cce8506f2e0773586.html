Statement

A correspondence C:\Theta\rightrightarrows X assigns subsets of X to points of \Theta. It is upper hemicontinuous if for every \theta and open G\supseteq C(\theta), some neighborhood U of \theta satisfies C(\theta')\subseteq G for all \theta'\in U. It is lower hemicontinuous if for every open G meeting C(\theta), some such U has C(\theta')\cap G\ne\varnothing throughout U. A real function is lower semicontinuous if for every point z and \varepsilon>0 there is a neighborhood on which its values exceed its value at z minus \varepsilon. Let X,\Theta be topological spaces, let f:X\times\Theta\to\mathbb R be lower semicontinuous, and let C:\Theta\rightrightarrows X be lower hemicontinuous with nonempty values. Suppose v(\theta)=\sup_{x\in C(\theta)}f(x,\theta) is finite for every \theta. Then v is lower semicontinuous.

Proof 1: Follow a near-maximizer through lower hemicontinuity

Throughout this proof, a neighborhood means an open set containing the specified point.

Fix {\displaystyle \theta \in \Theta }, and let {\displaystyle \varepsilon >0} be arbitrary. By definition of {\displaystyle v}, there exists {\displaystyle x\in C(\theta )} such that {\displaystyle v(\theta )<f(x,\theta )+{\frac {\varepsilon }{2}}}. Now, since {\displaystyle f} is lower semicontinuous, there exists a neighborhood {\displaystyle U_{1}\times V} of {\displaystyle (\theta ,x)} such that whenever {\displaystyle (\theta ',x')\in U_{1}\times V} we have {\displaystyle f(x,\theta )<f(x',\theta ')+{\frac {\varepsilon }{2}}}. Observe that {\displaystyle C(\theta )\cap V\neq \emptyset } (in particular, {\displaystyle x\in C(\theta )\cap V}). Therefore, since {\displaystyle C} is lower hemicontinuous, there exists a neighborhood {\displaystyle U_{2}} such that whenever {\displaystyle \theta '\in U_{2}} there exists {\displaystyle x'\in C(\theta ')\cap V}. Let {\displaystyle U=U_{1}\cap U_{2}}. Then whenever {\displaystyle \theta '\in U} there exists {\displaystyle x'\in C(\theta ')\cap V}, which implies

{\displaystyle v(\theta )<f(x,\theta )+{\frac {\varepsilon }{2}}<f(x',\theta ')+\varepsilon \leq v(\theta ')+\varepsilon } which was desired. {\displaystyle \square }
