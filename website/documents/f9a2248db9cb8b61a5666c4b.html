Statement

Let U\subseteq\mathbb C be open and let f_j:U\to\mathbb C be holomorphic. If f_j\to f uniformly on every compact subset of U, then f is holomorphic and, for each integer n\ge0, f_j^{(n)}\to f^{(n)} uniformly on every compact subset of U.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Real analysis supplies bounds, uniform convergence and derivatives. Holomorphic means complex differentiable, and entire means so on all of C. These direct extensions suffice for the full bound, image or convergence claim; no Riemann surfaces or complex-geometric structures occur.

Proof 1: Pass to the limit on fixed circles — outline

The conclusion can be deduced from Cauchy's integral formula: indeed the formula also holds in the limit and the integrand, and hence the integral, can be expanded as a power series. In addition the Cauchy formulas for the higher order derivatives show that all these derivatives also converge uniformly on compact subsets.

Here the formulas are applied on a fixed counterclockwise circle \gamma bounding a closed disk contained in U:

f_j^{(n)}(a)=\frac{n!}{2\pi i}\oint_\gamma \frac{f_j(z)}{(z-a)^{n+1}}\,dz,

where a is in its interior. Uniform convergence of derivatives is understood on compact subsets, as in the statement; the source's unqualified uniform-convergence claim requires this restriction. The source leaves the smaller-disk estimates and compact-covering step implicit.

Level justification

Pass uniform limits through fixed-circle Cauchy formulas, expand on smaller disks and use a finite compact cover for derivative estimates. Undergraduate complex analysis and uniform-convergence arguments suffice.
