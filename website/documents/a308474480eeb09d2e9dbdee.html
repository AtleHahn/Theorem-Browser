Statement

Let T_n denote the nth Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind. For every integer n\ge1 and real -1<x<1, T_n(x)^2>T_{n-1}(x)T_{n+1}(x).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The given formulation names the first-kind Chebyshev polynomials without supplying their definition. The needed additional preparation is small: learn T0=1, T1=x, T(n+1)=2xTn-T(n-1), or Tn(cos theta)=cos(n theta), to identify the actual polynomial family. This fits comfortably within 30 minutes of undergraduate background, but the complete statement as supplied is not self-explanatory from standard high-school mathematics and local definitions alone.

Proof 1: Positive difference from the Chebyshev identity — outline

For Chebyshev polynomials the identity is

T_n(x)^2-T_{n-1}(x)T_{n+1}(x)=1-x^2>0\qquad\text{for }-1<x<1.
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The local difference identity reduces the inequality to 1-x^2>0 on (-1,1). Understanding the complete statement also requires identifying T_n, whose recurrence or trigonometric definition is not given in the companion. Learning that single definition is a short preparation within the undergraduate allowance; no special-function theory or proof of the supplied Turán identity is required. The elementary final inequality does not remove this statement prerequisite.
