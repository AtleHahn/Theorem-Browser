Statement

Let s\in L^1(\mathbb R), P>0, and S(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb R}s(x)e^{-2\pi i\xi x}\,dx. The periodization s_P=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}s(\cdot+nP) converges in L^1([0,P]), and its kth Fourier coefficient is P^{-1}S(k/P).

Proof 1: Unfold the periodization integral

First, the periodization s_P(x) converges in L^1 norm to an L^1([0,P]) function which is periodic on \mathbb R, and therefore integrable on any interval of length P. We must therefore show that the Fourier series coefficients of s_P(x) are \frac1P S(k/P), where S(f) is the Fourier transform of s(x).

Proceeding from the definition of the Fourier coefficients we have:

\begin{aligned} S[k]&\triangleq\frac1P\int_0^P s_P(x)e^{-i2\pi(k/P)x}\,dx\\ &=\frac1P\int_0^P\left(\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}s(x+nP)\right)e^{-i2\pi(k/P)x}\,dx\\ &=\frac1P\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\int_0^P s(x+nP)e^{-i2\pi(k/P)x}\,dx, \end{aligned}

where the interchange of summation with integration is justified by dominated convergence. With a change of variables (\tau=x+nP), this becomes

\begin{aligned} S[k] &=\frac1P\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{nP}^{(n+1)P} s(\tau)e^{-i2\pi(k/P)\tau}\underbrace{e^{i2\pi kn}}_{1}\,d\tau\\ &=\frac1P\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}s(\tau)e^{-i2\pi(k/P)\tau}\,d\tau =\frac1P S\left(\frac{k}{P}\right). \end{aligned}

This identifies the coefficients of the possibly divergent Fourier series; it does not assert pointwise convergence for arbitrary L^1 functions.
