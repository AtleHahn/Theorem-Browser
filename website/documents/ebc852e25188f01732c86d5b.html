Proof 1: Take a two-element Folkman set

Folkman's theorem states that, whenever the positive integers are partitioned into finitely many subsets, for every positive integer m there exists a set S_m of m positive integers such that every sum of a nonempty subset of S_m belongs to the same part of the partition.

The required result is the special case m=2 of Folkman's theorem: for the resulting two-element set \{x,y\}, the nonempty subset sums are precisely x,y,x+y, and they have one color.
