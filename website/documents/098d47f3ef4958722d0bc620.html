Statement

Let H be a subgroup of a group G. Define N_G(H)=\{g\in G:gHg^{-1}=H\} and C_G(H)=\{g\in G:gh=hg\text{ for every }h\in H\}. Then N_G(H)/C_G(H) is isomorphic to a subgroup of the automorphism group \operatorname{Aut}(H).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory group theory supplies normal subgroups, cosets, kernels, actions and automorphisms. Complement, normalizer, centralizer or fixed-point sets are explicitly defined by these operations, so no representation or extension-field theory is needed.

Proof 1: Normalizer–centralizer theorem — outline

We are able to find a group homomorphism

f:N_G(H)\to\operatorname{Aut}(H),\qquad f(g)(h)=ghg^{-1}\quad(h\in H).

Clearly, the kernel of f is C_G(H). Hence we have a natural surjective homomorphism

\varphi:N_G(H)\to N_G(H)/C_G(H),\qquad g\mapsto gC_G(H).

The first isomorphism theorem then asserts that there exists an isomorphism between N_G(H)/C_G(H) and f(N_G(H)), which is a subgroup of \operatorname{Aut}(H).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Map a normalizing element to its conjugation automorphism, identify the kernel and use the first isomorphism theorem. Standard undergraduate group theory suffices.
