Statement

Let (A_n) and (C_n) be computable increasing sequences of finite subsets of \mathbb N, with unions A,C. Let f:\mathbb N\to\mathbb N be total and computable with oracle C. Suppose for every n,i, C_n\cap[0,f(i)]=C\cap[0,f(i)] implies i\notin A_{n+1}\setminus A_n. Then A\le_T C. Here [0,m]=\{0,\ldots,m\}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Oracle computation adds membership-query access to ordinary algorithms; computable finite-set stages and Turing reducibility then have a limited operational meaning.

Proof 1: Find the oracle-known stabilization stage

Given any {\displaystyle i}, the finite initial segment {\displaystyle C\cap [0,f(i)]} is a limit of a sequence of finite sets: {\displaystyle C_{n}\cap [0,f(i)]\uparrow C\cap [0,f(i)]}, and it is a finite set. So, we construct a C-oracle Turing machine, which takes {\displaystyle i} as input, and outputs the smallest {\displaystyle n} such that the limit is reached: {\displaystyle C_{n}\cap [0,f(i)]=C\cap [0,f(i)]}.

Then, given any {\displaystyle i}, compute the corresponding {\displaystyle n}. By the permitting condition, {\displaystyle i\not \in A_{n+1}\setminus A_{n},i\not \in A_{n+2}\setminus A_{n+1},\dots }, thus {\displaystyle i\in A} iff {\displaystyle i\in A_{n}}, which is decidable.

Level justification

Use the oracle to compute f(i) and the finite target segment, search its stabilization stage and decide membership from that stage. Basic algorithmic search and the explicitly stated oracle convention suffice without priority constructions or degree theory.
