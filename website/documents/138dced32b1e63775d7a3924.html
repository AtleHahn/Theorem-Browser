Statement

For real \psi, put t=\tanh(\psi/2)\in(-1,1). Projection of (\cosh\psi,\sinh\psi) from (-1,0) onto the y-axis has ordinate t=\sinh\psi/(1+\cosh\psi). Also t=(\cosh\psi-1)/\sinh\psi when \psi\ne0. The identities are

\sinh\psi=\frac{2t}{1-t^2},\quad\cosh\psi=\frac{1+t^2}{1-t^2},\quad\tanh\psi=\frac{2t}{1+t^2},\quad\coth\psi=\frac{1+t^2}{2t},\quad\operatorname{sech}\psi=\frac{1-t^2}{1+t^2},\quad\operatorname{csch}\psi=\frac{1-t^2}{2t},

with the expressions involving 2t restricted to t\ne0. Moreover e^\psi=(1+t)/(1-t) and e^{-\psi}=(1-t)/(1+t).

High school only

Proof 1: Project the hyperbola by the circle’s line-intersection recipe — outline

Use the same projection construction as for the unit circle: join (-1,0) to (\cosh\psi,\sinh\psi) and intersect the joining line with the y-axis. Its equation is y=(1+x)t. For the right branch of the hyperbola, the corresponding intersection recipe gives

x=\frac{1+t^2}{1-t^2},\qquad y=\frac{2t}{1-t^2},\qquad t=\tanh(\psi/2).

These are the displayed expressions for \cosh\psi and \sinh\psi. Their quotients and reciprocals give the other hyperbolic functions, while \cosh\psi\pm\sinh\psi=e^{\pm\psi} gives the exponential ratios.

Level justification
