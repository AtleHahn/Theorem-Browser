Statement

For a finite arrangement of distinct lines in the real projective plane, not all through one point, let t_k be the number of points incident with exactly k lines. Then t_2\ge3+\sum_{k\ge4}(k-3)t_k.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The real projective plane can be understood by homogeneous coordinates or antipodal identification, a limited extension of undergraduate linear algebra/topology. Line-arrangement multiplicities are finite incidence counts.

Proof 1: Count crossing multiplicities and faces — outline

For a graph giving a cell decomposition of the real projective plane, the formula V-E+F must equal 1, the Euler characteristic of the projective plane. Here V, E, and F are the number of vertices, edges, and faces of the graph, respectively. Any nontrivial line arrangement on the projective plane defines a graph in which each face is bounded by at least three edges, and each edge bounds two faces; so, double counting gives the additional inequality F\leq 2E/3. Using this inequality to eliminate F from the Euler characteristic leads to the inequality E\leq 3V-3.

For every k\geq2, let t_k be the number of points to which exactly k lines are incident. Here V=\sum_{k\geq2}t_k and E=\sum_{k\geq2}kt_k, since each such vertex has degree 2k. Then

\sum_{k\geq2}(k-3)t_k\leq-3,

or equivalently,

t_2\geq3+\sum_{k\geq4}(k-3)t_k.
