Statement

Let f∈Q[[x]], and let v∈Q[[x,y]] satisfy v=x+yf(v). With D=d/dx acting formally on Q[[x]],

v=x+\sum_{k\geq1}\frac{y^k}{k!}D^{k-1}\bigl(f(x)^k\bigr).

All sums are interpreted formally, coefficient by coefficient in y.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Calculus and power series supply composition, coefficients, differentiation and local inversion. Formal series retain the same coefficient operations without analytic convergence; Lambert W is specified by its inverse equation. These direct definitions suffice for the displayed coefficient identities.

Proof 1: Substitute the identity function into composition reversion

Use the formal Lagrange composition-reversion theorem: if v=x+yf(v), then for a formal series g,

g(v)=g(x)+\sum_{k\geq1}\frac{y^k}{k!}D^{k-1}\bigl(f(x)^kg'(x)\bigr),\qquad D=\frac{d}{dx}.

Set g(x)=x, as the source instructs. Then g(v)=v and g'(x)=1, giving the asserted formula.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Set g(x)=x in the supplied formal composition formula and simplify its derivative. Undergraduate power-series algebra suffices with the explicitly stated reversion formula as one small extension.
