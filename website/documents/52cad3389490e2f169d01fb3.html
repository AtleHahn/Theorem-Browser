Statement

Let n>0 be a nonsquare integer and let positive integers x_1,y_1 satisfy x_1^2-ny_1^2=1. Define x_k+y_k\sqrt n=(x_1+y_1\sqrt n)^k for k\geq0. Then these are integer Pell solutions and x_{k+1}=x_1x_k+ny_1y_k, y_{k+1}=x_1y_k+y_1x_k, with (x_0,y_0)=(1,0). They give infinitely many distinct solutions.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The integer constructions use finite algebra and explicitly stated recurrences.

Proof 1: Multiplication and coefficient comparison

Starting with x_1+y_1\sqrt n, calculate from

x_k+y_k\sqrt n=(x_1+y_1\sqrt n)^k,

expanding the right side, equating coefficients of \sqrt n on both sides, and equating the other terms on both sides. This yields the recurrence relations

x_{k+1}=x_1x_k+ny_1y_k,y_{k+1}=x_1y_k+y_1x_k.

They preserve integrality, starting with (x_0,y_0)=(1,0). Taking the product with the conjugate gives

x_k^2-ny_k^2=(x_1^2-ny_1^2)^k=1.

Since x_1>1 and y_1>0, the positive-coordinate recurrence strictly increases the solutions after the initial pair.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Multiply expressions in a square root, compare coefficients, take the conjugate product and use positive recurrence coefficients for strict growth. School algebra and integer induction suffice.
