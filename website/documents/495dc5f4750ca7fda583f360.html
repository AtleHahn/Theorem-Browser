Statement

Assume Peano arithmetic (PA) is consistent. Let PH be the arithmetic assertion: for every positive n,k,m with m\ge n, some N has the property that every k-coloring of the n-element subsets of \{1,\ldots,N\} has a subset Y with |Y|\ge m, |Y|\ge\min Y, and all its n-element subsets of one color. Then PA does not prove PH.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The claim is about provability and consistency of an arithmetic formal system.

Proof 1: Consistency implication and Gödel’s second theorem

Use the source-attributed Paris–Harrington implication, formalizable in PA, \mathrm{PH}\Rightarrow\operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{PA}). If PA proved PH, it would therefore prove its own consistency. Gödel’s second incompleteness theorem says that consistent PA cannot do so. Consequently PA cannot prove PH.
