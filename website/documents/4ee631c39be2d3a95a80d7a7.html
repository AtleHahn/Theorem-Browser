Statement

Independently draw with replacement from n\geq1 coupon types, each with probability 1/n, and let T be the number of draws until every type has appeared. Here n is an integer. Let H_n^{(r)}=\sum_{j=1}^n j^{-r} and set C_i(n)=\sum_{\pi\in S_i}\prod_{c\text{ cycle of }\pi}H_n^{(|c|)}, where S_i is the finite set of permutations of 1,\ldots,i and |c| is the length of the cycle. For every integer k\geq1, \mathbb ET^k=\sum_{i=1}^k S(k,i)(-1)^{k-i}n^iC_i(n), where S(k,i) counts partitions of a k-element set into i nonempty blocks.

The sampling experiment is explicit and discrete. Expectation and variance are weighted sums over possible integer waiting times; analysis supplies infinite sums and asymptotic notation, and algebra supplies permutation cycles where used. These concrete additions do not require general stochastic-process theory.

Proof 1: Apply the Stirling transform to falling moments

Use the falling-moment formula

\operatorname E(T^{\underline k}) =\sum_{i=1}^kL(k,i)(-1)^{k-i}n^iC_i(n),

where T^{\underline k}=T(T-1)\cdots(T-k+1) and L(k,i)=\binom{k-1}{i-1}k!/i!.

The raw moments of the distribution can be obtained from the falling moments via a Stirling transform,

\operatorname E(T^K)=\sum_{k=1}^KS(K,k)\operatorname E(T^{\underline k});

due to the identity

S(K,i)(-1)^K=\sum_{k=i}^KS(K,k)L(k,i)(-1)^k,

this provides

\operatorname E(T^K)= \sum_{i=1}^KS(K,i)(-1)^{K-i}n^i \sum_{P\in S_i}\prod_{c\text{ cycle of }P}H_n^{(|c|)}.

Level justification

Substitute the explicit falling-moment formula into the finite Stirling transform and collect coefficients by the supplied Lah identity. Discrete moments and finite polynomial/combinatorial algebra suffice.
