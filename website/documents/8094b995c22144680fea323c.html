Statement

Let M be a finite simplicial complex of dimension m\ge5, locally piecewise-linearly homeomorphic to \mathbb R^m. Suppose M has the homotopy type of S^m. Then M is homeomorphic to S^m, and there is a point x\in M such that M\setminus\{x\} is piecewise-linearly homeomorphic to \mathbb R^m; the sphere homeomorphism can be chosen piecewise linear away from x. A finite simplicial complex is built from finitely many Euclidean simplices glued along faces. A piecewise-linear homeomorphism is a homeomorphism linear on simplices after suitable triangulations or subdivisions. Having the homotopy type of S^m means there are continuous maps in both directions whose composites are continuously deformable to the identity maps. Here S^m=\{v\in\mathbb R^{m+1}:\|v\|=1\}.

Proof 1: Complementary skeleta and engulfing (external)

A link to the proof outline can be found here (Zeeman, Bull. AMS 67 (1961), p.270, lemma and proof paragraph; Stallings, Bull. AMS 66 (1960), Lemmas 3 and 9 and final proof, pp.486–488.).

Supporting sources: Stallings, Polyhedral Homotopy-Spheres, Lemmas 3 and 9.

The linked articles give an expert proof sketch with engulfing and regular-neighborhood dependencies. Choose complementary primal and dual skeleta of dimensions p,q with p+q=m-1 and p,q\le m-3. Homotopy-sphere connectivity allows Zeeman's engulfing lemma to place both in PL balls. Stallings's expansion and two-ball lemmas give a point whose complement is PL-Euclidean. One-point compactification yields the sphere homeomorphism, PL off that point. The choices (p,q)=(2,2) and (2,3) cover dimensions five and six.
