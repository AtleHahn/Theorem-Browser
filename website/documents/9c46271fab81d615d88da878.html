Statement

Let G=(V,E) be a finite undirected graph, allowing repeated edges and loops. Its vertices form V, and each edge has two ends attached to vertices. Write d(v) for the number of attached ends, counting a loop twice. Then \sum_{v\in V}d(v)=2|E|.

Proof 1: Count edge ends by vertices and by edges

Euler's proof of the degree sum formula uses the technique of double counting: he counts endpoint incidences in two different ways. The two ends of a loop are distinguished, in accordance with the degree convention. Vertex v belongs to d(v) incidences, where d(v) is the degree of v. Therefore, the number of incidences is the sum of the degrees. However, each edge in the graph belongs to exactly two incidences, one for each of its endpoints; therefore, the number of incidences is 2|E|. Since these two formulas count the same set of objects, they must have equal values. The same proof can be interpreted as summing the entries of the incidence matrix of the graph in two ways, by rows to get the sum of degrees and by columns to get twice the number of edges. A loop contributes two in its vertex row.
