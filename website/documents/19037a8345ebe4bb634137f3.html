Statement

Let K be an algebraically closed field, n\ge1 an integer, and A\in K^{n\times n}. Suppose q_1,\ldots,q_n are linearly independent vectors with Aq_i=\lambda_iq_i. Put Q=[q_1\ \cdots\ q_n] and \Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n). Then A=Q\Lambda Q^{-1}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra and introductory abstract algebra supply determinants, matrices, field roots, ring operations and divisibility. The displayed assertion uses these directly; no graduate concepts are needed to understand it.

Proof 1: Assemble the eigenvector equations as columns

The decomposition can be derived from the fundamental property of eigenvectors:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\mathbf {A} \mathbf {v} &=\lambda \mathbf {v} \\\mathbf {A} \mathbf {Q} &=\mathbf {Q} \mathbf {\Lambda } \\\mathbf {A} &=\mathbf {Q} \mathbf {\Lambda } \mathbf {Q} ^{-1}.\end{aligned}}}

Here Q is invertible because its columns are linearly independent.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Collect the eigenvector equations as AQ=Q Lambda, use independent columns to invert Q, and multiply. Standard linear algebra over a field suffices.
