Statement

Let G be a finite simple plane graph whose faces are bounded by cycles. Suppose exactly one face has length not congruent to 2 modulo 3, while every other face has length congruent to 2 modulo 3. Then G has no Hamiltonian cycle (a cycle visiting every vertex exactly once).

Proof 1: Reduce the face sum modulo three

Grinberg’s theorem states that a Hamiltonian cycle partitions the faces into inside and outside classes with \sum_{k\ge3}(k-2)(f_k-g_k)=0, where f_k,g_k count their length-k faces.

To prove the corollary, consider the sum \sum_{k\ge3}(k-2)(f_k-g_k) in Grinberg’s necessary condition, for an arbitrary partition of the faces into two subsets, counted by numbers {\displaystyle f_{k}} and {\displaystyle g_{k}}. Each face whose number of sides is 2 mod 3 contributes a multiple of three to the sum, because of the factor {\displaystyle k-2} in the term to which it contributes, while the one remaining face does not. Therefore, the sum is not a multiple of three, and in particular is not zero. Since there is no way of partitioning the faces into two subsets that produce a sum obeying Grinberg's theorem, there can be no Hamiltonian cycle.
