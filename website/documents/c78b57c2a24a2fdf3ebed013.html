Statement

The assertion that x^n+y^n=z^n has no positive integer solution for every integer n>2 is equivalent to the assertion for n=4 and all odd prime exponents.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The conditional equivalence concerns prime divisibility of positive integer exponents and power equations.

Proof 1: Pass to a divisor of the exponent

It is necessary to prove only that the equation a^n+b^n=c^n has no positive integer solutions (a,b,c) when n=4 or n is an odd prime number. This follows because a solution (a,b,c) for a given n gives a solution for every factor of n. For illustration, let n be factored into d and e, n=de. The general equation

a^n+b^n=c^n

implies that (a^d,b^d,c^d) is a solution for the exponent e:

(a^d)^e+(b^d)^e=(c^d)^e.

Thus, to prove that Fermat's equation has no solutions for n>2, it would suffice to prove that it has no solutions for at least one divisor greater than two of every n. Each integer n>2 is divisible by 4 or by an odd prime number (or both). Therefore, Fermat's Last Theorem is proved for all n>2 if it is proved for n=4 and for all odd primes p.

The reverse implication is immediate, since 4 and the odd primes are among the exponents greater than two.

Level justification

A positive solution at exponent de gives one at exponent e by raising all coordinates to d. Each integer above two has a divisor four or an odd prime. Elementary prime factors and power laws suffice.
