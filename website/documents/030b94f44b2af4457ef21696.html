Statement

Let X,Y be Banach spaces over \mathbb R or \mathbb C and let T:X\to Y be a compact linear operator. Its transpose T^*:Y^*\to X^*, defined by (T^*y^*)(x)=y^*(Tx) on the continuous duals, is compact.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The assertion and proof concern infinite-dimensional Banach-space duality.

Proof 1: Restrict the dual unit ball to the compact image

Dependency

On a compact Hausdorff space X, a pointwise bounded equicontinuous family in C(X,\mathbb R) has compact closure for the uniform norm. Equicontinuity means that for each x and \varepsilon>0 there is a neighborhood U of x with |f(y)-f(x)|<\varepsilon for every f in the family and y\in U. For complex-valued functions the same conclusion follows by treating real and imaginary coordinates.

Restriction to a compact image

Indeed, the image T(B) of the closed unit ball B of X is contained in a compact subset K of Y. The unit ball B∗ of Y∗ defines, by restricting from Y to K, a set F of (linear) continuous functions on K that is bounded and equicontinuous. By Arzelà–Ascoli, for every sequence {y∗ n}, in B∗, there is a subsequence that converges uniformly on K, and this implies that the image {\displaystyle T^{*}(y_{n_{k}}^{*})} of that subsequence is Cauchy in X∗.

Level justification

Restrict the dual unit ball to the compact image, apply Arzela-Ascoli, and transfer uniform convergence to the dual norm. Continuous duals, compact operators and norm completeness of the dual together are substantial functional analysis.
