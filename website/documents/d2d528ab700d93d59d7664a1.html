Statement

Let R be an integral domain and p a prime element of R. Then p is a prime element of R[x].

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory ring theory supplies primes, polynomial gcds, units and unique factorization. A fraction field generalizes ordinary fractions, and primitive means coefficient gcd one. These closely related polynomial definitions suffice.

Proof 1: Prime elements stay prime after adjoining a variable — proof

The quotient R[x]/(p) is isomorphic to (R/(p))[x], which is an integral domain. Hence (p) is a prime ideal of R[x].

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Identify the coefficientwise quotient polynomial ring as a domain and translate the prime-ideal criterion back to its generator. Undergraduate rings, quotient rings and prime ideals suffice.
