Statement

Let U\subseteq\mathbb R^n be open, n\ge1, and let f:U\to[0,\infty) have continuous partial derivatives through order two. The nonnegative square root g(x)=\sqrt{f(x)} has continuous first partial derivatives on U if and only if, at every x\in U with f(x)=0, all first and second partial derivatives of f vanish: \partial_i f(x)=0,\qquad\partial_i\partial_j f(x)=0\quad(1\le i,j\le n).

Undergraduate multivariable calculus and real analysis suffice. Taylor estimates control flat functions near their zero set; smoothing a distance cutoff gives the required derivative bounds, and the mean value theorem glues the first derivatives across the zero set.
