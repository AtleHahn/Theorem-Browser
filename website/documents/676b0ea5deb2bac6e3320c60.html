Large deviations quantify how rapidly atypical sample averages become improbable. This explains macroscopic statistical stability while retaining a precise account of rare fluctuations under the stated independence and moment assumptions.

Statement

Let X_1,X_2,\ldots be independent identically distributed real random variables with \Lambda(t)=\log\mathbb E e^{tX_1}<\infty for every real t. Put S_n=\sum_{i=1}^nX_i and I(x)=\sup_{t\in\mathbb R}(tx-\Lambda(t)). For every x>\mathbb EX_1, \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\log\mathbb P(S_n\ge nx)=-I(x), with \log0=-\infty.

Level justification

The scalar probability, expectation and real-analysis operations are accessible with undergraduate probability and analysis; no measure-space or LDP machinery is required to understand this formulation.

The proof uses convexity, exponential estimates and a probability law obtained by explicitly reweighting expectations. Finite exponential moments justify differentiation by dominated convergence, whose proof follows from simple-function approximation. Chebyshev’s inequality supplies the required law of large numbers, so undergraduate analysis and elementary probability suffice.
