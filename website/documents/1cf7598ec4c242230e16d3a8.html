Statement

Let H be a real or complex RKHS on a topological space X, with evaluation sections defined by f(x)=\langle f,K_x\rangle. If x\mapsto\|K_x\| is continuous, then convergence in H norm implies uniform convergence on every compact subset of X. In the complex case the inner product is linear in its first argument.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Completeness and bounded evaluation define the RKHS setting; compactness and uniform convergence are baseline topology/analysis. The supplied evaluation identity makes this a limited related extension.

Proof 1: Bound the section norms on a compact set — outline

On a compact subset S, the continuous function x\mapsto\|K_x\| is bounded by some B. The reproducing identity and Cauchy–Schwarz give \sup_{x\in S}|f(x)-g(x)|\le B\|f-g\|, so norm convergence implies uniform convergence on S.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understand RKHS as a complete inner-product function space with bounded evaluations, not merely a collection of finite vectors. These definitions extend undergraduate metric analysis. Compactness bounds the section norms; the supplied reproducing identity and Cauchy–Schwarz give uniform error at most B times the norm error.
