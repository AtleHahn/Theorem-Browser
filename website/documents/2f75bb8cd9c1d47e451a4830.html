Statement

Let X be a real random variable and let D=\{t>0:\mathbb E e^{tX}<\infty\} be nonempty. Write K(t)=\log\mathbb E e^{tX} for t\in D. For every real \varepsilon, -\log\Pr(X\ge\varepsilon)\ge\sup_{t\in D}\{t\varepsilon-K(t)\}, with -\log0=+\infty.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

From real analysis, interpret expectation as a probability-weighted integral of the positive function exp(tX). The statement explicitly defines its finite domain D, logarithm K and supremum expression. Learning this single-variable expectation interpretation is a small addition within about 30 minutes; the remaining logarithms and suprema are undergraduate analysis, without large-deviation theory.

Proof 1: Take logarithms and optimize positive parameters — outline

A related inequality sometimes known as the exponential Chebyshev's inequality is the inequality

{\displaystyle \Pr(X\geq \varepsilon )\leq e^{-t\varepsilon }\operatorname {E} \left(e^{tX}\right),\qquad t>0.}

For t\in D, let {\displaystyle K(t)} be the cumulant generating function,

{\displaystyle K(t)=\log \left(\operatorname {E} \left(e^{tX}\right)\right).}

Taking the supremum over t\in D in the Legendre–Fenchel expression for {\displaystyle K(t)} and using the exponential Chebyshev's inequality we have

{\displaystyle -\log(\Pr(X\geq \varepsilon ))\geq \sup _{t\in D}(t\varepsilon -K(t)).}

Here -\log0=+\infty, as in the statement.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

For each admissible positive t, take the negative logarithm of the supplied tail bound and then take a supremum of the right sides. Means of positive random variables and supremum/logarithm rules are undergraduate probability and analysis. The Legendre–Fenchel name adds no prerequisite because its exact expression is given.
