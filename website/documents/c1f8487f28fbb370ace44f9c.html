Simpler proof assessment

Likely alternative prerequisite level: Undergraduate level; lowest recorded proof level: Beyond undergraduate level. From any interior state, a run of 2m steps in one direction exits with probability at least 2^(-2m). Blockwise conditioning bounds the exit-time tail geometrically, proving finite expectation. First-step conditioning gives u(j)=1+(u(j-1)+u(j+1))/2 and u(+/-m)=0. The unique finite-system solution is u(j)=m^2-j^2, so u(0)=m^2. Finite probabilities, convergent geometric sums and difference equations replace optional stopping.

Statement

For a simple symmetric random walk (X_n) starting at 0, let \tau be the first hit of -m or m, with integer m\ge1. Then \mathbb E\tau=m^2.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The random walk is repeated independent fair plus/minus-one choices, and first exit is the first step reaching either stated integer. Expected time is the weighted sum of these discrete probabilities. These concrete sequence/probability definitions suffice without martingales or stopping-time theory.

Proof 1: Quadratic martingale — outline

The stopped walk in a finite interval has finite expected exit time, as supplied by standard finite-state Markov-chain theory. Use the martingale Y_n=X_n^2-n. Optional stopping gives 0=\mathbb E Y_0=\mathbb E Y_\tau=m^2-\mathbb E\tau, hence \mathbb E\tau=m^2.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the quadratic martingale and optional stopping at the integrable exit time. Martingales, stopping times and the optional-stopping hypotheses are a cumulative probability extension not confidently within the allowance, despite the elementary final expectation equation.
