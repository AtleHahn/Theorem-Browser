Proof 1: Lifting nonzero residues to roots of unity — proof

Take F(X)=X^p-X. Every a\in\mathbb F_p satisfies F(a)=0, and F^\prime(X)=pX^{p-1}-1\equiv-1\pmod p. Hensel lifting therefore gives a unique root u reducing to a. For a\ne0 this root is nonzero, and F(u)=u(u^{p-1}-1)=0 gives u^{p-1}=1. Conversely any such root is a root of F with that reduction, so uniqueness follows.

Lift the roots of X^p-X and cancel a nonzero p-adic factor. The full p-adic ring, its root-lifting limit and domain property together exceed the small-extension allowance; retain conservatively.
