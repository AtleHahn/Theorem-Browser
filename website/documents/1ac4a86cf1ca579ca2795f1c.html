Statement

For real or complex formal series F(x)=\sum_{j\ge1}a_jx^j/j! and G(x)=\sum_{k\ge0}b_kx^k/k!, write G(F(x))=\sum_{n\ge0}c_nx^n/n!. Then c_n=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_n}b_{|\pi|}\prod_{B\in\pi}a_{|B|}, where \Pi_n is the set of partitions of \{1,\ldots,n\}, with the empty partition giving c_0=b_0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Formal power series use coefficientwise polynomial arithmetic without a convergence requirement. The finite partition or coefficient sums are explicitly specified, making this a small extension of undergraduate algebra and calculus series.

Proof 1: Read normalized coefficients as formal derivatives — outline

In the formal power series

F(x)=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{a_n}{n!}x^n,

we have the nth derivative at 0:

F^{(n)}(0)=a_n\qquad(n\ge1).

This should not be construed as the value of a function, since these series are purely formal; there is no such thing as convergence or divergence in this context.

If

G(x)=\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{b_n}{n!}x^n

and

G(F(x))=H(x)=\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{c_n}{n!}x^n,

then the coefficient c_n (which would be the nth derivative of H evaluated at 0 if we were dealing with convergent series rather than formal power series) is given, by the partition form of the formal Faà di Bruno formula, by

c_n=\sum_{\pi=\{B_1,\ldots,B_k\}}a_{|B_1|}\cdots a_{|B_k|}b_k,

where \pi runs through the set of all partitions of the set \{1,\ldots,n\} and B_1,\ldots,B_k are the blocks of the partition \pi, and |B_j| is the number of members of the jth block, for j=1,\ldots,k. For n=0 the empty partition gives c_0=b_0.

Level justification

Read the normalized coefficients as formal derivatives and apply the supplied partition-form chain rule. Formal differentiation and composition are a small algebraic extension of polynomial rules, and set partitions are finite combinatorics.
