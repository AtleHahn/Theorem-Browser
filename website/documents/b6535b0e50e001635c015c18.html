Statement

Partition fifteen voters into three groups of five. Each group chooses between two options by majority, and the final decision is the majority of the three group decisions. An option supported by only six of the fifteen voters can win, whereas a direct majority would require at least eight supporters.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The construction and threshold comparison use only finite counting.

Proof 1: Two groups with three supporters each

Suppose there are 15 voters. In a direct majority system, a decision is accepted whenever at least 8 votes support it. Suppose now that the voters are grouped into 3 groups of size 5 each. A decision is accepted whenever at least 2 groups support it, and in each group, a decision is accepted whenever at least 3 voters support it. Therefore, a decision may be accepted even if only 6 voters support it: three supporters in each of two groups, with the remaining nine voters opposed.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Count three supporters in each of two winning groups. Only integer counting and majority are needed.
