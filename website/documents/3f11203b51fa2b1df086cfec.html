Statement

For every integer d\ge1, there are 2^d halfspaces in \mathbb R^d such that every 2^d-1 of them have a common point of \mathbb Z^d, while the intersection of all 2^d contains no point of \mathbb Z^d. Here \mathbb Z^d consists of the points with integer coordinates.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves finite-dimensional Euclidean halfspaces; integer coordinates; finite intersections. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Cutting off each cube vertex — outline

Choose, for each vertex of the unit cube [0,1]^d, a halfspace containing all the other vertices but excluding that vertex. These halfspaces form the required system.
