Statement

Let P be a nonconvex simple polygon in the Euclidean plane. A pocket is the simple polygon bounded by a consecutive boundary chain of P and an edge of its convex hull that is not an edge of P. Reflect that chain in the line of this hull edge, leaving the other edges fixed. The resulting polygon is simple and has strictly greater area.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The pocket and flip are defined using elementary plane geometry.

Proof 1: Separate the reflected pocket across a supporting line — outline

The reflected pocket lies entirely within the reflected image of the convex hull, on the other side of the line containing the hull edge. Therefore the reflection cannot introduce crossings, and the flipped boundary is another simple polygon with larger area.

Reflect the pocket across a convex-hull supporting line to place it on the opposite side, preventing new crossings and increasing the enclosed area. School plane geometry suffices.
