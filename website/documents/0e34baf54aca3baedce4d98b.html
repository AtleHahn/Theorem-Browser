Statement

Let A be Hermitian on \mathbb C^n, n\ge1, with eigenvalues \lambda_1\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_n, repeated with multiplicity. Write q_A(x)=x^*Ax for unit vectors x. For every 1\le k\le n,

\lambda_k=\max_{\dim M=k}\min_{x\in M,\|x\|=1}q_A(x) =\min_{\dim M=n-k+1}\max_{x\in M,\|x\|=1}q_A(x).

Proof 1: Eigenvector subspaces attain the intersection bound

Let v_1,\ldots,v_n be the corresponding unit-length orthogonal eigenvectors.

We first prove Poincaré's inequality: if M is a subspace of dimension k, there are unit vectors x,y\in M such that

q_A(x)\leq\lambda_k,\qquad q_A(y)\geq\lambda_{n-k+1}.

Part 2 is a corollary, using -A.

M is a k dimensional subspace, so the span N:=\operatorname{span}(v_k,\ldots,v_n) of these n-k+1 linearly independent eigenvectors must intersect M on at least a single line.

Take unit x\in M\cap N. That's what we need.

x=\sum_{i=k}^na_iv_i, since x\in N.

Since \sum_{i=k}^n|a_i|^2=1, we find q_A(x)=\sum_{i=k}^n|a_i|^2\lambda_i\leq\lambda_k.

We now prove the min-max theorem. Part 2 is a corollary of part 1, by using -A with index n-k+1.

By Poincaré's inequality, \lambda_k is an upper bound to

\max_{\dim\mathcal M=k}\min_{x\in\mathcal M,\ \|x\|=1}q_A(x).

By setting \mathcal M=\operatorname{span}(v_1,\ldots,v_k), the upper bound is achieved.

On each fixed subspace the unit sphere is compact, so the continuous quadratic form has its stated minimum and maximum.
