Likely alternative prerequisite level: High school only; lowest recorded proof level: Undergraduate level. Put x=s-a,y=s-b,z=s-c, which are positive for a nondegenerate triangle. Heron's formula reduces the claimed inequality to (xy+yz+zx)^2>=3xyz(x+y+z). Subtracting the right side gives one half of (xy-yz)^2+(yz-zx)^2+(zx-xy)^2. Positivity justifies squaring and reversing the substitution. School algebra and triangle area replace tangent convexity and Jensen.

Statement

Let a nondegenerate Euclidean triangle have side lengths a,b,c, opposite angles \alpha,\beta,\gamma, and area \Delta. Then a^2+b^2+c^2\ge(a-b)^2+(b-c)^2+(c-a)^2+4\sqrt3\,\Delta.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The statement compares side lengths and area of a Euclidean triangle using squares, square roots and elementary real inequalities.

Proof 1: Hadwiger–Finsler triangle inequality — source argument

From the cosines law we have:

{\displaystyle a^{2}=b^{2}+c^{2}-2bc\cos \alpha }

{\displaystyle \alpha } being the angle between {\displaystyle b} and {\displaystyle c}. This can be transformed into:

{\displaystyle a^{2}=(b-c)^{2}+2bc(1-\cos \alpha )}

Since {\displaystyle \Delta ={\frac {1}{2}}bc\sin \alpha } we have:

{\displaystyle a^{2}=(b-c)^{2}+4\Delta {\frac {(1-\cos \alpha )}{\sin \alpha }}}

Now remember that

{\displaystyle 1-\cos \alpha =2\sin ^{2}{\frac {\alpha }{2}}}

and

{\displaystyle \sin \alpha =2\sin {\frac {\alpha }{2}}\cos {\frac {\alpha }{2}}}

Using this we get:

{\displaystyle a^{2}=(b-c)^{2}+4\Delta \tan {\frac {\alpha }{2}}}

Doing this for all sides of the triangle and adding up we get:

{\displaystyle a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}=(a-b)^{2}+(b-c)^{2}+(c-a)^{2}+4\Delta {\Big (}\tan {\frac {\alpha }{2}}+\tan {\frac {\beta }{2}}+\tan {\frac {\gamma }{2}}{\Big )}}

{\displaystyle \beta } and {\displaystyle \gamma } being the other angles of the triangle. Now since the halves of the triangle’s angles are positive and less than {\displaystyle \pi /2} and the tangent function is convex on this interval, Jensen’s inequality gives:

{\displaystyle \tan {\frac {\alpha }{2}}+\tan {\frac {\beta }{2}}+\tan {\frac {\gamma }{2}}\geq 3\tan {\frac {\alpha +\beta +\gamma }{6}}=3\tan {\frac {\pi }{6}}={\sqrt {3}}}

Using this we get:

{\displaystyle a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}\geq (a-b)^{2}+(b-c)^{2}+(c-a)^{2}+4{\sqrt {3}}\,\Delta }

This is the Hadwiger-Finsler inequality.
