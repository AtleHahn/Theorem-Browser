Statement

Every orientation-preserving Euclidean isometry of \mathbb R^3 can be expressed as a rotation about a line followed by a translation parallel to that line. Either part may be the identity; the two motions commute.

A bright US college student should be able to understand the statement after about 30 minutes of focused background research; this rating concerns understanding the claim, not proving it.

Proof 1: Absorb the perpendicular translation by shifting the axis — outline

Consider a rigid body undergoing first a rotation about an axis and then a translation of displacement D in an arbitrary direction. Any direct rigid motion can be accomplished in this way due to Euler's theorem on the existence of an axis of rotation. The displacement D can be decomposed into components parallel and perpendicular to the axis.

Local planar calculation

Euclidean geometry is expressed in the complex plane by points p=x+yi, where i^2=-1. Rotations result from multiplication by \omega=\cos t+i\sin t. A rotation about the complex point p is obtained by

z\mapsto\omega(z-p)+p=\omega z+p(1-\omega),

where the last expression shows the mapping equivalent to rotation at 0 and a translation. Therefore, given a direct isometry z\mapsto\omega z+a, one can solve p(1-\omega)=a to obtain p=a/(1-\omega) as the center for an equivalent rotation, provided that \omega\ne1, that is, provided the direct isometry is not a pure translation.

Shift the axis

The perpendicular (and parallel) component acts on all points of the rigid body, but for some points the previous rotation acted exactly with an opposite displacement, so those points are translated parallel to the axis of rotation. For a nonidentity rotation, the planar calculation identifies these points: their perpendicular coordinate is p=a/(1-\omega), where a represents the perpendicular component of D. These points lie on the Mozzi axis, parallel to the original axis, through which the rigid motion can be accomplished through a screw motion.

For an identity rotation the displacement is already a pure translation, along any line parallel to it; for the identity displacement any axis may be used. Rotation about the screw axis and translation parallel to it commute.
