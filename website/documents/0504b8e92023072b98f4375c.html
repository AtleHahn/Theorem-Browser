Statement

There exists a complex square matrix with a Gershgorin row disk containing no eigenvalue of the matrix. For A=(a_{ij}), row i has disk \{z:|z-a_{ii}|\le\sum_{j\ne i}|a_{ij}|\}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Complex matrices; Eigenvalues and eigenvectors; Absolute values; Gershgorin disks. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Use a real off-diagonal two-by-two matrix — outline

The source supplies A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\4&0\end{pmatrix}. Its first row disk is centered at 0 with radius 1. The eigenvalues are 2,-2, so neither belongs to that disk; these are routine quadratic checks of the supplied witness.
