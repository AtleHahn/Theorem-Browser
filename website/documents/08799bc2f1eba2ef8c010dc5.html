Statement

Let f\in L^1(\mathbb R^n) be real-valued and put f_r(x)=|B(x,r)|^{-1}\int_{B(x,r)}f(y)dy. Then f_r(x)\to f(x) as r\downarrow0 for almost every x.

Proof 1: Continuous approximation and maximal control

For a locally integrable function u, define the centered maximal function

Mu(x)=\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{|B(x,r)|}\int_{B(x,r)}|u(y)|\,dy.

Use the weak maximal inequality

|\{Mu>t\}|\le\frac{5^n}{t}\|u\|_1\qquad(u\in L^1(\mathbb R^n),\ t>0).

In particular, Mf is finite almost everywhere, so the upper and lower limits of the averages below are finite there.

Let the real-valued f ∈ L1(Rn) and

{\displaystyle \Omega f(x)=\limsup _{r\to 0}f_{r}(x)-\liminf _{r\to 0}f_{r}(x)}

where

{\displaystyle f_{r}(x)={\frac {1}{|B(x,r)|}}\int _{B(x,r)}f(y)dy.}

By density of compactly supported continuous functions in L^1, we write f = h + g where h is continuous and has compact support and g ∈ L1(Rn) with norm that can be made arbitrary small. Then

{\displaystyle \Omega f\leq \Omega g+\Omega h=\Omega g}

by continuity. Now, Ωg ≤ 2Mg and so, by the weak maximal inequality, we have:

{\displaystyle \left|\{\Omega f>\varepsilon\}\right|\leq\left|\{\Omega g>\varepsilon \}\right|\leq {\frac {2\cdot5^n}{\varepsilon }}\|g\|_{1}}

Now, we can let {\displaystyle \|g\|_{1}\to 0} and conclude Ωf = 0 almost everywhere; that is, {\displaystyle \lim _{r\to 0}f_{r}(x)} exists for almost all x. It remains to show the limit actually equals f(x). But this is easy: it is known that {\displaystyle \|f_{r}-f\|_{1}\to 0} (approximation of the identity) and thus there is a subsequence {\displaystyle f_{r_{k}}\to f} almost everywhere. By the uniqueness of limit, fr → f almost everywhere then.
