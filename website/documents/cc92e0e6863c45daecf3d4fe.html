Statement

Let P,Q:O\to\mathbb R^3 be C^2 vector fields on an open set O\subseteq\mathbb R^3. The vector Laplacian acts componentwise, and (P\cdot\nabla)Q is the directional derivative of Q. Then P\cdot\nabla(\nabla\cdot Q)-Q\cdot\nabla(\nabla\cdot P)=\nabla\cdot[P(\nabla\cdot Q)-Q(\nabla\cdot P)].

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The relevant statement concepts are Partial derivatives; Gradient and divergence. Divergence, gradients and directional derivatives are undergraduate vector-calculus operations; the statement is an identity between explicitly given fields. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Cancel the product of the divergences

This result can be verified by expanding the divergence of a scalar times a vector on the right-hand side.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Expand divergence of a scalar times a vector twice. The two products (div P)(div Q) cancel, leaving the displayed difference of directional derivatives of divergences. This uses ordinary component partial differentiation and the product rule from vector calculus, not any field theory.
