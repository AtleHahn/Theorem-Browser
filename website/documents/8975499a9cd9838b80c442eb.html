Statement

A topological space X is called Blumberg if every function X\to\mathbb R has a continuous restriction to some dense subset of X. Let X_0,X_1 be compact Hausdorff spaces such that the continuum hypothesis implies that X_0 is not Blumberg, and its negation implies that X_1 is not Blumberg. Then the disjoint topological union X_0\sqcup X_1 is a compact Hausdorff space that is not Blumberg.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Basic point-set topology supplies compact Hausdorff spaces, dense subsets, continuity and disjoint unions. The Blumberg property is defined locally. The continuum hypothesis adds the single cardinality assertion that no cardinal lies strictly between the integers and reals; understanding the two conditional hypotheses needs neither its independence nor constructions of the assumed spaces. These additions fit about 30 minutes from the stated topology baseline.

Proof 1: Disjoint union and the two cases of CH — outline

Take the disjoint union of the two compact Hausdorff spaces, one of which is non-Blumberg if the continuum hypothesis is true, the other if it is false.

This construction uses the facts that a finite disjoint union of compact Hausdorff spaces is compact Hausdorff, and that an open-and-closed subspace of a Blumberg space is Blumberg.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Basic point-set topology supplies the finite disjoint union and clopen components. Extend a function from a component to the union, then restrict its dense continuity set back to the open component to apply the hereditary Blumberg property. The full statement also requires understanding CH as the assertion of no intermediate cardinal between the integers and reals; this and the explicitly defined Blumberg property are small additions. Use CH or its negation by cases, without proving independence or constructing either conditional example.

Application in proof:

Either non-Blumberg component prevents the union from being Blumberg.

Undergraduate level

Level justification:

Extend a real function from the clopen component arbitrarily to the union and restrict a dense continuity set back to that open component.

Review notes:

The intersection of a dense set with an open component is dense in that component, and continuity restricts. The two conditional assumptions then give non-Blumbergness by excluded middle.
