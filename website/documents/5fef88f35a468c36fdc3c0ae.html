Statement

The first-order set S=\{P(f(c)),R(c),\neg P(f(c))\lor\neg R(c),\forall xQ(x)\} is unsatisfiable, yet a rule sequence that repeatedly instantiates only the final universal formula can remain open forever.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

First-order terms, universal instantiation and open tableau branches are a small connected formal-syntax extension; the assertion concerns one explicit rule sequence rather than completeness theory.

Proof 1: Expand only positive universal instances

Expanding the disjunction gives two branches, one contradicting P(f(c)), the other contradicting R(c); hence S is unsatisfiable. Instead, keep applying the universal rule to \forall xQ(x), using ground terms c,f(c),f(f(c)),\ldots. This introduces only positive Q instances, never an opposite literal, and leaves the disjunction unexpanded. It therefore gives an infinite open sequence despite the available closure.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Contrast the finite closing disjunction expansion with indefinitely generating positive ground instances of the universal formula. Introductory first-order syntax, quantifiers and basic algorithmic iteration suffice.
