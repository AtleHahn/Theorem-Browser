Statement

Let N\in\mathbb Z and let f:[N,\infty)\to\mathbb R be nonincreasing. For every integer M\geq N, \int_N^{M+1}f(x)\,dx\leq\sum_{n=N}^{M}f(n)\leq f(N)+\int_N^M f(x)\,dx.

Level justification

Understanding the statement involves monotone functions; definite Riemann integrals; finite summation. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. Proof 1: Finite sum and integral comparison — proof

A monotone real function is Riemann integrable on every compact subinterval. Since f is a monotone decreasing function, we know that

f(x)\leq f(n)\quad\text{for all }x\in[n,\infty)

and

f(n)\leq f(x)\quad\text{for all }x\in[N,n].

Hence, for every integer n\geq N,

\int_n^{n+1}f(x)\,dx\leq\int_n^{n+1}f(n)\,dx=f(n),

and, for every integer n\geq N+1,

f(n)=\int_{n-1}^nf(n)\,dx\leq\int_{n-1}^nf(x)\,dx.

By summation over all n from N to an integer M\geq N, we get from the first estimate

\int_N^{M+1}f(x)\,dx =\sum_{n=N}^M\underbrace{\int_n^{n+1}f(x)\,dx}_{\leq f(n)} \leq\sum_{n=N}^Mf(n)

and from the second

\begin{aligned} \sum_{n=N}^Mf(n) &=f(N)+\sum_{n=N+1}^Mf(n)\\ &\leq f(N)+\sum_{n=N+1}^M\underbrace{\int_{n-1}^nf(x)\,dx}_{\geq f(n)}\\ &=f(N)+\int_N^Mf(x)\,dx. \end{aligned}

Combining these two estimates yields

\int_N^{M+1}f(x)\,dx\leq\sum_{n=N}^Mf(n) \leq f(N)+\int_N^Mf(x)\,dx.
