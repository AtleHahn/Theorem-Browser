Statement

Let K be a field of characteristic not two and f,d:K\to K satisfy f(0)=d(0)=0, f(1)=1, and (d(y)+d(x))(y-x)=2(f(y)-f(x)) for every x,y\in K. Then d(x)=2x and f(x)=x^2 for all x\in K.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory field/group theory and linear algebra supply all objects. Commutator, graph automorphism and 2-rank are explicit group or finite-coordinate definitions; the displayed identities and subgroup assertions require no specialist structural theory.

Proof 1: Substitute zero and one and eliminate f

The assumed identity is

(d(y)+d(x))(y-x)=2(f(y)-f(x)).\tag{i}

With f(0)=d(0)=0 one gets

d(y)y=2f(y),\tag{ii}

and from f(1)=1 we get

d(1)=2.\tag{iii}

Equations (i) and (ii) yield

d(y)x=d(x)y,\tag{iv}

and with (iii), taking x=1, we get

d(y)=2y\qquad\text{for all }y\in K.\tag{v}

A consequence of (ii) and (v), since the characteristic is not two, is

f(y)=y^2,\qquad y\in K.

Level justification

Substitute zero and one, eliminate f and divide by two in a field of characteristic different from two. Introductory abstract algebra suffices for the arbitrary-field setting.
