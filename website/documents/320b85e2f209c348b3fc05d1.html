Statement

Let n\ge1 be an integer. For a Hermitian matrix X=\sum_j\lambda_jP_j, scalar functions act spectrally: h(X)=\sum_j h(\lambda_j)P_j. Here P_j are the orthogonal eigenprojections and \operatorname{Tr} is the matrix trace. Let A,B be positive-semidefinite Hermitian n\times n matrices and 0\le\alpha\le1. With the convention A^0=I, \operatorname{Tr}(BA^\alpha BBA^{1-\alpha}B)\le\operatorname{Tr}(B^2AB^2).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves positive-semidefinite Hermitian matrices; spectral real powers; matrix trace. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Substitute matrix powers and cycle the trace — outline

We use the exponent-one case of the generalized trace inequality: for any X\ge0, Y\ge0 and c\ge0,

\operatorname{Tr}(YXY^{2c}XY)\le \operatorname{Tr}(Y^{c+1}X^2Y^{c+1}).

The above inequality generalizes the stated one, as can be seen by replacing X by B^2 and Y by A^{(1-\alpha)/2} with \alpha=2c/(2c+2) and using the cyclicity of the trace, leading to

\operatorname{Tr}(BA^\alpha BBA^{1-\alpha}B) \le \operatorname{Tr}(B^2AB^2).

This substitution covers 0\le\alpha<1. At \alpha=1, cyclicity of the trace gives equality directly.
