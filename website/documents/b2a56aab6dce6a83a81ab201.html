Statement

Let D\subseteq\mathbb C be a nonempty connected open set, and let f:D\to\mathbb C be holomorphic with f\not\equiv a for some a\in\mathbb C. Then f^{-1}(a) is discrete and countable.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Real analysis and calculus supply limits, derivatives, series and integration. Complex differentiability, primitives and logarithms are direct extensions, and circle integrals are parametrized ordinary integrals. The full claims use no Riemann surfaces, residues or abstract differential forms.

Proof 1: Identity and countability of discrete planar sets

The source applies the local identity lemma to f and the constant a: an accumulation point in D would force local equality, and the identity theorem then forces f\equiv a, contrary to the hypothesis. Thus the fiber is discrete; the source uses that a discrete subset of the plane is countable.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the supplied local and global identity statements to exclude an accumulation point, then count a discrete planar set. Undergraduate metric topology and complex differentiability suffice for these explicit theorem applications.
