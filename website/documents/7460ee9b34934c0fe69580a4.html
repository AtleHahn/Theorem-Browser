Statement

A Pythagorean triple means positive integers (a,b,c) with a^2+b^2=c^2; it is primitive if \gcd(a,b,c)=1. The positive squares (a-b)^2,c^2,(a+b)^2 form an increasing arithmetic progression with common difference 2ab.

Proof 1: Source argument

If (a,b,c) is a Pythagorean triple such that a^2+b^2=c^2, then

(a-b)^2,\quad c^2,\quad (a+b)^2

are three squares in arithmetic progression, since

(a-b)^2+(a+b)^2=2(a^2+b^2)=2c^2.

The common difference is c^2-(a-b)^2=2ab>0. Equal positive legs would force a rational value for \sqrt2, so the first square is positive.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Expand the sum and difference squares, use the Pythagorean identity and rule out equal legs by irrationality of square root two. School algebra and rational numbers suffice.
