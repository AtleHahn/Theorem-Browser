Statement

Let S be a semigroup, a set with an associative operation xy. A congruence \sim is an equivalence relation with x\sim y,u\sim v\Rightarrow xu\sim yv. Let I\subseteq S be a two-sided ideal, meaning SI\subseteq I and IS\subseteq I. Define x\rho y when x=y or both x,y\in I. Then \rho is a congruence and the classes form a semigroup under [x][y]=[xy].

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A semigroup is explicitly an associative operation, and ideal/congruence conditions are given. Equivalence classes and quotient multiplication directly generalize introductory group/ring quotients, with no additional theory.

Proof 1: Collapse the ideal to one class — outline

Use the relation x\rho y if x=y or both elements lie in I. It collapses the ideal and otherwise keeps distinct elements separate. The ideal conditions give compatibility with multiplication, and the congruence quotient construction yields the Rees factor semigroup.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Check multiplication compatibility using the two ideal inclusions and apply the congruence quotient rule. Undergraduate quotient algebra suffices with semigroup and ideal explicitly defined; no structural semigroup theory is needed.
