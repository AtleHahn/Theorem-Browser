Statement

Let (X,\Sigma,\mu) be a measure space, let 1<p,q<\infty satisfy 1/p+1/q=1, and let g\in L^p(\mu), h\in L^q(\mu). Then \int_X|gh|\,d\mu\le\left(\int_X|g|^p\,d\mu\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_X|h|^q\,d\mu\right)^{1/q}.

Proof 1: The source integral family and three-lines interpolation (outline)

Use the source family f(z)=\int_X |g|^{pz}|h|^{q(1-z)}\,d\mu,\qquad0\le\Re z\le1. On the set where either factor is zero, interpret the pointwise family as identically zero; this does not change f(1/p)=\int|gh|. On the remaining set put A=|g|^p, B=|h|^q and use real logarithms for the complex powers. If x=\Re z, the integrand has modulus A^xB^{1-x}\le xA+(1-x)B\le A+B. This integrable bound gives boundedness, continuity on the closed strip and holomorphy inside by the standard dominated holomorphic integration theorem. The boundary suprema are at most \int|h|^q on x=0 and \int|g|^p on x=1. Apply the three-lines theorem at x=1/p to obtain the displayed inequality.
