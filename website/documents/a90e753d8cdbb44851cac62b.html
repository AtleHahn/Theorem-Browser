Statement

Every finite triangle-free planar simple undirected graph G=(V,E) has a proper coloring with at most three colors: there is a map

c:V\longrightarrow\{1,2,3\}

with c(u)\ne c(v) for every edge \{u,v\}\in E.

A simple graph has no loops or parallel edges. It is triangle-free if no three distinct vertices are pairwise adjacent, and planar if it can be drawn in the plane with edges intersecting only at shared endpoints.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All graph notions are explicitly described using finite sets and drawings. Understanding the quantified coloring assertion requires elementary reasoning only.

The proof uses finite case analysis, induction, elementary plane geometry and rational arithmetic. Euler’s formula follows by deleting edges down to a tree, and the discharging step only redistributes fractions among vertices and faces.
