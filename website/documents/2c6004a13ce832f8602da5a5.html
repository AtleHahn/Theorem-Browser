Statement

Assume the axiom of choice. For any set X of pairwise disjoint nonempty sets, there is C\subseteq\bigcup X such that C\cap A has exactly one element for every A\in X.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Undergraduate sets, functions, orders and infinite sequences suffice. Choice, well-ordering or maximal-chain principles are explicit set-selection/order assertions where invoked, not a demand to learn their independence proofs. The displayed quantifiers and constructions can be understood through familiar subsets and functions.

Proof 1: Range of a choice function

By the axiom of choice, let f be a choice function on X, so f(A)\in A for every A\in X. Replace the choice function with its range, C=\operatorname{ran}(f).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Take the range of the supplied choice function. Disjointness ensures an element chosen from a different set cannot give a second element in the intersection. Introductory sets and functions suffice with the choice axiom as an explicit selection principle.
