Statement

A commutative Noetherian local ring (R,\mathfrak m) is regular if its Krull dimension equals the minimum number of generators of \mathfrak m. Krull dimension is the supremum of lengths of strict chains of prime ideals.

Every such regular local ring is an integral domain, and every nonzero nonunit is a product of irreducible elements, uniquely up to permutation of the factors and multiplication by units. An irreducible element is a nonzero nonunit that cannot be written as a product of two nonunits.

Level justification

Factorization is familiar, but understanding arbitrary local regularity through prime-chain dimension and maximal-ideal generators requires a substantial commutative-algebra extension.
