Statement

Let D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}, let U be a bounded Jordan domain, and let f:D\to U be a conformal homeomorphism. Fix \zeta\in\partial D and 0<r<1, and parametrize the open arc \gamma_r=D\cap\{z:|z-\zeta|=r\} from one of its two endpoints to the other. If the image curve f\circ\gamma_r has finite length L, then it has endpoint limits a,b\in\partial U and |a-b|\le L; the endpoint limits may coincide.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Calculus supplies curve length and limits, topology supplies Jordan boundaries, and conformal bijection means a holomorphic bijection. These are limited extensions sufficient to understand the endpoint and distance assertion without boundary-prime-end theory.

Proof 1: Finite length and inverse continuity — outline

For a curve g:(u,v)\to\mathbb C, its length is

\ell(g)=\sup_{u<t_1<\cdots<t_k<v}\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}|g(t_{i+1})-g(t_i)|.

Finiteness of \ell(f\circ\gamma_r) therefore gives limiting points a,b at the two ends, with |a-b|\le\ell(f\circ\gamma_r). These points must lie on \partial U: since f is a homeomorphism between D and U, a sequence converging to a point of U would have a preimage sequence converging in D, whereas the arc approaches its endpoints on \partial D.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Finite total variation of the explicit planar curve gives Cauchy limits at both ends, and inverse continuity excludes interior image limits. The conformal hypothesis adds only the complex-differentiable homeomorphism definition here; no analytic continuation or conformal mapping theorem is used. Real analysis of curve length and metric topology suffice.
