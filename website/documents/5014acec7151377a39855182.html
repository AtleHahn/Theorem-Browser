Statement

Let H be a real or complex Hilbert space, with inner product \langle x,y\rangle linear in x. Write [y,x]=\langle x,y\rangle, so brackets are linear in their second argument. The map J:H\to H^\times defined by J(y)(x)=[x,y] is a bijective linear isometry; here H^\times denotes the continuous conjugate-linear functionals with operator norm.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra supplies inner products, adjoints and scalar-valued linear maps; analysis supplies completeness and norm bounds. The statement explicitly characterizes the representing map or adjoint, so understanding the existence claim adds only the complete inner-product setting, without weak topology or spectral theory.

Proof 1: Compose the Riesz map with pointwise conjugation

Let \Phi(y)(x)=[y,x] be the antilinear Riesz isometry onto H^*, and let C(f)=\overline f be the antilinear isometry onto H^\times. Then J=C\circ\Phi, because \overline{[y,x]}=[x,y]. The composition of these two antilinear bijective isometries is a linear bijective isometry.
