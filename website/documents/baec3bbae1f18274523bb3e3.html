Statement

For real a<b, integer m\ge1, 1\le p\le\infty, u\in W_p^m(a,b) and 0\le k\le m, \inf_{v\in P_{m-1}}\|u^{(k)}-v^{(k)}\|_{L^p(a,b)}\le C(m,k)(b-a)^{m-k}\|u^{(m)}\|_{L^p(a,b)}, where P_{m-1} is the space of real polynomials of degree at most m-1.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement uses Sobolev spaces and weak derivatives, beyond undergraduate calculus and linear algebra.

Proof 1: Specialize the approximation theorem to one dimension outline

Let m\ge1 be an integer, 1\le p\le\infty, and P_{m-1} the real polynomials of total degree at most m-1. For a bounded domain \Omega\subset\mathbb R^n, use the Sobolev seminorm |u|_{W_p^k}=(\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\|D^\alpha u\|_p^p)^{1/p} for finite p, and \max_{|\alpha|=k}\|D^\alpha u\|_\infty for p=\infty; \alpha is a multiindex and |\alpha| its sum.

Use the Bramble–Hilbert approximation theorem: for a bounded Lipschitz domain \Omega of diameter d, and u\in W_p^m(\Omega), a polynomial v\in P_{m-1} satisfies |u-v|_{W_p^k}\le C(m,\Omega)d^{m-k}|u|_{W_p^m} for 0\le k\le m.

In one dimension and for a function {\displaystyle \textstyle u} that has {\displaystyle \textstyle m} derivatives on interval {\displaystyle \textstyle \left(a,b\right)}, the lemma reduces to

{\displaystyle \inf _{v\in P_{m-1}}{\bigl \Vert }u^{\left(k\right)}-v^{\left(k\right)}{\bigr \Vert }_{L^{p}\left(a,b\right)}\leq C\left(m,k\right)\left(b-a\right)^{m-k}{\bigl \Vert }u^{\left(m\right)}{\bigr \Vert }_{L^{p}\left(a,b\right)}{\text{ for each integer }}k\leq m{\text{ and extended real }}p\geq 1,}

where {\displaystyle \textstyle P_{m-1}} is the space of all polynomials of degree at most {\displaystyle \textstyle m-1} and {\displaystyle f^{(k)}} indicates the {\displaystyle k}th derivative of a function {\displaystyle f}.

Level justification

The argument specializes a Sobolev approximation theorem to one dimension, but the full claim still uses weak derivatives and Lp Sobolev norms for all exponents. These are graduate analysis prerequisites, not merely classical Taylor approximation.
