Statement

A Kripke model has worlds, a relation R of accessibility and truth values for propositional atoms at each world. The formula \Box A holds at w exactly when A holds at every v with wRv. On a reflexive frame, no world satisfies \Box(a\land\neg\Box a).

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A binary relation and ordinary propositional truth values suffice. The modal box is explicitly defined as universal truth over accessible points; each claim uses only this one added semantic rule, without a formal axiom system or proof coding.

Proof 1: Apply the reflexive rule and then pass to a successor

On reflexive frames the tableau rule

(T)\frac{A_1;\ldots;A_n;\Box B} {A_1;\ldots;A_n;\Box B;B}

copies \Box B from the precondition to the consequent, in spite of this formula having been "used" to generate B. This is correct, as the considered world is the same, so \Box B also holds there. This "copying" is necessary in some cases. It is for example necessary to prove the inconsistency of \Box(a\land\neg\Box a): the applicable rules are in order (T),(\land),(\theta),(K), from which one is blocked if \Box(a\land\neg\Box a) is not copied.

Here (\land) expands a conjunction, (\theta) discards unneeded formulae, and (K) passes from \Box A_1;\ldots;\Box A_n;\neg\Box B to A_1;\ldots;A_n;\neg B at an accessible world. Thus the retained boxed formula is used after discarding the current-world literal a; the successor contains a\land\neg\Box a and \neg a, and expanding the conjunction closes the branch.
