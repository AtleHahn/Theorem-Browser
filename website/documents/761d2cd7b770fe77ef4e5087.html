Statement

In a real or complex inner-product space, if \langle x,y\rangle=0, then \|x+y\|^2=\|x\|^2+\|y\|^2, where \|v\|^2=\langle v,v\rangle.

Proof 1: Orthogonality removes the mixed terms

In an inner product space, the norm is determined using the inner product:

\|x\|^2=\langle x,x\rangle.

Using the properties of the inner product,

\|x+y\|^2=\langle x+y,x+y\rangle =\langle x,x\rangle+\langle x,y\rangle+\langle y,x\rangle+\langle y,y\rangle.

If x is orthogonal to y, meaning \langle x,y\rangle=0, then conjugate symmetry also gives \langle y,x\rangle=0, and the equation for the norm of a sum becomes

\|x+y\|^2 =\langle x,x\rangle+\langle x,y\rangle+\langle y,x\rangle+\langle y,y\rangle =\|x\|^2+\|y\|^2,

which is Pythagoras' theorem.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Expand the inner product of a sum and use orthogonality and conjugate symmetry to cancel both mixed terms. Undergraduate inner-product linear algebra suffices.
