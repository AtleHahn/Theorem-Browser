Statement

If Y is a normed space and S\subseteq Y satisfies \sup_{s\in S}|\ell(s)|<\infty for every continuous linear functional \ell\in Y^*, then \sup_{s\in S}\|s\|<\infty.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A Hilbert/Banach setting adds completeness to normed linear algebra. Operator powers, orthogonal projection, continuous scalar functionals and unit-ball norm bounds are direct extensions. The statements use only these explicit identities/convergence conditions, without weak topology or spectrum.

Proof 1: Evaluations on the Banach dual

Write Y^\prime=Y^* for the continuous dual and identify s\in Y with the evaluation functional \ell\mapsto\ell(s) on Y^\prime. The elements of {\displaystyle S} define a pointwise bounded family of continuous linear forms on the Banach space {\displaystyle X:=Y',} which is the continuous dual space of {\displaystyle Y.} By the uniform boundedness principle, the norms of elements of {\displaystyle S,} as functionals on {\displaystyle X,} that is, norms in the second dual {\displaystyle Y'',} are bounded. But for every {\displaystyle s\in S,} the norm in the second dual coincides with the norm in {\displaystyle Y,} by a consequence of the Hahn–Banach theorem.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply uniform boundedness on the continuous dual and identify evaluation norms in the second dual using Hahn-Banach. Continuous duality, dual completeness and the canonical bidual embedding together require functional analysis.
