Statement

For any complex square matrix A=(a_{ij}) and eigenvalue \lambda, |\lambda|\leq\max_i\sum_j|a_{ij}|.

Proof 1: Source matrix argument

Write n for the size of A, and |x| for the vector norm inducing the matrix norm. Any matrix induced norm satisfies the inequality {\displaystyle \scriptstyle \|A\|\geq |\lambda |} for any eigenvalue {\displaystyle \scriptstyle \lambda } because, if {\displaystyle \scriptstyle x} is a corresponding eigenvector, {\displaystyle \scriptstyle \|A\|\geq |Ax|/|x|=|\lambda x|/|x|=|\lambda |}. The infinity norm of a matrix is the maximum of row sums: {\displaystyle \scriptstyle \left\|A\right\|_{\infty }=\max \limits _{1\leq i\leq n}\sum _{j=1}^{n}|a_{ij}|.} Hence the desired inequality is exactly {\displaystyle \scriptstyle \|A\|_{\infty }\geq |\lambda |} applied to the matrix A.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply the induced norm bound to an eigenvector and use the maximum absolute row-sum formula for the infinity norm. Finite-dimensional linear algebra and norms suffice.

Proof 2: Gershgorin disk bound

Gershgorin’s theorem places \lambda in a disk with center a_{ii} and radius \sum_{j\ne i}|a_{ij}|. The triangle inequality gives |\lambda|\leq\sum_j|a_{ij}|, hence the maximum-row bound.
