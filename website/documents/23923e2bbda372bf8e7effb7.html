Statement

All graphs are finite, simple and undirected; containing a graph means containing an isomorphic subgraph, not necessarily induced. Let r\geq2,t,q\geq1 be integers and \varepsilon>0, with q\geq t/\varepsilon. Suppose G has n vertices, minimum degree at least (1-1/r+\varepsilon)n, and a copy of K_r(q) with parts A_1,\ldots,A_r. Put A=\bigcup_i A_i, B=V(G)\setminus A, and X=\{v\in B:|N(v)\cap A_i|\geq t\text{ for every }i\}, where N(v) is the neighbor set. If e(A,B) counts cross-edges, then (1-1/r+\varepsilon)nqr-(qr)^2\leq e(A,B)\leq |X|qr+(|B|-|X|)(q(r-1)+t-1), and consequently \varepsilon qrn\leq(t-1)n+|X|(q-t+1)+(qr)^2. Here K_r(q) has r disjoint q-element parts and all cross-part edges.

Level
High school only
Level justification

All parts, neighbors and edge counts are defined for a finite graph. The conclusion consists of explicit integer-count inequalities.

Proof 1: Count cross-edges from the two sides — outline

Counting the number of edges between A and B it can be derived that

e(A,B)\leq |X|\cdot qr+(|B|-|X|)(q(r-1)+t-1).

The minimum-degree condition yields

e(A,B)\geq\left(1-\frac1r+\varepsilon\right)\cdot n\cdot qr-(qr)^2.

After manipulating these two inequalities, using that |B|\leq n, it can be concluded that

\varepsilon qrn\leq(t-1)n+|X|(q-t+1)+(qr)^2.

Level justification

Count cross-edges from vertices on each side, use the minimum degree and rearrange the finite inequalities. Only graph neighbor counts and algebra are needed.
