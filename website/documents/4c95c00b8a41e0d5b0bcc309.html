Statement

In the projective plane over a commutative field, let A,B,C be three distinct lines through G and a,b,c three distinct lines through a distinct point H. Assume all six lines distinct and different from GH, and the following joins well-defined. Then the lines X=(A\cap b)(a\cap B), Y=(A\cap c)(a\cap C) and Z=(B\cap c)(b\cap C) are concurrent; juxtaposition of two points denotes their joining line.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Points and lines in the field projective plane can be understood as one- and two-dimensional subspaces of K cubed, with incidence by containment. The joining-line notation is supplied; this is a small extension of undergraduate field linear algebra, not algebraic geometry of curves.

Proof 1: Exchange points and lines under projective duality

Use Pappus’s point theorem in the dual projective plane. The original lines A,B,C become three collinear dual points, and a,b,c become another three collinear dual points. The three opposite-side intersections in that dual theorem correspond to the original lines X,Y,Z. Collinearity of those dual points means concurrency of X,Y,Z. This is the source’s projective-duality deduction.
