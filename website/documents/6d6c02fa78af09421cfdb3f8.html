Statement

Consider a closed Gordon–Newell network in equilibrium with N\ge1 customers and m queues. Each queue has one first-come-first-served server, with independent exponential service times of positive rate \mu_i. After service at node i, a customer moves to node j with probability p_{ij}, where P=(p_{ij}) is an irreducible stochastic matrix. No customer enters or leaves the network; routing choices and service times are independent. All queues have unlimited capacity, and queue lengths count waiting and in-service customers. Fix a destination node i. At a stationary arrival to i, remove the arriving customer from all queue counts while it is in transit, before it joins i. The distribution of the resulting vector \mathbf k=(k_1,\ldots,k_m), whose entries sum to N-1, equals the equilibrium distribution \pi_{N-1} for the same network with one fewer customer:

\Pr\{\text{arriving customer sees }\mathbf k\}=\pi_{N-1}(\mathbf k).

The comparison network keeps the same service rates and routing matrix. For N=1 its state is the all-zero vector. Irreducible means that every node can be reached from every other by a route of positive probability.
