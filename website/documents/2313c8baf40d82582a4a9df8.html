Statement

Let H be a finite-dimensional real inner-product space, C a linear subspace and x\in H. If m\in C minimizes \|x-c\| over c\in C, then m-x is orthogonal to C.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Finite-dimensional real inner-product spaces; Distance minimizers; Orthogonality. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Differentiate squared distance along the subspace

Consider a finite dimensional real Hilbert space {\displaystyle H} with a subspace {\displaystyle C} and a point {\displaystyle x.} If {\displaystyle m\in C} is a minimizer or minimum point of the function {\displaystyle N:C\to \mathbb {R} } defined by {\displaystyle N(c):=\|c-x\|} (which is the same as the minimum point of {\displaystyle c\mapsto \|c-x\|^{2}}), then the derivative of the squared-distance function must be zero at {\displaystyle m.}

In matrix derivative notation:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\partial \lVert x-c\rVert ^{2}&=\partial \langle c-x,c-x\rangle \\&=2\langle c-x,\partial c\rangle \end{aligned}}}

Since {\displaystyle \partial c} is a vector in {\displaystyle C} that represents an arbitrary tangent direction, it follows that {\displaystyle m-x} must be orthogonal to every vector in {\displaystyle C.}

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Differentiate squared distance along each subspace direction and set the derivative to zero. Inner products and directional calculus are standard undergraduate material.
