Statement

For f:\mathbb Z\to\mathbb C with f(0)=1, positive definiteness is equivalent to the existence of a unique Borel probability measure \mu on \mathbb T=\mathbb R/\mathbb Z such that

f(k)=\int_{\mathbb T}e^{-2\pi ikx}\,d\mu(x).

A function f on \mathbb Z is positive definite when

\sum_{p,q=1}^N f(k_p-k_q)c_p\overline{c_q}\geq0

for all finite choices k_p\in\mathbb Z and c_p\in\mathbb C.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Even with the finite quadratic-form definition of positive definiteness explicit, the representation concerns general probability measures and measure-theoretic Fourier integrals.

Proof 1: Specialize the dual-group representation

Use Bochner's theorem for locally compact abelian groups: normalized continuous positive-definite functions are exactly the Fourier transforms of unique regular Borel probability measures on the dual group. Apply this theorem to the discrete group \mathbb Z. Every function on it is continuous, and its dual is \mathbb T, with characters k\mapsto e^{-2\pi ikx}. Substitution gives the displayed representation and its uniqueness.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply Bochner to the discrete integer group and identify its character group with the circle. Regular Borel measures, Fourier transforms on locally compact groups and dual groups require graduate harmonic analysis.
