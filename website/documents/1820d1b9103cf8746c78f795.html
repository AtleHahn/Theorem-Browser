Statement

Let p be a real polynomial of degree d and a<b. Define q(t)=p(a+(b-a)t) and m=(a+b)/2. Roots of p in (a,m) correspond, with multiplicity, to roots in (0,1) of q_0(t)=2^dq(t/2); roots in (m,b) correspond to roots there of q_1(t)=2^dq((t+1)/2). A root at m corresponds to q(1/2)=0.

Proof 1: Two half-interval substitutions

The affine map t\mapsto a+(b-a)t sends (0,1) bijectively to (a,b). The substitution t/2 maps (0,1) to its left half, while (t+1)/2 maps it to its right half. Composing gives the stated subintervals; multiplication by 2^d does not change any roots or their multiplicities. The midpoint is the image of 1/2 and is checked separately.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Track the two affine half-interval substitutions and observe that nonzero scalar multiplication and invertible affine changes preserve root multiplicity. School polynomial factorization and intervals suffice.
