Statement

Let n\geq1, and let \xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n be independent real-valued random variables. Let \alpha_j,\beta_j\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\} for j=1,\ldots,n. If the two random variables

L_1=\sum_{j=1}^n\alpha_j\xi_j,\qquad L_2=\sum_{j=1}^n\beta_j\xi_j

are independent, then every \xi_j has a Gaussian distribution. Gaussian laws here include the degenerate case of an almost surely constant variable; equivalently, each law is that of \mu_j+\sigma_j Z for some \mu_j\in\mathbb R, \sigma_j\geq0, where Z has the standard normal distribution.

Level justification

The complete statement needs elementary undergraduate probability and the normal family, with degeneracy explicitly explained; no characteristic-function or group-generalization prerequisites are needed.
