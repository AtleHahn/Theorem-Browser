Let \Gamma be a rectifiable Jordan curve in \mathbb C and R its interior. If f:\overline R\to\mathbb C is continuous and holomorphic on R, then \int_\Gamma f(z)\,dz=0.

Proof 1: Split into two real integrals and use Cauchy–Riemann

We regard the complex plane as {\displaystyle \mathbb {R} ^{2}}. Now, define {\displaystyle u,v:{\overline {R}}\to \mathbb {R} } to be such that {\displaystyle f(x+iy)=u(x,y)+iv(x,y).} These functions are clearly continuous. It is well known that {\displaystyle u} and {\displaystyle v} are Fréchet-differentiable and that they satisfy the Cauchy-Riemann equations: {\displaystyle D_{1}v+D_{2}u=D_{1}u-D_{2}v={\text{zero function}}}.

Now, analyzing the sums used to define the complex contour integral in question, it is easy to realize that

{\displaystyle \int _{\Gamma }f=\int _{\Gamma }u\,dx-v\,dy\quad +i\int _{\Gamma }v\,dx+u\,dy,}

the integrals on the RHS being usual line integrals. Here D_1=\partial/\partial x and D_2=\partial/\partial y. We use Green’s theorem in its form for functions continuous on \overline R, Fréchet-differentiable in R, with Riemann-integrable scalar curl D_1B-D_2A. These remarks allow us to apply this theorem to each one of these line integrals, finishing the proof.

Level justification

The proof needs rectifiable Jordan boundaries, boundary-continuous holomorphic functions and a generalized Green formula requiring only interior Frechet differentiability and integrable curl. This is a substantial extension beyond the standard C1 Green theorem of multivariable calculus.
