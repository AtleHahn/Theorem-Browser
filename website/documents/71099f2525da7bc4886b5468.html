Statement

Let G be a finite group and let \rho be its regular representation on \mathbb C[G]. Let V_1,\ldots,V_r represent the isomorphism classes of irreducible complex representations, with representations \rho_i and dimensions d_i. For independent polynomial variables (x_g)_{g\in G}, put T=\sum_{g\in G}x_g\rho(g) and T_i=\sum_{g\in G}x_g\rho_i(g). Then

\det T=\prod_{i=1}^r(\det T_i)^{d_i}.

The number r of indexed factors equals the number of conjugacy classes of G.

Proof 1: Regular-representation decomposition and block determinants

Let V=\mathbb C[G], and use the usual multiplicity \dim V_i of each irreducible representation in the regular representation.

By Maschke's theorem, {\displaystyle \mathbb {C} [G]} is a semisimple algebra, so it is possible to break down {\displaystyle V} into a direct sum of irreducible representations,

{\displaystyle V=\bigoplus _{i=1}^{r}V_{i}^{\oplus \dim V_{i}}}

where each {\displaystyle V_{i}} is an irreducible representation of G. This lets us write:

{\displaystyle \det T=\prod _{i=1}^{r}\left(\det(T|_{V_{i}})\right)^{\dim V_{i}},}

where each {\displaystyle \det(T|_{V_{i}})} is a polynomial factor of {\displaystyle \det T}.

A result from character theory states that the number of nonisomorphic irreps of regular representation {\displaystyle V} equals the number of conjugacy classes of {\displaystyle G}. This explains why the number of indexed polynomial factors is equal to the number of conjugacy classes.

Here T|_{V_i}=T_i and \dim V_i=d_i.
