Statement

Let G\subseteq\mathbb R^n be a nonempty connected open set, n\ge1, and let f:G\to\mathbb R be differentiable with every partial derivative zero. Then f is constant.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Differentiability, partial derivatives, connectedness and open subsets of Euclidean space are standard undergraduate calculus and topology. No additional concepts are required.

Proof 1: Use the relatively open and closed zero set

For a line segment [x,y]\subset G, the gradient mean-value estimate gives |f(y)-f(x)|\leq|\nabla f((1-c)x+cy)|\,|y-x| for some 0<c<1.

As an application of the gradient mean-value estimate, we prove that {\displaystyle f} is constant if the open subset {\displaystyle G} is connected and every partial derivative of {\displaystyle f} is 0. Pick some point {\displaystyle x_{0}\in G}, and let {\displaystyle g(x)=f(x)-f(x_{0})}. We want to show {\displaystyle g(x)=0} for every {\displaystyle x\in G}. For that, let {\displaystyle E=\{x\in G:g(x)=0\}}. Then {\displaystyle E} is closed in {\displaystyle G} and nonempty. It is open too: for every {\displaystyle x\in E} ,

{\displaystyle {\Big |}g(y){\Big |}={\Big |}g(y)-g(x){\Big |}\leq (0){\Big |}y-x{\Big |}=0}

for every {\displaystyle y} in open ball centered at {\displaystyle x} and contained in {\displaystyle G}. Since {\displaystyle G} is connected, we conclude {\displaystyle E=G}.

Level justification

Use the segment mean-value bound to make the zero set locally open, then connectedness and continuity make it all of G. Multivariable calculus and basic topology suffice.
