Statement

Let D be a nonzero finite-dimensional associative unital division algebra over \mathbb R, with real scalars central, and put n=\dim_{\mathbb R}D. Write L_a for left multiplication by a\in D and \operatorname{tr}(a)=\operatorname{tr}(L_a). Suppose a,b,c\in D satisfy a^2=b^2=c^2=-1 and pairwise anticommute. Then c=ab or c=-ab.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

A finite-dimensional division algebra is a real vector space with associative bilinear multiplication and inverses for nonzero elements. Central scalars and anticommutation are explicit multiplication conditions. These direct algebraic definitions suffice without classifying division algebras.

Proof 1: Multiply three anticommuting units (outline)

Put u=abc. Rearranging factors using anticommutation and the three squares gives u^2=1. The division property applied to (u-1)(u+1)=0 yields u=\pm1. Since (ab)^2=-1, this gives c=\mp ab.
