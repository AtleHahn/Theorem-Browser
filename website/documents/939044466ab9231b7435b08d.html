Statement

Let P>0 and u,v\in L^1(\mathbb R) be complex-valued. Define their almost-everywhere periodizations u_P(x)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z}u(x-mP) and v_P(x)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z}v(x-mP). These define integrable functions on one period. For almost every x, the following integrals exist absolutely and are equal: \int_{\mathbb R}u_P(x-t)v(t)\,dt=\int_0^P u_P(x-t)v_P(t)\,dt.

Proof 1: Partitioning into periods — outline

Partition the real line into periods and substitute \tau\mapsto\tau+kP:

\begin{aligned} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}u_P(x-\tau)v(\tau)\,d\tau &=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} \left[\int_{kP}^{(k+1)P}u_P(x-\tau)v(\tau)\,d\tau\right]\\ &=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} \left[\int_0^P\underbrace{u_P(x-\tau-kP)} _{u_P(x-\tau),\ \text{by periodicity}}v(\tau+kP)\,d\tau\right]\\ &=\int_0^P u_P(x-\tau) \underbrace{\left[\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}v(\tau+kP)\right]} _{v_P(\tau)}\,d\tau. \end{aligned}

The L^1 assumptions justify these interchanges for almost every x by Tonelli's and Fubini's theorems.
