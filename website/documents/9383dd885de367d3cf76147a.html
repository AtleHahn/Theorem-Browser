Statement

For every Gaussian prime \pi not associated to 1+i, its norm N\pi=\pi\overline\pi is congruent to one modulo four.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory algebra supplies fields, polynomial roots and Gaussian integers. A Gaussian prime is an irreducible in Z[i], and associates differ by a unit. No field norms beyond the explicitly given conjugate product or extension arithmetic are needed.

Proof 1: Compare the inert and split norm cases

The prime classification says such a prime is either associated to an ordinary prime q\equiv3\pmod4, with norm q^2\equiv1\pmod4, or lies over a split ordinary prime p\equiv1\pmod4, with norm p. Units do not change the norm, so both cases give the assertion.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Use the explicit Gaussian-prime classification and compute norms in its two nonramified cases. Undergraduate ring algebra suffices with Gaussian integers and their sum-of-squares norm as a small extension; no general number-field splitting theory is required.
