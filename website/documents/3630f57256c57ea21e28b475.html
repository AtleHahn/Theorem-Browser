Statement

Let \Omega be a set and let \mathcal A be a family of subsets containing \Omega and closed under finite intersections. Let \mathcal H be a collection of real functions on \Omega such that:

  1. \mathbf1_A\in\mathcal H for each A\in\mathcal A.
  2. \mathcal H is closed under addition and multiplication by real constants.
  3. If nonnegative f_n\in\mathcal H increase pointwise to a bounded function f, then f\in\mathcal H.

Then \mathcal H contains every bounded real function measurable with respect to the sigma algebra \sigma(\mathcal A) generated by \mathcal A.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Sets, inverse images and countable operations are familiar from analysis and topology. A sigma-algebra is a family closed under complements and countable unions, and measurability is the corresponding inverse-image condition. Here no general integration, null-set quotient or stochastic process is required; the limited set-family definitions suffice.

Proof 1: Indicator lambda system and simple approximation — outline

The assumption \Omega\in\mathcal A, closure of \mathcal H under addition and multiplication by real constants, and its closure under nonnegative increasing sequences with bounded limit imply that

\mathcal G=\{A\subseteq\Omega:\mathbf1_A\in\mathcal H\}

is a \lambda-system (it contains \Omega and is closed under complements and countable disjoint unions). Since \mathbf1_A\in\mathcal H for A\in\mathcal A, the \pi–\lambda theorem implies \sigma(\mathcal A)\subseteq\mathcal G.

Linearity implies that \mathcal H contains all \sigma(\mathcal A)-measurable simple functions, and then the monotone-limit assumption implies that \mathcal H contains all bounded functions measurable with respect to \sigma(\mathcal A). Here one uses increasing nonnegative simple approximations for nonnegative bounded functions; a bounded signed function is shifted by a constant and then shifted back.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Build a lambda-system of indicator sets, apply pi–lambda generation and approximate bounded measurable functions by simple functions. The combined generated sigma-algebra, lambda-system and measurable approximation machinery exceeds a confidently small extension.
