Statement

Let Ax^4+Bx^3+Cx^2+Dx+E have real coefficients, A\ne0, and a nonreal root z. Its other conjugate root is \bar z. Put w=B/A+2\operatorname{Re}z and v=E/(A|z|^2). The remaining two roots are (-w\pm\sqrt{w^2-4v})/2.

High school only

Proof 1: Compare coefficients after removing the conjugate pair

Let x_1=z denote the given nonreal solution. Since all the original coefficients A,B,C,D,E are real, there is another solution x_2 which is the complex conjugate of x_1. If the other two roots are denoted by x_3 and x_4, the quartic equation can be expressed as

(x-x_1)(x-x_2)(x-x_3)(x-x_4)=0.

Its left side is the product of the two quadratic factors (x-x_1)(x-x_2) and (x-x_3)(x-x_4). Since x_2=\overline{x_1},

\begin{aligned} (x-x_1)(x-x_2) &=x^2-(x_1+\overline{x_1})x+x_1\overline{x_1}\\ &=x^2-2\operatorname{Re}(x_1)x+[\operatorname{Re}(x_1)]^2+[\operatorname{Im}(x_1)]^2. \end{aligned}

Let

a=-2\operatorname{Re}(x_1),\qquad b=[\operatorname{Re}(x_1)]^2+[\operatorname{Im}(x_1)]^2,

so that the first factor is x^2+ax+b. Also let there be unknown variables w,v such that the second factor is x^2+wx+v. Multiplying the two factors produces

x^4+(a+w)x^3+(b+wa+v)x^2+(wb+va)x+vb.

Comparing this with the original quartic divided by A, it can be seen that

a+w=\frac BA,\qquad b+wa+v=\frac CA,\qquad wb+va=\frac DA,\qquad vb=\frac EA.

Therefore

w=\frac BA-a=\frac BA+2\operatorname{Re}(x_1),v=\frac E{Ab} =\frac E{A\bigl([\operatorname{Re}(x_1)]^2+[\operatorname{Im}(x_1)]^2\bigr)}.

The denominator is nonzero because x_1 is nonreal. The equation x^2+wx+v=0 can be solved for x, yielding

x_3=\frac{-w+\sqrt{w^2-4v}}2,\qquad x_4=\frac{-w-\sqrt{w^2-4v}}2.
