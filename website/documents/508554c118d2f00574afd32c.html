Statement

Assume Zorn’s lemma. Let (P,\lesssim) be a preordered set (the relation is reflexive and transitive) such that every chain, including the empty chain, has an upper bound. Then some x\in P satisfies x\lesssim y\Rightarrow y\lesssim x for every y\in P.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Sets and partial orders supply chains, upper bounds and maximality. Well-order means every nonempty subset has a least member, and choice/Zorn is a stated principle. The explicit equivalences and fixed-point conditions require no ordinal arithmetic or forcing theory.

Proof 1: Quotient by mutual comparison — outline

Stated dependency

Zorn’s lemma: a partially ordered set in which every chain (including the empty chain) has an upper bound has a maximal element. A chain is a totally ordered subset; an element m is maximal if m\leq y implies m=y.

Argument

This version trivially follows from the usual Zorn's lemma. Indeed, consider the quotient

{\displaystyle Q=P/\sim }

where {\displaystyle x\sim y} means {\displaystyle x\lesssim y} and {\displaystyle y\lesssim x}. Then {\displaystyle Q} is a partially ordered set satisfying the hypothesis of Zorn's lemma and thus has a maximal element. {\displaystyle \square }

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Quotient mutual comparison to form a poset and apply the stated Zorn principle. Equivalence classes and elementary order theory suffice.
