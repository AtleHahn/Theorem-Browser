Statement

Let L>0 and let \alpha:[0,L]\to\mathbb R^3 be a smooth closed unit-speed curve, so \alpha(0)=\alpha(L) and |\alpha'(t)|=1. Put \gamma(t)=\alpha'(t)\in S^2. There is no unit vector v such that \gamma(t)\cdot v>0 for every t\in[0,L].

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves Smooth curves in three-dimensional Euclidean space; Derivatives and definite integrals; Dot products; Unit sphere and hemispheres. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Integrate the strictly positive tangent projection

The curve {\displaystyle \gamma } does not lie in an open hemisphere. If so, then there is {\displaystyle v\in \mathbb {S} ^{2}} such that {\displaystyle \gamma \cdot v>0}, so {\displaystyle \textstyle 0=(\alpha (L)-\alpha (0))\cdot v=\int _{0}^{L}\gamma (t)\cdot v\,\mathrm {d} t>0}, a contradiction.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The integral of the tangent projection is zero by closure and the fundamental theorem, contradicting positivity if all projected tangents are positive. This is ordinary calculus of vector-valued curves.
