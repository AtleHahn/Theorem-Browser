Spherical parallelograms between the same parallels

Statement

On the unit sphere, let two nondegenerate spherical parallelograms share the base AB and the same midpoint great circle through the midpoints of their lateral sides. A spherical parallelogram has congruent opposite sides and opposite angles; sides are minor great-circle arcs. Equivalently here the other two vertices lie on the same small circle parallel to the antipodal small circle through A,B. The two parallelograms have equal area.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The parallelograms, great/small circles and antipodal construction are specified as concrete sphere geometry, with ordinary area.

Proof 1: Congruent curvilinear triangles and lenses — outline

Let {\displaystyle \square ABC_{1}D_{1}} and {\displaystyle \square ABC_{2}D_{2}} be spherical parallelograms with the great circle {\displaystyle m} (the "midpoint circle") passing through the midpoints of sides {\displaystyle BC_{1}} and {\displaystyle AD_{1}} coinciding with the corresponding midpoint circle in {\displaystyle \square ABC_{2}D_{2}.} In the crossing configuration, let {\displaystyle F} be the intersection point between sides {\displaystyle AD_{2}} and {\displaystyle BC_{1}.} Because the midpoint circle {\displaystyle m} is shared, the vertices of the two top sides {\displaystyle C_{1}D_{1}} and {\displaystyle C_{2}D_{2}} lie on the same small circle {\displaystyle l} parallel to {\displaystyle m} and antipodal to a small circle {\displaystyle l^{*~\!\!}} passing through {\displaystyle A} and {\displaystyle B.}

Two arcs of {\displaystyle l} are congruent, {\displaystyle D_{1}D_{2}\cong C_{1}C_{2},} thus the two curvilinear triangles {\displaystyle \triangle BC_{1}C_{2}} and {\displaystyle \triangle AD_{1}D_{2},} each bounded by {\displaystyle l} on the top side, are congruent. Each parallelogram is formed from one of these curvilinear triangles added to the triangle {\displaystyle \triangle ABF} and to one of the congruent lens-shaped regions between each top side and {\displaystyle l,} with the curvilinear triangle {\displaystyle \triangle D_{2}C_{1}F} cut away. Therefore the parallelograms have the same area. (As in Elements, the case where the parallelograms do not intersect on the sides is omitted, but can be proven by a similar argument.)
