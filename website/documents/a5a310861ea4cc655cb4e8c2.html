Statement

For a prime p\equiv3\pmod4, the form Q(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)=x_1^2+x_2^2-px_3^2-px_4^2 takes both positive and negative real values, but its only rational zero is (0,0,0,0).

Level
High school only
Level justification

Rational coefficients, nonzero integer tuples and homogeneous quadratic polynomials are elementary algebra.

Proof 1: Descend after dividing all four coordinates by the prime — outline

The coefficients 1 and -p show that the form is indefinite. By clearing denominators, a nonzero rational zero would give a nonzero integral zero. That no such zero exists can be proved by the method of infinite descent using the fact that, if the sum of two perfect squares is divisible by a prime p\equiv3\pmod4, then each summand is divisible by p. The source specifies this descent method without writing out its steps.

Level justification

Clear rational denominators and repeatedly divide an integral zero by p using the supplied two-square divisibility fact. Minimal nonzero size gives the descent contradiction, while coordinate choices show both signs. This is integer arithmetic and elementary algebra.
