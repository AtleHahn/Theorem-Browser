Statement

Let (E,\mathcal A,\mu) be a finite measure space and let (f_n)_{n\ge1} be real integrable measurable functions with \sup_n\int_E|f_n|\,d\mu<\infty. There are an integrable real function f and strictly increasing indices n_1<n_2<\cdots such that, for every strictly increasing sequence k_1<k_2<\cdots,

\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m f_{n_{k_j}}\longrightarrow f\quad\mu\text{-almost everywhere as }m\to\infty.

The limit f is the same for all these subsequences; the exceptional null set may depend on the subsequence. In particular, on a probability space this applies to any sequence of real random variables with uniformly bounded expected absolute values.
