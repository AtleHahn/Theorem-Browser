Statement

For a field K, the subring K[t^2,t^3]\subset K[t], its localization at (t^2,t^3), and the completion of that local ring are Cohen–Macaulay.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the full hypotheses involves Semigroup rings over a field; Localization and completion; Cohen–Macaulay rings. These require substantial specialist preparation beyond a brief introduction from undergraduate mathematics. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Apply the Gorenstein implication

The ring K[t^2,t^3], its indicated localization and completion are Gorenstein. Any Gorenstein ring is Cohen–Macaulay, so each of these rings is Cohen–Macaulay.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The application requires localization and completion of local rings and the Gorenstein and Cohen-Macaulay properties. These are substantial commutative-algebra concepts despite the two-step deduction.
