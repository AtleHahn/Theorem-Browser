Statement

A Pythagorean triple means positive integers (a,b,c) with a^2+b^2=c^2; it is primitive if \gcd(a,b,c)=1. Put s=(a+b+c)/2. The inradius and exradii opposite a,b,c are respectively s-c,s-b,s-a,s, all positive integers.

Proof 1: Source argument

For a general triangle with sides a, b, c and area K, the inradius r and exradii ra, rb, rc, associated with sides a, b, c, satisfy K = rs = ra(s − a) = rb(s − b) = rc(s − c), where {\displaystyle s={\tfrac {1}{2}}(a+b+c)} is the semiperimeter. When the angle opposite c is right, so that {\displaystyle K={\tfrac {1}{2}}ab} and {\displaystyle a^{2}+b^{2}=c^{2}}, it is immediate that {\displaystyle K=s(s-c)=(s-a)(s-b),} which gives

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}r&=s-c={\tfrac {1}{2}}(a+b-c),&r_{a}&=s-b={\tfrac {1}{2}}(a+c-b),\\r_{b}&=s-a={\tfrac {1}{2}}(b+c-a),&r_{c}&=s={\tfrac {1}{2}}(a+b+c).\end{aligned}}}

If (a,b,c) is a Pythagorean triple, then s, and hence all four radii, are positive integers. Here integrality of s follows from the parity of primitive Pythagorean triples (one even leg and an odd hypotenuse), followed by integer scaling.
