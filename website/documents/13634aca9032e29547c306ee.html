Statement

Let f:X\to S and g:S^{\prime}\to S be morphisms of quasi-compact quasi-separated schemes, with g flat. Form the Cartesian square X^{\prime}=X\times_S S^{\prime} with projections f^{\prime},g^{\prime}. For a complex \mathcal F of quasi-coherent sheaves on X, the derived base-change morphism g^*Rf_*\mathcal F\to Rf^{\prime}_*g^{\prime*}\mathcal F is a quasi-isomorphism.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Derived pushforwards, quasi-coherent complexes and flat base change require scheme theory and derived categories. Under the statement-understanding criterion, a bright US college student would need more than approximately 30 minutes of focused background research to understand the full claim.

Proof 1: Flat pullback agrees with derived pullback

We use derived base change as an external theorem: for a homotopy Cartesian square of quasi-compact quasi-separated derived schemes, Lg^*Rf_*\mathcal F\to Rf^{\prime}_*Lg^{\prime*}\mathcal F is a quasi-isomorphism for complexes of quasi-coherent sheaves.

The flat base-change assertion in the statement is in fact a special case since for g flat the homotopy pullback (which is locally given by a derived tensor product) agrees with the ordinary pullback (locally given by the underived tensor product), and since the pullback along the flat maps g and g' are automatically derived (i.e., {\displaystyle Lg^{*}=g^{*}}). The auxiliary assumptions related to the Tor-independence or Tor-amplitude in other derived flat base-change formulations also become unnecessary.

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Specialize the derived theorem by identifying flat tensor and pullback functors with their derived versions. Schemes, quasi-coherent complexes, derived functors and homotopy Cartesian squares are advanced algebraic geometry.
