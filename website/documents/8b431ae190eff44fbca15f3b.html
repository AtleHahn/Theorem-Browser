Statement

Let (X,d_X),(Y,d_Y) be metric spaces. If uniformly continuous functions f_n:X\to Y converge uniformly to f, then f is uniformly continuous.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Basic topology and real analysis supply normality, compactness, products, bases, continuity and uniform convergence. The maps and quantified conditions are explicit, with choice assumed where needed; no algebraic or differential topology is involved.

Review notes

Proof 1: The three-term estimate with a uniform delta

Given \varepsilon>0, choose N with d_Y(f_N(t),f(t))<\varepsilon/3 for all t. Uniform continuity of f_N gives a single \delta>0 such that d_X(x,y)<\delta implies d_Y(f_N(x),f_N(y))<\varepsilon/3. The same three-term triangle estimate d_Y(f(x),f(y))\le d_Y(f(x),f_N(x))+d_Y(f_N(x),f_N(y))+d_Y(f_N(y),f(y))<\varepsilon now holds for every pair at distance less than \delta.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Choose one uniformly close approximant and its common delta, then apply the three-term triangle estimate for all point pairs. Standard undergraduate metric analysis suffices.
