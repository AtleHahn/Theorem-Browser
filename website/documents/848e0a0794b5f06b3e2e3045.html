Statement

Let S be a compact Riemannian surface without boundary, with Gaussian curvature K, area measure dA and Euler characteristic \chi(S). Then \int_S K\,dA=2\pi\chi(S); in particular the Cohn–Vossen inequality is an equality.

Proof 1: Apply compact Gauss–Bonnet

If S is a compact surface (without boundary), then the Cohn–Vossen inequality is an equality by the usual Gauss–Bonnet theorem for compact manifolds. Here Gauss–Bonnet gives \int_S K\,dA=2\pi\chi(S).

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply Gauss-Bonnet on a closed Riemannian surface. Intrinsic curvature, Riemannian area and Euler characteristic remain the required geometric background despite the one-line deduction.
