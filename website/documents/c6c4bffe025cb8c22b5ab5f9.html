Statement

For 0<p<1, a binomial law with trial count n has positive masses exactly on \{0,\ldots,n\}. Allowing the nonnegative integer trial count n to vary does not give an exponential family with parameter-independent support.

Proof 1: Apply the fixed-support requirement

The support of an exponential family must remain the same across all parameter settings in the family. This is why the binomial distribution with varying number of trials is not an exponential family: the parameter in question affects the support, particularly the maximum possible value.

For 0<p<1, that support is \{0,\ldots,n\}.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Under the explicitly specified fixed-support convention, different trial counts have different sets of positive masses. Support and a parametric family are basic undergraduate probability notions; no statistical inference or advanced exponential-family theory is needed.
