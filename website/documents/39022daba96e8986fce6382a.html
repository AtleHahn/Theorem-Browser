Statement

Every three-dimensional zonohedron (a convex polyhedron formed as the Minkowski sum of finitely many line segments, namely all sums of one point from each segment) has a parallelogram face.

Proof 1: Translate an ordinary crossing into a face

The dual Sylvester–Gallai theorem says that a nontrivial arrangement of finitely many real projective lines has a point belonging to exactly two lines. An arrangement is nontrivial when its lines do not all pass through one point.

Arrangements of lines have a combinatorial structure closely connected to zonohedra, polyhedra formed as the Minkowski sum of a finite set of line segments, called generators. In this connection, each pair of opposite faces of a zonohedron corresponds to a crossing point of an arrangement of lines in the projective plane, with one line for each distinct generator direction. The number of sides of each face is twice the number of lines that cross in the arrangement.

An ordinary point therefore corresponds to a pair of four-sided faces. Faces of a zonohedron are centrally symmetric, so these are parallelograms.
