Statement

In the circular fixed-lot model with N,S>0, constant transportation rate t\ge0, and radius b=(NS/\pi)^{1/2}, suppose differentiable lot rent R(x) and indirect utility V(R(x),Y-tx) satisfy spatial equilibrium dV/dx=0 and Roy’s identity S=-V_1/V_2, with the displayed quotients defined and fixed S. Put m=(2t/3)(S/\pi)^{1/2}. Then R(x)=R(b)+(t/S)(b-x) and the differential land rent is DLR=\int_0^b[R(x)-R(b)]2\pi x\,dx=(m/2)N^{3/2}. Here Y is fixed income and V_1,V_2 are partial derivatives with respect to rent and net income, respectively, evaluated at (R(x),Y-tx).

Proof 1: Roy’s identity, the rent gradient and area integration

The associated indirect utility function is

V(x)=V\bigl(R(x),Y-tx\bigr),

where R(x) is rent per unit area at distance x and Y-tx is net income. Spatial equilibrium occurs when variables adjust such that each resident can achieve the same level of utility anywhere.

Totally differentiating V(x) and setting V'(x)=0 gives

\begin{aligned} V'(x)&=V_1R'(x)-V_2t=0,\\ &\Rightarrow R'(x)=\frac{V_2}{V_1}t. \end{aligned}

By Roy's identity,

S=-\frac{V_1}{V_2},

where S=S\bigl(R(x),Y-tx\bigr) is the Marshallian demand function for lot size at distance x, which we assume to be fixed with respect to distance.

Hence, the rent gradient (the rate at which rent changes with distance) is simply R'(x)=-t/S, implying the equilibrium rent charged at distance x satisfies the linear equation

R(x)=R(b)+\frac tS(b-x).

This just says the reduction in transportation costs conferred by locating closer to the urban center is offset by the increase in rents, thus ensuring spatial indifference.

Integrating R(x)-R(b) over the area of the city gives differential land rents:

\begin{aligned} DLR&\equiv\int_0^b[R(x)-R(b)]2\pi x\,dx\\ &=\frac m2N^{3/2}, \end{aligned}

where b=(NS/\pi)^{1/2} and m=\tfrac{2t}{3}(S/\pi)^{1/2} as in the statement.
