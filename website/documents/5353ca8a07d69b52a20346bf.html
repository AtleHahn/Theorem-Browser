Statement

The notation \mathrm K_{j=1}^{\infty}a/b means the limit of the finite nested fractions a/(b+a/(b+\cdots+a/b)). Finite convergents are evaluated as rational functions after simplification; convergence here means that they are eventually finite and tend to a finite complex value. For z\in\mathbb C, the continued fraction x=\mathop{\mathrm K}_{j=1}^{\infty}1/z converges if and only if z^2\notin(-4,0]. Equivalently, z\ne0 and z is not purely imaginary with imaginary part strictly between -2 and 2.

Proof 1: Normalize denominators and substitute the inverse square — outline

By applying an equivalence transformation the condition that guarantees convergence of

x=\mathop{\mathrm K}_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac1z =\cfrac1{z+\cfrac1{z+\cfrac1{z+\ddots}}}

can be determined. A simple equivalence transformation shows that

x=\cfrac{z^{-1}}{1+\cfrac{z^{-2}}{1+\cfrac{z^{-2}}{1+\ddots}}}

whenever z\ne0.

The normalized period-one convergence theorem states that

y=1+\mathop{\mathrm K}_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac u1

converges for every complex number u except when u is a negative real number and u<-1/4. Its limit is the value of

\frac12\left(1\pm\sqrt{4u+1}\right)

that has the larger absolute value, or the double root when u=-1/4. Applying this theorem with u=z^{-2}, the result for y can be restated for x.

The infinite periodic continued fraction x=\mathop{\mathrm K}_{j=1}^{\infty}1/z converges if and only if z^2 is not a real number lying in the interval -4<z^2\leq0 — or, equivalently, x converges if and only if z\ne0 and z is not a pure imaginary number with imaginary part between -2 and 2 (not including either endpoint).

Level justification

Normalize the finite convergents by the nonzero complex denominator and apply the explicitly stated period-one convergence criterion with u=z^-2. Translate the forbidden interval by reciprocation; z=0 gives nonconvergent alternating finite/infinite convergents. Complex arithmetic and sequence limits suffice.
