Statement

Let X_1,X_2,\ldots be independent identically distributed symbols in a finite or countable alphabet with probability mass function p, and suppose H=-\mathbb E\log p(X_1)<\infty. Use a fixed logarithm base greater than one and let p_n(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=\prod_{i=1}^n p(x_i). Then -n^{-1}\log p_n(X_1,\ldots,X_n)\to H almost surely, and also in probability.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The complete countable-alphabet claim needs independence of an infinite sequence, expectation as a possibly infinite weighted sum, entropy, and the distinction between almost-sure and in-probability convergence. Real analysis supplies series and limits but does not itself supply probability on infinite sequences or these two modes of convergence. Proof 1: Apply the laws of large numbers to information variables

The joint distribution is just the product of marginals. Consequently -n^{-1}\log p_n(X_1,\ldots,X_n) is the average of the i.i.d. variables -\log p(X_i), with finite expectation H.

Given {\displaystyle X} is an i.i.d. source which may take values in the alphabet {\displaystyle {\mathcal {X}}}, its time series {\displaystyle X_{1},\ldots ,X_{n}} is i.i.d. with entropy {\displaystyle H}. The weak law of large numbers gives the asymptotic equipartition property with convergence in probability,

{\displaystyle \lim _{n\to \infty }\Pr \left[\left|-{\frac {1}{n}}\log p_n(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots ,X_{n})-H\right|>\varepsilon \right]=0\qquad \forall \varepsilon >0.}

since the entropy is equal to the expectation of

{\displaystyle -{\frac {1}{n}}\log p_n(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots ,X_{n}).}

The strong law of large numbers asserts the stronger almost sure convergence,

{\displaystyle \Pr \left[\lim _{n\to \infty }-{\frac {1}{n}}\log p_n(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots ,X_{n})=H\right]=1.}
