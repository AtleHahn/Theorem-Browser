Statement

Every closed oriented smooth Riemannian surface (M,g) and every ε>0 admit a C1 isometric embedding into the Euclidean ball B(0,ε)⊂R³.

Proof 1: Scale a smooth surface embedding and apply Nash–Kuiper

Use the Nash–Kuiper theorem: if (M,g) is an m-dimensional Riemannian manifold and f:M\to\mathbb R^n is a short smooth embedding, where n\ge m+1, then there is a sequence of continuously differentiable isometric embeddings converging uniformly to f. “Short” means that the pulled-back Euclidean metric is bounded above by g.

Any closed and oriented two-dimensional manifold can be smoothly embedded in \mathbb R^3. Any such embedding can be scaled by an arbitrarily small constant so as to become short, relative to any given Riemannian metric on the surface. It follows from the Nash–Kuiper theorem that there are continuously differentiable isometric embeddings of any such Riemannian surface where the radius of a circumscribed ball is arbitrarily small. A translation centers this ball at the origin.
