Statement

Let O be an ovoid in a three-dimensional projective space: a set meeting every line in at most two points and whose tangent lines at each point fill a tangent plane. Its cycles are its plane sections. Let A_1,A_2,A_3,A_4,B_1,B_2,B_3,B_4 be eight distinct points of O. If five of the six quadruples \{A_i,B_i,A_j,B_j\} for 1\le i<j\le4 lie on cycles, and these five quadruples lie on at least four distinct cycles, then the sixth quadruple also lies on a cycle.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Projective three-space is interpreted by subspace incidence, a limited extension of undergraduate linear algebra. The ovoid and its plane-section cycles are explicitly defined, including tangent-plane incidence.

Proof 1: Identify the common point of the secants outline

Relabel the indices so that the missing cycle is c_{13}, and write c_{ij} for the supplied cycle through A_i,B_i,A_j,B_j.

  1. The planes containing c_{23},c_{34},c_{24} intersect in a point P. Hence P is the intersection point of the spatial lines A_2B_2 and A_4B_4.
  2. The planes containing c_{12},c_{14},c_{24} intersect in a point P'. Hence P' is the intersection point of A_2B_2 and A_4B_4 too.

Thus P=P', and the lines A_1B_1 and A_3B_3 also intersect at P. Consequently A_1,B_1,A_3,B_3 lie in a plane section of the ovoid, and are concyclic.

Routine omissions

The source leaves these routine nondegeneracy checks implicit: equality of any two planes in either displayed triple would put the three secants in one plane, making all three cycle planes equal and leaving at most three distinct cycles in total. With the triple planes distinct, their pairwise intersections are the indicated distinct secants, so a common line would force two secants to coincide, contradicting eight distinct ovoid points and the at-most-two-points-on-a-line property. Relabelling the missing pair and identifying the pairwise plane intersections with the corresponding secants are implicit.
