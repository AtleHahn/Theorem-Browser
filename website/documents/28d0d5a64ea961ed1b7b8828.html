Statement

Let T>0 and let f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R be a measurable T-periodic function with \int_0^T|f(t)|^2\,dt<\infty. Then

{\displaystyle \left\vert \int _{0}^{T}f(t)\,\mathrm {d} t\right\vert ^{2}+2\sum _{k=1}^{\infty }\left\vert \int _{0}^{T}\cos(2\pi kt/T)f(t)\,\mathrm {d} t\right\vert ^{2}+2\sum _{k=1}^{\infty }\left\vert \int _{0}^{T}\sin(2\pi kt/T)f(t)\,\mathrm {d} t\right\vert ^{2}\leq T\int _{0}^{T}\vert f(t)\vert ^{2}\,\mathrm {d} t.}
Level

Proof 1: Specialize the complex Fourier bound — outline

Local complex Fourier bound

Let T>0 and let f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb C be a measurable T-periodic function with \int_0^T|f(t)|^2\,dt<\infty. Then \sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}|\int_0^T e^{-2\pi ikt/T}f(t)\,dt|^2\le T\int_0^T|f(t)|^2\,dt.

Local Bessel bound

Let H be a real or complex inner-product space, with inner product linear in the first argument, and let \|x\|^2=\langle x,x\rangle. Let (e_\alpha)_{\alpha\in A} be an orthonormal family, where A is any index set. With the nonnegative sum defined as the supremum of its finite subsums, \sum_{\alpha\in A}|\langle x,e_\alpha\rangle|^2\le\|x\|^2 for all x\in H.

Finite orthonormal subfamily

For any finite orthonormal subfamily enumerate its vectors as e_1,\ldots,e_n.

The inequality follows from the non-negativity of the norm of a vector. For any natural number {\displaystyle n}, let

{\displaystyle x_{n}=\sum _{k=1}^{n}\langle x,e_{k}\rangle e_{k}}

This vector {\displaystyle x_{n}} is the projection of {\displaystyle x} onto the subspace spanned by the first {\displaystyle n} basis vectors. The vector {\displaystyle x-x_{n}} is orthogonal to this subspace, and thus orthogonal to {\displaystyle x_{n}} itself. By the Pythagorean theorem for inner product spaces, we have {\displaystyle \|x\|^{2}=\|x_{n}\|^{2}+\|x-x_{n}\|^{2}}. The proof proceeds by computing {\displaystyle \|x-x_{n}\|^{2}}:

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}0\leq \left\|x-\sum _{k=1}^{n}\langle x,e_{k}\rangle e_{k}\right\|^{2}&=\left\langle x-\sum _{k=1}^{n}\langle x,e_{k}\rangle e_{k},\;x-\sum _{j=1}^{n}\langle x,e_{j}\rangle e_{j}\right\rangle \\&=\|x\|^{2}-\sum _{k=1}^{n}{\overline {\langle x,e_{k}\rangle }}\langle x,e_{k}\rangle -\sum _{j=1}^{n}\langle x,e_{j}\rangle \langle e_{j},x\rangle +\sum _{k=1}^{n}\sum _{j=1}^{n}\langle x,e_{k}\rangle {\overline {\langle x,e_{j}\rangle }}\langle e_{k},e_{j}\rangle \\&=\|x\|^{2}-\sum _{k=1}^{n}|\langle x,e_{k}\rangle |^{2}-\sum _{j=1}^{n}|\langle x,e_{j}\rangle |^{2}+\sum _{k=1}^{n}|\langle x,e_{k}\rangle |^{2}\\&=\|x\|^{2}-\sum _{k=1}^{n}|\langle x,e_{k}\rangle |^{2}\end{aligned}}}

Supremum of finite subsums

This is proved by noting that if {\displaystyle F\subseteq A} is finite, then

{\displaystyle \sum _{\alpha \in F}|\langle x,e_{\alpha }\rangle |^{2}\leq \lVert x\rVert ^{2}}

and then by definition of infinite sum

{\displaystyle \sum _{\alpha \in A}|\langle x,e_{\alpha }\rangle |^{2}=\sup {\Bigl \{}\sum _{\alpha \in F}|\langle x,e_{\alpha }\rangle |^{2}:F\subseteq A{\text{ is finite}}{\Bigr \}}\leq \lVert x\rVert ^{2}.}

Fourier specialization

Use the Fourier orthonormal system e_k(t)=T^{-1/2}e^{2\pi ikt/T}, k\in\mathbb Z, in L^2([0,T]), whose inner product is \langle f,g\rangle=\int_0^T f(t)\overline{g(t)}\,dt. Applying the local bound to this system gives

{\displaystyle \sum _{k\in \mathbb {Z} }\left\vert \int _{0}^{T}e^{-2\pi ikt/T}f(t)\,\mathrm {d} t\right\vert ^{2}\leq T\int _{0}^{T}\vert f(t)\vert ^{2}\,\mathrm {d} t.}

Real-valued specialization

For real-valued f, the complex Fourier inequality specializes to

{\displaystyle \left\vert \int _{0}^{T}f(t)\,\mathrm {d} t\right\vert ^{2}+2\sum _{k=1}^{\infty }\left\vert \int _{0}^{T}\cos(2\pi kt/T)f(t)\,\mathrm {d} t\right\vert ^{2}+2\sum _{k=1}^{\infty }\left\vert \int _{0}^{T}\sin(2\pi kt/T)f(t)\,\mathrm {d} t\right\vert ^{2}\leq T\int _{0}^{T}\vert f(t)\vert ^{2}\,\mathrm {d} t.}
