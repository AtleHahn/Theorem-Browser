Statement

Let G\leq S_n be a primitive permutation group on \{1,\ldots,n\}. Suppose G contains a permutation consisting of one cycle of length p^a and n-p^a fixed points, where p is prime, a is a positive integer, and p^a<n-2. Then G=A_n or G=S_n.

Here S_n is the group of all permutations and A_n its subgroup of even permutations. Primitive means that G is transitive and that every nonempty block is either a singleton or the whole set. A block is a subset B for which, for every g\in G, either gB=B or gB\cap B=\varnothing. The cycle hypothesis requires a single nontrivial cycle, not merely an element of order p^a; the inequality requires at least three fixed points.

Level justification

Introductory group theory suffices; primitivity is explicitly defined through translated subsets and the other notions are finite permutations and integer arithmetic.
