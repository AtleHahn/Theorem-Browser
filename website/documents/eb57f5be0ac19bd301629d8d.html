Statement

Let (C_k)_{k\ge0} be a decreasing sequence of nonempty closed and bounded subsets of \mathbb R. Then \bigcap_{k\ge0}C_k\ne\varnothing.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Understanding the statement involves closed and bounded real sets; nested sequences; countable intersection. These prerequisites fit undergraduate coursework or a brief extension of it. A bright US college student should be able to understand this statement after approximately 30 minutes of focused background research; this assesses understanding the claim, not its proof.

Proof 1: Take limits of the increasing minima

Each nonempty, closed, and bounded subset {\displaystyle C_{k}\subseteq \mathbb {R} } admits a minimal element {\displaystyle x_{k}}. Since for each {\displaystyle k}, we have

{\displaystyle x_{k+1}\in C_{k+1}\subseteq C_{k}},

it follows that

{\displaystyle x_{k}\leq x_{k+1}},

so {\displaystyle (x_{k})_{k\geq 0}} is an increasing sequence contained in the bounded set {\displaystyle C_{0}}. The monotone convergence theorem for bounded sequences of real numbers now guarantees the existence of a limit point

{\displaystyle x=\lim _{k\to \infty }x_{k}.}

For fixed {\displaystyle k}, {\displaystyle x_{j}\in C_{k}} for all {\displaystyle j\geq k}, and since {\displaystyle C_{k}} is closed and {\displaystyle x} is a limit point, it follows that {\displaystyle x\in C_{k}}. Our choice of {\displaystyle k} is arbitrary, hence {\displaystyle x} belongs to {\displaystyle {\textstyle \bigcap _{k=0}^{\infty }C_{k}}} and the proof is complete.

Proof 2: Heine–Borel followed by the compact intersection theorem

Use Heine–Borel: every closed bounded subset of \mathbb R is compact. Also use the topological intersection theorem: a decreasing sequence of nonempty compact closed subsets of a topological space has nonempty intersection. Each C_k meets these hypotheses in \mathbb R, so the conclusion follows.
