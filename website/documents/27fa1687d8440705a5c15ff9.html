Statement

Let \mathbb F be \mathbb R or \mathbb C, and let X be a Hausdorff topological vector space over \mathbb F: addition X\times X\to X and scalar multiplication \mathbb F\times X\to X are continuous, and distinct points have disjoint open neighborhoods.

The following conditions are equivalent: X is finite-dimensional; X is locally compact; and the origin has a compact neighborhood. Here a compact neighborhood contains an open neighborhood, and locally compact means that every point has such a compact neighborhood.

When these conditions hold, X is linearly homeomorphic to \mathbb F^{\dim_{\mathbb F}X} with its usual topology: there is a linear bijection between these spaces that is continuous and has continuous inverse.

Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Linear algebra and basic point-set topology supply the prerequisites. A topological vector space adds only continuity of the two familiar operations, stated explicitly; no local convexity, duality or other interdependent TVS structures are required.
