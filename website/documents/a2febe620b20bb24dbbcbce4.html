Statement

There exist flat tori that admit no finite triangulated circle packing, where a flat torus is a Euclidean plane modulo a lattice and a triangulated packing has each complementary gap bounded by three mutually tangent circles.

Proof 1: Compare countably many packing types with continuously varying shapes — outline

In the case of flat tori (the quotient spaces of the Euclidean plane by a lattice, obtained by gluing opposite edges of a parallelogram), the geometry can vary continuously through uncountably many similarity classes but, by the uniqueness of toroidal circle packings, only countably many distinct tori up to similarity have finite triangulated packings. Here there are only countably many finite triangulations, and uniqueness determines at most one torus shape for each. Thus not every flat torus admits such a packing.
