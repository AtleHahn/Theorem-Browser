Statement

Let a\in\mathbb R\setminus\mathbb Q, and give \mathbb T^2=\{(z,w)\in\mathbb C^2:|z|=|w|=1\} its usual topology. The subgroup H=\{(e^{2\pi it},e^{2\pi iat}):t\in\mathbb R\}, with the relative topology from \mathbb T^2, is not locally path connected. Consequently this topological group is not a Lie group.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

The torus and subgroup are explicit, and relative/local path topology is undergraduate topology. A Lie group adds smooth local Euclidean coordinates and smooth group operations, a limited definition here; no Lie algebra or representation theory is required.

Proof 1: Returning parallel segments obstruct local path connectedness — outline

Use the irrational-winding fact that H is dense in \mathbb T^2. In the relative topology, a small open subset of H is composed of countably infinitely many returning, almost parallel line segments on the surface of the torus. This means that H is not locally path connected: the individual segments are the local path components, but are not relative neighborhoods.

One can find points in an arbitrarily small neighborhood U in the relative topology of the identity, yet they cannot be connected to the identity with a path staying in U. The group H with the relative topology is not a Lie group, since Lie groups are locally path connected.

Level justification

The relative-topology and returning-segment argument uses undergraduate point-set topology. The complete Lie-group conclusion additionally means a smooth manifold group with smooth multiplication and inversion; this small local smooth-chart definition is needed, although only local Euclidean path connectedness is used.
