Statement

Consider an M/M/c queue in stationary steady state, where c is a positive integer or c=\infty. The arrival process is Poisson with rate \lambda>0, and service times are independent exponential random variables with a common positive rate, independent of the arrival process. There are c servers; for c=\infty every arriving customer begins service immediately. The case c=1 is the M/M/1 queue.

Then both of the following hold:

  1. The departure process is a Poisson process with rate \lambda.
  2. For every time t, the number of customers in the system at time t is independent of the entire departure process before t.

The number in the system includes customers waiting and customers in service. Stationary steady state means that the law of the queue is invariant under time translations.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The full statement requires stationary stochastic queue dynamics, Poisson point processes, and independence of a present state from an entire past process. These cumulative probability concepts are not part of the stipulated undergraduate baseline and cannot confidently be learned within the short allowance, even with local queue definitions.

Proof availability

The source gives a time-reversal argument for Poisson departures from an M/M/1 queue, but its attempted independence proof incorrectly asserts independence between a forward arrival and the later queue size. It supplies no valid full independence argument or proofs for all the multiserver and infinite-server cases stated above. The original interdeparture-time proof is described only historically.
