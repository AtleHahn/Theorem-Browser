Statement

Let X be a locally compact Hausdorff space. Let C_c(X) be the complex-valued continuous functions with compact support, and let C_0(X) be the complex-valued continuous functions vanishing at infinity: for every \varepsilon>0, the set \{x:|f(x)|\geq\varepsilon\} is compact. Equip C_0(X) with the supremum norm. A complex-linear functional is positive if its value on every real-valued nonnegative function is a nonnegative real number.

Every positive linear functional \psi on C_c(X) has a unique representing positive Borel measure \mu with the following regularity properties:

\psi(f)=\int_X f\,d\mu\quad(f\in C_c(X)),\qquad \mu(K)<\infty\quad(K\subseteq X\text{ compact}),\mu(E)=\inf_{E\subseteq U,\ U\text{ open}}\mu(U)\quad(E\text{ Borel}),

and

\mu(E)=\sup_{K\subseteq E,\ K\text{ compact}}\mu(K)

whenever E is open, or E is Borel with \mu(E)<\infty. Uniqueness is among measures satisfying these properties. The measure extends to a complete measure on a sigma-algebra containing the Borel sets (every subset of a null set is then measurable).

Every continuous complex-linear functional \psi on C_0(X) has a unique representing finite complex Borel measure \mu whose total variation |\mu| has the preceding regularity properties, and

\psi(f)=\int_X f\,d\mu\quad(f\in C_0(X)),\qquad \|\psi\|=|\mu|(X).

Here |\mu|(E) is the supremum of \sum_j|\mu(E_j)| over finite Borel partitions of E, and \|\psi\|=\sup_{\|f\|_\infty\leq1}|\psi(f)|. The functional \psi is positive if and only if its representing measure is positive.
