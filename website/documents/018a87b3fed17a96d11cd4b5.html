Statement

Let \Omega\subset\mathbb R^n be measurable with 0<|\Omega|<\infty, 1\le p<\infty, and u\in L^p(\Omega). For u_\Omega=|\Omega|^{-1}\int_\Omega u, \|u-u_\Omega\|_p^p\le\frac1{|\Omega|}\int_\Omega\int_\Omega|u(y)-u(x)|^p\,dx\,dy.

Proof 1: Apply Jensen to the average difference

Let u_\Omega=\frac1{|\Omega|}\int_\Omega u(x)\,dx. By applying Jensen’s inequality, we obtain:

\|u-u_\Omega\|_{L^p(\Omega)}^p =\left\|\frac1{|\Omega|}\int_\Omega(u(y)-u(x))\,dx\right\|_{L^p(dy)}^p =\int_\Omega\left|\frac1{|\Omega|}\int_\Omega(u(y)-u(x))\,dx\right|^p\,dy\leq\frac1{|\Omega|}\int_\Omega\int_\Omega|u(y)-u(x)|^p\,dx\,dy.
Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Apply integral Jensen to the normalized Lebesgue average and integrate in the second variable. General Lp functions and Lebesgue double integrals require measure theory.
