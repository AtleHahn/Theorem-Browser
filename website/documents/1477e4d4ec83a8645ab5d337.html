Statement

For a prime p and nonzero integer u, let \nu_p(u) be the largest k\ge0 with p^k\mid u. Let x,y be odd integers and n\ge2 be even with x^n\ne y^n. Then \nu_2(x^n-y^n)=\nu_2(x-y)+\nu_2(x+y)+\nu_2(n)-1.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The valuation is explicitly defined using elementary divisibility; the result is high-school number theory.

Proof 1: Separate the first sum in the difference-of-squares factorization — outline

Use the coprime-exponent formula: if q is prime, q\nmid uv, q\mid u-v, and q\nmid m, then \nu_q(u^m-v^m)=\nu_q(u-v), for nonzero valuation arguments. Write n=2^ab with b odd and a\ge1. Remove the odd exponent using the coprime-exponent formula. The source’s analogous difference-of-squares factorization is x^{2^a}-y^{2^a}=(x-y)(x+y)\prod_{k=1}^{a-1}(x^{2^k}+y^{2^k}). Each factor in the product is congruent to two modulo four, since x,y are odd and k\ge1. There are a-1 such factors. Adding their valuations gives the formula; for a=1 the product is empty.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Separate the first sum factor from the repeated square factorization and count the remaining factors congruent to two modulo four. Elementary modular arithmetic suffices.
