Statement

Let R be a commutative ring with identity, n\ge1 an integer and A\in M_n(R). Write \operatorname{adj}(tI_n-A)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} B_it^i. Then AB_i=B_iA for every i.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Undergraduate linear algebra supplies determinants, cofactors, matrices and permutation signs; introductory algebra supplies commutative rings and polynomial coefficients. The displayed formula uses these directly, so moving the entries from a field to a commutative ring is a small extension, with no additional structural theory required.

Proof 1: Characteristic-adjugate coefficients commute with the matrix

Local adjugate lemma

The adjugate matrix adj(M) of an {\displaystyle n\times n} matrix M is the transpose of its cofactor matrix. This is a matrix whose coefficients are given by polynomial expressions in the coefficients of M (in fact, by certain {\displaystyle (n-1)\times (n-1)} determinants), in such a way that the following fundamental relations hold,

{\displaystyle \operatorname {adj} (M)\cdot M=\det(M)I_{n}=M\cdot \operatorname {adj} (M)~.}

These relations are a direct consequence of the basic properties of determinants: evaluation of the (i, j) entry of the matrix product on the left gives the expansion by column i of the determinant of the matrix obtained from M by replacing column i by a copy of column j, which is det(M) if i = j and zero otherwise; the matrix product on the right is similar, but for expansions by rows.

Being a consequence of just algebraic expression manipulation, these relations are valid for matrices with entries in any commutative ring (commutativity must be assumed for determinants to be defined in the first place).

Compare coefficients

Put B=\operatorname{adj}(tI_n-A) and m=n-1. The two adjugate identities give B(tI_n-A)=(tI_n-A)B.

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}\left(\sum _{i=0}^{m}B_{i}t^{i}\right)\!(tI_{n}-A)&=(tI_{n}-A)\sum _{i=0}^{m}B_{i}t^{i}\\\sum _{i=0}^{m}B_{i}t^{i+1}-\sum _{i=0}^{m}B_{i}At^{i}&=\sum _{i=0}^{m}B_{i}t^{i+1}-\sum _{i=0}^{m}AB_{i}t^{i}\\\sum _{i=0}^{m}B_{i}At^{i}&=\sum _{i=0}^{m}AB_{i}t^{i}.\end{aligned}}}

Equating the coefficients shows that for each i, we have AB_i=B_iA as desired.

Level justification

The local cofactor argument proves the adjugate identity over a commutative ring. Apply it on both sides of tI-A, expand as matrices with polynomial entries, cancel the common t terms and equate coefficients. Determinants, polynomial coefficients and elementary ring algebra suffice. No Cayley–Hamilton theorem or noncommutative algebra theory is assumed.
