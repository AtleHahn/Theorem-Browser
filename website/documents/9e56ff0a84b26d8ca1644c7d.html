Statement

Let f,g be continuously differentiable in a neighborhood of a real number c, with f(c)=g(c)=0 and g^{\prime}(c)\ne0. Then \lim_{x\to c}f(x)/g(x)=f^{\prime}(c)/g^{\prime}(c)=\lim_{x\to c}f^{\prime}(x)/g^{\prime}(x).

Proof 1: Divide the two difference quotients

Suppose that {\displaystyle f} and {\displaystyle g} are continuously differentiable at a real number {\displaystyle c}, that {\displaystyle f(c)=g(c)=0}, and that {\displaystyle g'(c)\neq 0}. Then

{\displaystyle {\begin{aligned}&\lim _{x\to c}{\frac {f(x)}{g(x)}}=\lim _{x\to c}{\frac {f(x)-0}{g(x)-0}}=\lim _{x\to c}{\frac {f(x)-f(c)}{g(x)-g(c)}}\\[6pt]={}&\lim _{x\to c}{\frac {\left({\frac {f(x)-f(c)}{x-c}}\right)}{\left({\frac {g(x)-g(c)}{x-c}}\right)}}={\frac {\lim _{x\to c}\left({\frac {f(x)-f(c)}{x-c}}\right)}{\lim _{x\to c}\left({\frac {g(x)-g(c)}{x-c}}\right)}}={\frac {f'(c)}{g'(c)}}=\lim _{x\to c}{\frac {f'(x)}{g'(x)}}.\end{aligned}}}

This follows from the difference quotient definition of the derivative. The last equality follows from the continuity of the derivatives at {\displaystyle c}. The limit in the conclusion is not indeterminate because {\displaystyle g'(c)\neq 0}.
