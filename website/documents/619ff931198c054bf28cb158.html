Statement

For a real integrable measurable W on [0,1]^2, put \|W\|_\square=\sup_{S,T}|\int_{S\times T}W|, with measurable S,T\subseteq[0,1]. Then \|W\|_\square=\sup_{u,v:[0,1]\to[0,1]}|\int_{[0,1]^2}W(x,y)u(x)v(y)\,dx\,dy|, where u,v are measurable.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement requires measurable-function integration beyond ordinary calculus.

Proof 1: Indicator functions and bilinear extrema

Here's the reason for the equality in the statement to hold: By taking {\displaystyle u=\mathbb {1} _{S}} and {\displaystyle v=\mathbb {1} _{T}}, we note that the left hand side is less than or equal than the right hand side. The right hand side is less than or equal than the left hand side by bilinearity of the integrand in {\displaystyle u,v}, and by the fact that the extrema are attained for {\displaystyle u,v} taking values at {\displaystyle 0} or {\displaystyle 1}.

Level justification

The argument concerns arbitrary Lebesgue-integrable kernels and measurable bounded functions, invoking an extremal-indicator principle in that function space. This requires measure-theoretic integration beyond the baseline.
