Statement

If G,H are nonabelian simple groups, then G\times H is perfect but not simple.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Introductory group theory supplies cyclic groups, actions, products, normal subgroups and commutators. Perfect means generated by commutators, and the counted fixed points/tuples are explicit; no character theory is required.

Proof 1: Coordinate commutators and a normal factor

Each factor is generated by commutators: its commutator subgroup is nontrivial and normal, hence the whole simple group. In the product, [(a,b),(c,d)]=([a,c],[b,d]), so the coordinate commutators generate the product. The factor G\times\{1\} is a nontrivial proper normal subgroup, proving failure of simplicity.

Level justification

Use normality of the nontrivial commutator subgroups in the simple factors, compute commutators coordinatewise and exhibit a proper normal factor. Undergraduate groups suffice; perfect means generated by commutators, a small related definition.
