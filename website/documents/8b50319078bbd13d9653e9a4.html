Statement

For a,b∈R³, use the Euclidean norm and dot product, and define a\times b=(a_2b_3-a_3b_2,a_3b_1-a_1b_3,a_1b_2-a_2b_1). Then \|a\times b\|^2=\|a\|^2\|b\|^2-(a\cdot b)^2.

Level
High school only
Level justification

The coordinates of the cross product are explicitly given. Dot products and squared norms here are finite coordinate sums, so the complete identity is elementary algebra.

Proof 1: Identify the three minors with cross-product coordinates

The n=3 real Lagrange identity gives the difference \|a\|^2\|b\|^2-(a\cdot b)^2 as the sum of the squares of the three minors a_1b_2-a_2b_1, a_1b_3-a_3b_1, a_2b_3-a_3b_2. These are, up to sign and order, exactly the three defined cross-product coordinates. Their squared sum is \|a\times b\|^2.

Level
High school only
Level justification

Match the three squared minors in the supplied identity with the explicitly defined coordinates. Elementary coordinate algebra and sums of squares suffice.

Proof 2: Apply norm multiplicativity to imaginary quaternions

A quaternion p is defined as the sum of a scalar t and a vector v:

{\displaystyle p=t+\mathbf {v} =t+x\ \mathbf {i} +y\ \mathbf {j} +z\ \mathbf {k} .}

The product of two quaternions p = t + v and q = s + w is defined by

{\displaystyle pq=(st-\mathbf {v} \cdot \mathbf {w} )+s\ \mathbf {v} +t\ \mathbf {w} +\mathbf {v} \times \mathbf {w} .}

The quaternionic conjugate of p is defined by

{\displaystyle {\overline {p}}=t-\mathbf {v} ,}

and the norm squared is

{\displaystyle |p|^{2}=p{\overline {p}}=t^{2}\ +\ x^{2}+\ y^{2}\ +\ z^{2}.}

The multiplicativity of the norm in the quaternion algebra provides, for quaternions p and q:

{\displaystyle \left|pq\right|=\left|p\right|\left|q\right|.}

The quaternions p and q are called imaginary if their scalar part is zero; equivalently, if

{\displaystyle p=\mathbf {v} ,\quad q=\mathbf {w} .}

Lagrange's identity is just the multiplicativity of the norm of imaginary quaternions,

{\displaystyle |\mathbf {v} \mathbf {w} |^{2}=|\mathbf {v} |^{2}|\mathbf {w} |^{2},}

since, by definition,

{\displaystyle |\mathbf {v} \mathbf {w} |^{2}=(\mathbf {v} \cdot \mathbf {w} )^{2}+|\mathbf {v} \times \mathbf {w} |^{2}.}
Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Read the scalar and vector parts of the imaginary quaternion product and apply norm multiplicativity. Undergraduate linear algebra suffices with quaternions as explicitly defined four-coordinate multiplication, a small extension rather than a general algebra theory.
