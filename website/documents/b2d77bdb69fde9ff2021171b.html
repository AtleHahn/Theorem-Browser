Statement

Let (\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P) carry a filtration (\mathcal F_n)_{n\in I}, where I=\{0,\ldots,N\} or I=\mathbb N_0. Every adapted integrable real process X has an almost surely unique decomposition X=M+A, where M is a martingale and A is integrable and predictable with A_0=0. Predictable means A_n is \mathcal F_{n-1}-measurable for n\ge1. Explicitly, A_n=\sum_{k=1}^n(\mathbb E[X_k\mid\mathcal F_{k-1}]-X_{k-1}) and M_n=X_0+\sum_{k=1}^n(X_k-\mathbb E[X_k\mid\mathcal F_{k-1}]).

Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

The statement specifies an integrable adapted process, martingale, predictable drift and conditional-expectation formulas on a filtered probability space.

Proof 1: Conditional-increment construction and predictable-martingale uniqueness

Existence.

Using conditional expectations, define the processes A and M, for every n ∈ I, explicitly by

{\displaystyle A_{n}=\sum _{k=1}^{n}{\bigl (}\mathbb {E} [X_{k}\,|\,{\mathcal {F}}_{k-1}]-X_{k-1}{\bigr )}}

and

{\displaystyle M_{n}=X_{0}+\sum _{k=1}^{n}{\bigl (}X_{k}-\mathbb {E} [X_{k}\,|\,{\mathcal {F}}_{k-1}]{\bigr )},}

where the sums for n = 0 are empty and defined as zero. Here A adds up the expected increments of X, and M adds up the surprises, i.e., the part of every Xk that is not known one time step before. Due to these definitions, An+1 (if n + 1 ∈ I) and Mn are Fn-measurable because the process X is adapted, E[|An|] < ∞ and E[|Mn|] < ∞ because the process X is integrable, and the decomposition Xn = Mn + An is valid for every n ∈ I. The martingale property

{\displaystyle \mathbb {E} [M_{n}-M_{n-1}\,|\,{\mathcal {F}}_{n-1}]=0}    a.s.

also follows from the displayed definition of M_n, for every n ∈ I \ {0}.

Uniqueness.

To prove uniqueness, let X = M' + A' be an additional decomposition. Then the process Y := M − M' = A' − A is a martingale, implying that

{\displaystyle \mathbb {E} [Y_{n}\,|\,{\mathcal {F}}_{n-1}]=Y_{n-1}}    a.s.,

and also predictable, implying that

{\displaystyle \mathbb {E} [Y_{n}\,|\,{\mathcal {F}}_{n-1}]=Y_{n}}    a.s.

for any n ∈ I \ {0}. Since Y0 = A'0 − A0 = 0 by the convention about the starting point of the predictable processes, this implies iteratively that Yn = 0 almost surely for all n ∈ I, hence the decomposition is almost surely unique.
