Statement

Let n\ge1. A function is harmonic on an open subset of \mathbb R^n if it is twice continuously differentiable there and its Laplacian \Delta u=\sum_{j=1}^n\partial^2u/\partial x_j^2 vanishes.

If f is nonnegative and continuous on the closed Euclidean ball \overline B(x_0,R), where R>0, and harmonic in its interior, then for r=|x-x_0|<R,

\frac{1-r/R}{(1+r/R)^{n-1}}f(x_0)\le f(x)\le \frac{1+r/R}{(1-r/R)^{n-1}}f(x_0).

For a general domain \Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^n and a bounded domain \omega with \overline\omega\subset\Omega, there is a constant C>0, depending only on \Omega and \omega, such that every nonnegative harmonic function u on \Omega satisfies

\sup_{x\in\omega}u(x)\le C\inf_{x\in\omega}u(x).

Here a domain is a nonempty connected open set.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Harmonicity is defined by a sum of second partial derivatives; the remaining notions are standard multivariable calculus and point-set topology. No PDE solution theory is needed to understand the assertion.
