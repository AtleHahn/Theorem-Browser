Statement

Let C be a closed contour and let f be holomorphic and nonzero on a neighborhood of C. Then \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_C f'(z)/f(z)\,dz is the winding number of the path f(C) around 0.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Complex differentiability and contour integration are short extensions of differentiation and parametrized integrals; winding counts total argument change around zero. These linked definitions suffice without a complex-analysis course.

Proof 1: Substitution along the image contour

The contour integral {\displaystyle \oint _{C}{\frac {f'(z)}{f(z)}}\,dz} can be interpreted as 2πi times the winding number of the path f(C) around the origin, using the substitution w = f(z):

{\displaystyle \oint _{C}{\frac {f'(z)}{f(z)}}\,dz=\oint _{f(C)}{\frac {1}{w}}\,dw}

That is, it is i times the total change in the argument of f(z) as z travels around C; this follows from

{\displaystyle {\frac {d}{dz}}\log(f(z))={\frac {f'(z)}{f(z)}}}

for a local branch of the logarithm, and the relation between arguments and logarithms.

Level
Within undergraduate level
Level justification

Substitute w=f(z) in a contour integral and use the integral definition of winding number. Undergraduate complex analysis suffices; no general topological degree theory is used.
