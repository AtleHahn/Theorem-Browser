Statement

Let S\subset\mathbb Z^2 be a finite noncollinear set with integer pairwise distances, and choose noncollinear A,B,C\in S. To decide whether another integer-coordinate point can be adjoined preserving integral distances, enumerate all crossings of the loci |d(A,X)-d(B,X)|=i for 0\le i\le d(A,B) and |d(B,X)-d(C,X)|=j for 0\le j\le d(B,C), where i,j are integers. Test crossings outside S for integer coordinates and integer distances to every point of S. There are finitely many candidates, and the test finds an extension exactly when one exists.

Proof 1: Enumerate and test the conic crossings — outline

Find all crossings of the two distance-locus families and check which crossings have integer coordinates and integer distances from the given set. The conic-intersection proof puts every possible new point among these crossings and bounds their number by 4(d(A,B)+1)(d(B,C)+1). Thus the checks are finite and account for every possible extension.
