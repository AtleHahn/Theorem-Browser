Statement

Let S^3=\{x\in\mathbb R^4:\|x\|=1\} and let f:S^3\to S^3 be a smooth diffeomorphism of finite order: f^m=\operatorname{id} for some integer m\geq1. If its fixed-point set

\operatorname{Fix}(f)=\{x\in S^3:f(x)=x\}

is a knot (the image of a smooth embedding of the circle in S^3), then it is the unknot: it is smoothly ambient isotopic to a standard round circle. An ambient isotopy is a smooth family of diffeomorphisms of S^3, starting at the identity, carrying one embedded circle to the other.

Level justification

The explicit sphere and isotopy definitions make the claim accessible with undergraduate topology and multivariable calculus plus a short introduction to knots. Proof machinery is not a statement prerequisite.

Proof 1: Equivariant Ricci surgery and an unknotted fixed circle (external)

A link to the proof can be found here (§5.4 Corollary5.4 pp.29–30; §5.5 Theorem E proof p.30; construction §§2–5.3.).

Apply Theorem E to the cyclic group generated by f on S^3. Smooth conjugacy turns f into an orthogonal map. Its fixed set is the sphere’s intersection with a linear fixed subspace; since this is a circle, that subspace has dimension two. The round circle bounds a smooth disk in S^3, whose inverse image under the conjugacy is an embedded disk bounded by \operatorname{Fix}(f). The smooth disk characterization of the unknot gives ambient isotopy. The identity map cannot satisfy the knot hypothesis. The PDE step is the equivariant Ricci surgery leading to Corollary 5.4.
