Statement

Let T=\{(x,\sin(1/x)):0<x\leq1\}\cup(\{0\}\times[-1,1]) be the closed topologist’s sine curve. Projection \pi:T\to[0,1] induces isomorphisms on reduced Vietoris homology, but not on reduced singular homology in degree zero (with integer coefficients). Thus the Vietoris–Begle conclusion cannot simply be transferred to singular homology.

Level
Beyond undergraduate level
Level justification

Proof 1: Compare acyclic fibers with path components

The coordinate projection \pi(x,y)=x is a continuous surjection between compact metric spaces. Its fiber at a positive x is a point, and its fiber at zero is the vertical segment. These contractible fibers have zero reduced Vietoris homology in every degree. The Vietoris–Begle theorem states that a continuous surjection between compact metric spaces with fibers acyclic in every degree induces isomorphisms on reduced Vietoris homology in every degree. It therefore identifies the reduced Vietoris homology groups of T and [0,1].

For singular homology, use the external fact that the closed topologist's sine curve is not path connected, whereas the segment is path connected. Reduced singular H_0 detects path components, so \widetilde H_0^{\rm sing}(T)\ne0 and \widetilde H_0^{\rm sing}([0,1])=0. The projection cannot induce an isomorphism between these groups.
